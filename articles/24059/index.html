How Do FDEs Work? A Stage-by-Stage Breakdown of Remote, On-Site, and Travel Work Arrangements

Original title: FDEの働き方は？リモート・常駐・出張の実態を段階別に整理

FDE総研 · 2026-11-19 · 10 min read

Summary

This article from FDE总研, a media outlet run by VACAN, organizes how Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) actually work across three arrangements: on-site residency, regular visits, and remote work. It argues that the key metric is not the job label but how much work genuinely requires being on-site, which peaks during project kickoff and declines through build and operations phases. It notes Japanese job postings rarely quantify travel frequency, lists ambiguous posting patterns to watch for, and compares FDE workloads with SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer roles. It closes with interview questions on travel allowances, commuting location definitions, and compatibility with parenting or rural living.

Key points

  • FDE work combines residency, regular visits, and remote work by project phase.
  • On-site necessity is highest in kickoff, moderate in build, low in operations.
  • Job postings often omit travel frequency; verify details in interviews.
  • Experience level matters more than English ability for independent on-site judgment.
  • Confirm travel allowances, travel-time treatment, and stay limits in writing before joining.

What is the working style of an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? What combinations of on-site presence, visits, and remote work are involved?

According to FDE Soken's analysis, the basic working style of an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is to combine three modes—"on-site presence, regular visits, and remote work"—depending on the project stage. As shown by the original model in Palantir's official careers page, FDE is a role premised on embedding yourself at the customer's site.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer, also known as Forward Deployed Software Engineer/FDSE) is an engineering role that embeds itself at the customer's site and handles everything consistently, from problem identification through implementation to operational adoption. It is known as a role popularized by Palantir Technologies. FDE Soken is a specialized media outlet covering FDE careers and practice, operated by VACAN, Inc.

However, knowing the definition doesn't resolve the question of "do you go to the customer's site every day?" To judge an FDE's working style, you need to look not only at the form of the work location but also at the "degree of on-site necessity"—how much of the work simply cannot proceed without being on-site.

How do the three FDE work arrangements differ?

FDE work arrangements fall into three categories—"on-site presence," "regular visits," and "remote work"—and the distance from the customer and travel burden differ significantly for each.

Regular visitsOnce a week to a few times a monthProgress reviews, workshops, on-site testingModerate

Why does FDE Soken view working styles in terms of "degree of on-site necessity"?

FDE Soken believes that the "degree of on-site necessity" represents the actual burden on FDEs more accurately than the name of the work arrangement.

Even for the same "remote-friendly" job posting, in industries where customer data cannot be taken outside the company, or in settings involving physical equipment, the work itself can only be done at the customer's site. In VACAN's practice as well, for projects like congestion visualization that depend on site conditions such as facilities and sensors, there are times when requirements are hard to finalize through online meetings alone. For such projects, the deciding factor is whether to schedule on-site confirmation days in advance.

How are FDE remote work, office attendance, and business trip frequencies distributed in public job postings?

As far as FDE Soken observed in public job postings as of November 2026, many FDE postings mention business trip frequency only as "visits to customer sites" without specifying concrete percentages. Job listings are compiled on FDE Soken's job page.

The job title "FDE" is only beginning to spread in Japan, and even with the same title, the actual content varies by company. For this reason, we recommend not inferring the working style from the job title alone, but judging based on a combination of the job posting's description and what you confirm in interviews.

How are business trip and remote work conditions written in FDE job postings?

In FDE job postings, business trip and remote work conditions are often written in broad terms such as "visits to customer sites" or "remote work combined."

The four most common types of descriptions are as follows.

  • "Full remote available": However, visits to customer sites may be specified separately.
  • "Hybrid (X days per week in office)": It may not be stated whether "in office" here means your own office or the customer's site.
  • "Business trips involved": Frequency, duration, and destination range may not be stated.
  • "Possibility of being stationed at customer sites": The maximum duration may not be stated.

Note that in Palantir's US job postings for FDE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer), willingness to travel as needed is sometimes listed as a requirement. However, avoid applying overseas conditions directly to Japanese companies.

What items in FDE job postings are easy to overlook?

The easiest item to overlook in FDE job postings is the distinction between whether "in office" refers to your own office or the customer's site.

How does time spent on-site change across project stages?

According to the FDE Research Institute's analysis, the general tendency is that an FDE's time on-site is "longest during the launch phase and shortens as the project moves into the operation phase." This is because observing the customer's business to understand it can often only be done on-site. For designing remote work policies, the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's Telework Guidelines are also a useful reference.

How does an FDE's time on-site differ across the launch, build, and operation phases?

In general, an FDE's on-site presence is concentrated in the launch phase, shifts to periodic visits during the build phase, and becomes primarily remote during the operation phase.

Build phase (implementation and verification)ModerateOn-site testing, gathering user feedbackImplementation, code review, data integration
Operation phase (adoption and improvement)LowRegular retrospectives, identifying next issuesMonitoring, improvements, handling inquiries

When does an FDE's on-site stay run longer than planned?

An FDE's on-site stay tends to run long when the customer's data or systems cannot be accessed externally, and when there are many stakeholders on-site.

From the perspective of frontline practitioners, if "who decides what" is not settled during the launch phase, visits for confirmation tend to increase. When estimating the risk of prolonged engagements in VACAN's practice, we make sure to check the following two points in advance.

  • Whether remote access to the customer's environment is permitted
  • Whether the person with decision-making authority on-site is clearly identified

How do answers about an FDE's working style change by years of experience, current role, English proficiency, and company size?

In the FDE Research Institute's comparison framework, an FDE's working style changes based on four conditions: "years of experience," "current role," "English proficiency," and "company size." Among these, we believe years of experience has the greatest influence, as it determines whether you are entrusted with on-site decision-making. For application procedures, please check each company's official recruiting page.

How does an FDE's working style change by years of experience and current role?

FDEs with less experience tend to do more on-site work accompanied by senior colleagues. On the other hand, people with customer-facing experience in their current role are likely to be entrusted with solo visits at an earlier stage.

How does an FDE's working style change by English proficiency and company size?

FDEs with high English proficiency are more likely to handle overseas customers, and the larger the company, the wider the geographic range of projects they are likely to cover.

When conditions conflict, for example in a case like "high English proficiency but few years of experience," the FDE Research Institute recommends prioritizing years of experience in your judgment. This is because overseas projects combine travel burdens with time differences, and without experience making decisions independently on-site, the workload can easily become excessive.

Differences by company size are as follows.

  • Startups: The geographic range of assigned projects is often limited, but the range of phases one person covers tends to be broader.
  • Large companies and foreign-affiliated firms: Responsibilities may be divided by phase, and the frequency of business trips varies by assignment.

How does the burden of business trips and on-site assignments differ between FDEs and SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers? Who is not a good fit?

In the FDE Research Institute's comparison framework, FDEs differ from SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers in how the burden falls, because they are expected to handle "on-site judgment" and "hands-on implementation" simultaneously. The archetypes of these roles are introduced in the Palantir official blog.

FDEs can be distinguished from other adjacent roles in that both their time on-site and their ratio of implementation are moderate to high.

Who is not well suited to the FDE working style?

FDE is not a good fit for people who have difficulty accepting sudden changes to plans, and for those who want to focus solely on implementation.

The conditions for and against suitability as seen by the FDE Research Institute are as follows (selection criteria: on-site necessity, travel burden, implementation ratio; evaluation date November 19, 2026).

  • Suitable conditions: Finds fulfillment in identifying issues at the customer's site. Can accept that the working style changes by phase.
  • Unsuitable conditions: Wants to work in the same place with the same rhythm every week. Wants to avoid situations involving direct conversation with customers as much as possible.

What should you verify regarding travel allowances, travel time, and balancing the role with living in a rural area or childcare?

The FDE Research Institute recommends that when considering a career move to an FDE role, you confirm three points in writing before joining: "travel allowances," "how travel time is treated," and "the upper limit on on-site stays." The tax treatment of travel expenses is explained in the National Tax Agency's Tax Answer No. 2508.

What should you verify about FDE travel allowances and the treatment of travel time?

Travel allowances and travel time for FDEs are handled differently by each company, so you need to check the specifics in the work rules and travel expense regulations.

  • Standards for per diem and accommodation allowances (fixed amount or actual expenses)
  • Whether travel time is counted as working hours
  • Whether substitute days off are granted for travel on holidays
  • How temporary trips home are handled during long-term stays

Can you work as an FDE even if you live outside a major city or are raising children?

Even for FDEs living outside major cities or raising children, working at a company that can adjust the phases or regions they cover makes balancing work and life a real possibility.

What determines how easy it is to balance work and life is not so much the frequency of business trips themselves, but how far in advance schedules are decided. From the perspective of field practitioners, whether visit schedules for the launch phase are shared early on directly affects whether they can be coordinated with family plans. For information on systems that support balancing work and childcare, see the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's guide to the Child Care and Family Care Leave Act.

In the interview, asking the following three questions will give you more material for making a decision.

  • Whether assignments take the covered region into account
  • Whether the launch phase is handled on a rotating basis
  • What kind of working arrangements members with children use when taking on projects
