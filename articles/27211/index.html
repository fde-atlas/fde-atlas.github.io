Today's Judgment

Both domestic signals today emphasize the same thing: first pick the right small scenario and write clear, verifiable goals, then let reusable assets and real results expand step by step.

The advice distilled from Beijing's AI salon for SMEs is plain: prioritize mature workbenches, start with high-frequency, low-complexity single-point scenarios, have the business owner use them heavily and personally, and cultivate AI talent from existing business backbone staff. Nantong, meanwhile, pushed 53 state-owned enterprise AI supply-demand scenarios to service providers, with some scenarios already reaching preliminary cooperation intentions.

One starts from the demand side of SMEs, the other from the scenario supply side of state-owned assets, yet both arrive at the same path: don't start with an all-encompassing private deployment platform; first let one small scenario produce verifiable results.

Key Signals

Beijing SME AI Salon: Start from high-frequency, low-complexity scenarios, cultivate AI talent from business backbone staff

  • Source: Beijing Municipal Government / Beijing Daily
  • The Beijing Municipal Government published a report on the "2026 Autumn AI Digitalization Salon" on September 25. Participants summarized common sticking points for SMEs: fragmented general-purpose tools, unconnected data, private deployment solutions exceeding actual needs, unclear ROI, and a lack of backbone staff who understand both the business and how to use AI. On-site advice: prioritize mature standardized workbenches, start with high-frequency, low-complexity single-point scenarios; have the business owner use them heavily and personally; select and cultivate AI application talent from existing business backbone staff. Follow-up activities plan to continuously open real scenarios and connect research and financial resources.
  • Why it matters: These sticking points cover nearly all the main reasons SME AI projects fail. "Private deployment solutions exceeding actual needs" deserves particular vigilance from service providers: many projects get steered toward heavy-investment platform building before requirements are even clarified. The advice to "cultivate from business backbone staff" also aligns with the thinking of Shanghai's enterprise technology center FDE general class: business backbone staff serve as scenario owners, while FDEs connect the process, data, evals, and delivery. Note: this is a government-reposted salon recommendation, not a sample survey; no participating companies, project counts, or before/after metrics were published.

Nantong Releases 53 State-Owned Enterprise AI Supply-Demand Scenarios: Some Reach Preliminary Cooperation Intentions

  • Source: Jiangsu Provincial People's Government / Nantong Municipal Government Office
  • Nantong's municipal enterprises collectively released the first batch of 53 AI supply-demand scenarios, including 32 application demands and 21 supply capabilities, covering manufacturing, port logistics, energy and environmental protection, elderly care, urban operations and maintenance, safety monitoring, warehousing and supply chain, and cultural tourism. Sunshine Elderly Care, Jiangshan Co., and Tongyang Port released demands on-site; smart grain storage for reserve grain and Tongyang Port's smart safety monitoring "reached preliminary cooperation intentions" after matchmaking.
  • Why it matters: Releasing demands and supplies simultaneously at the same event is one way the state-owned asset system turns "enterprises wanting to use AI" into an accessible entry point. Like Hunan's scenario opportunity cards and Beijing's three lists, it solves the "findability" problem. The FDE's next step is not to quote prices immediately, but to complete each card with pain-point metrics, data permissions, business owners, technical boundaries, pilots, engineering acceptance, business acceptance, and procurement paths. State-owned enterprise projects also require extra attention to procurement compliance, qualifications, payment schedules, change control, and final payment acceptance responsibility. Note: "preliminary intention" does not mean a contract or launch; task briefs, service providers, amounts, and acceptance results have not been made public.

Supplementary Signals: Service Provider Resource Pool Recruitment and Plugin Ecosystem

  • Source: Shanghai Municipal Government; Anthropic / Claude
  • The Shanghai Municipal Commission of Economy and Informatization is recruiting AI application service providers and service teams, with the service scope explicitly including consulting and planning, delivery and implementation, operations management, and security governance. Anthropic has opened a submission portal for the Claude plugin directory; enterprise tools, agents, and service partners are beginning to distribute through the plugin directory.
  • Why it matters: The government resource pool writing "delivery and implementation" and "operations management" into service providers' capability scope shows that services recognized by procurement parties now go beyond consulting and development. The plugin directory represents another supply model: capabilities are productized and distributed through platforms. Both are just entry points; being selected for the resource pool or listed in the plugin directory does not mean having projects or results.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Converge the first phase with new clients to five "ones": one high-frequency task, one responsible person, one data boundary, one baseline, one acceptance checkpoint. When a client proposes private deployment or middle-platform requirements, first ask them to provide existing systems, data, permissions, total cost, and exit conditions before discussing solutions.
  • Don't let the model guess what can be measured; don't let the model repeatedly review what rules can validate: delivery of content, moderation, and quality-inspection AI agents can be designed along this evidence chain: deterministic measurement → assertions with time anchors → confidence and conflict lists → gold-standard regression → state ledger and rule validation → finished-product read-back comparison. Large models handle semantic judgment and conflict arbitration; measurable fields go to deterministic tools.
  • Treat government supply-demand scenarios as leads and complete them per the opportunity card: only enter pricing after all eight items—pain-point metrics, data permissions, business owner, technical boundaries, pilot, engineering acceptance, business acceptance, and procurement path—are complete; for items missing, only do paid diagnostics or keep observing.
