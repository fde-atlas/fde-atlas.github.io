Forrester: about $9 billion committed to embedded engineers

Kernavique · 2026-07-30 · 2 min read

The items are Anthropic's Ode venture ($1.5 billion), OpenAI's Deployment Company ($4 billion, 19 backers), AWS ($1 billion), and the Microsoft Frontier Company ($2.5 billion, 6,000 people). On July 30, 2026, Forrester analyst Ashutosh Sharma wrote that four technology providers committed roughly $9 billion to embedding engineers with customers between May 4 and July 15, 2026. The post lists Anthropic’s $1.5 billion venture, OpenAI’s Deployment Company with $4 billion, AWS with $1 billion and the Microsoft Frontier Company with $2.5 billion. The figure is Forrester’s sum of those announcements.

Happened . Written up by Kernavique on . Source: Forrester.

What happened

Forrester lists the parts as follows: Anthropic’s $1.5 billion venture, launched July 15 as Ode with Anthropic; OpenAI’s Deployment Company, with $4 billion of initial investment and 19 backing firms; AWS, with $1 billion committed June 30; and the Microsoft Frontier Company, with $2.5 billion and 6,000 people.

Separately, Forrester notes Google Cloud’s $750 million partner ecosystem commitment. It also describes Uber sending 30 AI-proficient engineers into finance, legal and HR, forming sixteen “agentic pods” over two months. The post, as we read it, does not state where the Uber figures come from.

Forrester argues that temporary engagements with vendor engineers are right for today, and that the capability should move inside the enterprise. This is the analyst firm’s own post, and the $9 billion is not new spending data.

“The next constraint on growth is no longer just model performance but organizational readiness.”

Our reading

What it means

The facts above are the source’s. What follows is Kernavique’s reading of them.

If you are a forward deployed engineer

The post gives no roles, places or pay for forward deployed engineers. It records large sums committed by four providers, while Forrester’s own view is that these vendor engagements are temporary.

If you are hiring them

Forrester’s advice is to treat vendor engineers as temporary and build the capability in-house. If you follow it, the four providers’ commitments may add to the competition for the engineers you would need to hire.
