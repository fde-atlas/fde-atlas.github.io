Can Freshers Become AI Forward Deployed Engineers?

Cloud Soft Solutions · 2026-11-12 · 9 min read

Summary

This guide from Cloud Soft Solutions argues that fresh graduates can become AI Forward Deployed Engineers, but cautions that roles titled FDE often require industry experience, so beginners should target adjacent entry-level jobs like Junior GenAI Engineer or RAG Engineer first. It lays out a sequenced skill path—Python, LLMs, RAG, agentic AI, AWS/Azure, DevOps—recommends two or three complete, deployed enterprise projects over quick demos, and offers a 90-day roadmap ending in a portfolio. It closes by promoting the company's FDE PRO training program in Hyderabad. The piece is honest that the plan is intensive and placement isn't guaranteed.

  • Freshers can enter the field, but direct FDE roles usually ask for experience.
  • Learn in order: Python, LLMs, RAG, agentic AI, AWS/Azure, then DevOps.
  • Build two or three complete projects with deployment, security and evaluation.
  • A 90-day roadmap covers Python basics through a deployed project portfolio.

Companies no longer only want people who understand machine learning or can write prompts. They want engineers who can build AI applications, connect them to enterprise systems, deploy them on the cloud and solve a real business problem. That role is called Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE).

Students and recent graduates often ask: can a fresher become an AI Forward Deployed Engineer without industry experience?

Yes, you can build the skills and the practical experience this career needs. Becoming a production-ready FDE takes more than an AI course or a certificate, though, and many FDE job postings ask for experience. This guide is honest about that. It covers the entry-level roles you can apply for today, the skills to learn in order, the projects that impress interviewers, and a 90-day plan.

The idea behind it: don't just learn AI. Learn to build, deploy and deliver AI solutions that companies hire for.

1. What is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

A Forward Deployed Engineer works closely with customers and business teams and turns real business problems into working software. An AI FDE combines several disciplines:

  • Python application development
  • Generative AI and large language models (LLMs)
  • Agentic AI and agent workflows
  • Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
  • AWS, Azure and cloud architecture
  • APIs and enterprise integrations
  • Docker and Kubernetes
  • Terraform (infrastructure as code)
  • CI/CD and DevOps
  • AI security, monitoring and evaluation

Example: a bank wants to automate customer support with AI. A chatbot developer might build a bot that answers basic questions. An AI FDE owns the whole outcome:

  • understanding the bank's requirements;
  • connecting the knowledge base;
  • building the RAG application and integrating the bank's APIs;
  • adding security guardrails;
  • deploying the solution on AWS or Azure;
  • monitoring how well it works.

For the full definition, read What is a Forward Deployed Engineer? and FDE vs AI Engineer.

2. Can freshers get FDE jobs without experience?

Yes, but there is a difference between learning FDE skills and being hired straight into a role titled "Forward Deployed Engineer". Many companies expect FDE candidates to have experience in software development, cloud deployment, enterprise integration and talking to customers.

So target both direct FDE openings and the related entry-level AI roles that lead to FDE work:

  • Junior Generative AI Engineer
  • Python AI Developer
  • Junior Agentic AI Engineer
  • AI Application Developer
  • Junior RAG Engineer
  • Cloud AI Engineer
  • AI Implementation Engineer
  • AI Solutions Engineer
  • Junior Backend Developer – AI
  • Associate Forward Deployed Engineer

Job titles and eligibility vary by employer. Some accept a strong project portfolio; others insist on industry experience. What matters is building skills that let you move from an entry-level AI engineering job into an FDE role. You can see which of these roles are open right now on our jobs board for freshers and the Forward Deployed Engineer jobs page.

3. Why AI and FDE skills are creating new jobs

Companies are experimenting with AI in banking, healthcare, insurance, retail, manufacturing, education and IT operations. Putting AI into an enterprise environment is hard, so they need engineers who can:

  • connect AI models to existing business applications;
  • build enterprise knowledge assistants;
  • automate repetitive business workflows;
  • build AI agents that work with APIs and databases;
  • protect enterprise data;
  • deploy AI applications on AWS and Azure;
  • monitor performance and answer quality;
  • control infrastructure and model costs.

Typical use cases by industry:

Industry Example AI use cases
Banking Assistants for loan documents, customer support and policy lookup
Healthcare Medical document search, admin workflow automation, knowledge assistants
Insurance Claims document processing, policy lookup, customer service automation
IT operations Agents for incident analysis, log investigation and ticket classification
Retail Product recommendation assistants, support, inventory workflows

4. Skills freshers must learn, in order

Follow a structured path instead of jumping straight into advanced AI frameworks. FDE engineer skills employers look for explains how to prove each one.

Skill 1: Python programming

Learn:

  • fundamentals, data structures and functions;
  • object-oriented programming;
  • exception and file handling;
  • REST APIs with FastAPI;
  • testing, logging and debugging.

Hands-on: a Python REST API for an AI-powered customer support app.

Skill 2: Generative AI and LLMs

Learn:

  • how LLMs work: tokens, embeddings and transformers;
  • prompt engineering, including zero-shot and few-shot prompting;
  • temperature and other model parameters;
  • context windows and inference;
  • hallucinations and other limits.

Hands-on: a document question-answering app using an LLM.

Skill 3: Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

Learn:

  • document ingestion and text extraction;
  • chunking;
  • embeddings and vector databases;
  • semantic and hybrid search;
  • reranking;
  • retrieval evaluation;
  • grounded answers with citations.

Hands-on: an enterprise knowledge assistant over company documents. The pgvector RAG tutorial is a good start.

Skill 4: Agentic AI and multi-agent systems

Learn:

  • AI agents, tool calling and function calling;
  • LangChain and LangGraph;
  • state management and conditional routing;
  • multi-agent design;
  • human-in-the-loop approvals;
  • the Model Context Protocol (MCP).

Hands-on: an IT support agent that investigates incidents, pulls runbooks and drafts fixes for a human to approve.

Skill 5: AWS and Azure

AWS Azure
EC2, S3, IAM Virtual Machines, Storage, Microsoft Entra ID
Lambda, API Gateway Azure Functions
Amazon Bedrock, Bedrock Knowledge Bases, Bedrock Agents, Guardrails Microsoft Foundry (Azure OpenAI models, agents), Azure AI services
OpenSearch Azure AI Search
CloudWatch Azure Monitor
EKS Azure Kubernetes Service

Get confident on one cloud first, then add the second. See AWS for AI engineers and Azure for AI engineers.

Skill 6: DevOps and cloud-native engineering

Building the AI app is only half the job; you also have to deploy and run it. Learn:

  • Git and GitHub;
  • Linux;
  • Docker and Kubernetes;
  • Jenkins or GitHub Actions;
  • Terraform;
  • CI/CD pipelines;
  • monitoring and logging;
  • cloud security and IAM.

Hands-on: containerise a RAG app, provision its infrastructure with Terraform, and deploy it through an automated pipeline.

5. Projects that help freshers get AI jobs

Build two or three complete projects, with documentation, security, testing, deployment and evaluation. That beats five quick demos. More ideas are in 10 AI engineer projects every FDE should build.

Project 1: Banking AI knowledge assistant

  • Tech: Python, Amazon Bedrock, Knowledge Bases, S3, OpenSearch, Guardrails, Terraform.
  • Build: upload and index policy documents; answer with source references; add role-based access and protection against prompt injection; log activity; evaluate answer quality.

Project 2: Multi-agent IT operations assistant

  • Tech: Python, LangGraph, AWS, Docker, Kubernetes.
  • Build: read alerts; retrieve runbooks; analyse logs; suggest likely causes and fixes; ask a human to approve risky actions; keep an execution history.

Project 3: Enterprise HR assistant

  • Tech: Microsoft Foundry (Azure OpenAI models), Azure AI Search, FastAPI, Microsoft Entra ID.
  • Build: answer policy, leave and payroll questions with citations; require sign-in; show each employee only the documents they're allowed to see; monitor responses.
  • Guide: Foundry RAG with Azure AI Search.

Project 4: AI customer support automation

  • Tech: Python, RAG, LangGraph, REST APIs, AWS.
  • Build: classify queries; draft replies; integrate with a ticketing system; escalate hard cases; track support metrics.

Project 5: Production-ready GenAI app with DevOps

  • Tech: AWS, Python, Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, Jenkins, monitoring.
  • Build: containerise the app; provision the infrastructure; set up CI/CD; add alerts; track latency and model cost.

6. A practical 90-day roadmap

Days Focus Deliverable
1–15 Python, data structures, FastAPI, SQL/PostgreSQL, Git, Linux A working Python app with REST APIs and a database
16–30 LLMs, prompting, embeddings, document processing, RAG, vector databases A document-based knowledge assistant
31–45 LangChain, LangGraph, agents, tool integration, MCP basics, human approvals A working multi-step agent
46–60 AWS or Azure: deployment, IAM and security, cloud AI services, logging A cloud-hosted AI app
61–75 Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, CI/CD, monitoring, troubleshooting An automated cloud deployment
76–90 One enterprise project end to end, architecture diagrams, documentation, GitHub portfolio, system design, mock interviews, an FDE-focused résumé A portfolio of deployed projects with code, diagrams, docs and demos

Note: this is an intensive plan, not a promise that you'll be job-ready or placed in 90 days. Progress depends on your programming background, practice time and how deep your projects go. For the longer, staged path, see the FDE engineer roadmap.

8. What companies expect from freshers

Recruiters want to know whether you can do real engineering work:

  • Programming: can you build a Python application without relying on AI-generated code?
  • AI knowledge: can you explain how RAG, embeddings, LLMs and agents work?
  • Cloud: can you deploy an app on AWS or Azure?
  • Integration: can you connect an app to external APIs and databases?
  • Security: do you understand authentication, authorisation, secrets and data protection?
  • DevOps: can you containerise and deploy an app?
  • Communication: can you understand a business requirement and explain your solution?
  • Problem-solving: can you troubleshoot failures and improve the system?

A candidate who shows these skills through real projects usually beats one who only lists certificates. Present them well with the FDE résumé and portfolio guide, and practise with 150 FDE interview questions.

9. Can non-IT freshers become AI FDEs?

Yes, but expect the journey to take longer. Graduates from BCA, B.Sc, B.Com, B.Tech and other backgrounds can move into AI engineering, depending on their programming foundation and each employer's eligibility rules. Work through these stages in order:

  • Programming fundamentals
  • Python development
  • SQL and databases
  • Basic computer science
  • APIs and back-end development
  • Generative AI and RAG
  • Cloud and deployment
  • Enterprise projects

What counts is being able to show practical skills. See also non-IT to software jobs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a fresher become an AI Forward Deployed Engineer?
Yes. Freshers can build the required skills and apply for entry-level FDE, AI engineering and implementation roles. Direct FDE positions often ask for industry experience.

Is FDE a good career option in 2026?
It suits engineers who enjoy building applications, integrating AI, deploying on the cloud and solving customer problems. The skills stay useful even when companies use different job titles.

Is Python mandatory for FDE engineers?
Python is the most useful language for AI engineering, along with APIs, databases and solid software engineering.

Do freshers need AWS or Azure?
Learning at least one major cloud platform well is highly recommended; add the second later.

Is Agentic AI enough to get a job?
No. You also need programming, APIs, databases, cloud, deployment, security and AI evaluation skills.

How many projects should a fresher build?
Two or three complete enterprise-style projects with deployment, documentation, testing and evaluation.

Does Cloud Soft Solutions provide placement assistance?
Training includes placement-oriented preparation such as résumé guidance, portfolio development and mock interviews. Placement assistance is not a guaranteed job; selection depends on the candidate and the employer.

Can DevOps engineers transition into FDE?
Yes. DevOps engineers already know cloud, Terraform, Kubernetes, CI/CD and monitoring; adding Python, RAG, LLMs and agentic AI moves them toward AI engineering roles.
