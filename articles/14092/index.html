Remote forward deployed engineer jobs: what remote means

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 16 min read

Summary

Based on a September 2026 census of 103 job boards, this article examines what "remote" actually means for forward deployed engineer roles. Of 806 FDE postings, only 138 (17.1%) were remote-eligible, and even remote-listed roles typically require substantial travel—Glean's posting demands 25–50%. Large employers like OpenAI, Palantir and Google had no remote-flagged FDE postings, while ElevenLabs (30 of 38) and Databricks (14 of 117) had many. The article also documents cases where structured job-board fields contradict the posting text, and advises readers to confirm remote arrangements with recruiters in writing.

Key points

  • Only 138 of 806 FDE postings (17.1%) were remote-eligible in the September 2026 census.
  • ElevenLabs had 30 of 38 postings remote; OpenAI, Palantir and Google had none flagged remote.
  • Remote FDE roles still involve travel: Glean 25–50%, Cohere up to 40%, MongoDB up to 30%.
  • Structured fields and descriptions often conflict, as with Microsoft's remote-flagged postings requiring office days.
  • PostHog is a rare fully remote, async FDE role focused on automation rather than customer site visits.

How many FDE postings are remote-eligible

In September 2026 we ran a census that read 103 employer job boards and found 806 live postings whose titles match the FDE patterns, from 92 employers. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data

Our count marked a posting remote-eligible when the job board’s remote field said so, or when a location string contained “remote”. It did not read the descriptions, and hybrid did not count. On that basis, our count found 138 of 806 remote-eligible. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data

The share barely moves when you narrow it. In our count, 72 of 415 US-located postings (17.3%) and 99 of 650 engineering-titled postings (15.2%) were remote-eligible. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data

Three limits to keep in mind:

  • The unit is a posting, not a person. In our count, the same role can appear as several postings, one per requisition, and each counts. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data
  • Some employers are missing. Our census did not include Accenture, Deloitte and other employers without a public job API. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data
  • Keyword-searched boards may be undercounted. Google, Microsoft, Amazon and the Workday boards, Salesforce among them, were read by keyword search rather than whole in our census. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data

Another tracker’s figure

FDE Pulse, a job-market tracker, reported that 99 of the 1,321 FDE postings it tracked as of April 2026 (7%) listed the role as remote-eligible. Source 8Forward Deployed Engineer Hiring Trends 2026PublisherFDE PulseSource typeindependent analysis

FDE Pulse uses its own method and a different date, so its figure and ours measure different things. Don’t set them side by side.

Remote, except at the customer

An FDE works inside a customer’s systems and with the customer’s people. A remote label tells you where you live between visits. The travel line tells you how many visits there are.

Here is what remote-listed postings say about travel:

  • Glean, “Remote - US”: as of September 2026, the role requires 25–50% travel. Source 1Founding Forward Deployed EngineerPublisherGleanSource typecompany job posting
  • Cohere: its FDE postings, including some listed as remote, state travel of up to 20–40% to work on-site with customers and partners.   • ElevenLabs, North America: remote-first anywhere in the United States, with a preference for New York or San Francisco, and “Expect regular travel for customer onsites”, with no percentage. ElevenLabs’ board as a whole reads the same way. In our count, 30 of its 38 postings were marked remote and all 38 mentioned travel. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data

Across our whole census, 512 of 806 postings mentioned travel somewhere in the description. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data

The location line hides more than the word remote:

  • Country and time zone. “Remote - US” is not “remote anywhere”, and “based remotely in the United States (East Coast)” narrows it again. Check that you can work in the country the posting names.
  • A preferred city. “Preference for New York or San Francisco” tells you where the office is, and where the team may gather. It is worth asking how much that preference weighs.

For what a travel percentage means in weeks away, and how it gets spread across a quarter, read how much forward deployed engineers travel.

When a posting’s remote fields disagree

A job posting has two layers. The structured fields (workplace type, location, remote flag) are what a board’s remote filter usually reads. The description is what the hiring team wrote. The two do not always agree.

  • Microsoft. In our count, 17 of its 29 FDE postings had a work-site field reading “0 days / week in-office - remote”, while its workLocationOption field read “onsite”. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data Some of its FDE postings say employees who live within fifty miles of the Redmond campus must be in the office at least 3 days a week, while their work-site field reads remote. Source 19Microsoft Careers: Senior Forward Deployed Engineer (WPD)PublisherMicrosoftSource typecompany job posting
  • ElevenLabs, Brazil. The text says the role is remote-first anywhere in Brazil, with a São Paulo preference, and the job-board record marks the workplace type as OnSite. Source 20ElevenLabs job boardPublisherElevenLabs (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job posting
  • Cursor, US. The posting’s primary location is San Francisco, with New York and Remote (United States) as secondary locations, and its workplace type is OnSite. Source 21Forward Deployed EngineerPublisherCursor (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job postingSource 22CareersPublisherCursorSource typecompany hiring page
  • Baseten. The FDE posting is based in San Francisco, lists Remote (United States) among its secondary locations, and has a workplace type of Hybrid. Source 23Forward Deployed Engineer @ BasetenPublisherBaseten (Ashby)Source typecompany job posting

We can’t tell you which layer is right for any of these. Nor can the filter. What it means for you:

  • A remote filter can hide a posting that is open to remote candidates in its text, and show you one whose text asks for office days.
  • A “Remote” secondary location is a question to ask, not an answer.
  • Ask the recruiter which applies to your location, and get the answer in writing before you plan around it.

The fully remote exception

One company describes a fully remote FDE on purpose. PostHog describes its first FDE as fully remote, async and focused on building automation, and calls it “a weird experiment for those used to the typical FDE setup”. Source 24WTF is a forward deployed engineer? (and why everyone is hiring them)PublisherPostHog (Jina Yoon)Source typecompany blog

Note what that sentence is: one company describing its own role. It does not show that fully remote FDE work is spreading. It does show a different shape of job: in PostHog’s description, the role is async and focused on building automation, not on weeks at a customer site.

If you find a posting that says fully remote with no travel, ask how customer work happens instead. Who runs the discovery calls? How does the FDE see the customer’s environment? Who is in the room when something breaks? The answers tell you whether it is an FDE role or a support role with a new title.

How to search for remote FDE roles

A good search takes a little longer than setting a filter.

  • Search every title, not one. Our census matched “forward deployed”, “deployed engineer”, “”, “ deployment engineer” and the abbreviation “FDE”, and some postings were caught only by the abbreviation. Source 2Our analysis: FDE posting censusPublisherfdeinterviewprep.com researchSource typeour analysis of public data The lesson on who hires FDEs, and under which titles lists the variants.
  • Filter for remote, then open everything. For each posting, copy three lines: the location, the workplace type, and the travel sentence.
  • Also search on-site and hybrid postings for “Remote”. As the Cursor and Baseten examples show, a Remote secondary location can sit under an OnSite or Hybrid label.
  • Read the posting on the employer’s own board. That is where you see the secondary locations and the workplace type, which is where the examples in this post come from.
  • Check the country. Some remote roles are tied to one country: Databricks’ FDE ladder includes a Staff Forward Deployed Engineer posting in Remote India. Source 25Sr. Staff Forward Deployed Engineer, SingaporePublisherDatabricks (Greenhouse)Source typecompany job postingSource 26Sr. Manager, AI Forward Deployed Engineering (AI FDE), United StatesPublisherDatabricks (Greenhouse)Source typecompany job posting
  • Read the clearance and citizenship lines on any public-sector posting before you apply.

Our lesson on how to read an FDE job posting line by line turns the reading steps into a worksheet. For how many FDE postings there are overall, and how to read growth claims about them, see whether FDE demand is real.

Keep a remote log

For every posting you save, log four things: remote label, stated travel, preferred city, and whether the fields agree with the text. After a dozen postings you will see which employers fit the life you want, and you will have your questions ready.

What to ask before you apply

Ask these in the recruiter screen, in these words or your own:

  • “The posting says remote. Where are the customers I’d be working with, and how often would I be on site with them?”
  • “Is the travel figure spread evenly, or does it come in blocks around launches?”
  • “The board lists this as on-site, but the description says remote-first. Which applies if I live where I do now?”
  • “Is there a preferred city, and does living elsewhere change the level, the pay or the accounts I’d get?”
  • “Do remote team members come to an office for onboarding or team gatherings?”
  • “If my main account moves to a customer in another region, what happens to the role?”

The onboarding question has a published example. Baseten’s blog post “Ten reasons to join Baseten”, last updated in December 2025, says the whole team meets in person for offsites twice a year, and that remote teammates get a travel-to-office budget. Source 27Ten reasons to join BasetenPublisherBasetenSource typecompany blog Ask whether the employer you are talking to has something similar.

When they ask you about travel

Expect the question back. A recruiter may ask what travel you can commit to, and “I’m flexible” is the weakest answer, because it gives away the one thing you are trying to protect. A stronger shape:

“I’m set up to work remotely from Chicago. I can be on site with customers every month, including longer stretches around a launch. What I can’t do is live out of a hotel for most of a quarter. What does the travel on this team look like in practice?”

It states a limit, shows you understand the job, and turns the question back to facts. Our question page on what travel you can commit to has a model answer and the follow-ups, and why this company? helps when the next question is why you want a remote role there in particular.

Before you apply to a remote FDE role

  • You read the travel sentence, not only the location
  • You checked whether the workplace type matches the description
  • You know which country, time zone or city the posting names
  • You checked the clearance and citizenship lines
  • You have a travel limit you can say out loud
  • You have three questions from the list above ready for the recruiter

A remote role still puts you in front of customers, and our curriculum trains the skill that work runs on: scoping a customer’s problem, building inside their constraints and explaining it to them.
