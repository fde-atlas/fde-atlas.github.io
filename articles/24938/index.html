Why FDE Is Essential for Driving DX | Delivering Results in the Field

Original title: DX推進にFDEが必要な理由｜現場で成果を出す

日本Fde協会 · 2026-07-05 · 7 min read

Summary

Written by the head of the Japan FDE Association, this article argues that digital transformation (DX) efforts stall because companies lack people who can bridge strategy and implementation. It identifies three common barriers: strategies with no one to implement them, systems that frontline staff refuse to use, and proofs of concept that never reach production. Comparing in-house SEs, SIers, consultants, and FDEs, the author contends only FDEs can handle problem discovery, implementation, and on-site adoption end to end. Practical advice includes securing executive commitment, starting small in one department, and combining FDEs with existing consultants and SIers.

Key points

  • DX stalls at three walls: no implementers, unused systems, PoCs stuck before production.
  • Only FDEs cover discovery, implementation, and adoption in one role; other roles cover parts.
  • Real problems surface on-site, e.g., manual data re-entry across three systems at a manufacturer.
  • Start small in one department with executive commitment, then expand organically.
  • Prototype in 1-2 weeks; full production adoption typically takes 3-6 months.

"We're working on digital transformation, but we're not seeing results"—I've heard this from executives and DX leaders across every industry. They had consultants create a strategy. They ordered systems from an SIer. They even got their in-house SEs to cooperate. Yet "the field won't use it" and "it ends at PoC." As the Representative Director of the Japan FDE Association, I've found the cause of this problem is always the same: there is no one in the field who can bridge the gap between strategy and implementation.

In this article, I explain why FDE talent is essential for DX initiatives. By the time you finish reading, you should be able to explain to your management team how FDEs can contribute to your company's DX efforts. If you're hearing the term "FDE" for the first time, it's best to start with "What Is an FDE? A Practitioner Explains the Differences from SEs and SIers".

Three Barriers That Stall DX Initiatives

Barrier 1: There's a Strategy, but No One to Implement It

The DX strategy documents produced by consulting firms may be impressive, but there's no one who can translate them into the company's actual systems and workflows. This is the most common barrier. A SaaS company executive once consulted me, saying, "We spent tens of millions of yen on consultants to create a strategy, but no one in-house can implement it." The strategy document went straight into a desk drawer. Unless there are businesspeople who understand technology, or engineers who understand the business, on the ground, a DX strategy remains a pie in the sky.

Barrier 2: The System Was Built, but the Field Won't Use It

You ordered a DX system from an SIer, but field staff say "the old way was easier" and won't use it. This "last mile" problem is DX's greatest enemy. Most of the time, the cause is that field input was never reflected in the development process. Systems built from requirements documents often diverge from the reality of the work.

Barrier 3: Stuck at PoC, Never Moving to Production

"Let's start with a PoC"—but then there's no decision on how to deploy the PoC results to the production environment, and the project stays in the validation phase indefinitely. I've personally experienced "PoC stagnation" many times in developing AI SaaS products. Bridging PoC to production requires both technical judgment and business decision-making. A person who can do only one or the other cannot overcome this barrier.

Three Roles FDEs Play in DX Initiatives

Role 1: Embedding in the Field to Find the "Real DX Issues"

DX issues are never found in conference rooms. When I joined a manufacturing client, management had set "digitizing production control" as their DX theme, but walking the shop floor revealed that staff were manually transcribing data from three different systems every day. The real DX issue wasn't system replacement—it was data integration. Because FDEs embed themselves in the field, they can discover the "real DX issues on the ground" that never surface in management meetings.

Role 2: Bridging PoC to Production

Even if a PoC proves "AI works," integrating it into production workflows requires both technical judgment and business decision-making. Which data gets updated and how often, how it integrates with existing systems, how field staff workflows change. What my AI SaaS development experience taught me is that between PoC and production lies not a "technical wall" but an "organizational wall." FDEs break through this wall at the field level. For concrete steps, see "How to Approach AI-Driven Business Improvement | Steps from an FDE Practitioner".

Role 3: Staying Until It Takes Root

DX success is determined not on "the day the system is deployed" but on "the day the field can no longer go back to working without it." Consultants leave after delivering recommendations; SIers leave after delivery. FDEs stay in the field. They iterate on improvements while operating alongside field staff, nurturing the system into one that is genuinely used. From my experience building an organization from zero to 60 people at a startup, I learned that "embedding a system" takes three times the effort of "building one."

Key Points When Using FDEs for DX Initiatives

Management Commitment Is a Prerequisite

No matter how capable an FDE is, results won't come if management's stance is "DX is IT's job." FDEs solve business problems with technology. Management must clearly define DX goals and grant FDEs authority as a precondition. Directors of our association who manage multiple businesses share the conviction that "you can't run a business without knowing the field," and a structure that allows direct dialogue between management and FDEs is the key to success.

Start Small and Show Results

Trying to push DX across the entire company all at once leads to failure. First, have an FDE deliver results in one department, one business process. Share those results internally so that "we want to do this in our department too" spreads naturally—this flow is the healthiest. In a financial institution project, we started by building a single data analysis dashboard for traders and expanded from there to other departments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Is it possible to develop FDE talent in-house?

A. Yes, it is. However, developing both technical skills and business acumen takes time. The most efficient approach is to first partner with an external FDE to build a track record of DX results, and have in-house members learn FDE-style working methods through that process.

Q. How long does it take when using FDEs for DX initiatives?

A. From problem discovery to prototype takes about 1-2 weeks, and including production deployment and adoption, the guideline is 3-6 months. However, since small results can often be achieved within the first 1-2 weeks, you can judge the return on investment at an early stage.

Q. We already have a contract with an SIer or consulting firm. Can we also use an FDE alongside them?

A. Yes, you can. In fact, collaboration is highly effective—for example, an FDE translating a consultant's strategy into implementation, or adjusting an SIer's development from a hands-on, field-level perspective. An FDE does not replace your existing partners but serves as a bridge between them.
