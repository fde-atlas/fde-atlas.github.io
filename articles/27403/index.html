Today's Judgment

Enterprise AI adoption is filling in a link that was often previously omitted: standardize and open up scenarios, run them on-site with controlled permissions, and then use the results to decide whether to expand permissions, procure, and replicate.

On the government side, Hunan has pushed "scenario opportunity cards" forward for 10 high-value AI+energy scenarios, with the government acting as a credit intermediary, converting assets like factories, mines, and roads into structured demand with pain points and performance metrics. On the enterprise side, Seeyon's semi-annual report shows that upgrades and additional purchases by established top customers contributed more than 80% of the contract growth in the first half of the year, and the way these customers are served is precisely through FDE embedded on-site presence plus continuous operations.

Taken together, the two signals suggest: the value of an FDE is not just connecting AI agents into business operations, but also bringing government scenario cards, ecosystem partners, and customer upsells back to the same set of acceptance evidence.

Key Signals

Hunan releases 10 "AI+Energy" high-value scenarios: scenario opportunity cards become structured

  • Sources: Hunan Provincial Government; Yiyang Municipal Development and Reform Commission repost
  • On September 21, the Hunan Energy Association released 10 recommended high-value "AI+Energy" scenarios, covering energy planning, power grid inspection, electricity markets, virtual power plants, and other directions, with construction directions and pilot foundations noted. Hunan's development and reform system also introduced the "application scenario supermarket" mechanism: the government acts as a credit intermediary; scenario owners convert assets like factories, mines, and roads into "scenario opportunity cards" containing business pain points and performance metrics, while technology providers turn their capabilities into matchable tags. The steel AI energy-carbon management platform of Liansteel with MCC CISDI Information and Hualing Yunchuang was cited as a matching case.
  • Why it matters: Opening scenarios does not equal project implementation, but "opportunity cards" go one step further than vague solicitation by adding structure: with pain points and performance metrics written upfront, supply and demand have a basis for matching. The FDE opportunity lies in the segment opportunity card → data/permission verification → paid diagnosis/pilot → engineering acceptance → business acceptance → procurement/replication. Note: the 10 scenarios are an association recommendation list, not 10 signed or accepted projects.

Seeyon: FDE ecosystem co-creation, with upgrades and additional purchases by established customers contributing over 80% of contract growth

  • Sources: Sing Tao Global Network; Seeyon 2026 Semi-Annual Report
  • On September 11, Seeyon launched FDE ecosystem co-creation partnerships with multiple partners, proposing to establish an FDE front-end center covering demand co-creation, solution design, scenario validation, and continuous operations, and to feed customer on-site assets back into product R&D. The semi-annual report disclosed: the company has established dedicated service teams for its top 500 established customers, supporting upgrades through FDE embedded on-site delivery and continuous operations; upgrades and additional purchases by established customers together contributed more than 80% of contract growth in the first half of the year.
  • Why it matters: This is a case of a domestic listed software company tying FDE to business results in its financial report. It places on-site teams, partner methodology output, asset write-back, and upsells to established customers into the same commercial chain, and shows that the business value of FDE must be judged by continuous operations, upgrades, and additional purchases—it cannot be seen by looking only at headcount on-site or initial go-lives. Note: the 80% is the combined contribution of upgrades and additional purchases, and should not be understood as FDE alone driving 80% of the growth; the partner collaboration has not disclosed named customers or revenue-sharing results.

Additional Signals: Industrial scenario lists expand, FDE hackathon introduces real problem statements

  • Sources: Suzhou Municipal Bureau of Industry and Information Technology; FDE hackathon event page
  • On September 20, Suzhou released its third batch of 35 typical "AI+Industrial" scenarios, including directions such as multi-system automated testing for high-end chips. On September 21, an FDE hackathon set two real problem statements—"embodied intelligence data factory" and AI-driven park renovation—and arranged for customers to observe on-site.
  • Why it matters: Government lists are expanding the entry point for business opportunities, and talent events are introducing real problem statements—both are moving in the right direction. But both currently remain at the "entry point" level; project-level before/after metrics, sign-offs, and procurement have not yet been made public.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Upgrade requirement sheets into "scenario opportunity cards": pain points, current baseline, target metrics, data/interfaces, business owner, budget, pilot scope, and exit criteria—only enter quoting and development when all eight items are complete; requirements with missing fields go only into diagnosis.
  • Set a four-tier autonomy ladder for agents that execute actions: shadow suggestions → human-approved execution → scoped automation → continuous automation. Each tier upgrade requires separately defining data scope, action permissions, quality thresholds, exception escalation, and rollback, signed off by the business owner. Actions like writing, publishing, shipping, payment, and customer outreach should not be fully automated from day one.
  • Build a "post-launch operations sheet" for each project: continued usage, feature upgrades, repeat purchases, customer referrals, on-site asset write-back, and decreasing delivery cycles. Seeyon's financial report shows that the commercial returns of FDE are mainly reflected in established customers.
  • Treat deviation operations as a product entry point: execution → deviation logging → attribution → correction → re-execution. Go-live is the beginning of operations, and deviation data is the raw material for the next round of product iteration.
