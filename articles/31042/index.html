Companies test new ways of deploying FDE talent

ETHRWorld.com · 2026-09-30 · 6 min read

Summary

As AI makes coding cheaper and faster, companies are redefining what they want from engineers: not just building software, but identifying the right problem, understanding business context, and carrying AI solutions into production. This is elevating the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role. CIEL HR analysis shows FDE hiring grew 130% year over year, with 52 organizations recruiting as of July 2026. Early-stage FDEs earn Rs 35-45 lakh annually, experienced specialists Rs 70-90 lakh, and select AI infrastructure mandates up to Rs 1.4 crore. Demand concentrates on engineers with 3-5 years of experience who can question what should be built, not just how. Employers now prioritize domain depth, enterprise integration skills, and end-to-end ownership over pure coding ability.

Key points

  • FDE hiring grew 130% in the past year; 52 organizations are recruiting.
  • Early FDE roles pay Rs 35-45 lakh; senior specialists reach Rs 70-90 lakh.
  • Demand peaks at engineers with 3-5 years of experience.
  • Employers value domain knowledge and production ownership over coding alone.
  • At Ascendion, over half of production code is AI-written with human review.

Full text

Companies increasingly seek engineers who can understand customer and business contexts, use AI effectively, integrate solutions into existing systems and own outcomes through production. This is also reshaping hiring, with greater demand for mid-level talent and broader, cross-functional capabilities.

But instead of simply driving demand for more developers, companies are changing what they expect from engineers. It is the ability to identify the right problem, understand the business context, and take an AI built solution all the way into production.

That is reshaping the

Forward Deployed Engineer(FDE) role. Across companies, FDEs are increasingly being positioned between business, technology and the customer, with domain knowledge, judgement and ownership becoming as important as coding ability.

Experts point out that 3 to 5 years of experience bracket is the emerging sweet spot, not because of tenure itself, but because these engineers are more likely to have enough experience to question what should be built, not just how to build.

According to CIEL HR's analysis, hiring for FDEs, professionals who combine software engineering, AI expertise, enterprise integration and customer engagement, has grown 130 percent over the past year, reflecting the rapid transition of AI initiatives from experimentation to enterprise-scale implementation.

The study found that 52 organisations across IT services, IT products, and Startups are currently (July 2026) recruiting for Forward Deployed Engineers, reflecting the growing need for professionals who can help businesses implement AI in real-world environments.

Early-stage FDE roles currently command annual compensation of Rs 35 to 45 lakh, while experienced specialists can earn Rs 70 to 90 lakh. Select AI infrastructure mandates are offering compensation packages between Rs 80 lakh and Rs 1.4 crore, reflecting the scarcity of deployment-ready talent.

How FDE hiring is changing in 2026

FDE hiring is shifting from pure technical execution to end to end problem ownership. Experts said that in 2026, employers are placing greater weight on business understanding, customer context, cross-functional collaboration and the ability to integrate and deliver AI solutions in real-world environments.

For Ascendion, an FDE is the one who is embedded in the client environment, customising solutions and agents to align to the client’s context and improving the overall productivity of the team and the systems associated. What has changed in the last year is exactly this: the previous year was about building a convincing proof of concept. Now, the conversation has moved to production.

“Clients want to know who will sit inside their environment, connect AI to systems that have run for twenty years, get their teams to use it, and stay accountable when something breaks,” mentioned Saraswathi Chandrasekharan, Chief People Officer, Ascendion.

Rupeezy also follows a similar approach. In 2025 they hired engineers to build. In 2026 they are being hired to work out what is worth building. The cost of building software has collapsed, and work that took a quarter now takes a fortnight.

Hemant Dubey, VP - Engineering, Rupeezy further added that once the build gets that cheap, the bottleneck moves to the front of the process: understanding the problem properly and deciding what actually deserves to be solved.

The company has been running that model internally first. Engineers sit with their marketing, support and operations teams, learn how the work actually gets done, and build automation for that specific workflow, rather than shipping a generic tool and hoping it gets adopted. It is an FDE in everything but the title, and the people who do it well are not always the strongest coders.

FDEs need to bridge technology and business

Neeraj Rathod, Executive head of People & Culture, Global Apps, BPS & Data Practices, NTT DATA highlighted that the skills that matter now are domain depth that earns client credibility and the ability to define the ontology and data layer.

Even Rupeezy prioritises domain knowledge first, particularly because broking is not a typical software environment. Understanding settlement, margins and what happens to an open position on expiry day is critical.

An engineer without this context can write technically sound code that still produces the wrong outcome, with issues potentially surfacing only much later through a client. As a result, FDEs spend significant time with the business team at Rupeezy, often weeks rather than a single kickoff call.

Infrastructure, however, remains a key consideration. FDEs need to understand how services communicate, how pipelines work, whether systems use queues or direct calls, and what could break when volumes increase tenfold. They are also expected to take their own changes into production.

FDEs take on more decisions

Experts highlighted that AI is reducing the time FDEs spend on coding and routine tasks, shifting their focus towards judgement, problem solving and higher value decision making.

Vinay Dhingra, Senior Director, HR & Administration, Corporate Affairs, IT, Honda Motorcycle & Scooter India mentioned that as HMSI, the focus is on building awareness and practical application capabilities so that AI can be leveraged responsibly to improve productivity, quality and decision-making.

Going forward, engineers will increasingly need to combine strong engineering fundamentals and business understanding with the ability to effectively use AI-enabled tools.

At Ascendion, more than half of the code put into production is written by AI and reviewed by a human in the loop. This is changing the role of engineers, from simply writing code to determining the most productive way to build it.

Ascendion is operating this model at scale, with around 11,000 plus engineers and more than 12,000 plus agents working as one delivery system. While agents handle repetitive work, people remain responsible for making decisions.

Pavan Nanjundaiah, Head of AI Transformation, Tredence also added that today, their FDEs spend more time on solution design, architecture, and ensuring real-world ROI. The company already has about 60 FDEs actively deployed and given how fast companies are moving past AI experimentation, they expect this team to scale to 200 over the next year.

What experience level is seeing the most demand?

Experts highlighted that compared with 2025, organisations are becoming more comfortable giving mid level engineers greater ownership, while senior talent is increasingly reserved for architecture, technical leadership and complex deployments.

For Madhusudhana Rao Podila, Chief Product and Technology Officer, Troogue, the strongest demand appears to be for engineers with three to five years of experience.

In 2025, companies relied more heavily on senior engineers because the role and delivery models were still evolving. With better tools and clearer implementation patterns, organisations are now becoming more comfortable giving capable mid-level and early-career engineers greater responsibility.

Freshers also have an opportunity, but will usually need mentoring before independently owning deployments.

Similarly for Rupeezy, the hiring of FDE happens for mostly 3 to 5 years. As per the company, a good 2-year engineer can write whatever they need but what they can't do yet is sit with the ops team for a week, work out which part of their day is worth automating, and then tell a business head that what they're asking for shouldn't be built.

The company hires freshers elsewhere in Rupeezy engineering. They bring them in to shadow the senior ones on live work.

The evolution of the FDE role points to a broader change in technology hiring. As AI takes over more of the mechanics of building software, the premium is shifting towards people who can define the problem, understand the context, make trade-offs and take responsibility for the outcome.

For enterprises, the question is increasingly not just whether an engineer can build an AI solution, but whether they can make it work inside a real business, with its customers, systems, processes, risks and people.
