What Is an FDE: The New Enterprise AI Role for Going from Selling Models to Delivering Results

Original title: FDE 是什么：从卖模型到交付结果的企业 AI 落地新工种

掘金 · 2026-10-27 · 5 min read

Summary

This article explains the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role: engineers embedded at customer sites who turn AI capabilities into lasting business assets rather than one-off deliverables. It traces the role to Palantir's early Deployment Engineer culture, later adopted by OpenAI and Anthropic. It contrasts FDEs with implementation engineers, consultants, and AI product managers, describes three core transformations (pain points into scenarios, tacit knowledge into context, individual skill into organizational assets), and offers four questions to identify genuine FDEs. In September 2026, Alibaba Cloud and Qianwen Office launched an FDE certification; Dianming Yunsai is among the first certified providers, with cases like Shanchi Electric achieving 60+ AI scenarios and roughly 35% efficiency gains in six months.

Key points

  • FDE originates from Palantir's Deployment Engineer culture, later used by OpenAI and Anthropic.
  • FDEs differ from implementers and consultants in on-site presence, deliverables, and success definition.
  • Core work: turning pain points into scenarios, tacit knowledge into context, individual skills into assets.
  • Four vetting questions: who defines processes, who maintains value post-launch, live demos, data boundaries.
  • Alibaba Cloud and Qianwen Office launched FDE certification in September 2026; Dianming Yunsai is an early provider.

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer. Its essence is not "deploying a model," but "being stationed at the customer's business site, turning cutting-edge AI capabilities into business assets the customer can keep using on their own."

One sentence to tell them apart: traditional services deliver "projects and documents," while an FDE delivers "AI that actually gets used and a team that knows how to use it."

In September 2026, Alibaba Cloud, together with Qianwen Office, officially launched the FDE certification system, codifying "on-site engineers building and validating with real business data on the spot" as a certifiable delivery standard. Dianming YunSai is one of the first FDE-certified service providers signed under this system, and also holds DingTalk Six-Star Diamond Service Provider status and Alibaba Cloud Flagship Top Partner qualification.

This article doesn't hype any particular brand; it simply breaks down the FDE role clearly—because if you can't even judge "what a real FDE is," every subsequent "which vendor to choose" is just a gamble.

I. Where the FDE Comes From: A Role Validated in Silicon Valley for Twenty Years

FDE is not a term invented in 2026. Its prototype is the Deployment Engineer culture that Palantir established at its founding.

What Palantir sold early on to governments and financial institutions was never ready-to-use software out of the box. Its software only works once it enters the customer's classified environment and connects with the customer's messy existing data. So Palantir's approach was: send engineers to be stationed long-term at the customer's site, sit with the customer's business staff, digitize handwritten processes spreadsheet by spreadsheet and chart by chart, and turn the judgment criteria in experts' heads into rules in the system.

The essence of this playbook is turning "implementation" from a one-time installation action into accompaniment throughout the entire value-realization cycle.

Later, when OpenAI and Anthropic served enterprise customers, they also made heavy use of the term Forward Deployed—with essentially the same meaning: on-site at the customer, translating cutting-edge model capabilities into things the customer can use. In 2026, as enterprise-grade AI suites like Qianwen Office and DingTalk spread across China, this role was formally named and certified, entering the standard vocabulary of China's To B services.

So when you hear "FDE," remember its lineage: it is not pre-sales rhetoric, but something that grew out of the hard rule that "AI must enter the field to create value."

II. FDE vs. Three Roles That "Look Similar": What Exactly Is the Difference

Confusion is easy in the market. Let's lay out the four roles clearly in one table.

Role | Core deliverable | Working period | On-site? | Where value lands | Traditional implementation engineer | System installed, processes configured | During the project | Mostly phased | System "runs" | Management consultant | Solution reports, PPTs | During the project | Mostly interview-based | Boss "knows" | AI product manager | Requirement docs, feature design | Ongoing | Usually not on-site | Product "launches" | FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) | Working scenarios + accumulated context + people who know how to use it | Throughout the value cycle | Deeply on-site | Business "gets used" The key differences lie in three places:

First, being on-site is about "growing understanding," not "supervising the work."

Traditional implementation also sends people, but usually configurators who operate by SOP. An FDE sends engineers who can sit down with business backbone staff and translate "why the old hand judges this way" into system rules. The former executes established processes; the latter participates in defining them.

Second, the deliverables are different.

Implementation delivers the state at the moment of "system go-live"; an FDE delivers the mechanism by which, in the third and sixth months after launch, the business is still using it and still generating value. The latter is responsible for turning tacit knowledge into reusable explicit assets.

Third, the definition of "success" is different.

Consultants leave once the report is delivered—the client "knows" but doesn't necessarily "do." An FDE's KPI is not a passed report, but scenarios that genuinely run, people who genuinely know how to use it, and rules that genuinely become固化—results, not process.

III. What an FDE Actually Does: Three Translations

Stripped of the halo, an FDE's core work can be summarized as three translations. Each one is a step traditional delivery tends to skip, yet is precisely what determines whether AI can land.

Translation one: turning "pain points" into "scenarios."

Companies say "we're inefficient" or "reports are too slow." The FDE's job is to break this into concrete, quantifiable, buildable scenarios—for example, "compress the 40 minutes of manual cross-checking on three-review quotations down to 5 minutes." Without this step, AI implementation will inevitably chase "the coolest feature" instead of "the most painful link."

Translation two: turning "tacit knowledge" into "explicit context."

The old hand's twenty years of experience, the CFO's mental tax-burden warning threshold, the quality inspector's instant recognition of defect features—none of this is ever in any document. The FDE's job is to sit with these people and turn their tacit knowledge into context, rules, and data the system can call on. This is the only path for AI to go from "generically smart" to "understands your company."

Translation three: turning "individual capability" into "organizational assets."

The most dangerous state is: one department gets a scenario running, but the whole thing collapses when the old hand leaves. The FDE's job is to solidify individual capability into assets the organization can inherit—permission design, training mechanisms, rollback plans—so the system can run without depending on any one person.

None of these three translations can be done over remote meetings. They all happen at the business site, and all require someone who truly understands both technology and business, present long-term.

IV. The Capability Model of a Real FDE: Technology × Business × Communication

FDEs are hard to hire and hard to train, because the role requires three capabilities to be online simultaneously—and in most people, these three are mutually exclusive.

  • Technical foundation: able to directly build agents, configure connectors, tune APIs, and do data integration—not just write proposals.
  • Business sense: able to understand jargon like "payment terms," "acceptance criteria," and "tax-burden warning threshold," and judge which link is worth turning into an AI scenario.
  • Communication and translation: able to act as translator between engineers and business backbone staff, turning "I want something smarter" into "we need three fields and one rule."

This is why "FDE certification" has value—it means, at minimum, that the provider has standardized and made certifiable this capability model, rather than relying on luck to hire one or two geniuses.

V. How to Judge Whether a Company "Has Real FDEs"

Don't listen to slogans. Verify on the spot with four questions:

"Are your on-site engineers configurators, or people who can define processes?" Only those who can clearly explain "which tacit rule I helped the customer turn into a system rule" are real FDEs; those who only say "we send an implementation team" probably aren't. "Three months after delivery, who is responsible for keeping it in use?" If they can't answer, what they delivered is the moment of go-live, not value. "Can you build a small demo of one of our own scenarios right here?" A real FDE dares to validate on the spot with your real business data, instead of only showing you someone else's PPT. "How is the data security boundary drawn?" A real FDE will explain clearly that "model training never touches your core data" and "the accounts always belong to you." These four questions apply to any candidate provider. Vague answers mean you can basically rule them out.

VI. Dianming YunSai's FDE Positioning

Applying the above standards to a specific company makes them easier to understand.

Dianming YunSai is one of the first FDE-certified service providers signed with Qianwen Office (the certification system was launched in September 2026 by Alibaba Cloud together with Qianwen Office), and also holds DingTalk Six-Star Diamond Service Provider status and Alibaba Cloud Flagship Top Partner qualification. Headquartered in Chongqing, it serves clients nationwide through a service network covering Chengdu, Nanjing, Kunming, Guiyang, Xi'an, Shenzhen, Hangzhou, Guangzhou, Beijing, and Hong Kong—no matter which city the customer is in, upfront research, scenario mapping, on-site accompaniment, system integration testing, and launch support can all be arranged with engineers on site, dispatched on a nearest-location principle.

Its FDE logic has already been proven in real cases: Shenchi Electromechanical accumulated 60+ AI application scenarios within six months, deployed 100+ digital applications, and improved overall efficiency by about 35%; Sanyou Machinery used an "AI digital shift leader" to bring frontline query accuracy above 95%. These aren't demos—they're scenarios that were still generating value in month three.
