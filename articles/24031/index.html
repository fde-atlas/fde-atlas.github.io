What do high-performing FDEs have in common? Principles that truly work in the field

Original title: FDEで成果を出す人の共通点は？現場で本当に効く原則

FDE総研 · 2026-07-23 · 6 min read

Summary

This FDE Soken article draws on VACAN Technologies' practice to identify three principles shared by high-performing Forward Deployed Engineers: capturing the customer's real problem rather than taking requests at face value; reaching agreement with working outputs instead of documents; and staying engaged until the solution is actually adopted in daily operations. The author argues that technical skill is only a precondition — the working method (problem definition, output, adoption) determines success, and the three steps form a sequence where each depends on the previous one. Supporting evidence includes parent company VACAN's deployments at over 20,000 locations and 50+ patents, plus published results like roughly 70% fewer front-desk inquiries and 95%+ willingness to reuse, showing outcomes should be measured by business metrics rather than implementation volume.

Key points

  • Three principles: find the true problem, advance with working outputs, support until adoption.
  • Wrong problem definition makes fast implementation useless or even harmful.
  • Working prototypes expose misunderstandings faster than documents and speed decisions.
  • Results are measured in business metrics, e.g. ~70% fewer front-desk inquiries.
  • VACAN's 20,000+ site deployments and 50+ patents underpin the methodology's repeatability.

What principles do successful FDEs share?

Successful FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) never miss these three points: "capturing the true problem," "moving forward with working output," and "accompanying clients until adoption takes hold." Rather than flashy technology, it is this grounded approach that determines results.

FDEs are responsible for customer outcomes, and their success is reflected less in specific skills than in how they approach their work. Here, drawing on VACAN Technologies' practices and public information, we organize the common traits that lead to results.

The three principles at a glance

The pattern for results works as a continuous flow: problem definition, output, and adoption. If any one is missing, results stall partway through.

Common traitContentWhat happens if it's missed
Capturing the true problemDefining the essential problem in the fieldEven fast builds don't produce results
Working outputGetting agreement with working things, not documentsDiscussions don't move forward
Accompanying until adoptionImproving until the solution keeps being usedEnds as a one-off implementation

These three points are not independent items; they have an order in which the earlier stage determines the effectiveness of the later one. If the problem is misidentified, implementation is wasted, and without agreement on something working, adoption is never reached.

Why "approach" matters more than technical skill

Advanced technology is a prerequisite, but on its own it does not guarantee results. It only becomes valuable when used in the customer's field.

An FDE's job is not to showcase implementation skills but to take responsibility for change on the customer's side. That is why the approach—what to build (the problem), how to get agreement (output), and how to make it stick (adoption)—matters more than technology selection. VACAN Technologies embraces this stance as an organization.

Common trait 1: What does "capturing the true problem" mean?

The starting point of results is correct problem definition—not building requests as stated, but entering the field and grasping the essence. Miss this, and everything downstream is wasted.

Defining the essential problem, not the request

What customers say they want and what the field is actually struggling with often don't match. Bridging that gap is where the FDE's work begins.

Tamaki Ryu says: "Go deep into the customer's field, capture the true problem, organize and structure it... and move things forward" (Wantedly). What matters here is the combination of "going deep" and "structuring." Observing the field alone or merely organizing requests is not enough; only by structuring the facts gathered from observation into a problem does what should be built become clear.

Why downstream work fails if problem definition is missed

If problem definition is off, no matter how fast the downstream implementation is, it won't produce results. Building fast can even become the danger of moving quickly in the wrong direction.

For example, if you assume slow operations are "because the tools are old" and replace them, no improvement occurs if the real cause lies in operational rules or fragmented information (a generalized example of a lesson learned). Problem definition is the most upstream process, and a misalignment there wastes all downstream effort. That is why successful FDEs pay the most attention to problem definition before implementation.

Common trait 2: Why move forward with working output?

Successful FDEs get agreement with working things, not documents. Tangible deliverables you can actually touch surface misunderstandings faster and more accurately.

Getting agreement with working things, not documents

There is a big gap between the "it should work" explained in words and diagrams and the "yes, this is right" felt by touching something that actually works. VACAN closes that gap with working output.

VACAN Technologies positions itself as "a technology companion firm that solves field problems with working output" (official website). Document-based agreement leaves room for interpretation, but working things make "right or wrong" clear and move discussions toward concrete improvements. This reduces rework and forms the foundation for faster decision-making.

How to read results examples in public materials

Results ultimately show up in the customer's operational metrics. As reference, the parent company Vacan's products have results examples shown in public materials.

Specifically, results such as "front desk inquiries reduced by about 70%" and "over 95% of users answered they 'want to use it again'" have been published (PR TIMES. Note: these are Vacan's product track record, and criteria vary by time period and subject). What to note here is that results are expressed not in the volume of implementation but in operational metrics such as inquiry reduction rates and intent to reuse. Working output is not the goal; it only counts as a result when it moves the field's metrics.

Common trait 3: What does "accompanying until adoption" mean?

It means continuing to improve until the solution keeps being used, rather than stopping once it's built. The goal is not the moment of implementation but the state where it has taken root in daily operations.

Improving until it keeps being used

Implementation is the beginning of results, not their completion. Whether it continues to be used in the field is what separates a one-off implementation from real results.

Many projects end at "delivery," but successful FDEs look beyond delivery to how the solution is actually used. Unused features are equivalent to zero results, and only by iterating on improvements while observing actual usage does the invested effort pay off as operational improvement.

The meaning of "dig a small well and connect it to a channel"

VACAN's philosophy of "dig a small well, and eventually connect it to a channel" refers to continuous adoption itself. It is the stance of growing small, certain results into a widely used system.

What this phrase (official website) conveys is an order of operations: rather than aiming for a large system from the start, first create results that are reliably used in one field (a small well), then roll them out and connect them to adoption (a channel). Adoption is not a state completed by a single implementation; it is understood as an ongoing process of digging, connecting, and expanding.

What forms the foundation supporting results?

These approaches only work when backed by track record and technology. In VACAN's case, that foundation lies in the parent company Vacan's platform.

vCore™ and the backing of track record and patents

Vacan, VACAN's parent company and foundation, has its own platform vCore™, supported by implementations at more than 20,000 locations and over 50 patents. This accumulated foundation supports the reproducibility of the approach in the field.

The track record of implementations and patents serve as evidence that the pattern of identifying issues, producing outputs, and embedding practices is run not as a spur-of-the-moment idea but as a reproducible methodology (official website. *Figures as of 2026, publicly disclosed group-wide totals). The technical foundation and the on-the-ground pattern are not in conflict; it is precisely because the foundation exists that the pattern functions stably.
