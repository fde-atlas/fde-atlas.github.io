RDE (Reinvention Deployed Engineer) is an engineer role introduced by Accenture under its "Reinvention" strategy, focused on redesigning client companies' business processes and organizations with AI as the premise.

In December 2025, Accenture announced it would expand its partnership with Anthropic and deploy approximately 30,000 people trained on Claude as RDEs to client companies, which has started drawing attention in Japan as well.

In this article, we explain the definition of RDE, the background behind its emergence, how it differs from the similar concept of FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), and the actual state of the freelance project market in Japan, incorporating our company's first-hand information.

An RDE is defined as "an engineer who redesigns a client company's business processes, organization, and performance metrics as a whole with AI as the premise, and sees the work through from implementation to adoption."

Its distinguishing feature is that it goes beyond mere AI implementation support, delving as far as restructuring business rules and evaluation metrics themselves. It is a role defined by Accenture for its own consulting business.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) Currently available FDE project information

This is an in-house opening at DeFactory, where a pair of at least two people—a consultant (PdM) and an engineer—join a client company and handle everything from identifying issues to implementation and internal adoption/in-house development. Hourly rates are 5,500 yen and up for engineers and 6,000 yen and up for PdMs, with fixed billing (hourly rate × estimated utilization).

The name RDE comes from Accenture's corporate strategy, "Reinvention."

It is a coined term that replaces the "Forward" in "Forward Deployed Engineer" with "Reinvention," Accenture's signature concept, positioning these professionals as people who not only handle one-off AI implementations but rebuild companies themselves with AI as the premise.

By the end of March 2026, Accenture had reorganized itself with the aim of becoming a "Reinvention partner" for client companies, and RDEs are being developed as its core talent.

Behind the emergence of RDE is the need to counter the argument that generative AI makes consultants unnecessary.

As generative AI became capable of replacing traditional consulting tasks such as market research and document creation, project structures, timelines, and compensation models changed, and a sense of crisis grew within Accenture that undifferentiated consulting firms would be weeded out.

To respond to this situation, Accenture shifted from traditional consulting that "ends with recommendations" to an implementation-focused talent model that "implements AI in production environments and sees measurable results through to completion," defining the RDE as the role to carry this out.

For a detailed explanation of the definition and role of the position that formed the basis of RDE's implementation philosophy, see What is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)?.

The biggest difference between RDE and FDE is that while FDEs focus on "technical implementation of specific products," RDEs go as far as "redesigning business processes and organizations."

For more on the FDE concept itself, which originated at Palantir, see What are the required roles of Palantir-style FDEs and FDRs?.

The essence common to both is the attitude of "not stopping at recommendations, but taking responsibility until results are achieved in the field." RDE is easier to understand if you see it as an evolved form that combines the implementation philosophy of FDE with Accenture's characteristic perspective on business transformation.

The main work of an RDE is to break down a client company's business challenges into forms that AI can solve, and consistently handle everything from prototype creation to production operation and changes to business processes.

Specifically, this includes the following tasks.

  • Breaking down challenges: Deeply understand the client's business processes and distinguish between areas that AI can and cannot solve
  • Prototype development: Quickly build working prototypes for the identified challenges
  • Implementation into production: Integrate prototypes into actual business systems and operate them safely
  • Redesigning business processes: Change role assignments and business flows themselves in line with AI adoption
  • Measuring and embedding results: Measure effectiveness based on predefined success metrics and embed the changes into daily operations

What sets this apart from traditional consulting that ends with delivering a proposal is that it goes as far as changing business processes, organizations, and role assignments, not just implementing AI tools.

The requirements for an RDE fall into four main areas: "AI and engineering skills," "industry and business understanding," "strategic consulting capabilities," and "the ability to see production operations through to completion."

  • Advanced AI and engineering skills: Hands-on technical ability to build working systems, including generative AI utilization, system implementation, and cloud technologies
  • Deep understanding of industries and business processes: The ability to quickly grasp a client company's business structure and workflows and identify the essence of its challenges
  • Strategic consulting and soft skills: The ability to build consensus among stakeholders in different positions, from executives to frontline staff
  • Ability to see production operations through to completion: The execution capability to take responsibility beyond the prototype stage, all the way to a state where the solution is continuously used in actual operations

What distinguishes an RDE from a typical AI engineer is the need to combine not only technical skills but also industry understanding and consulting-style consensus-building abilities.

Typical entry points into an RDE career include those from SIers or IT consulting, data/AI engineers, DX staff at operating companies, and those with PM, PMO, or PdM experience.

  • SIers and IT consultants: Experience in building consensus with clients and driving projects forward can be applied directly
  • Data engineers and AI engineers: AI and data infrastructure implementation skills are connected to the context of business process transformation
  • DX staff at operating companies: Experience deeply understanding their own company's business processes can be applied to roll out solutions to client companies
  • PM, PMO, and PdM: Experience completing projects while engaging multiple stakeholders becomes a key strength

None of these experiences alone is sufficient; you need to consciously combine the three elements of "technology," "business understanding," and "consensus-building." Since this overlaps significantly with the skill set required of FDEs, please also refer to What Are the Required Skills and Career Strategies for FDE Engineers?.

Since RDE is currently a role that Accenture offers only to its own full-time employees and consultants, it has not yet appeared in the freelance project market. For details on the overall freelance rate landscape for FDEs, see What Is the Freelance Rate Market for FDEs? (Real Data).

In conclusion, since it is expected to take some time before RDE projects appear in Japan's freelance market, accumulating experience as an FDE now is the most realistic choice.

RDE is a role that Accenture develops among its own full-time employees and consultants, so its spread to the freelance market is likely to take even longer than FDE's.

Even for FDE itself, it took several years for the concept, which originated at Palantir, to spread to Japan's freelance market. RDE is even newer and is being driven by a major consulting firm, so the timing of projects becoming available is uncertain.

The "technical skills," "industry understanding," "consensus-building ability," and "ability to see production operations through to completion" required of RDEs largely overlap with the skill set required of FDEs.

In other words, gaining hands-on experience as an FDE now is not a detour; it is preparation that will directly serve as a strength when RDE-style work becomes available as projects in the future.

With no RDE projects in existence yet, FDE projects are the practical option for taking action now.
