AI Agent Development | Custom-Built Business AI Connected to Your Internal Data, with Contract Development and Resident Engineers

Original title: AIエージェント開発｜社内データとつなぐ業務AIを受託開発・エンジニア常駐で

株式会社Aiworker · 2025-11-30 · 13 min read

Summary

This is a service page from Japanese company AIworker describing its AI agent development offerings, built around connecting AI to internal systems and data. Clients choose between fixed-scope contracted development (verification from 3 million yen, full development from 10 million yen) and an embedded engineer model called AIworker FDE (from 350,000 yen per week for one day, 1.5 million yen for full-time). The page argues that AI adoption fails when tools are not wired into internal data, workflows, and maintainable handover. Case studies include reducing manual personnel-data checks from 58 to 36 items and cutting quote preparation from 150 to 30 minutes, with over 100 corporate clients served since July 2024.

Key points

  • Two engagement models: contracted development or embedded engineers (AIworker FDE).
  • Contracted work starts at 3M yen for verification, 10M yen for full development.
  • Embedded model costs from 350,000 yen/week (1 day) to 1.5M yen/month full-time.
  • Measured results include quote time cut from 150 to 30 minutes.
  • Focus on handover: documentation, trained staff, and internal FDE development.

Challenges that remain after introducing AI

What is happening on the ground

From those considering AI agent development, we often receive consultations like the following. In every case, the stumbling block is not the AI tool itself, but the stage of embedding AI into internal data and business procedures.

AI tools were distributed, but the way work is done has not changed

Even when rolled out company-wide, the procedures of specific tasks rarely change.

Stuck after a small pilot

Even though a prototype was built, the project has not moved to production due to integration with internal systems or permission design.

Internal data and AI are not connected

Information from core systems and data platforms is not in a form the AI can reference.

The systems vendor has no one knowledgeable about generative AI

Even after consulting the existing development company, no one could be found to handle the AI portion.

Worried that outsourcing leaves nothing behind internally

Clients are concerned that when the person in charge changes, no one will be able to fix the system.

To make AI usable, you need to connect it to internal data, place it within business procedures, and leave it in a form that can be fixed internally.

We will help you sort out which business process to start with, in 30 minutes.

Development Case Studies

Examples built by connecting to internal data and systems. Company names are withheld; industry and size are shown. Only figures verified with actual data are listed; unmeasured items are noted as such.

Connecting a survey analysis AI to the internal data platform

ChallengeThe client wanted to analyze surveys from residents, owners, and corporations, but the internal data platform (Snowflake) had rules restricting connection sources, so the AI could not reference it directly.
Support providedWhile complying with internal rules, we fixed the AI's connection source and connected it to the data platform, developing an AI that analyzes surveys.
ResultsRunning in production, with the reference source selectable from Web, internal data, or both.

Cross-checking attendance, payroll, and secondment data

ChallengeAttendance, payroll, and secondment data were scattered across companies and systems, and the connections between them were verified manually by staff.
Support providedWith weekly residency, we built, together with staff, the allocation of attendance data to three companies, pre-checks of payroll data, and detection of unregistered secondees.
ResultsThe number of secondment verifications requiring visual review dropped from 58 to 36 (actual data), and the allocation of attendance data for approximately 630 employees was automated. Thresholds can be changed by the HR group themselves.

Creating work orders from core system project data

ChallengeStaff were manually transcribing 7,000–8,000 work orders per year, with approximately 100 formats for approximately 100 client companies.
Support detailsTogether with the team responsible for the core system, we decided on an approach that connects an independent mechanism via data integration rather than building it into the core system. It creates drafts in the customer's format from case information and past work reports.
StatusUnder development toward the first release in December 2026; results have not yet been measured.

In addition, we reduced the time to write scout messages to about one-third for a recruiting firm, and shortened company profile creation from several days to a few hours for an M&A advisory firm. Examples of estimates and documents are introduced on the Estimate AI and AI for Document Work pages.

Models, accuracy, and security

Here is our approach to the three points most often asked about in information system department reviews.

Models used

We first check the environments your company has already contracted, such as Microsoft 365 and Copilot Studio, ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, and then choose. Which model was chosen and why is documented in the design document.

How accuracy is verified

We validate using materials of the same type as production and compare against pass criteria set before starting. Processes with a single determinable answer, such as total calculations, are computed with rule-based programs.

Permissions and security

We design the scope of viewable data, user permissions, and operation logs in line with your company's information security policies. We also prepare the documents required for the review.

Our strengths

We handle everything from organizing business issues, development, and on-site support to employee training under one roof. As a result, we can carry the process through continuously, from building the system to ensuring it is actually used and can be maintained in-house.

Design capability that starts by observing the work

We design from on-site procedures and decision criteria without waiting for requirements to be finalized

Our approach starts by observing the work of the people involved and documenting what experienced staff look at when making decisions. We select one high-impact task, define the pass criteria first, and then build.

Development capability that connects to internal systems

We don't stop at prototypes; we connect to core systems and data platforms and run in production

We build everything from APIs and data integration to login and permission management and operation logs. One example is a case where AI was able to reference internal data while still complying with existing information security policies.

Handover in a form that can be maintained in-house

Decision criteria and settings remain in-house even when the person in charge changes

We build systems so that in-house staff can change decision criteria and referenced data, and deliver them together with manuals. If needed, we also provide training for in-house staff and in-house FDE development.

Process

In either approach, we define the pass criteria before building. This makes it easy to verify prototype results numerically, so deciding whether to move to production becomes easier.

Contracted development

1Observe the work and define the scope We interview staff about procedures and decision criteria, and estimate the effect from the number of people involved, time saved, and labor costs.
2Test small with real materials We build a prototype using your materials and compare it against the pass criteria agreed in writing before starting.
3Build the production system We build the integration with internal systems, login and permissions, and operation logs.
4Operation, maintenance, and handover We monitor usage and accuracy, make improvements, and hand over in a form that can be maintained in-house.

On-site engineers (AIworker FDE)

1Define scope and permissions Before starting, we define the scope handled in-house, the scope handled by us, and system permissions.
2Observe the work and get the first one running In the first two weeks, we examine the work and get the first system running.
3Build in order of impact We prioritize the list of desired items and move to the next issue without changing the contract.
4Make it maintainable by your staff We hand over as we build, moving from one department to the next once it runs in-house.

What remains with your company after implementation

Delivering only an AI agent leaves no one able to maintain it when the person in charge changes. That is why we also leave behind the materials, data, and people needed to keep the system running.

A system used in actual operations

  • Data organized into a form AI can reference

Decision criteria and accuracy records

  • Documentation of what experienced staff looked at when making decisions
  • Pass criteria and validation results; records that can be explained in internal approvals and audits

In-house staff who can maintain it, and manuals

  • Staff who can change decision criteria and referenced data
  • Manuals covering how to change settings and add data
