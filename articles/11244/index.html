India’s AI Talent Problem Is No Longer About Engineers. It’s About Who Can Deploy AI

Analytics India Magazine · 2026-09-17 · 4 min read

Summary

A report by AIM Research and FDE Academy offers one of the first data-driven looks at India's Forward Deployed Engineer talent market. Its core finding: India has ample engineers but too few who can combine technical depth, business understanding and customer-facing skills to move AI from demos to production. Demand for field-deployed AI builders (95%) far exceeds available talent (45%). These roles command the highest pay, and demand is expected to outpace supply through 2027–28 as enterprises scale AI beyond pilots.

Key points

  • Field-deployed AI builders show a 50-point gap: 95% employer demand vs 45% talent supply.
  • 98% of FDE job postings require customer and consulting skills, above core engineering's 94%.
  • Median pay for field-deployed AI builders ranges from ₹32 lakh to ₹155 lakh by experience.
  • AI deployment experience carries a 30–50% salary premium; GenAI specialisation 25–40%.
  • 70% of FDE professionals have 8 or fewer years of experience; Bengaluru holds 34% of talent.

Full text

India has no shortage of engineers. It may, however, be running short of engineers who can take AI from a demo to a production system.

A new report from AIM Research and FDE Academy is attempting to put numbers behind that emerging talent market. The Forward Deployed Engineer Talent Landscape in India is among the first deep dives into India’s nascent FDE ecosystem, tracking its talent supply, employer demand, skills, compensation and future trajectory at a time when the role itself is still taking shape.

It also explores the talent employers are looking for, where the gaps are emerging and why FDEs could become one of the most important AI talent categories over the next few years.

Until now, there has been little data on who these engineers are, what companies are looking for, how much they are being paid and where the biggest gaps lie. The report attempts to fill that gap through professional and employer surveys, along with secondary research covering job postings, compensation benchmarks, company disclosures and other industry data.

The report makes one thing clear: India does not necessarily have a shortage of technology talent. It has a shortage of people who can combine technical depth with business understanding and customer-facing skills.

For the past two years, much of the AI conversation in India has revolved around building models, hiring AI researchers and training engineers on GenAI. But enterprises are now running into a more mundane problem: someone has to make all of this work.

An AI model may perform beautifully in a controlled demonstration. Inside a bank, hospital or large enterprise, however, it has to work with existing data, APIs, legacy systems, security controls, business processes and people. Someone needs to understand the customer's problem, decide where AI can actually help, build the solution, integrate it into the enterprise and stay around when things inevitably break.

That is the job increasingly associated with the Forward Deployed Engineer.

The report does not treat FDE as a conventional job title. Instead, it defines it as a capability that combines engineering and software development, enterprise architecture, product thinking, consulting and stakeholder management, domain expertise and end-to-end deployment ownership.

And the gap is already visible.

According to the report, employers show 95% demand for field-deployed AI builders, while available talent stands at just 45%. That creates a 50-percentage-point gap, the largest among the FDE role families studied.

This is also changing what companies mean when they say they want an “AI engineer”.

The report finds that customer and consulting capabilities are required in 98% of FDE job postings, compared with 94% for core engineering and 79% for applied AI. Cloud and AI operations feature in 84% of postings, while architecture and enterprise integration appears in 63%.

This is where the FDE role starts to look different from the conventional software engineer.

The engineer who can build an application is useful. The engineer who can sit with a customer, understand a business process, figure out why the model is failing, integrate it with an ERP or CRM system and take responsibility for the outcome is considerably harder to find.

The implication is simple: enterprises are increasingly looking for engineers who can operate beyond the codebase.

The market has moved beyond hiring for basic AI literacy and is now looking for "AI accountability," says Ankush Sabharwal, Founder & CEO of CoRover and Creator of BharatGPT. He notes that employers want engineers who can work with real production data, diagnose model failures in a client environment, and take end-to-end ownership of the fix.

Sabharwal says this requires a combination of systems debugging, domain understanding, and change management, making it distinct from traditional software engineering or AI research.

The market is already putting a price on that combination of skills.

Field-deployed AI builders command the highest median compensation across every experience level examined in the report. Their median salary rises from ₹32 lakh for professionals with 0-3 years of experience to ₹155 lakh for those with above 15 years of experience.

Production experience is also becoming a major differentiator. AI deployment experience carries a 30% to 50% salary premium, while GenAI and LLM specialisation carries a 25% to 40% premium.

For India’s engineers, this creates an interesting career opportunity because the FDE market is still young. Seventy percent of FDE professionals have 8 years of experience or less, with the 4-8-year band accounting for 42% of the talent pool.

The talent, however, remains concentrated in familiar technology hubs. Bengaluru accounts for 34% of India’s FDE talent, followed by Delhi NCR at 14%, Hyderabad at 13% and Pune at 12%. Bengaluru alone has more FDE talent than Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Pune combined.

What happens next could be more important than the role itself.

The report expects FDE demand to outpace supply through 2027–28 as enterprises scale AI from pilots into production. At the same time, companies are increasingly building FDE capabilities internally, while regulation is creating additional demand for governance, assurance and risk expertise.

This is creating a gap between what professionals want to do and what employers will increasingly need them to do. Professionals continue to favour field-deployed AI and MLOps roles, while employers are placing greater emphasis on transformation, governance and risk. In governance and risk, employer priority stands at 28%, compared with 18% professional interest.

For companies, the report suggests that hiring by job title may no longer be enough. They will need to assess whether candidates have actually taken systems into production, integrated enterprise platforms, handled incidents and delivered measurable business outcomes.

For engineers, the message is equally direct.

The premium in AI is moving beyond knowing how to build a model or connect an API. It is moving towards knowing what to build, where to deploy it, how to make it work inside an enterprise, and what to do when reality does not behave like a demo.

India has plenty of engineers who can build. And the next AI talent race will be for those who can own the deployment.
