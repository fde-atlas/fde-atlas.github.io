Who Owns the Customer After the FDE Leaves

Falkster.AI · 2026-10-06 · 1 min read

Summary

This article examines what happens to a customer account after the forward deployed engineer departs around day 60, when a customer success manager takes over with far less context. The author argues only what is written down survives the handoff and proposes a one-page document covering the promise, signals, a watch list, keys, and a recall rule. A named person on the customer side must own changes to the customer layer. The piece cites Gartner's September 29, 2026 forecast that 70% of enterprises will abandon vendor-built agentic AI by 2028 because they cannot evolve it themselves. It is part 6 of The FDE Transition series.

Key points

  • FDE attention holds deployments together until roughly day 60, then disappears.
  • CSMs cover too many accounts to understand the configuration they inherit.
  • A one-page handoff document should capture promise, signals, watch list, keys, and a recall rule.
  • A named customer-side owner must control the customer layer, or the builder stays load-bearing.
  • Gartner forecasts 70% of enterprises will abandon vendor FDE-built agentic AI by 2028.

Full text

The forward deployed engineer's attention is what holds a new deployment together. At day 60 that attention goes away, and the account moves to a customer success manager who covers many more customers and can't read the configuration. What survives that move is whatever got written down.

So write one page. The promise, the signal, the watch list, the keys, and a recall rule. The CSM owns the account from there. A named person at the customer owns the ability to change the customer layer, and if nobody does, the deployment is still being held up by the engineer who built it. Gartner's September 29, 2026 forecast, that 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor forward deployed engineering by 2028 because they are "unable to evolve it on their own," is that blank line added up across a market.

Part 6 of The FDE Transition, Month Two: The FDE Leaves, the Workflow Has to Stay, makes the case in full. The handbook chapter on building the FDE team covers the pairing behind it: adoption is a deliverable, owned by name.
