When to hire your first forward deployed engineer

Kernavique · 2026-11-21 · 9 min read

Summary

Kernavique compiles opinions from investors and founders on when to hire a first forward deployed engineer, stressing that none of it is measured data. CRV suggests three to 10 enterprise customers each needing significant customization; Looker's former CEO Frank Bien cites roughly $25,000 annual contract value per customer. Before hiring, the founder should do the work. The article catalogs common mistakes, including the services trap, field learning never reaching the product, and pay tied to engagement revenue, and advises settling role type, level, pay basis, and location before opening the position.

Key points

  • CRV's signal: three to 10 enterprise customers needing heavy customization.
  • Frank Bien's threshold: about $25,000 annual contract value per customer.
  • The founder should do the job first; no data backs any threshold.
  • Key mistakes: services trap, field learning not reaching product, revenue-linked pay.
  • Decide role version, level, pay basis, and location before hiring.

When do founders and investors say to create the function?

Everything below is opinion from investors, founders and former practitioners. None of it is data. We read each page on November 21, 2026, unless a line gives November 20.

Customer count. CRV, a venture firm, places the hire “when you have three to 10 enterprise customers, each requiring significant customization” (CRV, May 18, 2026). It adds two signals: core engineers spending a meaningful share of their time on customer-specific work, and deals or customers lost to implementation complexity.

Stage. Northzone, a venture firm, advises using the role early, while a company is still finding its product or market. Once the product works out of the box, it advises splitting the role into solutions engineers and architects (Northzone, June 4, 2026).

Product. Jina Yoon of PostHog suggests one when “The product requires a lot of hands-on implementation AND you can eat the cost.” (PostHog blog, February 11, 2026.) James Honsa, co-founder of Genera, says it fits a product deployed across a diverse range of use cases, and not one heading for product-led, freemium growth (First Round Review, February 24, 2026, updated October 2, 2026).

Too early. CRV says it is too early when you do not yet know what the hire should be building.

Does contract value matter?

The sources say it does. Only one gives a threshold from someone who ran the company.

Frank Bien, former CEO of Looker, said: “We needed to be in the neighborhood of $25,000 a year per customer.” (First Round Review, February 24, 2026, updated October 2, 2026.)

  • CRV says target annual contract value should be high enough, with meaningful expansion potential. It gives no figure (May 18, 2026).
  • Marc Andrusko, partner at a16z, argues the model scales better with a concentrated base of customers on high annual contract values. He describes founders aiming to land a deal of $1M+ with a Fortune 500 company or a government agency (a16z, January 16, 2026). That is an aim, not a threshold.
  • Jake Stauch, CEO of Serval, puts these engineers on Serval’s largest customers, meaning companies with more than 1,000 employees (First Round Review, same dates).
  • Joe Schmidt of a16z advises: “Pick the right customers and start small in scope.” (a16z, June 4, 2025.)

Who does the work before the first hire?

The founder. CRV says the founder should be the first person to do this job (May 18, 2026). Lloyd Tabb, co-founder of Looker, describes Looker forward deploying during its free trial, so the trial felt like a customized service (First Round Review, same dates).

An existing services group. Doug McClure of PagerDuty dates its first engagement of this kind to 2019 and writes: “We just didn’t have a name for it yet.” The function launched officially the week of his post. Before it, a services group reached nearly 40 engineers and consultants at its peak (PagerDuty engineering blog, March 24, 2026, read November 20, 2026).

What should you settle before opening the role?

This section is our advice, drawn from the statements above.

  • Which version of the role. Kabir Sial separates a product builder from a platform operator. Decide which one you need. See how to hire a forward deployed engineer.
  • Level. No source we read gives a rule. Monte Carlo’s ad expects its early hire to help write the playbook, which is our reason to favor someone who has done the job before.
  • Pay. Northzone warns against tying pay to engagement revenue. Joe Schmidt notes that implementation leads who want to carry a quota are rare.
  • Location. PostHog’s first hire is fully remote. Shilpa Balaji, formerly of Palantir, describes living onsite with the customer as core to the job (First Round Review, same dates). Choose one and state it.
  • What comes next. See building a forward deployed engineering team. If the need may be temporary, compare a forward deployed engineer or a consultancy.

Which questions decide it?

  • Do you have three to 10 enterprise customers that each need significant customization? (CRV)
  • Are core engineers spending a meaningful share of their time on customer work? (CRV)
  • Are you losing deals or customers to implementation complexity? (CRV)
  • Does your own model show contract value that can carry the cost? (Frank Bien, CRV)
  • Do you know what the hire should build? If not, CRV says the founder goes first.
  • Who will carry field learning back to the product? (Anjor Kanekar)
  • Is the product heading for product-led growth? If so, James Honsa says the role is a poor fit.

What has nobody measured?

  • A threshold from employers. No employer we read has published a contract-value threshold of its own. Frank Bien’s figure for Looker is the only one.
  • Ratios. No employer read on November 20, 2026 publishes a ratio of forward deployed engineers to customers or to product engineers. Kabir Sial’s figure of 5-10 accounts per engineer, for the platform-operator kind, is one practitioner’s statement.
  • Outcomes by timing. We found no study comparing companies that hired at different stages. Nothing we read tests any of these views.

In practice

Answer the seven questions with your own numbers before you write the ad. If most answers are no, the founder keeps the work a while longer.

If most are yes, settle the version, level, pay basis and location first. Kernavique can run a forward deployed engineer executive search for the first hire or the leader of the team.

Questions

  • When should a company hire its first forward deployed engineer?
  • There is no measured answer, only opinion. CRV, a venture firm, names three to 10 enterprise customers that each need significant customization (May 18, 2026). Northzone, also a venture firm, advises using the role early, while the company is still finding its product or market (June 4, 2026). We read both on November 21, 2026.
  • How many enterprise customers do you need first?
  • CRV is the only source we read that gives a count: three to 10 enterprise customers, each needing significant customization (CRV, May 18, 2026, read November 21, 2026). It is one firm’s view. No employer we read has published a count of its own.
  • What contract value justifies a forward deployed engineer?
  • One person who ran a company gives a figure. Frank Bien, former CEO of Looker, said Looker needed around $25,000 a year per customer (First Round Review, February 24, 2026, updated October 2, 2026). CRV says annual contract value should be high enough, with room to expand, and gives no number (May 18, 2026).
  • Who does the work before the first hire?
  • CRV says the founder should be the first to do it (May 18, 2026). Lloyd Tabb, co-founder of Looker, describes Looker forward deploying during its free trial (First Round Review, February 24, 2026). At PagerDuty an earlier services group came first, by its own account of March 24, 2026.
  • What mistakes do companies make with the first hire?
  • Named sources describe several. Marc Andrusko, partner at a16z, warns of a services trap where productizing never happens (January 16, 2026). CRV names low-value accounts and field learning that never reaches the product (May 18, 2026). Tiffany Siu of First Round says founders imagine one all-in-one person (February 24, 2026).
  • Is there data on the right time to hire?
  • Not that we found. The statements in our research, read on November 20 and 21, 2026, are judgments from investors, founders and former practitioners. No employer we read publishes a ratio of forward deployed engineers to customers, and we found no study comparing companies that hired at different stages.
