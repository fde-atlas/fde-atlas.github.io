Summary

This Japanese article, by DeFactory CEO Shohei Tokumitsu, is a job-market guide for engineers, PdMs, and consultants considering a move into Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roles in Japan. As of spring 2026, roughly 35 FDE openings are continuously posted (about 26 at Japanese firms, 9 at foreign firms), with a median upper salary of ¥15 million—over twice the average software engineer salary (~¥7 million); foreign firms like Palantir and OpenAI pay ¥25–80 million. The author attributes demand to generative AI adoption, stalled PoCs, and enterprise last-mile implementation gaps, and details three hiring criteria (technical, business, culture-fit), strategies by career background, freelance rates (¥600k–1.7M/month), and reverse questions to spot fake FDE roles.

Key points

  • About 35 FDE job openings in Japan as of spring 2026, expected to reach ~100 in 2–3 years.
  • Median top salary at Japanese firms is ¥15M, over 2x the average software engineer's pay.
  • Demand driven by generative AI rollout, PoC stagnation, and last-mile implementation needs.
  • Hiring screens for technical skill, business/consulting ability, and self-driven culture fit.
  • Includes reverse questions to distinguish genuine FDE roles from mislabeled positions.

"I hear that a new job category called FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is seeing increasing job openings in Japan as well, but how large is the market actually?" "What kind of salary can I expect?" "What skills do I need to switch careers to FDE?"—Inquiries like these are rapidly increasing from engineers considering a career change to FDE roles, which have seen rapid growth in job openings in Japan between 2024 and 2026.

The FDE role, which originated at US-based AI companies such as Palantir and OpenAI, began seeing serious job openings in Japan after 2025. As of spring 2026, approximately 35 openings—about 26 at Japanese companies and about 9 at foreign companies—remain continuously posted, with the median upper limit of offered annual salaries at 15 million yen, more than double the average for software engineers in Japan (about 7 million yen).

This article provides a thorough, technology-advisor-level explanation for active engineers, PdMs, consultants, and CS professionals seriously considering a career change to FDE, covering the latest trends in the job market, the 3 criteria companies evaluate during selection, appeal strategies by professional background, the special demand in the generative AI era, how to identify quality job openings, and how to proceed with your job search. We deliver a practical guide for avoiding "FDE in name only" positions and landing a genuine high-rate FDE role.

Conclusion: As of 2026, FDE job openings in Japan are in a phase of rapid growth. Openings that were nearly nonexistent a year ago have expanded to around 35 and are expected to reach around 100 within the next 2–3 years. Offered annual compensation is forming in the ¥10–20 million range, remaining at an extremely high level of more than double the average software engineer's salary.

For a basic understanding of the FDE role itself, also see What is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Roles, careers, and going freelance, explained by a pro. This article focuses in depth on "job changes and job openings."

Conclusion: The surge in FDE job openings comes down to three structural factors: "1) explosive demand for generative AI implementation," "2) the problem of projects stalling at the PoC stage," and "3) the last-mile challenge with enterprise customers." Even as AI technology advances, there is a severe shortage of people who can handle the "last mile of implementation"—embedding AI into customers' business operations—and demand for FDEs is growing as the role that fills this gap.

Notable Japanese companies rapidly growing their FDE hiring in 2025–2026 include LayerX (AI/LLM business division), AI Shift, Knowledge Work, Money Forward, Taylor, InsightX, SoftBank, Members, and Salesforce Japan. As of spring 2026, FDE hiring is spreading across a wide range of business types, from AI/SaaS startups to major SIs.

Rather than a sell-and-done product model, it is a role that engages deeply with customers and takes responsibility through value realization, positioned as a role that directly impacts a company's revenue, churn rate, and LTV.

Conclusion: The three criteria that companies scrutinize most strictly in FDE hiring are:

"① Technical ability (implementation skills using Python, SQL, and cloud infrastructure)," "② Business ability (consulting skills for client engagement and requirements definition)," and "③ Culture fit (the ability to work autonomously under uncertainty)." Since candidates who pass all three are extremely rare, being prepared to tell concrete stories for each criterion is the key to passing the selection process.

Conclusion: The first thing evaluated in FDE selection is technical ability. The three main criteria in technical screening are: "hands-on experience writing Python and SQL," "experience building data pipelines on cloud infrastructure," and "production experience with LLM APIs and RAG."

Conclusion: The second evaluation criterion in FDE hiring is "the ability to understand a customer's business and translate latent issues into system requirements." With technical skills alone, you will be assessed as "just an implementation engineer," so demonstrating consulting fundamentals through concrete stories is the decisive factor in passing the selection process.

Here is a summary of the components of FDE business ability.

  • Interviewing and issue extraction: Drawing out essential issues from a customer's vague requests
  • Requirements definition: Translating extracted issues into implementable system requirements
  • Stakeholder coordination: Aligning the interests of the customer's executives, frontline staff, and IT department
  • Prioritization design: Determining feature priorities to maximize impact with limited resources
  • ROI estimation: Presenting return on investment in a form executives can use to make decisions
  • Change management: Designing operations when customer business transformation is involved
  • Verbalization and documentation: Documenting the rationale behind decisions in a traceable form

These are not the "exclusive domain of former consultants"; engineers can also acquire them by deliberately accumulating experience through internal side projects, leading PoCs, and client engagement. The essential rarity of FDEs lies in being "bilingual" in both technical skills and business ability.

Conclusion: The third evaluation criterion in FDE hiring is culture fit: "Can you deliver results while working autonomously in an ambiguous, fast-changing environment?" Passive talent who waits for clear instructions is not valued; what is tested is the attitude of gathering context yourself, making decisions, and proving value in short cycles.

Here is a summary of the behavioral traits that make up the culture fit required of FDEs.

Behavioral traitSpecific behaviors

Tolerance for uncertaintyContinuing to move forward even when specifications are unsettled and requirements change

You need to be prepared to demonstrate these behavioral traits with "specific episodes" in both your resume and interviews. It is important to take inventory so that you can tell multiple episodes each about "experience working autonomously in ambiguous situations," "experience building trust in a short period," and "lessons learned from failure."

Conclusion: Most people aiming to move into an FDE role come from three backgrounds: backend/data engineers, IT consultants/PMs, and customer success/sales engineers, each with different strengths, weaknesses, and skills to fill in. A strategic pitch tailored to your background is the key to a successful career change.

Conclusion: Those from backend/data engineering backgrounds are the group most likely to meet FDE hiring criteria on the technical side. However, they often lack "customer-facing experience," "understanding of business challenges," and "the ability to explain things to executives," and how to reinforce these is the key to passing the selection process.

Specific actions to compensate for these weaknesses are as follows.

  • Internal PoC leadership experience: Deliberately seek out experience handling everything from product planning to implementation

  • Gaining customer engagement opportunities: Actively join sales visits and customer meetings

Conclusion: As of 2025–2026, the generative AI trend has pushed FDE job openings into a "boom" state. FDEs with experience implementing LLMs, RAG, and agents are treated as rare talent who get "hand-picked" in the job market, and their salary ranges sit a tier higher. This boom is expected to continue through 2027–2030.

Conclusion: AI-specialized FDEs are a subcategory whose job openings and salaries have both surged in 2025–2026. While typical FDE job salary ranges are ¥10–15 million, AI-specialized FDEs are offered ranges exceeding ¥15–25 million. The rapid increase in demand for real-world LLM deployment is driving this boom.

Here is a summary of typical job requirements for AI-specialized FDEs.

  • LLM API implementation experience: Production operation of OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, etc.
  • RAG building experience: Design and operation of vector DBs, embeddings, and retrieval strategies
  • Agent design experience: Implementation with LangGraph, Mastra, AutoGen, etc.
  • Evaluation harness building: Quality monitoring with LangSmith, LangFuse, etc.
  • MLOps skills: CI/CD, model versioning, retraining pipelines
  • Governance design: PII protection, audit logs, compliance

AI-specialized FDEs with these skills are extremely rare in the Japanese market as of 2026. Because supply has not caught up with demand, you can negotiate exceptionally favorable terms for both offered salary and stock options. For details on LLM implementation skills, also see the Complete Guide to LLM Engineers, and for agent development, see the Complete Guide to AI Agent Development.

Conclusion: Experience with RAG building and prompt engineering carries extremely high evaluation weight in both document screening and technical interviews. The reason is that "these are the biggest bottlenecks in companies' production operations, and the absolute number of people with experience is overwhelmingly insufficient."

Conclusion: To reach a salary range above ¥20 million in the FDE market, "simply introducing tools" is not enough. People who can design the entire data platform architecture and handle AI/LLM integration end-to-end hold an overwhelming advantage in the market.

Here is a summary of the components of data platform architecture design capability.

  • DWH/data lake design: Selection and design of BigQuery, Snowflake, Redshift, Databricks
  • ETL/ELT design: Building pipelines with dbt, Airflow, Dagster, etc.
  • Data governance: Data catalogs, lineage, quality management
  • Master data management: Integrating master data for customers, products, and transactions
  • Access control: Row/column-level security, PII protection
  • Cost management: DWH cost optimization, storage tiering
  • AI/ML integration: Feature stores, vector DBs, data platforms for RAG
  • Observability: Data quality monitoring, SLA management, data incident response

FDEs who can design these elements in an integrated way are treated not as "mere implementers" but as "architects," and their salaries can be significantly higher. For career strategy as an IT architect, also see What Is an IT Architect? Roles, Essential Skills, and How to Go Independent as a High-Rate Freelancer.

Conclusion: The biggest key to succeeding in an FDE career change is avoiding "FDE in name only" jobs (where the actual role is something else) and securing a genuine FDE position. By mastering the specific points to check in job postings, interviews, and reverse questions, as well as how to choose agents and write your resume, you can raise both your screening pass rate and your post-hire satisfaction.

Conclusion: Even jobs advertised as FDE positions are often actually "just contracted development," "a rebranding of customer success," "a redefinition of data engineering," or "SE work." You can prevent post-hire mismatches by seeing through these via reverse questions in interviews.

In a genuine FDE job posting, it is standard for end-to-end involvement from upstream to downstream to be explicitly stated.

Conclusion: 30–50% of FDE job openings are non-public and circulate only through specific high-class IT recruitment agents. Using 2–4 agents in parallel and leveraging each one's strengths is the standard strategy for increasing your career change success rate.

Here are the selection criteria for agents strong in FDE career changes.

  • AI/Data Domain Expertise: Whether the agent has knowledge of the AI/LLM field
  • Quality of Job Openings: Number of FDE-specialized positions held and track record of top-tier placements
  • Relationship with Companies: A deep pipeline allowing direct communication with hiring managers
  • Agent's Understanding of Your Career: Whether they can help you take inventory of your career together
  • Long-Term Relationship Building: Whether you can maintain contact even after a successful job change
  • Salary Negotiation Skills: Track record of raising compensation at the offer stage
  • Ratio of Non-Public Job Openings: Whether they hold many non-public openings, not just public ones

As a point of caution when choosing an agent, it is important not to use "agents whose salary range is 8 million yen or below." Since the volume zone for FDE positions is in the 10-20 million yen range, agents focused on lower ranges cannot provide appropriate job proposals.

Conclusion: To pass the document screening for FDE positions, simply "listing your tech stack" is not enough. Descriptions that combine "what technologies you used, what business problems you solved, and how much ROI you generated" greatly influence your document screening pass rate.

The job market for transitioning to FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) entered a phase of rapid growth in Japan between 2024 and 2026. Openings, which were nearly zero a year ago, have expanded to around 35 and are expected to reach around 100 within the next 2 to 3 years. Offered salaries have formed a band of 10 to 20 million yen, maintaining an extremely high level exceeding twice the average annual salary of software engineers in Japan.

Let us once again summarize the key points for a successful FDE career transition.

  • Understanding the Job Market: Grasping the structure of Japanese and foreign companies, salary ranges, and missions by company stage
  • Addressing the three evaluation criteria: Preparation for technical skills, business skills, and culture fit
  • Strategies by previous role: A clear roadmap that leverages your strengths and reinforces your weaknesses
  • Targeting AI-specialized FDE roles: Taking advantage of the exceptional demand in the LLM, RAG, and agent domains
  • Avoiding "FDE in name only" roles: Identifying genuine positions through reverse questions and agent selection

Especially as of 2026, with the explosion of demand for generative AI implementation, AI-specialized FDE roles continue to command an "unlimited ceiling" in compensation.
