This beginner-oriented Japanese article from Paiza explains the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role: engineers stationed at customer sites who handle requirements gathering, prototyping, deployment, and post-launch improvement to make their company's platform and AI/LLM technology actually work in real business workflows.   • Unlike SES contractors, FDEs proactively identify problems and feed insights back into their own product.
  • Salary depends heavily on company, location, English proficiency, and LLM implementation experience.

What is an FDE?

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, and broadly speaking, it is a type of engineering role.

First, we will explain what an FDE does, how the role differs from that of a typical engineer, and why it has been attracting attention in recent years.

If you are curious about what an FDE does, or are interested in it as a potential position or future career path, please use this as a reference.

FDE is one of the engineering roles

"Forward Deployed" was originally a military term meaning "deployed on the front line."

Building on that, FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is translated as "front-line deployment engineer."

As the name suggests, the main job of an FDE is to be stationed for a certain period at the very front line known as "the customer's site," providing end-to-end support from the introduction of the company's platform to its full adoption.

Rather than focusing solely on implementation within the company's development team, an FDE is a position that is placed at the "front line" where customers are located.

Differences from traditional engineers

Traditional engineers mainly work within their own company, designing and implementing features and building systems.

In contrast, an FDE identifies customer problems and carries out the design, implementation, and operational improvements needed to solve them. Through this, the FDE's role is to optimize the company's product for the field.

An FDE's work goes beyond mere development. Leveraging customers' latent issues and raw data, FDEs also take on tasks such as building operational frameworks and providing consulting to deliver greater value.

Compared to traditional engineers, FDEs work in closer proximity to customers and are characterized by a strong field-driven aspect.

Why FDEs are attracting attention

Why are FDEs attracting attention now? The biggest reason is that in recent years, companies across all industries have been shifting from the phase of "introducing and trying out" technologies such as generative AI and LLMs to the phase of "using them to achieve results in actual business operations."

LLM technology on its own is highly general-purpose. As a result, it does not fit the specific business processes and workflows unique to each company's field.

This is why the role of the FDE, who understands field-level challenges and then integrates and optimizes LLMs into existing systems and proprietary data infrastructure, is attracting attention.

No matter how excellent an AI model may be, it is meaningless if it cannot be used effectively in the field. FDEs play the role of resolving these bottlenecks from both technical and business perspectives.

Main responsibilities of an FDE

As described above, we have covered the overview of the FDE role and why it is attracting attention.

So, what specific work does an FDE actually do?

Generally, an FDE's work follows the flow of requirements gathering → design → implementation → deployment → improvement.

In this section, we will explain the FDE's responsibilities along this flow.

Understanding customer challenges and gathering requirements

First, the FDE gathers requirements based on an understanding of the customer's challenges.

For an FDE, understanding the challenges is not just a matter of hearing about specifications. It is a crucial process of deeply engaging with the customer's business operations and defining the problems that truly need to be solved.

Naturally, customers are not IT professionals who are skilled at using AI. In most cases, while they may have a vague desire to "improve things using AI," the problems to be solved are not clearly defined.

The FDE therefore observes daily workflows and tasks that staff are struggling with on-site to identify where problems and bottlenecks lie. The FDE then translates these into requirements in a form that is technically feasible and delivers value—this is what requirements gathering means for an FDE.

Prototype development and production deployment

In an FDE's work, large-scale development does not happen from the start.

First, the FDE develops prototypes in short cycles based on the requirements. These are tested by users in the field, and improvements are made immediately based on the feedback received.

Through this repetition, the FDE's job is to advance agile development while continuously improving and growing the system.

Also, production deployment does not end simply with handing over the system. The FDE also makes adjustments to ensure smooth data integration with the field's core systems and proper incorporation into business workflows.

Business improvement using AI and LLMs

FDEs are responsible for business improvement using AI platforms such as OpenAI and their own company's products.

However, simply introducing them is not the end of the process. It is necessary to deeply integrate the company's core data with LLMs to enable text processing, search, automation, and more.

First, the vast amounts of data scattered across the customer's company—documents, emails, logs, and so on—are structured using LLMs. Then, using technologies such as RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), tasks that previously required specialized expertise are automated and enhanced.

Once this is achieved, business improvements become possible, such as having AI learn the know-how of skilled craftsmen to support younger workers' decision-making, or having LLMs propose optimization plans for complex supply chains.

Post-deployment improvement and product feedback

As mentioned before, an FDE's work does not end with delivering the system. Afterward, the FDE's job is to incorporate insights gained from actual operation in the field.

After deployment, the FDE analyzes user behavior in the field and data trends. For example, they evaluate things like "whether the AI's suggestions are being adopted" or "whether work speed has improved."

By promptly addressing new needs and friction with actual operations, they continue to improve accuracy.

In addition, these insights gained in the field need to be brought back to the company and fed back to the development team. Promoting the reflection and evolution of the company's own product through this process is also an important part of an FDE's job.

Skills required for FDEs and characteristics of well-suited people

As mentioned above, the specific responsibilities of an FDE are wide-ranging.

So, what skills are required for an FDE's work? And what kind of people are well suited to being an FDE?

As engineers, a certain level of programming ability is naturally essential. In addition, skills such as understanding customers' business operations and identifying problems are also required.

In this section, we will explain the skills required for FDEs and the characteristics of people well suited to the role.

Fundamentals of programming and system design

Since the role involves system design and implementation, a certain amount of programming is naturally required.

Beyond simply being able to write code, system design skills that connect data with business operations are also required.

In particular, the ability to efficiently process vast amounts of data and convert it into valuable forms on the platform (mainly knowledge of Python and SQL) is essential.

In addition, knowledge of web applications, APIs, databases, cloud, and so on makes it easier to translate customer issues into implementation.

The ability to correctly understand business operations and issues

For FDEs, the "ability to correctly understand business operations and issues" is just as important a skill as technical ability.

Simply taking the customer's words at face value is not enough to define requirements adequately. What is needed is the insight to go deep into workplaces full of jargon and identify the true issues hidden within complex business workflows.

For this reason, people who are suited to the role are those who not only love technology but also have a strong interest in the customer's business and how organizations work.

Communication skills

The communication skills referred to here are not the ability to casually chat with and befriend anyone.

In an FDE's work, there are many occasions to talk with people other than engineers. There, you need to hear about on-site workflows and issues, and conversely, explain the systems you provide.

For this reason, FDEs are required to have the ability to explain technical constraints in business language, and conversely, to translate on-site issues into system requirements.

A learning attitude that adapts to change

An FDE's work requires knowledge in areas where technological innovation is extremely rapid, such as generative AI and LLMs. For this reason, you cannot simply reuse knowledge you have already acquired; you need an attitude of constantly gathering information and continuing to learn.

Moreover, it is not just about chasing new information; you also need the speed to quickly understand the industry dynamics and data structures that differ from customer to customer.

In a situation where yesterday's correct answer may not work today, people who can continuously absorb the latest knowledge are well suited to being an FDE.

How FDEs differ from related roles

There are various related roles in the areas surrounding FDE.

To deepen your understanding of FDEs, we will explain how they differ from SES, SIers, solution architects, and ordinary web engineers.

If you have been wondering, "I understand what an FDE does, but how is it different from these roles?", please use this as a reference.

Differences between FDEs and SES

FDEs and SES are often confused because both tend to work close to the customer.

SES generally operates under a quasi-delegated contract aimed at providing labor, performing development and maintenance according to the customer's instructions.

On the other hand, the purpose of an FDE is, as mentioned above, to use the company's own products and AI through business operations to solve customer issues and improve the product.

Also, SES tends to be in a passive position, being embedded in the customer's organization.

In contrast, an FDE proactively identifies issues from on-site data and provides feedback to the product, which is a key difference.

Differences between FDEs and SIers

An SIer's main work is contract development that builds systems from scratch according to customer requests. In many cases, the goal is the completion and delivery of the system, with an emphasis on individual customization.

On the other hand, an FDE's work specializes in how to adapt the company's own platform to the field and generate value.

Also, while SIers emphasize implementing according to specifications, FDEs differ in that they engage with on-site data to identify issues themselves and take responsibility all the way through to providing feedback that grows the product.

Differences between FDEs and solution architects

A solution architect's (SA) main role is to bridge the customer's business requirements to the cloud and technology stack, and draw up the optimal blueprint (architecture).

They ensure technical feasibility and provide guidelines for construction, but they rarely accompany the project through to actual operation.

In contrast, an FDE takes responsibility for the deployment that makes the drawn blueprint work in reality. They constantly face the field, repeatedly implementing, introducing, and improving until the company's platform is deeply embedded in the customer's business.

In short, the SA proposes and designs the optimal solution, while the FDE keeps things running until value is produced in the field.

Differences between FDEs and ordinary web engineers

A web engineer's customers are mainly an unspecified large number of consumers. For this reason, their work emphasizes scalability to handle high traffic and the comfort of the UI/UX.

In contrast, an FDE embeds themselves in a specific company. What they handle is not publicly available data, but complex internal company data.

Also, an FDE focuses not just on feature development but on "how to change on-site decision-making," which is another difference from ordinary web engineers.

FDE job openings, hiring, and careers

From here, we will explain the key points to look at when reviewing FDE job postings.

Even though they are all called FDEs, the core duties and salary levels differ from company to company.

When researching job postings, it is important not only to clarify the conditions you prioritize, but also to know which points to look at.

FDE hiring as seen at OpenAI and LayerX {{45}In fact, companies such as OpenAI and LayerX are recruiting for FDE positions.

In fact, companies such as OpenAI and LayerX are posting FDE job openings.

What the two companies have in common is that they define the FDE role not merely as implementation support, but as work that involves deeply engaging with customers and tackling problem-solving.

At companies promoting the adoption of AI in this way, demand for FDEs can be said to be rising rapidly.

Job duties to look for in job postings

When looking at FDE job postings, the listed content can tell you whether the position leans more toward implementation or toward proposal work.

For example, if the posting includes content such as "building data pipelines," "SDK/API integration," or "prototyping based on requirements," practical technical skills will likely be emphasized.

Conversely, if the focus is on content such as "redesigning business processes," "defining ROI (return on investment)," or "building consensus with stakeholders," consulting-oriented work is being sought.

It is best to choose a job based on whether you want to use your skills to build tools or to transform organizations.

Points to note when researching annual salaries

FDE salaries vary greatly depending on individual conditions, since the role combines specific technical and business skills.

In particular, at foreign AI companies, English proficiency is essential, and salary levels tend to jump significantly.

In addition, factors such as work location (domestic or global) and whether you have experience implementing LLMs or AI also directly affect salary.

Therefore, when researching the salaries of FDE job openings, you should not rely solely on typical engineer salary benchmarks, but carefully review the conditions stated in each job posting.

Frequently asked questions about FDE (Q&A)

Q. Does FDE work require programming skills?

A. Yes, it does. FDE work involves not only hearing out customer issues, but also frequently building actual prototypes and systems. For this reason, programming skills that allow basic development are essential.

Q. Can someone with no experience aim to become an FDE?

A. It would be difficult for someone with no development experience at all to become an FDE. A realistic path is to first become a general engineer, accumulate knowledge and experience in programming, web application development, databases, and so on, and then aim for an FDE role.

Q. Is English proficiency necessary for FDEs?

A. It depends on the company. At foreign companies or companies that need to collaborate with overseas teams, job postings requiring English proficiency are not uncommon. However, if customers are mainly domestic companies, English may not be required.

Q. Do FDE job duties differ from company to company?

A. The emphasis of the duties required of an FDE differs by company. Some companies focus on implementation, while others place greater weight on customer negotiations and implementation support. Review job postings carefully and check the scope of implementation and the nature of customer interactions.

Q. Can FDEs work remotely?

A. It depends on the company and the project. FDE duties include meetings with customers and implementation support. Some cases may be primarily online, while others may require in-person interaction. Review job postings carefully and check the job duties and working environment.

Summary

This article explained the duties of an FDE, the skills required, and the differences from related occupations such as SES.

  • FDEs identify customer issues and handle design, implementation, and operational improvement.
  • They need not only programming skills but also the ability to accurately understand customers' business operations and issues.
  • Unlike SES, they proactively identify issues and carry work through to feedback.
  • Salaries vary depending on the company, work location, experience, English proficiency, and other factors.
