Introducing the FDE Model | A Practical Guide to Adopting an FDE-Style Development Framework in Your Company

Original title: FDEモデル導入｜FDE型の開発体制を自社に取り入れる実務ガイド

FDE総研 · 2026-08-06 · 5 min read

Summary

This FDE総研 pillar page collects practical guides on adopting an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) model in-house. It compares three ways to build a team (hiring, internal training, external support) with a 90-day plan, covers evaluation systems, contract types (quasi-mandate vs. contract for work), cost benchmarks, case studies including Palantir deployments, and failure analysis. The core argument: FDE adoption fails mainly from poor purpose-setting, alignment, and exit design rather than technical skill, and should proceed through a four-stage path from pilot selection to horizontal rollout.

Key points

  • Team building options: hiring, internal development, or external accompaniment, compared on cost, ramp-up time, and risk.
  • Cost benchmarks: full-time FDEs at ¥8–15M annual salary; contracted FDEs at ¥0.8–2M monthly.
  • Contracts: quasi-mandate during exploratory phases, contract-for-work once specifications are fixed.
  • Failures stem from purpose, agreement, and exit design, not FDE technical ability.
  • Adoption follows four stages: selection, minimal deployment, templating, and horizontal expansion.

How should your company adopt an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)-style development structure? Based on firsthand field experience, this guide explains the practical aspects of building the structure, selecting projects, defining evaluation metrics, and the process from PoC to production operation.
