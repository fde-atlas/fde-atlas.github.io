"Isn't 'FDE' Ultimately Just a Rebranding of On-Site Client SEs?" We Asked Accenture

Original title: 「FDE」って結局、客先常駐SEのリブランディングじゃないの？ アクセンチュアに聞いてみた

ITmedia AI＋ · 2026-06-01 · 5 min read

Summary

ITmedia interviews Accenture's Hoshina Katsuyo and Kataoka Toshiyuki about whether FDEs are just rebranded client-site engineers. They argue the real shift is from billing by man-hours to outcome-based contracts, with on-site presence merely a means to deliver results quickly. Accenture launched a joint FDE organization with Microsoft and defined its own product-agnostic RDE role, built around roughly 400 people from its 2025 acquisition of Yumemi, aiming to train thousands of T-shaped professionals in small teams.

Key points

  • Palantir pioneered the FDE role; OpenAI, Anthropic and Google now hire similar positions.
  • Accenture's RDE is product-agnostic, unlike FDEs tied to specific platforms.
  • Outcome-based contracts replace traditional man-hour billing in the AI era.
  • Yumemi's ~400 staff and outcome-committed development culture anchor the RDE system.
  • Accenture aims to train thousands of T-shaped talent, moving back from large-scale division of labor to small elite teams.

Among companies aiming to spread AI products, such as US-based Palantir Technologies (hereafter Palantir) and US-based OpenAI, the use of the new profession "FDE" (Forward Deployed Engineer) is advancing. This role involves being stationed at customer sites and handling everything from AI implementation to delivering results.

However, this way of working overlaps with the traditional "on-site resident engineer." Could IT vendors and consulting firms simply be rebranding professions that have existed for years and starting a new business model based on billing by person-months? We put this question to Accenture, which launched an FDE organization jointly with US-based Microsoft in April and also advocates its own "RDE": Mr. Hoshina Sei (AI and Data Lead, Head of the AI Center) and Mr. Kataoka Toshiyuki (Managing Director).

What is an FDE?

Palantir is said to be the first to formalize the FDE role. According to the company's official blog, an FDE (or FDSE) is a software engineer who deploys Palantir's platform to address customers' most difficult challenges. While conventional engineers build one feature for many customers, FDEs emphasize implementing many features for a single customer.

Similar roles appear in job postings from AI platform companies such as OpenAI, US-based Anthropic, and US-based Google. Consulting firms and IT vendors are also rushing to launch FDE organizations in partnership with platform companies.

Accenture is also making progress, launching an FDE organization jointly with Microsoft and leveraging FDEs through collaboration with Google Cloud. In addition, it has internally defined a new role called RDE (Reinvention Deployed Engineer), which supports customers without depending on specific products, in contrast to FDEs that focus on deploying particular products.

Isn't this just a "rebranding of on-site resident SEs"?

So how do FDEs and RDEs differ from traditional on-site resident engineers? Mr. Hoshina says, "(On-site resident engineers and FDEs/RDEs) are polar opposites in mindset. We have zero intention of trying to sell conventional consultants or engineers under a different guise."

What Mr. Hoshina emphasizes is the shift from effort-based to outcome-based standards. Consulting and system integration have generally charged fees based on personnel working hours. However, the question lingers: "In an era when generative AI can dramatically compress development hours, is this billing model really correct?" (Mr. Hoshina).

What Accenture practices is an outcome-based contract model in which compensation is received for delivering pre-agreed results within a set period. Being stationed at customer sites is positioned as a means of working closely with customers to deliver results quickly.

In 2025, it acquired Yumemi, which plans and develops digital services. The roughly 400 professionals gained there form the core of the new RDE structure.

[Correction history: June 2, 2026, 8:00 a.m. The acquisition of Yumemi was listed as "2024," but the correct year is "2025."]

According to Mr. Kataoka, the founder of Yumemi who now leads the RDE structure at Accenture, even before the acquisition, Yumemi had been working on development committed to outcomes by displaying customer ROI on the admin screens of some services. In the SaaS industry, where it is common to push customers to adapt their business processes to the service, Yumemi also focused on customizing services for each individual customer. When FDEs became a hot topic in the industry, he recalls, "I realized, ah, this (the development structure Yumemi had been practicing) is what an FDE is."

In response to the emergence of generative AI, Yumemi also moved to abolish role divisions such as engineers, designers, and product managers, redefining everyone as "product creators." Mr. Kataoka highlights that this initiative was "ahead of recent trends." The aim is to build the RDE structure by combining Yumemi's outcome-committed development culture with Accenture's vast assets.

Who will be the RDEs? Scaling "T-shaped talent" to the thousands

Accenture's RDEs are expected to support customers in teams of a few people, gathering professionals with expertise in AI engineering, the relevant industry and business processes, change management (efforts to support organizational transformation), and specific products. However, Mr. Hoshina points out, "For example, if you team up someone who knows strategy but not implementation with someone who is good at implementation but lacks a strategic perspective, it won't work well because their areas of knowledge don't overlap." Teams are therefore composed of people who have acquired the full range of knowledge needed to support customers while also honing their own specialties.

Accenture has also undertaken a company-wide reorganization to strengthen its RDE organization. Mr. Hoshina is accelerating talent development by launching internal training programs and hands-on projects. While he cannot disclose specific numerical targets, he says the goal is to develop talent "in the thousands."

From "division of labor in a giant organization" back to "small elite teams"

Not just at Accenture, the IT services industry has increasingly specialized into areas such as consulting, BPR, system development, and operations. However, as AI advances, each of these processes is being dramatically streamlined, and an era may come where it is more efficient to run a wide range of work with a small elite team. "Division of labor and standardization were necessary as the industry matured. But AI has been a game changer," says Mr. Kataoka.

That said, both Mr. Hoshina and Mr. Kataoka say, "We are not strongly attached to the names FDE or RDE. It's not that we want to do FDE work; our goal is, above all, to create business outcomes for customers." Not a mere rebranding, but rebuilding an outcome-driven way of working and the organization that supports it for the AI era -- they reiterated that intent.
