Switching Careers to Become an FDE: A Skills Gap Analysis and 8-Week Plan

Original title: 转行做 FDE：技能差距分析与 8 周计划

FDE Times · 2026 · 13 min read

Summary

FDE Times analyzes 863 open FDE job postings from 181 companies to map skill gaps for career switchers, paired with an 8-week preparation plan. Customer-facing work appears in 84% of postings, ahead of Python (59%), API and systems integration (58%), and LLM/agent/RAG experience (57%). Only 54 postings are junior roles, and the authors suggest engineers without deployment experience first spend 12–18 months doing deployment work. The piece also breaks down transferable skills and gaps by current role, from backend to consulting.

Key points

  • Based on 863 open FDE postings from 181 companies.
  • Customer-facing work required by 84% of postings, the top skill.
  • Python (59%), API integration (58%), LLM/RAG (57%) lead technical demands.
  • Only 54 junior roles; 12–18 months deployment experience advised first.
  • Role-by-role breakdown of transferable skills and gaps included.

Your 8-week prep plan

8 weeks is prep time, not a job search timeline. Our new-grad FDE hiring analysis for engineers in Vietnam concluded that for most engineers without deployment experience, doing 12–18 months of deployment work first and then moving into an FDE role is the more realistic path.

  • Week 1 Customer-facing work and communication Interview three people about a workflow they dislike, and write down the real problem, not the feature they ask for by name.
  • Week 2 LLMs, agents, and RAG Without using a framework, hand-write a tool-calling agent loop on your own data, and document every step.
  • Week 3 TypeScript, JavaScript, and frontend Build a single-page demo with React and TypeScript on top of a public API, including loading and error states.
  • Week 4
  • Week 5 Machine learning Train a baseline model on tabular data, and write down when you would not use deep learning.
  • Week 6 Complete a portfolio project Deliver a project end to end, with a README, an eval set, and a short write-up of your decisions.

  • Week 8 Apply Rewrite your resume around measurable outcomes, then apply to the matching open roles below.
  • Week 0 Python Build a small command-line tool that pulls data from an API, cleans it, and writes it to a database, with tests.
  • Week 0 APIs and system integration Wire two APIs together end to end, with retries, idempotency keys, and a webhook receiver.
  • Week 0
  • Week 0 Cloud platforms (AWS, GCP, Azure) Deploy a small service to a cloud account with least-privilege access, then list what would need to change to run it inside a customer's VPC.

Frequently asked questions

Can a backend engineer switch to FDE?

Yes. Backend skills are exactly what most roles ask for first: of 863 open FDE roles, 59% require Python, 58% require APIs and system integration, and 41% require distributed systems or system design. What backend work rarely trains is the customer side: 84% of roles require customer-facing work. Our career-change analysis found that many software engineers lack experience directly engaging customers, uncovering undocumented workflows, and getting LLMs into production.

Is customer-facing experience required?

For most roles, yes: 84% of open FDE roles require customer-facing work, stakeholder management, or executive communication—a higher share than any technical skill. Our analysis notes that Anthropic requires 4+ years of technical, customer-facing experience and recommends building that experience in your current role first.

How long does it take to switch to FDE?

It depends on how big your gap is. The planner on this page uses 8 weeks to close your five biggest skill gaps, ship a project, and practice interviews; that's preparation time, not a job-search timeline. For engineers without deployment experience, our analysis of new-grad FDE hiring concludes for engineers in Vietnam that a more realistic path is to first accumulate 12–18 months of deployment experience before moving into an FDE role. Of 863 open roles, only 54 are junior by job title.

Do FDEs have to travel?

Often required. 45% of open FDE roles mention travel or on-site work with customers, rising to 59% for senior positions. Our analysis of travel requirements shows a common range of 25–50% of time spent at customer sites; a survey of remote FDE roles found that even Cohere, which supports remote work, requires 20–40% travel. Always ask for the specific percentage before accepting an offer.

Is moving from software engineer to FDE a step down?

Based on the data we have, no. US FDE roles with published salary ranges have a median base salary of $200k (168 roles, see FDE salary data); we don't collect software engineer salaries, so we can't compare the two directly. The role has its own promotion ladder: our salary ladder analysis found that OpenAI's FDE Manager I requires 8+ years of experience; our career path guide lists paths such as Principal FDE, product manager, engineering manager, and starting a company. What changes is how you're evaluated: by the end result for a customer, not by an individual component you own.

What does the FDE interview test?

According to the courses referenced in our problem decomposition round guide, the interview process has four rounds: problem decomposition, customer simulation, technical, and behavioral. The problem decomposition round scores structured decomposition, empathy for the end user, reasoning with data, and judgment about scope; the most common mistake is jumping straight to a solution. See the round-by-round breakdown of the FDE interview for details.

Do you need AI or LLM experience to become an FDE?

Increasingly yes: 57% of open FDE roles require LLMs, agents, or RAG, 21% mention evals, and 39% require broader machine learning. If you don't yet have that experience, when it's one of your biggest gaps, the plan above schedules LLMs and evals in the first few weeks, starting with LLM fundamentals.

Which skills should you learn first?

Start with the skills you lack that are most demanded in open roles. Across all 863 roles, the most demanded are customer-facing work and communication (84%), Python (59%), APIs and system integration (58%), LLMs, agents, and RAG (57%), and TypeScript, JavaScript, and frontend (45%). Check off the skills you already have in the planning tool, and the rest will be ranked by demand for your level.

Methodology

Data. 863 open Forward Deployed Engineer roles from 181 companies, sourced from official job boards (Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, Workday, plus the official career sites of Amazon, Microsoft, ByteDance, and Tencent), refreshed multiple times a day; pages are regenerated when a role changes.

Skills. A fixed list of 15 skills, each identified in the requirements section of a job description using a fixed matching rule. Sections such as benefits, salary, "about us," and equal opportunity statements are removed first, along with the company's own name, so that a company describing itself or its own products isn't counted as requiring a skill. A role either requires a skill or it doesn't; strength of the requirement is not measured.

Levels. Inferred from job titles: intern, graduate, junior, and associate count as junior; senior, staff, principal, lead, and manager count as senior; everything else counts as mid-level. With 5+ years of experience, both senior and mid-level roles are matched.

Readiness. For each open role at your level, take the share of its required skills that you've checked off, then average across those roles. 100 means you have every skill listed by every role at that level; it does not measure how well you know those skills. Roles where no skills can be identified are excluded.

What it can't see. Industry knowledge, security clearances, language ability, and education requirements are not in the list. A role that lists "Python" only as an example is counted the same as one that makes Python a hard requirement.
