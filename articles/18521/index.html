What to expect for Exa's Forward Deployed Engineer interview

23 July 2026

Exa is building the search engine for AIs, backed by a $250M Series C led by a16z at a $2.2 billion valuation. Its Search API already powers agents, Fortune 500s, and AI labs. This Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role sits on the GTM team in San Francisco and is described as the "technical tip of the spear." You embed with customers' engineering teams, take accounts from first cold outbound all the way to production integration and closed deal, and ship the demos, fixes, and features that make Exa's API work in the real world. It is explicitly not a support role: you are an engineer who sells and a seller who ships.

Exa wants someone who can write and deploy production code in the morning and close a deal in the afternoon. That means comfort context-switching across accounts, codebases, and industries, a high tolerance for ambiguity and intensity, and the confidence to hold your own in high-stakes conversations with CTOs, engineers, and PMs. The role is in-person in SF, involves travel for customer engagements, and rewards people who have shipped user-facing products end to end and driven real commercial outcomes.

Quick Stats

* Typical process: 4 to 5 rounds, roughly 2 to 4 weeks

* Format: Recruiter phone screen, then a mix of video and in-person SF rounds (final onsite is in person)

* Core focus: Full-stack engineering, API integration and debugging, customer-facing sales, product sense, high-agency ownership

* Difficulty: Hard, because you must clear a real technical bar AND prove you can sell and communicate in the same loop

What Exa Looks For

* Several years of programming experience: you could build and deploy a production server from scratch

* A track record of high performance in demanding environments (top CS program, startup, competitive sport, or similar)

* Genuine comfort with direct customer interaction: you have sold, supported, or held your own in high-stakes conversations

* High tolerance for context-switching, ambiguity, sustained intensity, and travel

What to Expect

A recruiter or GTM lead opens with a call to understand your background, your motivation for Exa specifically, and whether the hybrid engineer-plus-seller profile actually fits you. Expect to talk through your resume at a high level, your experience shipping products and interacting with customers, and logistics like being in-person in SF, willingness to travel, and visa status (Exa sponsors STEM OPT, OPT, H1B, O1, and E3). This is where you deliver a crisp "why Exa, why this role" pitch.

Example Questions

* "Why do you want a Forward Deployed Engineer role rather than a pure engineering or pure sales job?"

* "Tell me about a time you both built something technical and had to convince someone to adopt it."

* "What do you know about Exa and the problem of search for AIs?"

* "Are you comfortable being in-person in San Francisco and traveling for customer engagements?"

Tips

* Lead with a two-part story that proves you can ship code and win people over, since that duality is the whole role.

* Show you understand Exa's mission (search for the AI era, powering agents and AI labs) rather than reciting generic company facts.

* Rehearse this quick pitch with Nora's Standard Mode, which mirrors the classic phone-screen mix of motivation, background, and logistics.

Because the posting requires that you "could build and deploy a production server from scratch," expect a hands-on technical round. This is typically live coding or a practical build exercise rather than pure algorithm puzzles: implementing an API integration, wiring up an endpoint, parsing and handling responses, or debugging across the stack. You may be asked to talk through how you would architect a small service and deploy it. Interviewers want to see clean, working code and pragmatic decision-making under time pressure.

* "Build a small server that queries an external API and returns transformed results. Walk me through your choices."

* "Here is a broken integration. Debug why the API responses are not rendering on the frontend."

* "How would you design and deploy a production service that wraps a search API for a customer?"

* "Walk me through handling rate limits, retries, and errors when calling a third-party API."

* Practice full-stack fluency: be ready to move from backend API behavior to frontend rendering, since the role spans the whole stack.

* Narrate your debugging out loud and prioritize a working solution over a perfect one, which mirrors how FDEs unblock customers fast.

This is the round that separates an FDE from a normal engineer. Expect a role-play or scenario where you demo Exa's value, pitch to a skeptical technical buyer, or unblock a stuck customer. Interviewers will play a CTO, engineer, or PM and gauge how you adapt your message, handle objections, and drive toward a commercial outcome. You may be asked to walk through how you would take an account from cold outbound to closed deal, or how you would turn a support conversation into an expansion.

* "Pitch Exa's Search API to me as if I'm a CTO who already uses a competitor."

* "A customer is stuck integrating our API and getting frustrated. Walk me through how you unblock them."

* "How would you take an account from first cold outbound to a production integration and closed deal?"

* "Tell me about a time you drove a commercial outcome or closed revenue."

* Frame technical answers in terms of customer value and business impact, not just implementation details.

* Show you can read the room and re-pitch for a CTO versus an engineer versus a PM, exactly the audiences the posting names.

* Prepare STAR stories about selling, supporting, and unblocking people using Nora's Behavioral Mode so your customer-facing examples land cleanly.

The final stage is typically an in-person onsite in San Francisco with several team members, blending a deeper technical or take-home-style exercise with behavioral and culture-fit conversations. Exa describes itself as high agency and low ego, and this role's job description "changes monthly," so expect probing on how you handle ambiguity, context-switching, sustained intensity, and ownership. You may present past work, walk through a demo you built, or work through a realistic customer problem end to end. Meeting the team in person also lets Exa gauge whether you would be "the person customers think of when they think of Exa."

* "Describe a time your responsibilities changed suddenly and you had to figure things out with no playbook."

* "Tell me about the most technically ambitious thing you have shipped end to end."

* "How do you prioritize when three customers are all blocked at once and your queue is on fire?"

* "Why is organizing the world's knowledge for machines a problem you care about?"

* Bring concrete artifacts: a product you shipped, a demo you can walk through, or a real technical win you can explain to both engineers and non-engineers.

* Emphasize high-agency, low-ego stories where you took ownership without waiting to be asked, which matches Exa's stated culture.

* Use Nora's Behavioral Mode to tighten ambiguity, intensity, and ownership stories, and Nora's Technical Mode to stay sharp for any onsite build or system-design segment.

1) How many rounds are there?

Expect roughly 4 to 5 rounds: a recruiter screen, a technical coding/systems round, a customer-facing sales simulation, and an in-person SF onsite that blends technical and behavioral evaluation. Exact structure varies, and this reflects the typical loop for a hybrid engineer-plus-seller role at a company of Exa's stage rather than a published process.

2) What topics are most common?

* Building and deploying production services, API integration, and full-stack debugging (backend behavior to frontend rendering)

* Customer-facing skills: pitching to CTOs, engineers, and PMs, unblocking customers, and driving deals from outbound to close

3) How long does the process take?

Usually about 2 to 4 weeks, depending on scheduling and travel to San Francisco for the in-person onsite. Fast-moving startups like Exa can compress this considerably for strong candidates.

4) How should I prepare?

* Sharpen your ability to build and deploy a small production server and debug across the full stack, since that technical bar is explicit in the posting.

* Prepare a set of stories that prove both sides of the role: shipping user-facing products and driving commercial or customer outcomes.

* Research Exa's mission and Search API so you can pitch it credibly to a technical buyer during a role-play.

* Practice with Nora AI: Standard Mode for the recruiter screen, Technical Mode for the coding and systems round, Behavioral Mode for the sales simulation and onsite ownership stories, and Salary Negotiation Mode once an offer lands (the range is $150K to $250K plus equity).
