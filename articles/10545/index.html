From "usable" to "useful": How is Chengdu's AI empowering all industries? | Chengdu Development

Full text

Cover News reporter Zhao Yi

On October 24, the first Chengdu Artificial Intelligence Ecosystem Conference and the 2026 Tencent Cloud Western Architects Summit were held, where Tencent's WorkBuddy FDE talent development program made its national debut, attracting 105 companies and about 280 trainees in its first cohort alone. In the same week, the 23rd China Computer Conference was held in Chengdu for the first time, and the "Tianfu Huiyan" integrated AI platform for empowering scientific and technological innovation went live. In addition, Chengdu High-tech Zone's policy of "up to 200 million yuan in computing power vouchers plus 400 million yuan in model vouchers per year" has already taken effect. In 2025, Chengdu's core AI industry exceeded 150 billion yuan in scale, with over 1,200 companies and about 30,000 P of available computing power.

As AI accelerates its entry into the production frontline, "being usable" is only the starting point—"being used well" is the core question. The answer lies in whether AI can truly embed itself in the daily operations of specific industries. From "growing itself" to "empowering thousands of industries," Chengdu's AI sector is crossing a critical threshold. This threshold defines not only the industry's scale ceiling, but also the depth of development with which a city reshapes its industrial capacity through AI.

Scene of the Chengdu Artificial Intelligence Ecosystem Conference and the 2026 Tencent Cloud Western Architects Summit (photo by Meng Tingting)

Cultivating local AI technical talent

Opening the "narrow door" of industrial implementation

On October 24, the first Chengdu Artificial Intelligence Ecosystem Conference was held at Tianfu Changdao Digital Cultural and Creative Park in Chengdu High-tech Zone, attended by about a thousand architects, developers, and enterprise technology decision-makers from Chengdu, Chongqing, and Xi'an. The most anticipated part of the conference was the national debut of Tencent WorkBuddy's FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) talent development program.

Turning the training for a single role into a full-fledged national program shows just how much weight FDE carries. It directly addresses a problem many companies have encountered but rarely dealt with head-on. Zhang Yu, Vice President of Tencent Cloud, put it bluntly: AI tools already exist, pilots have been done, but what's missing is someone who understands the business, understands AI, and can actually fit the solution into business processes. In the past, companies paid a high price to bring in external teams, and "once the project was done, the team left, taking the capability with them."

FDE takes a different approach: instead of doing projects for companies, it helps companies develop their own people. Topics go into the classroom, solutions go back to the field, and capabilities stay with the company. After the intensive training, there is also 90 days of online support to help companies truly get their first scenario up and running.

National debut of the FDE talent development program (image provided by the organizer)

The market's response is even more convincing. The first cohort saw 105 companies and more than 280 trainees sign up, covering 15 industries including construction and real estate, healthcare, manufacturing, and finance, with over 70% of registrants using WorkBuddy basically every day. The trainee roster includes not only R&D and operations staff, but also finance, HR, sales, and company executives; one company even sent 14 employees to train at two locations.

Among them, first-cohort registrant Sichuan Gezhi Yanchuang Technology Co., Ltd. was incubated from a University of Electronic Science and Technology of China team. Its self-developed "Gezhi INNO 1" robot has already been launched, and the company has built an FDE service workbench based on WorkBuddy, attempting to connect customers' ERP, OA, and production data to help customers build business and production operation dashboards. For the company, this is no longer just a training program, but a talent strategy move.

105 companies and 280 people sitting in a classroom is not sporadic demand that a single training class could absorb. It shows that demand from Chengdu companies for talent capable of embedding AI into business processes has emerged at scale. The FDE debut landing in Chengdu fills exactly this gap.

The "Tianfu Huiyan" integrated AI platform for empowering scientific and technological innovation officially goes live (image provided by the organizer)

Building a solid computing power policy foundation

Lowering the threshold for enterprise adoption

After talent returns to their posts, whether classroom solutions can become actions at work depends on two conditions: first, whether there is computing power and models available to call upon, and second, whether the company can afford the cost of accessing computing power and deploying models. Only when both conditions hold does the training have room to land.

The technical condition is about whether the foundation is wide enough. On October 22, the 23rd China Computer Conference (CNCC2026) opened in Chengdu—the first time this top academic conference in the computer field has been held in Chengdu—with more than 10,000 computing-related professionals attending. During the conference, the "Tianfu Huiyan" integrated AI platform for empowering scientific and technological innovation, built by Sichuan Province, officially went live. The platform is built on a "1+3+N" model: centered on 1 scientific foundation large model, supported by 3 major platforms—domestic computing power, scientific data, and dry-wet intelligent laboratories—and expanding into N application scenarios, connecting the full process of prediction, experimentation, validation, and feedback, and precisely addressing researchers' four major pain points: unspecialized models, insufficient computing power, hard-to-find data, and slow validation.

According to reports, the "Tianfu Huiyan" 1.0 scientific foundation large model has about 35 billion activated parameters, relies on about 2 million pieces of professional corpus from Sichuan's advantageous fields such as materials and healthcare, has completed full-stack adaptation and post-training on a domestic computing platform, improving task completion rates by 20% and reducing computing power consumption by about 70%; the platform's computing power has reached 408 P and will expand to 600 P by year-end. The official positioning is straightforward: moving scientific research from "repeated trial and error" to "intelligent iteration."

But being technically compatible does not mean companies can afford it—cost is crucial. For example, Chengdu High-tech Zone's policy comes down to concrete numbers: up to 200 million yuan in computing power vouchers per year, with subsidies at 50% of intelligent computing settlement costs, up to 20 million yuan per entity per year; up to 400 million yuan in model vouchers per year, subsidizing no more than 50% of third-party large model usage fees, up to 10 million yuan per entity per year.

Building on being the first in the country to introduce computing power vouchers, Chengdu plans to add "token vouchers": the city will issue no more than 100 million yuan in total per year, with a cumulative annual cap of 2 million yuan per entity; token voucher support per entity will not exceed 30% of the actual purchase amount, and no more than 50% for small and micro enterprises. With a wide enough technical foundation and manageable costs for companies, AI entering the industrial chain has its prerequisites in place.

Zhiyuan Southwest Embodied Intelligence Industrial Base in Pidu District (image source: Publicity Department of the CPC Pidu District Committee)

Going deep into real industrial scenarios

Anchoring AI's continuous "empowerment"

The foundation and costs have laid the "tracks" right up to the workshop door. What remains to be seen is whether the AI train can run—and run steadily—on the production frontline.

Manufacturing is the most direct "exam hall." Data shows that nearly 80% of Chengdu's industrial enterprises above designated size have completed digital transformation, the penetration rate of digital R&D and design tools is 94.6%, and 2,734 new technological transformation projects of over 5 million yuan have been implemented cumulatively. Chengdu Guangshu Huilian's AI intelligent analysis platform has been deployed in mine main transport, auxiliary transport, construction processes, and equipment status monitoring; supported by local computing power, its image intelligent analysis performance has improved about 3-fold. At the Zhiyuan Southwest Embodied Intelligence Industrial Base in Pidu District, the data collection and training center for humanoid robots connects directly to the computing resources of the Chengdu Intelligent Computing Center, iterating models with real-scenario data and shortening R&D cycles. What these cases have in common is not "using AI," but that AI has been placed in key positions in the production process and has begun to take responsibility for outcomes.

The cultural and creative industry is another distinctive track for Chengdu. The Xinghai Intelligent Computing Ecosystem Platform, built by the Chengdu Intelligent Computing Center together with ecosystem partners, is the country's first platform to achieve production-grade film and video generation based on the Ascend 910A. It has supported the production and release of the 20-episode animated series "Yi Shi Zhi Yu" (The Devoured Domain), with costs as low as 40 yuan per minute, compared to the industry norm of 90 to 200 yuan per minute.

Embodied intelligence is taking a different path: policy proactively organizes scenarios. In June 2026, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission of the State Council jointly launched a special action for real-scenario training of humanoid robots and embodied intelligence. Chengdu has already seen more than 60 real-scenario training scenarios emerge; according to the plan, by November 2026 the number of deployed complete machines will exceed 6,400, with a goal of achieving ten-thousand-unit-level deployment by year-end. The "hard metrics" for embodied intelligence here are not any single yield or cost figure, but the conversion rate "from sample to product"—whether scenarios can be continuously supplied, whether deployment can reach scale, and whether products can be delivered stably in real operation.

To keep AI accountable for these "hard metrics," policy support is still needed. Chengdu's goals are clear: by 2027, the core AI industry will exceed 260 billion yuan in scale, with 100 "innovative products" and 100 demonstration application scenarios implemented, and adoption rates of new-generation intelligent terminals, intelligent agents, and other applications exceeding 70%. The country's first local government regulation on artificial intelligence, the "Chengdu Measures for Promoting AI Industry Development," took effect on September 1.

From students in the FDE classroom, to the computing power foundation of "Tianfu Huiyan", to computing vouchers and model vouchers that bring costs down to a level enterprises can afford, Chengdu's AI industry initiatives are strung together like pearls. Only when AI is no longer just a technical demo but becomes a continuously running part of the production line can Chengdu's AI industry truly make the leap from "growing itself" to "empowering thousands of industries". Only by making "using it well" the norm will Chengdu have the most solid foundation for high-quality development.
