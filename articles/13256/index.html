FDE Sprint: the one-page offer

Nasser Ghanemzadeh · 2026-09-23 · 5 min read

Summary

Nasser Ghanemzadeh, through his firm Eupraxic, offers FDE Sprint: in ten business days he ships one AI autopilot workflow into a client's product, running on their data and codebase. Drawing on Sequoia's copilot-versus-autopilot distinction, he defines an autopilot as doing the work itself with human approval, rather than assisting. The fixed-price engagement includes a signed Sprint Brief, a scoring rubric, a maintenance playbook, a handoff session, and 30 days of async support. It targets B2B SaaS founders of 5-50 person companies, costs $5,000 through the November 17 kickoff ($7,500 from December 1), and carries a full refund guarantee if no prototype runs by Day 5 or acceptance criteria aren't met by Day 10.

Key points

  • Ten-day fixed-price sprint delivering one production-ready AI workflow, not a demo.
  • Autopilot framing from Sequoia's essay: work gets done, a human approves it.
  • $5,000 through November 17 kickoff, rising to $7,500 from December 1.
  • Full refund if no Day 5 prototype or Day 10 acceptance criteria miss.
  • Targets 5-50 person B2B SaaS firms with an unfilled AI/FDE role.

Here's what we're doing.

In ten business days I ship your first autopilot into your product, through my firm, Eupraxic: one workflow that does the work instead of helping a user do it. It has a clear input, a clear output, and a person on your team who approves the result. It runs on your data, in your codebase, deployed to staging or delivered as a pull request your engineers can merge. No deck and no demo video.

A copilot is a tool that helps a professional do the work faster. The professional stays responsible for the result, and your customer pays for it per seat. An autopilot does the work itself and hands back a finished result with a score. A person approves that result instead of producing it, and the buyer pays for the work. I take the terms from Sequoia's essay "Services: The New Software" by Julien Bek (March 2026).

You know AI has to be in the product this year. The roadmap is full, and nobody on the team has taken an AI workflow to production. This is the shortest honest path from "we should do something with AI" to "it's live."

What you have on Day 10

  • The workflow, in staging or production, or as a production-ready pull request in your repo.
  • A recorded walkthrough of the workflow and the decisions behind it.
  • A scoring rubric your team uses to judge output quality, and a playbook they use to maintain and extend the workflow without me.
  • A 30-day roadmap and a 60 to 90 minute handoff session with your engineers.
  • 30 days of async support after handoff. Something breaks in week three, you write, I fix.

How the ten days run

Day 1 we scope: one workflow, one user, the input, the output, and the acceptance criteria, written into a Sprint Brief you sign off by email. Days 2 and 3 are design. Days 4 and 5 produce a working prototype on your real data, and you watch it run on Day 5. Days 6 to 9 are edge cases, error states, logging and prompt tuning. Day 10 is handoff.

Who it's for

Founders and CEOs of B2B SaaS companies with 5 to 50 people and paying customers, who have a workflow in mind (or three, and want help choosing), and whose team can give me codebase and data access plus one decision-maker who answers within a business day.

Two signs it fits: you posted an AI engineer or forward deployed engineer role and it has been open for more than a month, or you paid a contractor or agency for an AI build and it never reached production.

Who it's not for

Anyone who wants to "add AI" without a specific workflow, needs a full AI transformation, has data that isn't accessible, wants a general chatbot, or needs guaranteed production deployment regardless of their infrastructure.

Price

$5,000 for Sprints kicking off through Tuesday November 17. From the Tuesday December 1 kickoff, $7,500. Half on signing, half on Day 10. The price is set against what the workflow replaces: a contractor month, an engineer role you haven't filled, or six weeks of two engineers. The deposit is invoiced on signing.Pay the deposit.

Guarantee

No working prototype on your data by Day 5? Stop the Sprint and get a full refund. The workflow in your signed Brief isn't running to its acceptance criteria by Day 10? Full refund. The Brief is the contract, which is why Day 1 is the most important day of the ten.

Quick facts

  • One Sprint at a time.
  • Next kickoffs: Tuesday November 17 (deposit by Thursday November 12) and Tuesday December 1 (deposit by Thursday November 26).
  • Next kickoffs: new dates are being set. Ask on the Scoping Call.
  • Needed before Day 1: read-only codebase access, sample data, and a data processing agreement with your model provider.
  • In a first Sprint, a person still approves anything that leaves your company. Autonomy comes later, once the rubric scores hold.
  • Not included: a second workflow, refactoring outside the workflow, new infrastructure, compliance review, model fine-tuning.
  • A second workflow is its own Sprint. Book it within 6 months of your handoff and it runs at the price you paid for the first.
  • After handoff, I suggest running the workflow for one account first and widening from there. Help with the rollout is scoped separately and is never a condition of the Sprint; there's no retainer to keep the first one running.
  • Prefer a full scoping session before committing? The Discovery Day is two hours with you and the person who does the workflow today. I write the workflow out step by step and mark each step as rule-following or judgement, draft the one-sentence Brief and a first rubric, note what the workflow replaces and what that costs you now, and give a go or no-go. You keep the one-page memo. The $500 is credited to the Sprint.

Who I am

I ran Nivo as CEO and CPO to 200,000 users and an acquisition, and I ship AI workflows every week in Vectig, my own product. The investor update assistant on the Sprint page is built into Vectig; in my own test runs it took about 8 minutes from open to send instead of 90. I wrote a book on this work, Forward Deployed AI Engineering. There are no Sprint case studies yet, which is why the price is $5,000 through November and why the guarantee is full.

How to start

Score your workflow (two minutes), then book a 15-minute Scoping Call. On the call we pick the workflow, check your data can carry it in ten days, and put a rough number on what it's worth to you. If it fits, you get the Brief draft and the deposit invoice, and your date is locked once it's paid. If it doesn't fit, I'll say so and tell you what to do instead.

P.S. One slot on Tuesday November 17, one on Tuesday December 1. The paid deposit locks the date. The Brief locks the scope.
