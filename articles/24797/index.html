FDE vs. AI Consultant: "Making Recommendations" or "Implementing and Delivering Results"

Original title: FDEとAIコンサルタントの違い｜「提言」か「実装して成果を出す」か

FDE Jobs · 2026-06-21 · 3 min read

Summary

This article compares forward deployed engineers (FDEs) with AI/IT consultants using a comparison table. The core difference lies in deliverables and where responsibility ends: consultants produce strategy, recommendations, and design documents, with responsibility typically ending at delivery, while FDEs produce working systems that run in production at customer sites and are accountable for adoption and business results. The roles overlap in upstream skills like structuring ambiguous problems and negotiating with decision-makers, and the boundary is blurring as consulting firms take on AI implementation while FDEs engage in problem definition. The decisive question is whether you personally build and run the system in production. For consultants moving to FDE roles, strengthening implementation skills in Python, APIs, and LLM/RAG is key. The article also outlines which temperament suits each role.

Key points

  • Consultants deliver recommendations and design docs; FDEs deliver working systems.
  • Responsibility ends at delivery for consultants, at business outcomes for FDEs.
  • Both roles overlap in problem structuring and executive communication.
  • The boundary blurs as consultants implement AI and FDEs define problems.
  • Consultants moving to FDE roles should build Python, API, and LLM/RAG skills.

The Fundamental Difference: Are the Deliverables "Recommendations" or a "Working System"?

Both FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) and AI/IT consultants are deeply involved in solving customer problems, but their deliverables and the endpoints of their responsibility are decisively different. A consultant's core deliverables are strategy, recommendations, and design documents, whereas an FDE's deliverable is the system itself—one that actually runs, is used, and produces results at the customer's site.

It is easiest to understand it this way: consultants "show what should be done," while FDEs "build and run it themselves to completion." For the differences between FDEs and software engineers or SIers, see The Difference Between FDEs and Software Engineers, SIers, and SEs.

Overlapping Areas and Why They Are Often Confused

The two overlap in upstream capabilities such as "structuring the customer's ambiguous problems" and "engaging with decision-makers." In fact, people from consulting backgrounds are one source of FDE candidates. The confusion arises because consulting firms have recently begun taking on AI implementation work, while FDEs increasingly handle upstream problem definition, bringing the two fields closer together. The difference ultimately comes down to "whether you are the one who gets hands-on at the end and runs it in production to completion."

Which One Suits You?

If you want to "influence management through analysis and recommendations" or "engage with a wide range of industries and topics," consulting is a good fit. If you want "the satisfaction of seeing what you built used in the field and producing results" or to "go all the way to solving problems with technology," FDE is the better choice. When moving from consulting to FDE, the key is to fill in the implementation skills (Python, APIs, LLM/RAG) that consultants often lack. For how to make the transition, see How to Become an FDE with No Prior Experience.
