Google Forward Deployed Engineer Levels and Pay: What Does Each FDE Level Earn?

Design Gurus · 2026-11-20 · 3 min read

Summary

This article breaks down Google's forward deployed engineer (FDE) ladder and pay. US postings list four numbered levels, FDE I through FDE IV, plus Staff titles, with base pay ranging from roughly $102,000 at FDE I to $300,000 at FDE IV. Bonus targets of 15% (FDE III) and 20% (FDE IV) are listed on top of equity. Leveling depends mainly on years of relevant experience, but the hiring committee sets the final level from interview feedback. The FDE III/IV figures come from current Google Cloud postings; FDE I/II figures come from earlier postings. Third-party trackers map the levels roughly to Google's L3–L6 software engineer levels, though Google has not confirmed this. The article also compares related roles, such as Customer Engineer III, which has a lower base but a 42.86% bonus target tied to sales, and offers advice on contesting a low level assignment and preparing for the interview loop.

Key points

  • FDE I–IV US base pay spans roughly $102,000 to $300,000.
  • Bonus targets are 15% at FDE III and 20% at FDE IV.
  • Third-party trackers map FDE I–IV to Google L3–L6, unconfirmed by Google.
  • The hiring committee, not the recruiter, sets the final level.
  • Customer Engineer III pays a lower base but a 42.86% bonus target.

Google posts forward deployed engineer roles at four numbered levels, FDE I to FDE IV, plus Staff titles. US base pay ranges from about 102,000 at FDE I to 300,000 at FDE IV. On top of base pay, Google's postings list a bonus target of 15 percent at FDE III and 20 percent at FDE IV, plus equity and benefits. Your level depends mostly on your years of relevant experience. The hiring committee's reading of your interviews also affects it. Pay also varies by city, and the figures below are US ranges only.

The FDE III and FDE IV figures come from several current Google Cloud postings in New York, San Francisco, Seattle, Pittsburgh, and Chicago, which list identical ranges. The FDE I and FDE II figures come from earlier postings collected by fde.directory, because no US posting at those levels was open when this page was written.

How the Levels Map to Google Software Engineer Levels

Third-party trackers map FDE I to FDE IV roughly onto Google's software engineer levels L3 to L6. Under that mapping, FDE III sits near L5, which Google treats as a senior engineer who works with little supervision. What is the L5 level at Google? explains the expectations at that level. Google does not publish this mapping for FDE roles, so treat it as an estimate.

What Changes at Each Level

  • FDE I and II. You build parts of a customer system under the direction of a senior FDE. Postings ask for about two years of Python and some experience designing AI systems on a cloud platform.
  • FDE III. You lead the build for one customer. Postings ask for five years of Python and experience taking AI systems from idea to launch.
  • FDE IV. You own the technical outcome for a large customer and guide its engineers. Postings ask for eight years of experience, and some ask for Terraform, enterprise integration, or experience fixing live systems with heavy traffic.
  • Staff. You set direction across several accounts, mentor other FDEs, and work with executives. Staff postings ask for eight years of shipping AI systems and experience with executive stakeholders.

Pay in Related Google Roles

Several nearby roles help you compare offers. A Kaggle and DeepMind FDE contract role lists 174,000 to 232,000 with a 15 percent bonus target. A YouTube GenAI FDE role that asks for four years of experience lists 128,000 to 184,000. A Customer Engineer III in Cloud AI lists a lower base of 152,000 to 221,000 but a much higher 42.86 percent bonus target, because that role is measured on sales. Google forward deployed engineer vs. customer engineer explains the difference.

How Leveling Is Decided

Your recruiter proposes a target level from your experience, but the hiring committee sets the final level from the interview notes. Strong design and behavioral feedback can support a higher level, while weak coding feedback can lower it. If you believe you were leveled too low, ask the recruiter which round limited the level, and bring evidence of the scope you have already owned.
