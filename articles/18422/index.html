Some companies are spending big money to compete for talent, as accelerated AI adoption fuels demand for FDEs

Original title: 部分企业“砸”重金抢人 AI加速落地带火FDE

财联社 · 2026-11-06 · 4 min read

Summary

A Caixin/CLS report on the rising demand for Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) as AI moves into sectors like pharma, law, finance and manufacturing. In August, China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology for the first time encouraged building FDE teams in a national policy document. Full text

Cailian Press, November 6 (Reporter Fu Jing) The FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), a hybrid role designed to solve the problem of putting AI applications into practice, is currently in high demand.

"Recently we have been in contact with a large number of FDE practitioners, and we can observe that an industry ecosystem is gradually taking shape. But what exactly constitutes a true FDE — the industry has not yet formed a unified standard." said Zhu Bing, head of Wanlian Yida Enterprise Services Group, in an interview with Cailian Press reporters during the 2026 World Internet Conference Wuzhen Summit.

AI is currently moving from consumer-facing applications into more "serious" industries such as pharmaceuticals, law, finance, and industrial production. The surge in demand for FDEs is closely tied to AI's accelerated adoption across thousands of industries. However, the latest interviews by Cailian Press reporters reveal that while enterprises are increasingly receptive to AI, industrial AI still faces practical challenges, and FDEs may not be the only solution.

The FDE Ecosystem Is Taking Shape Rapidly

It is understood that an FDE is not a traditional single-domain engineer or laboratory researcher, but someone dedicated to deeply integrating models with enterprises' specific scenarios and providing customized deployment solutions.

In August this year, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology issued the "Notice on Launching a Special Action to Cultivate AI Application Service Providers," which proposed "encouraging service providers to build FDE teams and work on-site with users to ensure AI scenarios are implemented." This marked the first time FDE was written into a national-level policy document.

Zhu Bing told Cailian Press reporters that FDEs are an indispensable part of the evolution of AI implementation. "Implementing AI requires not only model and product capabilities, but also people who understand enterprise business scenarios and industry know-how to bridge the 'last mile' of AI implementation — work that often falls to FDEs."

During the summit, Yang Aili, co-head of the TMT and Overseas Research Group and Chief Analyst for Global AI Innovation and the Internet at China Securities (CSC), also explained that the overseas approach to making model capabilities truly take root in enterprises is precisely the FDE model — for example, OpenAI established a deployment company, and Anthropic announced in October an investment of 100 million dollars to train Forward Deployed Engineers.

Looking at the moves of leading domestic vendors, Tencent and its ecosystem partners recently released an "AI Ecosystem Co-Creation Plan" covering four ecosystem categories: models, software, hardware, and FDE services. It will launch an FDE elite training program with the goal of cultivating 10,000 FDE industry implementation engineers within the next year. Meanwhile, some vendors are spending heavily to compete for talent: according to media reports, ByteDance offers up to 1.05 million yuan in annual packages for Doubao FDEs, and Moonshot AI offers up to 1.09 million yuan for Kimi enterprise edition FDEs.

However, market standards for FDEs have not yet been established. Zhu Bing explained that Wanlian Yida is working with Zhongguancun Zhiku to advance the development of group standards for FDEs and OPC, training and certification systems, and an FDE service fulfillment guarantee mechanism. "Standards and fulfillment guarantees are prerequisites for the healthy development of the market. We hope to leverage our platform capabilities to connect enterprise demand with the FDE supply side."

Nevertheless, Yang Aili believes that implementing AI in industry still faces issues such as security and trustworthiness, and FDEs may not be the only solution.

How Is Industrial AI Progressing?

"Enterprises today are more pragmatic, increasingly receptive to AI, and beginning to build up AI talent reserves. From the supply side, six months ago we saw many scenarios of AI hardware being implemented in industry but relatively little software. At this World Internet Conference Wuzhen Summit, it is clearly noticeable that genuinely operational agents and AI software systems are on the rise." Zhu Bing shared this observation with Cailian Press reporters.

As observed by Cailian Press reporters, many enterprises have begun exploring AI transformation, but business processes and digital foundations vary greatly across industries and enterprise sizes. How AI capabilities can truly be integrated into business operations remains a practical challenge.

Zhu Bing said that after engaging with a large number of clients, he found that a platform approach alone can hardly help enterprises implement AI applications — many enterprises do not know where to start or how their organizations should adapt to AI. In his view, AI implementation is not just about giving an enterprise an application or tool; it also requires extensive services, as well as consulting and methodological support.

It is understood that in July this year, Wanlian Yida launched its AI enterprise service platform "Wan Da Sheng." Built on its self-developed Agent OS agent operating system, an all-domain computing foundation, and a nationwide network of localized FDE regional services, the platform covers more than 160 service categories including business registration, finance and taxation, intellectual property, legal, and human resources. Over the past few months, Wanlian Yida's enterprise services business has, on one hand, solidified its infrastructure through Agent OS (including an agent development and management system, a one-stop workbench responsible for multi-agent collaboration and primary agent scheduling, and internal and external interfaces that integrate external capabilities into enterprise internal scenarios), and on the other hand, rapidly enhanced AI capabilities oriented toward business tracks, evolving its positioning from building a platform connecting demand and supply sides to becoming a provider of AI services. It is understood that Wan Da Sheng also released an "AI-native enterprise services ecosystem" at this summit.

Recent interviews by Cailian Press reporters revealed that as industrial agents are implemented at scale, the daily Token consumption of some domestic enterprises has reached the tens-of-billions level. However, enterprises' Token consumption has shifted from encouragement strategies to an emphasis on refined operations.

On this, Zhu Bing believes that Token consumption volume alone cannot accurately reflect the degree of an enterprise's AI adoption, but conversely, if Token consumption is very low, it usually indicates that the enterprise has not yet truly "put AI to use."

So what metric should be used to measure the value of AI implementation? Zhu Bing proposed to Cailian Press reporters an evaluation concept called the "Token value exchange ratio," namely the value created per unit of Token. This approach does not merely look at Token consumption volume, but combines Token consumption with business output, efficiency, and cost to measure the value output corresponding to each unit of Token investment.

Taking the video creation scenario as an example, three metrics need to be examined simultaneously: the number of attempts required to generate a high-quality video, production turnaround time, and the price difference between the overall cost and comparable market production prices. These metrics are then combined with Token consumption and converted into a "Token value exchange ratio" as a reference for evaluating product competitiveness. Zhu Bing noted that this metric is an evaluation concept he proposed, not an industry-wide standard.
