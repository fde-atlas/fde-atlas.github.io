How Forward Deployed Engineering Accelerates Enterprise AI Adoption

Forbes · 2026-02-10 · 4 min read

Summary

Faruk Muratovic, a Deloitte engineering leader, argues that many enterprises fail to convert AI strategy into measurable impact because business and technical teams operate in silos, pilots stall against compliance and legacy-system constraints, and over-reliance on vendors prevents internal capability building. He advocates forward deployed engineering (FDE): small, cross-disciplinary teams embedded in business units that start from concrete business goals, iterate rapidly on enterprise-grade platforms, and are measured by business outcomes rather than technical milestones. The article outlines five pillars of effective FDE and contends that, with agentic AI maturing, organizations that act now will gain a competitive edge over those waiting.

Key points

  • Stalled AI pilots stem from silos, compliance friction, and vendor dependence.
  • FDE embeds small cross-disciplinary teams directly inside business units.
  • Success is measured by business outcomes and KPIs, not technical milestones.
  • Five pillars: outcome leadership, embedded collaboration, agile teams, industry solutions, platforms.
  • With agentic AI maturing, acting now beats waiting for perfect conditions.

Faruk Muratovic, Deloitte Engineering leader and principal, Deloitte Consulting LLP.

Artificial intelligence is reshaping business, yet many organizations still struggle to turn their AI vision into a measurable impact. The challenge is both technical and operational. Leaders need to scale AI, but strategy doesn’t always translate into execution.

Forward deployed engineering (FDE) can help meet these challenges. By embedding agile, AI-enabled engineering teams directly into business units, organizations can build the foundation for a delivery model that blends business context, platform capability and quick iteration—thus enabling them to rapidly move from urgent need to business impact with a small, techno-functional team.

A C-Suite Dilemma: The Reality Behind AI Adoption

Even the most effective organizations face difficulty deriving value from their AI strategy and implementation. Many initiatives stumble because they don’t meet the business case or have appropriate domain experience. Further, silos between business, functional and technical teams hamper the integrated effort needed to achieve widespread adoption. Exacerbating the issue, compliance constraints, cybersecurity demands and legacy systems cause friction that can hobble scaling pilots into enterprise-ready systems.

Reliance on vendors to do all the heavy lifting in AI implementation may also be an issue. Vendors are a critical part of AI implementation, but organizations also need to build internal capabilities to achieve long-term success. Finally, it’s often difficult for tech leaders to articulate value in business terms, which makes it harder to secure C-suite buy-in or funding.

Forward Deployed Engineering: Where Technology Meets Business Needs

Forward deployed engineering embeds small, outcome-driven teams inside the business to align modern engineering, platform maturity and business goals. FDE teams work directly with business users to define needs and develop and iterate solutions on a platform that provides acceleration, rapidly tests the solutions and measures results. The FDE model begins with a clear business goal rooted in a real problem, and success is measured through tangible business outcomes.

Because FDE teams are embedded into business functions, they can reduce tech-strategy misalignment and build trust between the technology itself and the business. Teams are also cross-disciplinary—product managers, domain experts with technical skills, data and AI engineers and AI specialists—which helps them become more agile and adaptable. The FDE model runs on a platform approach, using enterprise-grade tools so that solutions are compliant, scale easily and are ready for real-world deployment.

What Good FDE Looks Like

In leading organizations, FDE facilitates close collaboration between engineers and business process owners from the outset. This relationship reduces uncertainty and ensures that solutions meet business needs. Governance, compliance and change management are incorporated early, which results in more sustainable solutions and reduces implementation risks.

Effectiveness can be tied directly to KPIs and business outcomes based on concrete solutions to problems, and teams are accountable for measurable results rather than technical milestones. Knowledge transfer is consistent and purposeful, helping organizations build internal capabilities. Further, architecture leaders stay engaged through the entire process to make sure that solutions can scale effectively to the enterprise.

Continuous learning loops are another characteristic of strong FDE teams. They provide real-world feedback on product, model and process improvements so that solutions improve with use. Supporting all of this are five core pillars:

1. Business-outcome leadership that drives results through aligned outcomes

2. Embedded collaboration that unites engineers and domain experts

3. Agile teams with deep technical and functional expertise that can rapidly execute and accelerate time to value

4. Industry-specific solutions tied to unique sector needs

5. Platform-powered solutions that leverage scalable, robust tech foundations

Taken together, these elements create FDE teams that can deliver lasting, scalable impact.

Why Act Now

For most enterprises, the window of competitive advantage is narrow. Agentic AI capabilities are quickly growing, platforms have reached maturity levels that can support scale, and leading organizations are already seeing value from AI adoption. Organizations that wait for the perfect time could find themselves at a disadvantage compared to those that are acting now.

Forward deployed engineering provides a build-ready model for turning experimentation into reality. As AI systems become more autonomous and integrated into core operations, the FDE model may become a crucial tool for helping organizations translate their vision into sustained enterprise value.

The Path From Sandbox To Enterprise Scale

Forward deployed engineering is more than a model—it’s a mindset. Business context and technical capabilities align, bringing strategy and engineering together to capture measurable value from AI. Simply put, FDE represents a direct path from strategy to impact.

By embedding cross-functional, platform-enabled teams into business units, FDE can end the cycle of stalled pilots, supercharge innovation and build lasting AI capabilities. The future of enterprise AI belongs to organizations that can translate strategy into success, and FDE is how they can do it.
