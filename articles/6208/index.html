The Palantir Mafia

getPIN.xyz · 2024-10-10 · 21 min read

Summary

This article profiles the Palantir Mafia, the network of Palantir alumni founders. It recaps Palantir's history—founded in 2003 by Peter Thiel, Alex Karp and others, backed by In-Q-Tel and Founders Fund, and listed in September 2020 at a $20.6 billion valuation—then catalogs the top 100 of 111 alumni-founded startups that have raised $11.6 billion collectively, most in finance, health, and SaaS. Affirm raised the most and is public; Partiful is highlighted as a fast-growing startup. Many founders previously worked as FDEs or Deployment Strategists.

Key points

  • Palantir was founded in 2003 and went public in 2020 at a $20.6B valuation.
  • Alumni have founded 111 companies, raising $11.6B in total.
  • Finance, health, and SaaS are the most common startup sectors.
  • Affirm raised the most capital and reached a $5.39B market cap.
  • Many founders were FDEs or Deployment Strategists, e.g. Vontive and August Health.

The Palantir Mafia is one of the most well known tech groups, and is often called the "Palantir Pack". Before we dive into this group of former employees, let's take a quick look at Palantir.
‍

Officially established in May 2003, Palantir was founded by Peter Thiel, Alex Karp, Joe Lonsdale, Stephen Cohen, and Nathan Gettings.
‍

Quick fun fact: The company's name comes from The Lord of the Rings where the magical palantíri were "seeing-stones."
‍

Early financial support came in the form of $2 million from the US Central Intelligence Agency's venture arm, In-Q-Tel, and an additional $30 million from Founders Fund. Palantir went public in September 2020 at a valuation of $20.6 billion.
‍

The biggest winners during Palantir's IPO were:

  • Founders Fund - stake worth $1.4 billion
  • SOMPO Holdings - stake worth $1 billion
  • Disruptive Technology Solutions - stake worth $550 million
  • UBS AG - stake worth $300 million
  • 8VC - stake worth $363 million
    ‍

Palantir's technological advancements evolved over a span of three years, involving computer scientists and analysts from intelligence agencies. These developments were facilitated through pilot programs supported by In-Q-Tel. The foundational concept for their software stemmed from technology initially created at PayPal to identify and combat fraudulent activities, particularly those carried out by Russian organized crime syndicates.

The Palantir Mafia

Below we highlight the top 100 companies from the Palantir Mafia, but a total of 111 companies have been founded and the alumni group has collectively raised $11.6 billion as per Crunchbase data. Finance, Health, and SaaS are the industries that the most startups have been founded in by former employees.

Most money raised: Affirm

Affirm is a buy now, pay later service that allows merchants to provide flexible payment plans for their customers.
‍

Affirm was founded in 2012 by Nathan Gettings (co-founder of Palantir), Max Levchin (co-founder of PayPal), Jeff Kaditz, and Alex Rampell.
‍

As of 2023, Affirm has a market cap of $5.39 billion and over 2,071 employees. In April–June 2023, Affirm processed 2.04% of the total US e-commerce payment value, which was $5.5 billion.

‍Cool startup to keep an eye on: Partiful

We've been keeping an eye on Partiful for a while now, and their growth has been really impressive. We're also huge fans of their colorful branding and design! Partiful helps people create a party page for all types of events, whether it's a house party, dinner, or a rager. The company was founded in 2019 and is headquartered in New York City.
‍

Joy Tao, a former product engineer at Palantir, is a co-founder and CTO of Partiful. Shreya Murthy is the other co-founder and CEO of Partiful. Other members of the Partiful team have backgrounds at Facebook, Snapchat, Lyft, Princeton, Brown, and MIT.
‍

In November 2022, Partiful raised $20 million in a Series A funding round. The round was led by Andreessen Horowitz and Initialized Capital. The deal valued the startup at over $100 million.
