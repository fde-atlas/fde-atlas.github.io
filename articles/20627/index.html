What is an FDE Engineer? The Palantir-Originated Definition and How Japanese-Style FDE Differs

An FDE engineer (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who works on-site with customers, handling everything across the board from identifying issues to technical design, implementation, production deployment, and adoption. It is a role that US-based Palantir Technologies says it originated, and AI companies such as OpenAI now hire for it as well. However, in Japan, the term has begun to be used in a broader sense than its original meaning.

In its original sense, an FDE deploys the company's own product or platform into customer operations and feeds insights gained in the field back into the product. In Japan, on the other hand, there is a growing number of cases where the term FDE is used for professionals who work alongside customers to drive AI adoption through to implementation, regardless of whether their company has its own product.

This article organizes the original definition of FDE, how it differs from the "Japan-style FDE" that is spreading in Japan, how FDE differs from AI consulting, SIers, and SES, and the conditions companies need to meet when entering the FDE business.

What Is an FDE Engineer (Forward Deployed Engineer)?

FDE literally means "an engineer deployed to the front line." While a typical development team builds common products in-house, an FDE works close to the customer's actual business operations.

The role is not simply receiving requirements from the customer and implementing them. FDEs observe business operations, identify what is hindering results, examine available data and existing systems, build working mechanisms, and take responsibility all the way until those mechanisms are actually used in the field.

OpenAI describes the FDE role as working at the intersection of customer delivery and core platform development, responsible for discovery, technical scoping, system design, building, and production deployment. The role also includes reflecting evaluation results from the field into product and model roadmaps.

Source: OpenAI "Forward Deployed Engineer - Tokyo" (accessed October 6, 2026)

A Role That Started at Palantir

The company that first established the FDE role is US-based Palantir Technologies, which provides a data analytics platform. In its job postings, the company describes the Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE) as a role it pioneered, placing engineers directly with customers to tackle their most pressing challenges.

The same posting describes FDSEs as working alongside customers to understand their problems and designing and building solutions using Palantir's platform. Building mechanisms that work in the customer's field and using the company's own platform are the original premises of the FDE role.

Source: Palantir Technologies "Forward Deployed Software Engineer" (accessed October 6, 2026)

The Original FDE Model Requires an In-House Product

Not everyone who writes code at a customer's site is an FDE. At the core of the original FDE model is a shared asset that can be deployed to multiple customers, such as an in-house product, model, SaaS, or data platform.

The basic cycle is as follows:

  • Apply the in-house product to the customer's operations, data, and systems
  • Identify site-specific constraints and problems that the product alone cannot solve
  • The FDE designs and implements solutions together with the customer, driving them through to production use
  • Feed the knowledge gained from each project back into common features, components, evaluation methods, and the roadmap
  • Enable faster, higher-quality deployments for the next customer

This final step of "feeding back into the product" is crucial. If the work is merely completing individual projects, it largely overlaps with excellent contract development or SI work. In the FDE model, customer-specific work is transformed into the company's shared capabilities, strengthening both the product and delivery.

The Meaning of "FDE" Is Expanding in Japan

In Japan, the term FDE is also used in a broader sense to mean "professionals who work on-site with customers, driving AI adoption from issue analysis through implementation and adoption." This should not simply be dismissed as incorrect; it needs to be understood as a separate usage that is emerging in Japan's AI adoption market.

For example, FDX describes FDEs as a role that "handles everything end to end, from strategy planning through development and transition to operations." In Fujitsu's recruitment information, FDEs are positioned as a role capable of planning, designing, and developing IT solutions, as well as making proposals that maximize customer value.

Source: FDX "Forward Deployed Engineer", Fujitsu "Forward Deployed Engineer" (accessed October 6, 2026)

Because each company differs in the scope of duties and whether it has its own products, things cannot be simply divided into "product-driven overseas, consulting-driven in Japan." Nevertheless, in the Japanese market, FDE tends to be used as a new role name that spans traditional consulting, SI, contract development, and customer success.

In this article, when it is necessary to distinguish this broader usage from the original FDE, we refer to it as "Japan-style FDE" or "FDE-type support."

Your own product is not necessarily required for the Japanese-style FDE. However, if nothing can be reused even after accumulating projects, the business rationale for adopting the FDE name becomes weak.

Differences between FDE and AI consulting, SIers, and SES

In practice, these boundaries overlap. Compare them not just by name, but by who is responsible for what and what remains after a project ends.

RoleMain responsibilitiesWhat remains after the project
AI consultingStrategy, prioritization, roadmaps, requirements definitionPolicies, research, plans, requirements
SIer / contract developmentDesign, development, and delivery based on agreed requirementsSystems, specifications, operations documentation
SESHandles the technical work requested within the client's organizationDepends on the contract and role
FDESpans the entire process from problem discovery to implementation, adoption, and verifying resultsWorking systems plus products and shared assets reusable for the next client

An FDE is not someone who merely produces documents or merely assigns people to write code. The role connects client outcomes with technical implementation and feeds the lessons learned back to the company.

The work an FDE handles

1. Discovery and business understanding

Rather than turning management issues directly into development requirements, the FDE examines frontline workflows, data in use, decision criteria, exceptions, permissions, and existing systems. The problem to solve is sometimes found in a place other than the features the client initially requested.

2. Setting the technical scope

The FDE decides what to delegate to AI, what humans should verify, and where to connect to existing systems. It is also important to separate what can be tested quickly from what is needed for production operation.

3. Implementing directly

Rather than handing a proposal to the development team and stopping there, the FDE writes code as needed and moves prototypes and production systems forward. Because client dialogue and implementation are less likely to be separated, misunderstandings can be corrected early.

4. Evaluating and moving to production

For AI systems, it is not enough that they run; the FDE checks response quality, exception handling, permissions, logs, costs, and the scope of human approval. Usage rates and business KPIs are also measured, so the work does not stop at building a PoC. The checkpoints before moving from PoC to production are organized in How to move an AI PoC into production?.

5. Feeding lessons back into shared assets

Rather than continually accumulating client-specific code, the FDE organizes reusable connectors, evaluation sets, industry templates, runbooks, and product features. This is the key step that distinguishes the FDE model from generic individual support.

Why FDE is attracting attention in Japan now

While generative AI is easy to start using, many decisions are required before it can be used reliably in business operations. Simply contracting a general-purpose model does not solve connecting to internal data, business rules, evaluation, security, exception handling, or frontline training.

When "strategy makers," "requirements gatherers," "developers," and "operators" are fully separated as in the past, assumptions are lost with every handoff in fast-moving AI projects. The FDE model, which brings client understanding and implementation closer together, is attracting attention as a way to reduce this fragmentation.

However, simply assigning the FDE title does not solve the problem. The business must be designed as a whole, covering authority, contracts, evaluation, security, profitability, and contribution back to shared assets.

Five conditions companies need to enter the FDE business

1. Target customers and problems are well defined

With "supporting AI adoption for all companies," neither sales nor talent development can be standardized. Decide the scope where you can win first: industry, department, business process, technologies used, and so on.

2. A standard process exists from discovery to operations handover

For interviews, use case evaluation, prototyping, production rollout, evaluation, and operations handover, align the deliverables and decision criteria for each stage.

3. People who can span client engagement and implementation

One person does not need to do everything. You need a structure where business staff and engineers share the same hypotheses, evaluation metrics, and accountability to the client.

4. A mechanism for reusing project knowledge

Manage common connectors, prompts, evaluation data, security checks, proposals, SOWs, and operations documentation so they can be used in the next project.

5. Measuring profitability and productization, not just revenue

In addition to revenue, gross margin, and utilization rates, check the proportion of reused components, the production migration rate, usage rates after operations handover, and improvements contributed back to the shared platform.

Can you start an FDE business without your own platform?

It is possible to start a business as a Japanese-style FDE. However, you should not consider the absence of your own platform as the final form.

You do not need to develop large-scale SaaS from the start. First, prepare components for authentication, logging, evaluation, and data connectivity used across multiple projects, industry-specific business templates, and standard project processes. Once these accumulate, you can evolve from one-off human support to repeatable services, and further into products.

In other words, what matters is not just "whether you already have a platform," but whether you have the will and mechanisms to grow shared assets from client projects.

Conclusion: FDE is not a job title but a business structure connecting clients and products

The original FDE deploys its own product at client sites and feeds the lessons learned back into the product. In Japan, the meaning has expanded, and the term is also used for roles and support models that handle AI adoption from problem analysis through implementation and adoption.

Even when making the Japanese-style FDE a business, deeply engaging with clients alone is not enough. You need a cycle that returns project knowledge into shared components, standard processes, talent development, and ultimately products.
