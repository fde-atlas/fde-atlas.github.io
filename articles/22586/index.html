FDE Forward Deployed Engineer

Original title: FDE 前置部署工程师 — 鹿宁柒的交付模式 · 鹿宁柒

Luningqi Technology (Chengdu) Co., Ltd. · 2024 · 6 min read

Summary

This page from Luningqi Technology (Chengdu) describes its Forward Deployed Engineer delivery model. Engineers embed on-site to observe real workflows before coding, focus on one or two high-value workflows, and treat production integration, real-user pilots, and agreed business KPIs as acceptance criteria rather than mere feature completion. It outlines a six-week delivery cadence, a two-role split between a strategist and the FDE, reusable assets (source code, SOPs, eval sets, playbooks), and a conservative ROI estimate with typical projects of ¥30k–300k paying back in 3–6 months, plus a 30-day KPI support period and comparisons with traditional outsourcing.

Key points

  • FDEs shadow staff on-site before writing any code.
  • Only 1–2 high-value workflows are prioritized per project.
  • Acceptance relies on baselines, client-confirmed eval sets, and business metrics.
  • Deliverables include source code, SOPs, training, and industry playbooks.
  • Typical projects cost ¥30k–300k with 30-day KPI support.

Full text

FDE · Builder

Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)

A senior engineer who delivers production systems on the front line, accountable for architecture, integration, real-world usage, and business outcomes.

  • Embedded on-site, shadowing real workflows and exceptions
  • Owns architecture, data and system integration, and critical code reviews
  • Organizes pilot runs with real users, handling usage feedback and production issues
  • Delivers acceptance evidence, SOPs, and training, and supports KPI tracking as agreed

Production delivery · FDE

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) starts from front-line workflows and business goals, connecting data, systems, and human operations into a runnable workflow. Going live is not the finish line: real users trial the system, results are verified against agreed acceptance criteria, and training handover is completed. AI coding agents are just a means to improve implementation efficiency, not the deliverable.

  • High-value workflows to connect first
  • 1–2 workflows
  • Small-scale production validation
  • Real business
  • KPI support commitment
  • 30 days

Why it works

  • Choose workflows worth changing first. The engineer confirms roles, exceptions, and business value on-site, prioritizing only 1–2 workflows to avoid mistaking a feature list for business goals.
  • Production integration matters more than a demo. Permissions, data quality, existing systems, exception handling, and rollback paths are all in scope; AI accelerates implementation, but engineering judgment remains the FDE's responsibility.
  • Accept based on evidence confirmed by the customer. Baselines, acceptance sets, pilot records, business metrics, and usage feedback together form the acceptance basis; results can be re-verified, and issues feed into the next round of fixes.

Two roles

The Deployment Strategist defines business value and acceptance criteria; the FDE handles system integration and production delivery. Together, they are accountable for the outcome.

Strategist · Framer

Turns vague requirements into high-value workflows, measurable baselines, and acceptance standards both parties agree on.

  • Interviews leadership and front-line staff, selecting 1–2 priority workflows
  • Defines business metrics, baselines, acceptance sets, and confirmers, written into the SOW
  • Breaks production validation into milestones, each with verifiable artifacts
  • Jointly reviews business data and usage feedback to finalize acceptance conclusions

Delivery path

Focus on 1–2 high-value workflows first, then integrate data and systems, run a small-scale production pilot, then accept, train, and hand over.

  • 01Day 1-5Business diagnosis. Interviews, order shadowing, system review, and spreadsheet digging to reconstruct actual operations and exceptions. Produces a current-state process map, business baseline, and issue list.
  • 02Week 1Lock in 1–2 high-value workflows. Ranked by business value, data availability, and production risk; confirm metric definitions, acceptance sets, and milestones with the customer.
  • 03Week 1-4Integrate data and business systems. Complete permissions, data, and API integration in the customer's environment; the FDE owns architecture, reviews, and production integration; AI only assists implementation efficiency.
  • 04PilotSmall-scale production validation. Run a pilot with a group of real users or a batch of real business, covering exceptions and rollback paths, continuously recording results and usage feedback.
  • 05Acceptance & handoverAcceptance, training, and handover. Jointly review baselines and acceptance evidence, handle shortfalls as agreed; deliver source code interfaces, monitoring, SOPs, and training materials, and complete the retrospective and support arrangements.

From onboarding to KPI acceptance, the week-by-week rhythm of a typical mid-size project — synthesized from multiple real deliveries, details anonymized.

  • Field notes · Week 1: Onboarding and shadowing. The FDE joins the customer's group/workshop and shadows a full shift; only asking, not building — mapping the real process and aligning KPI definitions with the boss.
  • Field notes · Week 2: Data reconnaissance. Digging through ledgers, databases, and sampled real documents; identifying steps that "can be verified by numbers," producing a prioritized improvement list.
  • Field notes · Week 3: First version underway. The FDE sets the architecture and acceptance criteria, using AI assistance for implementation, review, and integration; weekly demos of a runnable version to the customer with feedback collection.
  • Field notes · Week 4: Small-scale pilot. Trial run on one real production line / one group of real users; fix errors on the spot, and solidify what works into SOPs.
  • Field notes · Week 5: Full rollout. Full switchover + 30 minutes of veteran staff training; monitoring dashboards delivered, with alerts for anomalies.
  • Field notes · Week 6: KPI acceptance. Reconciling numbers against contract definitions — meeting targets enters a 30-day support period; missing targets means free optimization until they're met.

Production adoption & acceptance

Feature completion is not acceptance completion. Whether it's usable, actually adopted, and whether business results meet targets — all confirmed jointly by the customer and delivery team based on evidence.

  • Establish a baseline Before starting, confirm the current process, data window, business metric definitions, and owners, leaving a verifiable starting point.
  • Co-build the customer acceptance set Have the customer confirm representative real cases, edge-case exceptions, expected results, and pass criteria, rather than only checking the demo path.
  • Small-scale trial run Let a group of real users or a batch of real business enter the production environment, keep manual rollback, and record issues.
  • Review business metrics Compare actual data such as processing time, error rework, or backlog against the agreed definitions, without assuming a uniform improvement rate.
  • Collect usage feedback Record who uses it, at which step, and how; distinguish unwillingness to use, inability to use, poor usability, and incorrect results.
  • Joint review Both parties verify the acceptance evidence and reach conclusions on pass, fix, or rollback; unresolved issues move into the next round of validation.

Feedback and review return to the baseline and acceptance set until the agreed acceptance and handover are complete.

Reusable delivery assets

The system is handed to the customer, and so is the methodology. Assets are delivered as agreed per project; public content contains no customer data or proprietary information.

  • Source code and interfaces Production source code, deployment configuration, data dictionary, and API documentation, explaining dependencies and how to take over.
  • Acceptance set and monitoring Customer-confirmed acceptance samples, check rules, metric definitions, and anomaly monitoring, supporting continuous re-validation.
  • SOPs and training Role-based operating SOPs, training materials or screen recordings, and handover checklists so actual users can operate independently.
  • Industry playbooks Distilled process judgments, system integration key points, and acceptance methods for reuse in future projects instead of starting from scratch.

FDE vs outsourcing

  • Requirements understanding

    • FDE on-site
    • On-site shadowing, seeing the process firsthand before defining requirements
    • Traditional outsourcing
    • Relying on questionnaires and meeting notes, imagining things remotely

  • Response chain

    • FDE on-site
    • The engineer is right in your group chat, answering on the spot
    • Traditional outsourcing
    • Sales → project manager → developer, three rounds of message relay

  • Code ownership

    • FDE on-site
    • Source code, documentation, and data all belong to the customer
    • Traditional outsourcing
    • Often tied to the vendor's platform or framework

  • Acceptance criteria

    • FDE on-site
    • KPIs written into the contract; free optimization until targets are met
    • Traditional outsourcing
    • Acceptance based on "features completed," with results left unaddressed

  • Billing transparency

    • FDE on-site
    • 50/50 in phases, each payment tied to a milestone
    • Traditional outsourcing
    • Large upfront payment, changes billed separately
