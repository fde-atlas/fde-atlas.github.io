Summary

This knowledge-graph style article defines what makes a role a forward deployed engineer (FDE): whether customers get production results and whether the team leaves reusable capability. It cites US base salary samples from OpenAI, Anthropic and Palantir at USD 110K-345K per year, noting figures must be re-verified. It lays out a six-stage delivery chain from scenario discovery to asset reuse, each with exit evidence, and a four-layer value framework covering business outcomes, organizational adoption, engineering quality, and reuse efficiency. Metrics require explicit definitions. The initial model draws on a July 2026 Tencent Research Institute report.

Key points

  • US base salary samples: USD 110K-345K per year
  • FDE test: production results plus reusable capability
  • Six-stage delivery chain, each with exit evidence
  • Four-layer value: business, adoption, engineering, reuse
  • Every metric needs defined numerator, denominator, baseline

02 / Core Judgment

To judge whether a job is an FDE role, look at only two things.

Whether the customer gets production results, and whether the team leaves behind reusable capability.

Customer customization

The project can be delivered, but the team still relies on starting from scratch next time.

Project delivery

Experience stays with individuals and retrospectives; other teams can hardly use it directly.

FDE closed loop

On-site evidence flows into Skills, connectors, eval sets, or product capabilities.

Scalable FDE

Assets shorten the cycle for the next customer and reduce duplicated work.

03 / Delivery Pipeline

A demo is only the starting point of risk exposure

Production value comes from a complete pipeline. Every stage has exit evidence; you can't advance just by counting documents.

  • Scenario discovery: confirm users, tasks, baselines, system boundaries, and business owners. Output: scenario map
  • Runnable validation: run the critical path with real problems and actively hunt for failure points. Output: runnable prototype
  • Controlled pilot: limit users and risk scope, establish evals and rollback conditions. Output: eval set
  • Production launch: complete permissions, auditing, monitoring, human takeover, and operational responsibility. Output: production plan
  • Organizational adoption: write the new capability into roles, processes, training, and operating rhythms. Output: adoption plan
  • Consolidation & reuse: distinguish customer differences from industry commonalities and turn them into maintainable assets. Output: asset package

05 / Value Assessment

Value requires evidence at four levels simultaneously

Call volume and model scores only explain part of the picture. An FDE must also prove user adoption, production reliability, and faster delivery next time.

Business results

Are tasks completed better

  • Task success rate
  • End-to-end process cycle time
  • Changes in quality, revenue, or risk

Organizational adoption

Do target users keep using it

  • Adoption rate and active frequency
  • Business volume covered by the new process

Engineering quality

Can the system withstand production

  • Failure rate and recovery time
  • Human takeover and regression evals

Reuse efficiency

Is the next delivery lighter

  • Asset reuse rate
  • Reduction in launch cycle time and work hours

Metric discipline

Every percentage needs a defined methodology

Specify the numerator, denominator, baseline, exclusions, time window, and data owner.
