Summary

This Japanese article maps career paths after becoming an FDE, based on OpenAI, Databricks, and Palantir Tokyo job postings (October 2026), Lightcast data via Fortune, and commentary from The Pragmatic Engineer and First Round Review. It argues FDE careers split into a vertical track (FDE to Deployment Lead to Manager, with real manager openings at OpenAI and Databricks) and lateral exits (product manager, solutions architect, engineering manager, founding startups). Lightcast data shows FDE job postings grew 1,000% year-over-year in Jan–Aug 2026, with median advertised salary above $188,000 versus ~$145,000 for software engineers. The article warns against 'FDE in name only' roles without real coding, and outlines how people in their 30s and 40s are evaluated on customer-facing delivery, ongoing coding ability, and domain expertise rather than age.

Key points

  • FDE careers split into vertical leveling and lateral exits like PM, SA, EM, or founding startups.
  • OpenAI Tokyo posts three levels: FDE (5+ years), Deployment Lead (7+), Manager (8+ years plus 2 years managing).
  • Lightcast data: FDE postings up 1,000% YoY; median advertised pay over $188,000.
  • Palantir alumni analysis found FDE was the most common prior role among founders, including Sourcegraph's Quinn Slack.
  • For career changers in their 30s–40s, evaluation centers on customer delivery, coding ability, and industry knowledge, not age.

Where can you go after becoming an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Is it a career you can continue as you get older? Can you enter the field in your 30s or 40s? Since FDE is a new role, there is almost no information available about "what comes next," and many people find themselves unable to commit to a career change. The bottom line: FDE offers both a vertical path of rising through ranks and a horizontal path into roles such as product management or entrepreneurship, and OpenAI and Databricks actually have manager-level openings in their Tokyo job listings.

This article draws on primary sources such as the official job postings of OpenAI, Databricks, and Palantir (as of October 2026), The Pragmatic Engineer, a16z, First Round Review, and Fortune (Lightcast data) to organize FDE ranks, next career exits, public examples of Palantir alumni careers, the kinds of experience that raise your market value and the working styles that lower it, and the evaluation criteria for entering as an FDE in your 30s or 40s.

Key Takeaways

  • FDE career paths go in two directions: a vertical path of rising through individual ranks (FDE → Senior → Deployment Lead → Manager) and a horizontal path of changing roles (product manager, solution architect, engineering manager, entrepreneurship, business leader).
  • OpenAI's Tokyo job listings (as of October 2026) require 5+ years for FDE, 7+ years for Deployment Lead, and 8+ years plus 2+ years of management experience for Manager, FDE, confirming that three rank levels actually exist within the FDE organization.
  • According to Lightcast data (Fortune, September 2026), FDE job postings grew 1,000% year-over-year from January to August 2026, with a median advertised salary of over $188,000, exceeding the roughly $145,000 for traditional software engineers.
  • In March 2026, The Pragmatic Engineer reported a recruiter's view that "many companies want FDEs, but only about 10% of the market's engineers are what they're looking for," noting that what companies reject is not the true FDE but the "FDE in name only" who does no implementation.
  • For those entering FDE roles in their 30s or 40s, what is evaluated is not age but customer-facing experience, industry knowledge, and a track record of leading teams, and the manager openings at OpenAI and Databricks are precisely seeking that experience.

Conclusion: FDE Career Paths Go in Two Directions: "Vertical Ranks" and "Horizontal Exits"

FDE career paths become clearer when divided into two directions. One is the vertical path of rising through the ranks within the FDE organization. Looking at OpenAI's Tokyo job listings, three positions are being recruited simultaneously: Forward Deployed Engineer, Deployment Lead (DL), and Manager, Forward Deployed Engineer (as of October 2026), with required years of experience rising stepwise at 5+, 7+, and 8+ years respectively. The other is the horizontal path of moving into a different role using the experience gained as an FDE. The practice of feeding insights gained in the field back into the product is contiguous with the work of product managers and solution architects, and First Round Review describes FDEs as "often former founders, or future founders."

FDE Ranks: The Ladder from Junior to Senior to Lead/Manager Actually Exists

Some people worry that "FDE might be a disposable role with no path into management." However, actually reading the Tokyo job postings as of October 2026 confirms that there are multiple ranks within the FDE organization and that people-leading positions are being recruited.

OpenAI Tokyo: Three Levels: FDE, Deployment Lead, and Manager

OpenAI is hiring three FDE positions in Tokyo (as of October 2026, per OpenAI's careers page). Forward Deployed Engineer requires "5+ years of engineering or technical implementation experience, including customer-facing work," and is an individual contributor role responsible for discovery, technical scoping, system design, building, and production deployment. Deployment Lead (DL), FDE requires "7+ years of experience leading customer-facing technical delivery," and is described as a role that owns technical delivery plans across multiple workstreams and drives day-to-day execution across FDEs, researchers, and customer engineers. The role also includes setting value hypotheses, baselines, and KPIs, and reporting pre- and post-deployment impact measurements to executive sponsors. Manager, Forward Deployed Engineer requires "8+ years of engineering or technical delivery experience, including 2+ years managing FDEs or customer-facing engineers," and the role involves leading and growing the FDE team and bringing field insights to Product, Research, Sales, and GTM. All three positions require bilingual Japanese and English proficiency; on-site work at customer locations is described as "often 25-50%, sometimes more" in the DL posting and "up to 40% travel (mostly domestic)" in the Manager posting.

Databricks Tokyo: A manager leading about 10 FDEs

Databricks is also hiring a "Manager, Forward Deployed Engineering" in Tokyo (as of October 2026, per Databricks' careers page). The responsibilities include "leading, hiring, and developing a high-performing team of about 10 FDEs," collaborating with customers and internal stakeholders to shape complex projects, serving as the team's senior technical escalation point, and coaching engineers on design, stakeholder management, and career development. Requirements include experience leading technical teams in customer-facing environments, plus "current technical credibility, able to do design reviews and debug production issues," hands-on proficiency in Python and SQL, and fluency in Japanese with business-level English. In other words, an FDE manager is defined not as someone who has stopped writing code, but as someone who possesses both technical and management skills.

Palantir: An explicit statement that it does "not rely on traditional career ladders"

The Tokyo job posting from Palantir, the origin of the FDE role (Forward Deployed Software Engineer - Japan Government, as of October 2026), is written somewhat differently. It describes the FDSE's job as "similar to a startup CTO, executing high-stakes projects end to end with a small team and minimal supervision," and applicants can apply with "1+ year of post-graduation professional experience." Regarding careers, it explicitly states that it "does not rely on traditional career ladders, but celebrates individual strengths, skills, and interests" and that "there are many paths to success," suggesting a philosophy of expanding one's role based on personal strengths rather than advancing along a grade table. An August 2025 article in The Pragmatic Engineer also pointed out that entry-level experience requirements vary widely by company: Palantir hires with "about 1 year of post-graduation experience," while Ramp's senior roles require "5+ years."

  • Entry-level experience requirements: Palantir FDSE requires 1+ years, OpenAI FDE requires 5+ years (both Tokyo, as of October 2026)
  • Lead roles: OpenAI Deployment Lead requires 7+ years, with ownership of planning, execution, and impact measurement across multiple projects
  • Manager roles: OpenAI Manager requires 8+ years plus 2+ years of management experience; Databricks Manager leads a team of about 10
  • Common thread: Even for manager roles, the requirements still include the technical ability to review code and debug production issues

The next exits from FDE: Product manager, SA, EM, entrepreneurship, business owner

The lateral exits after a few years as an FDE can be inferred by working backward from the responsibilities listed in job postings. An FDE's work is a continuous flow: understanding the customer's business, identifying problems, solving them with your company's product, running it in production, and feeding those insights back into the product. Each part of this flow overlaps with the core work of adjacent roles.

In a February 2026 article, First Round Review quotes Serval co-founder Jake Stauch: "FDEs have strong product sense and a genuine interest in sales. These people are often former founders or future founders." In the same article, James Honsa, who led legal engineering at Ironclad, describes great FDEs as "people who could have been staff engineers at major tech companies," suggesting that FDE experience can also lead to senior technical grades.

That said, whichever exit you pursue, whether you have a track record of "shipping production-quality code yourself" during your FDE years changes the range of options available to you. FDE experience without implementation may carry over to solution architect or customer success roles, but rarely to product development or engineering management. This point also overlaps with the "ways of working that lower your market value" discussed later.

Public examples of entrepreneurship and careers of Palantir alumni

The most useful reference for thinking about the "aftermath" of FDEs is alumni from Palantir, where the role has existed the longest. Here, we will separately introduce cases confirmed through the individuals' own statements and interviews, as well as tallies compiled by VCs.

Quinn Slack (Co-founder and CEO of Sourcegraph): His experience as an FDE stationed at banks became the motivation to start a company

Quinn Slack, co-founder of Sourcegraph, a code search tool for developers, spoke on a podcast in October 2021 (Heavybit's "EnterpriseReady") about his experience working embedded at JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America for about 10 months as a Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer. He described a working style where he "wore business casual and entered the bank building during the day (to understand the problems), and at 5 p.m. changed into sweatpants in a conference room, opened his laptop, and wrote code," and said he built tools to help avoid foreclosures after the housing loan crisis. Through this experience, he directly felt the "pain" of talented people struggling within the codebases of giant corporations, which became the direct motivation for founding Sourcegraph.

Matt Grimm (Co-founder and COO of Anduril): From early on-site deployment at Palantir to a defense tech company

Matt Grimm, co-founder and COO of defense tech company Anduril, is introduced in the profile of the podcast "The Twenty Minute VC" (November 2024) as "an early hire at Palantir who was deployed to Iraq and Afghanistan so that the U.S. military could use the best technology for its missions." Since public information does not explicitly state his job title as "FDE," we mention him here only as "an example of entrepreneurship from a deeply field-embedded working style."

VC tally: FDE is the most common previous role among Palantir alumni founders

European VC Concept Ventures has published an analysis tallying companies founded by Palantir alumni. According to it, Palantir alumni have founded more than 111 companies, more than half of the founders held engineering roles at Palantir, and the most common role among them was Forward Deployed Engineer. Note that this tally was compiled independently by a VC and is not an official Palantir announcement, but it is one piece of evidence supporting numerically the view that "FDE is a role that tends to produce founders."

Paths other than entrepreneurship: hiring lead, advisor

Entrepreneurship is not the only path for former Palantir FDEs. Shilpa Balaji, who appears in a First Round Review article, served as an FDE at Palantir, then became the company's FDE hiring lead, and is now at a company called Promise. An August 2025 article in The Pragmatic Engineer introduced Anjor Kanekar, who worked as an FDE at Palantir for 7 years and has taken on advisory roles. These are examples of people who spent long careers as FDEs moving into hiring and advisory work that "sells FDE expertise."

Experience that raises market value, and working styles that lower it

The market value of FDEs is currently high in numerical terms. According to a tally by Lightcast (labor market data) reported by Fortune in September 2026, job postings containing "forward-deployed" increased 1,000% year-over-year from January to August 2026, and were up 4,600% compared to 2023. Growth for tech jobs overall over the same period was 13% year-over-year. The median advertised salary exceeds $188,000, surpassing the roughly $145,000 for traditional software engineers, and some Anthropic postings reportedly reach $400,000. Compensation levels in Japan are covered in a separate article.

Related article FDE Salaries: Compensation Ranges Seen from Job Postings in Japan and Abroad

However, this is the market rate for the "FDE title," and it does not automatically raise an individual's market value. The First Round Review article lists risks on the company side of adopting FDEs: "without discipline, FDEs build random features disconnected from the core product" and "they spend endless time on solutions that only work for one customer." Seen from the individual's side, this means that a working style that merely accumulates customer-specific customizations does not become an achievement you can explain in the job market.

Experience that raises market value

  • Experience measuring pre- and post-deployment effects in numbers: OpenAI's DL job posting makes it a duty to "set value hypotheses, baselines, and KPIs, and report pre- and post-deployment measurements to executive sponsors." Being able to speak about customer outcomes in numbers carries over to your next role
  • Experience turning field patterns into reusable form: OpenAI's FDE, DL, and Manager job postings all ask for "systematizing working patterns into tools, playbooks, and components." People who leave behind deliverables that don't end with one customer can easily move to both product and management sides
  • Experience continuing to write production-quality code: Even Databricks' manager posting lists "current technical skill capable of design reviews and debugging production incidents" as a requirement. Stray too far from implementation, and you can no longer return to senior technical grades
  • Experience delivering production LLM/generative AI systems: OpenAI's FDE posting includes "experience building and deploying systems powered by LLMs or generative models" as a requirement. One of the rarest experiences as of 2026
  • Experience going deep into a specific industry: OpenAI's DL posting asks for "domain expertise in at least one major sector such as healthcare, energy, finance, semiconductors, or IT." Industry knowledge is an asset that becomes a stronger advantage the longer an FDE's career

Working styles that lower market value

  • Being used continuously as an "engagement lead" who doesn't write code (an example of an engineer's disillusionment with an FDE role introduced by The Pragmatic Engineer)
  • Repeating per-customer custom development at a company without its own product (you can no longer explain how you differ from SES or contracted development)
  • Being able to describe results only in terms of "hours worked" or "number of tickets handled" (not tied to customers' business outcomes)
  • Merely accumulating customizations for one customer, with no knowledge fed back into the product

"Is FDE Losing Popularity?" How to Read The Pragmatic Engineer's Argument

When researching the future prospects of FDEs, you will come across an article published on March 27, 2026 by The Pragmatic Engineer (Gergely Orosz) titled "Is the FDE role becoming less desirable?" Since this is an unavoidable topic when considering your career, let's accurately grasp its content.

The Facts Presented in the Article

Citing a Wall Street Journal report, the article states that FDE job postings on Indeed grew more than tenfold in 2025 compared to 2024, and mentions in earnings calls of listed companies increased from 8 to 50. On the other hand, it introduces a remark by Patrick Kellenberger of recruiting firm Betts Recruiting—"Everyone wants an FDE, but at most 10% of the market actually wants the role"—pointing out a large gap between demand and applicants. Furthermore, the article cites the voice of an engineer who actually took an FDE job: "This FDE job was a typical IT services mindset. The company wanted to use me as an engagement lead and didn't let me get involved in software development at all," noting that there are cases where the job involves "little software engineering and even less platform engineering." Orosz's conclusion is: "When demand is high and pay is decent, yet engineer interest is low, there is always a reason."

How Career-Minded People Should Read It

Reading this article as "FDE has no future" is premature. What the article criticizes are job postings that carry the FDE title while actually being sales engineer or IT services engagement lead roles. In other words, what should be avoided is not the profession itself but "FDE in name only." Conversely, job postings like those from OpenAI and Databricks, which explicitly state as job duties writing production code and feeding field insights back into the product, differ from the disappointment depicted in the article.

It is also necessary to understand the structure on the demand side. In his June 2025 article "Trading Margin for Moat," Joe Schmidt of a16z argued that complex enterprise AI requires hands-on implementation work, and that AI startups should become service-driven providers of customers' business systems even at the cost of early gross margins, pointing out that 22 of OpenAI's 311 public job postings were FDE or implementation/solution specialist roles. He cited ServiceNow, whose gross margin rose from 63.2% at IPO to 79% in 2024, and Workday, from 54.1% to 75%, stating that heavy implementation is a temporary disadvantage that becomes a long-term moat. If this assessment is correct, FDE demand is not a passing phenomenon of the generative AI boom but a structural one that will continue as long as enterprise AI spreads.

In summary, the accurate way to read The Pragmatic Engineer's argument is not "avoid the FDE profession" but "learn to screen job postings." How to identify FDE-in-name-only roles is explained in detail in an article that organizes the differences from SES and consulting.

Evaluation Criteria for Entering FDE in Your 30s and 40s

The question "Isn't FDE a job for young people, making it impossible to enter in your 30s or 40s?" requires answering in two separate eras.

The Palantir Era: New Graduates Centered, "10+ Years at Big Tech" Was Avoided

In a First Round Review article, Shilpa Balaji, who served as an FDE and recruiting lead at Palantir, stated that at Palantir "people fresh out of college made up the majority of FDEs," recalling that "people who had spent 10+ years at FAANG (major tech companies) were a 'no-fly zone.'" The reason was that experience too specialized to a particular environment was considered a poor fit for the attitude of tackling unknown problems without bringing in existing playbooks. The fact that Palantir's Tokyo FDSE job posting still allows applications with "1+ year of practical experience" shows this philosophy continues.

Generative AI Companies in 2026: Job Postings Requiring 5, 7, and 8+ Years of Experience

Meanwhile, as of October 2026, OpenAI's Tokyo job postings require 5+ years of experience for individual-contributor FDEs, 7+ years for Deployment Leads, and 8+ years for Managers. The DL posting lists "expertise in at least one major sector" and "executive presence to clearly communicate technical trade-offs to leadership," while the Manager posting requires "2+ years of management experience." Databricks' Tokyo manager posting also presumes experience leading technical teams in customer-facing environments. These requirements are difficult to meet in your 20s, and experience gained in your 30s and 40s becomes directly evaluated.

Summarizing the evaluation criteria for people moving into FDE roles in their 30s and 40s, it comes down not to age itself but to the following three points. First, do you have experience delivering technical results at customer sites? This includes on-site assignments at SIers, presales, and implementation projects in consulting. Second, are you continuously writing production code yourself? The fact that even manager job postings list technical skills as a requirement means, conversely, that "managers who cannot code" are not in demand. Third, do you have deep knowledge of a specific industry or business domain? Domain knowledge in finance, manufacturing, healthcare, and similar fields is an asset that younger professionals cannot possess.

Checklist for reading job postings from a career path perspective

Whether a career path is open when joining an FDE role and afterward can be judged to some extent from the job posting itself. This section extracts the items you should check from the job postings of the companies reviewed in this article.

  • 01 Does the company have openings or current employees in senior FDE roles (Senior, Lead, Manager)? If senior roles are being recruited, as at OpenAI and Databricks, the organization has designed job levels
  • 02 Does the job description explicitly state "writing production code"? OpenAI's FDE posting says "write and review production-quality code for both frontend and backend"
  • 03 Does the job include "feeding field insights back into the product"? Postings without this may be closer to the "IT services type" criticized by The Pragmatic Engineer
  • 04 Are results measured in terms of customer business outcomes (KPIs, post-deployment impact)? Be cautious with postings that define results by hours worked or number of tickets handled
  • 05 Does the company have its own product, and do its customers span multiple industries? First Round Review lists three prerequisites for FDEs to work: being enterprise-focused, having flexibility in product direction, and having a diverse customer base
  • 06 Is the percentage of time spent working at customer sites specified? OpenAI's DL role states 25-50%, and Palantir states 25-75%. If it is not stated, confirm it during the interview

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q What career paths are available after becoming an FDE?

There are two main directions. One is advancing through the levels within an FDE organization; OpenAI's Tokyo postings actually have three tiers: FDE (5+ years), Deployment Lead (7+ years), and Manager (8+ years). The other is changing roles, with typical destinations including product manager, solution architect, engineering manager, customer success or business unit leader, and founding a startup.

Q Does the FDE role have a future? Is it just a passing fad?

According to Lightcast data, FDE job postings grew 1,000% year-over-year from January to August 2026, and demand is expanding. a16z argues that complex enterprise AI requires hands-on deployment work, and that service-led growth becomes a long-term competitive advantage. On the other hand, The Pragmatic Engineer reports disappointment with FDE postings in name only that involve no implementation, so the future of the role should be judged by the substance of the postings, not the job title itself.

Q Can people in their 40s continue working as FDEs?

Yes. OpenAI's and Databricks' Tokyo postings include Manager and Deployment Lead roles that lead teams, requiring 8+ years of experience, 2+ years of management experience, and specialized knowledge of a specific industry. These are requirements that directly value the experience of older professionals. However, note that even for manager roles, technical skills such as design reviews and production debugging remain requirements.

Q Can people in their 30s or 40s with no prior FDE experience switch into FDE roles?

Since experienced FDEs are almost nonexistent in the market, companies hire people transitioning from adjacent roles. What is valued in candidates in their 30s and 40s comes down to three things: experience delivering technical results at customer sites (on-site SIer assignments, presales, consulting implementation projects, etc.), continuously writing production code yourself, and deep knowledge of a specific industry. Compared to organizations focused on new graduates like Palantir, lead and manager roles at generative AI companies and SaaS companies tend to be easier entry points.

Q Is the market value of FDEs high?

According to Lightcast figures reported by Fortune in September 2026, the median advertised salary for FDEs exceeds $188,000, surpassing the roughly $145,000 for traditional software engineers. However, this is the market rate for the title; an individual's market value is determined by experience measuring customer outcomes in numbers, a track record of continuously writing production code, and whether you have deliverables that fed field insights back into the product.

Q Can an FDE become a product manager?

FDE duties include understanding customers' business operations and feeding field insights back into the product roadmap; OpenAI's Deployment Lead posting also lists "sharing field signals with Product and Research" as a duty. This experience overlaps with the core work of a product manager. What tends to be lacking is prioritization across many customers and designing revenue models, and the key to making the transition is leaving behind generic deliverables that work across multiple customers during your time as an FDE.

Q What happens to FDEs from Palantir afterward?

Sourcegraph co-founder Quinn Slack says his experience stationed at banks as a Palantir FDE motivated him to start his own company. According to Concept Ventures' tally, there are more than 111 companies founded by Palantir alumni, and FDE was the most common previous role among their founders. Beyond entrepreneurship, some FDEs have moved into recruiting lead or advisor roles.
