How did the forward deployed engineer role start?

FDE Club · 2026-11-19 · 6 min read

Summary

This field note traces the origins of the forward deployed engineer role using published sources only. All sources credit Palantir with creating the role, though they disagree on timing: a16z says 2011, The Pragmatic Engineer says the early 2010s, and First Round Review says nearly two decades ago. Palantir called these engineers Deltas, a name a16z reads as title arbitrage, and the role was reportedly inspired by Alex Karp's observation of French restaurants. Until Foundry launched around 2016, Palantir employed more FDEs than software engineers. From 2025, AI companies adopted the role rapidly, with job postings up over 1,000 percent year-on-year, though critics like Ted Mabrey argue many firms replicate the form without the function.

Key points

  • Palantir created the FDE role; sources disagree on whether it started in 2011 or the early 2010s.
  • The role existed because customers could not deploy Palantir's software on their own.
  • Palantir named the role Delta, reportedly inspired by how French restaurant waiters extend the kitchen.
  • Until Foundry launched around 2016, Palantir had more FDEs than regular software engineers.
  • From 2025, AI companies adopted FDEs rapidly; postings grew 1,165 percent year-on-year per Bloomberry.

Field notes

The forward deployed engineer role started at Palantir, which sent engineers to work inside its customers' organisations and called them Deltas. Published sources date the title to the early 2010s, or to 2011. Palantir had more of them than software engineers until about 2016. AI companies took up the role from 2025.

This note traces where the role came from and how it spread, drawn from published sources only. Where the sources disagree, the note says so. For what the role is today, start with the guide: What is a forward deployed engineer?

When and where did the role start?

At Palantir, in the accounts that are public. The sources agree on the company and differ on the year. The number in brackets is the source in the list at the end.

When What happened Source
2003 Palantir is founded [1]
Nearly two decades before February 2026 Palantir first devises the forward deployed engineer, in First Round Review's account [3]
2011 Palantir gives the new title to its solutions and integration engineers, in a16z's account [2]
Early 2010s The "Forward Deployed Software Engineer" role is created at Palantir, named "Delta" [1]

Early 2025 OpenAI's head of solutions architecture makes the case for a forward deployed engineering team [1]

The Pragmatic Engineer writes that the role "was created at data mining giant Palantir in the early 2010s" [1]. a16z gives a year: "In 2011, Palantir did something funny" [2]. First Round Review, writing in February 2026, says the role was "first devised by Palantir nearly two decades ago" [3]. That would place it in the second half of the 2000s. No source in this note gives a document from the time that settles the date.

Bloomberry, which read about 1,000 postings, also credits the company: "Palantir was the one who coined the original title" [5].

Why did Palantir create the role?

Because its customers could not take its software and run it on their own, in the accounts the sources give.

The Pragmatic Engineer notes that Palantir "works a lot with government agencies and companies that I'd consider “traditional”". Its author adds his own view: without a role like this, those businesses would struggle "to integrate Palantir's system into theirs" [1].

First Round Review describes Palantir's engineers working on site with large organisations, "from local government agencies to healthcare giants to supply chain operations" [3]. A former Palantir engineer who later led recruiting for the role there says in the same article that "Palantir's roadmap in the early days was shockingly uncharted". She adds: "Early on at Palantir, we rarely said, 'This is what the product should be.'" In her account, "recent college grads made up a bulk of the FDE roster" [3].

Two sources tie the idea to a restaurant. Ted Mabrey writes that the role "was famously inspired by Karp's observation of how excellent French restaurants operate" [4]. Alex Karp is Palantir's co-founder and chief executive. First Round Review gives the same image in one line: "The waiters are an extension of the kitchen staff." It calls this "the culinary inspiration behind the forward deployed engineer" [3]. In Mabrey's reading, the point is that the team that serves the customer is part of the kitchen. It knows the product well enough to say no to a poor choice and to suggest a better one [4].

Why was the role called Delta?

The sources give the name and one reading of it.

At Palantir the forward deployed software engineer is a "Delta". A software engineer on the core product is a "Dev". The Pragmatic Engineer quotes Palantir's own framing of the split: a Dev's focus is "one capability, many customers", and a Delta's focus is "one customer, many capabilities" [1].

a16z adds a second reading. It writes that Palantir took its solutions and integration engineers, then seen as lower status work, and gave them the new title. "They even called them “Deltas,” like Delta Force" [2]. Its author calls this "title arbitrage": a new name changed how the same work was seen inside and outside the company [2].

How did the role change at Palantir itself?

It shrank as the product grew, and it stayed large.

The Pragmatic Engineer reports: "Up until circa 2016, Palantir had more FDEs than it had “normal” software engineers." [1] That year Palantir launched Foundry, an integrated data platform for commercial and civil government customers. "From then on, more FDEs went back to working as software engineers" [1]. The same article adds: "Even today, no company employs more FDEs than Palantir" [1].

For many years the role had critics. First Round Review writes that skeptics "wrote off the FDE as glorified consulting for many years" [3]. The same article says Palantir's later success "cast new spotlight on the role it pioneered" [3]. The line with the solutions engineer is in the note on that comparison.

Did other companies have something like it before the name spread?

Some did, under other names, in the accounts First Round Review collects.

  • The co-founders of Looker "built and scaled a proto-FDE team", in the article's words [3].
  • Ironclad had a "legal engineering" team. The article calls it "Ironclad's equivalent of an FDE team" [3].

Neither source claims that these teams copied Palantir, or that Palantir copied anyone. They show that the work existed before the title became common.

How did the role spread to AI companies?

Fast, and mostly from 2025.

The Pragmatic Engineer gives one account from the inside. Colin Jarvis joined OpenAI in 2022 as a solutions architect and later led solutions architecture. At the start of 2025 he "presented a business case for setting up a Forward Deployed Engineering Team". The group "started with two FDEs". By August 2025 it had more than 10 [1]. The same article reports that a16z had called the role "the hottest job in tech" [1].

The counts of postings say the same thing:

  • Bloomberry compared postings titled "forward deployed engineer" from January to October 2025 with the same months of 2024. It found growth of 1,165 percent, and wrote that "October 2025 had the highest # of job postings for forward deployed engineers ever" [5].
  • First Round Review, citing the Financial Times, reports that monthly listings "shot up by 800%" from January to September 2025 [3].
  • a16z wrote in January 2026: "Today, there are hundreds of companies hiring FDEs." In the same piece: "Palantir owns FDE" [2].

Is today's role the same as Palantir's?

Not always, according to people close to the original.

Ted Mabrey wrote in September 2024 that many companies were "replicating the form but not the function of the FDE" [4]. In his account, some take on work that used to go to outside systems integrators, "internalizing costs previously outsourced to SI's", and gain nothing from it over the long term [4].

Bloomberry's reading of about 1,000 postings points the same way. It credits Palantir with the title and then shows that employers now use it for several different jobs [5]. What those postings ask for is in the note on postings in Switzerland. The roles open today are on the roles list.
