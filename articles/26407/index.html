Today's Judgment

This issue focuses on Genesys's expansion of its FDE services and Google Cloud's skill security governance documentation. The common thread: enterprise AI agent delivery involves not just building, but also integration, governance, operations, and change control. The number of skills does not equal reusable capability, and passing a scan does not mean there is no risk in production.

Key Signals

1. Weekly Watch: Genesys Expands FDE Capabilities, Emphasizing Moving from Pilot to Production

Genesys's public announcement introduces its expanded forward deployed engineering capabilities, advancing AI agent use cases around customer integration, governance, and operations, and feeding delivery experience back into reusable applications.

Evidence boundary: This is the company's own service announcement, not independent customer acceptance. The two thousand-plus customer experience professionals mentioned in the announcement cover a variety of service roles and cannot be described as two thousand FDEs; this issue does not infer team headcount expansion, pricing, or revenue from this.

Source: Genesys official announcement

2. Google Cloud Skill Scanning: Inspecting Instructions, Scripts, and Attachments, with Human Review Retained

Google Cloud Agent Registry official documentation states that after a skill version is uploaded, instruction files, scripts, and supporting assets can be inspected to identify risks such as data exfiltration, credential exposure, and prompt injection; organizations can set blocking or audit modes.

The documentation also explicitly notes that model scans may produce false positives or false negatives, and findings must be reviewed and verified by developers. After scan configuration, existing versions are not automatically retroactively checked; coverage of older versions needs to be verified separately.

Evidence boundary: This is product documentation; no independent deployment testing was conducted this issue, and dynamic documentation pages are not treated as evidence of first release today. Automated scanning cannot replace task-level permission restrictions, runtime monitoring, and verification in customer environments.

Source: Google Cloud: Scan skills for security issues

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Skill inventory records should capture the publisher, maintainer, version, applicable tasks, tool permissions, and acceptance samples.
  • Scan findings should first be verified manually, with regression testing after fixes; check coverage of old and new versions separately.
  • Acceptance should include cross-person reuse, exception takeover, and maintenance handover, not just first-generation success.
  • External reporting should distinguish vendor capability claims, actual operational records, and customer sign-off; do not substitute asset counts for quality and returns.
