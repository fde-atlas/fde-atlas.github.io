How to Become an FDE? Explaining the Required Skills and Career Path

Original title: FDEになるには？必要スキルとキャリアパスを解説

株式会社Sai · 2026-07-25 · 7 min read

Summary

This article by Japan's Sai explains the skills and career paths for becoming a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). It argues the core requirement is cross-boundary ability anchored in implementation skill, broken into four pillars: breadth to build working solutions alone, problem discovery from observing customer operations, communication with both executives and frontline staff, and the ability to decompose work into units delegable to AI agents. It maps transitions from SIer SEs, web engineers, and IT consultants, noting each group's typical gap. No certifications are required; employers value shipped work and customer-facing experience. The author recommends building with AI coding tools daily, learning business vocabulary, and following products through to actual usage, while cautioning that FDE's accountability for outcomes may not suit specialists.

Key points

  • FDE needs four skills: implementation breadth, problem discovery, communication, and AI leverage.
  • Implementation skill means finishing end-to-end, not depth in one domain.
  • AI skill is decomposing work into units delegable to AI agents.
  • SEs, web engineers, and consultants each face different transition gaps.
  • No required certifications; 2-3 years of base experience is advised first.

  • For FDE implementation skills, breadth—the ability to single-handedly build everything needed to solve a problem—and follow-through matter more than depth in a specific domain.

"The FDE way of working sounds interesting, but is it right for me?"—Many engineers likely felt this way after reading the difference between FDEs and traditional engineers.

In this article, we explain the skill set required of an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) and realistic career paths for pursuing FDE roles in Japan today.

The Four Skills Required of an FDE

① Implementation skills—the breadth to "build things end to end on your own"

The foundation of an FDE is, above all, implementation skills. However, the breadth to build everything needed for problem solving end to end matters more than depth in a specific domain. Simple web apps, data collection and processing, integration with external services, deployment to the cloud—what's needed in the field is this combination.

You don't need to brace yourself thinking "I must be a full-stack developer." With generative AI support as a given, areas outside your specialty can be filled in as you research. What matters is the follow-through to take even unfamiliar technologies and turn them into something that works, without hesitation.

② Problem discovery skills—the ability to dig one step before "what you're told"

An FDE's work starts not from a requirements document but from observing the field. Not "What should we build?" and not even "What are you struggling with?"—starting with "Please show me how the work is done"—this shift in stance is the first hurdle.

Specifically: the ability to diagram business workflows, form hypotheses from data, and keep asking "why?" to reach the real problem behind surface-level requests. This overlaps with the fundamental techniques of consulting.

③ Communication—speaking both management's and the field's language

An FDE must speak to executives in terms of return on investment and to field staff in terms of daily operations. It sounds difficult, but the essence is simple: structure the conversation around what the other person cares about.

Another important skill is the "ability to bring people along." No matter how good a system is, it won't be used if the field feels it's being forced on them. Celebrating small wins together and visibly reflecting the field's feedback—this accumulation makes change stick.

④ AI utilization—using AI agents as "subordinates"

Today's FDEs make full use of generative AI and AI agents in both development and business operations. The reason one person can enter the field and deliver something working within weeks is that delegating development tasks to AI agents is a given way of working.

More than prompt craftsmanship, the ability to break work down into units that can be handed to AI is the core. This can be said to be a central FDE skill, one that applies directly when automating customers' business processes as well.

Paths to Becoming an FDE by Career Background

From Contract Development / SIer SE

This is the most common pattern. Having experienced both customer relations and implementation, the foundation is already there. The gap is experience "stepping outside the specifications." At your current job, start by going to customers to ask about the background of requirements, or following up on usage after delivery—accumulate small steps across boundaries like these.

From Web Engineer / In-House Development

Implementation skills and speed are powerful weapons. What's missing is experience facing external customers and understanding the domains specific to business systems. Deliberately seek out work where "you can see the face of the person you're building for"—such as freelance side work or projects collaborating with customer success—and the path will open up.

From IT Consultant

Structuring problems and handling clients are already strengths, but the challenge is experience "building things with your own hands." Fortunately, generative AI has dramatically lowered the barrier to implementation. Start by writing internal tools or analysis scripts yourself, and accumulate experience of "running what you proposed"—that's the shortcut.

Do You Need Certifications to Become an FDE?

In short, no certification is required to become an FDE. FDE is a profession evaluated on whether you can find problems in the field and build solutions end to end, and what employers look at is track record, not certifications. Experience building something that works, experience facing customers, and being able to explain it in your own words—these three carry more weight than any certification on a resume.

That said, certifications are not meaningless. The Fundamental and Applied Information Technology Engineer exams serve as proof of foundational knowledge, and cloud or security certifications can reassure customers depending on your area of responsibility. Rather than "getting certified before aiming to become an FDE," reinforce as needed while accumulating building experience—that order is the right one.

Three Things You Can Prepare Starting Today

  • Make AI agent development part of your daily routine: Use AI coding tools like Claude Code to repeatedly build small tools all the way to completion
  • Learn the language of business operations: Understand the structure of operations found in every company, such as sales, accounting, and inventory management. Casual conversations with people in the field are the best learning material
  • Build the habit of seeing things through "until they're used": Don't stop at building—observe how it's used and improve at least one thing. This habit builds the FDE mindset

The Appeal and Risks of an FDE Career

The appeal is that it's a position whose value keeps rising in the AI era (see why FDEs are increasing worldwide). Work that only involves writing code will be replaced by AI, but work that finds problems in the field and takes responsibility for outcomes will become rarer instead. Because it deepens your understanding of the business, it also connects to future entrepreneurial or executive careers such as CTO/COO. In fact, companies have begun considering whether to hire FDE talent or use external resources, which confirms the growing demand for this profession.

On the other hand, responsibility for outcomes is a source of pressure, and there is also the burden of continuously learning multiple domains. For those inclined to "master a single technology," the traditional specialist path may bring more happiness.

Frequently Asked Questions from Aspiring FDEs

Q. Can I become an FDE without prior experience?

Going straight into this role with no programming or customer-facing experience isn't realistic. The steady path is to first build 2-3 years of foundational experience as an engineer or consultant, then transition via one of the career paths above. That said, the spread of generative AI keeps lowering the barrier on the "building" side, and the time it takes to make the transition is definitely shrinking.

Q. How is this different from a regular SE or consultant?

In a word, it's the "scope of responsibility." SEs are responsible for building things, and consultants for making proposals, but an FDE is responsible all the way through to results being achieved. For details, see Differences between FDEs and traditional engineers, which includes diagrams.

Q. Is English necessary?

If you mainly work on domestic projects, it's not required. However, much of the information and early case studies about FDEs comes from overseas, so the ability to read primary sources in English makes a big difference in how fast you learn.
