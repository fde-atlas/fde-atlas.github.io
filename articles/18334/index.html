What's the difference between an FDE and an SES? A realistic path for going from SES engineer to FDE.

Original title: FDEとSESの違いとは。SESエンジニアからFDEになるための、現実的な経路

日本Aiセンター株式会社 · 2026-07-31 · 4 min read

Summary

This Japanese article from 日本Aiセンター株式会社 argues that the difference between FDEs and SES engineers lies not in contract type but in how work is entrusted: SES engineers build from given specifications, while FDEs start from identifying problems and carry solutions through to adoption. The author rejects framing FDE as an upgrade of SES, yet notes market pricing increasingly favors people who can lead AI agents to solve problems end-to-end. A four-step path from SES to FDE is proposed, illustrated by the author's own career, and the company runs an FDE-focused staffing service supporting engineers through these steps.

Key points

  • The FDE/SES distinction is about delegation, not contract form.
  • FDE is not superior to SES, but market pricing is shifting toward FDE-style work.
  • Four steps: join AI-approved sites, delegate implementation to agents, build solved-problem track record, document it in your skill sheet.
  • The author transitioned without job change or certifications, via project selection.
  • 日本AIセンター runs an FDE window and supports engineers through the four steps.

What is an FDE? (30-second version)

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who embeds in the customer's worksite and handles everything end to end, from problem discovery through implementation and adoption. The role spread from the front lines of overseas AI companies, and it is now attracting so much attention in Japan that more companies offer it and feature articles are being published. We also run a dedicated FDE desk. This article keeps the definition explanation to a minimum and gets to the main topic: "how FDEs differ from SES."

The difference is not the "contract" but "how you are entrusted"

A common misconception is "SES = quasi-delegation, FDE = a different contract type," but this is wrong. FDEs also work under resident, quasi-delegation arrangements. In fact, we provide FDE-type talent through SES commercial channels. The difference shows not in the contract but in how you are entrusted at the worksite.

SES的な任され方 FDE的な任され方
How work startsYou receive defined specs and tasksYou identify "what the problem is" together with the client
How implementation is doneWriting code by hand is what gets valuedMost implementation is delegated to coding agents, and people focus on design, verification, and judgment
How work endsDelivery and burning through hoursThe system keeps running on site and the team can operate on its own
Basis of evaluationHours worked and skill matchThe size of the problems you solved

"FDE is a superset of SES"—not true. But pricing is starting to change

We do not subscribe to narratives that hype FDEs while trashing SES. Work that builds defined specs to a high standard will remain, and the value of people who excel at it will not disappear. That said, the market prices things honestly. As we wrote in our article on rates, even in resident roles, people who can lead AI and solve problems end to end are starting to command higher rates. Rather than asking "which is better," if you look at "which style of entrustment is a growing market," there is a rational case for betting on the FDE type.

The path from SES to FDE is seamless

Changing jobs or getting certifications is not required to become an FDE. SES worksites are places where you touch the client's business and systems every day, which is exactly the first half of FDE work (understanding the site). What is missing is only the second half: the experience of "leading AI agents to solve problems end to end." The path has four stages.

① Get into an AI-approved worksite. At sites where coding agents cannot be used, you cannot practice the second half. We have already written about how to spot AI-approved worksites and how to find Claude Code projects.

② Hone a working style where you delegate implementation and move to design, verification, and judgment. This means doing, in real work on site, the shift from "writing" to "reviewing and deciding" described in our article on AI-driven development.

③ Pick up the site's pain points and build a track record of solving them end to end. Pick one of those "quietly annoying problems" that fall outside your assigned tasks, team up with AI agents to turn it into something that works, and make it stick. Even if small, this becomes the substance of "I have done FDE work."

④ Write "what you delegated and your own judgment" on your skills sheet. This is the three-part set described in our article on skills sheets. If you can write this much, your documents will be read as those of FDE-type talent.

I followed this order myself. I was originally an engineer working at SES sites, and the turning point was building our own system with Claude Code. Once I learned a working style of delegating implementation while I focused on design and judgment, both our development speed and my own role changed. Then, when choosing my next project, I chose to enter a worksite doing AI-driven development. No special job change or certification—just an accumulation of worksite and project choices, and my working style had become that of an FDE.

At Japan AI Center

We run the FDE-style working approach in our own management, development, and PR operations, and provide talent selected and trained from that practice to companies through our dedicated FDE desk. For engineers, we support the four stages above as a company. Neither connecting you to AI-approved projects nor polishing your skills sheet is left up to you alone.
