SOW-Generator

Original title: SOW-Generator

FDE中国社区 · 2026-07-12 · 4 min read

Summary

This article documents SOW-Generator, a skill package (v1.1, maturity: usable) from the FDE China community for drafting Statements of Work for AI PoC/MVP projects. It targets scope drift and acceptance disputes caused by verbal agreements. The method defines seven core SOW sections—goals and boundaries, deliverables, milestones, acceptance metrics, data and permissions, risks and assumptions, and change management—and insists a PoC only validates core hypotheses without committing to full production. Inputs include scenario cards, stakeholder maps, budget, and compliance constraints; outputs are the SOW body, milestone table, and acceptance metrics appendix. It also specifies four stage gates from PoC preparation to retrospective assetization, role divisions between FDE, AIBP, and business owners, and extra constraints for private deployment, Xinchuang, and security compliance. A sample case reports a customer-service PoC accepted in four weeks with no disputes.

Key points

  • SOW has seven core sections; PoCs only validate core hypotheses.
  • Inputs: scenario cards and stakeholder maps; outputs: SOW and metrics.
  • Four stage gates from PoC preparation to retrospective assetization.
  • FDE leads delivery; AIBP owns Ground Truth and business acceptance.
  • Covers private deployment, Xinchuang, and compliance constraints.

Problem Definition

AI project scope tends to drift; verbal agreements lead to acceptance disputes and disputes over additional costs.

Methodology Framework

Core SOW sections: objectives and boundaries, deliverables, milestones, acceptance criteria, data and permissions, risks and assumptions, change mechanism. PoC principle: only validate core assumptions; do not commit to full-scale production.

FAQ

Q1: When running [SOW-Generator], how do I avoid "scope too large—building production in one go"?

A: Follow the README methodology step by step and showcase interim deliverables in weekly Demos; if PoC materials are incomplete, first produce a "to-be-validated list" before proceeding.

Q2: What additional constraints apply in private deployment/Xinchuang/MLPS environments?

A: Specify the deployment form explicitly in the prompt constraints section; when data cannot leave the domain, domestic components, or audit logs are involved, reference Private-Deployment-Gateway and RBAC-Audit accordingly.

Q3: What if the division of responsibilities with the AIBP is unclear?

A: Return to AIBP-Collaboration-Playbook to update the RACI; AIBP is responsible for Ground Truth and business acceptance, while the FDE is responsible for deliverable technical assets.

Q4: After the PoC passes, how do we transition to Beta/production?

A: Mark the next-stage Skill in the deliverables (e.g., RAG-Evaluation→Private-Deployment-Gateway→FDE-Adoption-Growth), and trigger the launch gate via the checklist.
