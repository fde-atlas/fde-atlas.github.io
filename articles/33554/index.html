Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) and Site Reliability Engineers (SREs) both work on complex engineering problems, but they are responsible for different outcomes. An FDE works closely with customers to build and deploy technology that solves specific business problems. An SRE focuses on keeping software services reliable, available and efficient at scale.

The roles share technical foundations such as programming, cloud infrastructure, automation and production troubleshooting. However, their day-to-day responsibilities and measures of success can look quite different.

Understanding what each role involves can help you choose a career path that matches your technical interests, preferred working style and long-term goals.

What does a Forward Deployed Engineer do?

A Forward Deployed Engineer works directly with customers to understand their technical and business requirements, then builds or adapts software to solve their problems. The role combines software engineering with customer discovery, systems integration and deployment.

Unlike an engineer who works mainly on a product’s general features, an FDE often operates close to the environment where the solution will be used. This could involve working with a customer’s existing applications, databases, cloud infrastructure and internal workflows.

Typical responsibilities include:

  • Understanding customer workflows and identifying technical requirements.
  • Turning loosely defined problems into practical engineering solutions.
  • Building applications, APIs, integrations and AI-powered systems.
  • Connecting software with customer data and existing infrastructure.
  • Testing, deploying and troubleshooting solutions in customer environments.
  • Working with product and engineering teams to improve deployments.

Not every FDE position involves artificial intelligence. Some focus on broader software development, data integration or enterprise implementation. The exact responsibilities depend on the employer and the product being deployed.

Example of an FDE’s work

Imagine a manufacturing company wants to identify delays in its supply chain using AI.

The FDE meets the operations team to understand where delays occur and what information is available. They connect relevant data sources, build an application that analyses supply chain information and integrate it with the company’s existing systems.

The goal is not simply to create an AI model or demonstrate a prototype. It is to deliver a solution that fits the customer’s workflow and can be used in practice.

What does a Site Reliability Engineer do?

A Site Reliability Engineer applies software engineering principles to operational problems. The role focuses on keeping an organisation’s software services reliable, available, scalable and responsive.

An SRE works on the systems that support applications and services. This may include cloud infrastructure, distributed systems, deployment pipelines, monitoring and incident management. Rather than relying entirely on manual operational processes, SREs use automation and engineering practices to improve how systems behave in production.

Typical responsibilities include:

  • Monitoring service health, availability and performance.
  • Investigating production failures and reducing downtime.
  • Automating repetitive operational tasks.
  • Improving infrastructure scalability and resilience.
  • Defining Service Level Indicators (SLIs) and Service Level Objectives (SLOs).
  • Managing error budgets and reliability trade-offs.
  • Conducting incident reviews and preventing recurring failures.

Google’s Site Reliability Engineering resources explain how practices such as service-level objectives and error budgets help teams balance reliability with the pace of software development.

Example of an SRE’s work

Consider an e-commerce platform that frequently experiences outages during major sales.

The SRE investigates the failures, studies traffic and performance metrics, improves monitoring and alerting, and works with developers to identify weaknesses in the infrastructure. They may also introduce automation to manage traffic spikes and reduce the risk of future incidents.

The goal is to keep the service dependable, even when usage increases significantly.

FDE vs SRE: What are the key differences?

The main difference between an FDE and an SRE lies in the problem each role owns. An FDE is generally responsible for delivering a solution that works in a customer’s environment. An SRE is primarily responsible for the reliability and operational performance of services.

The following comparison describes common role patterns. Individual employers may assign responsibilities differently.

The distinction is not that FDEs build software while SREs only manage infrastructure. Both roles involve engineering, coding and production troubleshooting. Their priorities determine how they apply those skills.

Where do FDE and SRE responsibilities overlap?

FDEs and SREs share several technical responsibilities because both work with systems that need to function outside a controlled development environment.

Their work may overlap in:

  • Cloud infrastructure: Both may deploy and troubleshoot applications on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud.
  • Automation: Both use scripts and tools to reduce repetitive work and improve consistency.
  • Production troubleshooting: Both investigate issues that affect applications and users.
  • Monitoring and performance: Both may use logs, metrics and monitoring tools to understand system behaviour.
  • Security and access management: Both need to consider permissions, authentication and secure deployments.
  • System reliability: Both need to build and maintain solutions that work under real-world conditions.

Reliability is therefore not exclusive to SRE. An FDE may need to fix a deployment failure or improve an application’s monitoring. The difference is that reliability is the central engineering objective of an SRE, while it is one part of an FDE’s broader responsibility to deliver a working customer solution.

What skills and tools do FDEs and SREs need?

Both roles require strong engineering fundamentals, but the depth of knowledge needed in each area differs. FDEs typically need a combination of application development, integration and customer-facing skills. SREs need deeper experience with infrastructure, distributed systems and operational reliability.

Core skills for a Forward Deployed Engineer

A strong FDE skill set usually combines technical execution with the ability to understand a customer’s problem.

Important areas include:

  • Programming and application development: Build maintainable applications and services, rather than relying only on prototypes.
  • API integration and data handling: Connect applications with databases, enterprise software and external services.
  • Cloud deployment and debugging: Deploy applications, configure environments and troubleshoot production issues.
  • Applied AI: Understand LLM APIs, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), AI agents and evaluation when working on AI-focused deployments.
  • Customer communication: Gather requirements, clarify ambiguous requests and explain technical decisions to stakeholders.

Core skills for a Site Reliability Engineer

SREs need to understand how software behaves across infrastructure and distributed environments.

Key skills include:

  • Operating systems and networking: Understand Linux, network protocols, DNS and system-level troubleshooting.
  • Distributed systems: Work with services that communicate across multiple machines and components.
  • Monitoring and observability: Use logs, metrics, traces and alerts to understand service health.
  • Incident management: Investigate failures, identify root causes and coordinate recovery.
  • Automation and infrastructure as code: Use programming, configuration management and tools such as Terraform to reduce manual operational work.

Tools such as Kubernetes and Terraform are common in many SRE environments, but they are not mandatory for every position. The required stack depends on the organisation’s architecture and the systems an engineer is responsible for.

What does a typical workday look like for an FDE vs an SRE?

Both roles involve coding, meetings, debugging and collaboration. The difference is the context in which these activities take place. The following are illustrative workdays, not fixed schedules.

A Forward Deployed Engineer might:

  • Meet a customer to understand a workflow or integration problem.
  • Review the customer’s existing applications, infrastructure and data.
  • Build or modify an application or AI workflow.
  • Test integrations in the customer’s environment.
  • Demonstrate the solution and collect feedback.
  • Resolve deployment issues and plan the next iteration.

A Site Reliability Engineer might:

  • Review service health dashboards, alerts and performance metrics.
  • Investigate an increase in latency or error rates.
  • Work with developers to resolve a reliability issue.
  • Automate a repetitive operational task.
  • Improve monitoring, infrastructure or deployment processes.
  • Participate in incident reviews and reliability planning.

An FDE’s schedule may change depending on customer meetings, project stages and deployment requirements. An SRE’s work may be shaped by incidents, release schedules and reliability priorities. Both roles can involve urgent production work.

FDE vs SRE: Which career should you choose?

Neither career is universally better. Your decision should depend on the engineering problems you enjoy solving, how you prefer to collaborate and the kind of technical specialisation you want to develop.

An FDE career may suit you if you:

  • Enjoy solving different business problems through software.
  • Like working directly with customers and stakeholders.
  • Prefer a combination of application development, AI integration and deployment.
  • Are comfortable with changing requirements and ambiguous problems.
  • Want to understand how technology is used in real business environments.
  • Enjoy taking ownership of a solution from discovery to deployment.

An SRE career may suit you if you:

  • Enjoy distributed systems, infrastructure and cloud engineering.
  • Prefer working on system performance and reliability.
  • Like monitoring, debugging and resolving production incidents.
  • Enjoy automation and reducing repetitive operational work.
  • Want to specialise in availability, scalability and resilience.
  • Are comfortable with incident response and possible on-call rotations.

Consider one question: Would you rather build a solution around a customer’s problem, or improve the reliability of a system that many users already depend on?

Your answer can be a useful starting point. However, job titles do not always reveal the full scope of a position. Review the actual responsibilities, technical expectations, customer interaction, travel requirements and on-call arrangements before deciding.

Can an SRE or DevOps engineer transition into an FDE role?

Yes. An SRE or DevOps engineer can move into an FDE role, and existing infrastructure experience can provide a useful foundation.

Skills such as cloud deployment, networking, identity and access management (IAM), containerisation, production troubleshooting, incident response and automation are relevant to many FDE positions. These capabilities help engineers understand the environments in which customer solutions need to operate.

The transition may require strengthening other areas, particularly:

  • Application development beyond infrastructure automation.
  • Python and API-based integrations.
  • SQL and data engineering fundamentals.
  • LLM applications, RAG and AI evaluation for AI-focused FDE roles.
  • Customer discovery and requirements gathering.
  • Technical documentation and solution proposals.

A practical way to prepare is to build an application that connects to an external API, processes data and runs on a cloud platform. Add testing, logging and monitoring, then document the business problem, architecture and deployment decisions.

Practise explaining the project to someone without a technical background. This helps demonstrate both engineering ability and the communication skills needed to work with customers. This is a suggested preparation path, not a formal hiring requirement.

How can you prepare for an FDE career?

Preparing for an FDE career requires more than learning to use AI tools or building a few prototypes. You need to demonstrate that you can take a problem from its initial definition to a working deployment.

Focus on these areas:

  • Programming: Build confidence in Python, software engineering fundamentals and application development.
  • Data handling: Learn SQL, data processing and API-based integration workflows.
  • Applied AI: Understand LLM APIs, RAG, agent workflows and methods for evaluating AI outputs.
  • Cloud and deployment: Practise deploying applications and managing production environments.
  • System design: Learn to build scalable, secure and maintainable systems.
  • Customer problem-solving: Practise translating business requirements into technical solutions.
  • Project development: Create projects that demonstrate problem definition, development, integration, testing and deployment.

Your portfolio should show how a working application was built and delivered, not just that you experimented with an AI model. Include the problem statement, architecture, technology choices, deployment process, limitations and decisions you made along the way.

This gives prospective employers a clearer picture of how you approach engineering problems in real environments.

Conclusion

FDE and SRE are distinct engineering paths with shared technical foundations. An FDE role suits engineers who enjoy customer-facing problem-solving, software development and deploying solutions in varied environments. An SRE role suits those who want to specialise in infrastructure, distributed systems and production reliability. Before choosing, look beyond the job title. Compare the responsibilities, technical expectations, working conditions and problems you will be expected to own.

Frequently asked questions

1. Is an FDE a DevOps engineer?

No. An FDE may use DevOps practices and infrastructure tools, but the role has a broader focus on solving customer problems through software development, integrations and deployment. DevOps engineering focuses more specifically on improving software delivery and operational workflows.

2. Is SRE a coding job or an operations job?

SRE combines both. It applies software engineering to operational problems, using programming and automation to improve service reliability, availability and performance.

3. Can a software developer become a Site Reliability Engineer?

Yes. A software developer can prepare for SRE work by learning Linux, networking, distributed systems, cloud infrastructure, monitoring, incident management and automation.

4. Can a DevOps engineer become a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Yes. DevOps experience in cloud platforms, deployment, networking and troubleshooting can provide a useful foundation. The transition may require stronger application development, data integration, applied AI and customer-facing problem-solving skills.

5. Do Forward Deployed Engineers need to know machine learning?

Not every FDE role requires deep machine learning expertise. However, AI-focused positions may require familiarity with LLM APIs, retrieval-augmented generation, AI agents, model evaluation and deployment. Requirements depend on the employer and the applications being built.

6. Do Site Reliability Engineers need to know Kubernetes?

Kubernetes is common in cloud-native infrastructure and many SRE roles, but it is not a universal requirement. The relevant technology stack depends on the systems an SRE is responsible for operating.

7. Is SRE better than DevOps for long-term career growth?

Neither is automatically better. SRE focuses on reliability engineering, distributed systems and service performance. DevOps focuses on improving collaboration, automation and software delivery. Career growth depends on technical interests, experience and employer requirements.

8. Can an FDE work remotely?

Some FDE roles allow remote or hybrid work, while others require customer-site visits or in-person collaboration. The arrangement depends on the customer, deployment environment and employer. Candidates should check travel and location requirements in individual job postings.

9. Do Forward Deployed Engineers have on-call duties?

Some do, particularly when they own production deployments or support customer-critical workflows. The frequency and responsibility vary by employer. Check whether a role includes incident response, deployment support or a formal on-call rotation.

10. What is a Forward Deployed Reliability Engineer?

A Forward Deployed Reliability Engineer is a hybrid role that combines customer-facing deployment work with reliability engineering responsibilities. Palantir’s current careers listings include a Forward Deployed Reliability Engineer role and a Forward Deployed Site Reliability Engineer role. Their existence illustrates how the responsibilities can overlap, although the exact scope depends on the job description.

11. What is the difference between an FDE and a platform engineer?

An FDE typically builds or adapts solutions for specific customer environments. A platform engineer builds internal platforms, tools and infrastructure that help other developers create and operate applications more efficiently. The roles can overlap in infrastructure and automation.

12. Which programming language should I learn first for an FDE career?

Python is a practical starting point because it is widely used in backend development, data processing and AI application development. Candidates should also learn APIs, SQL, application development and the languages required by their target employers.
