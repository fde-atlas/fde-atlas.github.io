Summary

FDE Baike has published a glossary of ten key terms for enterprise AI deployment, covering concepts such as FDE, RAG, semantic layer, on-site deployment, lighthouse projects, and ontology. Each entry pairs a one-sentence direct answer with a deeper explanation and related terms. The glossary also introduces original frameworks like the Five Field Gates, a five-step acceptance checklist for FDE delivery, and Knowledge Backflow, the practice of consolidating field experience into organizational assets. Full text

GLOSSARY

Understand the key concepts in the field of FDEs and enterprise AI deployment. Each entry: a one-sentence direct answer + an in-depth explanation + related terms. Continuously updated.

An FDE is a hybrid engineering role that enters the front lines of enterprise business: identifying real business problems, building usable systems with AI and engineering capabilities, following through to delivery and results, and distilling the experience…

FDX broadly refers to all forward deployed roles: when the FDE's "enter the field" logic extends to product (FDP), design (FDD), research (FDR), and other functions…

The five-step FDE loop, with an acceptance gate at each step: the pain point gate, the entry point gate, the prototype gate, the business case gate, and the self-sustaining gate—one gatekeeping question per gate; if you can't answer it, don't move on. …

Experience backflow refers to consolidating the experience, methods, and patterns gained at customer sites back into the organization's knowledge base, SOPs, and product platform, so that the next delivery can reuse them rather than starting every project from scratch…

On-site residency means service provider personnel work long-term at the customer's office and business front line, directly engaging with real processes, data, and people; it is the basic working mode of the FDE model. …

A lighthouse project is a deployment strategy of first selecting, within an enterprise, the scenario most likely to produce results and most willing to cooperate, achieving visible outcomes, and then using it as a template to replicate and roll out to other departments. …

In the enterprise AI context, ontology refers to the structured modeling of an enterprise's objects (customers, orders, equipment), attributes, and relationships; it is the language for AI to understand and operate on real business…

RAG is a technical architecture in which a large language model first retrieves relevant content from an enterprise knowledge base before generating an answer; it is the mainstream implementation for enterprise knowledge Q&A scenarios. …

A semantic layer is an intermediate layer that translates enterprise data into a unified business vocabulary (metrics, dimensions, terminology), so that humans and AI reference the same set of trusted definitions, avoiding "same name, different meaning." …

AI deployment readiness measures how many conditions an enterprise currently has in place for successful AI deployment, typically assessed across business clarity, data and knowledge foundations, organizational talent, scenario expectations, and knowledge consolidation…
