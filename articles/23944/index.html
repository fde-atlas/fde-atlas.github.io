How to Run FDE Hiring | A Guide for Recruiters Covering Job Postings, Selection, and 90-Day Retention

Original title: FDE採用の進め方｜求人票・選考・90日定着までを採用担当向けに整理

FDE総研 · 2026-08-13 · 19 min read

Summary

This FDE総研 guide walks recruiters through hiring Forward Deployed Engineers in Japan, arguing the process must differ fundamentally from standard software engineer hiring. It covers defining the role before recruiting, writing job descriptions around three practical behaviors, a four-stage selection process including case interviews and customer role-play, sourcing candidates from five adjacent roles rather than only existing FDEs, and a 30/60/90-day onboarding plan paired with a four-layer evaluation framework to ensure retention.

Key points

  • FDE hiring should assess on-site problem solving, not coding rank.
  • Job descriptions should limit must-haves to problem definition, independent implementation, and production operations.
  • Case interviews should start with deliberately incomplete information to test questioning.
  • Sourcing from five adjacent roles beats restricting to prior FDE experience.
  • A 30/60/90-day shadowing plan plus four-layer evaluation (outcome, delivery, assetization, team contribution) drives retention.

Why is FDE hiring different from typical engineer hiring?

According to the FDE Research Institute, FDE hiring is not about ranking technical skills but about determining whether a candidate can deliver value in the client's field, which requires rebuilding the selection process from the ground up across four stages.

The job title FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) was popularized by Palantir Technologies as "a role stationed at the client site that launches software for each business problem," and later spread to startups and SaaS companies. In Japan as well, as AI product and business system implementations can no longer succeed on a "sell and done" basis, the same type of role has begun to appear under other names such as Solutions Engineer, Implementation Engineer, and Field Engineer. A common mistake here is recruiting FDEs by merely slightly rewriting existing backend or SRE job descriptions.

The evaluation target shifts from "implementation skill" to "on-site problem solving"

FDE outcomes are measured not by the amount of code written but by whether the client's business actually changed. Therefore, evaluation criteria should be decided before posting the job.

In typical product development, requirements are organized by the product manager, and engineers implement a defined scope with high quality. An FDE's work starts from an earlier stage: observing the client's business flows, deciding independently what to automate for impact, shipping something functional in a short time, and observing reactions. Therefore, what to assess in selection is not whether the candidate can produce the optimal algorithm, but whether they can keep making decisions even with incomplete information.

In practical terms, what FDEs encounter in their first one to two months is often not technical challenges but organizational ones, such as "I don't know who the real decision-maker is" or "the on-site staff don't want to use the new system." If you hire someone who cannot push through these alone, the project stalls even if they are technically excellent.

Recruiting without a clear role definition leads to post-hire mismatches

If you recruit without internal agreement on the FDE's scope of responsibility, new hires will feel "this isn't what I was told," which becomes a major cause of early turnover.

If you cannot fill in the FDE column of this table in your own company's words, spending time on building internal consensus before starting hiring will ultimately move things faster. Also writing down "what an FDE does NOT do" keeps your selection criteria and onboarding design consistent.

The cost of a hiring failure is high, and the talent pool is small

FDEs often face clients alone. A hiring mistake can spread to a loss of trust in the project itself, making the cost of failure greater than for typical roles.

An FDE hiring mistake doesn't just reduce internal productivity; it undermines trust in the project itself. Moreover, few people in Japan are currently job-hunting under the FDE title. You end up hiring under conditions where "applications won't come if you just wait, and failure is costly," so you need to invest effort upfront in defining requirements and designing sourcing/outreach.

How does FDE differ from SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer, and who is it not suited for?

Under the FDE Research Institute's comparison framework, FDEs are distinguished from SWEs, PdMs, and SIers by handling problem definition, self-driven implementation, and production operations end-to-end alone.

Translating the differences from the four similar roles into hiring criteria

The differences between FDEs and SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers show up in "how far they decide on their own, and how far they build and operate things themselves."

Characteristics of candidates unsuited to FDE roles

What FDE Soken weighs most heavily in selection is whether a candidate views situations where specifications are undecided as "I can't move unless someone decides for me."

The following are signs of a tendency to be poorly suited to FDE roles. A single sign is not conclusive, but if multiple interviewers notice the same ones, it is best to proceed with caution.

  • Speaks in a way that dismisses the customer's circumstances as "irrational" or "lacking understanding"
  • Can only talk about the results of past projects in terms of the scope they personally implemented
  • Views situations where specifications are undecided as "I can't move unless someone decides for me"
  • Suddenly becomes vague when the topic turns to operations and handover

Even people with these tendencies can demonstrate strengths in product development environments where requirements are well defined. Interviewers should align on the understanding that rejecting someone as an FDE is separate from lowering their evaluation as an engineer.

When you should choose another approach instead of hiring an FDE

If your company is not yet at the stage where it can fully define the FDE role, choose internal consensus-building or a trial via contracted work before making a full-time hire.

If the FDE column in Table 1 cannot be filled, the customers to assign have not yet been decided, or there is no senior employee to accompany them—if any of these three applies, it is better not to rush the hiring. When starting with a small project via contracted work, checking key points for FDE contracting and FDE fee benchmarks in advance makes it easier to judge the scope of the contract and the budget.

How should the FDE job description (JD) be defined?

For an FDE JD, narrow the required qualifications down to three practical behaviors, and fully spell out the assigned customers, project phases, and scope of discretion together with the evaluation system.

Narrow required qualifications down to the "three practical behaviors"

If an FDE JD lists too many required qualifications, candidates will disappear. Narrow it down to around three items covering only the behaviors essential to executing projects.

A common failure seen in FDE JDs is listing ten or more required qualifications. In practice, the behaviors essential to an FDE often fit into FDE Soken's three axes.

  • Problem definition: Can interview the customer's business themselves, structure the problems, and articulate them (does not wait to be handed requirements)
  • Independent implementation: Can build a prototype alone in a short period (what matters is whether they can carry it through to the end, more than specific languages or frameworks)
  • Production operations: Can take responsibility through production operations (data handling, incident response, documentation for handover)

Other skills such as specific cloud platforms, machine learning, or industry knowledge should be moved to preferred qualifications. Even as preferred qualifications, candidates who truly have the necessary skills will naturally gather.

Table 3: How to differentiate FDE JDs (organized by FDE Soken, as of November 2026)

CategoryExample content to includePoints to note
Required qualificationsExperience from customer interviews through requirements definition, experience implementing a prototype alone, production operations experienceUp to 3-4 items. Keep years-of-experience requirements to a minimum
Preferred qualificationsSQL/data infrastructure, cloud operations, industry domain knowledge, EnglishPut specialized skills here
Conditions to state explicitlyFrequency of customer site visits, travel scope, whether on-call is required, scope of discretionHiding these leads to mismatches after joining
Expressions better left out"Someone who will do anything," "emphasis on grit"Conveys an ambiguous role definition, and the more talented candidates will walk away

Describe "what the FDE does at the customer site" through a typical day

For an FDE JD, describing an actual day and how projects progress raises the quality of applicants more than abstract role descriptions.

From a single sentence like "You will solve customer problems with software," readers can glean almost nothing. In practical terms, what FDE applicants care about most is "Will I have discretion?" and "Will I be close to customer decision-making?"—JDs that convey this attract responses. FDE Soken recommends structuring the JD in the following order.

  • The target customer's industry and the project phases to be handled
  • Project duration and the composition of accompanying team members
  • What to do in the first week after joining
  • The standard flow through to release
  • Frequency of customer site visits, travel scope, and whether on-call is required
  • Scope of discretion and what the role does not do
  • Evaluation criteria and the behaviors that lead to raises

For products like VACAN that turn real-time on-site conditions into data, it is necessary to go as far as designing operations at the customer's site. For that reason, the FDE role description should also be written to reflect that on-site nature. How detailed to make the JD should be decided based on the nature of your own product.

Decide the compensation range and evaluation approach in advance

FDEs fit poorly into existing grade tables. Decide the compensation range and evaluation criteria with HR before starting hiring.

Evaluating FDEs on engineering grades makes contributions close to sales invisible. Conversely, using sales-style evaluation means the burden of implementation goes unrewarded. If you cannot explain at the offer stage "on what criteria will I be evaluated, and what do I need to do to get a raise?", the more talented candidates will hold off on deciding. How to create grade criteria tables and KPI sheets is covered in detail in How to build an FDE evaluation system.

Which talent pools are most effective for sourcing FDE candidates?

According to FDE Soken's analysis, rather than narrowing to "people with FDE experience," defining conversion candidates from five adjacent roles and scouting them yields a deeper candidate pool.

Identify the adjacent roles that convert easily first

FDE sits at the intersection of multiple existing roles, and people who converted from five adjacent roles often become the core of the team.

Table 4: Five adjacent roles that convert easily to FDE (organized by FDE Soken, as of November 2026)

Original roleStrengthsPoints that need to be filled in
Lead engineers at contracted development firms and SIersCustomer relations, requirements definition, deadline managementProduct thinking, mindset of continuous improvement
In-house SE / information systemsBusiness understanding, on-site coordination, operational establishmentDevelopment speed, prototyping
Data engineers / analystsData preparation, SQL, design of business metricsApplication implementation, UI
Solution engineers / pre-salesProposal skills, customer understanding, demo buildingTaking responsibility through production implementation and operations
Engineers who handled everything at startupsSelf-driven capability, broad implementation skillsAdapting to the decision-making processes of large enterprise customers

If you write out the professional backgrounds in this table as recruitment targets, both your scout messages and casual interview content become more concrete. Conversely, if you narrow it down to "only those with FDE experience," you'll have almost no candidates.

Think in terms of four channels: scouting, referrals, content publishing, and contracting

FDE positions are hard to fill by waiting for applications on job boards. A realistic approach is to combine proactive outreach channels with content publishing.

  • Direct scouting: Send people in the five adjacent professions a message that describes the concrete details of the projects they would handle, rather than the JD
  • Referrals: Introductions from internal sales or customer success staff tend to be effective (people who know customers' operations also know who would be a good fit)
  • Publishing technical content and case studies: Share implementation case studies and technical articles from the field to reach people who think "I want to work in this kind of environment"
  • Converting contractors to full-time: Work together on a small project to evaluate each other, then convert to a full-time position

The fourth approach is especially effective for roles with few candidates. However, it requires handling customer information properly and clarifying the scope of the contract, so work out the terms with your legal and information security departments before proceeding.

How does the FDE hiring strategy change based on candidates' years of experience, current role, English proficiency, and your company's size?

For FDE hiring, make judgments first using the three axes from FDE Soken (problem definition, independent implementation, and production operations), and use years of experience and English proficiency as prerequisites for fine-tuning.

Key points by combination of years of experience and current role

For FDE candidates, rather than years of experience itself, look at whether they have experience covering the full cycle from customer interviews to production operations in their current role.

Table 5: Selection steps to emphasize by current role (FDE Soken comparison axes, as of November 2026)

Current roleWhat to verify as experienceSelection step to emphasize
Lead at a development vendor or SIerWhether they have continued improving after delivery2. Field case interview
In-house SE / information systemsWhether they have produced a prototype in a short period3. Technical implementation
Data engineer / analystWhether they have delivered something to users as an application3. Technical implementation
Solutions engineer / pre-salesWhether they have taken responsibility for production operations beyond the demo3. Technical implementation and questions about operations
Startup engineerWhether they have reached agreement with decision-makers at large enterprises4. Customer role-play

Even candidates with short careers are worth advancing if they can concretely describe experience taking a single project through the full cycle. Conversely, if a candidate has many years of experience but can only talk about results within their own implementation scope, evaluate them carefully in the case interview.

What changes based on English proficiency and company size

In FDE job descriptions, place English under preferred requirements. What changes with company size is whether you can specify the assigned customers and accompanying support structure in the JD.

English proficiency is only necessary when the assigned customers or the product development team are in English-speaking regions. Raise it to a mandatory requirement only in that case; otherwise keep it as a preferred requirement so you don't narrow your candidate pool.

What changes with company size is the onboarding structure after joining. In organizations with multiple existing FDEs or customer-facing staff, you can implement the 90-day accompaniment plan (described later) as is. On the other hand, small organizations hiring their first FDE often have no senior colleague to accompany them. In that case, consider starting with contract work, or explicitly designate a sales or customer success person as the accompanying mentor and state this in the JD as well.

Priorities when requirements conflict

When conflicts arise, such as a candidate with strong English but shallow customer-facing experience, prioritize the three axes from FDE Soken over English proficiency or years of experience.

The order of judgment recommended by FDE Soken is as follows.

  • Problem definition: Can they ask the right questions when information is insufficient (verify in the field case interview)
  • Independent implementation: Can they build something usable on their own from messy data (verify in the technical implementation)
  • Production operations: Can they talk concretely about operations and handover (verify in the technical implementation and final interview)
  • Prerequisites: English proficiency, industry knowledge, years of experience (add points as preferred requirements)

For candidates who are weak in one of items 1-3 but strong in item 4, it's worth keeping the option of working with them on a contract basis before making a decision.

What is the FDE selection process and what should you evaluate?

Structure FDE selection in four stages, and evaluate three aspects—field cases, customer interaction, and implementation—separately using an evaluation rubric.

The standard four steps and what to look for at each

If you clarify what to evaluate at each step of FDE selection, variation in evaluations across interviewers becomes smaller.

Table 6: Four steps of FDE selection (compiled by FDE Soken, as of November 2026)

StepFormatMain focusApproximate duration
1. Casual interviewOnlineWhether understanding of the role aligns, motivation to interact with customers30-45 minutes
2. Field case interviewDialogue formatProblem definition in ambiguous situations, prioritization60 minutes
3. Technical implementationTake-home assignment or pair workAbility to carry something through alone, explaining design decisions60-90 minutes
4. Customer role-play / final interviewIn-person recommendedAbility to explain to customers, how to deliver bad news60 minutes

For step 3, a format of "hand them messy data and have them shape it into something usable" is closer to actual FDE work than algorithm tests. What you're evaluating is not the elegance of the implementation, but whether they can explain what they discarded along the way and what they prioritized.

Start the case interview with "insufficient information"

In FDE case interviews, deliberately present problems with missing information. What questions the candidate asks is the most important basis for judgment.

For example, present it at a level like "A retail chain has asked for help understanding store congestion levels. Where do you start?" Strong candidates will first ask: who is having the problem, what is currently being used to measure it, how success will be measured, and what is needed by when. On the other hand, candidates who immediately start discussing technical architecture tend to struggle as FDEs, even if they are strong at product development.

Always include "someone who talks to customers" among the interviewers

Include sales or customer success staff in FDE selection so that evaluation from the customer's perspective is always part of the process.

When only engineers conduct the interviews, evaluations get skewed toward technical depth. Including employees who actually face customers as interviewers adds the judgment of "could I put this person in front of a customer?", which improves accuracy. Use a shared evaluation sheet, and assign each interviewer a perspective to observe in advance, so that hiring discussions don't devolve into a mere exchange of impressions.

Table 7: Outline of the FDE evaluation sheet (compiled by FDE Soken, as of November 2026)

Evaluation perspectiveAssigned interviewerBehaviors to observe
Problem definitionEngineering managerThe order of questions that verify assumptions, and how priorities are set
Self-driven implementationFDE or senior engineerExplanation of what was discarded and what was kept
Production operationsFDE or SREConcreteness of incident response and handover
Customer handlingSales / Customer SuccessHow bad news is communicated, and how the customer's circumstances are received

How should hired FDEs be retained and evaluated?

Retaining FDEs depends on whether you can present a 90-day onboarding plan and a four-layer evaluation covering outcomes, delivery, asset creation, and team contribution in a development plan.

Structure the first 90 days in stages: "observe, do, own"

Rather than suddenly assigning a customer to an FDE alone, moving gradually from shadowing to independent ownership stabilizes their ramp-up.

Table 8: FDE 30/60/90-day plan (compiled by FDE Soken, as of November 2026)

PeriodMain activitiesTarget milestones
Days 1–30Shadowing existing projects, learning the product and data structuresAble to explain the customer's business flow in their own words
Days 31–60Handling small improvement requests independently, taking part in some customer meetingsAble to see a small request through to release
Days 61–90Owning one project as the primary owner (with manager reviews)Able to complete a full cycle from problem definition to operational handover

From on-the-ground experience, skipping this staged design and handing over a project solo from day one means the person can manage well enough on their own, so knowledge never gets shared within the company. As a result, siloing and burnout tend to progress simultaneously. It is closer to reality to view the shadowing period not as an "education cost" but as an "investment in reducing quality variance."

Design evaluation across four layers: outcomes, delivery, asset creation, and team contribution

Evaluating FDEs solely on customer outcomes leaves them at the mercy of the luck of their assigned projects. Combine reproducible process metrics across four layers.

Table 9: The four layers of FDE evaluation (compiled by FDE Soken, as of November 2026)

LayerExample metricsNotes
OutcomesImprovement in customer business metrics, continued usage, emergence of additional requestsAffected by project scale, so not used on its own
DeliveryDays to first value delivery, presence or absence of major post-release incidentsEasy to compare across teams
Asset creationProvision of generalized features, templates, and documentationMost important for preventing siloing
Team contributionSharing project knowledge, reviewing other FDEs' work, feedback to the productEasily buried under individual results, so evaluate it explicitly

In particular, if "asset creation" is not included in evaluation, FDEs tend to accumulate one-off implementations tailored to the customer in front of them. Making the practice of feeding customer-specific implementations back into the core product an evaluation target also keeps the relationship between the FDE organization and the product organization healthy.

The key to retention is presenting a career path

Since FDEs handle customer relations, implementation, and operations alone, showing the next career options early helps prevent turnover.

By the nature of the work, FDE workloads tend to be uneven. If you can show "where they can go from here" at the time of joining, they find it easier to accept the immediate workload. Common paths include leading the FDE team, becoming a product manager, a solutions architect, or deepening expertise in a specific industry. Whichever path they take, the deep understanding of customer sites gained as an FDE becomes a strength.

Whether you can articulate this career path at the hiring stage directly reflects the quality of your requirements definition. If the JD, selection process, onboarding, evaluation, and career path are connected as a single line, FDE hiring becomes "difficult but reproducible."

How should job seekers use these FDE hiring criteria?

Engineers aiming to become FDEs should prepare, along FDE Soken's three axes, to talk about past projects in the order of "problem definition, self-driven implementation, production operations."

If a company's selection process resembles the four stages in this article, your resume will communicate better with "whose problem was it and what were they struggling with," "what did you discard and what did you build," and "how did you hand over operations" rather than a list of technologies used.
