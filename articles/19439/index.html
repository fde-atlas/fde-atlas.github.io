Minna Systems Supports Win Carrier Inc. as Its First FDE Project in Gifu Prefecture

Original title: みんなシステムズ、岐阜県でのFDE第1号案件としてウィンキャリア株式会社を支援

プレスリリース・ニュースリリース配信シェアNo.1｜PR TIMES · 2026-10-09 · 9 min read

Summary

Minna Systems' first FDE project in Gifu Prefecture built a monitoring-visit app for And Support, a care consultation office run by Win Career. Engineers embedded on-site for two days discovered an unspoken pain point: staff made repeat visits solely to collect signatures. The resulting iPad app records conversations, drafts reports via the Claude API, and captures signatures on the spot, cutting roughly two hours of paperwork per case to minor edits. The release also notes why few care-support systems exist: low per-case fees leave little IT budget. Minna Systems launched its FDE Japan platform in August 2026, aiming to cover all 47 prefectures.

Key points

  • Engineers found the signature revisit problem only by shadowing staff on-site.
  • App handles recording, AI-drafted reports, and on-the-spot signatures on one iPad.
  • Paperwork per case dropped from about two hours to minor corrections.
  • Care-support software is scarce because low fees limit IT spending.
  • FDE Japan platform launched August 2026, targeting all 47 prefectures.

What engineers discovered by accompanying workers in the welfare field was a challenge that had never been raised: "repeat visits just to collect signatures"

Minna Systems Co., Ltd. (Head office: Sumida-ku, Tokyo; Representative Director: Yuki Noda) has developed and introduced an app that streamlines monitoring (regular visits to check on users' current status) at "And Support," a consultation support office operated by Win Carrier Co., Ltd. (Head office: Gifu City, Gifu Prefecture; Representative Director: Kumiko Niwa), as its first FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) project in Gifu Prefecture. FDE is a development approach in which engineers work directly at customers' sites and handle everything from identifying challenges to implementation.

The initial request was "automating report creation." However, by actually visiting the site, engineers discovered a challenge that had not been mentioned in the request: "repeat visits made solely to obtain signatures." During their two-day on-site stay, they implemented a system that allows everything from recording to report creation and signature collection to be completed in a single visit.

Despite being called "consultation support," the job actually involves a great deal of paperwork

Consultation support is a coordinating role: listening to users' wishes, proposing suitable services, and preparing documents for submission to the government. It is a position similar to that of care managers in elderly welfare.

Although it is called "consultation," the workload is actually heavily weighted toward paperwork. Each consultation worker at And Support is responsible for 100 to 150 clients. Visits take place once every three months, or in some cases once every six months, so workers have no choice but to rely on records rather than memory.

However, when workers focus on taking records, they lose the capacity to listen to what is being said in front of them. Both listening and record-keeping are indispensable. This has long been the difficulty facing the consultation support field.

Moreover, document preparation after returning from a visit took about two hours per case, even with no other tasks at hand.

No app suited to And Support's operations could be found

To reduce the burden on consultation workers, Win Carrier Co., Ltd. searched for business apps. While the welfare industry has numerous business apps, none specialized in consultation support work could be found.

An option to repurpose an app designed for employment support facilities for consultation support was also considered, but because the document structures differed and manual data transfer would be required, it was judged that work efficiency could not be significantly improved, and the idea was abandoned.

The scarcity of systems for consultation support is thought to stem from the industry's revenue structure. Consultation support has low per-case compensation rates, making it difficult to generate profit even when serving many clients. As a result, there is little budget available for expenses such as systems, and services requiring ongoing subscription fees are hard to adopt.

Engineers went to the field to "automate report creation"

Against this backdrop, Minna Systems was consulted. The initial request was to "automate the part of creating reports from interview content."

From Minna Systems, two engineers from the Gifu satellite office and Tokyo headquarters, along with Representative Director Noda, joined the site, and a consultation worker recreated an actual monitoring session (checking on a user's status and providing consultation).

What was discovered on-site: "repeat visits just for signatures"

By observing the on-site work up close, engineers learned that after preparing documents, workers were making another visit solely to obtain the user's signature. The workflow for each case was as follows:

  • Visit and conduct the interview
  • Return to the office and prepare documents (about 2 hours)
  • Visit again, have the user review the content, and collect the signature

Since each step took place on a different day, even when things went smoothly it took at least three days. Because appointments with other clients were also scheduled, completing everything within a week was considered fast. The trip to the visit location could take 30 to 40 minutes each way, and on some days spent going just for a signature, workers could not even meet the client.

This process was not part of the original request. For those in the field, it had become second nature over the years as "just the way things are," so it was never raised as a challenge. It was only by walking through the actual workflow together with the engineers that this challenge came to light.

The engineers proposed, "We can complete everything, including the signature, on the spot," and during the two-day on-site stay, they implemented an app covering recording, report creation, and signature collection.

From recording to report and signature, all with a single iPad

  • Conversations during monitoring are recorded on an iPad
  • The AI (Claude API) transcribes the recording and automatically creates a draft report
  • The consultation worker reviews and edits the report before finalizing it
  • If the user reviews the report on the spot, they can sign directly on the iPad
  • If edits are needed later, the same screen can be accessed from the office computer for editing and printing

Turning the two hours of document preparation into time for other work

Since the app was introduced, consultation workers have expressed their delight: "I just made a few edits after getting back from the visit, and it was already done!" Document preparation that used to take about two hours per case now requires only minor edits, freeing up time for other tasks.

Even after the introduction on August 28, 2026, improvements have continued, incorporating feedback from actual use in the field.

Improving work efficiency also helps secure talent. In a survey conducted by the Independent Administrative Institution Welfare and Medical Service Agency on organizations operating disability welfare services, 52.6% of offices responded that staff are "insufficient" (as of January 2024). As this talent shortage continues, reducing on-site workloads and creating an environment where workers can focus on people-facing work is an important perspective when considering talent retention.

What is FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Development that starts with finding challenges in the field

It is a development approach in which engineers visit customers' sites, experience the actual work firsthand to identify challenges, and use technologies such as AI to shape solutions on the spot.

Since it is the engineer's role to consider what can be achieved with which technologies and make proposals, customers can get started simply by describing what they are struggling with.

In August 2026, Minna Systems launched the FDE platform "FDE Japan" (https://fde-japan.jp/) with the mission of "creating a state in which every company in all 47 prefectures can access the latest AI technology through FDE."

Client Testimonial

Kumiko Niwa, Representative Director, Win Career Co., Ltd.

No matter how hard I searched, I couldn't find an app suited to care coordination work. Even when using some app, tasks like transferring data still arose, so it never solved the problem fundamentally. I consulted with others in the same industry, but they all seemed to be struggling with the same issues.

When a care coordinator who had actually used the app created by Minna Systems showed me the documents and said, "Look, I just made a few corrections after coming back from a visit, and it's already done," I was truly delighted. When introducing the app to others in the industry, I tell them, "It's like magic."

Since each person's workload has become lighter, I believe we can now bring more people into care coordination work going forward.

Unlike facility operations, care coordination work has no constraint requiring you to commute to a specific location before starting work.

With digitalization having advanced significantly through this initiative, ways of working that are not bound by location or time become achievable.

Even when we post job openings, the image of heavy paperwork and take-home work precedes us, making it difficult to welcome the next generation of talent. However, I feel that if people think, "I won't be buried in paperwork, and I can value time facing our clients," many would be inclined to give it a try.

Also, care coordination work centers on visiting clients. Now that documents can be completed on-site during visits, the need to return to the office just for paperwork has decreased, enabling even more flexible ways of working.
