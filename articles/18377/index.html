Leveraging External FDEs vs. Developing Them In-House | 5 Criteria for Executive Decision-Making

Original title: 外部FDEを活用する vs 社内で育成する｜経営判断のための5基準

FDX株式会社 · 2026-05-06 · 11 min read

Summary

This article from FDX株式会社 argues that choosing between hiring external FDEs and training internal ones is a false dilemma; a hybrid model usually works best. It proposes five decision criteria: urgency, whether FDE skills will be reused, availability of internal candidates, knowledge-transfer risk, and 18–24 month total cost of ownership. Its recommended three-phase model spans 18 months: external FDEs lead for months 0–6 while 1–2 internal candidates shadow them, co-lead in months 6–12, then internal staff take over with external FDEs limited to reviews. It cites cost ranges (senior external FDEs at ¥3–5 million monthly), 24-month TCO simulations favoring hybrid, phase-specific KPIs, and FAQs on pricing, training time, and retention.

Key points

  • External vs. internal FDE is a false choice; hybrid usually performs best.
  • Five criteria decide the model: urgency, skill reusability, talent market, transfer risk, TCO.
  • Recommended model: external-led (0–6 months), co-led (6–12), internal-led (12–18+).
  • 24-month TCO simulation shows hybrid balances cost and internal capability best.
  • Each phase needs explicit KPIs and a defined graduation date for external FDEs.

Five decision axes determine which pattern is optimal.

Summary (3 lines)

  • "Bringing in external FDEs vs. developing in-house" is not a binary choice. The third option, the "hybrid FDE" model, tends to produce the best results.
  • There are five decision axes: urgency, domain generality, talent market, handover risk, and 18-24 month TCO.
  • FDX recommends a three-stage transition model: "Phase 1 (0-6 months): external FDE-led + 1-2 in-house candidates shadowing → Phase 2 (6-12 months): co-leadership → Phase 3 (12-18 months+): in-house-led + external review."

1. Conclusion (upfront) | Hybrid is the optimal solution

Many companies considering FDE introduction agonize over the binary choice of "external or in-house," but the hybrid model produces the best results in practice.

The reasons are as follows.

  • External only: Implementation is fast, but no in-house assets remain. Operational continuity after the FDE leaves is low.
  • In-house only: In-house capability develops, but immediate business impact is slow (1-2 years for talent development). Management cannot wait that long.
  • Hybrid: External FDEs deliver immediate business impact while in-house candidates develop alongside them. Best of both worlds.

However, there are cases where hybrid is not the optimal solution. Check the decision axes.

2. Decision axis 1: Urgency (severity of business issues × time constraints)

Question: By when is business impact needed?

  • Within 3 months: External FDE-led is essential. In-house development cannot be ready in time.
  • 6-12 months: Hybrid is optimal. External FDEs lead while in-house candidates run in parallel.
  • 18 months or more: In-house-led is possible. There is time to develop talent carefully.

Points management often overlooks

Many cases involve writing "business transformation with AI from next fiscal year" into the mid-term plan, but delaying implementation, which ultimately raises urgency. There is often a large gap between management's and frontline staff's perception of urgency, so alignment among multiple stakeholders is necessary.

3. Decision axis 2: Domain generality (will FDE skills be used going forward?)

Question: Is there a prospect of continuously using the skill sets required of FDEs going forward?

  • Continuous use (broad business domains, multiple projects planned) → High investment value in in-house development. Hybrid or in-house-led.
  • One-off (a single specific project) → External FDE alone is acceptable. Training investment cannot be recouped.

Trends by industry

  • Manufacturing, finance, healthcare: Ongoing AX/business transformation needs mean high in-house value. Suited to hybrid or in-house-led.
  • Retail and services: Project-based, spot usage is common; external FDE-led cases are frequent.
  • IT and telecommunications: Constant demand for improving in-house services; in-house-led is advantageous long term.

4. Decision axis 3: Talent market (are there candidates in-house?)

Question: Are there in-house candidates who could become FDEs?

FDE candidates are typically people like the following.

  • 5+ years of software engineering experience + interest in business improvement or business planning
  • 10+ years of SE/consulting experience + willingness to write code
  • Data scientist + product development experience
  • Mid-level engineer + interest in management agendas and ability to communicate

When there are no in-house candidates

  • In-house development has no foundation and becomes a long-term investment on the scale of 5-7 years
  • In the short term, pursue external FDE-led work in parallel with acquiring FDE talent from the hiring market
  • FDE talent is scarce in the hiring market. Annual salary ranges of ¥12-25 million are typical

When there are 1-2 in-house candidates

  • The hybrid model is ideal. Have them shadow external FDEs and stand up the capability in 12-18 months.

When there are 3 or more in-house candidates

  • In-house-led + external FDEs used in a limited role for review and mentoring is the realistic solution.

5. Decision axis 4: Handover risk (concern about knowledge flowing outside)

Question: How do you evaluate the risk of passing your company's business know-how to external parties?

  • High (highly confidential operations, sources of competitive advantage, regulated operations) → In-house-led or limited external FDE use (strict NDAs, restricted data access)
  • Medium (general business transformation) → Hybrid is possible. Clarify IP and data handling in contracts.
  • Low (public operations, areas with no issue even if externalized) → External FDE-led is fine.

Contractual measures

  • Strict confidentiality obligations (NDAs)
  • Clear attribution of intellectual property rights
  • Clauses prohibiting parallel contracts with competitors
  • Business-unit-level management of data access permissions

6. Decision axis 5: Cost structure (short-term vs. long-term TCO)

Short-term costs (6-12 months)

  • External FDE-led: ¥3-5 million per month × duration = immediate high cost
  • Hybrid: External FDE ¥2-4 million + in-house candidate personnel costs (existing)
  • In-house-led: Hiring or training costs (salary range + training period)

Long-term TCO (18-36 months)

  • External FDE-led: External FDE costs keep accumulating from the third cycle onward
  • Hybrid: Switches to in-house-led after 12-18 months, drastically reducing external costs
  • In-house-led: slower to start, but significantly lower costs from the second cycle onward

In terms of TCO, the hybrid model is often the cheapest

When simulating the 24-month TCO of three models for a generative AI project involving business process redesign, the results are as follows.

Model12 months24 monthsIn-house capability assets
External FDE-led¥40–60 million¥80–120 millionLimited
Hybrid¥35–50 million¥55–80 millionHigh
In-house-led¥20–30 million (with delayed results)¥45–65 millionMaximum

The figures vary depending on project scale, but the hybrid model often offers the best balance in 24-month TCO.

7. Diagram: Decision Flowchart

The flow evaluates the five decision axes in order and arrives at one of three recommended patterns, as follows.

  • Urgency (within 3 months / 6–12 months / 18 months or more)
  • Domain reusability (ongoing / one-off)
  • Availability of internal candidates
  • Transfer risk (high / medium / low)
  • Importance of 24-month TCO

Destinations

  • 100% external FDE-led: high urgency × one-off domain × no internal candidates × low risk
  • Hybrid: medium urgency × ongoing domain × 1–2 internal candidates × medium risk
  • 100% in-house-led: low urgency × ongoing domain × 3 or more internal candidates × high risk

8. FDX Recommendation: 3-Phase Hybrid Model

The hybrid FDE model recommended by FDX Inc. is a 3-phase structure that transitions from external-led to in-house-led over 18 months.

Phase 1: External FDE-led (0–6 months)

External FDE: leads all decision-making, implementation, and stakeholder management Internal candidates: 1–2 members accompany at all times, participating in meetings, creating documentation, and handling simple implementation

KPIs

  • Business impact metrics (degree of KPI improvement)
  • Skill assessments of internal candidates (monthly)
  • Building a network of decision-makers within the client company

What not to do: using internal candidates as "handy errand-runners." Assign senior-level members to work alongside the FDE.

Phase 2: Co-led (6–12 months)

External FDE: leads strategic decisions, high-difficulty implementation, and executive dialogue. Delegates day-to-day decisions to internal candidates Internal candidates: lead day-to-day project advancement, on-site interviews, and medium-difficulty implementation

KPIs

  • Ratio of tasks internal candidates can handle independently (target: 70% or more)
  • Continuous improvement of business impact metrics
  • Decision-making authority of internal candidates within the client company

What not to do: letting the external FDE become an "outside voice." Continue to engage in executive dialogue and strategic decisions.

Phase 3: In-house-led (12–18 months and beyond)

External FDE: limited to 1–2 reviews per month, consultation on difficult issues, and support for launching subsequent projects Internal candidates: take full leadership, positioned as in-house operations owners

KPIs

  • Dependence on external FDE (target: 10 hours or less per month)
  • Number of decisions led in-house
  • Ability of internal candidates to launch the next project

What not to do: keeping the external FDE on "just in case" without clear criteria for completing the transfer. Have documented graduation criteria.

9. Hybrid Model Operations Checklist

Before starting

  • Alignment with management (investment approval for the overall FDE model)
  • Selection of internal candidates (at least 1, ideally 2)
  • Clarification of the target business domain
  • Definition of the 18-month plan and KPIs for each phase
  • Contract arrangements (a combination of quasi-delegation + success-based compensation)

Phase 1 (0–6 months)

  • Internal candidates' business participation rate of 80% or more
  • Monthly skill assessments
  • Documented pairing structure between the FDE and internal candidates

Phase 2 (6–12 months)

  • Internal candidates' independent task ratio rising month over month
  • Internal candidates explaining proactively in management reviews

Phase 3 (12–18 months+)

  • Documented transfer completion criteria (specific KPIs, skill checklists)
  • Fixed graduation date for the external FDE
  • Team plan for the next project

Common to all phases

  • Clear in-house career path for internal candidates (formal institutionalization of FDE-type positions)
  • Evaluation system aligned with the FDE model (business impact evaluation rather than hours-based evaluation)

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1. What are the market rates for external FDEs?

A. Senior FDEs (10+ years of experience with a track record in business process redesign) cost ¥3–5 million per month, mid-level FDEs (5–10 years) ¥2–3.5 million, and junior FDEs (3–5 years, in support roles) around ¥1.5–2.5 million. For team engagements, a 3–5 member configuration at a monthly range of ¥10–20 million is typical.

Q2. How long does it take to develop in-house FDEs?

A. For those from SE or consulting backgrounds, it takes 12–18 months to reach mid-level FDE proficiency and 24–36 months to reach senior FDE level. This assumes constant accompaniment by an external FDE. Developing them through self-study and OJT alone takes 3–5 years.

Q3. For how many months should an external FDE be engaged?

A. It depends on project scale, but for the hybrid model, 18 months is standard (Phase 1: 6 months, Phase 2: 6 months, Phase 3: 6 months). Even for short-term projects, securing at least 6 months is essential, otherwise business process redesign, implementation, and transfer cannot all be completed.

Q4. Won't in-house-trained FDEs leave the company?

A. FDE talent commands high market value, so attrition risk is a realistic concern. Countermeasures: (1) formally institutionalize FDE-type positions internally and align salary ranges with industry standards, (2) give them authority as positions reporting directly to management, (3) support participation in cross-industry FDE communities, (4) design retention around engagement rather than lock-in.

Q5. Can we start in-house development candidates with just one person?

A. You can, but a one-person setup is high risk. Reasons: (1) single point of failure (if they leave, everything disappears), (2) one-on-one pairing between the FDE and the in-house candidate becomes too close, lowering handover quality, (3) high mental load on the in-house candidate. We recommend proceeding with a team of at least two, ideally three.

Q6. What if we can't wait 18 months for the hybrid three-phase approach?

A. For urgent projects, an accelerated plan is possible: shortening Phase 1 to three months and compressing Phases 2 and 3. However, there is a risk of lower transfer quality. At minimum, Phase 1's business impact verification plus Phase 2's co-leadership period should be secured.

Q7. What is the risk that even after bringing in an external FDE, internal resistance prevents progress?

A. A realistic risk. Countermeasures: (1) clear executive sponsorship (at the division head level or above), (2) involve internal key people first (business-side leaders and IT-savvy supporters), (3) create small early success stories in the early part of Phase 1 to build internal trust, (4) pair the external FDE with in-house candidates to avoid the feeling of "outside imposition."

  • "External vs. in-house" is not a binary choice; the hybrid model is the third option
  • Determine the optimal pattern using five decision axes (urgency, domain generality, talent market, transfer risk, TCO)
  • FDX recommends an 18-month three-phase transition model (external-led → co-led → in-house-led)
  • The condition for success is having clear KPIs and "things not to do" for each phase
  • To retain in-house development assets, institutionalizing FDE-type positions internally and establishing evaluation systems are essential
