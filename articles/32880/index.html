How to hire a forward deployed engineer

Kernavique · 2026-10-11 · 17 min read

Summary

Kernavique's guide explains how to hire a forward deployed engineer, arguing that one title covers several distinct jobs that must be distinguished before writing an ad. Drawing on postings from OpenAI, Palantir, Databricks, Scale AI and others, plus Bloomberry's analysis of 1,000 listings, it offers an adaptable job description template, a four-part interview loop with a scorecard, pay benchmarks, sourcing advice, and evidence that these roles are permanent hires. It stresses testing both production coding and customer-facing skills like scoping messy problems.

Key points

  • Bloomberry's 1,000-posting analysis: 60% builder roles (70-90% coding), 30% sales-engineer-like, 10% internal tools.
  • Screen for production code, Python (66% of postings), stated travel shares, platform skills, and clearance eligibility for federal work.
  • Suggested interview loop: realistic coding, scoping an underspecified problem, a customer role-play, and a hands-on platform task.
  • Most candidates come from software engineering (45% of 100 profiles); Palantir grows its own via new-grad and internship roles.
  • Across 526 postings no contract roles appeared; base salary ranges span $88,970 (Salesforce) to $376,000 (Figma).

To hire a forward deployed engineer, hire for two things at once: production code and customer delivery. Decide first which version of the role you mean, because one title covers several jobs. Write the ad around what the engineer will own, the travel, the platform and the pay basis. Test hands-on coding and the scoping of a messy customer problem. Look first at software engineers who have moved toward customers, and plan a permanent hire.

One title, several jobs

A forward deployed engineer builds production software inside a customer’s environment and stays until people use it. OpenAI’s San Francisco ad (published September 11, 2026) puts discovery, scoping, system design, build and production rollout in one person, and counts feedback that changes product and model roadmaps as part of success. Palantir’s Forward Deployed AI Engineer ad likens the job to “a hands-on AI startup CTO”.

We could not re-read OpenAI’s ad on November 20, 2026. Everything we quote from it on this page is as read on October 11, 2026.

The title is used loosely. Bloomberry, which analyzed 1,000 postings, says companies use it “to describe three different jobs” (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026). By its own classification, which has no stated method:

  • Builder, 60% of jobs: “70-90% coding, 30-50% travel”.
  • “Sales Engineer+”, 30% of jobs: “30-40% coding, <20% travel”.
  • Internal tools builder, 10% of jobs.

Decide which one you are hiring before you write the ad. The builder needs a software engineer’s bar. For how employers’ own ads separate the role from the solutions architect, the sales engineer and the product software engineer, see forward deployed engineer vs solutions engineer.

What to screen for

  • Production code and customer delivery, together. OpenAI asks for 5+ years “of engineering or technical deployment experience that includes customer-facing work”, production code across frontend and backend, and experience building or deploying LLM-powered systems (ad published September 11, 2026). Palantir asks for past LLM work, ML basics (“Evaluation, Training, Problem Decomposition”) and strong coding.
  • Python. It appears in 66% of the postings Bloomberry analyzed (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026).
  • Travel. In ads we read on October 11, 2026, OpenAI asks for up to 50% travel, Palantir up to 25% and Databricks 20%. Agree it in the first conversation.
  • Platform skills. Databricks’ Sr. Forward Deployed Engineer ad lists “Databricks Certification”. Foundry ads from Booz Allen Hamilton and Accenture Federal Services ask for named Foundry work, such as ontology development, Workshop and data pipelines.
  • Clearance for federal work. Booz Allen’s Foundry ad in Arlington, Virginia, requires the ability to obtain a Secret clearance. In the job feeds we read on November 20, 2026, 63 of 298 US postings titled forward deployed, at 11 employers, mention a security clearance in the ad text. See cleared forward deployed engineer roles.

A job description you can adapt

This is our template. It is built from what employers put in their own ads, and it is advice, not any employer’s wording. Text in square brackets is yours to fill in. The note after each line names the ads we drew on and when we read them.

What the engineer will own. You will take [the customer problem] from discovery through build to production use, inside the customer’s environment. Name the stages. OpenAI’s ad puts discovery, scoping, system design, build and production rollout in one person (read October 11, 2026). Scale AI’s Frontier Agents Engineer ad says its engineers “architect, integrate, deploy, and operate production AI systems” (read November 20, 2026).

The split of code and customer work. About [share] of your week is writing production code. The rest is spent with customers. Give a number, because the title does not. Bloomberry’s builder type is “70-90% coding” and its second type “30-40% coding” (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026). Ramp’s engineering blog says of its team: “We spend most of our time building features, many of which are shipped in the core product.” (no date shown; read November 20, 2026).

Travel. Expect travel to customer sites [share] of the time. Write a figure, not “as needed”. Databricks’ ad says “Travel to customers 20% of the time”. Scale AI’s Public Sector ad says 25% travel, with up to 50% as necessary. Palantir’s US Government ad in Washington, D.C., asks only for “Willingness and interest to travel as needed.” All three were read on November 20, 2026.

Platform. You will work in [languages] on [platform]. [Certification] is [required or preferred]. Databricks’ ad names Python, Scala and JavaScript/TypeScript, and lists “Databricks Certification”. Scale AI’s Public Sector ad asks for “Python and TypeScript (or comparable modern languages)”. Both were read on November 20, 2026.

Clearance. This role [needs no security clearance, needs an active clearance at a named level, or needs eligibility to obtain one]. Say which. Palantir’s US Government ad asks for “Active US Security Clearance at or above the Top Secret level or willingness to obtain/upgrade to or above Top Secret.” Scale AI’s Public Sector ad asks for “An active TS/SCI clearance, or eligibility to obtain one.” Both were read on November 20, 2026. No ad we read that day says the employer will sponsor a clearance.

Pay range and its basis. The range for this role is [low] to [high], and it is [base salary or on-target earnings]. [Equity and bonus] are [added on top or not offered]. Say what the range counts. Of 42 US ads we read on November 20, 2026, 24 label the range as base, 16 give a salary range without saying base and 2 quote on-target earnings. Split the range by level and location if yours differ, as Scale AI does. Figma’s ad gives one range, $153,000 to $376,000, with no levels or locations broken out.

How success is judged. In your first [period], success means [an outcome the customer can see]. OpenAI’s ad counts feedback that changes product and model roadmaps as part of success (read October 11, 2026). Databricks’ ad expects engineers to “complete projects according to specification with exceptional customer empathy”. Ramp says: “We tie our team metrics to our customers and business outcomes as closely as possible.” Both were read on November 20, 2026.

One check before you publish. Bloomberry found that exactly 0% of the postings it analyzed were quota-carrying. If you plan to set a sales quota, you are describing a different job.

How strong teams interview

Few employers publish how they interview for this role. Two public sources help. Our forward deployed engineer interview guide sets out what each employer publishes and what is third-party.

Anthropic’s careers page (no date shown; re-read November 20, 2026, with the same wording) describes its technical interviews in general, not this role: “For technical roles, we use live coding tools like Colab and CodeSignal.” Applicants can look things up, and “We care about what you can do, not where you learned to do it.” Where it makes sense, Anthropic invites applicants to use Claude. The lesson: make the coding round look like the job, and judge the work, not the pedigree.

Palantir’s certification tiers separate knowing a platform from using it. A Palantir Community post of July 27, 2026, describes the Associate certifications as “timed, open-book, multiple-choice exams, valid for two years”, and the Specialist level as “Hands-on, live exams in a real Foundry and AIP environment”. The post does not say whether its author is Palantir staff. For Foundry work, ask which tier an engineer holds, and test the way the Specialist tier does.

From what employers ask for in their ads, a loop that tests both halves of the job has four parts:

  • Hands-on coding on a realistic integration, with lookups allowed.
  • Scoping an underspecified customer problem. OpenAI puts discovery and scoping in the role; Palantir names problem decomposition.
  • A conversation with someone playing the customer, to see how the engineer explains trade-offs and pushes back.
  • For platform roles, a practical check in the platform itself.

An interview scorecard

This is our suggested scorecard. We built it from what employers ask for in their ads and the little they publish about interviewing. It is not any employer’s method.

What to test How to test it What good looks like
Production code A live exercise on a realistic integration, in a plain coding tool, with lookups allowed Working, readable code; fluent in basic syntax and standard libraries; can explain each choice
Scoping an unclear customer problem Give an underspecified problem and ask what the engineer would need to know before building Breaks the problem into parts; says what data is needed and what to ask the customer; proposes a first version and names what it leaves out
Customer conversation A conversation with someone playing the customer, with a requirement that should be challenged Explains trade-offs in plain words; pushes back with a reason; checks what the customer understood
Platform skill For platform roles, a practical task in the platform itself Completes the task hands-on, not from memory of the documentation
Clearance and travel fit Not a test. State the travel figure and any clearance requirement, and ask directly, early A clear yes to the stated travel; for cleared work, holds the clearance the ad names or meets its eligibility wording

Where the rows come from:

  • Production code. Anthropic’s careers page says applicants for technical roles can look things up, and asks them to be comfortable with basic syntax and standard libraries. That guidance is general, not specific to this role.
  • Scoping. OpenAI’s ad puts discovery and scoping in the role (read October 11, 2026). Palantir’s names problem decomposition.
  • Customer conversation. Ramp’s engineering blog says it assesses four things: drive and work ethic, engineering fundamentals, customer empathy and communication (read November 20, 2026). Two of the four belong to this row.
  • Platform skill. The Specialist tier in the Palantir Community post is a hands-on exam in a live environment.
  • Clearance and travel fit. The ads state both in very different ways, as the job description section shows.

Two notes on scoring. Ramp treats technical performance as “a bar to pass but avoid optimizing it further.” We suggest the same: mark the code row pass or fail, then choose among those who pass on the other rows.

Ramp also calls drive “the single best predictor of real-world performance.” The scorecard has no row for it, because it is hard to test in an hour. Ask instead for things the engineer built and carried to the point where people used them.

Where these engineers come from

Bloomberry looked at 100 LinkedIn profiles of current forward deployed engineers, without saying how they were chosen (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026). Their prior roles:

Prior role Share of the 100 profiles
Software engineer 45%
Solutions engineer or architect 22%
Data 15%
Technical consultant 10%
Founder or early employee 8%

Bloomberry’s reading is that most start as software engineers who develop customer-facing skills, not the other way around. Look first at engineers who have shipped production systems and already spend time with users. Solutions engineers and consultants are worth screening if they still write production code.

Palantir grows its own. It advertises a New Grad forward deployed software engineer role in New York at $135,000 to $145,000 a year, and a “Year at Palantir” internship for the role at $5,900 to $10,500 a month (Palantir, both read October 11, 2026).

Consulting firms are building Foundry benches too. In a press release of July 21, 2025, Deloitte said it has the largest cohort of trained and certified forward deployed engineers outside Palantir and expects to more than triple it. Palantir and Accenture Federal Services said they would train and certify Accenture Federal’s Data & AI team of 1,000 professionals on Foundry and AIP (Intelligence Community News, July 1, 2025). These firms are both a source of trained engineers and a competitor for them.

What employers publish on pay

Pay now has its own page. Forward deployed engineer pay by employer lists 42 US job ads from 31 employers, read on November 20, 2026, grouped by the pay basis each ad states. It is a convenience sample, not a market measure.

Three figures from it. The 24 ranges labeled base salary run from a lowest figure of $88,970 (Salesforce, national range) to a highest of $376,000 (Figma). Scale AI publishes three levels of one role, all base: $180,000 to $225,000 at 4+ years, $216,000 to $270,000 at 5+ years and $252,000 to $315,000 at 8+ years. Anthropic advertises a Staff Forward Deployed Engineer in New York City, San Francisco and Seattle at $280,000 to $320,000, labeled “Annual Salary” and not stated as base.

That last ad corrects this page. In October we said Anthropic listed the individual role only in Munich, Paris and London.

Compare offers on the same pay basis. Our forward deployed engineer role page covers the role in more detail.

Contract or permanent

On October 11, 2026, we checked the public job feeds of 62 AI and tech employers. None listed a forward deployed engineer role as contract or temporary. The contract and temporary postings we found were for operations and similar support work. We identified them by each feed’s employment-type field, or by “contract” or an hourly figure in the ad, so a contract role with neither marker would be missed.

On November 20, 2026, we read a wider list: the feeds of 177 employers, the same 62 among them. They carried 526 postings with “forward deployed” in the title, 298 of them with a US location. On the original 62 feeds the count was 308, against 312 in October.

The November check tested less than the October one. No posting titled forward deployed on an Ashby or Lever feed carried a contract, temporary or part-time employment type. Palantir’s internships are typed as internships. Greenhouse feeds have no employment-type field, so Greenhouse employers were not tested, and we did not repeat the search of ad text. For those employers the October finding stands as of October 11, 2026.

Both are convenience samples, but they point one way: these employers hire forward deployed engineers as permanent staff. Who is hiring forward deployed engineers lists the employers, and the forward deployed engineer hiring index for Q4 2026 tracks the counts.

So plan a permanent hire. If a deployment cannot wait, engineers on contract through their own companies can carry the work while the search runs.

How long it takes

There is no independent data on how long it takes to hire a forward deployed engineer. We found no employer-published or independent time-to-fill figure, only estimates from vendors that sell hiring services or interview preparation, which we do not repeat.

What you control is your own loop: how many rounds, how fast feedback goes back and how quickly you decide. If you run the search with Kernavique, we agree the shortlist timing with you at the brief call, based on the role and the market.

In practice

Write down which version of the role you need, then fill in the job description above: what the engineer will own, the split of code and customer work, the travel, the platform, any clearance and the pay basis. Build a loop that tests both code and customer work, and score it on the same rows for every applicant. Look first at software engineers who have moved toward customers, and benchmark the offer against the ranges in our pay by employer briefing on the same pay basis.

Questions

  • What is the difference between a forward deployed engineer and a sales engineer?
  • Mainly how much of the job is writing code. Bloomberry classes 60% of postings with the title as builders doing “70-90% coding” and 30% as a sales-engineer variant doing “30-40% coding” (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026). If the person will own production code, hire against the builder profile. Our briefing on forward deployed engineer vs solutions engineer compares the roles in employers’ own ads.
  • What should we screen for first?
  • Shipped production code and customer-facing delivery, together. OpenAI’s San Francisco ad (published September 11, 2026) asks for 5+ years of engineering or technical deployment experience that includes customer-facing work. Then check travel, platform skills and, for federal work, clearance.
  • What should a forward deployed engineer job description include?
  • Seven things: what the engineer will own, the split of code and customer work, travel as a figure, the platform and languages, any clearance requirement, the pay range with its basis, and how success is judged. Of 42 US ads we read on November 20, 2026, 18 did not state travel and 16 gave a salary range without saying whether it was base. Those are the gaps to close first.
  • Where do forward deployed engineers come from?
  • Most often from software engineering. In Bloomberry’s sample of 100 LinkedIn profiles, 45% were previously software engineers, 22% solutions engineers or architects, 15% in data, 10% technical consultants and 8% founders or early employees (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026). Bloomberry does not say how the profiles were chosen.
  • Should we hire on contract or permanently?
  • Plan a permanent hire. On October 11, 2026, none of the 62 AI and tech employers whose public job feeds we checked listed a forward deployed engineer role as contract or temporary. On November 20, 2026, across the feeds of 177 employers, no posting titled forward deployed on an Ashby or Lever feed carried a contract, temporary or part-time type; Greenhouse feeds have no such field and were not tested. If the work cannot wait, engineers on contract through their own companies can carry a deployment while the search runs.
  • How long does it take to hire a forward deployed engineer?
  • There is no independent data on it. We found no employer-published or independent time-to-fill figure, only estimates from vendors, which we do not repeat. If you run the search with Kernavique, we agree the shortlist timing with you at the brief call, based on the role and the market.
  • What does a forward deployed engineer earn?
  • It depends on the employer, the level and the pay basis. In 42 US job ads from 31 employers read on November 20, 2026, the 24 ranges labeled base salary ran from a lowest figure of $88,970 (Salesforce, national range) to a highest of $376,000 (Figma). Scale AI publishes three levels of one role, from $180,000 to $225,000 up to $252,000 to $315,000, all base. The sample is not a market measure.
