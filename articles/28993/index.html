NTT Data subsidiary launches "FDE-style consulting." Where "implementation-focused talent" stands today as it aims for 10 billion yen in revenue

Original title: NTTデータ子会社が仕掛ける「FDE型コンサルティング」。売上100億を目指す“実装する人材”の現在地

Business Insider Japan · 2026-09-09 · 5 min read

Summary

Fortience Consulting, an NTT Data group company formerly known as QUNIE, launched an "FDE-style consulting" model in June, positioning it as a core business with a revenue target of about 10 billion yen in five years. Consultants go beyond advising to build AI agents and applications themselves. The key difference from conventional agile-style support is reducing the "baton pass" between business-side and development-side teams, cutting miscommunication and speeding up delivery. Generative AI has lowered implementation barriers, enabling rapid on-site iteration, including on-premises handling of sensitive data. However, the company says FDE-style work suits only projects that transform frontline operations; roadmap planning and governance remain conventional, and core-system design is handled with SI partners like NTT Data's technology companies.

Key points

  • Fortience Consulting targets 10 billion yen revenue in five years from FDE-style consulting.
  • Main difference from agile support: fewer handoffs between business and development teams.
  • Generative AI enables on-site trial-and-error, including with sensitive on-premises data.
  • Not all AI projects use the FDE model; support format depends on project goals.
  • In practice, teams combine people with different specialties rather than one all-rounder.

There is a job type called “Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)” that embeds deeply in client companies' operations, handling everything from identifying challenges to system design and implementation. It is known for having been adopted by Palantir for many years, and in recent years AI companies have embraced it as well.

What happens when a consulting firm adopts this approach?

Fortience Consulting (formerly QUNIE), part of the NTT Data group, launched “FDE-style consulting” in June. It goes beyond understanding clients' business operations to building AI agents and applications. The company positions this as its core business and aims for revenue on the scale of 10 billion yen in five years.

However, what Fortience espouses is not “FDE” but “FDE-style consulting.” Do the consultants themselves write code? How does it differ from traditional accompaniment-style consulting or agile development?

We asked four people: Naoya Kikuyama, Representative Director and Senior Managing Executive Officer; Junichi Sudo, Executive Officer; Taishi Sato, Managing Director; and Yuki Taneda, Senior Consultant.

The difference from “accompaniment consulting + agile” is the “baton pass”

What Fortience envisions with the FDE style is an approach of entering the client's workplace, understanding the business, and building small, testing, and improving iteratively.

Looking only at this approach, the difference from traditional “accompaniment-style business consulting + agile development” is hard to see. Mr. Sato cited the “baton pass” between the business side and the development side as the difference.

According to Mr. Sato, in traditional agile-style support, business consultants define the issues and hand them to members skilled in development. The consultants also verify whether development is proceeding as intended, but the handoff from the business side to the development side remains.

In the FDE style, the person who understands the business and the person who implements it are the same individual, or on closely aligned teams. Mr. Sato explains that this reduces errors caused by the “telephone game” and also increases speed.

Reducing the “baton passes” from those who think to those who build is the major difference from traditional approaches.

That said, Fortience does not intend to run all AI projects in the FDE style.

Mr. Kikuyama says the FDE style is suited to cases where the business operations themselves are being transformed and the mechanisms for that are built together with AI agents, but including roadmap formulation and governance development, “calling everything FDE-style is a bit of a stretch.”

AI-based projects will keep increasing, but the form of support changes depending on the project's purpose.

AI has lowered the barrier to “building”

Behind the demand for the FDE style is also the fact that generative AI has lowered the barriers to implementation and trial and error.

Mr. Sudo, who covers the manufacturing sector, describes the difference from the pre-AI era as “things we couldn't do are now possible.”

For example, test cases that previously could only examine around 1,000 patterns due to time and computational resource constraints can now, with generative AI, test far more patterns. He says it has become easier to discuss long-tail and irregular events as well.

By running AI in on-premises environments, highly confidential information that cannot be sent to external clouds can now be used for inference without being taken outside.

What Mr. Sudo emphasizes is repeating AI-driven exploration on the client's premises. Rather than taking things back to consider, you test on the spot, look at the results, and test again. The growing need to run this short cycle is also part of the background behind demand for the FDE style.

How far do consultants go in building? Some projects are divided among four people

With the barrier to implementation lowered, the consultants' role has also changed. According to Mr. Kikuyama, in addition to hiring people strong in technology, more consultants who already have business knowledge are expanding their scope to the implementation side.

How far does the “implementation” that consultants handle in the FDE style extend?

According to Mr. Taneda, Fortience handles everything from designing the future business model to building AI agents and applications, and through operation and adoption. Within this scope, they often write code and use tools.

According to Fortience, for core systems and system design related to stable operation, they team up with SI companies (system integrators).

Simple logic is built on the Fortience side, while for designing production environments intended for many users and performance optimization, roles are sometimes divided with group technology companies such as NTT Data Advanced Technology.

In a project for a machinery manufacturer that Mr. Sudo introduced, they are building a system that structures corporate relationships and trends from internal and external information and uses AI to support decisions on proposal targets and investments. The Fortience side consists of about four people, dividing up preprocessing, inference, graph databases, and so on according to each person's specialty.

Does one person handle everything from business understanding to implementation, or are people with different specialties combined? Mr. Sudo answered, “In practice, it's the latter combination.”

In the aforementioned project, a person with manufacturing expertise who had also been involved in system development in the past handles the technical side. Ideally, in the future, one person would have both business and implementation capabilities, “that would really be best” (Mr. Sudo).
