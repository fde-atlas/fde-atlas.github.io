Salaries breaking NT$9 million to grab talent! Who are the FDEs that OpenAI is scrambling for? Why are they even more in demand than AI scientists?

The first half of the AI industry was a war of "models," but in the second half, the real battleground has shifted—to how to successfully deploy these powerful AI models in enterprise settings. AI giants like OpenAI and Anthropic stopped competing solely on parameters and benchmarks long ago; they have quietly pivoted their strategy: aggressively recruiting for a key role called the "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)."

These "amphibious engineers" who can go deep into customer sites and write production-grade code with their own hands are replacing pure AI scientists, becoming the last mile of bringing AI to market.

What is an FDE? The "mystery force" of AI deployment

The FDE emerged precisely to bridge the huge gap between "proof of concept" and "deployment at scale." They are not just technical talent—they can closely dissect enterprise needs and use AI to solve problems that "truly hurt."

OpenAI established its FDE team as early as early 2025, and expects to expand to more than 50 people. According to Indeed data, FDE job postings are growing by as much as 800% per month. Annual salaries reach US$220,000–280,000 (about NT$7.1–9 million), plus stock—potentially worth tens of millions.

Why are companies scrambling to hire them? Because they understand technology and people, and even better, business processes.

How powerful are these "amphibious" FDEs?

The FDE role originated as a military term and was popularized by the data company Palantir. They typically operate in pairs:

  • Echo: like a "scout," they dig deep into pain points that even customers themselves haven't noticed, and translate them into precise requirements.
  • Delta: like an "assault team," they take the intelligence Echo brings back and deploy workable AI solutions directly at the customer's site.

This is not armchair strategizing—it's hands-on combat. For example, OpenAI sent an FDE team to John Deere farms to work alongside farmers and mechanics, ultimately building an AI spraying system that can precisely identify weeds, reducing pesticide use by 60–70%, creating enormous economic and environmental benefits.

They're not just engineers—they're founder-training-class members

The FDE role combines technical, business, and communication skills, and Silicon Valley sees it as a "cradle of future founders." Garry Tan (CEO of YC) believes such roles help startups "overtake on the curve" in competition against legacy software giants like Salesforce.

Companies are willing to pay top dollar for them because:

  • Externally: FDEs can discuss KPIs and ROI in the boardroom, and go down to the factory floor to talk process optimization.
  • Internally: they can break through legacy IT systems, data silos, and security regulations to get AI stably deployed in the real world.

AI commercialization returns to the "service-heavy" era

This FDE boom signals that the AI industry is gradually shifting from "product-led growth" (PLG) back to the traditional "service-led growth" model. APIs alone are far from enough—enterprises want deep integration, secure deployment, and process restructuring.

And the new generation of AI-era FDEs can use AI tools to accelerate processes, making "service-heavy" work lighter and more efficient than ever before.

The outcome of the AI war is no longer about whose model is strongest, but about who can make AI actually run in the field and deliver value. And this group of FDEs shuttling across customer sites worldwide is the ace on the battlefield.
