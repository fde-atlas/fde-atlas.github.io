FDE vs AI engineer: same models, different job

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 15 min read

Summary

This article distinguishes FDEs from AI engineers: both use the same model APIs, but an FDE serves one customer at a time inside that customer's systems and is accountable for production outcomes, while a product-team AI engineer builds features for their own company's users. Drawing on real postings from OpenAI, Anthropic, Sierra, Cognition, Databricks, Palantir and Salesforce, it shows titles often mislead and offers a three-question test: department, who owns the build after the demo, and where code runs. A LinkedIn study reported by Computerworld found FDE roles grew 42-fold from 2023 to 2025 versus 13-fold for AI engineers, though the author cautions against choosing a career on growth multiples alone.

Key points

  • Palantir's framing: software engineers do "one capability, many customers"; FDEs do "one customer, many capabilities".
  • OpenAI's FDE posting requires up to 50% travel; Anthropic estimates 25%, plus on-call readiness for production systems.
  • Lab applied AI engineer roles are advisory: pilots, prototypes and eval suites, with customers or partners often doing the build.
  • Titles blur: Sierra's agent engineers, Cognition's Devin deployers and Databricks' AI FDE are FDE jobs under other names.
  • Growth figures (42x vs 13x) are a weak basis for choosing; pick by the daily work you want.

You build with LLMs every day, and a recruiter has just sent you a role. Or you hold an AI engineer offer and an FDE offer, and the two job descriptions read almost the same. The short answer: both build with the same models, but the FDE owns one customer’s outcome inside that customer’s systems, while an AI engineer on a product team builds for their own company’s product and users. For the wider comparison with software engineering, read the FDE vs software engineer guide.

Below: real postings side by side, the titles that blur the line, what each interview probes, and how to choose.

The one-line difference

One customer, many capabilities; or one capability, many customers. Palantir put the split that way: a software engineer’s focus is “one capability, many customers”, and a forward deployed engineer’s is “one customer, many capabilities”. Source 1Dev versus Delta: Demystifying engineering roles at PalantirPublisherPalantir BlogSource typecompany blogSource 2A Day in the Life of a Palantir Forward Deployed Software EngineerPublisherPalantir BlogSource typecompany blog Swap “capability” for “LLM feature” and that is the FDE vs AI engineer difference.

Both jobs start from the same model APIs. What differs is who the user is, where the code runs, and who answers for the result.

  • The FDE works for one customer at a time, inside that customer’s data, permissions and systems, and is judged by whether that customer’s system works in production.
  • The AI engineer on a product team builds LLM features into their own company’s product, in their own codebase, for every user at once.

The FDE half has a clear source. Sierra’s Natalie Meurer said at the AI Engineer World’s Fair, in a talk posted in July 2026, that across the many versions of the FDE role, the one constant is that every forward-deployed engineer is accountable to the customer. Source 3The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering (Natalie Meurer, Head of Agent Engineering, Sierra; AI Engineer World's Fair 2026)PublisherAI EngineerSource typerecorded talk or interview

The AI engineer bullet is our summary: the title covers too many jobs for one definition.

The Pragmatic Engineer, in an August 2025 piece, names engineer, , and technical delivery engineer as close to the FDE, and says the difference is that FDEs work with customers and also contribute to the product their company sells. Source 4Forward Deployed Engineers (Gergely Orosz)PublisherThe Pragmatic EngineerSource typenews report Keep that last part in mind: an FDE is not only a consultant with an API key.

The AI engineer on a product team

This one is our framing, drawn from what the job involves rather than from a cited posting. You build retrieval, prompts, evaluations and agent tooling into your own company’s product. Your user reaches your work through that product, your code ships through your company’s release process, and a product manager helps set priorities. A customer’s strange data reaches you through the product’s support process, not through a week on their site.

Read the verbs

Put the three side by side and the verbs tell you the job:

  • FDE postings: own, scope, build, roll out, be on call.
  • Lab applied AI postings: advise, prototype, evaluate, hand over.
  • Product AI engineering: ship, measure, iterate, scale.

All three write code. The question that separates them is who owns the build after the demo.

Our test for any posting

Ignore the title and answer these from the posting itself:

  • Which department is it in? At OpenAI this settles it: the FDE posting sits in Forward Deployed Engineering, most Applied AI Engineer postings in Go To Market. Source 5Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) - SFPublisherOpenAI (Ashby)Source typecompany job postingSource 9Applied AI ArchitectPublisherOpenAI (Ashby)Source typecompany job posting At Anthropic it does not: the FDE sits on the Applied AI team Source 7Job Application for Forward Deployed Engineer at AnthropicPublisherAnthropic (Greenhouse)Source typecompany job posting, and so does the Munich Applied AI Engineer. Source 11Applied AI Engineer, Enterprise (Munich, Germany)PublisherAnthropic (Greenhouse job board)Source typecompany job posting When the department is shared, question 2 decides.
  • Who owns the build after the demo? “Deploy”, “production rollout” and “on call” point to FDE work. “Prototype”, “pilot” and “advise” point to the advisory side.
  • Where does the code run? In the customer’s environment, in your company’s product, or in a demo repo the customer extends.

The lesson on reading an FDE job posting line by line takes two real postings apart this way, and who hires FDEs, and under which titles lists the other names the role goes by. If the posting looks like pre-sales wearing an FDE title, FDE or a solutions architect role in disguise? covers the lines to look for, and if it reads more like account support, FDE vs technical account manager draws that line.

Is one growing faster?

What each interview tests

We have found no employer that publishes how it weights its rounds, so what follows is our method, not a rubric: prepare from the verbs in the posting, since those are the duties you will be hired to do.

For an FDE loop, the posting’s “discovery, scoping, rollout” means you should expect to be handed a vague customer problem and asked what you would ask before you design anything, then to write working code. Some loops make the customer live: one Blind poster reported, in July 2026, that a Google FDE (L4) architecture round was a role-play with the interviewer as a company’s CTO. Source 22FDE Interview Experience at Google (L4) - BlindPublisherBlindSource typecandidate report on BlindSource 23Is Google FDE interview same as SWE? - BlindPublisherBlindSource typecandidate report on Blind Practice scoping a first slice before you design anything; the lesson on walking skeletons shows how.

For an applied AI or product AI engineer loop, “evaluation harnesses” and “evaluation suites” in the posting mean you should be ready for a question like a customer asks how you know your AI system works. The post how do you know your AI system works? gives a complete answer, with the words to use. Also rehearse when you would fine-tune, retrieve or just prompt.

For both, expect questions about the models themselves, such as which of our APIs have you built with, and what did you find limiting?

One prompt, two answers

Try this prompt: “A support team wants an LLM to draft replies to their tickets.” Here is how the two answers differ.

The FDE answer starts with the customer. Where do the tickets live, and can I get read access this week? Who approves a draft before it goes out? What does a wrong answer cost them: an annoyed user, or a refund? What would their head of support call a win in a month? Then: one queue, one ticket type, drafts shown to agents and never sent automatically, running in their helpdesk by Friday.

The AI engineer answer starts with the product. How does this work for every customer, not one? What eval set tells us drafts are good enough to ship? What latency and cost per draft can we afford? How do we roll out behind a flag and watch the metrics?

Both answers are good, in the right room. The mistake to avoid is giving the second answer in an FDE loop: designing the platform when the interviewer wanted one customer’s first working slice. The fix is to name the customer, the first user and the first slice before you say the word “platform”. To practice the FDE answer against a customer who pushes back, run the free practice case.

The words for “why FDE and not AI engineering?”

You will be asked some version of this. The question why FDE and not a software engineering role? has a framework and a model answer. For an AI engineer making the move, use its three parts: one piece of evidence, the reason as a choice about the work, and the cost in one clause. An illustrative answer about a fictional team:

“On my current team I build the retrieval layer for our support product. The best week I had was the one I spent with a single enterprise customer whose contracts broke our chunking: retrieval found the right clause for six of their twenty test questions. I rebuilt the chunking with their legal team, got it to seventeen, and we shipped the fix to every customer. I want that week to be the job: owning one customer’s result in their systems, and writing the code that gets it there. I know that means travel and less depth in one codebase, and I want that trade.”

Tell your own version, from your own work. The bridge story lesson shows how to build it.

How to choose, and how to move between them

This part is ours. Choose by who you want your user to be.

The FDE is likely your job if

  • You want to see the person who uses what you built.
  • Messy data, missing access and unclear owners sound like the interesting part.
  • You are fine with the travel the posting states.
  • You would rather own one result end to end than one component across many.

The AI engineer is likely your job if

  • You want to go deep on one product and one codebase over years.
  • Model quality at scale, latency and cost are the problems you enjoy.
  • You want a stable team and a roadmap, not a new customer each quarter.

At least one employer accepts either background. Cognition’s Applied AI Engineer posting asks for 3+ years as a software engineer, technical consultant, deployment strategist, forward deployed engineer, solutions engineer or similar. Source 16Applied AI Engineer @ CognitionPublisherCognition (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job posting Jia Wu, a deployed engineering lead at Cognition, said at the AI Engineer World’s Fair, in a talk posted in July 2026, that business sense can be learned, while being the technical expert in the room is very hard to teach. Source 24How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Cognition (Jia Wu; AI Engineer World's Fair 2026)PublisherAI EngineerSource typerecorded talk or interview

If you are an AI engineer moving to FDE, your model skills carry over. What you need to show is the customer half: scoping with a real person, saying no to scope, and explaining what the model gets wrong to someone who is not an engineer. If you are an FDE moving to a product AI role, show the depth half: evaluations you built, and systems you kept running after launch.
