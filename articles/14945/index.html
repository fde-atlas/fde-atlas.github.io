Can you get a clearance? · FDE recruiter screen question

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 3 min read

Summary

This article explains how to handle the security clearance question in recruiter screens for government-facing FDE roles. Palantir's Washington, D.C. Forward Deployed Software Engineer posting (as of September 2026) asks for an active Top Secret clearance or willingness to obtain one, while AWS's FDE US Government posting requires US citizenship, an active TS/SCI clearance, and a polygraph. The author advises answering in two parts: state your clearance status and eligibility factors (especially citizenship) precisely, without promising the government will grant one, and then give a genuine, experience-backed view on the mission, asking about polygraphs, secure facilities, and pending-investigation work. The main trap is saying yes to both halves just to pass the screen.

Key points

  • Palantir's US Government FDE posting requires active Top Secret clearance or willingness to obtain one.
  • AWS's FDE US Government role requires citizenship, active TS/SCI, and a polygraph.
  • State clearance level, whether it is active, and when it lapsed—'held' and 'active' differ.
  • If you hold none, discuss citizenship and eligibility; never promise approval.
  • Back your mission answer with personal history and ask about stepping off disputed uses.

How to answer

The clearance question can arrive before any call: the application form for Palantir’s , US Government posting asks about clearance status. Answer the eligibility half exactly.

  • Say what you hold. The level, whether it is active, and when it lapsed if it did. “Held” and “active” are different answers.
  • If you hold none, say what bears on eligibility. Citizenship first, since a posting can require it. The government grants a clearance after an investigation, so don’t promise one; say you know of nothing that would stop it.
  • Read the posting, then ask what it leaves out. Ask about a polygraph, a secure facility, and what you would work on while an investigation is pending. If it requires an active clearance you don’t hold, say so first and ask about an unclassified role. Describe past cleared work only as far as you could in public; “I can’t go into that” is an expected answer.

Then give a view on the work. Name the government work you want and why, from your own history. Then give your line, or the question you would want answered first: how the team raises a concern about a use, and whether an engineer can step off one.

The trap is saying yes to both halves to get through the screen. An eligibility answer you can’t stand behind surfaces later, and a mission answer with no story behind it gives a screen that looks for evidence in backstories nothing to find.

Follow-ups

What the interviewer may ask next, once your first answer is on the table.

  • Some of this work can only be done on site in a secure facility. How does that fit with where you live?
  • Is there any work for a government customer you would not do?
  • What would you do if an engagement moved toward a use you disagreed with?
  • Are you willing to take a polygraph?

Where answers go wrong

  • Says “yes, I can get one” without checking citizenship or anything else that bears on eligibility, which the recruiter will find out later in the process.
  • Answers the mission half in the company’s own adjectives, or dodges it, when the question asks for a view.

Answer this in two minutes

Write the answer you would say out loud. The clock starts with your first word.
