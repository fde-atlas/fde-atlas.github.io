This Japanese article analyzes rates for freelance Python engineers using market surveys and DeFactory's own data from 405 received job listings, of which 52 involved Python. It argues the generative AI boom made 2024 Python project volume roughly 10x the prior year, with average monthly rates of 7-8 million... rather 700,000-800,000 yen, and AI projects reaching 1-1.5 million yen. The core claim is that project type and intermediary layers (commercial flow) matter as much as skill: direct contracts can raise annual take-home pay by over 2 million yen. It also highlights FDE roles paying 1.2-1.5 million yen monthly.

Key points

  • Python freelance projects grew ~10x in 2024 due to generative AI demand.
  • Average monthly rate is 700,000-800,000 yen; AI projects reach 1-1.5 million yen.
  • Author's sample of 52 Python listings averaged 900,000 yen per month.
  • Shallower intermediary layers (direct contracts) can add ~2.4 million yen annually.
  • Freelance FDE roles command 1.2-1.5 million yen monthly in Japan.

"Now is the time to be a Python engineer." There is plenty of data to back up this claim.

A rapid increase in the number of projects, overwhelming demand in the AI field, and scarcity compared to other languages. These factors combine to give Python freelancers an advantageous position in the market. Let's start by grasping the overall market picture.

Limited to engineers with 5+ years of professional experience

High-rate, direct-contract project information

Python freelance projects have seen explosive growth since the generative AI boom began.

According to a Midworks survey, the number of Python projects surged roughly 10-fold between January and November 2024.

Behind this surge is the spread of generative AI, exemplified by ChatGPT. Python has become the de facto standard language for implementing and fine-tuning LLMs (large language models), creating a virtuous cycle in which demand fuels further demand.

As of December 2025, Python projects listed on Freelance Start alone include 2,613 on TECHBIZ, 2,497 on Findy Freelance, and 2,057 on Midworks—each major agency holds an abundance of projects. With so many projects available, you generally don't need to worry about "not finding work."

However, projects with popular conditions such as fully remote and high rates fill up quickly after being posted, so it's important to register with multiple agencies and keep your information-gathering antennas up.

Why is Python so widely chosen? The reason lies in its "overwhelmingly rich library ecosystem."

TensorFlow and PyTorch for machine learning, Pandas and NumPy for data analysis, Django and FastAPI for web development. Python offers mature libraries for every field, and its strength is its high versatility, allowing it to consistently handle everything from developing machine learning models to providing web APIs and automating business processes.

In particular, the spread of generative AI has expanded demand for MLOps engineers who implement and operate LLMs. Python is also the de facto standard in the MLOps domain, giving it a structural reason for continued demand going forward.

Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry is also promoting "AI talent development" as a national policy, and corporate AI investment is expected to expand further. The tailwinds for Python engineers should continue for the foreseeable future.

Python freelance projects hold a market share of just over 10%, ranking fourth in the industry after Java, PHP, and JavaScript.

However, the number of engineers has not yet caught up with the number of projects. Compared to Java and JavaScript, Python engineers are relatively few, and the supply-demand balance works in favor of the sellers (the engineers).

This scarcity creates "a strong tendency for rates to be much higher than for other languages." Even with the same years of experience, it's not uncommon for Python engineers to be offered monthly rates 100,000–200,000 yen higher than Java engineers.

Demand is high, yet engineers are in short supply. As long as this structure persists, the advantages of Python freelancers will be maintained.

Combining surveys from multiple agencies, the average monthly rate for Python freelancers is in the 700,000–800,000 yen range. That translates to an annual income of over 9 million yen—a difference of more than 2 million yen compared to the average annual salary of a full-time Python engineer (about 6 million yen).

For AI and machine learning projects, 1–1.5 million yen per month is realistic. Use the quick reference table below to check your market value based on your years of experience.

Taking all this data together, the standard rate for Python freelancers is in the 700,000 to 800,000 yen per month range. That translates to an annual income of roughly 9 million yen, making Python a language that can command high earnings even by overall IT industry standards.

However, these are only "averages." If you target AI projects or direct-contract projects, which will be discussed later, you can aim even higher.

The impact of years of experience on rates is greater than you might imagine.

According to a survey by Engineer Style, the average rates by years of experience are as follows.

  • 1-3 years: 300,000-600,000 yen
  • 3-5 years: 600,000-800,000 yen
  • 5+ years: 800,000-1,000,000 yen or more

Source: Engineer Style, "Freelance Engineer Rates by Years of Experience"

As you gain experience, rates rise dramatically, and projects paying over 1 million yen per month are no longer rare.

Looking at the trends across freelance agents, there is a common consensus that "Python projects pay in the 800,000 yen range per month with about 5 years of practical experience, and many exceed 1 million yen for advanced engineers."

The key point is that "not just Python experience, but overall development experience" is evaluated. Even with 5 years of Java experience followed by 2 years of Python, many cases are evaluated as having 7 years of development experience.

We recommend using your current years of experience to form an image of your future rates and build a career plan.

If you want to aim for high rates, AI and machine learning projects are essential.

Rates for AI and machine learning development projects are extremely high, at around 1,000,000 to 1,300,000 yen per month. There are various projects ranging from the PoC (proof of concept) stage to commercialization phases, and the required skills differ depending on the phase.

Research-oriented projects place a strong emphasis on advanced mathematical knowledge (linear algebra, probability and statistics), while implementation-oriented projects require knowledge of MLOps (machine learning operations infrastructure) and the cloud.

In particular, since 2024, LLM (large language model) related projects have surged, and if you have experience with RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) or fine-tuning, you will be in high demand.

In the AI boom, Python x AI is the quintessential high-rate combination. By targeting this field, you may be able to maximize your income.

The average annual income of a full-time Python engineer is said to be about 6 million yen. Meanwhile, freelancers earn the equivalent of over 8.7 to 9 million yen annually. A simple calculation shows a difference of more than 2 million yen.

There are two main reasons for this gap.

The first is that "project-based rates mean skills are directly tied to compensation." Full-time employees are subject to seniority systems and internal evaluations, but for freelancers, market value is directly reflected in their rates.

The second is that "there is the option of direct contracts, which cut out middleman margins." Unlike full-time employees working within multi-layered subcontracting structures, freelancers can choose their transaction flow. This will be discussed in detail later, but this "choice of transaction flow" greatly affects take-home pay.

However, freelancers have no employee benefits and face the risk of projects ending. It is a trade-off between stability versus freedom and high income. It is important to judge based on your own preferences and lifestyle.

Data analysis and big data analytics are the classic high-rate field for Python freelancers.

You collect, organize, and analyze large-scale data, using libraries such as Pandas, NumPy, and SciPy to derive business insights. Visualization requires Matplotlib or Seaborn, and BI tool integration requires connections to Tableau or Power BI.

The typical rate centers around 800,000–1,000,000 yen per month, and if you can handle upstream processes (requirements definition and design), rates exceeding 1,000,000 yen come into view.

With the expansion of AI demand, the number of "Python × data analysis" projects has been increasing year by year, and market value is trending upward. Data analysis skills are one of the highest-ROI skills for Python engineers looking to boost their market value.

AI and machine learning development is the specialty field where Python freelancers can expect the highest rates.

The main work involves model development and tuning using TensorFlow or PyTorch, such as image recognition, natural language processing (NLP), and recommendation engines. Since 2024, LLM-related projects (prompt engineering, RAG construction, fine-tuning) have been rapidly increasing.

Rates are extremely high, at around 1,000,000–1,300,000 yen per month. Many projects allow full remote work, and the flexibility in working style is also attractive.

However, the barrier to entry is relatively high. Beyond Python implementation skills, understanding of machine learning algorithms, a foundation in mathematics and statistics, and knowledge of cloud services (AWS SageMaker, GCP Vertex AI, etc.) are required.

If you are prepared to aim for high rates, investing in the AI field is worth considering.

Also, among the areas where Python engineers can demonstrate value in AI projects, the role that has been rapidly expanding in domestic demand since 2024–2025 is FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer).

An FDE belongs to a vendor company with AI or SaaS products while working on-site at client companies, handling everything end-to-end from building data infrastructure to implementing LLMs and AI models in business operations and establishing their operation. Python, SQL, and data pipeline construction skills are the technologies FDEs use most in their daily work, making it one of the most realistic high-rate positions on the natural career path for Python engineers.

For freelance FDEs, projects in the 1,200,000–1,500,000 yen per month range are already forming in Japan, which directly aligns with the goal of "wanting to take on AI projects with Python." More detailed freelance FDE rates are organized by skill level.

Web application development offers a stable number of projects and is an area that is easy for freelance beginners to get into.

The focus is on developing web systems and web services using Python as the server-side language. Django is the standard framework, but FastAPI's popularity has been soaring in recent years. For API development and microservices projects, FastAPI experience is advantageous.

In many cases, the frontend requires React or Vue.js, and integration with databases such as PostgreSQL or MySQL is required. Being able to handle full-stack development gives you an advantage in winning projects.

Rates center around 600,000–850,000 yen per month. While not as high as AI projects, this is an area with many projects where you can earn steadily.

IoT system development is a highly specialized field that serves as a bridge between hardware and software.

Data sent from IoT devices in factories, construction sites, and the like is accumulated in the cloud, where Python is used for real-time analysis and visualization. Main tasks include anomaly detection from sensors and building data processing infrastructure.

Integration with cloud services such as AWS IoT Core and Azure IoT Hub is essential, and knowledge of databases (including time-series databases) is also required. Rates are relatively high, at around 800,000–1,000,000 yen per month.

However, due to the need for verification environments, on-site work is often required, and you may be asked to come into the office two to three days a week. If you insist on full remote work, you need to be careful when selecting projects.

The ways Python freelancers work are diversifying. While the spread of remote work allows them to work from anywhere, there are also risks unique to freelancing.

Understand both the advantages and disadvantages, and choose a working style that suits you.

Many Python projects support remote work. There are plenty of projects that allow full remote work, making Python a language where location-independent work is easy to achieve.

In particular, data analysis, machine learning, and API development can easily be completed online on a daily basis, allowing for flexible working styles that fit your lifestyle. Even those living in rural areas have an environment in place to join high-rate projects in Tokyo.

Since the COVID-19 pandemic, the spread of remote work has advanced rapidly. Working from home has become the norm across the IT industry, and remote-friendly is becoming the standard for Python projects as well.

It is no longer rare for people living in rural areas or overseas to join projects. Engineers who work on high-paying projects while balancing childcare, moving to rural areas, or being a digital nomad overseas are increasing.

On the other hand, in highly confidential fields such as finance and healthcare, some on-site work may remain. Since working styles can be constrained depending on the industry, be sure to check the work arrangement when selecting projects.

If you insist on full remote work, projects at SaaS companies and startups are good targets.

While freelancing offers a high degree of freedom, it also comes with risks that salaried employees do not face.

First, there are no employee benefits. Health insurance switches to the National Health Insurance and pensions to the National Pension, increasing your insurance premium burden. There is no retirement allowance or bonus either.

Next is income instability. There is always the risk that a project will suddenly end and your income will stop. Especially during economic downturns, the number of projects decreases and your utilization rate may drop.

Also, you need to handle administrative tasks yourself, such as tax filing and expense management. Without knowledge of taxes and social insurance, you may suffer from unexpected expenses.

Salaried employees have stable income and benefits, but on the other hand, their annual income tends to have a ceiling and they have less freedom in how they work. Which to choose depends on your own preferences and life stage.

To consistently win high-rate projects, you need both technical skills and "strategy."

Honing not only your Python coding ability but also peripheral skills such as frameworks, cloud, and mathematics/statistics, along with a strategic approach of "choosing projects with short sales chains," is the key to maximizing your take-home pay.

To win high-rate projects, mastery of frameworks and libraries, not just Python alone, is essential.

For web development: Django, Flask, FastAPI. For machine learning: TensorFlow, PyTorch. For data analysis: Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-learn. You are expected to handle the major tools in each field at a professional level.

Particularly noteworthy is "FastAPI." It is a lightweight framework with asynchronous processing support that is rapidly gaining adoption in API development. Job postings since 2024 increasingly mention FastAPI, so mastering it will broaden your project options.

We recommend staying up to date on the latest framework trends and using job postings as a reference when planning your studies.

In modern development, opportunities to work not only on application development but also on infrastructure building are increasing.

The basics of cloud services such as AWS and GCP, container technologies such as Docker/Kubernetes, and fundamental Linux server operations. Understanding these will give you an advantage in winning projects.

In AI and data analysis fields, cloud knowledge is essential for using large-scale computing resources. Experience with ML platforms such as AWS SageMaker, GCP Vertex AI, and Azure Machine Learning can lead to higher rates.

In web development as well, more and more cases involve working on CI/CD pipeline construction (GitHub Actions, Jenkins, etc.). A "Python + cloud" skill set directly boosts your long-term market value.

If you aim to become an AI engineer or data scientist, knowledge of mathematics and statistics is unavoidable.

Understanding machine learning algorithms requires a foundation in calculus and probability/statistics. Linear algebra (matrix operations) is the basis of deep learning, and optimization theory is essential for understanding the training process.

Statistical knowledge is also useful for understanding data preprocessing and evaluation metrics. Statistics is often required in practice, such as in A/B test design, anomaly detection logic, and evaluating the accuracy of predictive models.

Python offers a wealth of advanced libraries, but engineers who understand the underlying principles are highly valued. Experience in competitions such as Kaggle can also be a selling point in interviews.

Just as important as technical skills is how you choose your "contract chain." Many engineers underestimate this, but it greatly affects your take-home pay.

Projects have a "contract chain." The more intermediaries there are—prime contractor → secondary contractor → tertiary contractor...—the more margin is deducted along the way, reducing your take-home pay.

Take-home pay comparison by contract chain

  • [Tertiary contract] End company → Prime contractor → Secondary contractor → You Client payment: 1.2 million yen → Take-home: 800,000 yen (margin: 400,000 yen)
  • [Direct contract] End company → You (via BizDev Tech) Client payment: 1.2 million yen → Take-home: 1 million yen (margin: 200,000 yen)

→ Even with the same skills and the same project, that's a difference of 2.4 million yen per year!

Choosing projects with a "shallow contract chain" whenever possible is the key to securing high rates. Prioritize direct contracts with end companies and projects from agents that take primary contracts.
