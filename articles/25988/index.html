Onboarding Tool Integration Checklist: How Implementation Teams Should Test CRM, Support, PM and Chat Connections

Quivly · 2026-10-29 · 6 min read

Summary

This Quivly article argues that onboarding platforms should be judged on integration depth, not the number of connectors listed on a pricing page. Shallow integrations force implementation teams and FDEs to act as manual glue, copying information between systems until the plan drifts out of date. The author provides a checklist for testing CRM, help desk, project management, chat/email, and product usage data connections, plus universal questions about sync direction, triggers, field mapping, identity matching, permissions, failure handling, and ownership. It recommends running scenario-based tests during a trial—closing a test deal, escalating a ticket, slipping a task, revoking a token—and lists warning signs of shallow integrations, such as iframe embeds, daily-only syncs, and unsupported custom fields. The piece closes with a brief description of Quivly Projects.

Key points

  • Integration depth matters more than connector count; shallow links make staff the integration.
  • Checklist covers CRM, help desk, project tools, chat/email, and product usage data flows.
  • Universal questions: sync direction, triggers, field mapping, identity matching, failure handling.
  • Test during trials with real events: close a deal, escalate a ticket, revoke a token.
  • Warning signs include iframe embeds, nightly-only syncs, and no custom field support.

Almost every onboarding platform says it integrates with your CRM, help desk, project tool and chat. For a forward deployed engineering (FDE) or implementation team, that claim tells you very little. What matters is what the integration actually does on a busy Tuesday: whether a closed deal creates a project with the right template, whether an escalated ticket shows up as a blocker on the plan, and whether a customer reply in a shared channel lands on the task it belongs to.

This checklist is for implementation leads and FDEs who need to test integration depth before they commit to a tool. It covers what to check for each system, how to run real integration tests during a trial and which warning signs usually mean the integration is a logo on a pricing page rather than part of the workflow. If you are still deciding how your stack should be wired, start with our guide to connecting your CRM, support, project and chat tools for onboarding.

Why Integration Depth Decides Whether an Onboarding Tool Gets Used

Implementation work spans systems by nature. The commercial context lives in the CRM, technical issues live in the help desk, the plan lives in a project tool and day-to-day conversation happens in chat and email. When an onboarding platform only connects to these systems on the surface, someone on your team becomes the integration: copying deal notes into a kickoff doc, pasting ticket links into status updates and re-typing customer answers into tasks.

That manual glue work is invisible in a demo and very visible three weeks into a rollout. FDEs stop updating the platform because the real information is somewhere else, the plan drifts out of date and leaders go back to asking for status in chat. A tool with fewer integrations that each work deeply will usually serve an implementation team better than one with a long list of shallow connectors.

The Integration Checklist, System by System

Use the table below as a starting point. For each system, the question is not "does it connect?" but "what happens automatically, in which direction and who sees it?"

System Flows into onboarding Flows back What to test
CRM Closed deal, contract dates, products, stakeholders, sales notes Onboarding stage, go-live date, risk status Does a closed deal create a project from the right template, with owners and dates filled in?
Support or help desk Tickets from onboarding accounts, priority, escalations Project context for agents on the ticket Does an escalated ticket show on the plan as a blocker on the right milestone?
Project management Engineering tasks onboarding depends on Customer deadlines and dependencies When an engineering task slips, does the plan show the new date?
Chat and email Customer replies, questions and approvals Requests, reminders, status updates Can a customer answer a request in chat or email and close the task?
Product and usage data Activation events, logins, feature adoption Milestones reached, accounts falling behind Does a milestone complete when the event fires, with no manual tick?

CRM

The CRM handoff is where most implementations lose context. Check whether the platform can map deal fields to project fields, pick a template based on product, segment or contract value and carry the sales notes and stakeholder list into the project. Ask whether the sync is one-way or two-way, and whether onboarding status and go-live dates write back so account and sales leaders can see progress without opening another tool.

Support and Help Desk

During onboarding, a support ticket is often an implementation blocker in disguise. The platform should be able to show open tickets for an onboarding account on the project itself, flag escalations and let an FDE link a ticket to the milestone it affects. Test what happens when a ticket is closed: does the blocker clear, or does someone have to remember to update the plan?

Project Management and Engineering Tools

Many implementations depend on internal engineering work such as a custom connector, a configuration change or a bug fix. Check whether onboarding tasks can reference work items in your engineering tracker and whether date changes there show up on the customer plan. Without that link, the customer-facing plan will promise dates the engineering team has already moved.

Chat and Email

Customers rarely log into a vendor tool to answer a question. Check whether requests can go out through the channels customers already use, such as Slack, email or a portal, and whether replies flow back onto the right task. Also check how the platform handles noise: a good integration turns a relevant message into a proposed task or blocker and leaves the rest alone.

Product Usage and Data

For technical rollouts, the clearest proof of progress is product data. Check whether the platform can ingest activation events from your event tracking tool or data warehouse and tie them to milestones, so a milestone completes when the customer actually reaches it rather than when someone remembers to mark it done.

Questions to Ask About Every Connector

Beyond system-specific checks, a handful of questions apply to every integration on the list:

  • Direction: Is the sync one-way or two-way, and which system wins when the same field is changed in both places?
  • Trigger versus schedule: Does data move when an event happens, or on a schedule? For blockers and escalations, a nightly sync is too slow.
  • Field mapping: Can you map custom fields, or only standard ones? Most implementation teams rely on custom fields for scope, tier and technical details.
  • Identity matching: How does the platform match accounts and contacts across systems when names and domains differ?
  • Permissions: Does the integration respect the access rules in the source system, and can internal notes be kept away from customer-facing views?
  • Failure handling: When a sync fails, who is told, and is there a log you can read without opening a support ticket?
  • Ownership: Can your operations team configure and maintain the integration, or does every change require the vendor?

How to Test Integrations During a Trial

Integration claims are easiest to verify with real events. During a trial or pilot, connect a sandbox or a small slice of real data and run a short script of scenarios:

  • Close a test deal in the CRM and time how long it takes for a project to appear with the right template, owners and dates.
  • Open and escalate a ticket for that account and check whether it shows up as a blocker on the plan.
  • Slip an engineering task that a milestone depends on and see whether the customer plan reflects the change.
  • Send a customer request through chat or email, reply as the customer and confirm the answer closes the task.
  • Fire a product event that represents a milestone and check whether the milestone completes on its own.
  • Break something on purpose, such as revoking a token, and see how quickly and clearly the failure is reported.

Score each scenario on whether it worked without manual steps. For a structured way to weigh these results against other criteria, see our onboarding platform scorecard and pilot plan.

Warning Signs of Shallow Integrations

  • The integration only embeds a link or an iframe to the other system rather than moving data.
  • Data syncs once a day, so blockers and escalations show up after the customer has already noticed.
  • Custom fields are not supported, or require professional services to map.
  • Customer replies arrive as a notification but never attach to the task they answer.
  • The demo uses a pre-built environment and the vendor cannot connect to your sandbox during the trial.
  • Nobody can explain what happens when a sync fails.
