What to Know Before Discussing FDE Salary Levels

"Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) earn 8 to 15 million yen"—searching online, you will quickly find such definitive ranges, but most of them cite no sources. This is because FDE is merely a practical job title that appears neither in Japan's Standard Classification of Occupations nor in the occupational categories of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Basic Survey on Wage Structure." If you ask the Statistics Bureau for "the average FDE salary" as a single number, no answer will come back.

Therefore, this article stops reproducing unsourced salary estimates and approaches the reality of FDE compensation using only verifiable primary sources. Specifically, the following two types.

Primary Sources Used in This Article

(1) Actual "Forward Deployed Engineer" job postings from Anthropic and Databricks (US postings disclose salary ranges). (2) Industry-level data from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Basic Survey on Wage Structure" (since there is no FDE occupational category, figures for the closest category, "Information and Communications," are used as reference values). Source URLs and verification dates are noted in the text for both.

In conclusion, we can clearly explain why the nature of FDE work and the skills it requires tend to lead to high salaries. On the other hand, it is not possible to honestly state a single figure for "FDE salaries in Japan" based on publicly available information. This article gets as close to reality as possible using three pillars: the specificity of the job duties, disclosed information from actual job postings, and official statistics. For an explanation of the FDE role itself, see What Is an FDE? (An Overview of the Role).

The Actual Duties of an FDE That Affect Salary

To judge whether a salary is reasonable, you first need to know specifically what the job involves. Here, we examine the job duties based on descriptions in actual job postings.

Job Duties Seen in Anthropic's Job Posting

The responsibilities of the "Forward Deployed Engineer" position (on the Applied AI team) that Anthropic is hiring for are described in the posting as follows.

  • Embed directly with strategic customer companies and build production applications using Claude models within the customer's systems
  • Deliver technical artifacts actually used in production workflows, such as MCP servers, subagents, and agent skills
  • Provide white glove deployment support for AI adoption in enterprise environments
  • Articulate repeatable deployment patterns and feed them back to the product and engineering organizations
  • Travel to customer sites as needed—roughly 25% depending on location

Application requirements include "4+ years of experience as a software engineer with FDE or consulting experience," "production LLM experience (prompt design, agent development, evaluation frameworks)," and "implementation skills centered on Python" (Source: Anthropic Careers "Forward Deployed Engineer" job posting, verified September 16, 2026).

Job Duties Seen in Databricks's Job Posting

The "Forward Deployed Engineer" posting for Databricks's Tokyo location (Job ID: CSQ427R277) describes the job duties as follows.

  • Build and productionize solutions to customers' data and AI challenges on the Databricks platform
  • Lead the overall architecture and take end-to-end design responsibility spanning data engineering, AI, and application development {{30} Engage in deep customer immersion, understanding challenges with a wide range of stakeholders from on-the-ground engineers to executives
  • Immerse deeply with the customer team (customer immersion) and understand challenges with a wide range of stakeholders, from on-the-ground technical staff to executives
  • Build reusable accelerators and frameworks that also influence Databricks's product roadmap

Application requirements include "8+ years of experience in data engineering, data platforms, or software engineering," "implementation skills in Python/Scala/JavaScript, etc.," "working knowledge of at least two of AWS/Azure/GCP," "a deep understanding of distributed processing with Apache Spark," and "native-level Japanese and business-level English." Travel is estimated at about 20% (Source: Databricks Careers "Forward Deployed Engineer" Tokyo job posting, verified September 16, 2026).

What the two companies have in common is threefold: (1) embedding inside customer companies to do the implementation, (2) taking responsibility through production deployment and adoption, and (3) requiring both engineering skill and the ability to engage with customers. The breadth of this scope of duties and the weight of this responsibility are thought to underpin the salary levels discussed below.

Salary Ranges Disclosed in Actual US Job Postings

In several U.S. states, including California and New York, employers are legally required to disclose salary ranges in job postings. As a result, job listings for Anthropic and Databricks at their U.S. locations explicitly state the actual salary ranges.

How to Read These Figures

All of the above are the annual base salary ranges disclosed in the job postings at the time of recruitment, and they do not represent the actual earnings of people hired, amounts after raises, or the value of equity compensation (such as RSUs) granted. The JPY conversions are mechanical calculations using a reference rate as of September 16, 2026 (approximately ¥155.08 per USD, per exchangerate-api.com) and will vary with exchange rate fluctuations. In addition, Databricks' "Zone 1" is a category for high cost-of-living areas such as the San Francisco Bay Area, and different ranges may apply in other regions.

For reference, Anthropic's FDE job posting notes that "for sales roles, the range includes an OTE (On Target Earnings) range with commission targets in addition to base salary," and explicitly states that for FDE positions the range is base salary only. The level of equity compensation (RSUs) is not stated in the job posting and cannot be confirmed from public information.

Salary Not Disclosed for the Same "FDE" Roles in Japan

What is interesting is that even for the same company and the same job title, salary is not disclosed in job postings for positions based in Japan. Databricks is recruiting "Forward Deployed Engineers" for its Tokyo office as well (job posting last updated September 15, 2026), but the posting itself contains no "Pay Range Transparency" section like those in U.S. postings, and no salary range is listed.

In Japan, the Employment Security Act requires that job postings state matters related to wages, but ranged notations such as "¥XX million–¥XX million" or practices of making individual offers after interviews are also permitted, and the law does not mandate disclosure of the actual amounts (the newly added disclosure requirements in the April 2024 amendment to the implementing regulations concerned the scope of changes to job duties and work locations and criteria for renewing fixed-term contracts; the obligation to disclose wages itself predates this). This difference—regulations less far-reaching than the salary range disclosure laws of U.S. states such as California and New York—is the direct reason why "FDE annual income in Japan" cannot be determined from primary sources alone.

If you want to check the job postings of companies recruiting FDEs in Japan, we recommend checking each company's recruiting page directly from the list of hiring companies (Companies Hiring FDEs / Jobs by Company).

What Japanese Official Statistics Show: The Basic Survey on Wage Structure

A representative official statistic for checking wage levels in Japan is the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Basic Survey on Wage Structure." From the latest "2025 Basic Survey on Wage Structure" (covering wages for June 2025), published on March 24, 2026, we look at the scheduled cash earnings (monthly amount, excluding overtime pay and bonuses) of regular workers (full-time workers).

Caveats When Applying This Statistics to FDEs

"Information and communications" is an industry classification, not an occupation classification. This statistics contains no occupational category for FDEs; it is an average that includes workers of all occupations and seniority levels in the information and communications industry, from help desk staff to executives. Moreover, the amounts are monthly scheduled cash earnings (excluding overtime pay and bonuses), not annual income itself. If you simply multiply by 12, you get a "mechanical lower-bound estimate of annual income excluding bonuses" of about ¥4.87 million for the information and communications industry and about ¥4.09 million across all industries, but these are reference values only (Source: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, "2025 Basic Survey on Wage Structure: Overview of Results," Wages by Industry, Tables 5-1 and 5-2, published March 24, 2026).

The only fact that can be read from this statistics is that "wage levels in the information and communications industry are roughly 19% higher than the all-industry average." Since FDE is a role with relatively high specialization and rarity even within this industry (see the preceding section), it is natural to assume it tends to exceed the industry average, but there is no official occupation-specific data to back this up.

The Concept of Total Compensation—Don't Compare on Base Salary Alone

As the job postings above show, in the U.S. IT and AI industries, total compensation is generally made up of annual cash compensation (base salary) plus equity compensation (RSUs—restricted stock units—or stock options), performance bonuses, sign-on bonuses, and the like. Even in full-time employment in Japan, RSUs and performance-linked bonuses are sometimes granted at foreign-affiliated companies and listed or pre-IPO companies.

Points to Consider When Comparing Offers

Comparing multiple offers on base salary alone leads to misjudging the actual total compensation. If there is equity compensation, check the "grant amount," "vesting period," and "current valuation"; if there is a bonus, check the "linked performance metrics" and "most recent payout record (if available)." However, since no public data generalizes these levels into figures such as "X%," this article avoids making definitive market-rate claims.

"Valued Experience" That Can Be Read from Job Postings

Even if we cannot determine the absolute amount of salary, the Responsibilities and Qualifications sections of actual job postings reveal specifically what companies expect from FDEs and what they value. Based on the Anthropic and Databricks job postings discussed earlier, the following elements are commonly emphasized.

Experience seeing projects through to production deployment and adoption

Both companies repeatedly use the word "Production" and value experience delivering systems that actually run, not just PoCs (proofs of concept). Organizing your implementation projects so you can describe them in the order of "challenge, your role, approach, and quantified results" is effective both in interviews and salary negotiations. How to build a portfolio is explained in How to Build an FDE Portfolio.

Practical knowledge of multi-cloud, distributed processing, and LLM operations

Databricks' job posting explicitly requires "two or more of AWS/Azure/GCP," "an understanding of Apache Spark's distributed processing internals," and "practical knowledge of MLOps," while Anthropic's posting asks for "production experience with LLMs, including prompt design, agent development, and evaluation frameworks." Both the breadth of technical skills and the ability to handle the messy challenges that arise in production operations are evaluated. For an overview of required skills, see Skills Required for FDEs.

Experience engaging with enterprise customers

Descriptions such as "building trust with a wide range of stakeholders, from executives to on-the-ground technical staff" and "working autonomously amid high ambiguity" are common to both companies' postings. Not only technical skill but also the ability to deliver results within a customer organization is clearly set as an evaluation criterion.

Language skills and cross-office collaboration

Databricks' Tokyo posting requires "native Japanese and business-level English," and Anthropic's posting also assumes collaboration with its overseas headquarters. For FDE positions at foreign companies, negotiation and documentation in English are required in both hiring and evaluation. For details, see Do FDEs Need English Skills?.

Choosing FDE as a Career, Not Just for the Salary

Summarizing the facts confirmed in this article: (1) the nature of FDE work and its evaluation criteria can be confirmed concretely from job postings, (2) actual postings at US-based companies disclose reasonably high salary ranges, and (3) the "FDE salary in Japan" cannot be pinned down to a single figure from public information alone.

Rather than chasing an absolute salary figure, building up the elements that job postings repeatedly demand—"a track record of seeing projects through to production," "technical breadth across multi-cloud and LLM operations," "the ability to engage with enterprise customers," and "English proficiency"—will ultimately broaden your options and offers.
