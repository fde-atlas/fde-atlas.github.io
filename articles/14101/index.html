Forward deployed software engineer

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 2 min read

Summary

This article traces the Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE) title across employers. Palantir claims to have pioneered the role, calls FDSEs "Deltas" internally, and places them in Business Development. OpenAI posts FDSE separately from Forward Deployed Engineer, requiring 7+ years of full-stack experience and custom work on its APIs. EY's Prague FAAS postings span scripts and VBA macros to Vue, TypeScript, and FastAPI apps on Kubernetes. The takeaway: read the duties, not just the acronym, and know whether you want Dev's "one capability, many customers" or Delta's "one customer, many capabilities" focus.

Key points

  • Palantir pioneered FDSE, calls them "Deltas", and sits them in Business Development.
  • Palantir contrasts Dev (one capability, many customers) with Delta (one customer, many capabilities).
  • OpenAI lists FDSE separately from FDE, requiring 7+ years of full-stack experience.
  • EY Prague FDSE work ranges from VBA macros to Vue, TypeScript, and FastAPI on Kubernetes.
  • Palantir's new-grad application forces candidates to declare FDSE versus Software Engineer upfront.

Full text

Forward deployed software engineer (FDSE) is Palantir’s title for its role. Palantir’s FDSE posting says it pioneered the position, and Palantir’s blog says FDSEs are called “Deltas” internally and sit in Business Development, with a mandate to achieve technical outcomes for customers. OpenAI posts a Forward Deployed Software Engineer title separately from its Forward Deployed Engineer title; the FDSWE posting lists 7+ years of professional full-stack engineering experience among its qualifications and describes building custom software on OpenAI’s APIs, so check which of the two your recruiter means. Why it matters in interviews

Palantir’s new-grad FDSE application asks candidates to confirm interest in FDSE rather than Software Engineer, so the choice between the two is on record before the first interview.
