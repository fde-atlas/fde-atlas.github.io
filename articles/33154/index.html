Forward deployed engineer résumé: what the ads ask for

Kernavique · 2026-11-21 · 9 min read

Summary

Kernavique read 30 forward deployed engineer job ads in full on November 21, 2026, one per employer from the most active US posters, and counted what they ask for. 28 ads name customers, clients, users or stakeholders; 23 name Python; 23 state a number of years; 18 use the word “production”; only 10 mention a degree, and none asks for a cover letter. The article turns these counts into résumé advice: lead with shipped software and customer-facing work, list languages with Python first, make years easy to total, and show ownership of a project from vague brief to delivery. It notes limits: one day's sample, mostly AI and tech employers, and ads are not screening rules.

Key points

  • 28 of 30 ads require customer or user-facing work.
  • Python is named in 23 ads; JavaScript or TypeScript in 14.
  • 23 ads state years of experience, from 1+ to 7+.
  • 20 ads mention no degree; only 5 make it a hard requirement.
  • No ad asks for a cover letter.

What did we read?

On November 21, 2026, we read 30 forward deployed engineer ads in full. They come from the 30 employers with the most US postings under that title on the 76 public job feeds we follow, one ad per employer. Intern, new grad and manager ads were left out.

The counts are what the ads say. The advice is ours, and each piece is tied to a count. None of it is a promise of an interview.

What does that mean for each résumé section?

This section is our advice. Every count is of the 30 ads read on November 21, 2026.

Summary line. Say that you build software and work with customers, in that order or the other. We suggest this because 28 of the 30 ads have a requirement line naming customers, clients, users, stakeholders or audiences.

Experience. Lead each role with work that went live. We suggest this because 18 of the 30 ads use the word “production”. Glean asks for “a track record of shipping production software”.

Customer work. Give it its own lines, not a clause at the end. “Customer-facing” is in 20 of the 30 ads. OpenAI wants “5+ years of engineering or technical deployment experience that includes customer-facing work”.

Who you worked with. Name the level of the people across the table. “Stakeholder” is in 21 of the 30 ads and “executive” in 10. Tennr asks for someone “Skilled at communicating technical concepts to both executive and technical audiences”.

Ownership and unclear briefs. Show one project you carried from a vague need to a delivered system. “End-to-end” is in 19 of the 30 ads, “ownership” in 18 and “ambiguity” or “ambiguous” in 17. Snorkel AI asks for “Experience translating ambiguous customer or stakeholder requirements into technical specifications, workflows, and delivered solutions.”

Prototypes. If a prototype of yours became a live system, say so in one line. “Prototype” is in 14 of the 30 ads.

Skills. List the languages you can work in, with Python first if it is one. Python is named in 23 of the 30 ads, and JavaScript or TypeScript in 14.

Dates. Make your years easy to add up. Twenty-three of the 30 ads state a number of years.

Travel. If you are willing to travel, one line saying so is cheap. Fifteen of the 30 ads use the word about the role.

What do employers publish about applications?

Very little. On November 21, 2026, we found no page from Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic or Databricks that says which projects to list, how long a résumé should be, or whether to send a cover letter.

What they do publish, read November 21, 2026:

  • OpenAI. Its Interview Guide says “We are not credential-driven”. It says résumé review typically takes one week.
  • Databricks. Its interview prep page says to “ensure your résumé highlights the most relevant skills and experience for each position.”
  • Anthropic. Its guidance on using Claude when applying, last updated July 10, 2025, says of applications and résumés: “Please create your first draft yourself, then use Claude to refine it.”
  • Palantir. A new grad ad, outside our 30, asks for a résumé or CV as a PDF and thoughtful answers to its application questions.

None of the 30 ads asks for a cover letter, on a keyword check of the ads read on November 21, 2026.

Four of the 30 ads read on November 21, 2026 describe a step. Cresta names a CoderPad assessment after résumé review. Glean names an AI exercise or discussion. Deepgram asks you to walk through a workflow you built. AssemblyAI mentions a take-home. Scale AI states a 90-day wait before it reconsiders someone for the same role.

What can you leave off or not worry about?

Only where the counts support it. Each count is of the 30 ads read on November 21, 2026.

  • A missing degree. Twenty of the 30 ads mention no degree. Of the 10 that do, 5 make it a plain requirement, 2 state an alternative and 3 call it preferred or nice to have. Deepgram accepts “a record of building that speaks for itself”. If you have a degree, keep it short.
  • A cover letter. None of the 30 ads asks for one. That does not show one hurts.
  • A security clearance. Three of the 30 ads mention one.
  • A long tool list. Apart from “API” or “APIs” (17 ads), no named tool is in more than 13 of the 30 ads. Match tools to the ad in front of you.

A short checklist

Our list. Each count is of the 30 ads read on November 21, 2026.

  • Customer work is visible in the top third (28 of 30 ads have such a line).
  • Each role leads with something in production (18 of 30 use the word).
  • Languages are listed, Python first if you use it (23 of 30 name it).
  • Years are easy to total (23 of 30 state a number).
  • One project shows ownership from unclear brief to delivery (“ownership” is in 18 of 30, “ambiguity” or “ambiguous” in 17).
  • No cover letter unless the ad asks (0 of 30 ask).

In practice

If you are applying. Read the ad, then mirror its own nouns where they are true of you. Check the range before you apply in our pay by employer briefing, and see how to become a forward deployed engineer if you are moving across from another role.

Where to send it. Browse open forward deployed engineer jobs, or use the form on our forward deployed engineers page. We read every résumé sent. We contact people only about specific roles.

What are the limits of this reading?

  • One day, 30 ads. It is a reading of mostly AI and tech employers on November 21, 2026. It is not a market measure and shows no trend.
  • One ad per employer. Another ad from the same employer could ask for different things.
  • The cut was partly arbitrary. Sixteen employers tied on 2 postings and 5 were taken by file order.
  • Two of the 30 are finance roles (Rogo and Harvey), which ask for a finance background under a forward deployed title.
  • Ads are not screening rules. No ad says how a résumé is scored, and employers publish little on that.

Questions

  • What should a forward deployed engineer résumé include?
  • Our advice is to show shipped software and customer work side by side. Of the 30 ads read on November 21, 2026, 28 have a requirement line naming customers, clients, users, stakeholders or audiences, and 18 use the word “production”. Twenty-three state a number of years, so make your dates easy to add up.
  • Do I need a degree to apply for forward deployed engineer roles?
  • Often not, going by the ads. Of the 30 ads read on November 21, 2026, 20 mention no degree and 10 mention one. It is a plain requirement in 5, a requirement with a stated alternative in 2 and preferred or nice to have in 3. Check the ad you are applying to.
  • Which programming languages should I list?
  • List the ones you can work in, and put Python first if it is one of them. Of the 30 ads read on November 21, 2026, 25 name at least one language. Python is named in 23, TypeScript in 10 and JavaScript in 9. Five ads name no language at all.
  • Do I need a cover letter?
  • None of the 30 ads read on November 21, 2026 asks for one, on a keyword check. The OpenAI and Anthropic application pages read the same day do not mention cover letters either. That does not show a cover letter hurts. It shows the ads do not ask.
  • How many years of experience do the ads ask for?
  • Of the 30 ads read on November 21, 2026, 23 state a number. The floor is 1+ in 2 ads, 2+ in 5, 3+ in 6, 4+ in 1, 5+ in 7, 6+ in 1 and 7+ in 1. Seven state no number, and none states zero.
