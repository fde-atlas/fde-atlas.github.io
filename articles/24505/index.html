What Is a Solution Architect? | Job Description and Differences from FDEs and Pre-Sales

Original title: ソリューションアーキテクトとは｜仕事内容と、FDE・プリセールスとの違い

FDE Consulting · 2026-10-21 · 8 min read

Summary

Based on public job descriptions from AWS and OpenAI, this article explains what a Solutions Architect (SA) does and how the role differs from presales, FDE, and PM. The key distinction is hands-on scope: SAs advise, design, and validate but do not build customer systems, while FDEs write production code themselves. The author compares typical tasks, AWS's SA sub-roles, OpenAI's Tokyo Applied AI Architect versus FDE openings, and offers guidance on which role a buyer should engage depending on whether they have in-house builders.

Key points

  • SAs design and advise but AWS explicitly states they do not perform implementation for customers.
  • AWS splits SAs into Account, Specialist, and Partner roles, with 1–3 or 10–15 active accounts each.
  • Presales ends at winning the deal; SAs stay involved through deployment and optimization.
  • OpenAI's Applied AI Architect hands implementation to delivery teams; FDEs code directly when needed.
  • Moving from SA to FDE usually requires gaining production coding and operations experience.

A Solutions Architect (SA) is a technical role at cloud and software companies that designs ways to solve customers' problems using the company's own products. Together with sales, they meet customers, persuade them to adopt the product from a technical standpoint, and also answer technical questions that arise during implementation.

As generative AI companies expand their headcount, roles with names similar to SA have increased: presales, sales engineer, AI architect, and FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer). All of them stand between customers and technology, but they differ in how much hands-on work they do and when their job ends. Based on the role descriptions published by AWS and OpenAI, this article organizes what an SA does and how the role differs from similar ones. The FDE role itself is explained in What is an FDE? The meaning and work of a Forward Deployed Engineer.

What a Solutions Architect Does

Among the public information describing the SA role, the most specific is AWS's Japanese recruiting page. It describes the SA as a position that engages in discussions with senior customers (including CxO level) to influence cloud strategy, while at the same time holding technical discussions with customers' architects and developers, verifying feasibility together with customers, and leading both migration of existing systems and innovation using new technologies.

The same page lists the following as typical SA tasks.

Typical tasks (AWS recruiting page)Supplementary details
Discovering cloud adoption opportunities through customer interviewsListen to customers' problems and find areas the product can solve
Discussions to overcome technical challengesThink through design and migration hurdles together with customers' engineers
Architecture design support and technical validationDraw up configurations and verify whether they work
Researching technology trends and creating technical contentProduce documents, blog posts, and samples
Speaking at events and running webinarsCommunicate technology to external audiences

AWS divides the SA position broadly into Account SAs focused on handling customer accounts, Specialists responsible for specific technical domains, and Partner SAs focused on working with partners. The number of customers each SA handles also varies by position: some positions have one person covering 1-3 companies, while others cover 10-15 active customers on a rotating basis, the page says.

Important to understanding the SA role is that AWS states on the same page that SAs do not perform hands-on implementation on behalf of customers. They may provide sample code or give demos, but they are not commissioned or contracted to do work on customers' systems. Design and advice are the SA's domain; building is done by the customer's engineers or partner companies.

Difference from Presales

In Japan, SA and presales are sometimes used interchangeably. As I understand it, presales refers to people who, before a deal is won, team up with sales to handle proposals and product demos from a technical standpoint.

AWS's recruiting page notes that this is a generalization since practices may differ by company, but says that compared to presales, SAs maintain relationships with customers over long periods. Getting customers to choose the product is one important mission, but supporting problem-solving during development and construction, as well as optimization support after release, are also important parts of the job.

In my view, the difference between the two lies in where the work ends.

Job titles alone in job postings don't tell you which role it is. Before applying or placing an order, it's a good idea to check whether the same person will continue to be responsible after the deal is won.

Architects and FDEs at Generative AI Companies

Generative AI companies sometimes maintain SA-like roles and FDEs separately. Lining up OpenAI's Tokyo job postings makes the difference between the two roles clear. As of October 21, 2026, OpenAI's careers page lists job openings for both an Applied AI Architect and a Forward Deployed Engineer based in Tokyo.

The architect is accountable for technical outcomes while handing implementation to a specialized team. The FDE builds things themselves all the way to running in production. Both are deeply involved at customer sites, but they differ in how much hands-on work they do. Differences Between FDE, PM, and SA

Let me organize the above alongside similar roles. Since every company defines these roles differently, this is my own way of organizing things.

SA and FDE are alike in that they both start from the customer's problem, but differ in whether they build. In my view, SA is a job of spreading the "correct usage" of the company's own products to customers, while FDE is a job of building "something that works" tailored to the customer's business. The difference between FDE and SES, SIer, consulting firms, and the like is written in What's the difference between FDE and SES?, the difference from AI engineers in What is an AI engineer?, and the difference from GTM engineers who build sales systems in What is a GTM engineer?.

Who is suited to be a solution architect, and what to learn

In my view, someone suited to SA finds more enjoyment in explaining designs and having others build them than in building things themselves. It also requires the ability to switch, on the same day, between talks aimed at executives and detailed talks aimed at engineers.

There are three main things to learn.

  • Deep knowledge of the products you handle and the technologies around them. For cloud, that means everything from networking and databases to security
  • The ability to explain designs with words and diagrams. Communicate enough that the customer could build it themselves
  • Understanding the customer's business. Connect technology choices to the customer's business metrics when you talk

As a benchmark for knowledge, cloud companies offer certifications. AWS has AWS Certified Solutions Architect at the Associate and Professional levels, and the Professional exam guide recommends two or more years of experience designing and implementing cloud solutions on AWS. I believe certifications are just an entry-level benchmark, and experience explaining designs in front of customers is valued more.

There is also a path from SA to FDE, taking on the building as well. In that case, what tends to be missing is experience writing and operating code that runs in production. I've summarized the path to becoming an FDE in How to become an FDE?.

Choosing between SA and FDE from the client's perspective

From the perspective of a company adopting AI or cloud, a product vendor's SA is someone who teaches you the correct usage of that product from the design stage onward. However, as AWS explicitly states, SAs do not build the customer's systems. Once the design is decided, the client must separately decide who will build it and who will operate it in production.

In my view, if the products to use are already decided and you have people in-house who can build, SA advice is enough. If you have no one to build, or you want to decide from the point of where in your business to apply AI, you need someone who will take on the building as well.
