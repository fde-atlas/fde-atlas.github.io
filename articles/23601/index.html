Can you become a Forward Deployed Engineer without a computer science degree?

Original title: 没有计算机学位能当前线部署工程师吗

FDE Times · 2026-10-16 · 5 min read

Summary

Comparing FDE job postings from Palantir, Anthropic, and Google, this article argues that degree requirements are looser than expected: Anthropic and Google accept equivalent experience in place of a bachelor's degree, while Palantir only lists relevant majors as preferred. However, the non-negotiable requirements are strict—production LLM experience, customer-facing work years (1 to 8 depending on company), and specific skills like MCP and AI agents. The author advises candidates without CS degrees to replace the credential with verifiable evidence: an agent project repo with evals, a self-built MCP server, incident-response stories, and independent products, choosing postings that match their actual years of experience.

Key points

  • Anthropic and Google accept equivalent experience instead of a bachelor's degree; Palantir lists majors as preferred only.
  • Hard requirements have no 'or' clause: Anthropic wants 4 years of customer-facing work, Google 8 years of development.
  • Palantir's fellowship for non-college graduates accepted 22 of 500+ applicants, showing selectivity remains high.
  • Recommended substitutes: agent repos with evals, self-built MCP servers, incident stories, and solo products.
  • Years of experience matter most: early-career candidates should target Palantir over Google's Staff level.

Anthropic and Google accept equivalent experience, Palantir only lists majors as a plus, and the key is what you put on your resume in place of that diploma.

Key Takeaways

  • Anthropic and Google allow equivalent experience in place of a bachelor's degree. Palantir only lists relevant majors as "preferred" and does not state a degree requirement.
  • The hard requirements differ across the three companies: Palantir requires 1+ years of work experience after graduation; Anthropic requires LLM experience in production environments plus 4+ years in customer-facing technical roles; Google requires 8 years of software development experience plus MCP and generative AI agent experience.
  • Relaxing degree requirements does not mean it is easy to get in: of the 500+ applicants to Palantir's scholarship program for people who never attended college, only 22 were admitted.

In Palantir's job posting for Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE), a technical background in fields such as computer science, math, or physics is only listed as "preferred." On the experience side, the posting requires at least one year of relevant work experience after graduation. The page does not state a degree requirement, nor does it include an "equivalent experience" clause.

Anthropic and Google phrase it differently: both write "or equivalent experience" directly next to the bachelor's degree line. So people without a CS degree still have a chance. But right next to that lenient degree requirement, each company has set some requirements with no "or" at all.

If you studied economics or physics, taught yourself to code, or never finished college, the real question is not whether you can apply, but what you put on your resume in place of the degree, and whether those substitutes match the posting's actual requirements.

Degree requirements are looser than you think

Anthropic's job posting for Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) in Applied AI is blunt: the minimum requirement is a bachelor's degree in a relevant field or equivalent experience. Another Anthropic FDE posting goes even further.

That posting accepts a bachelor's degree, or an equivalent combination of education, training, and experience. The field of study can be demonstrated through coursework, training, or professional experience.

Google's Staff Forward Deployed Engineer role states in its minimum requirements "a bachelor's degree or equivalent practical experience." A master's or PhD is only listed under "preferred." Palantir's FDSE posting for new graduates likewise only lists majors such as computer science and electrical and computer engineering as preferred qualifications.

A notable exception is Palantir's FDSE internship role for its US government business. Candidates must be enrolled in or have completed a technical degree, though AI experience can come from coursework, projects, or extracurricular activities. Read every posting carefully: within the same company, different levels have different rules.

Palantir goes further than its job postings. According to Fortune, the company launched the Meritocracy Fellowship, a paid, four-month program for high school graduates who never attended college. CEO Alex Karp has said that having worked at Palantir is the most weighty "credential" in the tech industry, far beyond anything else.

But look at the ratio: more than 500 people applied, and only 22 were selected. This program is not part of the FDE hiring process, so you cannot extrapolate FDE acceptance rates from it. It only points to one thing worth weighing: removing the degree from the requirements does not mean the company is any less selective.

Where is the real filter?

Read the four postings side by side and cross out the degree line, and the remaining requirements share one thing in common: every one of them has to be proven by you, not conferred by someone else.

That last column is exactly where people without a CS degree may unexpectedly have the edge. A degree proves you studied algorithms, but not that you deployed an agent to production or built an MCP server. And those are precisely the requirements Anthropic and Google put in the part with no "or."

Three things a degree cannot prove

To be fair: Gergely Orosz, citing Box CEO Aaron Levie in The Pragmatic Engineer, notes that FDEs need solid technical foundations, and people in this role often majored or minored in CS. But Levie also mentions two other things: systems thinking and an understanding of the business. Neither of those is printed on a diploma.

Picture an engineer: an economics background, self-taught Python, three years of backend work at a logistics company. The resume currently opens with "Bachelor of Arts in Economics," followed by a string of technical buzzwords. The person screening resumes reads the first line, sees the major does not match, and very likely puts it down right there.

But those three years can be retold as evidence. In the projects section, attach a repo link: an agent that answers questions about order status, with data fetched through an MCP server the engineer wrote themselves. That repo maps exactly onto the MCP and agent experience Google lists in its minimum requirements.

The repo contains about 50 test questions with expected answers, and the README records accuracy before and after prompt changes. The job posting does not ask for such an eval set. Even so, it is the most concise way to prove the agent works outside a demo environment, and the closest thing to what Anthropic calls production LLM experience.

The work experience section no longer lists technologies but tells the story of an incident. The operations team reported a data problem; you sat with them, found the cause, fixed the bug, and changed how they worked.

Anthropic's posting does not say whether serving internal users counts toward the 4+ years of "customer-facing" work, so do not assume it does. But the story is still the best material for showing you know how to collaborate with people who have a problem. The "Bachelor of Arts in Economics" line moves to the very bottom of the page.

If you have ever built a small product on your own, even a failed one, put it on your resume. You had to wire systems together yourself and figure out who needed the product and why. That is exactly the proof of the two capabilities Levie described that a degree cannot demonstrate.

Years of experience are the real ladder

Among the hard requirements, the biggest gap between the three companies is years of experience. Palantir requires 1+ years of work experience, Anthropic requires 4+ years of customer-facing technical experience, and Google's Staff-level role requires 8 years of software development experience.

So if you're just starting your second year of work, Palantir's FDSE role is more realistic than Anthropic's role or Google's Staff position.

Levie predicts that more and more FDE roles will become available to new graduates. This is a prediction, not data, so still choose roles based on your actual years of experience.

One detail worth noting. The phrase "post-graduation" in Palantir's posting suggests that although no degree requirement is stated, the company still has a common path in mind. Be ready to explain your background in two sentences—brief, and no need to apologize.

A degree is something someone else grants you once. Evidence is something you accumulate yourself, keep updating, and can present with a single link. For an FDE, this is the part of the job posting without an "or."
