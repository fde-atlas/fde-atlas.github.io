FDE Reverse Question Examples | Criteria for Evaluating Questions and Answers by Interview Stage

Original title: FDE 逆質問の例｜面接段階別に見る質問と回答の判断基準

FDE総研 · 2026-11-26 · 12 min read

Summary

This FDE総研 article explains how to prepare reverse questions (candidate questions) for Forward Deployed Engineer interviews. It argues FDE interviews differ from typical software engineer interviews: outcomes depend heavily on customer relationships, on-site discretion, and how performance is evaluated, so questions should target those areas rather than tech stacks. It organizes example questions into five categories (work, discretion, customers, evaluation, growth), advises which topics fit first-round, final, and offer-stage interviews, tailors priorities by experience level, current role (SIer/consulting), and English ability, and proposes four criteria for judging employer answers: concrete examples, disclosure of constraints, numbers, and consistency across interviewers.

Key points

  • FDE reverse questions should probe customer-facing responsibility, discretion, and evaluation, not just tech stacks.
  • Example questions are grouped into five categories: work, discretion, customers, evaluation, growth.
  • Match questions to stage: daily reality in first rounds, expectations in finals, terms and evaluation at offer stage.
  • Judge answers by concrete examples, disclosed constraints, numbers, and consistency across interviewers.
  • Priorities vary by background: junior candidates ask about training; SIer/consulting switchers ask about proposal authority and implementation scope.

How do reverse questions for FDE interviews differ from general engineer interviews?

FDE Soken defines reverse questions for FDEs as an opportunity to verify "discretion and scope of responsibility at the customer site."

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role popularized by Palantir. FDEs embed themselves at customer sites and take end-to-end ownership, from identifying problems through implementation to operational adoption, in the first person. In a typical SWE (software engineer) interview, reverse questions usually end with confirming the tech stack or development culture. For FDEs, however, post-hire performance is strongly influenced by "the relationship with the customer," "discretion in the field," and "how you are evaluated." As far as VACAN has observed in practice, deciding to join without asking about these three points tends to lead to significant mismatches after joining.

FDE Soken's comparison framework (as compiled by the editorial team as of November 2026) is a method of dividing the focus of reverse questions into the following four layers.

Layer to verifyTypical reverse question in a SWE interviewReverse question in an FDE interview
Scope of workWhich features or products you will work onWhich parts of the customer's problem, from definition to operations, you will own
DiscretionFreedom in technology choicesHow much you can decide yourself on proposals to customers and scope changes
Customer contactEssentially none (via the PdM)Which customer stakeholders you talk to, how often, and in what capacity
EvaluationDevelopment output and code qualityHow customer outcomes and company revenue are linked to your evaluation

Why questions about the tech stack alone are not enough

In FDE interviews, questions about the tech stack alone do not reveal the actual nature of the work after joining, making questions about customer contact indispensable.

An FDE's work changes in substance for each customer even when using the same technical foundation. For this reason, asking "how do you receive customer requirements, and where do you push back?" gets you closer to the reality of the job than asking "what do you use?" For technical questions, framing them around data integration pain points and constraints of the customer environment makes it easier to draw out on-the-ground realities.

The value of asking about the boundary of responsibility with the customer

Asking about the boundary of responsibility with the customer lets you understand in advance the FDE's post-hire discretion and where the burden falls when problems occur.

For example, when an incident occurs in the customer's environment, who handles first response greatly affects how you work. If the answer is vague, the boundary of responsibility may not be well organized as an organization. However, that answer alone does not determine whether the company is good or bad. Judge it together with answers to other questions.

What are examples of reverse questions to verify in an FDE interview?

FDE Soken organizes the reverse questions to verify in an FDE interview into five categories: "work, discretion, customers, evaluation, and growth."

For each category, here are example questions you can use as-is. Adapt the wording to your own situation.

CategoryExample reverse questionWhat to verify
WorkFor the most recent FDE, which part of the process from identifying customer problems to operational adoption do you spend the most time on?The actual allocation of work
DiscretionWhen a customer requests a requirement change, who makes the decision on scope?Decision-making authority
CustomersAre the customer's counterparts mainly frontline staff or executives?The level of customer contact
EvaluationTo what extent are FDE results tied to customer outcomes versus company revenue?The basis of the evaluation system
GrowthWhat career paths have FDEs taken when moving to other roles or into management?Career exit options

Questions to verify work and discretion

Questions about work and discretion are used to draw out, through concrete examples, how much of the customer's problem an FDE can decide and implement on their own.

Asking "Is there discretion?" only gets you an abstract answer like "Yes." Asking for an example, such as "Could you tell me about one recent project where the scope was changed?" reveals the quality of the answer. In VACAN's practice as well, we find that when candidates ask example-based questions, aligning expectations tends to go more smoothly.

Questions to verify evaluation and growth

Questions about evaluation and growth are used to confirm whether an FDE's results are measured by customer outcomes or by company revenue.

FDE is a role where contributions at customer sites are hard to see. In an organization where evaluation is based only on the volume of development output, the more deeply you engage in solving customer problems, the more disadvantaged you may become. Asking about the evaluation system is not rude. It is a natural question to prevent mismatches after joining.

How should reverse questions for FDEs be used differently in the first interview, final interview, and offer meeting?

FDE Soken recommends using FDE reverse questions for work realities in the first interview, expectations in the final interview, and conditions and evaluation in the offer meeting.

Depending on the interview stage, the position of the person you are talking to and the range they can answer about change. Rather than repeating the same questions at every stage, tailoring your questions to the person raises the quality of the information.

  • First interview (frontline FDEs and engineers): Ask about daily work, interactions with customers, and team structure
  • Final interview (managers and executives): Ask about the FDE organization's direction, expected roles, and future business plans
  • Offer meeting (HR and hiring managers): Confirm the evaluation system, compensation structure, and conditions for assignment and business trips

What to ask in the first interview

In the first interview, it is effective to ask about a frontline FDE's typical day and the reality of customer interactions, using concrete recent examples.

Frontline staff know best the daily struggles and difficult moments in customer interactions. Asking "What was the most challenging customer interaction you've had recently?" reveals realities that formal policies do not show. However, the answer reflects one person's experience and may not represent the whole organization. Asking the same question to multiple people and looking for commonalities improves accuracy.

What to ask in the final interview and offer meeting

In the final interview, it is appropriate to confirm expectations at the organizational level, and in the offer meeting, to confirm that evaluation and conditions are documented in writing.

Your final interview partner is the person responsible for business decisions. Questions like "Which customer segments do you plan to expand the FDE organization into going forward?" are appropriate. In the offer meeting, what matters is whether what you heard verbally can be confirmed in writing. For items that directly affect your life after joining, such as the evaluation system or frequency of business trips, ask to receive them in writing.

By what criteria should the quality of answers to reverse questions be judged?

FDE Soken proposes judging the quality of answers based on four criteria: "concrete examples, disclosure of constraints, presence of numbers, and consistency."

Company answers include expressions intended to attract candidates. To discern them, it is effective to look at how "concrete" the content is. The criteria below are the editorial judgment axes organized by FDE Soken, as evaluated as of November 26, 2026.

CriterionCharacteristics of a good answerCharacteristics of an answer to be cautious about
Concrete examplesSpeaks concretely about recent projects or the profile of team membersEnds with "we handle things flexibly"
Disclosure of constraintsSpeaks frankly about difficulties and challengesTalks only about the good points
Presence of numbersProvides figures within what can be disclosed, such as man-hours, duration, and headcountEvades questions about numbers
ConsistencyGives answers in the same direction even when interviewers changeStories contradict each other between interviewers

Look at concrete examples and disclosure of constraints

Answers with concrete examples and disclosure of constraints frankly convey the reality of FDE work and are trustworthy material.

From VACAN's practitioner perspective, interviewers who proactively talk about challenges and difficulties give the impression of a smaller gap after joining. Of course, this is a general tendency and does not apply to all companies. There may also be cases where concrete examples cannot be shared for confidentiality reasons, so also check whether the reason for not being able to share is explained.

Cross-check consistency across multiple interviewers

Compare answers from multiple interviewers and confirm whether the explanations about the FDE role and evaluation point in the same direction.

If the "closeness to customers" you heard in the first interview contradicts the "organizational policy" you heard in the final interview, there may be a gap in awareness between the field and management. The discrepancy itself is not necessarily a bad sign; it is a common situation in growing organizations. However, looking at how the interviewer explains that gap reveals the organization's transparency.

How do FDE reverse questions change based on years of experience, current job, and English ability?

FDE Soken organizes the priority reverse questions to ask according to each combination of years of experience, current job, and English ability.

When conditions overlap in questions, it is important to prioritize. Here we also show how to judge when conditions conflict, such as "short experience but high English ability."

Combination of conditionsWhat to prioritize askingReason
Under 3 years of experience, SaaS development role, high English abilityTraining system and presence of mentorsBecause mechanisms to compensate for lack of experience determine growth
5+ years of experience, SIer background, medium English abilityAuthority to make proposals to customers and evaluation systemTo confirm the difference in discretion when moving from contracted work to proposal-driven work
5+ years of experience, consulting background, high English abilityHow much involvement in implementationTo confirm the burden of moving from proposal-centered to implementation-centered work
Under 3 years of experience, low English abilityFrequency of situations using English and supportTo understand in advance the level required for the job

When experience is short but English ability is high

People with short experience but high English ability should prioritize asking about the training system and the gradual delegation of customer interactions, rather than viewing English ability as a strength.

When conditions conflict, the priority is "confirming mechanisms to compensate for shortcomings" first, and "promoting your strengths" second. English ability is a strength that is easily valued, but in the FDE field, understanding customer issues and implementation skills have a more direct impact on results. The less experience you have, the more reassuring it is to ask specifically how much you will handle on your own in the first six months.

When your current job is at an SIer or consulting firm

People from SIers or consulting firms should focus on confirming the major differences from their current job (proposal authority, scope of implementation, evaluation criteria).

People from SIers are used to implementation after requirements are fixed, whereas in FDE they are involved from the requirements definition stage. People from consulting firms are used to making proposals but may have little experience taking responsibility for implementation. In either case, narrowing your questions to the differences from your current job helps organize what you should ask.

Differences in questions by company size

The larger the company, the more you should prioritize asking about the evaluation system and stability of assignments; the smaller the company, the more you should prioritize discretion and business direction.

In FDE organizations at large companies, systems are well established, but the degree of freedom in customer interactions may be limited. At startups, discretion is large, but systems are often underdeveloped. Differences in size change the weight of the questions you should ask, but neither is superior to the other.

What are NG examples to avoid in FDE reverse questions?

FDE Soken organizes examples to avoid in FDE reverse questions into "questions you could find the answer to yourself," "questions about compensation only," and "vague questions."

NG examples are less about the question itself being bad and more about damaging your impression through how or in what order you ask.

NG exampleProblemExample of rephrasing
"Please tell me about your company's business"Content available in public information; seen as lack of preparation"I read the published case studies and would like to know more about XX"
Asking only "Is there a lot of overtime?"Interest in compensation stands out too much"I would like to know about working styles during busy periods and the mechanisms for adjusting afterward"
"Is it an environment where I can grow?"Too vague; you won't get a concrete answer"Please tell me the scope of responsibility for an FDE in their first year"
"Nothing in particular"May be taken as a sign of low interestPrepare at least three questions in advance

Questions you could research yourself and vague questions

Questions you could research yourself and vague questions give an impression of lack of preparation and also fail to draw out useful information.

Before asking, check the company's public information, recruiting pages, and presentation materials. For example, Palantir's recruiting information can be found on the official recruiting page. Then, asking as "points I want to know more about after reading the public information" conveys both preparation and interest.

Questions focused only on compensation

Questions about compensation are important, but combining them with questions about the work itself conveys your interest as an FDE.

Checking salary and days off is information you need to make a decision about joining. What you should avoid is asking only about compensation without touching on the work itself. If you first ask about the job and evaluation, and then bring up compensation as part of that flow, the conversation will feel natural. Details of the terms are best confirmed at the offer meeting.

How should you prepare reverse questions for an FDE role?

FDE Soken recommends preparing reverse questions for FDE roles in four steps: "checking public information → prioritizing → assigning by stage → comparing answers."

  • Read the company's public information (recruiting pages, presentation materials, case study articles) and write down what you don't understand
  • Sort the items you've written down into five categories: work, discretion, customers, evaluation, and growth
  • Assign 3 to 5 questions to each interview stage
  • Take notes on the answers and later compare consistency across interviewers

Checking public information and sorting into the five categories

Preparing reverse questions for an FDE role starts with the task of sorting only the points you can't find out from public information into the five categories.

Read the recruiting pages and presentation materials, and sort your questions into work, discretion, customers, evaluation, and growth. Any category that remains unfilled is an area you haven't verified for yourself. That is what you should prioritize asking about in the interview.

Assigning by stage and recording answers

For FDE reverse questions, assigning 3 to 5 questions per interview stage, recording the answers, and comparing them later will improve the accuracy of your judgment.

You don't need to ask every question. If time is limited, focus on the one or two categories that are most uncertain for your situation. The example questions in this article are general patterns organized by FDE Soken as an editorial decision. In actual interviews, please adapt the wording to each company's circumstances.
