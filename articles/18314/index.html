[Event Report] Beyond "Making It Work": What Is Asked of the Forward Deployed Engineer Is the Design Skill to Stop That One Dangerous Case

Original title: 【イベントレポート】「動く」の先へ。FDE（Forward Deployed Engineer）に問われるのは、危険な1件を止める設計力

プレスリリース・ニュースリリース配信シェアNo.1｜PR TIMES · 2026-11-05 · 5 min read

Summary

An event report from AXR covering an AI-driven development study session held on October 19, 2026 at NeuroHub in Tokyo, hosted by Kazunari Ito of Third Scope. Speakers from SoftBank, Baidu (Simeji), and Visnu discussed what forward deployed engineers (FDEs) must do now that AI can quickly produce working software: decide what to delegate to AI and who stops it. Visnu's Chiba proposed checking that AI can read internal data, measure results, and roll back failures, keeping irreversible tasks like fraud-related transfers out of AI's hands. SoftBank's Kimura framed the FDE's core role as translation—converting a customer's real goals into AI objectives, actions, and evaluation criteria—and argued a company's reputation hinges on stopping the one dangerous item out of 500 processed invoices.

Key points

  • Event held October 19, 2026 at NeuroHub, Tokyo, hosted by Third Scope COO Kazunari Ito.
  • Baidu's Furuya built a family learning app via voice input: 3 days hands-on, about 8 hours of AI runtime.
  • Visnu's Chiba's three checks before delegating to AI: readable, measurable, reversible.
  • SoftBank's Kimura: FDEs translate customer goals into AI objectives; stopping one dangerous invoice matters more than 499 correct ones.
  • Panelist Watare runs 5-6 projects solo using specialized agents for security, performance, and operability.

Speakers from SoftBank, Baidu, and Visnu discuss what to leave to AI and the mechanisms for stopping it

AXR Inc. (Head office: Shibuya-ku, Tokyo; Representative Director: Kazunari Maeda; hereinafter "AXR") has published a report on the "AI-Driven Development Study Session" (#Autumn FDE Study Session), an event for engineers and executives held on Monday, October 19, 2026 at its venue, NeuroHub.

The event was hosted by Kazunari Ito, Director and COO of Third Scope Inc., with AXR providing NeuroHub as the venue. Through three talks and a panel discussion, it summarizes what is required of FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers: engineers who go into companies' front lines, identify challenges, and build AI-based solutions).

Background

- With AI-driven development, it has become possible to build working things in a short time. Meanwhile, on the front lines of companies, there is ongoing discussion about what to leave to AI, what not to, and who should stop it.

- Speaker Kimura said, "The talk about it being usable has actually become a given," and noted that what is being questioned now is whether it can be "entrusted" to AI.

- AXR accepts events at NeuroHub with three aims: expanding horizontal connections, enabling participants to learn from each other, and creating new business opportunities.

Talk Content

1. Using voice input to hand requirements to AI without trimming them down

(Yuji Furuya, Product Manager, Product Department, Product Business Division, Baidu Inc.)

Furuya, who leads the keyboard app "Simeji," shared his experience of building a learning app for families using voice input alone, from the perspective of a non-engineer.

He spent three days hands-on and about eight hours running AI. Because "with manual input, you organize things in your head and trim down the requirements," he said he first spoke out whatever came to mind and left the organizing to AI.

2. Before entrusting AI, verify that it can "read, measure, and roll back"

(Ryosuke Chiba, Founder of Visnu Inc. and ERAS Inc.)

Chiba said, "How much you can entrust to AI is determined by whether the work's requirements are solidified."

What he agrees on with clients covers three things: whether AI can "read" internal information, whether effects can be "measured," and whether things can be "rolled back" when they fail. He said work that cannot be rolled back, such as transfers to fraudulent accounts, is not entrusted to AI.

3. The essence of an FDE is "translation": designing outcomes, actions, and accountability

(Nobuyuki Kimura, Section Manager, Digital Worker Promotion Department, Customer Success Division, SoftBank Corp.)

Kimura said, "I believe the essence of an FDE is translation," explaining that an FDE translates a client's true objectives into AI goals, actions, and evaluation criteria, and implements them.

Using the example of an AI that automatically processes 500 invoices, he pointed out that even if 499 are processed correctly, a company's evaluation changes depending on whether it can stop the one dangerous case.

Panel Discussion: What Is Happening on the FDE Front Lines

The moderator was Ito, and the guest was Takuma Watarai, Technical Advisor at AXR.

Running everything solo, from planning to operations: Watarai, who serves as both CTO of an operating company and technical advisor at AXR and runs five to six projects in parallel, said that nowadays it is faster to leave things to AI agents, and he often handles everything alone, from planning to deployment, operations, and improvement.

Protecting quality with "specialized agents": Security, performance, and ease of operation are each verified by dedicated agents, and he noted that pull request rejections have dropped considerably.

Skills required of FDEs: In hiring, he said they look not so much at whether candidates can write code, but at their grasp of computer science fundamentals, their thinking on quality and security, and their ability to break business challenges down into goals and metrics.

The first step in starting AI-driven development in an organization: He explained that you should start by organizing skill, agent, and specification definitions as "templates" and making them runnable in a pipeline.

From the Perspective of Delivering FDE as a Business

Finally, Sho Kamiyama, Head of Sales at AXR, introduced AXR's AX (AI Transformation) business.

Kamiyama said that the strength of FDE and AXR lies in being able to "present the option of stopping something, rather than adding to what is already being done." He shared an example from a precision equipment manufacturer where the task of recreating design documents from old code was completed in one month instead of the planned two.

Future Plans

What the presentations had in common was the idea of deciding upfront what to entrust to AI and how to stop it, rather than prioritizing the speed at which AI builds things. AXR will continue to create venues at NeuroHub where practical, field-ready knowledge can be shared.
