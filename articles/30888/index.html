Atlassian Forward Deployed Engineer: A Key Role in Helping Customers Implement AI Software Iterations

Key Takeaways

Business Media｜By Yeh An-Ting, compiled from foreign reports

Atlassian has invested heavily in artificial intelligence in recent years, and to bridge the gap in enterprise AI adoption, its Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) team plays a key role. These engineers work with customers and cross-functional teams to turn vague business problems into clear technical solutions, such as using the Rovo tool to build AI agents, automated workflows, connectors, and repeatable deployment patterns. Over the past year, demand for FDE roles in the tech industry has grown by as much as 729%, showing that major AI companies broadly believe that engineers closer to customers are better positioned to define the success of AI applications.

Atlassian provides collaboration and productivity software solutions to more than 350,000 companies worldwide, with products including the project management tool Jira, the team collaboration platform Confluence, the video messaging tool Loom, and the kanban-style project management web app Trello. Members of the FDE team not only gain a deep understanding of customers' workflows and identify high-impact use cases, but also feed these insights back to the product development teams. Atlassian's Forward Deployed Engineers differ from traditional customer engineers or consulting roles: they work closer to the core codebase and customer needs, are part of the R&D organization, and take on deep technical work.

Hülya Pamukcu Crowell, the company's Lead Principal Forward Deployed Engineer, noted that through close integration with customer workflows, FDEs can identify high-value use cases and bring that experience back to the product teams. FDE lead Sri Ramya Mallipudi also mentioned that every customer's needs are different, which requires engineers to closely track the latest product features and focus on solving the real problems customers face.

The FDE team's practical results have been significant. For example, Atlassian's FDEs once helped online travel group Expedia archive 600,000 Confluence pages, saving more than 6,700 hours of labor, and this success led both parties to expand their collaboration. Abhinav Thomas, a Forward Deployed Engineer who joined in March this year, has worked with several customers who reported that his solutions delivered significant value, with one case saving about 80% of the time. In addition, when one FDE developed a sandbox (a testing platform that simulates the real environment) to production export and import path for Rovo agents, it resolved in one stroke the deployment obstacles faced by more than 15 enterprise accounts, including networking equipment leader Cisco.

Atlassian leverages its deep data foundation to build the Teamwork Graph, which accumulates more than 20 years of data and 150 billion unique connections, providing engineers with a shared knowledge layer for building AI workflows and helping customers see business results faster. Becoming an FDE is seen as a career accelerator, giving engineers a customer perspective and cross-functional judgment that in turn influence product strategy. Sri Ramya Mallipudi said that the pace of learning and experience accumulation for FDEs is unmatched by other roles. FDEs are defined as AI practitioners rather than AI engineers; they are experts at using AI and understanding how well it fits product applications, able to provide customers with the most suitable solutions and quickly move from discovering problems to realizing real benefits.
