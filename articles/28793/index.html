Key takeaways

  • FDE Hub argues that all current FDE data measures titles rather than the job itself. Its State of FDE 2026 survey closes on October 9.
  • The survey asks practitioners directly about time allocation between discovery and development, post-delivery responsibilities, reporting lines, and salary ranges. The analysis dimensions were locked in before the survey opened.
  • Perspective AI's survey of 1,500 FDEs remains the benchmark, but Tech Scoop notes it is an industry survey, not a census.

Every FDE number circulating today measures the title, not the job. FDE Hub makes this criticism right on the front page of its State of FDE 2026 survey. The survey is accepting responses until October 9.

For developers considering a move toward the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) path, this is more than a methodological issue. Public FDE data shapes your expectations of the job: how much time you'll spend with customers, who you'll report to, and how far your responsibilities extend. If the data measures the wrong thing, your expectations will be wrong too.

Why 1,500 responses still aren't enough

The largest benchmark to date is the survey released by Perspective AI in May 2026, covering 1,500 FDEs across 154 companies, with data collected from February to April. Two findings drew the most attention: the median share of FDEs' weekly time spent with customers is 47%; and 78% of respondents expect their teams to double in size or more by 2027.

In an analysis published in August, Tech Scoop cautioned that this data should be viewed as an industry survey, not a census of FDE practitioners. The salary figures reflect the top of the market, not the profession as a whole; the 78% figure records respondents' expectations, not hiring that anyone actually observed. This distinction matters: expected growth is not the same as open positions.

The problem goes beyond sampling. Recruiting firm Paraform points out that most FDEs never appear on job boards. If recruiters can't find them by title, data counted by title is unlikely to cover the whole profession either.

A simple test: does your current title include "FDE"? If not, how different is your day-to-day work from an FDE's?

Ask the people doing the job — and define the questions first

FDE Hub's solution sounds simple: ask the people doing the job. The survey focuses on what titles can't capture: the split of time between discovery and development, who owns the system after delivery, reporting lines, and salary ranges.

What's more notable is the methodology. Milos Mandic, who is running the survey, has committed that the analysis dimensions were determined before the survey opened and will be published alongside the report. This means the analysts gave up the option of slicing the data, after the results come in, in whichever way makes the best headline.

According to Paraform, this profession exists because the last mile of enterprise AI is written as code in customers' messy environments. This kind of work doesn't fit neatly into any single title, and it shouldn't be measured by titles. If you're doing this kind of work, October 9 is your last chance to add one more practitioner — from Vietnam or anywhere else — to the data on this profession.
