How to Build an FDE Evaluation System: Grade Criteria Tables, KPI Sheets, and Compensation Linkage | FDE Research Institute

Original title: FDEの評価制度の作り方｜等級基準表・KPIシート・報酬連動

FDE総研 · 2026-11-12 · 10 min read

Summary

This FDE Soken article explains how companies should build internal evaluation systems for Forward Deployed Engineers, a role popularized by Palantir with no public certification. It proposes evaluating FDEs on three axes—customer outcomes, technical implementation, and cross-boundary coordination—since SWE-style reviews miss most FDE value. It details a four-grade ladder based on the scope an engineer can be entrusted with, a KPI sheet separating outcome and behavioral metrics with a case-premise section agreed at period start, dual review cycles (per-project plus semiannual), and compensation linking base pay to grade and case results to bonuses. It also compares FDE evaluation with SWE, consulting, PdM, and SIer roles, and lists five common operational failures and fixes.

Key points

  • Evaluate FDEs on customer outcomes, technical implementation, and cross-boundary coordination.
  • Define grades by entrusted scope (G1–G4), not by skills or certifications.
  • KPI sheets need a premise section agreed at period start to isolate case difficulty.
  • Base pay follows grade; case results feed variable compensation like bonuses.
  • Small teams should use fewer grades and always include customer feedback in reviews.

  • Monthly: Record the confirmation of results and changes and issues in the business KPIs of clients handled in 1-on-1s. Not reflected in compensation.
  • Quarterly: Score three axes on the KPI sheet—client results, self-sufficiency, and reusable assets—with supporting documentation.
  • Quarterly: Align evaluators—adjust for differences in difficulty across clients and check whether NPS is skewed toward a subset of responses.
  • Annually: Determine grades and revise compensation—decide promotions and compensation based on four quarterly evaluations and certification status.

Use monthly records for development purposes, and reflect only quarterly and annual evaluations in compensation.

  • Make improvement in the client's business KPIs the primary metric
  • Evaluate whether the client has become able to operate on their own
  • Award bonus points for leaving behind assets reusable in other projects
  • Include the client's voice among the evaluators
  • Make product quality and development speed the primary metrics
  • Evaluate technical difficulty and design quality
  • Emphasize contributions and reviews within the team
  • Evaluators are mainly the internal engineering organization

FDEs are evaluated based on client-side results, while SWEs are evaluated based on product-side results.

There is no public certification for FDEs, and the FDE Research Institute organizes internal evaluation design around three axes: "client results, technical implementation, and cross-boundary coordination" (origin of the role: Palantir official blog).

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer role that embeds itself in the client's operations to implement software and takes responsibility until results are achieved. The role was popularized by Palantir as "Forward Deployed Software Engineer," and in Japan as well, job postings are appearing for positions responsible for AI and data platform implementations. However, if you apply an evaluation system designed for SWEs (software engineers) as-is, much of an FDE's value falls outside the scope of evaluation. This article, aimed at both HR and business leaders at companies that employ FDEs and the engineers being evaluated, walks through how to build a system step by step, from grade criteria tables and KPI sheets to compensation linkage.

What is an FDE evaluation system? How does certification evaluation differ from internal HR evaluation?

There are no national or industry-standard certifications for FDEs, and the FDE Research Institute views evaluation as a matter of "designing internal HR evaluation around three axes." A useful public framework is the IPA Digital Skill Standard (DSS).

Certification-based evaluation remains only a supplementary indicator for FDEs

Cloud certifications and statistics exams can prove foundational skills, but they cannot measure an FDE's client results.

The core of an FDE's work is redefining the client's business problems and embedding a working system into their operations. Certifications can be used for screening at hiring or as milestones in development plans. However, if grades are determined by "how many certifications someone holds," the evaluation results will drift away from the ability to deliver results in the client's environment. The FDE Research Institute recommends that even when certifications are included as evaluation items, their weighting should remain supplementary.

Structure internal HR evaluation around three axes: "client results, technical implementation, and cross-boundary coordination"

In the FDE Research Institute's comparison framework, dividing FDE contributions into three axes—client results, technical implementation, and cross-boundary coordination—minimizes gaps.

Evaluation axisWhat is examinedDifference from SWE evaluation
Client resultsWhether the implemented system moved the client's business metricsSWEs are often evaluated on metrics of the company's own product
Technical implementationWhether something that works was built quickly under field constraintsWeighs not just code quality but "whether it actually runs in the field"
Cross-boundary coordinationWhether consensus was built among the client, the company's product, and salesOften carries less weight in SWE evaluation

The third axis, "cross-boundary coordination," is hard to quantify. Even so, since FDEs are responsible for bringing field insights back into the company's product, removing this from evaluation risks letting insights accumulate only in individual hands.

How do you create grade-based evaluation criteria and KPI sheets for FDEs?

The FDE Research Institute recommends dividing grades into four levels and writing criteria for each grade in terms of "the scope of clients entrusted." For how to write job competency descriptions, the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's Occupational Competency Evaluation Standards are a useful reference.

Divide the grade criteria table by "breadth of assigned scope"

FDE grades are easier to manage when divided by the breadth of clients and projects one person can be entrusted with, rather than by the amount of technology they can handle.

GradeTitle (example)Assigned scopeMaterials for promotion decisions
G1FDEImplements existing deployment patterns under a senior's supervisionTrack record of stably operating assigned features in the client's environment
G2Senior FDECompletes a single client's deployment independentlyConsistent track record from problem redefinition through operation and adoption
G3Lead FDELeads multiple clients or large projectsTrack record of feeding field insights back into the product or deployment procedures
G4Principal FDECreates new deployment patterns for new industries and use casesTrack record of leaving behind templates other FDEs can replicate

Titles and the number of levels can be adjusted to fit company size. What matters is writing each grade's criteria in terms of "what can be entrusted," not "what can be done." Writing it this way reduces judgment drift even when evaluators change.

Fill in the KPI sheet by separating outcome metrics and behavioral metrics

If the KPI sheet separates the client's outcome metrics and the individual's behavioral metrics into different sections, you can isolate the effects of luck and project difficulty.

An FDE's results are heavily influenced by client-side circumstances (budget freezes, staff transfers, etc.). Evaluating on outcome metrics alone creates an incentive to avoid difficult projects. Therefore, the FDE Research Institute recommends structuring the KPI sheet as follows.

  • Project assumptions section: The client's industry and size, issues at the time of assignment, and difficulty (agreed upon in advance by the evaluator and the individual)
  • Outcome metrics section: Baseline values and targets for business metrics agreed with the client (e.g., processing time, uptime, number of users)
  • Behavioral metrics section: Problem redefinition, number of validation cycles, consensus building with the client, documentation
  • Feedback metrics section: Number of feedback items submitted to the company's product and how many were adopted
  • Self-evaluation and evaluator comments section: Both parties write which axes they weighted most

From a practical standpoint, whether the "assumptions section" is written at the start of the evaluation period greatly changes end-of-period acceptance. If difficulty is claimed only at the end, it tends to look like a retroactive excuse.

How should you design the FDE evaluation cycle and its reflection in compensation and promotion?

FDE Soken recommends a "dual cycle" that does not fix evaluations at twice per half-year but includes one retrospective at the end of each project. The basics of goal management are also consistent with the approach of the IPA Digital Skills Standard.

Combine project-level retrospectives with semi-annual evaluations

FDE projects vary in length from a few weeks to over a year, so semi-annual evaluations alone miss some achievements.

CycleTimingPurposeWhere recorded
Project retrospectiveMilestones in deployment (end of PoC, production launch, adoption confirmation)Recording facts, collecting customer feedbackResults and behavior columns of the KPI sheet
Semi-annual evaluationTwice a yearComparison against grade criteria, finalizing evaluationsHR evaluation system
Annual grade reviewOnce a yearDecisions on promotion and demotionGrade criteria table

The key is to treat project retrospectives not as evaluations themselves but as a separate place to record facts. This avoids memory-based discussions during semi-annual evaluations.

Link compensation as "grade-based base salary plus variable component based on project results"

If compensation sets base salary by grade and reflects project results in a variable component (such as bonuses), short-term fluctuations in results do not affect base salary.

If customer outcomes are tied directly to base salary, people who can choose their projects gain an advantage. It becomes easier to explain if you assign grades (the scope one can be entrusted with) to base salary and the results of that period to bonuses. When comparing compensation levels with other companies, specify the comparison point as "as of YYYY-MM" and be careful not to place job postings with the same job title but different scopes of work side by side.

How does designing an FDE evaluation system change based on years of experience, current role, English proficiency, and company size?

According to FDE Soken's analysis, the design of an evaluation system varies by four conditions, with company size in particular determining the number of grades and how evaluators are positioned. The role definitions in the IPA Digital Skills Standard can be used to confirm assumptions.

How initial grades are determined changes based on years of experience and current role

Do not determine grades by years of experience alone; judge the initial grade based on whether the person's current role includes experience delivering results at customer sites.

PrerequisitesPoints to note in evaluation design
SWE background with less than 3 years of experienceStart at G1, weight the technical implementation axis, and set cross-boundary coordination as a development goal
Background in SIer or IT consultingCross-boundary coordination tends to score high, so verify the technical implementation axis on real projects
PdM backgroundStrong at redefining problems; make whether they can handle implementation themselves an early point of discussion
Projects requiring English proficiencyTreat English not as a grade requirement but manage it separately as an assignment condition

If English proficiency is included as a grade requirement, promotion stalls for people delivering results on domestic projects. Even when there are projects involving overseas customers or English documentation, it is easier to handle English as a separate condition of "whether one can be assigned to that project."

Company size changes the number of grades and how evaluators are positioned

For companies with only a few FDEs, it is realistic to limit grades to two or three levels and ensure that customer feedback is always included in evaluations.

Even if a small organization creates four or more grade levels, there are no peers within the same grade, so criteria tend to become a formality. Conversely, once FDE headcount reaches several dozen, appointing dedicated FDE evaluators (lead FDE or above) and separating their evaluations from department managers' evaluations tends to stabilize decision quality.

How does FDE evaluation differ from evaluations for SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers? Who is it unsuitable for?

Under FDE Soken's comparison framework, FDE is the role among the five that places the greatest weight on "whether one worked at customer sites." The archetypes of these roles are described in the Palantir official blog.

Compare the evaluation focus of each role

Even when the goal is the same "value to customers," where results are measured differs by role, and FDE is measured by deployment and adoption.

RoleMain evaluation focusDifference from FDE
FDEDeployment and adoption in customer environments and changes in business metrics—
SWEQuality and development speed of own productsIndividual customer outcomes tend to be out of scope
IT consultantQuality of recommendations, customer satisfaction, utilization rateWhether they handle implementation themselves is a dividing line
PdMProduct metrics and roadmap achievementFocuses on overall optimization rather than individual customers
SIerDelivery per requirements, quality, profit marginWeighs execution of agreed requirements over redefining them

Because of these differences, adapting SIer or IT consultant evaluation systems tends to stop at "was it delivered?" or "was it recommended?", while adapting SWE systems makes work at customer sites hard to see.

Some people are not a good fit for the FDE evaluation system

For those who want to focus solely on technology or who prefer to build once requirements are fixed, FDE evaluation criteria can feel burdensome.

FDE evaluation assumes that people will redefine ambiguous problems themselves and spend time coordinating with customers. For those who want to be evaluated on depth of design or implementation, the cross-boundary coordination axis may feel like a heavy burden. When choosing a career, checking in interviews whether the axis on which you want to be evaluated matches the focus of that company's FDE evaluation can reduce post-hire mismatches.

What are the common pitfalls in operating an FDE evaluation system, and how do you address them?

FDE Soken has identified five typical failures, most of which stem from "not recording evaluation assumptions at the start of the period." For operating competency standards, the usage guidance in the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's Occupational Competency Evaluation Standards is also a useful reference.

Five common failures and countermeasures

Most failures occur in operation rather than system design, and almost all can be prevented by agreements at the start of the period and mechanisms for recording.

FailureWhat happensCountermeasure
Evaluating only on customer outcomesMore people avoid difficult projectsAdd an assumptions column to the KPI sheet and agree on difficulty at the start of the period
Evaluators do not see FDEs in the fieldThe loudest voices get rated highlyInclude customer feedback and lead FDE comments in project retrospectives
Not evaluating cross-boundary coordinationField insights do not flow back into the productMake contribution-back metrics a separate column
Operating on the same schedule and forms as SWE evaluationMid-project results go unrecordedRun project-level retrospectives separately
Number of grades does not match organization sizeCriteria become a formalityConsolidate or split grades according to headcount

Start with the assumption that the system will be reviewed annually

Because the definition of the FDE role tends to vary by company, do not aim for a finished form from the start; operate with the assumption of reviewing once a year.

The role of FDE is not yet clearly defined in Japan. In the first year of implementation, start with minimal grade criteria tables and KPI sheets. Then, at each semi-annual review, record "items where the evaluator's and the employee's understanding diverged," which will serve as material for revisions the following year. From a frontline perspective, what determines FDEs' sense of acceptance is less the quality of the evaluation system itself than whether the conversation to align assumptions at the start of each period is properly conducted every time.
