The Difference Between Forward Deployed Engineer and Forward Deployed Expert | Organizing the Two Core FDE Talent Profiles Across Five Dimensions

Original title: Forward Deployed Engineer と Forward Deployed Expert の違い｜FDE 2つの中核人材像を5つの軸で整理

AX for Revenue Institute · 2026-05-29 · 12 min read

Summary

This article from AX for Revenue Institute distinguishes two core FDE profiles: the Forward Deployed Engineer, a role inherited from Palantir and adopted by AI companies like Anthropic and OpenAI, and the Forward Deployed Expert, a concept proposed by AlphaDrive in Japan. The two are complementary rather than competing. Engineers' core asset is engineering skill; their output is product. Experts' core asset is business-development know-how; their output is redesigning revenue structures and breaking through internal decision-making. The article argues that since generative AI collapsed the cost of building finished products, defining what to build has become the scarce capability, citing MIT NANDA's finding that only 5% of enterprise AI tools reach production and McKinsey's finding that only 39% of AI-using firms report EBIT impact. Which role to deploy depends on where the bottleneck sits.

Key points

  • FDE Engineer and FDE Expert are parallel poles, not a hierarchy.
  • Engineers build products; Experts redesign revenue structures and drive decisions.
  • AI made building cheap, so defining what to build is now the scarce skill.
  • MIT NANDA: only 5% of enterprise AI tools reach production.
  • Choose by bottleneck: implementation issues need Engineers, direction or budgeting issues need Experts.

The difference between FDE=Engineer and FDE=Expert lies not in "what they can build," but in "who determines what to build and who breaks through internal decision-making." The two are not opposing concepts; they are two distinct core talent profiles that stand side by side on the front lines of business implementation in the AI era.

Forward Deployed Engineer is the standard term inherited by the world's leading AI companies. Meanwhile, another core talent profile is emerging to stand alongside it: Forward Deployed Expert. What is the difference between the two, and which one does your company need? This article organizes the differences across 5 dimensions, dispels common misconceptions, and shows how to combine them in practice.

First, a premise: the two are not in conflict

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is an engineer who is embedded at the customer's site, writing code while converting problems into products. Starting with Palantir, this is the core talent profile inherited by the world's leading AI companies, including Anthropic and OpenAI. Forward Deployed Expert is another core talent profile built upon that foundation of respect.

The two are not in conflict. In fact, confirming their commonalities first makes the contours of their differences clearer.

First, both are Forward Deployed. They are embedded at the customer's site and make decisions amid the realities on the ground. They do not exchange specifications remotely from headquarters.

Second, both use AI as their implementation weapon. Since the collapse of the cost of building finished products with generative AI (Completion Cost Collapse), both use AI to build prototypes and validate them in the field. The Expert also leverages AI for code generation and prototype implementation. Now that AI has broadened the reach of engineering, business development specialists writing code on the ground is no longer an exception but a reality.

Third, for both, success is not "building and being done"; it is moving the business forward.

With these commonalities established, what exactly is different? The next section breaks it down into 5 dimensions. For the lineage and definition of Forward Deployed Engineer, see Forward Deployed Engineer.

Where do the two differ? — 5 dimensions

The differences between the two can be organized across 5 dimensions: primary asset, dialogue partners, deliverables, phase of responsibility, and stance toward AI.

PerspectiveForward Deployed EngineerForward Deployed Expert
Primary assetEngineering skills + AI implementation capabilityBusiness development practical knowledge + AI implementation capability
Dialogue with management and deciding "what"Technical and product consensusBusiness and revenue decision-making itself
Primary deliverableProductRedesign of revenue structure
Business development phase of responsibilityCompleting the implementation phaseHypothesis building through finished product launch through breaking through internal decision-making
Stance toward AIUses AI as a weapon to write code and build to completionUses AI as a weapon to determine what to build and drive the business

Primary asset. The Engineer's core asset is "the ability to build to completion"—the power to turn technically demanding requirements into a viable product. The Expert's core asset is "the ability to define what to build and mobilize the organization." Business development practical knowledge is the primary asset, and AI implementation capability functions as a secondary weapon that amplifies that knowledge.

Primary dialogue partners. The Engineer sits next to the technical lead. Requirements, architecture, data design, ownership of operational responsibility—these discussions proceed in the language of technology. The Expert sits next to the business owner and executive leadership. Business portfolio, KPI design, organizational consensus building, budgeting timing—these discussions proceed in the language of management.

Primary deliverable. The Engineer's deliverable is a "thing": the product. Working software, operational systems, implementations customers can touch. The Expert's deliverable is a "qualitative transformation of the business": the redesign of the revenue structure. The product is merely one element of it, which also encompasses billing models, value chains, and partnership structures.

Business development phase of responsibility. The Engineer is responsible for completing the implementation phase—the scope of converting settled requirements into something that works. The Expert is responsible for a broader scope, from hypothesis building through finished product launch and onward to breaking through internal decision-making. They stand on the ground before "what to build" is even determined, and continue updating hypotheses based on market response after launch.

Stance toward AI. The Engineer "builds with AI." They fully exploit generative AI as a weapon for code productivity, maximizing the speed of building to completion in the era after Completion Cost Collapse. The Expert "determines what to build with AI and drives the business." They use generative AI as a hypothesis validation device, deciding the very course of the business through dialogue with the market.

The Engineer handles "the front lines of building to completion," while the Expert handles "the front lines of determining what to build and breaking through internal decision-making." Business implementation in the AI era is complete when the two stand side by side on the same ground. For the substance of the Expert's capabilities, see Forward Deployed Expert, the definitive definition.

A common misconception: isn't this just "an engineer who also does business"?

FDE=Expert is not a rephrasing of "an engineer who can also do business." The expertise of sitting next to the business owner, breaking through internal decision-making, and redesigning revenue structures is not an "ancillary skill" of engineering—it is an independent body of practical knowledge.

This misunderstanding arises structurally. In the AI era, the idea of "filling the gap between technology and business with engineers who also understand business" has spread. That itself is a natural and valid solution. There are indeed cases where talent crossing over from the technical side navigates the field's difficult spots alone.

However, talent crossing over from the technical side and talent who stand in the field with business development practical knowledge as their primary asset differ structurally in the phases where they are needed, the counterparts they dialogue with, and the walls they must break through. This is not a denial of the former. It is a framing that the latter, "the other pole," exists as an independent expertise.

Why is the latter now emerging as an independent pole? It is because the manufacturing cost of finished products has collapsed (Chapter 3 of the book "AI収益進化論"). Through the leap of AI coding and generative AI, the scarcity of "being able to build" itself has rapidly declined. An internal Anthropic survey reported that 27% of employees' Claude-assisted work was "work that would not have occurred without Claude." The premise of the act of "building" has changed.

In a world where "being able to build" is no longer scarce, the power to define "what to build, for whom, and why," break through internal decision-making, and deliver to the market emerges as an independent scarce resource. According to MIT NANDA's survey, the production conversion rate of enterprise AI tools remains at 5%, and 95% of organizations have not obtained measurable financial returns. The key to crossing this "GenAI Divide" lies not on the technical side but on the side of business design and organizational breakthrough (MIT NANDA Project, 2025).

Therefore, the Expert is neither a "superset of engineers" nor "inferior." Alongside talent crossing over from the technical side, it exists independently as "the other pole" whose primary asset is business development practical knowledge. The roles are different.

Which is needed──combination in the field

In many fields, both are needed, not just one. Where the bottleneck lies determines which one stands at the forefront.

  • When implementation completion is stuck (requirements are fixed but cannot be built out) → Engineer at the forefront.
  • When "what to build" is undetermined / stuck in internal decision-making or budgeting / need to continuously update hypotheses based on market response → Expert at the forefront.
  • When running a new business continuously from zero to monetization → both stand side by side in the same field.

As an auxiliary line for judgment, there is one more important axis: whether, after AI initiatives hit a ceiling in the efficiency domain, they can continuously accompany the transition into the revenue evolution domain. According to McKinsey's survey, of the 88% of companies using AI, only 39% report EBIT impact, and most of those report less than 5% EBIT impact (McKinsey State of AI, 2025). Beyond the summit of the efficiency AI mountain, the revenue evolution AI mountain appears as a separate starting point for climbing.

AlphaDrive's FDE=Expert can start by accompanying efficiency AI initiatives and, after reaching Plateau Detection, continue seamlessly into the revenue evolution phase. It is a design where a single expert climbs both the efficiency AI mountain and the revenue evolution AI mountain continuously. It could also be described as a talent profile that can accompany all 4 steps of the AX for Revenue Loop.

The substance of the Expert: "AX Architect = BA capability × AI capability"

The substance of the FDE=Expert's capabilities is AlphaDrive's systematized "AXアーキテクト = BA capability × AI capability."

BA capability refers to business architect capability: the power to translate corporate strategy into business structure, the power to move stakeholders, and the power to build consensus across organizations. AI capability refers to business implementation capabilities specific to the AI era: AI Sprint, AI Orchestration, and Full-Product Launch. The multiplication of the two outlines the Expert's capabilities.

For detailed capability definitions, see Forward Deployed Expert and AXアーキテクト. In this article, we merely confirm that the substance of the Expert is neither a "businessperson who understands AI" nor an "engineer who understands business," but a talent profile systematized as an independent methodology.

While the world establishes "FDE=Engineer" as the standard term, AlphaDrive is presenting another solution in Japan in the context of AI business development: "permanently deploying AXアーキテクト in a Forward Deployed manner."

Summary: Expert beside the Engineer

The difference between FDE=Engineer and FDE=Expert is not superiority or inferiority. It is a difference in roles: "what can be built" versus "determining what to build and breaking through internal decision-making."

The Engineer handles the "field of building things out," and the Expert handles the "field of determining what to build and moving the organization." AI-era business implementation is completed when both stand side by side in the same field. AI moves from efficiency to the creation of revenue. To bring about that transition in the field, there is an Expert beside the Engineer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Which is the newer concept: Forward Deployed Engineer or Forward Deployed Expert?

Forward Deployed Engineer is the term established first. Originating with Palantir and inherited as a standard term by cutting-edge AI companies including Anthropic and OpenAI, it is widely recognized worldwide. Forward Deployed Expert is a framing that AlphaDrive presented in Japan in the context of AI business development, positioned as another core talent profile built upon that respect. The two do not stand in a chronological relationship; they exist in parallel as two poles with different roles.

Q2. Doesn't the Expert write code?

They do. The Expert also makes full use of AI for code generation and prototype implementation. After the Completion Cost Collapse, it has become a reality for business development experts to stand in the field while writing code. The difference lies not in "whether they write code" but in where the center of gravity is placed: whether their primary asset is business development practical knowledge or engineering skills.

Q3. Why isn't an "engineer who can also do business" enough?

It's not a matter of being insufficient. Talent that crosses over from the technical side is one effective solution for getting through difficult spots in the field. However, the expertise to sit beside the business owner, break through internal decision-making, and redesign the revenue structure is not something acquired as an "incidental skill" of engineering; it is an independent body of practical knowledge. The two do not conflict; they exist in parallel as distinct axes of expertise, differing in the phases where they are needed and in their counterparts for dialogue.

Q4. How should we decide which one our company needs?

Judge based on where the bottleneck lies. If completing implementation is the sticking point, the Engineer should take the lead. If "what to build" is undecided, things are stalled in internal decision-making or budgeting, or hypotheses need to be continuously updated based on market response, the Expert should take the lead. When running a new business continuously from zero to profitability, a realistic setup is having both stand side by side in the same field.

Q5. Why does an independent pole called "Expert" emerge in the AI era?

Because the manufacturing cost of finished products has collapsed. In a world where the scarcity of "being able to build" is rapidly declining, the power to define "what to build, for whom, and why," break through internal decision-making, and deliver it to the market emerges as an independent scarce resource. As the MIT NANDA survey shows, the production conversion rate for enterprise AI tools remains at just 5%. Bridging this gap requires not only the technical side but also expertise on the side of business design and organizational breakthrough.
