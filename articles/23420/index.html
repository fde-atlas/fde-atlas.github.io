Ramp FDE Interview: Hiring Criteria and How to Prepare

Original title: Ramp FDE 面试：招聘标准与准备方法

FDE Times · 2026-11-18 · 5 min read

Summary

Ramp's engineering blog reveals an unusual hiring stance: coding ability is treated as a threshold, and candidates who barely pass can still become excellent FDEs. Once the bar is met, Ramp stops optimizing for higher scores. The qualities that actually differentiate candidates are drive and diligence—Ramp's best predictor of performance—plus customer empathy and communication, which carry equal weight as separate criteria. The article compares Ramp's Senior FDE role with its 2026 AI Solutions role, which requires experience building agents, RAG systems, evals, and monitoring. It recommends preparing a demo project with evals and two or three tight requirements-gathering stories rather than grinding more algorithm problems.

  • Drive and diligence best predict real FDE performance.
  • Customer empathy and communication are equally weighted criteria.
  • The 2026 AI Solutions role demands agents, RAG, evals, monitoring experience.
  • Prep should focus on requirements-gathering stories and a demo project with evals.

Key takeaways

  • Ramp treats technical ability as a bar to meet, and stops optimizing once you're above it.
  • Drive and diligence are the best predictors of actual performance; customer empathy and communication are two separate criteria weighted equally.
  • The 2026 AI Solutions role requires experience building agents, RAG, evals, and monitoring, and the work is tightly connected to customers.

Ramp admitted something on its engineering blog that few companies are willing to say: some candidates with mediocre coding interview results—the "pass, with reservations" kind—went on to become outstanding FDEs.

If you're planning to grind a few more hard problems before your interview, this detail is worth reading closely. Ramp says it treats technical ability as a bar to meet and avoids optimizing beyond that bar.

So where should the rest of your prep time go? Ramp's published hiring criteria, its two FDE job descriptions, and an independent analysis of 14 FDE roles all point in the same direction: what truly separates candidates is drive, customer empathy, and communication skills.

You still need to clear the technical bar cleanly

"A bar" doesn't mean easy. Ramp states it assesses fundamentals: writing code, debugging, data structures, and system design. The company hasn't described the exact format of each interview round, so don't guess at question types—instead, make sure all four areas are solid.

Ramp adds that once a candidate reaches the bar, it stops optimizing for higher scores. For candidates, going far beyond the bar most likely won't earn any extra credit.

Many excellent engineers trip themselves up right here. They spend their final week grinding more hard problems, when what actually decides the outcome are the stories they never prepared. If you're confident you can pass the fundamentals assessment, save your practice time for the other parts.

The best predictor is drive

Ramp breaks its FDE hiring criteria into four independent parts: drive and diligence, technical fundamentals, customer empathy, and communication skills. Speaking specifically about drive, Ramp writes that it's the best predictor of on-the-job performance it has found.

The reason is visible right in the job description. The 2026 AI Solutions role describes the position as highly customer-facing and hands-on, and requires comfort with ambiguity. In a role like that, it's unlikely anyone will hand you work ticket by ticket.

Proving drive doesn't require having founded a company. A side project with real users, an internal tool you proposed and pushed until the whole team adopted it, an experience where you personally sought out users to understand why they abandoned a feature—all demonstrate the same quality.

When writing your resume, use initiator verbs like "proposed," "persuaded," and "launched to users." Avoid passive words like "participated in" or "supported."

Empathy and communication side by side

Ramp defines customer empathy as the ability to understand how customers feel, along with emotional intelligence. Communication is a separate criterion weighted equally: the company wants people who listen well and can express their ideas clearly and persuasively.

A Paraform FDE hiring guide, in covering Ramp's criteria, also lists these two side by side, with neither ranked above the other. And Ramp isn't an outlier. Interview Kickstart analyzed 14 FDE roles and found that 11 explicitly mentioned customer requirements discovery, and stakeholder communication appeared more frequently than many technical skills.

In other words, Ramp simply wrote down what most FDE roles in the sample already require. To see how these two criteria work in practice, it helps to walk through a concrete example.

Imagine this scenario: a customer's finance team wants "an agent that automatically reconciles every invoice with the ERP." If you take the request at face value, you've just signed up for a months-long project full of dozens of edge cases.

A good FDE asks questions before writing code. How many hours does reconciliation take each week today? Which types of invoices consume most of that time? Who handles the mismatches? What does a single error cost?

The answers will likely shrink the problem to one vendor, one document type, and one approval workflow—small enough to actually run first, then expand gradually. Telling this kind of story in an interview lets the interviewer hear all three things Ramp hires for at once: the drive that pushes work forward, the empathy shown in the questions, and the communication shown in the retelling.

Two roles, two kinds of work

Ramp is looking for the same type of person, but its FDE work comes in more than one form. Put the Senior FDE role next to the FDE AI Solutions role posted on July 29, 2026, and you'll see two different emphases of the same role.

A notable detail in the Senior role is the customer journey it covers: from first contact, to signing the contract, to onboarding and activation. This means the FDE is involved before the customer has even signed, and at this stage, listening and integration skills matter just as much.

The AI Solutions role leans toward AI products. The job aggregation site Aced likewise describes Ramp's FDE team as the team that designs end-to-end AI solutions for its largest enterprise customers.

How to prepare if you're targeting Ramp

Start with the technical bar, but set a time cap on it. Beyond the four fundamentals, the AI Solutions role also needs evidence that you've built agents or RAG systems with evals and monitoring.

Build a sample project that automates a repetitive accounting task, pair it with a set of evals, and document where the agent went wrong and how you fixed it—this is more persuasive than ten algorithm problems. It also involves financial data integration, which happens to cover the LLM skills listed in the job description.

Spend most of the remaining time polishing two or three tightly told requirements-discovery stories. Each story should include the original request, the questions you asked in return, the final scope, and the outcome. Practice telling them out loud first, then write a one-page design document, since the role also requires written communication skills.

When reading any company's FDE job description, watch for three things: whether they treat technical ability as a bar or a competition; whether the main work is integrations or AI products; and at what stage of the customer journey the FDE gets involved. These three answers determine what you should practice.

Ramp has already published its selection criteria. The advantage goes to candidates willing to practice the parts that never appear in coding problems.
