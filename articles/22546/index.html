How Enterprise AI Deployment Works

Invisible Technologies · 2026 · 3 min read

Summary

Invisible Technologies describes its forward deployed engineer (FDE) model: engineers write code directly against a client's real systems—legacy infrastructure, operational databases, and warehouses—without a discovery phase or rip-and-replace. Data stays in the client's environment, and fragmented data is treated as a starting condition rather than a blocker. The platform is model-agnostic, so AI models can be swapped as better options emerge. Workflows are validated against historical data with transparent comparisons, then deployed in weeks with defined monitoring of metrics like throughput, error rates, resource efficiency, and cost per transaction. The company frames FDE as a way of working that bridges the gap between advisors who scope and SaaS vendors who ship.

Key points

  • FDEs write code against real client systems from day one.
  • No clean, centralised data is required before starting.
  • Platform is model-agnostic with no vendor lock-in.
  • Validation uses historical data to quantify inefficiencies transparently.
  • Deployment takes weeks, with ongoing operational metric monitoring.

How long does it take to deploy AI with Invisible Technologies?

Deployment is measured in weeks rather than months because there is no six-month discovery phase and no rip-and-replace of existing systems. Forward deployed engineers start writing code against your real systems early, and within months you have a production system with audit-ready documentation and measurable results.

What is a forward deployed engineer, and why does the model matter?

A forward deployed engineer works inside your operation rather than delivering specifications to it, combining technical depth with a focus on the business problem. That means the team writing the code is the team that has seen how the work actually happens, which is what keeps a deployment from stalling between discovery and delivery.

Do we need clean, centralised data before we start?

No. The first step connects legacy systems, operational databases and data warehouses as they are, and your data stays in your systems. Fragmented data is one of the most common reasons AI programmes stall, so it is treated as a starting condition rather than a prerequisite.

How is an AI workflow validated before it reaches production?

Validation runs against your historical data, so you can see where current methods fell short before anything goes live: inefficiencies quantified, bottlenecks identified, resource gaps measured. The comparison is transparent rather than a black box, which is what makes the result defensible internally.

How do you measure whether a deployed workflow is actually working?

The system goes live with defined monitoring rather than a launch and a hope. Daily outputs are tracked by location, category or workflow, adjustments are made in real time as conditions shift, and operational metrics are measured directly: throughput, error rates, resource efficiency and cost per transaction.

Are we locked into a particular AI model or vendor?

No. The platform is model-agnostic, so the models best suited to your workflow are the ones used, and they can change as better options appear. Where a frontier model falls short on your domain, Invisible Technologies builds the reinforcement learning environments and training data to improve it.

Call it services-to-software. Or just call it done.
