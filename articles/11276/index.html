Forward Deployed Engineer Skills: 14-Point Checklist (2026)

Artificial Intelligence Uni (AIU.ac) · 2026-10-18 · 6 min read

Summary

This article organizes forward deployed engineer skills into a 14-point checklist across three groups: production engineering (Python, SQL, APIs, cloud, debugging, testing), applied AI (LLM work, evals and guardrails, knowing when not to use a model), and customer-facing judgement (discovery, decomposition, communication, product judgement, ownership through handover). Citing an analysis of roughly 1,000 postings by Perspective AI, it notes customer-facing skills appear in over 70% of listings, yet hiring loops directly screen only four skills: practical coding, systems and integration design, decomposing ambiguous briefs, and customer communication. It maps skills to a seven-stage deployment lifecycle and three performance measures, and promotes AIU's CFDE certification.

Forward deployed engineer skills fall into three groups: engineering that holds up inside a customer’s production systems, applied AI work, and customer-facing judgement. That gives 14 skills in all, and a typical hiring loop screens four of them directly. The other ten tend to surface through project stories and through how a first deployment is handled.

What forward deployed engineer skills look like in 2026 postings

A forward deployed engineer is an engineer who builds, deploys and hands over a system inside a customer’s own environment. The role began at Palantir in the early 2010s and is now a standard hiring track at AI-native companies and enterprise technology firms. Demand is visible: forward deployed engineer postings were roughly 729 percent higher in April 2026 than a year earlier, according to an Indeed index reported by Business Insider, as Deep Engineering notes. It also points out that the figure covers one job board rather than the whole market.

What do those postings ask for? Perspective AI analysed roughly 1,000 live postings and reports core engineering in over 95 percent, AI-specific skills in over 80 percent, and customer-facing and discovery skills in over 70 percent and rising. That is one vendor’s sample, so treat the percentages as indicative. SE Rockstars, drawing on Bloomberry’s analysis of about 1,000 postings, found working directly with customers named in 55% of postings, yet the hard requirements in most postings are years of engineering experience and a technology stack.

That mismatch is why a useful checklist covers both halves. The parent guide covers the role itself, so the sections below go straight to the skills.

What is a Forward Deployed Engineer? Role, lifecycle and how FDEs are measured

Engineering skills: the first six

These are the skills that make a deployment run in an environment nobody on the team designed. Most are listed as hard requirements, so most are screened by CV before any interview.

  • Production code in Python plus one other language. Hashnode’s 2026 guide expects fluency in Python, with Java, Go or TypeScript useful for full-stack work.
  • SQL and data pipelines. The same guide treats knowing SQL as the bare minimum, and expects an understanding of data processing, pipeline tooling and database trade-offs.
  • API integration and authentication. Uplers lists REST APIs, webhooks, OAuth and SSO, and comfort reverse-engineering an undocumented or half-broken API.
  • Cloud and containers. Hashnode names AWS, GCP or Azure, plus Docker and Kubernetes.
  • Debugging in unfamiliar systems. Uplers describes the instinct to check logs and monitoring first when something breaks, instead of guessing.
  • Testing and monitoring. Appily includes testing, monitoring and AI evaluation among the experience an FDE may need.

Skills 5 and 6 are the ones most often missing from a CV that otherwise looks strong. A system that works on the day of the demo and cannot be diagnosed by anyone else three weeks later has not been deployed in any useful sense.

AI and customer skills: the other eight

Applied AI (skills 7 to 9)

  • LLM application work and retrieval. Building with model APIs and retrieval pipelines, which Uplers asks for where the product touches retrieval.
  • Evaluation and guardrails. Hashnode says the bar has shifted to agentic orchestration, evaluation frameworks and AI observability and guardrails. If there is no way to measure an output, there is no way to hand it over.
  • Knowing when not to use a model. Uplers puts it as the judgment to know when a simple deterministic script beats an AI call.

For agents that act on a customer’s systems, the principle to practise is graduated autonomy.

A production agent earns write permissions after read-only trust is established.

Customer-facing judgement (skills 10 to 14)

  • Discovery. Perspective AI says postings increasingly ask FDEs to find the real problem before building, running structured customer conversations rather than taking in a spec.
  • Decomposition and scoping. Perspective AI lists problem decomposition and comfort with ambiguity among the execution traits broadly listed.
  • Stakeholder and executive communication. SE Rockstars, again citing Bloomberry, reports customer-facing ability as the most frequently required soft skill, at 47%.
  • Product judgement. Analytics Insight notes that FDEs must decide what to customise for one customer and what can become part of the broader product.
  • Ownership through to handover. Uplers suggests looking for a real story of staying on a problem until it closed.

The four skills hiring loops screen directly

Loops differ by company, but the shape is consistent. According to Perspective AI’s interview guide, the process runs three to six weeks from first recruiter call to offer and typically includes five distinct stages. Four of those stages test a skill from the checklist head on.

  • Practical coding (skills 1 and 5). A 45 to 60 minute real-world problem, testing practical engineering and debugging.
  • Systems and integration design (skills 3, 4 and 6). A technical deep dive on systems, architecture and integrations.
  • Decomposing an ambiguous brief (skills 10 and 11). An open-ended case study testing decomposition, judgement and trade-offs.
  • Customer-facing communication (skills 12 and 14). Perspective AI describes one company’s case study, where a hypothetical customer presents a problem and the candidate mocks up a solution. A behavioural round then probes ownership and collaboration.

The weighting matters. Perspective AI reports that one AI lab explicitly weights case studies, customer empathy and business judgement at roughly 50% of the evaluation. A candidate who has only practised algorithm puzzles is preparing for the smaller half.

The practice method is unglamorous. The same guide recommends decomposing ambiguous briefs out loud, on a timer, until there is a repeatable structure: clarify, decompose, prioritise, propose, name the risks. Do it with a colleague who plays a customer who wants the wrong thing.

How the skills map to a deployment, and where CFDE fits

Two things sit underneath the checklist: the order in which a deployment unfolds, and the way the work is judged. AIU teaches both.

AIU teaches the deployment lifecycle in seven stages: post-sale handoff, discovery, scoping, build, deploy, iterate and hand-back.

Read against that sequence, the customer skills cluster early and the engineering skills dominate the middle. Discovery, scoping and stakeholder communication (skills 10 to 12) decide whether the right thing gets built. Skills 1 to 9 carry the build and deploy work. Skills 6 and 14 matter most at the end, because a hand-back only works if the client’s own team can run, monitor and explain the system without the engineers in the room.

AIU teaches that Forward Deployed Engineers are measured on three things: deployment velocity, customer success and product feedback quality.

Those measures are why the customer skills carry so much weight in hiring. Fast deployment with poor adoption, or a good deployment that teaches the product team nothing, scores badly on at least one of them.

The Certified Forward Deployed Engineer (CFDE)™ is built as the first certification standard for the Forward Deployed Engineer role, and the 14 skills above are a sensible way to find your own gaps before choosing a route. The course teaches and a separate examination, administered by AIU, certifies, so the two are worth considering on their own terms.

If the checklist showed a gap in the customer-facing half, the next step is to look at how the programme approaches it.

Frequently asked questions

What skills do you need to be a forward deployed engineer?+

Forward deployed engineer skills split into three groups: production engineering (coding, SQL, APIs, cloud, debugging), applied AI (LLM applications, evaluation, knowing when not to use a model) and customer-facing judgement (discovery, decomposition, communication, ownership). According to <a href="https://getperspective.ai/blog/2026-fde-hiring-trends-what-1000-job-posts-reveal">Perspective AI</a>, customer-facing and discovery skills now appear in over 70% of postings.

Do forward deployed engineers need to be strong coders?+

Yes. The role is technical, and engineering experience is the part postings list as a hard requirement. <a href="https://www.uplers.com/article/forward-deployed-engineer-skills/">Uplers</a> says the bar is whether a candidate can own something end to end without waiting on another team, which is a higher standard than writing code that passes a test.

Which forward deployed engineer skills do interviews screen?+

Four are screened directly: practical coding, systems and integration design, decomposing an ambiguous brief, and customer-facing communication. <a href="https://getperspective.ai/blog/forward-deployed-engineer-interview-questions-2026-prep-guide">Perspective AI</a> reports that the whole process typically runs three to six weeks, so preparing each skill separately is realistic.
