This introductory article by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, explains the standard system development process in five stages: requirements definition, design, programming, testing, and release/maintenance. It compares waterfall and agile development, outlining each method's pros and cons, and briefly covers spiral, prototyping, and DevOps approaches. It also explains outsourcing costs, noting that roughly 80% of development expenses are labor costs calculated in person-months, and includes a glossary of common development phase abbreviations. The piece ends with promotion of DeFactory's development services.

Key points

  • System development follows five stages: requirements, design, coding, testing, release and maintenance.
  • Waterfall offers predictable budgets and schedules but handles mid-project changes poorly.
  • Agile delivers faster and adapts to change, but risks losing direction and complicating management.
  • About 80% of outsourced development costs are labor, estimated in person-months (e.g., 3 engineers × 3 months = 9 person-months).
  • Spiral, prototyping, and DevOps are introduced as alternative development approaches.

System development and web system development is about building mechanisms for business efficiency. Let's take a look at the basic workflow and costs involved in system and web development.

System development refers to building mechanisms using IT technology, aimed at improving and optimizing business operations to solve challenges companies face.

It is a general term for a series of tasks including design, programming, and testing.

The system development process is divided into five stages: "requirements definition," "design," "programming," "testing," and "release and maintenance/operation." While the approach to each stage varies depending on the development model, development processes are in principle structured to carry out system development in a planned manner. For detailed explanations of each process and comparisons of major development methodologies, see Complete Guide to the Software Development Process.

In system and web system development, work is generally divided into several processes to build high-quality systems efficiently. Breaking the development process into several phases helps prevent defects.

Here, we introduce the workflow of "waterfall development," a commonly used development methodology.

Requirements definition refers to "the delivery conditions agreed upon between the client and the developer."

  • What kind of system will be built and how
  • What features will be included

These are clearly articulated and documented.

In waterfall development, development proceeds based on the requirements definition, so it is important to ensure there are no discrepancies between the client and the developer at this stage. Budget, development period, and post-development operation and maintenance are also planned in detail.

The process of creating the user interface design ("basic design document") based on the requirements definition. The user interface refers to the external appearance visible to users, such as screens.

From the user's perspective, the specific features and characteristics to be included are refined.

Based on the "basic design document" created in external design, a "detailed design document" is created. The detailed design document assumes concrete implementation. Documents such as functional specifications are created from the client's perspective.

The process in which system engineers and programmers actually perform programming based on the detailed design created in internal design.

After programming is complete, testing is conducted to confirm whether each system functions correctly.

  • Unit testing: Confirms that each program behaves as defined in the requirements definition. Tests are executed at the program module (component) level.
  • Integration testing: Confirms that multiple programs combined together behave as defined in the requirements definition.
  • System (comprehensive) testing: Confirms that all programs operate according to the initially defined requirements. Tests are conducted assuming various scenarios, such as durability, processing speed, and behavior when bugs occur.

After testing is complete, the system is handed over to the client. To make it operational, methods such as "big bang migration" or "phased migration" are selected and executed according to the situation.

  • Operation: Tasks that handle improvements and updates
  • Maintenance: Tasks that respond when problems arise with the system

Understand the characteristics of the developed system and consider what kind of operation and maintenance would be best.

In system and web system development, in-house resources are often insufficient. So here, we introduce the costs to consider when outsourcing.

It varies depending on the scale and content of development and the number of engineers required, so it cannot be generalized, but it is said that about 80% of development costs are labor costs.

Generally, the unit "person-month" is used to calculate the costs of system and web system development.

A "person-month" is a workload unit calculated by multiplying the number of engineers and programmers involved in system development by "the monthly labor cost required per person."

[How to calculate labor costs] Number of engineers required for the project × Number of days required for development

For example, if 3 engineers with a monthly rate of 600,000 yen are mobilized for 3 months, it is "9 person-months"

(600,000 × 3) × 3 (months) = 5,400,000 yen

Engineer rates vary greatly depending on the experience of the individual and the development company commissioned, and if the development period extends beyond the plan, additional costs are incurred accordingly.

Costs other than labor costs include the following.

  • Equipment costs: computer, server, cloud service fees, etc.
  • Costs for the development environment: office rent, utilities, communication fees, phone charges, etc.
  • Engineers' expenses: travel costs, equipment purchases, etc.

When considering outsourcing, keep in mind not only labor costs but also the associated expenses.

In system and web system development, the methods used to achieve goals vary depending on current challenges and objectives. However, most methods generally follow the flow described above.

However, since methods differ depending on cost, development period, and project scale, it is a good idea to have a general understanding of what development methods are available.

As mentioned above, there are several types of system development, but here we will introduce waterfall development.

Here is a brief summary of the advantages and disadvantages of waterfall development.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
・Easy to set budgets ・Easy to estimate delivery timelines ・Less prone to major problems・Difficult to handle specification changes or irregular requests ・Takes time until release

Waterfall development is a traditional, mainstream development method. It proceeds sequentially, like a waterfall flowing from the upstream "requirements definition" phase down to the downstream "testing" phase.

Since work is generally done in order without skipping phases, it is easy to manage progress and maintain consistent quality, which is a key advantage.

On the other hand, because the project flow and phases are all fixed in advance, it is difficult to accommodate specification changes mid-project, and since you cannot move on until one phase is complete, it takes time before deliverables can be reviewed. These are the main disadvantages.

Because it takes time until product release, waterfall development is well suited to projects where the final product is clearly defined, or relatively large-scale projects that require considerable time for development.

"Agile" is a word meaning "quick and nimble." In waterfall development, all specifications are designed before development begins, but in agile development, not everything is decided at the start; development begins with only a rough direction in place.

The main advantages and disadvantages of agile development are as follows.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
・Fast delivery ・Flexible response to changes ・Easy to meet customer needs・The final product may differ from the original concept ・Few development companies can offer it ・Difficult for developers to secure talent

Agile development is a style of developing in small units. The short time to release and the ability to respond to sudden specification changes and user requests are its greatest advantages.

On the other hand, it carries the risk of losing sight of the system's direction during development. Also, since the whole project is divided into small units of work, it can be difficult to grasp the overall picture and manage schedules. These are the main disadvantages.

Related article: What Is the Greatest Advantage of Agile Development? Success Stories Beginners Should Know

Agile development is suited to small-scale projects with short time to release, where the final product image is not yet defined, or where changes are expected. If you need a system developed in a short period or with simple functionality, the flexible agile approach is likely the better fit.

In waterfall development, requirements are thoroughly defined, and features are implemented in phase order. In principle, the design is carefully done at the initial requirements definition stage to avoid rework, but rework can still occur.

In that case, work in the upstream phases increases, and work in the downstream phases comes to a halt. Since labor costs continue to accrue during rework, costs may balloon.

Agile development, on the other hand, is a method that sets only a rough direction and implements the minimum necessary features. For this reason, agile development tends to keep costs down.

Even if rework occurs due to specification changes or feature additions and modifications, it is limited to individual features and does not affect the system as a whole.

The larger the project, the more likely rework is to occur. One advantage of agile development is that even if rework occurs, it does not cause major damage.

Beyond waterfall and agile development, there are many other methods. Here we introduce three methods, including ones expected to gain attention in the future.

"Spiral" is a word meaning "helix." Like agile development, the spiral model divides the system into several units for development.

It is a development method that repeats the cycle of requirements definition, design, development, testing, and evaluation for each unit, improving the overall system's completeness in a spiral fashion. Based on feedback after testing, the process moves on to the next stage of the system, and this repetition raises quality until completion.

A development method in which a prototype is created early in system or web system development. Since reviews are conducted together with the system's users, misalignment in understanding can be resolved early, which is a key advantage.

However, creating prototypes requires time and cost, which can become a disadvantage.

A development method called DevOps, in which developers and operations staff collaborate on development. It is a relatively new development method.

The development team releases the system after testing is complete, and then the operations team handles the system's operation and maintenance.

Although their roles differ, they share the same goal of increasing business value through the system.

The purpose of DevOps is to bridge the gap between software development (Dev), which creates application code, and IT operations (Ops), which handles deployment.

To eliminate the challenges of agile development, such as rapid software testing and concerns about production deployment, DevOps aims to quickly deliver greater business value by optimizing the entire flow from conception to the delivery of products and services.
