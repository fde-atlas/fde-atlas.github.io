FDE Hiring Requirements: Why Breadth of Knowledge Is More Valuable

Original title: FDE 招聘要求：为什么知识广度更值钱

Writing on FDE Hub, Milos Mandic argues that breadth of experience, undervalued for years, is finally gaining value in the engineering job market. His argument targets engineering hiring as a whole, not just FDEs. The Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role is simply where this shift is most visible. This is an opinion piece with no supporting data, but the core argument is clear.

AI has made execution cheap, so value has shifted to a different capability: judging whether outputs fit the business across domains.

Why has breadth always been undervalued? Mandic points out that job descriptions have always favored people who drill deep in one direction, for a simple reason: specialists are easier to evaluate. There is no ready-made yardstick for generalists, and hiring processes are designed around what can be measured.

Cheap Execution, Expensive Judgment

The article's core point is: software engineering still requires judgment, but it is no longer the only thing that does. The leverage AI provides has a ceiling: models only amplify the user's own curiosity, judgment, and taste; they can't fill in what you lack. For FDEs, this is exactly the core of the job: sitting next to customers and deciding which outputs fit their business.

Mandic's advice to hiring managers: ask candidates how many domains they can distinguish right from wrong in.

What the Hiring Data Says

Job posting data supports this argument. Perspective analyzed 1,000 FDE job postings and found that the fastest-growing requirement is direct customer discovery, appearing in over 70% of recent postings. Coding is just the baseline. The language used to differentiate candidates centers on understanding the customer.

How fast this wave is moving depends on how you count. Perspective recorded that as of early 2026, FDE hiring grew more than 1,000% year over year. Paraform's figure is 350% growth from Q1 2025 to Q1 2026. Paraform also found that the supply of qualified candidates hasn't kept up with demand, and recommends employers focus screening on the two hardest-to-measure qualities: empathy for customers and tolerance for ambiguity.

A few big names have joined the demand side. AWS, following OpenAI and Anthropic, has also begun sending engineers to work inside enterprises.

Preparing for a New Type of Interview

Question you might faceWhat it testsHow to demonstrate it
"How many domains can you distinguish right from wrong in?"Cross-domain judgmentDescribe 2–3 projects in different industries, and for each, give one example of rejecting an output: it worked, but didn't fit the business
"Meeting a customer for the first time, what would you ask?"Customer discoveryProvide a list of questions, starting from their current workflow to pain points, rather than starting from solutions
"Requirements are vague—what do you do first?"Tolerance for ambiguityExplain how you first pin down a small hypothesis to validate, rather than waiting for a complete requirements document
"How do you use AI in your work?"The taste and judgment you bring to the modelGive an example: you understood the domain better than the model, so you corrected or discarded its output

For Vietnamese developers who have worked outsourced projects across multiple industries, this is a long-wasted advantage. A resume organized by tech stack reads as "knows a lot, but nothing deeply." A resume organized by business domain, spelling out what decisions were made in each industry, is exactly what the FDE market is looking for. When reading job descriptions, watch for words like "discovery," "customer-facing," and "ambiguity." They signal that the company is hiring for judgment, not just someone who writes code.
