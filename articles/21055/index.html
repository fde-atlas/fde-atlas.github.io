What is Solutions Engineering?

Solutions Engineering is a role that involves technically understanding a customer's business challenges and designing, demonstrating, and implementing solutions that combine your company's products, APIs, and workflows. While sales decides "what to sell," Solutions Engineering "proves how it works," contributing to both closing deals and adoption through demos, PoCs (proofs of concept), and implementation support.

For how the role boundaries of Sales Engineer / FDE / CSE are shifting in the AI era, The Complete Guide to Forward Deployed Engineers explains everything from their origins to the latest trends.

Why It Matters

As SaaS and AI products grow more complex, verifying that something "actually works" has become essential to purchasing decisions. As of 2026, products leveraging generative AI and LLMs are rapidly increasing, and customers prioritize "whether it fits our business workflows" over "feature lists." Solutions Engineering is the only position that advances sales conversations while fulfilling technical accountability.

Among small and medium-sized businesses, professional service firms, and e-commerce operators, consultations on introducing AI tools are also increasing, and demand is growing for partners who can take a Solutions Engineering approach—issue intake → PoC presentation → implementation support.

How Role Boundaries Are Shifting in the AI Era

From 2024 to 2026, the boundaries of Solutions Engineering have shifted significantly.

1. Arming SEs with Code-Generation AI By leveraging Claude Code and GitHub Copilot, more and more SEs are building demo environments in real time during sales conversations. What used to be "request a PoC from the dev team and wait two weeks" has become SEs presenting prototypes themselves within hours.

2. Rapid Expansion of FDE Demand In 2025, Anthropic officially established the Forward Deployed Engineer role, building a system to dispatch engineers to enterprise customer sites to implement Claude. Palantir pioneered this model, with FDEs embedded in customer environments responsible for proving product value and driving adoption.

3. Accelerated Integration via MCP (Model Context Protocol) With the spread of MCP, technical design for connecting customers' existing systems to LLMs is becoming a core part of Solutions Engineering. Both SEs and FDEs are expected to have MCP server design and implementation skills.

  • Anthropic: Established the FDE role within its commercial organization, responsible for Claude implementation for large enterprises
  • OpenAI: Handles primarily enterprise API projects as Solutions Engineers
  • Palantir: Deployment Strategists (equivalent to FDEs) were the prototype of the on-site customer-embedded model
  • Tufe Company: Its AI Division provides AI adoption support for SMBs and professional service firms using a Solutions Engineering approach

Practical Use Cases

AI Adoption Support for Accounting Firms For an accounting firm considering automating tax consultations, you build an internal document search system with a RAG architecture in a demo environment and show "a state where it actually works with the firm's own business manuals," accelerating decision-making. This is a typical example of Solutions Engineering (the SE phase).

Workflow Implementation for E-commerce Operators When building an order-automation flow integrated with Shopify using AI agents, the FDE phase involves designing around the customer's data structure and taking responsibility through to integration into production. At Tufe Company, the AI Division handles this role, covering everything from prompt engineering to system integration end to end.

Common Misconceptions and Cautions

Misconception 1: Solutions Engineering is synonymous with "technical sales" Technical sales (technical pre-sales) specializes in product explanation as a sales support role, while Solutions Engineering takes responsibility through design, implementation, and improvement cycles. Whether you can write code is a major dividing line.

Misconception 2: SEs and FDEs do the same job SEs focus on "proof" before closing; FDEs focus on "implementation" after closing. Their timing of customer contact and accountability differ. In AI products, this division of labor is especially important, and confusing the two risks lowering post-sale adoption rates.

Misconception 3: A role irrelevant to small and medium-sized businesses While it is often discussed in an enterprise context, small and medium-sized businesses, professional service firms, and store operators also need the same kind of "technical proof" when selecting and introducing AI tools. Whether the vendor can take a Solutions Engineering approach determines the success rate of implementation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What skills are needed to become a Solutions Engineer?

You need programming skills (Python, JavaScript, etc.), a foundation in API design, presentation skills, and the interviewing ability to quickly understand a customer's business operations. As of 2026, hands-on experience with LLMs, RAG, and MCP tends to be especially valued. Since both technical and interpersonal skills are required, the position itself remains highly scarce.

Q. What is the difference between an FDE and a Solutions Engineer in one sentence?

"An SE proves it works before you buy; an FDE actually makes it work after you buy." Many AI vendors adopt a division of labor in which the SE builds a PoC, demos it, and closes the deal, while the FDE works inside the customer's environment to handle production implementation. For details, also see the explanation of Forward Deployed Engineer.
