What Is the Forward Deployed Engineer Interview Process Like? (Round by Round)

Design Gurus · 2026-11-14 · 4 min read

Summary

This article walks through the forward deployed engineer (FDE) interview loop stage by stage: typically five to seven rounds over three to six weeks, starting with a recruiter screen and a practical coding screen, followed by an onsite of three to six interviews covering coding, decomposition or solution design, a project deep dive, and behavioral questions. Some AI companies add a take-home or simulated customer call. The decomposition round is described as the decisive one, with the most common mistake being to start with architecture instead of clarifying the user's needs. The piece also compares how Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Cognition differ in their loops.

Key points

  • FDE loops usually have five to seven rounds over three to six weeks.
  • Technical screens use practical, partly undefined, user-facing coding tasks.
  • OpenAI and Cognition use take-home projects for some FDE roles.
  • Decomposition is decisive; starting with architecture is the top failure mode.
  • Company loops differ: Palantir bans AI use; Anthropic has a values panel.

Most forward deployed engineer loops have five to seven steps and take three to six weeks. A forward deployed engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who works directly with one customer, finds out what that customer needs, and builds a working system with them. The process usually starts with a recruiter screen and a practical coding screen. An onsite of three to six interviews follows, and it mixes coding, a decomposition or solution design round, a project deep dive, and behavioral questions. Many AI companies also add a take-home or a simulated customer call, which a standard software engineer loop does not have.

Quick Overview

Stage Format What is evaluated
1. Recruiter screen About 30 minutes, call Motivation for customer work, background, communication
2. Technical screen 45 to 60 minutes, live or online Practical coding on an unclear, user-facing task
3. Take-home (some companies) Several days to one week Building and explaining a working system for a customer
4. Coding round About 60 minutes Clean code that grows as requirements change
5. Decomposition or solution design About 60 minutes Turning a vague customer problem into a buildable plan
6. Project deep dive 45 to 60 minutes Ownership and the reasons behind past decisions
7. Behavioral and hiring manager 30 to 60 minutes each Customer judgment, conflict, and trust

Stage 1: Recruiter Screen

The recruiter checks whether you understand the job and want it. Expect "Why forward deployed engineering?" separately from "Why this company?", and prepare two different answers. Recruiters also listen to how clearly you explain technical work to a non-technical listener, because that is a daily task in this role.

Stage 2: Technical Screen

The screen is practical rather than a puzzle. Prep guides describe tasks built around an end-user feature, using maps, strings, SQL, or a small API, and the task is often left partly undefined on purpose. Ask what the user needs before you write code, and say your assumptions out loud.

Stage 3: Take-Home

OpenAI and Cognition use a take-home for some FDE roles. A take-home is a project you complete on your own time and then defend in a live call. Interviewers grade the customer framing as much as the code, so explain each decision in terms of what the customer gets.

Stage 4: Coding Round

The coding round usually comes in stages. You build a first version, and the interviewer then adds a requirement that forces you to change it. Companies use this format because customer requirements change in the same way. Write a few quick tests before each change, and keep your code easy to extend.

Stage 5: Decomposition or Solution Design

This is the round that decides most FDE loops. You receive a vague problem, such as "help a hospital reduce billing errors," and you have about an hour to turn it into a plan. Palantir calls it decomposition. AI labs often call it solution design and add model, retrieval, and evaluation questions. The full method is in What is the Palantir decomposition interview?

The most common failure is starting with architecture. Ask who the user is, what data exists, and what "done" means for them first. Then propose the smallest system that delivers value, and explain how you would extend it.

Stage 6: Project Deep Dive

You present a system you built and defend it under questions. Interviewers ask what you owned, why you chose each approach, and what would fail first at ten times the load. Lead with the impact on the user, then cover the design.

Stage 7: Behavioral and Hiring Manager

Every behavioral round in this loop tests one thing: whether the company can trust you in front of a customer. Prepare stories about a difficult stakeholder, a time you pushed back on a deadline, and a project where requirements changed late. The hiring manager often re-tests your weakest area from the onsite.

How Loops Differ by Company

Palantir draws three onsite rounds from a pool of five, and AI use is not allowed. OpenAI relies on a take-home, a project deep dive, and solution design with retrieval and agents. Anthropic adds a values panel that can decide the result on its own. Cognition replaces much of the coding with customer simulations. See the company pages for OpenAI, Anthropic, and Cognition.

How to Prepare

  • Decide whether the role fits you. Read Forward deployed engineer vs. solutions architect vs. software engineer so your motivation answer is specific.
  • Prepare for every round. Grokking the Forward Deployed Engineer Interview teaches discovery, decomposition, the learning and re-engineering rounds, practical coding, and project deep dives, and ends with company loops and full mock interviews.
  • Practice decomposition out loud. Take one vague problem a day and spend ten minutes on questions before any design.
  • Keep a free reference open. Forward Deployed Engineer Interview is a free guide on GitHub with a page for each round, the reported loops by company, and a four-week plan.
  • Prepare practical coding. Focus on data handling, API calls, and multi-stage problems rather than rare algorithms. Grokking the Coding Interview covers the patterns behind most screens.
  • Learn applied AI design. Retrieval, agents, and evaluation appear in most AI-lab loops. Grokking the AI System Design Interview teaches them at interview depth.

  • Preparing for Google Cloud? Grokking the Google Forward Deployed Engineer Interview covers its reported loop round by round, including the vibe coding round and the Gemini stack.
  • Build customer stories. Grokking Modern Behavioral Interview shows how to structure stories with evidence.
