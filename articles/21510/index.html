Summary

Written by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, this article explains FDE consulting-style support for procurement decision-makers. The model has one team handle problem definition, implementation, and on-site adoption, eliminating the handoff losses typical of traditional consulting (strategy only) and SES (labor-only staffing). It compares monthly fees—FDE consulting at 1.5–3 million yen, SES at 0.6–1.2 million yen per person, traditional consulting at 2–4 million yen per person—and offers a checklist for when the model fits. DeFactory's data: 2 of 9 single-person engagements missed targets, so multi-person teams are recommended; across 11 freelance FDE deals averaging 1.35 million yen monthly, FDE involvement improved PoC-to-production conversion roughly 2–3x. The article also outlines a three-phase process, KPI design, and warning signs of services falsely branded as FDE.

Key points

  • FDE consulting support has one team own problem definition through implementation and adoption.
  • Monthly fees run 1.5–3 million yen, covering delivery, unlike strategy-only consulting.
  • It fits when PoCs stall, tools go unused, or in-house implementation resources are lacking.
  • DeFactory data: 2 of 9 solo engagements missed targets; small teams are recommended.
  • Buyers should verify KPIs, contract scope, and team structure to spot fake FDE services.

Unlike traditional consulting that ends with strategic recommendations, FDE consulting-style support is a model that provides end-to-end accompaniment from problem discovery through implementation to on-the-ground adoption.

Traditional IT consulting typically operated on a division-of-labor model, handling analysis, strategic recommendations, and requirements definition, then handing off actual implementation to separate vendors (SIers or SES providers).

This division of labor has structural weaknesses: "information and speed are lost with each handoff" and "recommendations drift from the reality on the ground."

FDE consulting-style support is an approach that structurally eliminates these handoff losses by having the same team take responsibility from problem definition through implementation and adoption.

Common patterns in division-of-labor support models

  • The consultant conducts current-state analysis and strategic recommendations, delivers a report, and the contract ends
  • The client company orders implementation from a different SIer or SES provider based on those recommendations
  • During implementation, "field constraints that weren't visible at the recommendation stage" are discovered one after another, leading to repeated specification changes
  • After going live, no one is responsible for supporting adoption among field staff, and the system goes unused and becomes a shell

With FDE consulting-style support, the same team handles steps 2 through 4 above without handoffs, so projects proceed with the two points of fragmentation—misalignment between recommendations and implementation, and between implementation and adoption—already resolved in advance.

Traditional consulting recommendations tend to end up as "pie in the sky" because technical feasibility and tacit field knowledge are not sufficiently incorporated at the recommendation stage.

SES providers, on the other hand, have the role of implementing requested specifications and do not have the scope of duty to question "whether that specification is truly the problem that needs solving."

FDE consulting-style support has the same entity handle both problem definition and implementation, which

structurally avoids both failure patterns: "recommendations were made but couldn't be realized"

and "implementation was done but missed the mark."

Before considering procurement, check whether three or more of the following apply to you.

  • A PoC (proof of concept) succeeded, but the transition to production deployment has stalled
  • AI tools or systems were introduced but go unused in the field and have become a shell
  • You know where to start, but lack in-house technical resources capable of implementation
  • You have previously asked consultants for strategy alone, and the effort stalled in the execution phase
  • Business data is scattered across multiple systems and departments and has not been integrated
  • You need an approach that "solidifies requirements while building," making it difficult to fully fix specifications upfront

If three or more apply, FDE consulting-style support is a strong option.

Conversely, if requirements are already fully fixed and you simply want to add development headcount, SES will be more cost-efficient.

The monthly fee of ¥2-4 million charged by traditional IT consulting is compensation for strategic proposals and requirements definition only.

Since the implementation phase must be separately ordered from an SIer or SES, the total cost becomes the proposal fee plus implementation costs.

The monthly fee of ¥1.5-3 million for FDE consulting-type support covers everything from problem definition through implementation and adoption.

Rather than simply comparing monthly fees, it is important to make procurement decisions by comparing outcomes, including whether PoC stagnation can be resolved and whether the solution takes root in the field.

DeFactory's FDE consulting-type support proceeds through the following three phases.

  • Phase 1: Field understanding and problem definition (approx. 1-2 months): Through interviews with management and field staff and observation of operations, we identify the problems that truly need to be solved. Success metrics (KPIs) are also agreed upon at this stage
  • Phase 2: Design, implementation, and production deployment (approx. 2-6 months): We build workflows including data infrastructure and AI implementation, and deploy all the way to production operation rather than stopping at PoC. We iterate by building small, testing in the field, and incorporating feedback
  • Phase 3: Adoption support and in-housing transfer (approx. 1-3 months): Through field training and documentation, we support autonomous operation within the company

Contracts are often designed in one-year units because running through these three phases one or two times allows the engagement to move beyond a one-off implementation and onto a continuous improvement cycle.

Accumulating domain knowledge requires a ramp-up period of 3-4 months, and short-term contracts cannot recoup this investment.

Looking back at DeFactory's projects by team structure, of the 9 engagements staffed by a single individual, 2 failed to achieve the results the client expected by the deadline.

In contrast, of the 2 engagements with a multi-person "tiny team" structure, no such shortfalls have occurred to date.

While the sample size is too small to be a rigorous statistical trend, the 2 shortfalls had in common that too much was demanded of a single person at a high level in both "problem discovery and articulation" and "implementation."

Based on this track record, DeFactory recommends multi-person team structures over individual assignments.

Based on DeFactory's track record of 11 closed freelance FDE deals from late 2025 through the first half of 2026, the average monthly fee was ¥1.35 million (¥1.1-1.8 million), and the rate of progression from PoC to production improved roughly 2-3x with FDE involvement.

  • Failure example: Industry: telecommunications / Team: 3 members (1 consultant, 1 full-stack engineer, etc.) / Client-side issue: alignment on understanding the situation and defining deliverables was insufficient, and the contract period was short, so results could not be achieved within the expected timeframe
  • Improvement example: Industry: telecommunications / Team: 3 members (1 consultant, 2 full-stack engineers) / Agentic coding shortened the planned development issues, and in-housing of development knowledge was achieved in 2 months instead of the expected 6

The way problems manifest differs by industry.

The following are typical usage examples; please refer to them as general application patterns rather than individual performance figures.

  • 製造業：
    • In many cases, production line and quality control field data is scattered across paper, Excel, and standalone systems, so an effective approach is to first build a data integration platform and then start AI adoption with specific operations such as inspection and inventory management.   • 金融・保険業：
    • This is an industry where security and compliance requirements are strict, and integration with existing core systems tends to be a challenge. Design capability for API integration and data extraction with legacy systems is especially important
  • SaaS・IT企業：
    • There are cases where FDE consulting-type support is used in a role responsible for embedding AI features into the company's own products or supporting deployment at client companies. Demand is also growing for accompanying support when establishing an in-house FDE organization
  • 物流・小売業：
    • Many themes are directly tied to field operations, such as demand forecasting and inventory optimization, and the presence or absence of a culture of collaboration with field staff greatly influences success

Even if you are unsure which pattern matches your industry, it is possible to sort out your situation individually through a consultation.

Regardless of industry, the support provided through DeFactory's FDE consulting-type support broadly falls into the following two patterns. Please refer to these as general support patterns rather than specific track records.

Pattern 1: End-to-end support from data infrastructure building to analysis and utilization

Not a few companies have data scattered internally that is not being used effectively.

This pattern involves building a data platform (data warehouse/data lake) to centrally consolidate sales data, customer data, and other data distributed across departments, and then providing consistent support through the analysis and utilization phase that enables data-driven decision-making, such as visualization with BI tools and demand forecasting using machine learning models.

Pattern 2: Skill transfer to field staff and realization of in-housing

Aiming to create a state where the client company itself can grow continuously using data and AI without relying on external help, we conduct study sessions and hands-on training for field staff in parallel with project execution.

This pattern involves lecturing on AI model operation methods and procedures for training on new data, and supporting the establishment of a structure that allows the company to operate autonomously even after the project ends (this corresponds to the concrete example in Section 4-1, "Phase 3: Adoption Support and In-Housing Transfer").

Note that if you are also considering strengthening data utilization in sales and marketing alongside AI adoption, GTM Engineer vs. FDE: Which Does Your Company Need? Key Differences and Hiring Tips Every Executive Should Know is also useful as a reference for decision-making.

To ensure that the effects of FDE consulting-style support don't end with a vague sense of "things got better somehow," it is important to agree on KPIs before signing the contract. DeFactory designs KPIs primarily from three perspectives.

A free 3-minute diagnostic will tell you whether an FDE is a good fit for your company.

  • Perspective 1: Operational efficiency metrics: How much the time spent on a specific task was reduced after implementation (e.g., time previously spent creating monthly reports)
  • Perspective 2: Adoption metrics: Whether the implemented tools and systems are being used continuously rather than only temporarily (e.g., weekly active usage rate)
  • Perspective 3: Transition speed metrics: The time it took to move from PoC to production operation, or whether the transition was achieved at all

Agreeing on KPIs in advance from these three perspectives helps you avoid ending up in a state where "it was implemented, but the results are unclear."

As awareness of FDEs spreads, services calling themselves "FDE consulting" have appeared even though their actual nature is SES or traditional consulting. Before placing an order, check the following.

4 things to check regarding contracts and structure

  • Whether outcome metrics (such as hours saved or adoption rate) are included in the proposal. If the discussion only covers man-hours, it is SES-style
  • Whether they propose "defining requirements together" rather than "acting only after requirements are handed to them"
  • If they only propose development from scratch, they may not have reusable technical assets
  • Whether they have organizational know-how, rather than a structure dependent on a single person

2 things to check regarding fit with your company's needs

  • Whether the contract scope includes not just implementation but also support for on-site adoption and training
  • A proposal based solely on 3-month renewals is an SES-style design that does not account for accumulating domain knowledge
