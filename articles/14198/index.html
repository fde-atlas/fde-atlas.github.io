Fast-paced environments · FDE recruiter screen question

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 2 min read

Summary

This article breaks down a recruiter-screen question for Cognition's L4 Deployed Engineer role in Singapore: whether you fit a fast-paced environment. It cites Colin Jarvis, then Head of Forward Deployed Engineering at OpenAI, saying customer scoping often mismatches on-the-ground data and systems, so teams should move fast, prove out brick walls, then adjust scope. The author argues pace means deciding what to drop when a plan meets reality, not working longer hours, and advises picking deadlines set by others (customer go-lives, regulators, outages), explaining your cut-and-keep mechanism, and giving a genuine dislike as a trade-off. The trap is answering with a story about nights and weekends, which shows effort but not decision-making.

Key points

  • Cognition's Singapore L4 recruiter screen tests fit with a fast-paced environment.
  • Colin Jarvis: customer scoping often mismatches reality; prove brick walls fast, then adjust scope.
  • Pace means deciding what to drop, not working longer hours.
  • Use deadlines set by others: go-lives, regulator dates, outages.
  • Common mistake: equating long hours with pace and skipping the trade-off.

How to answer

Pace can come up in the first call. One candidate reported, in September 2026, that the recruiter screen for Cognition’s mid-level (L4) Deployed Engineer role in Singapore checks whether you can fit into the company’s fast-paced environment. Source 1Cognition AI Forward Deployed Engineer Interview ExperiencePublisherAced (formerly Exponent)Source typecandidate’s personal write-up The Pragmatic Engineer quoted Colin Jarvis, then Head of Forward Deployed Engineering at OpenAI, in August 2025 as saying that what customers describe in scoping often doesn’t match the data and system reality on the ground, so the team wants to bias toward moving fast, proving out any brick walls and then adjusting the scope. Source 2What are Forward Deployed Engineers, and why are they so in demand? (Gergely Orosz)PublisherThe Pragmatic EngineerSource typenews report Speed here means deciding what to drop once the plan meets reality, not working longer hours.

  • Pick speed that came from outside. A customer go-live, a regulator’s date, an outage. Pace someone else set is closer to the job than a sprint you chose.
  • Show your mechanism. How you found what the plan got wrong, what you cut, what you refused to cut and why, and how you kept the people waiting on you informed. This is the part the question is really asking about.
  • Say what you liked, concretely. Decisions in hours, seeing users on the new version the same week.
  • Give a real dislike, as a trade-off. A cost the pace created, what it cost later and what you now do differently. A dislike you have already fixed a habit for is the strongest kind.
  • Say what you need to keep it up. One sentence, so the recruiter can judge the fit.

The trap is “I thrive under pressure” and a story about nights and weekends. It answers whether you’ll work hard, which nobody asked, and hides the thing they did ask: how you decide under a date you can’t move.

Follow-ups

What the interviewer may ask next, once your first answer is on the table.

  • That sprint had an end date. What if the pace here doesn’t?
  • When did the pace stop being sustainable, and what did you do about it?
  • How would you tell a customer that the date has to move?

Where answers go wrong

  • Tells a story about long hours and calls it pace, which says nothing about how they decide what to drop.
  • Gives a dislike that is really a boast (“I disliked that others couldn’t keep up”), so the trade-off half of the question goes unanswered.

Answer this in two minutes

Write the answer you would say out loud. The clock starts with your first word.
