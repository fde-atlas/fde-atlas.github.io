How to Become an FDE? Career Entry Points and How to Read Job Postings

Original title: FDEになるには？キャリアの入り口と求人票の読み方

FDX株式会社 · 2026-10-16 · 18 min read

Summary

This career guide for engineers explains that FDE job titles vary widely in substance: some roles build production systems on-site after a deal closes, while others resemble pre-sales support or traditional client-embedded work. Comparing postings from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Palantir (checked September 29, 2026), it notes required experience ranges from 1+ to 5+ years, with differing language and travel conditions. It maps entry paths from six prior roles (SIer, SES, consulting, pre-sales, data, in-house development), each with strengths to highlight and gaps to fill. It proposes six criteria for reading job postings and advises preparing case interviews and fully experiencing one project through production in the first year.

Key points

  • Job title alone doesn't reveal whether work is pre- or post-sale
  • Overseas FDE postings require 1+ to 5+ years, varying by company
  • Six prior career paths each have distinct strengths and gaps
  • Read postings via six criteria; ask about gaps in interviews
  • First year: take one project from definition to production adoption

Job postings with the title "FDE" are now becoming visible in Japan as well. But even when they share the same FDE name, some postings involve going to the customer's site after winning the contract and building the production system to completion, while others center on technical support for sales negotiations, with realities closer to traditional on-site dispatch work. If you choose where to apply based on the job title alone, you can easily end up in a job that differs from what you expected after joining.

This article is aimed at engineers considering a career change to FDE or joining as a new graduate, and organizes the following points.

  • The substance of FDE work is "defining problems at the customer's site, building to production, and making it usable in the field." Judge it not by the title, but by which phase you own and how results are measured
  • Even in overseas postings (OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir), required years of experience range from 1+ to 5+, and language and travel conditions differ by company (verified September 29, 2026)
  • Entry points exist from SIer, SES, consulting, pre-sales, data roles, and in-house development. Each prior role differs in "strengths you can bring" and "gaps you need to fill first"
  • Read job postings through 6 items. For items not written, you can determine them in interviews by asking "What did you build in recent projects, and how far did you take operations?"

The definition of FDE itself is summarized in "What is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)?", and the design of how companies incorporate FDEs into their organizations (skill maps, evaluation, salary ranges) in "Incorporating FDEs into an organization". This article focuses on the perspective of the applicant.

Who this article is for

  • Engineers considering a career change to FDE from SIer, SES, consulting, pre-sales, or similar roles
  • People comparing job postings with the FDE title and wanting to decide which to apply for
  • New graduates and early-career engineers who want to know what to build up now to work as an FDE in the future
  • People preparing for an FDE interview who want to organize what to prepare

What FDE work is (3-line summary)

  • Enter the customer's site, observe operations, and define for yourself "what to build to produce results"
  • Write code yourself, and take it beyond a PoC to production operation
  • Even after going to production, keep iterating improvements so it continues to be used in the field

The difference from a software engineer in one sentence: it is not a job of building from received specifications, but a job of owning everything from deciding the specifications to production adoption, alone or in a pair.

How projects end differs by company. The overseas postings reviewed in this article describe production deployment and ongoing collaboration with customers, and do not require handover to in-house teams. FDX emphasizes, as how a project ends, transferring operations to the customer's in-house team and leaving after leaving it self-sufficient. Whether handover is part of the role is not a general FDE condition but a company-specific policy, which you should confirm in the job posting.

The reality of FDE work

Project flow and how time is spent

FDE projects generally proceed in the order of "field observation and interviews," "prototyping," "evaluation and improvement," and "production rollout," after which they split into either continuously supporting adoption in the field or handing over to the customer's in-house team and leaving. How a week is spent changes greatly depending on the project stage, but generalized it looks like the following allocation (a generalization by the editorial team, not the actual working conditions of any specific company).

  • Beginning of the week: attend the customer's regular meeting, and pick up how what you delivered last week was used, plus complaints and exceptions raised in the field
  • Midweek: focus on implementation and evaluation. In projects using generative AI, creating eval data and measuring accuracy takes up a large share (how to build evals: "Eval design for AI agents")
  • Late week: show working things to the customer's contacts and agree on what to prioritize over the next week
  • End of the week: leave behind procedure documents and design notes, and share project learnings internally

What is easily overlooked is that there is as much time talking with people in the field as there is time writing code. Conversely, if weeks of only talking and not building continue, that is closer to a consultant's way of working than an FDE's.

Who is a good fit—and who is not

Aptitude for FDE is determined less by technical skill and more by "what kind of situation gives you a sense of fulfillment."

PerspectivePeople who fit wellPeople who may not fit
How work beginsWant to formulate your own questions from an ambiguous problem stateWant to start building only after specs are finalized
Sense of achievementFeel fulfillment when what you build is used in the field and changes operationsFeel the greatest fulfillment in technically elegant design or the pursuit of new technology itself
Distance from customersCan talk with field staff weekly and use pushback and complaints as materialWant to minimize customer interaction and focus on development
How work endsPrefer to build a state that runs without you and move on to the next siteWant to keep nurturing a single system over the long term
Where you workCan accept working styles that include being stationed at or traveling to customer sitesWant a fixed work location and minimal travel

Matching the right-hand column is not a flaw. It is an aptitude suited to thriving as a development engineer for in-house products or as a specialist in a specific domain. If you feel fulfillment in pre-contract technical decisions, a Sales Engineer may suit you better than FDE. The differences between the two are compared across 8 axes in "The difference between FDE and Sales Engineer". If you want to focus on dialogue with executives and defining outcomes, the Deployment Strategist (DS), a strategy role paired with FDEs, is also an option ("DS vs FDE").

There is no fixed previous role for becoming an FDE. Wherever you come from, there are strengths you can bring and gaps you need to fill first. When transitioning from SES, you are likely to be asked whether you can explain in words the difference between SES and FDE. For the differences in contract and scope of responsibility, see "FDE vs SES vs SI vs Strategy Consulting" and "Contract Models in the AI Era".

How new graduates and early-career engineers can get in

Paths to land a job titled FDE as a new graduate are currently limited. Overseas, Palantir recruits new graduates for "Forward Deployed Software Engineer, New Grad" roles in the US, UK, South Korea, and elsewhere. The New York listing targets those graduating in December 2026 or spring 2027 (verified September 29, 2026). Meanwhile, all current FDX openings require practical work experience. If you look for new-graduate FDE openings in Japan, check each company's new-graduate recruiting page down to the details of the job duties.

Three things that pay off if you build them while young:

  • Experience identifying a problem yourself, building an app using generative AI, and having someone actually use it
  • Development and operation of systems running in production, including incident response and how to read logs
  • Experience listening to non-engineers and translating that into requirements. Even just advising other departments within your company is fine

How to read job postings

The job title FDE does not guarantee the substance of the work. Reading job postings through the following six items reveals the reality. Items 1–5 help you judge how FDE-like the role is; item 6, how projects end, is not about good or bad but about checking whether it matches your preferences.

Examples where the job title alone does not reveal the project phase

Examples where the project phase cannot be read from the job title exist even at major overseas companies. As of September 29, 2026, the technical role confirmed at Anthropic's Tokyo office was "Applied AI Engineer" rather than "Forward Deployed Engineer." The job posting centers on serving as the primary technical advisor to prospective customers considering Claude, working alongside sales to build pilots and evals. It states that the technical advisor role continues after deployment, but the emphasis leans toward pre-sales. This differs in emphasis from US FDE postings, which center on building production applications within the customer's systems.

Even for closely related roles at the same company, the job title alone cannot determine whether the posting covers pre-sales or post-sales work. After narrowing down by job title search, always compare the job descriptions.

What is evaluated in interviews

In FDE selection processes, some companies include case interviews that present a business problem and ask how you would solve it, in addition to technical interviews. FDX's FDE selection also proceeds in the order of document screening, casual interview, technical interview and case interview, final interview, and offer discussion. The following four points are commonly evaluated in interviews.

  • How you break down an ambiguous problem. What you confirm first, and what you discard
  • Judgment on the trade-offs among scope, speed, and quality. OpenAI's job posting also lists making decisions among these three and adjusting plans to meet deadlines as part of the role
  • Concreteness of experience taking things to production. Not so much what you built, but where you got stuck and how you overcame it
  • Ability to explain technology in the other person's language. Whether you can speak with the interviewer as if they were a client

What helps in preparation is choosing one of your past projects and being able to talk through it in four stages: "problem definition," "what you built," "how it was used in production," and "subsequent operations." A project that stalled at the PoC stage is fine. If you can explain structurally why it stalled, that itself becomes material for evaluation (for organizing the reasons projects stall, see "5 Structural Reasons AI PoCs Fail").

The first year

The first year after joining is designed differently by each company. FDX's FDE job posting lists the following three points as expected outcomes for the first 12 months.

  • Being embedded in or accompanying one or two clients, guiding AI and automation projects to production release
  • Completing business requirements gathering through requirements definition, PoC, and productionization, either alone or in pairs
  • Handing over operations to the client's in-house team and leaving them in a self-sustaining state

Whichever company you join, what you should keep in mind in the first year is seeing one project through to production deployment and subsequent adoption, from start to finish. Rather than touching parts of multiple projects bit by bit, the experience of seeing one project through from beginning to end is more reproducible in your next project. For how companies develop FDEs in what order, the 18-month development roadmap in article 06 is a useful reference.

FDX's support

As an implementation partner for AX (AI Transformation), FDX works with clients through everything from decomposing business processes to implementation, on-site adoption, and operational handover. Support in the form of experts embedded on-site is summarized in Solutions and FDX Expert.

As one example of the work an FDE handles, in the client case of automated test case creation for core system renewal integration testing, AI analyzed 449 design documents to automatically generate 3,579 integration test specification cases, with the testing workload calculated at 32.0 person-days. This was a project that broke down the on-site problem of unmeasurable testing volume into a buildable unit: structuring the design documents.

FDX is currently hiring for Forward Deployed Engineer. Desired experience includes 5 or more years of professional experience as a software engineer, experience in client engagement, requirements definition, and problem definition, and experience implementing business applications using generative AI (personal projects acceptable). Work style is office attendance 2-3 days per week (including working at client sites during embedded periods), flextime, and side jobs allowed (excluding competitors and conflicts of interest). For Sales Engineers, who handle pre-sales technical decisions, a work arrangement that partially joins post-sales projects as an FDE can also be discussed.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

Q1. Can I become an FDE without prior experience?

A. The path to being hired directly as an FDE with no professional experience as an engineer at all is limited. This is because all of the mid-career postings confirmed in this article assume experience developing production systems or professional experience. However, the required years of experience range widely by company, from 1 or more to 5 or more years. The shortcut is to accumulate development experience while finding problems yourself, building solutions, and getting people to use them.

Q2. Can new graduates get a job as an FDE?

A. Overseas, there are examples such as Palantir recruiting new graduates for Forward Deployed Software Engineer roles. FDX's current postings require professional experience, so when looking for new-graduate postings in Japan, check the job descriptions on each company's new-graduate recruiting pages. At the new-graduate stage, choosing a workplace where you can gain experience developing systems that run in production and translating non-engineers' requests into requirements will make it easier to move into an FDE role a few years later.

Q3. Do FDEs need English?

A. It depends on the company and the customer. OpenAI's Tokyo FDE job posting requires fluency in both Japanese and English, and conducts interviews in both languages. For projects aimed at domestic customers of Japanese companies, the work often centers on understanding and explaining things in Japanese. In addition to the language requirements in the application criteria, it is a good idea to check whether the people you would use English with are customers or internal staff.

Q4. Isn't FDE just a trendy job title that will soon disappear?

A. The job title may change to another name. However, demand for the work itself—getting involved in the customer's business, defining problems, building solutions through to production, and embedding them in the field—will grow as generative AI adoption moves into the production stage. Rather than investing in the title, invest in experience of carrying a problem through from definition to production operation and adoption; that will remain valuable even if the name changes.

Q5. How much does an FDE earn?

A. Salary ranges are covered in article 06, which organizes the topic from the perspective of companies integrating FDEs into their organizations. For conditions for each position, it is most reliable to check each company's job posting. Beyond comparing salaries, unless you also check whether the stage you would handle and how results are measured suit you, you may not last even if the conditions are good.

Q6. Is SIer or SES experience valued for FDE roles?

A. Yes, it is valued. Experience with requirements definition and the ability to understand a client's business form the foundation of an FDE. What is questioned is whether you have experience judging for yourself what should be built, not just building to fixed specifications. It is a good idea to prepare examples where you made improvements beyond your assigned scope, and be able to explain them along with the reasoning behind your decisions.

Summary

  • The FDE's job is to define problems at the customer's site, build solutions through to production, and get them into a state where they are actually used in the field. Whether the role extends to handing over to in-house teams depends on each company's policy; FDX places importance on going that far.
  • Even in overseas job postings, required experience ranges from 1+ to 5+ years, and language and travel conditions also differ by company (as of September 29, 2026).
  • Entry points exist from any background: SIer, SES, consulting, sales engineering, data roles, or in-house development. Show the strengths of your previous role and fill any gaps before applying.
  • Read job postings through six items: the stage you would handle, how results are measured, who decides the problems, whether you write code, your affiliation and team, and how projects end. The stage you would handle cannot be determined from the job title alone.
  • In interviews, be ready to talk about past projects in four stages: "problem definition," "what you built," "how it was used in production," and "subsequent operations."
  • In your first year, prioritize seeing a single project all the way through to production operation and subsequent adoption.
