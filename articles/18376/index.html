Integrating FDEs into Your Organization | Skill Maps, Evaluation Criteria, and Development Roadmaps

Original title: FDEを組織に組み込む｜スキルマップ・評価基準・育成ロードマップ

FDX株式会社 · 2026-05-20 · 12 min read

Summary

This practical guide from FDX株式会社 argues that companies cannot simply plug FDEs into existing engineer HR systems. It defines a four-domain skill map (engineering, business, domain knowledge, communication), requires senior FDEs to reach Level 3 in at least three domains, and proposes evaluating FDEs on customer outcomes—NPS, business KPI improvement, and self-sufficiency transfer—rather than billable hours. It lays out an 18-month training roadmap, transition paths from SE, data scientist, and consulting roles taking 12–24 months, and 2026 Japan salary ranges from ¥8–12M for junior to ¥18–25M for senior FDEs, below US AI companies' $300K–500K plus equity.

Key points

  • FDEs need above-standard skills across four domains; a single-domain 'superman' profile doesn't work.
  • Evaluation should use customer NPS, business KPI gains, and handover/self-sufficiency metrics, not hours logged.
  • An 18-month roadmap moves trainees from shadowing to co-leading to independent project leadership.
  • Transitions from SE, data science, or consulting take 12–24 months, with different gaps to fill per background.
  • Japan market pay: ¥8–12M junior, ¥18–25M senior; US AI firms offer $300K–500K plus equity.

Key points (100 words): If you introduce FDEs into a company, the existing HR system won't work as-is. This article explains, from a practical perspective, the FDE four-domain skill map, customer-outcome-based evaluation criteria, an 18-month development roadmap, and transition paths from existing roles.

Reading "What is an FDE" and "External FDEs vs. internal development" first will deepen your understanding of this article.

Summary (3 lines)

  • FDEs need above-standard skills in all four domains (engineering / business / domain / communication). A "superman" who excels in only one domain won't function.
  • Evaluate based on customer outcomes, not hours worked. The three core metrics are customer NPS, business KPI improvement, and degree of self-sufficiency achieved.
  • Transitioning from existing roles (SE, data scientist, strategy consultant, PM) to FDE takes 12–18 months. The skills to reinforce differ by background domain.

1. The FDE Four-Domain Skill Map

FDEs are required to have skills in the following four domains.

Domain 1: Engineering

Core:

  • Programming (at least 2 languages, such as Python, TypeScript, Go)
  • Data design (SQL, data warehouses, data pipelines)
  • Cloud platforms (AWS / GCP / Azure, any one)
  • API integration and system connectivity

Additional skills for the generative AI era:

  • LLM API usage (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google AI)
  • Prompt engineering and eval design
  • Agent frameworks (LangChain, LangGraph, Claude Agent SDK, etc.)
  • Vector databases (Pinecone, Weaviate, pgvector, etc.)
  • AI governance (audit logs, guardrails, built-in human review)

Domain 2: Business

  • ROI estimation and business case creation
  • Business process redesign (BPR) methodologies
  • Investment decisions and budget negotiations
  • Presentations for executives

Domain 3: Domain Knowledge

  • Basic knowledge of the assigned industry (manufacturing, finance, healthcare, retail, etc.)
  • Understanding of business functions (sales, marketing, finance, HR, SCM, etc.)
  • Regulatory requirements (e.g., FIEA for finance, GCP/GMP and personal information protection laws for healthcare)

Domain 4: Communication

  • Dialogue with executives (CEO, CFO, CIO level)
  • Eliciting tacit knowledge from frontline staff
  • Coordination and consensus-building among stakeholders
  • Documentation (strategy / design / operations documents)

Skill Level Definitions

Evaluate in three stages.

  • Level 1 (Basic): Can execute when given instructions
  • Level 2 (Applied): Can make decisions and execute independently
  • Level 3 (Leading): Can involve others and take the lead

The level guidelines for FDEs are as follows.

RoleEngineeringBusinessDomainCommunication
Junior FDEL2L1L1L1
Mid-level FDEL2-L3L2L2L2
Senior FDEL3L2-L3L2-L3L3

Important: The condition for a senior FDE is not that any single domain reaches L3. All four domains must be at L2 or above, and at least three of them must reach L3.

2. Engineering Skills in Detail | Required, Recommended, Optional

Required (all FDEs)

  • At least 5 years of professional experience in at least one programming language
  • SQL (complex JOINs, window functions, performance tuning)
  • Git / version control
  • Basic cloud operations

Recommended (mid-level and above)

  • Using multiple languages appropriately (e.g., Python + TypeScript)
  • Infrastructure as Code (Terraform, CloudFormation)
  • Containerization (Docker, Kubernetes)
  • Production implementation experience with generative AI

Optional (specific domains)

  • Data science (statistics, ML)
  • Frontend development (React, Vue)
  • Mobile app development
  • Security (encryption, authentication and authorization)

Note: What is required of an FDE is not to be a superman who can do everything alone. After covering the required domains, the ability to coordinate and bring in the right specialists depending on the project's characteristics matters more. Gaps can be filled through team composition.

3. Business Skills in Detail

Required

  • Reading profit and loss statements (P&L)
  • ROI estimation (calculating NPV, IRR, payback period)
  • Describing business flows (BPMN, sequence diagrams, etc.)
  • Creating and maintaining stakeholder maps

Recommended

  • Reading balance sheets (BS)
  • Business reform methodologies (Lean, Six Sigma, TOC, etc.)
  • Corporate strategy frameworks (5 Forces, SWOT, Value Chain, etc.)
  • Fundamentals of market analysis

The core of "FDE-style business thinking"

The ability to think about business and systems simultaneously. For example, being able to do the following:

  • Translate "reduce business workload by 50%" into system requirements
  • Translate "improve data quality by 80%" into business process changes
  • Critical thinking to reality-check ROI figures rather than trusting them blindly

4. How to Acquire Domain Knowledge

Required knowledge by industry

  • Manufacturing: production management, SCM, quality management, ERP (especially SAP)
  • Finance: core banking systems, risk management, compliance, FIEA
  • Healthcare: electronic medical records, medical fee claims, GCP/GMP, personal information protection
  • Retail: inventory management, demand forecasting, e-commerce platforms
  • HR: HR policies, payroll, labor management, HRIS

Essential knowledge by business function

  • Sales: CRM, deal processes, pipeline management
  • Marketing: MA, lead generation, funnel analysis
  • Finance: accounting standards (J-GAAP / IFRS), consolidation, disclosure
  • SCM: supply and demand planning, procurement, logistics
  • HR: talent management, evaluation, compensation

Acquire domain knowledge by combining real projects with industry books

An FDE's domain knowledge cannot be acquired through classroom study alone. Combine customer interactions on real projects with parallel reading of industry standard books and trade publications. At least 6 months of hands-on project experience per industry is required.

5. Breaking down communication skills

Executive dialogue

  • Speak with numbers (revenue, cost, ROI, risk)
  • State conclusions concisely first (elevator pitch)
  • Connect to the executive agenda (mid-term plans, KPI trees)

Frontline interviews

  • Drawing out tacit knowledge (5 Whys, observation, job shadowing)
  • Understanding and persuading resistant stakeholders
  • Ensuring psychological safety

Cross-functional coordination

  • Mediating conflicts of interest between departments
  • Consensus-building techniques (voting, priority matrices, data-driven approaches)
  • Rigorous management of meeting minutes and action items

Documentation

  • Strategy documents (background, objectives, KPIs, roadmap)
  • Design documents (business flows, system architecture, data models)
  • Operations documents (procedures, troubleshooting, SLAs)

Documentation skills sit at the core of an FDE's output. They continue to be referenced within the customer's organization even after the project ends, remaining as an asset.

6. Evaluation criteria | Measured by customer outcomes, not hours

Three key KPIs

1. Customer NPS (Net Promoter Score)

  • Quarterly NPS surveys of the customer's executives and frontline staff
  • 9-10: promoters, 6-8: passives, 5 or below: detractors
  • NPS = % promoters - % detractors

2. Business KPI improvement

  • Achievement of business KPIs defined per project (e.g., % reduction in man-hours, % revenue increase, % lead time reduction)
  • Measured across three points: baseline → To-Be → actual results

3. Self-sufficiency achievement (Transfer Skills KPI)

  • Skill acquisition level of internalization candidates (level changes on the 4-area skill map)
  • Trend in the ratio of tasks led internally
  • Achievement of handover completion criteria

Secondary KPIs

  • Project deadline adherence rate
  • Budget adherence rate
  • Internal contributions (knowledge sharing, mentoring junior staff)

Evaluation frequency

  • Monthly: project progress and KPI progress review
  • Quarterly: 360-degree feedback, NPS aggregation, business KPI measurement
  • Annually: review of grade and compensation

What "not evaluating by man-hours" means

Evaluating FDEs by man-hours creates perverse incentives. Looking busy, slicing tasks into fine pieces, and adding meetings all count as "working." The FDE model asks for the opposite. Decisions that maximize business impact—and sometimes the resolve to deliberately not do something—are what get evaluated.

7. 18-month development roadmap

Phase 1: Shadowing and implementation support (0-6 months)

Goal: Shadow senior FDEs in their work and acquire the fundamentals of the 4-area skills.

Key activities:

  • Constant pairing with a senior FDE (daily accompaniment)
  • Attending customer meetings (observing at first, gradually speaking up)
  • Producing documents on their behalf (minutes, design notes, operating procedures)
  • Handling simple implementation tasks (code reviewed by senior FDEs)

Milestones:

  • Technical L2, business L1, domain L1, communication L1
  • Able to imitate a senior FDE's way of thinking

Phase 2: Co-leading (6-18 months)

Goal: Lead projects alongside senior FDEs. Able to make independent judgments on mid-sized tasks.

Key activities:

  • Proactively leading parts of customer meetings
  • Drafting business redesign proposals (reviewed by senior FDEs)
  • Leading mid-difficulty implementation tasks
  • Coaching internalization candidates (internal staff) side by side

Milestones:

  • Technical L2-L3, business L2, domain L2, communication L2
  • Able to lead projects independently as a mid-level FDE

Phase 3: Independent leadership + developing juniors (18 months+)

Goal: Lead projects independently. Able to develop new FDEs.

Key activities:

  • Handling customers as project lead
  • Pairing and mentoring new FDEs
  • Promoting internal knowledge sharing and standardization
  • Involvement from the sales stage (pre-sales)

Milestones:

  • Technical L3, business L2-L3, domain L2-L3, communication L3
  • Established career as a senior FDE

8. Transition paths from existing roles

Transitioning from SE / systems engineer

Strengths: technical skills (L2-L3) Areas to reinforce: business, communication, domain Transition period: 12-18 months (participation in hybrid FDE projects) Key learning investments: MBA-style business fundamentals, accumulating experience in executive dialogue

Transitioning from data scientist

Strengths: Technical + analytical thinking, generative AI-related skills Areas to reinforce: Production implementation, stakeholder management, ROI estimation Transition period: 12–18 months Key learning investments: Software engineering fundamentals (testing, CI/CD), rotation to the business side

Transitioning from strategy consulting

Strengths: Business, communication, industry knowledge Areas to reinforce: Technology (especially coding) Transition period: 18–24 months (mastering coding is the biggest hurdle) Key learning investments: Intensive programming study, hands-on experience implementing production systems

Transitioning from PM / project manager

Strengths: Communication, stakeholder management Areas to reinforce: Technology, business (especially ROI), deeper domain expertise Transition period: 18–24 months Key learning investments: Coding, deeper industry knowledge, financial literacy

Transitioning from business transformation consulting

Strengths: Business, domain Areas to reinforce: Technology (coding), generative AI-related skills Transition period: 12–18 months Key learning investments: Programming, AI implementation

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1. Can FDEs be evaluated under the same system as engineers?

A. No. Typical engineer evaluations focus on technical skills, but FDEs require evaluation across four areas: business, domain, communication, and technology. Moreover, unless "customer outcomes" are built into the evaluation metrics, FDE incentives become inward-facing. An FDE-style evaluation system needs to be designed separately.

Q2. What is the salary range for FDEs?

A. In the Japanese market, the rough benchmarks are ¥8–12 million for junior, ¥12–18 million for mid-level, ¥18–25 million for senior, and ¥25–40 million for principal FDEs (as of 2026). Major overseas AI companies exceed these, with ranges of $300K–$500K plus equity being typical.

Q3. Can an SE become an FDE?

A. Yes. Since SEs already have technical skills at the L2–L3 level, they can reach mid-level FDE by reinforcing the three areas of business, communication, and domain over 12–18 months. However, SEs who have only written code may initially struggle with "talking to executives" and "leading business process redesign." On-the-job training on real projects is essential.

Q4. How many years does it take to become a fully independent FDE?

A. With constant participation in hybrid FDE projects, junior → mid-level takes 12–18 months, and mid-level → senior takes another 2–3 years. Through self-study and OJT alone, it takes 5–7 years.

Q5. What is the difference between an FDE and a general senior engineer (IC: Individual Contributor)?

A. A senior engineer is someone capable of highly advanced technical system design and implementation, with responsibility confined to the technical domain. An FDE generates business impact across all four areas—technology, business, domain, and communication—with responsibility extending to the customer's business outcomes. Transitioning from senior engineer to FDE is possible, but requires expanding one's scope of responsibility and mindset.

Q6. When developing FDEs internally, what should you invest in first?

A. In order: (1) securing executive sponsorship, (2) formally institutionalizing FDE-style positions (grading and compensation), (3) creating opportunities to work alongside external FDEs (at least 6 months), and (4) piloting a business impact evaluation system. Deferring the system design makes it impossible to recoup the development investment.

Q7. Are FDEs at high risk of job-hopping?

A. FDEs have high market value and attract many external offers. Countermeasures: (1) competitive salary ranges, (2) an empowered position reporting directly to executives, (3) clearly defined FDE-style career paths (promotion routes to principal and executive-officer-level roles), (4) support for participating in cross-industry FDE communities, and (5) use of stock options and long-term incentives. A design philosophy centered on retention through engagement is essential.

Summary

  • FDEs need Level 2 or higher skills across all four areas (technology / business / domain / communication)
  • Don't measure performance in man-hours. Evaluate based on three customer-outcome metrics: NPS, business KPIs, and self-sufficiency
  • Development follows a standard 18-month roadmap, progressing from shadowing to co-leading to independent leadership
  • Transitions from existing roles take 12–24 months, and the skills to reinforce vary depending on the background area
  • In the Japanese market, annual salaries are roughly ¥8–12 million for junior roles and ¥18–25 million for senior roles
  • The key to making an FDE organization work lies in establishing evaluation systems, career paths, and contract structures across the entire company
