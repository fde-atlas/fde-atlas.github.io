Scope creep

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 1 min read

Summary

This piece explains scope creep: requirements grow through small additions nobody treats as changes, so delivery dates slip without an explicit decision. It cites Sierra's May 2026 post on its Strategist role, which names pushing back on scope creep, and OpenAI's Colin Jarvis, whose team runs only about 10 engagements at a time and declines more often than it accepts. For FDE interviews, the advice is to first decide whether a late ask is creep or a real requirement surfacing late; if it is creep, trade rather than absorb (swap one feature for another or move a date), then record the trade in a change log the same day and inform the sponsor, not just the requester.

Key points

  • Scope creep is unreviewed small additions that shift dates implicitly.
  • Sierra's Strategist role explicitly includes pushing back on scope creep.
  • OpenAI's FDE unit limits itself to about 10 concurrent engagements.
  • First classify a late ask as creep or a genuine late-surfacing requirement.
  • Trade scope instead of absorbing it, and log changes with the sponsor same-day.

Full text

Scope creep is requirements growing through small additions that nobody reviews as a change, with no matching change to the date, the people or the budget. Each addition sounds reasonable on its own (“can the export also include last quarter?”); together they move the delivery date without anyone deciding to move it.

In FDE interviews

Sierra’s post on its Strategist role, published in May 2026, names pushing back on scope creep as part of keeping deployments on track; the role is not an engineering title, and the post says its strategists come from product management, engineering, consulting and other backgrounds. It belongs in answers to behavioral questions about saying no to a senior customer, and in client scenarios where a product manager asks for more features by the same date; the lesson What customer rounds look like, and the core moves shows how those role-plays run. First decide whether the ask is creep or the real requirement surfacing late: if the region filter is how the sponsor will judge the project, absorbing it and cutting something else is the right call. Otherwise, trade instead of absorbing: “I can add the region filter by Friday if the audit export moves to the next release. Which matters more to you this month?” Then write the trade in the change log the same day and send it to the sponsor, not only to the person who asked.

The lesson Saying no, and holding scope covers the no that offers a path, trading scope for date and keeping a visible change log.
