AI created 1.3 million new jobs globally between 2023 and 2025, according to LinkedIn's 2026 Labour Report. These are not theoretical future roles, they are open positions companies are actively hiring for right now. The five categories are data annotator, head of AI, AI engineer, AI forensic analyst, and forward-deployed engineer, and together they represent what LinkedIn calls the "new collar" era of work.

What Are New Collar Jobs and Why Does AI Create Them?

New collar job is a term for roles that blend technical skills, human judgment, and hands-on work, a category that does not fit traditional "blue collar" or "white collar" labels. LinkedIn introduced the term in its 2026 Labour Report to describe the fastest-growing category of AI-driven employment.

These roles exist because AI systems require continuous human input, oversight, and correction to function correctly. AI models do not self-train, self-audit, or self-deploy, humans are required at every stage. The more AI scales, the more of these roles are needed. The data annotation market alone is projected to grow at 33% annually through 2030.

For context: global tech layoffs surpassed 240,000 in the same period, and 59% of companies directly blamed AI for workforce cuts in 2025. The net picture is not uniformly positive, but the 1.3 million figure represents real, fillable positions that require specific skills most workers have not yet developed.

The 5 New Jobs AI Created (With Numbers)

Between 2023 and 2025, these five new-collar categories emerged from AI adoption:

  • Data Annotators: 774,000 new roles
  • Head of AI: 298,000 new roles
  • AI Engineer: 177,000 new roles
  • AI Forensic Analyst: 49,000 new roles
  • Forward-Deployed Engineers / PMs: 9,000 new roles (42x growth since 2023)

Data Annotator: The Largest AI-Created Role

Data annotator is the person who labels, tags, and reviews raw data so AI models can learn what they are seeing. AI does not understand context, culture, safety risks, or bias on its own, humans teach it by labeling training data and providing feedback on model outputs.

With 774,000 new roles created in two years, data annotation is by far the largest category. The work is often contract or gig-based, which means access is relatively low-barrier. AI literacy and attention to detail matter more than formal credentials. If you are preparing to apply for annotation or related AI support roles, an AI resume builder can help you frame these skills clearly for hiring managers who may not yet have standard job descriptions for these positions.

Head of AI: The Senior Leadership Role

Head of AI is a senior executive role responsible for AI strategy, model selection, deployment oversight, and compliance. With 298,000 new positions in two years, demand has spiked sharply across every major economy: the U.S., UK, Australia, Canada, Germany, and India all saw significant increases in Head of AI appointments.

This role exists because AI moved from "experimental project" to "core business function" faster than most companies expected. The combination of strategic urgency and regulatory pressure around AI governance means boards want a named executive accountable for AI decisions. Most Head of AI hires come from senior engineering, data science, or product leadership backgrounds.

AI Engineer: The Fastest-Growing Technical Role

AI Engineer is the person who builds, deploys, and maintains production AI systems. With 177,000 new roles created and the number one ranking in U.S. job demand for the second straight year, this is the highest-volume technical new-collar role.

The distinction from a traditional software engineer is deployment context: AI engineers work with models, APIs, inference infrastructure, and prompt engineering rather than pure application development. Companies need these engineers because generative AI tools only create value when properly integrated into real products and workflows, a non-trivial technical challenge. If you are interviewing for AI engineering roles, practicing with an AI mock interview tool can help you prepare for the systems design and technical depth questions these roles require.

AI Forensic Analyst: The Specialized Investigator

AI forensic analyst is a specialist who audits, investigates, and explains AI system behavior after the fact, analyzing why a model made specific decisions, identifying bias or safety failures, and documenting evidence for regulatory review. With 49,000 roles, this is a smaller but highly specialized category.

The role matters most in regulated industries: healthcare, insurance, financial services, and government. In these sectors, AI decisions carry legal and compliance weight, so organizations need people who can explain AI behavior to regulators and courts, not just to engineers. Finance careers in particular are seeing growing demand for AI forensic and compliance skills as regulators scrutinize algorithmic decision-making.

Forward-Deployed Engineer: The Fastest-Growing New Role

Forward-deployed engineer (FDE) is a hybrid role sitting between the AI development team and the business teams that use AI products. Their job is ensuring AI systems actually get used effectively in the real world, not just built correctly in the lab. With 42x growth since 2023, this is the fastest-growing new-collar category by rate.

FDEs and forward-deployed PMs succeed by combining technical depth with communication and product judgment. They translate vague business needs into concrete AI requirements, and translate AI limitations into terms business stakeholders understand. The Final Round AI community has active discussions from people transitioning into these hybrid roles, including how to frame engineering experience for product-oriented positions.

How to Position Yourself for New Collar Jobs in 2025 and 2026

LinkedIn's chief business officer Mark Lobosco told reporters at Davos: "When you look at headlines and reports, there is more underneath. We are seeing AI as a net job creator." But the qualifying condition is skill readiness, companies are already struggling to fill these roles because most workers are not prepared for them.

The winning positioning across all five categories is the same: technical fluency plus human judgment. You do not need to be an AI researcher, but you do need to demonstrate that you understand how AI systems work, where they fail, and how human oversight adds value.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many jobs has AI created so far?

According to LinkedIn's 2026 Labour Report, AI created 1.3 million "new collar" jobs globally between 2023 and 2025. The largest category was data annotator with 774,000 new roles, followed by head of AI (298,000) and AI engineer (177,000).

What are new collar jobs?

New collar jobs are roles that blend technical skills with human judgment and hands-on work, without fitting traditional blue-collar or white-collar categories. They typically require AI literacy and specific technical skills but do not always require a four-year degree.

What is the highest-paying AI-created job?

Head of AI is typically the highest-paying new-collar role, with senior positions commanding $200,000–$400,000+ annually at major companies. AI Engineer roles at senior levels commonly pay $150,000–$250,000 depending on company and location.

Is AI a net job creator or job destroyer?

The data is mixed. LinkedIn's 2026 report shows AI created 1.3 million new roles. Simultaneously, global tech layoffs surpassed 240,000 in the same period. AI is both creating new categories and eliminating traditional roles, the net outcome depends heavily on whether workers develop the skills for new-collar positions.

How do I get a job in AI without a degree?

Data annotation roles are accessible without a degree and serve as an entry point into the AI industry. Building an AI literacy portfolio, demonstrated knowledge of how models work, where they fail, and how to evaluate outputs, opens doors to annotator, forensic analyst assistant, and AI support roles at entry level.
