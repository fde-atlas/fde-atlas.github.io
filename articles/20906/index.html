The Difference Between FDE and SES, SIer, SE, and Consulting | A Comparison Table Explaining Where FDE Engineers Stand

Summary

This Japanese article compares FDEs with seven similar roles—SES (client-site staffing), SIer/SE engineers, software engineers, solutions architects, IT consultants, and customer success—using official job postings from Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic, LayerX, and Sansan, plus a Gartner forecast. It argues FDEs are defined by three criteria: having a proprietary product, writing production code in customer environments, and feeding field learnings back into the product. It also lists five questions to spot “FDE in name only” jobs and notes Sierra and Decagon use FDE-like roles under different titles.

Key points

  • FDE differs from SES by owning a proprietary product that adapts to customer sites.
  • Unlike solutions architects, FDEs write production code in customer environments.
  • Palantir contrasts Devs (“one feature, many customers”) with FDEs (“many features, one customer”).
  • Gartner predicts under 20% of FDE engagements will convert recurring requests into core product features by 2028, warning of “FDE washing.”
  • Job seekers should verify the product, feedback loop, and evaluation metrics, not the job title.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who embeds themselves at the customer's site to implement and deploy their company's product. Because they work at the customer's location, they can look similar to SES engineers (on-site dispatch) or SIer engineers, and because they handle customer issues, they also overlap with IT consultants and solution architects.

This article organizes the differences between FDE engineers and 7 similar roles based on official information and interview articles from companies such as Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic, LayerX, and Sansan, as well as announcements from the research firm Gartner. It also explains how to spot "FDE in name only" positions where a job posting says "FDE" but the reality is different, as well as FDE-type roles that do not call themselves FDE.

Key Points of This Article

  • The decisive difference between FDE and SES (on-site dispatch) is the presence of an in-house product: FDEs adapt their company's product to the customer's site and feed the insights gained back into improving the product.
  • The difference between FDE and solution architects is the depth of implementation: while solution architects rarely write code on the customer's infrastructure, FDEs write production code in the customer's environment.
  • The difference between FDE and IT consultants is the way they engage: while consultants make one-off recommendations, FDEs implement solutions themselves and work with customers over the long term.
  • The difference between FDE and regular software engineers is the scope of focus: Palantir describes software engineers as providing "one feature to many customers" and FDEs as providing "many features to one customer."
  • On September 29, 2026, Gartner predicted that through 2028, fewer than 20% of FDE engagements will convert recurring customer requests into features of the vendor's core product, and pointed out the risk of "FDE-washing," i.e., selling consulting as FDE.

The Difference Between FDE and SES (On-Site Dispatch)

Both FDEs and SES engineers work at the customer's site. The difference is whether there is an in-house product. Suguru Shirai, EM (Engineering Manager) of the FDE group at LayerX, stated in an interview with Levtech LAB (March 2026) that the decisive difference from SES and technical consultants is "having a product," and explained that FDE is closer to a business model than a role.

Problems an FDE solves for one customer do not end as one-off responses; they are provided to the next customers as features of the product. This is why the FDE model works as a business. Conversely, when a company without an in-house product calls itself "FDE," its structure is no different from SES.

The Difference Between FDE and SIer / SE (Systems Engineer)

SIer engineers and SEs build systems individually based on customer requirements and deliver them. Sansan's engineering blog explains that the FDE's position differs from both "consultants whose role ends with the proposal" and "SIers who build from scratch based on requirements," describing FDEs as partners who leverage existing products, adapt them to the customer's specific operations and challenges, and stay involved until value is delivered.

  • Starting point: SIers start from the customer's requirements. FDEs start from both the in-house product and the customer's challenges.
  • How they build: SIers build individually. FDEs use the existing product as a foundation and implement the missing parts.
  • Boundaries: SIers conclude with delivery. FDEs stay involved through post-deployment use and adoption.

On the other hand, engineers who have experienced on-site work at customers or requirements definition at SIers are the people who have worked in ways closest to FDE. Experience developing in constrained environments and coordinating with customers is directly valued in FDE selection processes.

The Difference Between FDE and Software Engineers

Palantir explains the difference between its software engineers (Dev) and FDEs (Delta) as: Devs build "one feature for many customers," while Deltas build "many features for one customer" (as quoted by The Pragmatic Engineer). Product development engineers build features used by many customers within their own company's environment. FDEs, for the success of a single customer, combine products, various languages, and open-source tools to create solutions.

The technical hiring bar does not change. Palantir describes the scope of responsibility of an FDSE in its job postings as "similar to a startup CTO," and FDE is a role that writes code. JAPAN AI's job posting states that as a rough guide, an FDE's work consists of "about 20% customer dialogue and about 80% technical design and implementation."

The difference between FDEs and solution architects / sales engineers (key points)

According to a The Pragmatic Engineer article based on an interview with Colin Jarvis, who leads the FDE organization at OpenAI, OpenAI's FDEs are more hands-on than solution architects (SAs) and deal with more ambiguous situations. According to the same article, SAs play a role closer to advisory, rarely write code on customer infrastructure, and typically build MVPs and PoCs using anonymized or offline data. The division of labor is: solution architects and sales engineers make technical proposals before a deal is won, while FDEs build production systems in the customer's environment after the deal is won.

In OpenAI's and Databricks' Tokyo job postings, in addition to FDEs, there are roles responsible for technical proposals (OpenAI's Applied AI Architect and Databricks' Solutions Architect (Pre-sales)). However, OpenAI's Applied AI Architect is a role that serves as the technical point of contact for assigned customers, from pre-sales proposals through implementation and adoption. Anthropic's Tokyo job postings do not include a role called FDE; instead, they are split into a pre-sales Applied AI Architect and an Applied AI Engineer that spans both pre- and post-sales (as of October 16, 2026). A comparison of job postings for each role, the differences from customer engineers, Applied AI Engineers, and AI engineers, and what one needs to add when moving from each role to FDE will be covered in the next article.

The difference between FDEs and IT consultants

The Pragmatic Engineer notes that FDEs are sometimes mistaken for consultants, and explains that while consultants make one-off recommendations, FDEs work with customers over the long term and also contribute to the development of their own company's core products. Salesforce also says of its FDEs: "Implementation teams build the solution. FDEs make that solution deliver value."

In a September 29, 2026 announcement, research firm Gartner introduced its analysts' observation that the term "forward deployed" is being used as a label for implementation support, professional services, solution engineering, and AI consulting. Gartner further predicts that through 2028, fewer than 20% of FDE engagements will convert recurring customer requests into features of a vendor's core product, and warns of the growing risk of "FDE washing"—selling consulting services as FDEs. In other words, whether insights gained at customer sites are fed back into one's own product is the line that separates FDEs from consulting.

  • Deliverables: consultants produce recommendations and plans. FDEs produce systems that work in the customer's business.
  • Duration of involvement: consultants work for the contract period. FDEs stay from implementation through adoption.
  • Implementation: consultants generally do not implement. FDEs implement themselves.

IT consultants who can write code can be immediately effective in the problem discovery and consensus-building aspects of the FDE role. In the selection process, candidates are evaluated on whether they can see production code through to completion on their own.

The difference between FDEs and customer success

Customer success is a role that helps customers make use of existing features and establishes ongoing usage. As a rule, they do not write code. FDEs write production code on customer infrastructure and, if necessary, add features to their own company's product. Both share the aspect of conveying customer feedback to the product, but FDEs do not just convey it—they build it themselves.

Five questions to spot an "FDE in name only"

As the job title FDE has spread, jobs that are actually closer to SES or customer support have also started using the "FDE" name. Before applying and during interviews, check the following five points.

  • 01 What is the company's own product? Confirm whether there is a product that the FDE brings to customer sites.
  • 02 Through what channel are issues discovered by FDEs at customer sites reflected in the product? Without such a mechanism, the insights remain only at the customer site.
  • 03 What are the performance metrics for FDEs? Confirm whether they are evaluated based on customer outcomes, not hours worked or number of features.
  • 04 What percentage of working hours is spent on implementation? Confirm whether it is a role that writes code.
  • 05 What percentage of the work is on-site at customers, and how often is travel required? Palantir's job posting for a Tokyo-based FDSE explicitly states a travel rate of 25-75% (as of October 16, 2026).

The same applies in reverse: you cannot judge by job title alone. Sierra, an AI agent company, calls its FDE-style role of building agents together with customers "Software Engineer, Agent" (Agent Engineer), while its peer Decagon calls it "Agent Deployment Engineer." Both job postings list as job duties building production agents for customers themselves and applying issues discovered through work with customers to improve their own platform (as of October 16, 2026). Judged by the first and second questions above (whether there is an own product, and whether there is a channel for customer-site issues to flow back into the product), they have the same structure as FDEs.

How to become an FDE from a similar role

Since few people have direct FDE experience, companies hire candidates transitioning from similar roles. SIer and SES engineers can supplement their experience with LLM implementation, software engineers with experience speaking directly with customers, and IT consultants or pre-sales with experience seeing production code through to completion. This makes it easier to advance in FDE selection processes.

Summary

An FDE is an engineer who, as an employee of a company with its own product, works on-site with customers, writes production code in the customer's environment, and feeds the insights gained back into their company's product. FDEs differ from SES in whether the company has its own product, from solution architects in the depth of implementation, and from IT consultants in whether they build things themselves. When reviewing job listings, don't focus on the job title; instead, check three things: whether the company has its own product, the mechanism for feeding insights back into the product, and the evaluation metrics.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the difference between an FDE and SES?

It comes down to whether the company has its own product. FDEs adapt their company's product to customer sites and feed insights gained at customer locations back into product improvements. SES provides engineers to customer projects under quasi-delegation contracts, with compensation paid for hours worked.

Q: How is an FDE different from an SE or SIer engineer?

SIer engineers and SEs build systems individually based on customer requirements and deliver them. FDEs use an existing in-house product as a foundation, adapt it to the customer's specific business operations, and support them through post-deployment usage and adoption.

Q: What is the difference between an FDE and a consultant?

The difference lies in deliverables and whether implementation is performed. IT consultants produce recommendations and plans as deliverables and, in principle, do not implement them. FDEs use their company's product to build systems that work in the customer's business themselves, stay involved from deployment through adoption, and feed insights gained at customer sites back into their company's product. In September 2026, Gartner referred to selling consulting services as FDE as "FDE washing," and predicted that through 2028, fewer than 20% of FDE engagements will transform recurring customer requests into features of the company's core product.

Q: Do FDE engineers write code?

Yes. Anthropic's job posting for a London-based FDE (as of October 16, 2026) describes the FDE's job as building production applications using Claude within customers' systems. JAPAN AI's job posting states a rough breakdown of the work as "approximately 20% customer dialogue and approximately 80% technical design and implementation."

Q: Can you transition to an FDE from SES or SIer?

Yes. Experience with on-site work at customers, requirements definition, and development in constrained environments is valued in FDE selection processes. What needs to be supplemented is experience implementing applications that incorporate LLMs, and experience identifying problems and proposing solutions yourself rather than building from given requirements.
