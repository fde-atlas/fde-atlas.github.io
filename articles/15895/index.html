Differences from Adjacent Roles · OpenFDE

Original title: 与相邻岗位的区别

OpenFDE · 2026-10-16 · 2 min read

Summary

This article distinguishes FDEs from adjacent roles—Sales Engineers, Solutions Architects, backend/platform SWEs, and consultants—arguing the reliable test is the final deliverable: a running, measurable system in the customer's production environment, not documents or advice. It debunks two misconceptions (FDE as rebranded pre-sales or consulting), cites OpenAI, Google, and Palantir job descriptions, and proposes four essential elements of a genuine FDE plus a five-point checklist for evaluating job postings.

Key points

  • The key differentiator is the deliverable: a production system, not advice or demos.
  • Pre-sales supports the sales cycle; FDE work happens mainly after the deal closes.
  • Palantir separates FDSE from consultants, who rarely ship code back to core products.
  • Four essentials: customer site, real coding, production deployment, product feedback loop.
  • A five-item JD checklist covers coding requirements, embedding, adoption metrics, codified patterns, and tradeoffs.

The FDE role is most easily misread as "pre-sales with a new name" or "consulting with fancier packaging." Neither is accurate. The most reliable way to tell them apart isn't the title, but one thing: whether the final deliverable is a document or a recommendation, or a system that runs in the customer's production environment and can be measured.

A Sales Engineer supports the sales process, with the goal of closing deals; an FDE's goal is production deployment and measurable workflow impact. OpenAI↗ Google's phrasing is especially useful for drawing the distinction: an FDE is not an advisory role, but embeds in the customer's environment to code, debug, and ship together, solving productionization obstacles like integration, data readiness, and state management that block AI from reaching enterprise-grade maturity. The Two Most Common Misconceptions

Inaccurate. Pre-sales does demos and proposals during the sales phase and hands off after the contract is signed; an FDE's work mainly happens after the deal is signed—going on-site, writing code, running evals, deploying to production, and driving adoption. The deliverable changes from a slide deck to a system that actually runs.

Inaccurate. Consultants typically deliver one-off analyses or recommendations; an FDE's core is engineering implementation and product feedback. Palantir's official blog explicitly distinguishes Delta / FDSE from consultants: FDSEs do substantial engineering and technical work, and even contribute field code back to the core product. Dev vs Delta↗

The Four Elements of a True FDE

No matter how the JD is written or what the role is called, a true FDE must have all four of these at once—and that's what fundamentally distinguishes it from all adjacent roles.

Customer site + real coding + production deployment + a product feedback loop. Remove "customer site" and it's just an ordinary SWE; remove "real coding" and it degrades into pre-sales or an SA; remove "production deployment" and it's just demos; remove the "product feedback loop" and it becomes low-gross-margin custom services.

How to Tell Whether a Role Is a "True FDE"

When reading a JD, use this checklist to verify item by item. The more boxes it checks, the closer it is to a true FDE; if most fall through, it's probably just borrowing a trendy name.

Does the JD include hard requirements like "write and review production-grade frontend and backend code" or "full-stack build"? Or does it never mention coding at all? OpenAI, Palantir, and Scale all list strong coding as a baseline.

Does it mention embedded, on-site, travel (up to 50% for some OpenAI roles), or working side-by-side with customer engineers? Be wary of purely remote or purely HQ-based "solution roles."

Do the success metrics say "production adoption" and "measurable workflow impact," or "support sales" and "complete proposals"? Only the former is an FDE.

Does it mention "codify reusable patterns," "share field feedback with Research and Product," or "blueprints / product requests"? This is the dividing line between an FDE and an outsourced engineer.

Does it require delivering in a fast-moving, ambiguous environment and making tradeoffs among scope, speed, and quality? A true FDE doesn't just "build whatever the customer asks for."

OpenAI's and Google's FDE roles are high-quality references: both emphasize the full chain from customer problems to production systems, and then to reusable patterns and product feedback. OpenAI↗Google Careers↗

Stringing these five points together brings us back to the most condensed definition—on-site, code, launch, metrics, productization. To review this core from the beginning, see What Is an FDE.
