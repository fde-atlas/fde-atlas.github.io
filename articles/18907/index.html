What to expect for Legora's Forward Deployed Engineer interview

22 July 2026

Legora builds an AI-native workspace for legal teams, trusted by 1,000+ customers across 50+ countries including Cleary Gottlieb, Goodwin, Linklaters, White & Case, and Barclays. As a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) on the Customer Enablement team, based out of the Union Square office in New York, you sit at the intersection of product engineering and customer success. You embed directly with the most complex enterprise clients, write production-grade code to bridge Legora's platform with their existing legal tech stack, and leave behind connectors, automation, and tooling that outlast any single deployment.

This is not a pure coding role or a pure customer role. Legora is looking for a strong generalist engineer who can own the full technical scoping process end to end, debug hairy production issues under time pressure, and switch fluently between a technical conversation with an IT administrator and a strategic one with a managing partner. They want someone energised by early-stage, playbook-free problems who can move fast without cutting corners.

Quick Stats

* Typical process: 4 to 5 rounds, roughly 3 to 5 weeks

* Format: Recruiter phone screen, then video technical and behavioral rounds, with a likely onsite or final panel in NYC

* Core focus: Backend/generalist coding, enterprise integrations (SSO, REST, webhooks, DMS), customer-facing communication, scoping and ownership

* Difficulty: Hard. You are assessed as both an engineer and a customer-facing owner, and the enterprise legal tech domain (iManage, SharePoint, NetDocuments, SAML/OIDC) raises the bar

What Legora Looks For

* Strong generalist engineering: backend code, APIs, auth protocols, and data pipelines

* Experience in customer-facing technical roles (pre-sales, solutions engineering, or embedded delivery) where you owned outcomes, not just handed off specs

* Familiarity with enterprise integration patterns: SSO (SAML, OIDC), REST and webhook APIs, and document management systems like iManage, SharePoint, or NetDocuments

* Judgment on when to build something robust versus ship a good-enough unblock, plus comfort with ambiguity and clear communication across technical and executive audiences

What to Expect

A recruiter from the Customer Enablement or talent team walks through your background, motivation for the FDE role, and fit with Legora's culture (ownership over titles, outcomes over intentions, direct ego-free feedback). Expect questions about your customer-facing engineering experience, your comfort with an in-person NYC environment, availability, and compensation expectations against the posted $204K to $240K plus equity band. This is where you deliver a crisp pitch on why Legora and why this hybrid engineer-plus-customer role.

Example Questions

* "Walk me through your background and why a Forward Deployed Engineer role appeals to you."

* "What draws you to Legora and to working in legal tech specifically?"

* "Tell me about a time you owned a technical outcome for a customer end to end."

* "How do you feel about an in-person office culture and travel or embedding with enterprise clients?"

Tips

* Have a 60-second story that ties your engineering depth to real customer outcomes, not just shipped features.

* Show you understand the dual nature of the role: production code plus direct client relationships.

* Rehearse this quick pitch and the "why Legora" answer with Nora's Standard Mode, which mirrors the classic phone-screen mix so your motivation and background answers land tight.

A hands-on technical round with an engineer, focused on generalist backend skills rather than niche algorithms. Expect to write or reason through real code: consuming and building REST/webhook APIs, handling auth flows, parsing and moving data through pipelines, and designing a connector between Legora's platform and an external system. Because FDEs debug production issues under pressure, you may also get a live debugging or "here is a broken integration, fix it" exercise. The emphasis is practical: can you ship working solutions, not just discuss theory.

* "Design and sketch code for a connector that syncs documents between Legora and a system like iManage or SharePoint."

* "Walk me through how you would implement SSO for an enterprise client using SAML or OIDC."

* "Here is a webhook that intermittently fails to deliver. How do you diagnose and fix it?"

* "A customer needs an integration by end of week. How do you decide what to build robustly versus what to ship as a good-enough unblock?"

* Talk through tradeoffs out loud, especially the robust-versus-good-enough judgment the posting explicitly calls out.

* Be fluent in API auth, retries, idempotency, and error handling since these are the bread and butter of real integrations.

This round tests the customer-facing half of the job. Interviewers (often a Customer Success or Sales partner plus an engineering lead) probe how you scope requirements, communicate across technical and executive audiences, and handle friction. Expect STAR-style behavioral questions grounded in Legora's values (lean in, fight for excellence, grow together) and situational role-plays where you must translate a vague customer ask into a technical plan or de-escalate an unhappy stakeholder.

* "Describe a time you had to explain a technical constraint to a non-technical executive and a detailed fix to an IT admin in the same conversation."

* "Tell me about a deployment that went sideways. How did you surface the friction internally and resolve it?"

* "A managing partner is frustrated that an integration is behind schedule. Walk me through how you handle the meeting."

* "Give an example of when you pushed back on a customer request or a corner-cutting shortcut."

* Use STAR structure and pick stories that show ownership of outcomes, not just handoffs to another team.

* Show you can be the technical voice of the customer internally: name how you surfaced edge cases to product and engineering.

* Practice these with Nora's Behavioral Mode so your STAR stories on scoping, communication, and ambiguity feel natural and specific.

A likely final round at the Union Square office (or a structured virtual panel) that brings together engineering, product, and customer-facing leaders. This often includes a deeper system-design or architecture discussion for a full enterprise deployment, a cross-functional collaboration exercise, and values or culture-fit conversations. Expect the panel to pressure-test both your technical scoping instincts and your ability to operate in ambiguity without a playbook. Note: without published interview reports for this role, this final structure is our realistic estimate based on how Legora and similar-stage companies hire FDEs, so confirm the exact format with your recruiter.

* "Architect an end-to-end integration for a global law firm rolling out Legora across multiple offices with different DMS and identity providers."

* "How would you build reusable connectors and tooling that speed up future customer deployments?"

* "Tell me about a time you worked at the intersection of engineering, sales, and customer success to close or expand a technically complex deal."

* "What would your first 90 days as an embedded FDE with a major enterprise client look like?"

* Think in terms of reusable infrastructure that outlasts a single visit, since that is a core mandate of the role.

* Be ready to reason about scale: multi-region clients, varied auth providers, and messy legacy legal tech stacks.

* Mix Nora's Technical Mode (for the architecture and design portions) with Behavioral Mode (for the cross-functional and culture-fit portions) to simulate a full panel day.

Once you clear the panel, you move to the offer conversation. The posted range is $204K to $240K with equity, plus a strong benefits package (Aetna/Kaiser medical, MetLife dental, 401(k) match, unlimited PTO, One Medical, and daily lunch at the NYC office). This call covers base, equity, start date, and benefits. Given the role's blend of engineering depth and customer-facing ownership, there is room to advocate for your value based on relevant integration and solutions experience.

* "What are your compensation expectations for this role?"

* "How do you weigh base salary versus equity at a scaling company like Legora?"

* "Do you have competing offers or timelines we should know about?"

* "What would make this offer an easy yes for you?"

* Anchor to the posted $204K to $240K band and justify where you fall using your integration, backend, and customer-facing track record.

* Consider the full package (equity, 401(k) match, benefits) rather than negotiating base in isolation.

* Rehearse the back-and-forth with Nora's Salary Negotiation Mode so you can state your number confidently without underselling yourself.

1) How many rounds are there?

Expect around 4 to 5 rounds: a recruiter screen, a technical coding and integration round, a customer scenario and behavioral round, a final onsite or panel in NYC, and an offer conversation. Legora does not publish its exact FDE process, so treat this as a realistic estimate and confirm specifics with your recruiter.

2) What topics are most common?

* Generalist backend coding, REST and webhook APIs, auth protocols (SAML, OIDC), and data pipelines

* Enterprise integration patterns and document management systems (iManage, SharePoint, NetDocuments), plus customer scoping and cross-functional communication

3) How long does the process take?

Typically about 3 to 5 weeks from recruiter screen to offer, depending on scheduling and whether the final round is onsite in New York or virtual.

4) How should I prepare?

* Sharpen practical integration skills: build and debug REST/webhook flows, implement SSO, and reason about connector design against legal tech stacks.

* Prepare STAR stories that show end-to-end ownership, robust-versus-good-enough judgment, and communicating across IT admins and managing partners.

* Study Legora's product and customers so you can speak credibly about AI-native legal workflows and enterprise deployment challenges.

* Use Nora AI to rehearse: Standard Mode for the recruiter screen, Technical Mode for the coding and architecture rounds, Behavioral Mode for customer scenarios, and Salary Negotiation Mode for the offer stage.
