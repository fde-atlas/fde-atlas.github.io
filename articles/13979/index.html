FDEs with 80K Monthly Salaries: Why Are Big Tech Companies Racing to Send Engineers to Customer Sites?

Original title: 月薪 8 万的 FDE，大厂为什么抢着把工程师送进客户现场？

掘金 · 2026-08-12 · 7 min read

Summary

This Juejin article examines the rise of Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roles in China's AI job market. Major companies like ByteDance, Ant Digital and Zhipu are offering monthly salaries of 35,000–80,000 RMB for engineers who embed with clients to deliver AI projects. By early July, Liepin listed nearly 300 FDE positions with annual pay of 500k–1M RMB and over 40 above 1M. The author describes what FDEs actually do, which developers can transition, and warns that advertised salaries can be misleading and some roles tie value to billable hours. The piece argues FDE work only pays off long-term when field experience feeds back into reusable product capabilities.

Key points

  • ByteDance, Ant Digital and Zhipu offer FDE salaries of 35k–80k RMB monthly.
  • Liepin had nearly 300 FDE roles at 500k–1M RMB annual pay by early July.
  • Demand comes from model vendors, cloud providers, consultancies and startups serving SMEs.
  • Advertised salaries may include internships or channel ceilings; some roles bill by person-day.
  • Entry requires a real, measurable delivery; field experience should become reusable assets.

A batch of new job titles is emerging among AI positions on recruitment sites.

FDE, Agent engineer, AI solutions engineer, AI product delivery... The job titles vary, but the work is converging: engineers go into the customer's business site, connect models to data, systems, and workflows, and take responsibility for the results after launch.

Salaries quickly drew attention to the FDE role. Previously published job postings showed ByteDance's "Doubao AI Large Model FDE" offering a monthly salary of 35,000 to 70,000 yuan with 15 months' pay; a related position at Ant Digital Technologies offered 40,000 to 60,000 yuan with 15 months' pay; Zhipu recruited an FDE lead with a monthly salary range of 60,000 to 80,000 yuan.

These figures are concentrated at big tech companies and senior positions, but they send a clear signal: as model capabilities gradually become a standard commodity, companies are beginning to pay for the ability to "actually put AI to use."

What exactly does an FDE do

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, commonly translated in China as Forward Deployed Engineer. The role was first popularized by Palantir, whose engineers worked long-term inside intelligence agencies and large enterprises, modifying products within real data and workflows.

Large language models have made this way of working popular again.

When a company buys a model or activates an agent platform, it has only completed the technical preparation. At actual launch, engineers still need to identify business problems, confirm whether the data is usable, integrate legacy systems, set up permissions and human review, and test results with real tasks. If any step is not handled well, the project may stall at the demo stage.

The FDE fills exactly this gap. He writes code, but also participates in requirements judgment and project advancement. When a client says "build an AI customer service agent," the FDE needs to break it down further: which types of questions consume the most labor, where the answers come from, which operations can be automated, when a case must be handed to a human, and what metrics will be used for acceptance.

This also explains why companies are once again putting these engineers in front of customers. The requirements of AI projects often only become clear on site; when relayed repeatedly through sales, product, and delivery teams, the most critical business details are easily lost.

Positions have appeared in China, but the boundaries are not yet fixed

The headhunting team interviewed by Caixin began encountering the first batch of domestic FDE demands in the second half of 2025. By mid-2026, FDE, agent engineering, AI application deployment, and AI transformation delivery had become important categories in AI hiring.

Another review of recruitment platforms shows that as of early July, Liepin listed nearly 300 FDE positions with total annual salaries of 500,000 to 1 million yuan, and more than 40 positions with annual salaries above 1 million yuan. Million-yuan salaries mainly belong to leads and senior roles, while the market also has junior positions with monthly salaries of 20,000 to 30,000 yuan; FDE has begun to form a hierarchy of levels.

Current demand mainly comes from three types of companies. Model companies and cloud providers want to bring models to large clients; consulting, enterprise software, and IT service providers are upgrading their existing implementation capabilities into AI delivery; some startup teams serve small and medium-sized enterprises, handling everything from scenario analysis to system launch.

Job titles remain mixed. Many teams are hiring AI solutions engineers, agent application engineers, or industry delivery specialists, but the actual work is already FDE work.

The domestic scene also has several distinctive features. Large enterprises often run multiple old and new systems simultaneously, and finance, energy, healthcare, and manufacturing must also handle on-premises deployment, data permissions, auditing, and stable operation. The difficulties of many projects therefore lie in system compatibility, organizational coordination, and boundaries of responsibility; model performance accounts for only part of it.

When an electric vehicle company deployed AI customer service, the FDE team sat directly in the customer service seats, listening to real user calls. "Charging anomaly" and "vehicle repair" sound similar, but they connect to different knowledge bases and after-sales processes. It is not hard for a model to produce a fluent answer; the hard part is recognizing real intent, invoking the correct system, and escalating to a human promptly on high-risk issues.

This kind of work means domestic FDEs can hardly be model experts only; beyond engineering skills, they must also understand industry rules and the delivery site.

Which developers are best suited to transition to FDE

Backend, full-stack, data, and AI application engineers are the most direct group of candidates. They are already familiar with APIs, databases, and production systems; what they need to add is business judgment and client communication.

Developers who have worked on ERP, CRM, supply chain, payments, manufacturing, or healthcare IT also have an advantage. They know where the data lives, why legacy systems cannot be changed casually, and who is affected when something breaks. Model knowledge can be learned intensively; industry experience is hard to make up in a short time.

Solutions architects, implementation consultants, and technical product managers also have opportunities. They are familiar with requirements and project advancement; the key to transitioning is hands-on ability. If you can independently turn a solution into a working system, your industry understanding becomes real competitive strength.

This job also has clear personality requirements. FDEs frequently face vague requirements, on-site changes, and cross-team coordination; writing code is only part of the job. People who enjoy engaging with real business and are willing to take responsibility for outcomes usually adapt more easily. Those who want to focus long-term on fundamental research, or who need a stable pace and clear requirements, can continue to choose purer R&D positions.

Behind the high salaries, first understand what this job is really selling

The big numbers on job postings are easy to misjudge. A review of about 55 positions in Chengdu, Shanghai, Hangzhou, and Shenzhen found that the same company and the same type of role could show three different salary figures across the official website, headhunters, and internship channels. Some listings said 15-25K with 14 months' pay, but the details revealed internship work mainly involving "assisting, participating, and supporting." Salary ranges may mix in post-probation ceilings, quotes for senior candidates, or upper limits displayed by headhunter channels; until you receive a formal offer, none of these numbers count as real income.

Real high salaries also come at a clear cost. An FDE may need to follow up on sales opportunities, build demos, complete trial runs and on-site deployments, and then handle training, maintenance, and results reviews. Business trips, overtime, and production incidents may also come with the project. The salary compensates for the responsibility of moving from vague requirements to business results, and the uncertainty along the way.

A bigger pitfall comes from a mismatch between the role and the business model. Some JDs list sales conversion as a primary task; some execute strictly within contract scope for a long time, with engineers having no authority to adjust solutions or modify products; some projects bill by on-site headcount and person-days, so once the client signs off, the experience stays on site. Even if such positions carry the FDE title, personal value remains tied to billable hours, and they are easier to replace after the project ends.

In interviews, you can ask four questions directly: In the past six months, which product feature came from a problem an FDE discovered on site? When the contract description conflicts with the real problem, who has the authority to adjust the solution? Is the project billed by person-days, deliverables, or business results? After the project ends, which evals sets, connectors, workflow templates, or product capabilities get reused for the next client? Whether the high salary is worth taking, the answer lies in these details.

An FDE's first experience should start with real delivery

The entry path to FDE can come from an internship, an existing project, or a proactive delivery; the core is to build a system that people actually use and whose results can be measured.

Start by finding problems in an industry you know well. Former colleagues, small organizations without technical teams, or open-source projects you maintain may all provide the first scenario. The first deal can be done at a low price, but the delivery scope and acceptance metrics need to be agreed upfront. When a client says "build a knowledge base," first look at how materials are produced, how employees search them, and who bears the errors, then decide at which step AI should come in.

Then build the smallest complete pipeline. The project must use real data, the AI must enter the existing workflow, the system must be deployable and runnable, and there must be at least one user other than the developer. Logging, human takeover, and failure handling must also exist. A chat page running only on your own machine cannot prove that you have on-site delivery capability.

Before launch, record the original process's processing time, accuracy, or manual effort, then compare using the same set of metrics after launch. Failure cases should be categorized separately: whether the problem lies in the data, retrieval, model judgment, tool calls, or process design. The Forward Deployed Engineer's capabilities will show through in these failures, because what customers ultimately care about is whether the results meet requirements.

After a project is completed, organize the requirements interview questions, evals, connectors, and exception rules, then test them in a second similar scenario. Whether they can be reused reveals whether the delivery actually left behind a methodology, or just a block of billable hours.

A resume can be written along a clear chain: what the customer asked for, what bottleneck was discovered on-site, how long it took to get which part of the process running, and how the metrics changed. When the interviewer follows up, you should also be able to explain the alternative options, the rationale for trade-offs, and the issues that arose after launch.

Developers who are already proficient with Codex, Cursor, or Claude Code can also start from AI Coding adoption. Helping teams establish codebase rules, permission boundaries, eval methods, and usage workflows is likewise embedding AI tools into real work—only the customer site becomes the engineering team.

What a Forward Deployed Engineer Should Ultimately Leave Behind

Whether the FDE role can become a long-term business depends on whether frontline experience flows back into the product. If experience stays within a single project, the company can only take on more customers by adding more on-site staff; if experience enters the models, tools, and platforms, the next project can be done faster.

This is also the bet behind the high salaries. Enterprises hope an engineer can shorten the distance between models and business. Developers, in turn, need to turn field experience into reusable capabilities. Projects that disband as soon as they're done are not worth trading long-term growth for.
