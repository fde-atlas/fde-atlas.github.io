Today's Assessment

Today's three domestic signals together show: enterprise AI is beginning to formalize on-site delivery and production control as first-class objects.

On the jobs side — the FDE vertical job platform added 12 strongly matched postings in a single day, with employers expanding from AI-native companies to enterprise software, fintech, automotive software, and regional service providers, even including a "Vice President of Business Unit level" FDE program lead role; on the production side — MYbank connected its front-end agent Bailing 2.0 with eight back-end AI workbenches into a business chain, cutting a complex small/micro credit case from 3 days to 10 minutes; on the engineering side — openJiuwen compressed Agent self-improvement into a version loop of "candidate changes — retest against the same standard — adopt only if effective."

Job titles, agent entry points, and automated optimization are all just starting points; a truly operational FDE capability must simultaneously manage tasks, quality, permissions, cost, human accountability, and version evidence.

Key Signals

12 new FDE job postings in a single day: expanding from engineers to business unit leads

  • Source: Everyone Is an FDE Job Intelligence
  • The FDE vertical job aggregation platform marked 12 strongly matched postings on September 14, with named employers including China Electronic System Engineering, Elinzhe, Mobvoi, Yonyou Network, and others, covering directions such as the Feishu ecosystem, life sciences, automotive software, AI consulting, and project implementation. Most notable is Yonyou's "FDE Project Implementation Lead (Vice President of Business Unit level)" — some companies are no longer hiring individual contributors but building project management and business hierarchies around FDE. The salary range on the page is roughly 8k-80k.
  • Why it matters: demand is spreading from AI-native companies to enterprise software, fintech, ecosystem implementers, and regional service providers, and FDE roles are beginning to differentiate into five types: technical delivery, solutions, industry expert, program lead, and sales-oriented. But maintain professional skepticism about job data: aggregated leads ≠ active openings — complete job evidence has six layers: aggregated lead → confirmed active on the original page → full posting verified → actual hire → real client project → client sign-off and revenue. The 8k-80k salary range precisely shows that role definitions and levels have not yet converged, so it cannot be treated as an industry average salary.

MYbank Bailing 2.0 + eight AI workbenches: complex small/micro credit cut from 3 days to 10 minutes

  • Source: Securities Times | Original link
  • At the Bund Summit, MYbank unveiled Bailing 2.0, opening credit, bill, and tax/finance services to 42 million small and micro business operators; the back end built eight AI workbenches, bringing AI into core functions such as risk control, manual approval, marketing, and R&D. Public example: a restaurant operator's complex financing need was shortened from about 3 days to about 10 minutes; complex loan application processing capacity improved nearly 20x while asset quality remained stable. Key accountability design: Bailing handles follow-up questions, analysis, supplementary materials, and assisted processing, while key decisions such as credit limits are still made by authorized personnel/systems.
  • Why it matters: a front-end agent can get things done only because the back end has organized rules, historical cases, expert experience, data permissions, human review, and multiple workbenches — the FDE's object of work is never the chat box, but the entire business chain and its exception handling. The accountability boundary of "assisted processing but no key decisions" is also standard production design in regulated scenarios, far more honest than the "Agent auto-approves loans" narrative. Note: 42 million is the reachable scale, 3 days to 10 minutes is a public individual case, and 20x is the company's disclosed figure.

openJiuwen RSI: a "candidate — retest — adopt" version loop for Agent self-improvement

  • Source: InfoQ; openJiuwen official documentation
  • On September 14, openJiuwen released a dual-dimension RSI (self-improvement) framework: optimization targets cover the Harness (Prompt, Skill, Tool, Rail) and deliverables. The verification mechanism is key — first establish a baseline, locate problems from failed cases, and generate candidate versions; after the target cases are fixed, the full eval set must be replayed, and only changes that improve overall performance without breaking existing capabilities are adopted, with versions rollback-able. Evals show the SWE-bench Lite Dev pass rate rising from 61.0% to 87.0%.
  • Why it matters: enterprise Agents cannot go live on the basis of "we changed the prompt and it feels better." This mechanism provides the correct approach to automated optimization — six release gates for Agent self-improvement: real failures enter the set → freeze baseline/version → generate candidate changes → retest target cases → full regression and cost check → business owner approval, gradual rollout, rollback. Automated optimization only proposes candidates; the authority to adopt in production must remain with humans and auditable processes. This minimal loop also applies to manual Skill/knowledge base updates: every change is bound to a version, evals, and rollback. Note: the eval sample is small, and coding task results cannot be directly extrapolated to business scenarios.

Method comparison: Anthropic and Accenture simultaneously released seven guidelines on "from pilot to production"; their research states that only 23% of surveyed executives report sustained enterprise-wide AI impact, and 42% of organizations assign responsibility jointly to IT and finance with no single owner for cost/outcomes. The guidelines require defining users, tasks, outputs, quality thresholds, and TCO before piloting, and adopting four levels of oversight by risk — "automatic, sampled, item-by-item review, suggestion only" (original).

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Reverse-engineer a capability curriculum from job JDs: extract seven capability categories from current FDE hiring — scenario understanding, knowledge base/workflow, data and integration, eval debugging, training operations, industry knowledge, and project management — and fill gaps against your own or your team's weaknesses. Role differentiation means you don't need to be all-capable, but you should know which slot you occupy.
  • Draw a "responsibility map" for clients before discussing agents: learn from MYbank — front-end intent recognition, data retrieval, rule computation, manual approval, client confirmation, and post-loan monitoring, each annotated with the permissions and sign-off responsibilities of the Agent, system, employee, and client. Any step whose responsibility cannot be mapped is a step that should not be automated.
  • Build a versioned regression set for every project: failures, edge cases, and manual corrections that have already occurred all enter the sample set; a new Skill or knowledge update is released only after passing target retests and full regression. "It feels better after the change" is not a release criterion; passing regression is.
  • Remember the 23% figure: fewer than a quarter of enterprises have achieved sustained enterprise-wide AI impact — pilots are inherently deceptive, and the gap from pilot to production is precisely the reason FDE exists, as well as the most powerful industry baseline when articulating value to clients.
