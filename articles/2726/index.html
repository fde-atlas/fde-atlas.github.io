As AI and data platforms grow more complex, one role keeps showing up in every hiring pipeline: the Forward Deployed Engineer.

Companies like OpenAI and Palantir are scaling these teams fast. Job postings are exploding, with some reports citing an 800% spike in FDE job listings in 2025. Top-tier VC firms and tech media have dubbed it the "hottest job in startups".

The FDE sits between the company that builds the product and the customer who has to make it work.

What is a forward deployed engineer?

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), or Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE), is an elite, hybrid technical role. These are your best engineers, the ones you embed directly with your biggest customers to solve their hardest, highest-stakes problems.

They operate with the autonomy of a founder but the technical rigor of a staff engineer. "Deployed" from HQ to the customer's "forward" position: their offices, their data centers, their messy reality. Empowered to build, design, and ship production code to make that customer win.

Below is a breakdown of current salary data, core responsibilities, and the "decomposition" interview format that firms like Palantir and OpenAI use.

What is a forward deployed engineer? The definition and meaning

The forward deployed engineer definition is simple. It's built on two concepts:

  • "Forward Deployed": This is a military term for being on the front lines. For an FDE, it means working inside the customer's world, with their messy data and security rules.
  • "Engineer": An FDE is not a sales rep, a consultant, or a support agent. They write, debug, and ship production-grade code, build data pipelines, and deploy systems.

The meaning of the role in practice is to be the human bridge between a powerful, generic platform (like Palantir's Foundry or OpenAI's GPT models) and a customer's unique, specific problem. They make the technology work for that client.

What does a forward deployed engineer do? (Role and responsibilities)

The forward deployed engineer role has one central mission: own the customer's technical success. Whatever it takes.

A look at a forward deployed engineer job description

Look at any OpenAI forward deployed engineer job description, such as OpenAI's San Francisco FDE posting. You'll see these role responsibilities:

  • Embed deeply with customers. Lead complex, high-stakes deployments.
  • Map customer problems, structure the solution, and ship it. Fast.
  • Design, build, and deploy full-stack systems and custom data pipelines that create real value.
  • Act as the primary technical owner. Build trust. Guide the customer's own teams.
  • Find reusable patterns. Bring field insights back to your core product team.
  • Troubleshoot the most complex production outages. Be the last line of defense.

The day-to-day: An FDE's four-stage loop

Each deployment runs the same four stages, and feedback improves the product for the next customer.

An FDE's work is a four-stage loop:

  • Scoping: You get a vague problem, like "We need to cut fraud," find the real problem, and define a concrete technical plan.
  • Prototyping: You build a fast proof of concept (PoC) to show the customer what's possible and get feedback.
  • Deployment: You harden the PoC to be production-grade and secure, navigate the client's infrastructure, and get it live.
  • Feedback: You take what customers need back to the core product and research teams, so the product gets better.

Forward deployed engineer vs. software engineer vs. solutions architect

Don't confuse the FDE with other roles. The difference is about shipping and ownership.

Role Primary goal Key activity Customer interaction
Forward Deployed Engineer Customer success via custom, production-grade solutions. Building and deploying Deeply embedded, long-term partner.
Software Engineer (core) Build and maintain the core, scalable product for all users. Coding features Minimal; filtered through product managers.
Solutions Architect / Sales Engineer Get the technical "win" during the sales process. Designing and demoing Pre-sales; high-level and strategic.
AI Engineer Build AI features and agents into the company's own product. Prompting, evals, and model integration Usually internal; works with product teams.

The Sales Engineer sells the dream. The core Software Engineer builds the toolbox. The Forward Deployed Engineer uses that toolbox to build the custom, finished solution for the client. An AI engineer and an AI FDE share most of their skills, but the AI engineer ships into their own company's product, while the FDE ships into a customer's systems, under the customer's security rules.

Rough positions, to show how each role splits its time. Only the FDE owns production code and spends most of its time with customers.

Which companies hire forward deployed engineers?

This role is hottest at companies selling complex, high-value platforms, where it works as a go-to-market strategy for products that need deep technical integration to win.

The pioneer: Palantir

The Palantir Forward Deployed Software Engineer (or FDSE) is the original. Palantir built its business on this model, embedding engineers with government and commercial clients to solve large data problems. The Palantir forward deployed engineer role is known for its autonomy and high stakes.

The new wave: The AI and data giants

The AI boom created a new wave of demand, because a raw model API doesn't make a bank or a hospital productive on its own. Three of the biggest private AI companies all hire FDEs for that reason:

  • OpenAI: An OpenAI Forward Deployed Engineer leads end-to-end deployments of frontier models with strategic customers, and what they learn from evals feeds back into the roadmap.
  • Anthropic: Its FDEs work with customers to decide what gets built, then ship production AI applications on Claude.
  • Databricks: Its AI FDEs deliver professional services engagements and own production rollouts of customers' generative AI apps.

What is an AI forward deployed engineer?

An AI forward deployed engineer is an FDE whose job is getting AI models and agents into production at a customer. Andrew Ng described the role as an engineer embedded with a client to customize solutions, such as building and tuning agentic workflows for that client's needs.

In practice, the work is less about the model and more about everything around it. An AI FDE connects the model to the customer's data, builds the evals that prove an agent behaves, wires in authentication and logging, and decides where the model runs. Regulated customers often can't send data to a hosted API, so self-hosting open-weight models inside their own network is a common part of the job. Most AI FDEs also lean heavily on coding agents to move fast at customer sites; our Claude Code vs Codex comparison covers the two most common ones.

Much of an FDE's day also goes to customer meetings, so a background agent can keep work moving while you're in them. With Buildful, you hand off a scoped change, like a connector or a failing test, from Slack or Linear. It works in a cloud sandbox, runs the repo's checks, and opens a pull request on GitHub for you to review afterward. Check the customer's security rules first, because many won't allow a cloud agent near their code.

Where the jobs are

The demand is concentrated at AI labs, cloud providers, and companies selling into fintech, healthcare, and government, where FDEs navigate complex compliance requirements alongside technical integration. Expect travel, because the job is named for the time you spend at customer sites.

The high pay reflects how much pressure, impact, and travel the job involves.

How to become a forward deployed engineer

Getting a forward deployed engineer job is hard, because the interviews test technical skill, problem-solving, and grit at the same time.

What background do you need?

Companies look for people who have already acted like an FDE, even without the title. The best backgrounds are:

  • Early-stage startup engineer: If you were one of the first 10 engineers at a startup, you've talked to customers, worn all the hats, and shipped code to save the company.
  • Hands-on solutions architect: You build the PoC yourself and write custom scripts.
  • Data engineer / ML engineer: You've built and deployed data pipelines or productionized models.
  • Full-stack engineer with product sense: You talk to the product manager and question why something is being built.

The must-have skills: A "T-shaped" profile

FDE hiring looks for "T-shaped" people. Deep expertise in one area, broad skills in many others.

Deep technical bar (the vertical "I")

  • Coding: You need to be fluent in Python, and familiarity with Java, Go, or TypeScript helps for full-stack work.
  • Data: You need to understand data processing (Spark), data pipelines (Airflow), and database trade-offs (SQL vs. NoSQL, OLAP vs. OLTP).
  • Systems (DevOps/MLOps): You're deploying production code, so you need AWS, GCP, or Azure, plus Docker and Kubernetes. Experience with Terraform or other IaC tools is a plus.
  • AI / ML (for AI FDEs): AI FDEs now build agentic workflows, where AI systems execute multi-step tasks like filing claims or running QA suites.
    • Core concepts: RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), fine-tuning, vector databases, and agentic orchestration (LangGraph, CrewAI).
    • Evaluation frameworks (evals): You need to build and run evals that prove an agent won't go rogue in production.
    • AI observability and guardrails: Tools like LangSmith, Braintrust, or HoneyHive let you trace, monitor, and constrain agents, which is how you win over the customer's security team.
    • Frameworks: Hands-on experience with PyTorch or TensorFlow and HuggingFace.

Broad execution skills (the horizontal bar)

This is the harder half of the job.

  • Customer fluency and empathy: You can explain a complex system to a non-technical executive and translate their business problem into technical specs.
  • Grit and radical ownership: When a deployment fails at 2 AM, you don't file a ticket or blame another team. You own the problem from end to end.
  • Problem decomposition: You can take a massive, vague problem like "Our supply chain is broken" and break it into a clear, shippable plan.
  • Product sense: When you build the same custom script for three customers, you spot the pattern and tell the product team to build it into the platform.

The interview process: How to win

The process is multi-stage, and each round tests a different part of your "T".

  • Behavioral / fit interview: This tests the horizontal bar, with questions like "Tell me about a time you handled a demanding stakeholder" or "Describe a complex project you owned from 0 to 1."
    • How to win: Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result), and tell stories about you shipping code and solving a problem.
  • Technical deep dive(s): This tests the vertical bar.
    • Coding: Expect a practical task, such as "Here's a messy 1GB JSON file, parse it, clean it, and expose an API to query it."
    • System design: This will be data-heavy. For example, "Architect a RAG system for a company's internal wikis."
  • The case study / decomposition interview: This is the hardest round, and it's the famous Palantir forward deployed engineer interview, a format other FDE employers have picked up. You get a massive, ambiguous, real-world problem on a whiteboard.
    • Classic example: "A major city wants to use our platform to reduce 911 emergency response times. They have 911 call data, traffic data, and ambulance GPS data. You have 60 minutes. Go."
    • 2026 agentic example: "A global logistics firm wants an AI agent to handle automated rerouting for delayed shipments. They have SAP data, real-time weather APIs, and 500 different warehouse managers. How do you build the eval suite to ensure the agent doesn't overspend on shipping while maintaining a 99% delivery rate?"

How to prep and pass the "decomposition" interview

The interviewer cares less about the "right answer" than about how you think.

  • Do not jump to a solution. Your first instinct will be "Build an AI to predict traffic!" Don't.
  • Ask questions (clarify and scope): What's the actual goal? Who is the user: the 911 operator, the ambulance driver, or the public? What does the data look like, and what are the budget and timeline?
  • Decompose the problem: Break it into small, solvable chunks. Here, that might be ingestion (getting the messy data in one place), visibility (a real-time map for operators), and optimization (a model that suggests the best ambulance).
  • Propose an MVP (version 1): Suggest the simplest thing that delivers value. "Forget the AI model for now. V1 is a data pipeline and a real-time dashboard. Just seeing all the ambulances on a map would be a huge win."
  • Iterate and discuss trade-offs: The interviewer will push you on messy data and scale. Talk through your choices, like "I'd use Kafka for real-time ingestion, but a simple polling API might be better for the MVP."

A common mistake is showing off a specific tool before you understand the user's latency requirements. Be the engineer who cares more about the 911 operator's workflow than the database schema, and think out loud as you go.

Is forward deployed engineering a good job? The honest downsides

It's a great job for a specific kind of engineer, and a draining one for everyone else. The pay and the visibility are real, but so are the trade-offs, and it's worth knowing them before you apply.

  • Travel and context switching. You live in the customer's world, which can mean weeks on site and several customers at once. As a March 2026 Wall Street Journal headline put it, the hottest job in tech "isn't very glamorous."
  • Pressure on short timelines. You are measured on whether the customer gets results, often within a few months, and you don't control their data, their systems, or their politics.
  • Some listings use the title loosely. In a Reddit thread titled "Forward Deployed Engineer" roles seem like a scam, engineers argue that some of these jobs are closer to technical sales than engineering. Read the job description: if it doesn't mention shipping production code, it isn't the job this guide describes.
  • Less depth on one codebase. Core engineers spend years making one system excellent. FDEs trade that for breadth, which suits some careers and stalls others.

If you like customers, enjoy ambiguity, and want to see your code change a business within weeks, it's one of the best-paid ways to do that. If you want deep, quiet focus on one product, a core engineering role will make you happier.

The future of the FDE role

The integration wall

Most AI projects stall not because the model is bad, but because it can't reach the customer's legacy databases, pass their SSO and security review, or meet their data residency rules. Getting past that wall takes someone on site, with production access, who can ship, and that is where FDEs earn their pay.

Why FDEs are the new revenue multiplier

A platform that sits on a shelf earns nothing, and as models and platforms start to look alike, the ability to deploy them is what customers pay for. That makes the AI Forward Deployed Engineer one of the main ways AI companies turn a pilot into revenue.

FAQs

What does "forward deployed engineer" mean?

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a hybrid technical role where engineers embed directly with customers to solve their hardest problems. They write production code, build custom solutions, and own the customer's technical success end to end. They're embedded at the customer's site, working on the customer's problems, with the customer's data.

As of October 2026, the median forward deployed engineer in the US earns $220,000 in total compensation, according to 162 reports on Levels.fyi. What's the difference between a forward deployed engineer and a software engineer?

A core software engineer builds the product for all users. A Forward Deployed Engineer takes that product and builds custom, production-grade solutions for specific customers. The FDE is deeply embedded with the client, owns the relationship, and ships code to solve that customer's unique problems. Core engineers rarely interact with customers directly.

Who hires forward deployed engineers in 2026?

Palantir pioneered the role. Today, AI companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, Databricks, Scale AI), cloud providers (AWS, Microsoft, Google), fintech companies (Ramp), AI-native QA platforms (Bug0), and large enterprises like Citigroup, UnitedHealth Group, and Ford all hire FDEs. Job listings for the role spiked 800% in 2025, and in 2026 AWS put $1 billion into a new FDE unit, while Microsoft committed $2.5 billion to an implementation unit that includes FDEs.

How do I prepare for a forward deployed engineer interview?

FDE interviews typically include three stages: behavioral/fit interviews testing communication and ownership, technical deep dives testing coding and system design, and the famous "decomposition" case study. For the case study, don't jump to solutions. Ask clarifying questions, break the problem into solvable chunks, propose a simple MVP, then iterate. Think out loud and show structured, first-principles reasoning.

What skills do forward deployed engineers need in 2026?

FDEs need a "T-shaped" profile. Deep technical skills in coding (Python, TypeScript), data (SQL, Spark), and systems (AWS/GCP, Docker, Kubernetes). Plus broad execution skills: customer empathy, radical ownership, problem decomposition, and product sense. For AI FDEs in 2026, the bar has shifted to agentic orchestration (LangGraph, CrewAI), evaluation frameworks, and AI observability/guardrails, on top of RAG and fine-tuning fundamentals.

Is the forward deployed engineer role the same as a solutions architect?

No. A solutions architect designs solutions and demos them during the sales process. A Forward Deployed Engineer builds and deploys production-grade solutions after the deal is signed. The SA sells the dream. The FDE makes it real. FDEs write code, deploy systems, and own the customer's technical success long-term.

Is a forward deployed engineer a good job?

For engineers who like customers and ambiguity, yes: it's one of the best-paid roles in tech, and you see the impact of your code within weeks. The trade-offs are travel, pressure on short timelines, and less depth on a single codebase. Some listings also use the title for what is really a sales role, so check that the job involves shipping production code.

How much do forward deployed engineers make at OpenAI and Palantir?

At Palantir, Levels.fyi tracks 229 FDE packages ranging from $215,000 to $590,000 in total compensation, as of October 2026. How does Bug0 use forward deployed engineers?

Bug0 applies the FDE model to quality assurance through Bug0 Managed, a done-for-you QA service. Each team gets a dedicated forward-deployed engineer who builds its tests on Bug0's AI platform, covers critical flows in about a week, and verifies every failure before it reaches production. It shows how the FDE model is expanding beyond data platforms into narrower areas like testing.
