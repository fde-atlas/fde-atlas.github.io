Forward Deployed Software Engineer (Commercial) Interview Experience

Palantir·Submitted 9 days ago

You can’t treat coding and communication as separate skills here. Even after my code was right, they asked why I chose that approach and how I’d explain the trade-off to someone non-technical.

Interview process

The whole thing took about four weeks and went recruiter screen, live technical screen, three one-hour virtual onsite rounds, then a one-hour hiring-manager round. It started as a pretty contained coding process, but each step got less defined and more about whether I could take an ambiguous customer problem, understand an unfamiliar system, and own the real-world consequences of a technical decision. The main curveball was that behavioral evaluation was embedded inside the technical rounds the entire time. I could not just get the code right and move on because they kept asking why I chose that approach, what outcome it enabled, and how I would explain it simply. The re-engineering round was the one that stuck with me most because finding the duplicate-alert bug was only half of it, and then I had to talk through blast radius, cleanup, and customer impact.

Recruiter screen

Overview

It was a 30-minute recruiter screen, but it felt more probing than a normal one because they kept pushing me past generic motivation answers and wanted specific ownership and customer-facing examples.

Question types asked

Specific questions asked

Why do you want to work at Palantir?

Why Palantir rather than a conventional software-engineering company?

Why Forward Deployed Software Engineering specifically?

Why are you interested in the commercial side?

I walked through my background and tied it to wanting to solve real-world operational problems, not just write code in isolation. When I gave a generic answer at first, they pushed me to say what kind of operational problem I actually wanted to work on and why Palantir's model fit that better than a conventional SWE job.

Tell me about a time you worked directly with a user or stakeholder whose requirements were unclear.

I gave an example of working directly with a user or stakeholder with unclear requirements, and the recruiter kept pressing on what I specifically did versus what the team did. The theme of that round was that they did not want passenger-seat stories.

How comfortable are you spending time with customers and potentially traveling?

I treated that as a real part of the job, not a throwaway recruiter question. The whole screen made it clear they cared whether I was actually comfortable being customer-facing and operating in messy situations.
