Million-yuan salaries, hiring demand up 42x! What exactly is the FDE, "the hottest job in AI"?

Over the past two years, a role that didn't exist five years ago has seen its job count surge 42-fold. ByteDance is offering up to 1.05 million yuan a year, and OpenAI is offering $280,000 plus equity. It's not an AI algorithm engineer, nor a large model trainer—it's the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE).

I. A set of data that makes you sit up

First, a few numbers.

According to LinkedIn's "Global Labor Market Trends Insights Report" released in January 2026, over the past two years companies added at least 1.3 million AI-related jobs, covering data annotators, AI engineers, and Forward Deployed Engineers. These roles didn't exist five years ago, yet today they have become an indispensable part of the digital economy.

The most striking among them is the growth rate of the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). From 2023 to 2025, the number of new FDE positions grew 42-fold. By comparison, AI engineer positions grew 13-fold.

Data from the online recruiting platform Indeed shows that in April 2026, FDE job postings surged 729% year-over-year, reaching 5,330. Anthropic, OpenAI, Palantir, Stripe, and Google Cloud are all hiring for this type of engineer.

On compensation: ByteDance's "Doubao AI Large Model FDE" role pays a monthly salary of 35,000 to 70,000 yuan, with 15 months of pay per year—up to 1.05 million yuan annually. Ant Digital Technologies hires B2B FDEs at 40,000 to 60,000 yuan per month, with 15 months of pay per year. Zhipu Huazhang's FDE lead role pays 60,000 to 80,000 yuan per month.

In the US, OpenAI's official FDE role pays $162,000 to $280,000 a year (about 1.1 to 1.9 million yuan) plus equity incentives. Anthropic offers $200,000 to $300,000 a year (about 1.36 to 2.03 million yuan). Some headhunters have even offered FDEs with two years of experience $400,000 a year (about 2.71 million yuan) plus fully remote work.

II. What exactly is an FDE?

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, translated into Chinese as "Forward Deployed Engineer".

But don't be fooled by the word "deployed"—it has almost nothing to do with the traditional sense of "writing pages".

The "forward" in the name refers to "the very front line of the customer site", not "frontend development" in the tech stack. The FDE's core responsibility is: bridging AI products and customer business scenarios—mastering both large model technical principles and full-stack development, while possessing product thinking and on-site implementation skills, deeply involved in customer needs analysis, end-to-end deployment, and customized integration.

In plain language: truly landing AI products in customers' actual businesses, turning AI from "it runs" into "it's actually used".

The concept was pioneered by Palantir. Palantir is a big data company known for serving governments and large enterprises, and its FDE model is: send engineers to be stationed directly at customer sites, understand the customer's business pain points, then "translate" the company's technical capabilities into solutions the customer can use.

In the AI era, this model has been fully replicated in the field of large model deployment.

III. A real FDE work scenario

Lawted (a pseudonym), based in Shenzhen, was formerly a programmer at a major tech company. Not long ago, a logistics company approached him, hoping to use AI to optimize its business processes.

He went to investigate and discovered a shocking fact: the company has forty to fifty employees, and twenty to thirty of them are all doing the same thing—when a customer sends a PDF, they manually extract tracking numbers and shipping addresses, then enter them into Excel.

Lawted used AI coding tools to build a demo. A PDF that previously took a person several minutes to process was parsed by AI in seconds. The logistics company signed a letter of intent with him on the spot.

Today, Lawted's day goes like this: in the morning he visits various companies to research whether their business flows are suitable for AI transformation; in the afternoon he's on-site observing, seizing the moment when staff take a sip of water to ask questions.

This is the FDE's daily life—not writing code, but "solving problems".

IV. Why did FDEs suddenly become hot?

Behind the FDE explosion lie three deep-rooted reasons.

First, AI's capabilities are now "good enough," but "deployment" is still too hard.

In 2026, large models are already powerful enough. GPT, Claude, and ERNIE Bot can all write decent code, process complex documents, and answer professional questions. But the problem is—it's easy for these capabilities to stay at the "demo stage"; it's hard to truly embed them into a company's business processes.

A bank wants AI to automatically process loan approvals, a hospital wants AI to assist with medical record writing, a law firm wants AI to review contracts—every scenario is different, and every customer's data formats, business processes, and compliance requirements differ. Turning general-purpose AI into specialized solutions is the FDE's core value.

Second, the "last mile" problem of the AI era is worse than imagined.

Large model vendors are good at building models, not at delivery. OpenAI, Anthropic, and other companies have the world's strongest models, but deploying models into customers' private environments, integrating them with customers' business systems, and handling customers' data formats—this "dirty work" needs to be done by FDEs.

Yasha (a pseudonym) has eight years of product manager experience and later transitioned into software development, becoming an FDE two years ago. She told the media that two years ago the role was still "extremely niche"—"at the time, on LinkedIn, only the company I now work for and one other company were hiring." Now she frequently receives messages from headhunters.

Third, explosive demand for talent that "understands both technology and business."

Traditional software engineers understand technology but not business; traditional product managers understand business but not technology. The FDE requires both—able to discuss business pain points with a customer's CEO while also writing code for customized development.

One practitioner described the FDE's working state: "In the morning I visit various companies to research whether their business flows are suitable for AI transformation; in the afternoon I'm on-site observing, seizing the moment when staff take a sip of water to ask questions." This working mode of "both writing code and visiting customers" is completely unimaginable for traditional engineers.

V. How is an FDE different from traditional frontend development?

Many frontend developers, seeing the name "Forward Deployed Engineer," might think this is their new career path.

But it's important to clearly recognize: the FDE's "forward" means "front line," not "frontend development".

The core differences between the two:

DimensionTraditional frontend developmentFDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)
Core responsibilityWrite pages, adjust styles, connect APIsLand AI products in customer businesses
Work settingWrite code in the officeStationed at customer sites, research businesses, custom development
Skill requirementsFrontend tech stack (React/Vue, etc.)Full-stack development + product thinking + customer communication
DeliverablesCode and pagesWorking AI solutions
Career pathFrontend engineer → Frontend architectFDE → Solutions Architect → Independent consultant

The FDE is not an "upgraded version" of traditional frontend development, but an entirely new professional species.

VI. What does the FDE mean for frontend developers?

The rise of the FDE sends at least three signals to the front-end developer community.

Signal one: front-end skills are being monetized "across boundaries."

Traditional front-end development skills—HTML/CSS/JavaScript, React/Vue, build tools—are of course important, but in the AI era they are becoming "infrastructure" rather than "core competitiveness." What's truly valuable is the ability to use these skills to solve real business problems.

The core skills of an FDE include: full-stack development ability, calling and integrating large model APIs, analyzing and breaking down customer needs, and end-to-end deployment capability. Many senior front-end developers already possess most of these—it's just that no one had packaged this combination into a "job title" before.

Signal two: "understanding the business" is becoming scarcer than "understanding the technology."

In the front-end world of 2026, everyone is learning AI and new frameworks. But what's truly scarce is people who can understand both the boundaries of technology and the pain points of the business.

The way FDEs work illustrates exactly this: it's not about sitting at your desk writing code, but going to the customer's site to discover and solve problems. This ability to "come from the business and go back into the business" is something AI cannot yet replace.

Signal three: the "role upgrade" of front-end developers is accelerating.

Three years ago, a front-end developer's value was "writing good pages." Today, a front-end developer's value is "using technology to solve business problems." The emergence of the FDE role is the extreme expression of this role upgrade—it puts "solving problems," not "writing code," at the core.

7. Is the FDE a passing fad or a long-term trend?

Judging by current trends, the FDE is not a short-term windfall but a structural demand of the AI implementation era.

The reason is simple: the capabilities of large models keep improving, but "converting capability into value" will never be automated. As long as AI still needs to be embedded into specific business processes, someone is needed to do the "translation"—translating the customer's language into technical language, and translating technical language into solutions the customer can use.

Gartner predicts that by 2026, 75% of new application development will use AI-generated code. Once AI-generated code becomes the norm, "writing code" itself will become less and less valuable, while "deciding what code to write," "judging whether the code is correct," and "deploying the code to the customer and getting it running" will become more and more valuable.

The FDE is precisely the product of this trend.

A practitioner's words may best illustrate the point: "Fresh graduates can't do this job; it generally requires several years of experience as a product manager or developer." The FDE is not a role for newcomers, but a new path for experienced developers.

Final thoughts

Over the past two years, the number of FDE positions has surged 42-fold. ByteDance, Ant Group, Zhipu, OpenAI, and Anthropic are all competing for talent. A million-yuan annual salary is not a legend—it's a real market rate.

But the FDE is not a simple upgrade of "front-end development"; it's an entirely new professional species. It demands not just the ability to write code, but the comprehensive ability to understand the business, discover problems, and deliver end to end.

For front-end developers, the rise of the FDE sends a clear signal: in the AI era, the real moat is not how many frameworks you know, but how many real problems you can solve with technology.

If you're still agonizing today over "learning React or learning Vue," try looking at it from a different angle—if a customer stood in front of you tomorrow, could you use your technology to help them make a business process 10 times more efficient?

This question matters more than any framework choice.
