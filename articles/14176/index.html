Module 1: What FDE interviews assess

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 2 min read

Summary

This lesson from an FDE interview prep course catalogs the traits hiring leaders have stated publicly, covering AI companies (OpenAI, Baseten, Cognition, Sierra) and enterprise/fintech firms (Palantir, Salesforce, Ramp). It teaches readers to weigh each statement by four checks: whether the speaker is current, whether it is the company's own channel, whether it concerns FDE hiring specifically, and when it was said. Ramp's engineering blog is the strongest source, naming four axes: drive and work ethic, engineering fundamentals, customer empathy, and communication. Palantir accounts come from a former recruiter and a former FDE, describing past practice. Sierra's 2026 post covers general engineering interviews. No company publishes a scoring rubric or pass rates.

Key points

  • Ramp's blog names four evaluation axes for FDE candidates, the closest to published criteria.
  • Company statements on their own FDE hiring can be quoted back to interviewers with attribution.
  • Former Palantir recruiter Tiffany Siu said early FDEs passed the same loops as software engineers.
  • Nabeel Qureshi described Palantir's technical interview as reasoning about data, for one FDE type.
  • No leader gives scoring rubrics, pass rates, or official red-flag lists.

Full text

The evidence map sorted what employers publish, what candidates report and what nobody knows; this lesson reads the fourth source it named, the leaders who hire FDEs, who talk about people rather than rounds. The bridge story used their remarks on motivation; this lesson takes the ones on what they hire for.

By the end you will be able to name the traits leaders have put on the record, company by company, weigh who said each, and pick the round and the story where you will show it. They come from AI companies (OpenAI, Baseten, Cognition and Sierra) and from enterprise and fintech ones (Palantir, Salesforce and Ramp).

Who is speaking, and in what capacity

You will weigh any statement about hiring with four checks. Does the speaker work there now? Is it the company’s own channel? Is it about hiring, or all hiring? And when was it said? A former employee describes past practice; a company-wide post describes every engineer’s loop.

Ramp’s engineering blog names four axes for evaluating FDE candidates: drive and work ethic, engineering fundamentals, customer empathy and great communication. The weight decides what you do with a statement. A company’s own words on its own channel you may quote back to that company’s interviewer, with the source: “Your engineering blog puts customer empathy next to fundamentals.” A former employee’s remark, or a company-wide post, only tells you which story to prepare; never repeat it as what this panel wants.

No leader in our research gives a scoring rubric, a pass rate or a list of red flags meant as policy; Ramp’s four axes are the closest thing to published criteria.
