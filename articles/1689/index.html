Hiring demand for these roles has surged, with salaries exceeding $180,000

Preston Fore

2026-09-07

Silicon Valley tech giants are following Palantir's model, hiring engineers to work directly with customers—and paying them salaries of nearly $200,000.

Small

Default

Large

Forget building AI from a Silicon Valley office. One of tech's fastest growing jobs now requires technical staff to work directly on-site with customers, configuring AI tools, integrating software, and solving complex business problems.

According to data from labor market analytics firm Lightcast, job postings for "forward deployed" engineers grew more than 1,000% between January and August 2026 compared to the same period last year, and more than 4,600% compared to 2023. That far outpaces the broader tech job market, where postings grew just 13% year over year.

Similar growth has been reported for related positions on LinkedIn and Indeed, according to The New York Times.

This isn't an entirely new concept. Palantir has long been known for its "forward deployed" model, in which technical employees work closely with customers to develop and deploy software in the field.

Now, as companies race to effectively integrate AI into their business processes, this model is spreading rapidly.

"More and more companies are starting to use powerful AI models, but they struggle to turn those models into tools that actually work within their organizations. Connecting models to a company's proprietary data, existing systems, and specific workflows is often a major hurdle. Forward Deployed Engineers can help fill exactly this gap," Paul Farnsworth, president of tech job platform Dice, told Fortune.

Compensation for these roles is also substantial. According to Lightcast data provided by Farnsworth, the median annual salary in job postings for Forward Deployed Engineers exceeds $188,000, compared to about $145,000 for traditional software engineers.

At tech giants like Anthropic, some forward deployed roles can pay as much as $400,000.

A model pioneered by Palantir is being widely adopted by tech companies

Palantir currently has more than 40 forward deployed positions open, most of them in software development. Job seekers can also apply for roles dedicated to specific customers, ranging from large enterprises like Intel to defense organizations like NATO, as well as government agencies in countries such as Norway.

One Palantir job posting reads: "As a Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE), your role is somewhat similar to that of a startup CTO: you'll work in small teams with a high degree of autonomy, owning major projects from kickoff to delivery. Your day-to-day work may include discussing system architecture with fellow engineers, processing massive amounts of data, developing custom web applications, communicating with customer executives, or setting strategy for your team."

This hands-on, on-the-ground approach with customers has become a defining hallmark of Palantir's business model. The company believes it is precisely this model that has helped it stand out in competition against tech giants many times its size.

"We recruited the world's best software engineers, pulled them out of the comfortable offices of Palo Alto, and sent them to remote locations to serve frontline staff who are extremely capable in their business but not technical. In the process, we beat companies with far greater resources in the competition for top talent," Melin von Brentano, who leads Palantir's digital transformation strategy, wrote in a blog post last month.

By many measures, the model has indeed been a success. Palantir's market cap now exceeds $400 billion. In the most recent quarter, the company posted revenue of $1.94 billion, up 93% year over year.

Many of Palantir's competitors have begun adopting the model as well.

Microsoft, Meta, Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic are all currently hiring Forward Deployed Engineers. In addition, everyone from established tech giants like Nvidia to unicorn startups like Scale AI are extending the "forward deployed" model to other roles such as product management and technical architecture.

How to land a Forward Deployed Engineer role

Becoming a Forward Deployed Engineer isn't easy. The role requires technical expertise in programming, machine learning, generative AI, and technical infrastructure, as well as the consulting, communication, and leadership skills needed to work directly with customers.

For those hoping to land a Forward Deployed Engineer role, Farnsworth says the first step is to build a solid foundation in modern technology, then go further with APIs, data pipelines, cloud infrastructure, and the ability to deploy AI systems reliably to production. On top of that, candidates should focus on strengthening soft skills like problem-solving, communication, and business judgment.

"Today, simply knowing how to use the latest models or AI tools is far from enough. What really sets candidates apart is the ability to precisely connect technical knowledge to real business problems. Faced with an ambiguous situation, can you understand how the workflow actually operates, communicate effectively with both technical and non-technical stakeholders, and ultimately build a solution that delivers measurable results?" Farnsworth said.

Tech professionals can also gain this kind of experience by looking for opportunities in their current roles to apply AI to real business processes.

"I also recommend that tech professionals proactively look for opportunities in their current roles to deploy AI into real workflows, and document the actual results—whether that's revenue generated, time saved, errors reduced, or a process improved. Focusing on this will help applicants for forward deployed roles boost their competitiveness and ultimately land the job," Farnsworth added. (Fortune China)
