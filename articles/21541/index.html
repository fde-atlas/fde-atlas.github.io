Summary

This guide for IT/DX managers argues that generative AI data leaks occur through four routes—input, settings (training and retention), permissions, and external integrations—and that prohibition-only rules cannot stop them, since shadow AI on personal accounts and prompt injection attacks bypass employee behavior. It recommends combining five measures: input rules, enterprise plans and opt-out settings, permission tightening, logging with DLP, and attack testing. If a reportable personal-data breach occurs, an initial report to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission is due within roughly 3–5 days and a final report within 30 days (60 if fraud is suspected). Cited evidence includes IPA's Top 10 Threats 2026 (AI cyber risk ranked third for organizations), Samsung's code leak, OpenAI's 2023 outage, and the EchoLeak Copilot vulnerability. The article also compares in-house versus outsourced approaches, including FDE-style support, with a disclosure that the publisher DeFactory sells a related diagnostic service.

Key points

  • Leaks follow four routes: input, settings/training, permissions, external integrations.
  • Five combined measures beat prohibition-only rules; shadow AI grows when bans are the only policy.
  • IPA's 2026 threat list ranks AI cyber risk third for organizations, its first appearance.
  • Reportable breaches: initial report in ~3–5 days, final within 30 days (60 for fraud).
  • Prompt injection (OWASP LLM01) requires permission, egress, and logging controls, not rules.

Generative AI information leaks occur through 4 routes: input, settings (training and storage), permissions, and external integrations, and cannot be prevented by "don't use it" prohibition rules alone.

In IPA's (Information-technology Promotion Agency) "Information Security 10 Major Threats 2026," "cyber risks surrounding AI use" ranked 3rd for organizations, its first appearance on the list.

You cannot reduce risk to zero, but you can make leaks less likely, minimize damage when they occur, and detect them quickly.

To that end, combine the following 5 measures and also establish reporting procedures for when a leak occurs.

  • Input rules
  • Enterprise settings
  • Restricting permissions
  • Logs and DLP (a mechanism that detects and blocks the movement of confidential information out of the organization)
  • Attack testing

This article is aimed at information systems, DX, and management personnel who want to allow generative AI use within their company, and presents, in order, an overview of the leakage routes, a comparison of the 5 countermeasures, reporting deadlines when a leak occurs, and when to use outsourcing.

Sources are the official pages of IPA, the Personal Information Protection Commission, the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications and the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, and OpenAI. Figures and specifications are based only on publicly available information as of November 2026.

Generative AI information leaks occur through routes where information leaves the company or becomes visible to people who should not see it.

Since the point where each route can be stopped differs, first classify them into these 4 categories.

RouteWhat HappensExample/SourceWhere to Stop It
① InputEmployees paste confidential information or personal data directly into AIAt Samsung Electronics, engineers entered internal source code into ChatGPT, leading to a ban on its use (introduced in IPA's guidelines)Before input (rules, DLP)
② Settings, training, and storageEntered content is used for training, history is retained, or service-side bugs expose it to othersIn March 2023, an OpenAI outage exposed other users' conversation titles. For 1.2% of active ChatGPT Plus subscribers during the roughly 9-hour outage, names, emails, and billing addresses may have been visible (OpenAI official)Contracts and settings
③ PermissionsAn AI connected to internal documents answers questions from people who should not have access, using those documentsExample of connecting AI while all employees have access to HR materials (1-4). The international guideline OWASP also cites excessive AI permissions and autonomy (LLM06) as a major riskPermission design
④ External integrationsCommands embedded in email or web text cause AI to send internal data outsideMicrosoft 365 Copilot vulnerability "EchoLeak" (reported around June 2025; introduced in IPA's explanatory document; already fixed)Permissions, restrictions on external transmission, logs

Sources: IPA "Guidelines for the Introduction and Use of Text-Generation AI", OpenAI's March 20, 2023 outage report, OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025, IPA "10 Major Threats 2026" explanatory document (organization edition).

The examples are most useful when read in terms of "where they could have been stopped."

The Samsung example corresponds to ① input, the OpenAI outage to ② the service side, and EchoLeak to ④ external integrations.

① is stopped by human behavior, ② by contracts, and ③④ by mechanisms. Countermeasures that rely only on people cannot contain ③④.

Shadow AI refers to employees using AI services the company is unaware of, via personal accounts or similar, for work purposes.

IPA's explanatory document cites as a risk the fact that, with shadow AI, the workplace cannot recognize the business use of personal accounts.

According to a survey by a US AI company cited in the same document, 77% of users paste data into generative AI, and 82% of them used accounts outside their organization's control.

A company merely "not permitting" it does not mean the data goes unused.

According to OpenAI's official help, for ChatGPT's individual plans (Free, Plus, Pro), input content may be used to improve the models. If you do not want it used, turn off "Improve the model for everyone" in the settings.

On the other hand, ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, and Edu, as well as inputs via the API, are not used for training by default.

The steps to opt out of training use differ by service, both in location and default setting. If you use other companies' services such as Anthropic's Claude, Google's Gemini, or Microsoft's Copilot, check each company's official pages.

Sources: OpenAI "How your data is used to improve model performance", same site "Data controls in ChatGPT".

With RAG (a mechanism that searches internal documents and feeds them to the AI for use in answers) or AI like Copilot that connects to internal files, the range the AI can see directly becomes the range it can answer about.

For example, if HR materials remain accessible to all employees and you connect an AI, anyone can ask "What is the department head's salary?" The AI does not create a new hole; rather, the original lax permissions become exposed simply by asking.

Before connecting, review folder and file permissions first.

DLP (Data Loss Prevention, a mechanism that detects and blocks sensitive information from leaving) is used, for example, to stop sending text containing card numbers.

IPA's guidelines also list, as system-level measures, input/output log management, restricting adversarial prompt inputs, selecting services that allow opting out, and vetting data placed in vector databases (storage for documents searched in RAG).

The five measures are insufficient individually. ① works on people, ② on contracts, and ③-⑤ on systems.

With only "don't input confidential data," employees cannot judge what is confidential.

By dividing into the following 4 categories and deciding handling per category, operations become practical (the categories and handling are guidelines compiled by the editorial team).

CategoryExamplesMay it be input?
Public informationPublished articles, press releasesYes

ConfidentialUnpublished financials, source code, contract details with partnersIn principle no. Approval required if used

The AI Business Guidelines (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications / METI) also require users not to improperly input personal or confidential information.

Anonymization means not only removing names and contact details, but also ensuring individuals cannot be identified by combining other fields. After defining categories, including 3-5 examples from actual work in internal explanatory materials helps them take root.

If business contracts prove difficult after checking, a local LLM (AI run in your own environment, keeping data in-house) is also an option. However, updates and security management become your company's responsibility.

What a company can see is determined by contracts and settings.

What is within the scope of business plan admin functions or configured to be recorded on internal log infrastructure can be seen. If employees use personal accounts, the company cannot see it.

Write the scope of visibility into work rules and usage policies, and inform employees. If no one knows what is recorded, operations cause disputes.

Logs are not enough just to collect. Decide who views them, how often, and under what conditions.

Attack testing is the work of giving the AI malicious instructions or misleading documents and testing whether internal data leaks.

It is done to find the prompt injection issues in Chapter 3 and permission gaps. If your company lacks the knowledge, this becomes a matter of outsourcing to external experts (Chapter 5).

Rules have three limitations.

  • Some people do not know the rules, and some do not follow them
  • Personal account use is invisible to the company (1-2)
  • Attacks come not from employee mistakes, but from the AI's mechanisms themselves

The AI Business Guidelines ask users to use AI with the understanding that AI vulnerabilities cannot be completely eliminated.

Therefore, do not assume nothing leaks; design so that even if something leaks, damage is small and detected quickly.

Prompt injection is an attack that embeds commands in text such as emails or web pages to make the AI behave in unintended ways.

OWASP (an international nonprofit organization researching web and application security) ranks this as the No. 1 risk for generative AI applications (LLM01), noting that even using RAG cannot fully prevent it.

Vulnerability/RiskMechanismHow to Mitigate
Prompt injectionAI follows instructions embedded in text it readsSeparate the permission to read external text from the ability to send data outward
Excessive permissions (LLM06)AI has more viewing/operating permissions than necessaryLimit to only the necessary scope (least privilege = grant only what is needed)
Leakage of sensitive information (output)Company secrets or personal information end up mixed into responsesNarrow the data being referenced, inspect outputs
Contamination or defects in ingested dataDocuments targeted for search contain information that should not be shownVet data before putting it into the vector DB (IPA)
Shadow AIUse of personal accounts unknown to the companyProvide corporate services approved for use and track usage

Source: OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 (LLM01, LLM02, LLM06), IPA guidelines.

IPA's explanatory document cites reports (around June 2025) of the "EchoLeak" vulnerability in Microsoft 365 Copilot.

It was reported that when a malicious prompt was injected from outside, internal secret data that Copilot was permitted to access could potentially leak out (since fixed).

This example can occur even without any input from an employee. The place to stop it is not people, but permissions, restrictions on external transmission, and logs.

When leakage of personal data occurs, the Act on the Protection of Personal Information imposes an obligation to report to the Personal Information Protection Commission and to notify the affected individuals. The measures to take are organized in the Act's guidelines.

Laid out on a timeline, it looks like this:

Point in TimeWhat to DoNotes
Immediately after discoveryReport to the person in charge and take measures to prevent the damage from spreadinge.g., suspending the relevant account, deleting shared links, changing passwords
Within a few daysInvestigate the facts and identify the scope of impactWho entered what, into which service, with which settings
Generally within 3-5 days of becoming awareIf reportable, file an initial report with the Personal Information Protection CommissionProvide what is known at that point
PromptlyNotify the affected individualsDepending on the situation (may be deferred while the incident is still unclear)
Within 30 days of becoming awareFinal reportWithin 60 days if there is a risk of fraudulent intent
In parallelPreventive measures and, if necessary, public disclosureReview input rules, settings, and permissions according to the cause

Source: Personal Information Protection Commission, "Response to Leakage and Useful Materials", same, "Reporting Leakage of Personal Data", NCO, "Q&A Handbook on Related Laws".

Reporting is mandatory for leakage (or the risk thereof) that falls under any of the following.

CaseExample (as illustrated by the Personal Information Protection Commission)
Includes sensitive personal information (= personal information requiring special care, such as medical history or beliefs)Personal data including employees' health check results leaked
Risk of financial damageCredit card numbers from an EC site leaked
Risk of fraudulent intentUnauthorized access, data taken out by an employee
More than 1,000 affected individualsDue to a misconfiguration, personal data of over 1,000 people was viewable

Whether entering personal data into generative AI constitutes leakage depends on how the destination handles it (whether it is used for training, visible to third parties) and on the specific case.

Merely entering data does not automatically make it reportable, but when in doubt, it is best to consult an expert or the Personal Information Protection Commission's consultation desk.

Reports go, in principle, to the Personal Information Protection Commission. In fields where authority has been delegated to the ministry with jurisdiction over the business, report to that ministry.

  • A list of who to contact (person in charge, legal, IT)
  • Contact information for the providers of the AI services in use, and where to request confirmation of deletion and retention periods
  • A means to later review what was entered (whether logs exist)

If you start investigating only after an incident occurs, five days is short. Without logs, you cannot confirm what was entered.

If you have only one or two people in charge, a realistic split is: rules and contracts in-house, inspections and permission design outsourced.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) refers to an engineer who works on-site with customers, handling everything from organizing issues to implementation and adoption, or that form of support.

For every type, cost is determined by the scope and duration. Costs vary by scope and duration. Next to the price, write down how far settings, permissions, and logs are included, and compare.

  • Whether they take responsibility not just for creating rules but for implementing settings, permissions, and logs
  • Whether they can verify the specifications of the AI services your company uses from official information and explain them
  • Does it clearly specify where tasks you won't handle yourself, such as attack testing and investigation, will be delegated?
  • Does it avoid claiming "everything can be prevented" (assuming that complete elimination is impossible)?
  • After the contract, will the inventory, settings, and records remain with your company?

The workaround is to prepare corporate-use services that employees are allowed to use in advance.

Contracts and settings are not one-time tasks, and service specifications change.

The workaround is to set a schedule to review training, history, and administrator settings on official pages once every six months.

A common failure is connecting Copilot or similar tools while HR or accounting shared folders remain widely open.

The workaround is to inspect the permissions of the target folders before connecting.

The workaround is to decide who reviews, how often, and what triggers a review (e.g., large volumes of pasted content, late-night usage).
