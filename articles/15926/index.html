Two Types of FDEs: Engineering-Focused and Business-Focused

Original title: 两种FDE：工程型与业务型

圣路可 Fde · 2026-10-17 · 2 min read

Summary

The article divides FDEs into two complementary types: engineering FDEs integrate models into client systems through code, while business FDEs codify domain knowledge into rules, verify outputs with samples, and design human-AI division of labor. It maps them to Palantir's Delta (Forward Deployed Software Engineer) and Echo (Deployment Strategist) roles, citing Palantir's blog noting both blend product, engineering, and strategy work. It cites Anthropic's job posting requiring 8+ years of combined technical and client experience, Python proficiency, and 25%-50% travel. Recommendations: large enterprises with deep integration needs should prioritize engineering FDEs; SMEs and department-level scenarios can start with one business FDE plus an internal staff member, adding engineering support when needed. Internal staff should always participate so rules and configurations remain with the company.

Key points

  • Engineering FDEs deliver code-level integrations; business FDEs deliver rule libraries and verification reports.
  • The two types map to Palantir's Delta and Echo roles.
  • Anthropic requires 8+ years combined experience and 25%-50% travel for engineering FDEs.
  • Business FDEs need no programming background but must iterate on rules and testing.
  • Enterprises should involve internal staff so configurations stay in-house after projects end.

FDEs fall roughly into two types: engineering FDEs use code to connect models into customer systems, while business FDEs turn business experience into rules, verify results with samples, and design human-machine division of labor. The two differ in division of work, not in rank—many projects need both working together.

Correspondence with Palantir's Echo and Delta

According to Palantir's official blog, Delta (Forward Deployed Software Engineer) is more technical, responsible for deploying the platform to customer sites; Echo (Deployment Strategist) is closer to a product manager, responsible for clarifying customer problems and setting direction. The blog also notes that both roles actually blend product, engineering, and strategy work. Engineering FDEs are close to Delta, business FDEs close to Echo.

How enterprises should staff

  • Large enterprises needing deep system integration: primarily engineering FDEs, plus one business-savvy FDE responsible for rules and acceptance.
  • SMEs and department-level scenarios: primarily business FDEs, using platforms like WorkBuddy to complete configurations, and calling in engineering FDE support when interface and integration issues arise.
  • Either way: people from inside the enterprise must be involved, so that rules and configurations remain with the enterprise after the project ends.

FAQ

Can you be an FDE without knowing how to code?

It depends on which type. Engineering FDEs need solid programming skills. The core of a business FDE is business observation, rule extraction, sample verification, and permission design; configurations are completed with the help of platforms, so no programming background is required—but a willingness to test hands-on and iterate is.

How many FDEs does a project need?

It depends on the scope. For the first department-level scenario, you can start with one business FDE plus one internal expert from the enterprise; when multiple systems need to be integrated, add an engineering FDE.
