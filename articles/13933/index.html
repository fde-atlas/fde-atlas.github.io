What is an FDE? Shanghai Telecom innovates its AI delivery model

Original title: 何为FDE?上海电信创新AI交付模式

yeyulingfeng.com · 2026-04-29 · 5 min read

Summary

This article describes how Shanghai Telecom formed an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) team in late 2024 to help enterprises adopt AI. Instead of waiting for client requirements, the team proactively identifies use cases and develops on-site. It pairs an Echo team, which does pre-sales-style needs discovery, with a Delta team of engineers who embed at client sites. Case studies include a 3D seat-preview and dynamic pricing system for a Shanghai performance venue, and an intelligent form-filling system for a carmaker's call center that raised system scores from 7 to above 9 with roughly 5-second completion times. The team also leverages Shanghai Telecom's AI STORE product catalog in a standardized-plus-customized model.

Key points

  • Shanghai Telecom launched an FDE team in late 2024 to move services to client sites.
  • Echo handles needs discovery; Delta engineers develop on-site and iterate in real time.
  • A performance venue got 3D seat previews and dynamic ticket pricing.
  • A carmaker's call-center form-filling system improved from 7 to 9+ score, ~5 seconds per ticket.
  • The AI STORE catalog supports a standardized-plus-customized, sales-and-delivery-integrated model.

What is an FDE? Shanghai Telecom's innovative AI delivery model

From "turnkey" to "driving together" | Image source: AIGC

Author / IT Times Mao Yu

Editors / Qian Lifu Sun Yan

As the AI wave sweeps across every industry, countless enterprises struggle with where to start. Many companies cannot even articulate a clear AI transformation need, while AI service providers are unfamiliar with the realities of their businesses, resulting in a disconnect between technology and business. This often leaves small and medium-sized enterprises stuck at the "last mile" when embracing AI, reducing AI transformation to mere "talk on paper."

Against this industry pain point, at the end of last year, Shanghai Telecom formally established a brand-new FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) team, centered on "helping customers find needs, accompanying customers through implementation, and on-site delivery with iteration." This broke the traditional passive IT service model of "customers state requirements, providers deliver," moving services forward to the customer's business site and becoming a "close-at-hand partner" on enterprises' AI transformation journey.

Since its founding, the FDE team has sparked many "AI sparks" with enterprises across industries, and its "close-at-hand," "turnkey," and "zero-threshold" service model has been widely welcomed by enterprises.

To date, the FDE team has successfully delivered AI scenarios for enterprises in multiple industries, proving the value of this new service model through tangible, quantifiable results. From intelligent ticketing at performance venues to intelligent form-filling at an automaker's call center, the FDE team is using its ability to "understand AI, understand business, and understand customers" to open up the "last mile" of AI transformation for every industry.

Forward Services Break the Deadlock

From "Responding to Needs" to "Creating Needs"

"Almost all customers are interested in AI, but when asked 'what specifically do you want to use AI for,' many managers cannot answer." According to a person in charge of Shanghai Telecom's FDE team, today's business managers all know AI can improve efficiency, but they lack a clear understanding of technology boundaries, implementation scenarios, and return on investment, and thus cannot articulate specific needs.

The core value of the FDE team lies in breaking the traditional "passive response" model, moving services forward to the customer's business site and proactively helping customers "find needs, sort out scenarios, and define solutions."

The team is divided into two core units: the Echo team and the Delta team. The former is closer to the traditional "pre-sales support" role, going deep into the front lines of customers' businesses to identify potential AI application scenarios and respond quickly to customer needs; the latter consists of engineers with coding capabilities who, upon receiving a requirement, go to the customer's office for on-site development. The two teams work in coordination, forming a full-process closed loop of "need discovery — solution design — custom development — on-site delivery."

Take a large performing arts center in Shanghai as an example. After in-depth interviews with the customer's nine business departments, the Echo team focused on three core directions from a vast array of scenarios: 3D digital theater, intelligent marketing, and intelligent training. Among them, the 3D digital theater was built to address the pain point of "blind seat selection" in traditional ticket purchasing: when buying tickets, audiences can view a 3D simulation to directly see the viewing angle and obstructions of different seats, completely solving the user pain point of "ticket price not matching the experience." Meanwhile, the Delta team also developed an intelligent dynamic pricing system for the center, adjusting ticket prices in real time based on show popularity and market feedback to help operators maximize revenue.

"We don't just sell AI products to customers; we first help them figure out 'what problems AI can actually solve for you.'" According to the person in charge of Shanghai Telecom's FDE team, for traditional enterprises with weak IT capabilities, the team prioritizes easy-to-implement scenarios such as knowledge base deployment, office writing, text-to-image generation, AI coding, and OpenClaw; for information service enterprises with stronger IT capabilities, it offers personalized, customized delivery of computing power, model Token services, intelligent customer service, predictive maintenance, and more.

This "personalized for each customer" service model has greatly improved the FDE team's precision in need discovery, turning customers' AI transformation from "not knowing where to start" into "advancing step by step."

On-Site Delivery and Iteration

Making AI Truly "Used and Used Well"

Unlike the traditional R&D model of "developing in the office and maintaining after delivery," the Delta team of the FDE team adopts an "on-site development" model, with engineers stationed directly at the customer's site and iterating in real time around customer needs, solving the industry's chronic problem of "development disconnected from business."

"The core advantage of being on-site is that we can perceive customer usage feedback immediately and adjust and optimize the solution at any time." The person in charge of Shanghai Telecom's FDE team used an intelligent form-filling project at an automaker's call center as an example to explain in detail the value of on-site delivery.

The automaker's call center has a large number of seat agents. Under the traditional model, after calls with car owners and others, agents had to manually fill out work orders, which was time-consuming, laborious, and error-prone. The call center's initial requirement was "intelligent automatic form-filling," but during implementation, two core challenges emerged: insufficient accuracy and response speed that needed improvement.

After the FDE team came on board, Delta R&D engineers were stationed on-site and carried out comprehensive optimization targeting the pain points. For accuracy, the team built a dedicated hot-word library of automotive terminology for the customer, fine-tuned the model on specific terms such as vehicle models, parts, and 4S dealerships, and optimized semantic understanding logic, solving the problem of misrecognition of professional terminology. For response speed, the team adopted a "multi-model, scenario-based scheduling" solution, matching large models with different computing power and speeds to different business scenarios such as complaints and inquiries—for example, model A for speech-to-text and model B for content extraction—greatly improving response speed while maintaining accuracy.

Previously, the customer's traditional IT service system scorecard was stuck at 7 points and could not improve, and the project had stalled. After a period of on-site iteration by the Delta team, the system's overall score rose from 7 to over 9, and form-filling time stabilized at 5 seconds, with both indicators exceeding the customer's requirements.

Team Upgrade and Empowerment

Building a New Paradigm of Integrated Sales and Delivery

The establishment of the FDE team is not only an innovation in service model, but also a major upgrade in Shanghai Telecom's team building.

Currently, Shanghai Telecom has built an AI STORE application product list, including dozens of AI products and services such as Aiwen, OpenClaw, Xiaoqiantai, AiXueshu, digital humans, Aizhaopin, and translation cards. The FDE team performs small-scale integration and customized development based on customer needs, forming a flexible "standard products + customization" service model that can meet both rapid implementation needs and personalized business scenarios.

"Our goal is to build a team that integrates sales and delivery, so that customers have a professional team serving them closely throughout the entire journey from initial contact to final delivery." The person in charge of Shanghai Telecom's FDE team stated that the FDE team is not only a technical service team, but also the "forward deployer" of Telecom's AI capabilities, truly bringing Telecom's cloud resources, large model capabilities, and industry solutions to customer sites across every industry.
