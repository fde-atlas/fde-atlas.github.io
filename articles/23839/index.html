FDE Job Market: What 683 Job Posts Show

How to Become an FDE — Interview Kickstart · 2026 · 6 min read

Summary

The article analyzes 683 deduplicated FDE job descriptions drawn from 1,726 US LinkedIn postings over 30 days in November 2026, spanning 430 employers. Its core argument: FDE is not a junior role but a mid-to-senior position built on solid software engineering, layered with AI skills and customer-facing delivery. 75% of postings explicitly require AI skills (LLMs, agents, RAG, evals), and 94% embed engineers in customer environments. The Big Four consultancies (Deloitte, KPMG, PwC, EY) posted 701 roles, followed by tier-1 tech led by Databricks, AWS, Google, and Salesforce; 335 companies posted exactly one role, showing a highly fragmented market. Geographically, the SF Bay Area and New York account for 44%, with Washington DC third. The author advises candidates to close the AI skill gap and demonstrate customer-delivered work rather than framework expertise.

Key points

  • Sample: 683 deduplicated JDs from 430 employers, 1,726 postings in 30 days.
  • 75% of postings explicitly require AI skills: LLMs, agents, RAG, evals.
  • 94% embed engineers in customer environments; customer-facing work is core.
  • Big Four consultancies posted 701 roles, with Deloitte leading at 524.
  • SF Bay Area and New York hold 44% of roles; Washington DC ranks third.

Before the data: what these roles assume

An FDE is, first, a strong software engineer. These postings assume solid general software-engineering experience and fundamentals — building, shipping, and debugging real systems — and then layer the AI build, solution architecture, and customer-facing delivery on top. What employers ask for

The demand splits into four buckets, and the order inside them is the finding. The core-craft bucket is led by communication and problem-solving — not by a framework. That is the FDE thesis in employers' own words: the engineering is assumed, and the differentiator is the part of the job that faces a customer. Tools and platforms sit in their own bucket, and the AI panel on the right is the pipeline's concept rollup rather than raw strings — LLMs, agents and RAG, scored against the postings that actually name an AI stack.

Who is hiring — the Big Four lead, tier-1 tech is right behind

The Big Four consultancies post the most FDE roles: Deloitte, KPMG, PwC and EY put up 701 of the 1,726 postings, often for FDE teams built around a partner platform such as Palantir or Databricks. Right behind them is tier-1 tech: 68 distinct job descriptions from frontier AI labs and Big Tech, led by Databricks, AWS, Google, Salesforce. Past those names the field is wide: no company holds more than 4.7% of the job descriptions, and 335 companies posted exactly one. For a job search that means breadth: your route in is not one famous logo but a wide field of companies hiring one or two people each. Where the roles are — two coastal hubs, then DC

The map is coast-weighted. Two hubs, the SF Bay Area and New York, carry 44% of the sample between them. A government-driven Washington DC cluster is a clear third, with smaller groups in Chicago, Seattle and Texas (Dallas, Austin, Houston) behind it. 70% of postings pin to a named metro and the rest are nationwide or remote, so this is a customer-facing role with real on-site gravity rather than a fully-remote one.

How to read this as a candidate

  • The AI delta is your differentiator. Most US engineers already have the foundation. The skill signature — evals, RAG, agents — is the gap that gets you hired. Close it, and you are positioning into exactly what Joanne Chen calls "the lasting differentiator."
  • Customer-facing is non-negotiable. The vast majority of postings are customer-facing and embedded. A portfolio of solo demos will not pass; show work delivered for a stakeholder.
  • Ship features, not frameworks. No single AI framework dominates the postings. Employers want engineers who can ship LLM features end-to-end, not specialists in one orchestration library.
  • Breadth beats a logo list. With 335 companies posting exactly one role, most openings sit at companies you have not shortlisted. Search by the work, not by the employer.
  • This is a mid-senior market. Senior and Leadership are 37% of the postings that state a level. If you have 8+ years, the architecture and customer-leadership bands are where the pay is.
