Comparing Cloud Foundry Case Studies | Achievements of Domestic and International Companies and Migration Decisions for 2026

Original title: Cloud Foundry導入事例を比較｜国内外企業の成果と2026年の移行判断

FDE総研 · 2026-11-04 · 11 min read

Summary

This article surveys public Cloud Foundry adoption cases and offers a framework for 2026 migration decisions. Most published cases date from the Pivotal era (2013–2019) and involve large US firms like Allstate, The Home Depot, Ford, and Comcast; in Japan, usage is mainly via SAP BTP, with few verifiable named cases. The author warns vendor-reported metrics lack consistent measurement and argues success came from organizational change plus scoping which apps fit cf push, not the platform alone. Commercial routes are now limited to VMware Tanzu and SAP BTP, and Broadcom's shift to subscriptions is prompting reevaluation. A four-axis decision framework (app homogeneity, contracts, platform staffing, migration path) is proposed, along with FDE roles in assessment, app inventory, and first migrations.

Key points

  • Most public cases are from the 2013–2019 Pivotal era, mainly large US enterprises.
  • Japanese adoption centers on SAP BTP; few verifiable named domestic cases exist.
  • Success stemmed from organizational change and scoping, not the platform itself.
  • Commercial options are now VMware Tanzu and SAP BTP after Broadcom's licensing shift.
  • A four-axis framework guides stay/migrate decisions; FDEs handle assessment and pilot migrations.

Which companies have adopted Cloud Foundry?

Based on FDE Research Institute's investigation, most published case studies are large US enterprises in finance, retail, manufacturing, and telecommunications. In Japan, usage is mainly through SAP BTP.

Cloud Foundry is an open-source PaaS (application execution platform) where you simply send your application code with a

cf push

command, and it automatically handles building, deployment, routing, and scaling. Most adoption case studies were published during the era of Pivotal, which sold the commercial version from 2013 to 2019, as conference presentations or the company's customer stories.

Overseas: Large enterprise case studies published in the Pivotal era

Allstate, The Home Depot, Ford, Comcast, and others have publicly shared their adoption and use of Pivotal Cloud Foundry through conference materials and official announcements.

The figures showing each company's results (release frequency, development time reduction rates, etc.) mostly come from vendor materials and presentations. Since measurement methods and assumptions are not standardized, this article does not reproduce those figures. If you want to compare them, check the Cloud Foundry Foundation's official website and each company's primary sources to verify when and over what scope the figures were measured.

Japan: SAP BTP and vendor-provided platforms are the main entry points

There are two main routes to using Cloud Foundry in Japan: the Cloud Foundry environment of SAP Business Technology Platform (SAP BTP), and commercial distributions (Cloud Foundry products sold by vendors).

SAP BTP offers a "Cloud Foundry environment" as an application runtime, as well as a Kubernetes-based "Kyma environment." As a result, Japanese companies using SAP ERP sometimes use Cloud Foundry without being aware of it when building extension applications. On the other hand, the FDE Research Institute editorial team found only limited primary sources where Japanese companies publicly spoke about their Cloud Foundry results under their own names. For this reason, this article does not name specific Japanese companies.

Points to Note When Reading Case Studies

When reading case studies, it is important to distinguish who published them. Vendor announcements, presentations by the user companies themselves, and third-party analyses differ in reliability.

Type of SourceExampleHow to Read It
Vendor primary announcementsPivotal/VMware/Broadcom customer storiesResults tend to be presented favorably. Verify the measurement conditions
Presentations by user companies themselvesTalks at Cloud Foundry Summit, etc.Challenges and failures are often discussed. Check the year of the presentation
Third-party analyses and reportingResearch firm reports, tech mediaCheck the sample population and period, and do not generalize beyond that scope

Most published case studies are from 2015 to 2019. Using them directly for migration decisions as of 2026 will create discrepancies with the current state, where licensing schemes and Kubernetes-related tooling have matured.

What Do Organizations That Succeeded with Cloud Foundry Have in Common?

According to FDE Research Institute's analysis, what determined success was not platform performance, but changes to the development structure and whether the scope of standardization was clearly defined.

Comparing published case studies, there are almost no accounts suggesting that results came from simply introducing Cloud Foundry. Examples that also changed development processes and team role divisions stand out.

Common Point 1: They ran the platform team as a "product" for internal users

In successful cases, the platform team treated internal developers as "customers." They set service quality targets (SLOs) and defined the scope of what users could do on their own.

Cloud Foundry embodied early on what is now called "platform engineering." From a practitioner's perspective, if the platform team becomes a help desk that handles requests one by one, the convenience of

cf push

tends to be underutilized.

Common Point 2: They narrowed the scope to "applications that run with cf push"

Cloud Foundry is best suited for environments with many applications of similar configuration, such as web apps and business APIs. Organizations that achieved results did not force other types of applications onto it.

  • Prioritize deploying stateless web and API applications
  • Separate databases and message queues as external services via Service Broker (the mechanism for connecting external services)
  • Keep apps that require GPU processing or special network configurations on a separate platform

Deciding these boundaries up front prevents the platform from becoming complicated by exception handling.

Commonality 3: Introduced together with a transformation of development practices

Many case studies from the Pivotal era are discussed alongside Pivotal Labs-style pair programming, XP (Extreme Programming), and product-oriented organization building.

Keep in mind that it is difficult to separate the benefits of the platform itself from the effects of organizational transformation. It is safer to avoid reading these cases as "you will get the same results just by adopting Cloud Foundry."

Where does Cloud Foundry stand as of 2026?

As of November 2026, Cloud Foundry continues as an OSS project. The main routes for commercial use are VMware Tanzu under Broadcom and SAP BTP.

Licensing changes after the Broadcom acquisition

Broadcom, which acquired VMware, announced in December 2023 the end of perpetual license sales and a shift to a subscription model. For existing users, this has become a trigger to reconsider migration.

Because the cost structure may change at contract renewal, the decision of "whether to keep using it" cannot be made on technical grounds alone. Specific pricing differs by contract and cannot be compared from public information, so this article does not cover it.

Korifi: Recreating the Cloud Foundry experience on Kubernetes

The Cloud Foundry Foundation is advancing a project called "Korifi" that recreates the

cf push

experience on Kubernetes.

With Korifi, you can take an intermediate approach: keep the developer experience as is while shifting only the execution platform to Kubernetes. However, features may not all be identical to traditional Cloud Foundry. Before using it in production, you need to verify how it works with your own applications.

Cloud Foundry vs. Kubernetes and other PaaS: Which should you choose?

The basic rule of thumb: choose Cloud Foundry if you prioritize standardizing the developer experience, Kubernetes if you prioritize flexibility and ease of adoption, and a managed PaaS for small-scale workloads.

Comparison of the three options

FDE Soken's four-axis evaluation

At FDE Soken, recognizing that existing comparison articles tend to focus on "feature differences," we recommend judging based on the following four axes.

Evaluation axisConditions that favor continuing/adopting Cloud FoundryConditions that favor Kubernetes or other PaaS
1. App homogeneityMost apps are similarly structured web apps such as Java/SpringLanguages and structures vary widely; many stateful apps
2. Existing contracts and assetsExisting Tanzu/SAP BTP contracts and familiar build mechanisms (buildpacks)New adoption, or costs change significantly at renewal
3. Staff responsible for the platformA platform team exists but few have Kubernetes experienceKubernetes-experienced staff can be secured
4. Ease of future migrationMigration targets (e.g., Korifi) have already been evaluatedFuture portability is the top priority

If three or more of the four axes lean toward the same side, your direction is essentially decided. If it splits 2-2, the practical approach is to run a trial migration with a few key applications and judge based on the actual effort required.

How does the adoption decision change based on company size and existing assets?

Whether you have existing Tanzu/SAP BTP contracts and how many dedicated platform staff you can assign greatly change the rationality of continuing to use Cloud Foundry.

Steps for existing users considering migration

Existing users should not migrate everything at once; it is safer to proceed step by step in the following order.

  • Inventory your applications and list languages, dependent services, traffic, and owning teams
  • Classify the migration difficulty of each application using the criteria in steps ① through ④
  • Run a trial migration with two or three easy-to-migrate applications and record the effort required and failure patterns
  • Working backward from the contract renewal date, decide whether to continue, migrate gradually, or migrate fully

From a practitioner's perspective, skipping step ③, the trial migration, makes it easy to misestimate effort. In particular, settings that buildpacks implicitly handled on your behalf (runtime versions, certificates, and passing of environment variables) tend to be overlooked.

What does an FDE do on Cloud Foundry projects?

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer: an engineer who embeds at the customer's site and implements) works together with the customer on assessing migration feasibility, inventorying applications, and implementing the first few migrations.

The FDE role was popularized by Palantir. Its distinguishing feature is taking responsibility not just for delivering documents but for building something that works at the customer's site. Cloud Foundry migration and continuation decisions involve technology, contracts, and organization, making it an area where the FDE approach shines.

Assessment phase: inventory and 12-factor compliance check

In the assessment phase, the FDE's role is not just to read documents but to run the applications in the customer's actual environment and verify how difficult migration will be.

  • Check how closely each application follows 12-factor (the 12 principles of cloud-native application design)
  • Diagram the dependencies on external services connected via Service Brokers
  • Understand the contract renewal date, the platform team's structure, and how complete the operational rules (Runbooks) are

Implementation phase: migrate without breaking existing cf push workflows

In the implementation phase, the biggest issue is moving the execution platform without changing the operations developers are used to.

Set up build and deploy mechanisms such as Korifi on Kubernetes, and migrate the first few applications together with the customer's engineers. The milestone is complete when the procedures have been handed over to the customer. If the FDE migrates everything alone, no operational know-how remains on the customer's side.

Guidelines by experience level

  • Early career: Gain hands-on experience in deployment and operations with either Cloud Foundry or Kubernetes first.
  • Experienced platform engineers: If you can also handle contract and organizational issues on top of platform experience, you are more likely to be valued as an FDE.
  • English skills: Primary information on Cloud Foundry and Korifi is mostly in English. Reading comprehension sufficient to read documentation and GitHub Issues is an advantage.

Who it doesn't suit It tends to be a heavy burden for people who want to focus solely on building platforms or who want to avoid coordinating with customers. That is because the FDE's role is not the technology choice itself, but enabling customers to operate that choice on their own.
