Paris FDE Salary Comparison: Anthropic vs. Startups

Original title: 巴黎 FDE 薪资对比：Anthropic 与初创公司

FDE Times · 2026-11-07 · 5 min read

Summary

The article compares two Paris FDE job postings: Anthropic's €205,000–220,000 base salary comes from a posting closed over six months earlier, while startup Leadbay offers €50,000–70,000 plus 0.1–0.5% equity to zero-experience candidates. The author argues the three-to-four-fold gap reflects different posting dates, compensation structures, and seniority levels, not simply lab versus startup markets. It also explains French payroll mechanics—roughly 20–23% employee deductions and up to ~40% employer costs—and notes equity at AI labs now makes up 60–70% of total pay, while startup equity may take 5–7 years to sell. It closes with advice for early-career engineers evaluating such offers.

Key points

  • Anthropic's €205,000–220,000 range is from a closed posting over six months old.
  • An October 2026 analysis found no open Paris FDE postings with public salary ranges.
  • Leadbay hires zero-experience candidates and requires about six client-visit days monthly.
  • French gross salary differs from net pay; employer costs can reach ~40% on top.
  • Lab equity is 60–70% of total pay; startup equity may take 5–7 years to sell.

Key takeaways

  • Anthropic's €205,000–220,000 range in Paris comes from a closed job posting published more than six months ago. An analysis from October 2026 found no currently open postings with public salary ranges in Paris.
  • Leadbay offers €50,000–70,000 plus 0.1–0.5% equity, hires people with zero experience, and requires at least about 6 days of client visits per month.
  • In France, gross salary is neither take-home pay nor employer cost. Startup equity may take 5–7 years before it can be sold.

There are two Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) postings in Paris. Anthropic's lists a base salary of €205,000–220,000 per year. Paris startup Leadbay's lists €50,000–70,000. Viewed side by side, the former is roughly three to four times the latter, and it's easy to conclude: if you want to be an FDE in Paris, you have to join a lab.

That conclusion comes too quickly, and it's worth seeing exactly where it goes wrong. The two figures were recorded at different times, the compensation packages are structured differently, and the experience requirements differ as well.

If you're weighing a European FDE offer, or learning to read job postings and choose an entry path, the method for breaking down these two numbers is more valuable than the numbers themselves.

A salary range only makes sense once you know when it was recorded, what it includes, and what level of person it's paid to.

The €205,000 figure is older than you'd think

Anthropic's "Forward Deployed Engineer, Applied AI" posting for Paris now exists only as a copy on Accel's job board, and the copy clearly states that the role is no longer accepting applications. It was published more than six months ago. So €205,000–220,000 is data from early 2026, not an offer you can still apply for.

An analysis of Anthropic FDE salary ranges published on October 20, 2026 shows the current situation is harder to pin down. According to that analysis, the US range is $280,000–320,000, and London's is £225,000–255,000.

Among the postings reviewed at the time of that analysis, neither Paris nor Munich had a public range. Paris data is therefore both scarce and old: a single data point, taken from a closed posting.

This data point is below the US range, but don't rush to convert currencies or calculate percentages. Perspective's 2026 FDE salary report surveyed 1,200 FDEs and offers a rule of thumb: ranges outside the US are typically 50–70% of US ranges.

The report itself notes that its data covers only the US, so this rule is for reference only. It tells you roughly how large the gap is, not what any specific person should earn.

Base salary isn't the whole compensation package

What's harder is equity. Anthropic's posting describes the role as delivering AI applications inside customer organizations.

On compensation, it only mentions competitive salary, plus one optional item: when employees donate equity, the company will match it (an equity donation match). It does not specify any concrete equity grant.

Meanwhile, Perspective's report states that in 2026, equity accounts for 60–70% of total compensation at frontier labs, up from 35–45% two years earlier.

Comparing only base salaries between a lab and a startup means comparing a small slice of one compensation package against nearly all of another.

So when negotiating with a lab, the first question should be how equity works, not what the base salary is.

Leadbay is the opposite: its posting states equity of 0.1–0.5% outright. But the same report says total compensation at applied AI startups is significantly lower than at frontier labs. Its advice is blunt: be prepared to wait 5–7 years before you can sell the equity.

Until then, 0.5% equity won't pay the rent in Paris.

In France, gross salary isn't what lands in your account

The third layer is the one most often overlooked by engineers from other markets. According to 2026 URSSAF social contribution rates compiled by Legisocial, the employee's share is roughly 20–23% of gross salary, while the employer's additional share can reach about 40%.

Applied to the midpoint of Leadbay's range: on a gross salary of €60,000, after deducting 20–23%, roughly €46,200–48,000 remains before income tax. On the company's side, if contributions reach 40%, the total cost of the role could be around €84,000.

This is an illustration using average rates, not a real payslip, and actual rates vary by situation. But it shows that the same gross figure means three different things depending on where you stand.

So when comparing a Paris offer with one in London or the US, don't compare gross to gross.

These are really two different jobs

The final layer has nothing to do with money. Leadbay's posting accepts candidates with zero experience and requires at least about 6 days of client visits per month, sometimes up to 3 days a week. The role targets people just entering the field, who learn through frequent on-site work.

As for Anthropic's posting, the available data doesn't show experience requirements. The only certainty is that it's a role delivering AI applications inside customer organizations, and the company's FDE ranges in the US and London are both above two hundred thousand.

One explicitly recruits newcomers, while the other, with the same title, offers three to four times the salary; the two are likely hiring different types of people. Attributing the entire gap to differences between two markets is a hasty conclusion.

Paris's overall salary level also matters. A comparison by L'ADN found that among 16 regions surveyed, Paris had the lowest average tech industry salary, at about €49,000, while Silicon Valley's was about $134,000.

The article attributes the gap to market maturity and cost of living. Against that benchmark, Leadbay's €50,000–70,000 is by no means low for a role open to people with zero experience.

Just starting out? How should you choose?

If you've just graduated, or have written code for a few years but never dealt directly with clients, you should treat a role like Leadbay's as an investment, not just a low-paying job.

You haven't been an FDE, yet you'd earn the average for Paris's tech industry. At least about 6 days a month with clients. That's on-site experience you can later describe with concrete numbers.

For those with a few years of engineering experience, the trade-off deserves more caution: it may mean trading a newcomer's salary for experience you lack. If that gap is precisely what's keeping you out of high-paying FDE roles, it's worth doing; otherwise, find a role that matches your current level.

Either way, when writing your resume, don't just say "worked with clients." Spell out how many site visits you made, what you delivered, and how clients used it.

When an FDE role is described as delivering AI applications inside customer organizations, concrete evidence of having done that kind of work is more persuasive than a list of technical buzzwords.

When reading any FDE posting, in Paris or elsewhere, check five things before looking at the salary: Is the posting still open, and when was it published? Is the figure gross or take-home? How much equity, and when can it be sold? How many days of client visits are required? Are newcomers accepted?

The €205,000 in a closed posting and the €60,000 with 0.5% of unsellable equity cannot be measured with the same yardstick.

Public data on the Paris FDE market is still too scarce for anyone to give a "standard" range. While waiting for more publicly posted salaries, those who can break a number down into layers will negotiate better than those who merely memorize it.
