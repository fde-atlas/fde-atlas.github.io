An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role that brings a company's own product into the customer's workplace and handles everything from identifying issues to implementing and embedding AI. This article explains typical salaries, required skills, career paths, and how companies can secure FDE talent.

Published: Updated:

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineering role that brings a company's own product into the customer's workplace and handles everything from identifying issues to implementation and establishing operations, consistently and end to end. The name comes from the initials of Forward Deployed Engineer.

Since the start of 2026, opportunities to see this job title in Japan have suddenly increased*1. Companies that build AI have been launching dedicated implementation organizations one after another, and major consulting firms have also established similar departments. At the same time, in Japan the term is increasingly used in a broader sense than its original definition, and its meaning is beginning to blur.

This article organizes the original definition of FDE, how the term is spreading in Japan, the scope of the work, differences from SES and IT consultants, typical salaries and required skills, and career paths. It also explains, from a structural perspective, "how to secure FDEs" for companies looking for talent equivalent to FDEs. The content is aimed at both those aspiring to become FDEs and companies seeking people to drive AI initiatives.

What Is an FDE?

An FDE is an engineer who, while belonging to a company with its own product, works on-site at customer companies and, alone or in a small team, handles everything from identifying issues to implementing systems and establishing operations. This way of working was established by Palantir in the US, and with the spread of AI adoption it has become established as an independent job title*1.

While a typical software engineer develops products in their own company's office, an FDE is embedded in the customer's workplace. The distinguishing feature is that rather than implementing given requirements, they start by observing the field and defining for themselves "what should be solved."

Pronunciation and Variations in the Name

It is pronounced "efu-di-i" and written in katakana as "Forward Deployed Engineer" (Fowado Depuroido Enjinia). Forward Deployed was originally a military term referring to stationing troops overseas, rather than in the home country, even in peacetime. The practice of placing people at the customer's site instead of the company's own office became the name itself.

The name varies by company; Palantir calls the role Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE)*2. Conversely, similar-sounding titles are often entirely different roles; for example, Loglass positions its own "AI Solution Engineer" as a separate role that precedes the FDE stage*3. When searching for jobs, it is more reliable to judge by the job description rather than the title.

Three Elements That Distinguish a True FDE from Other Roles

  • Has their own product: rather than building from scratch on a contract basis, they adapt their own product to the customer's business
  • Stands on the side of defining problems: rather than receiving requirements, they observe the field and decide for themselves what problems to solve
  • Handles everything from implementation to establishing operations: they don't stop at design or proposals, but see things through to the point where the field keeps using the solution

These three elements connect directly to the "reasons companies stumble when trying to hire FDEs" discussed later. However, looking at job postings in Japan that call themselves FDE, many lack one or more of these three elements. The next chapter organizes the actual situation.

The Scope of FDE in Japan

What has been described so far is the original FDE image established by Palantir. However, the "FDE" used in Japan in 2026 has expanded considerably beyond this definition. This is not about blaming misuse, but confirming the fact that the range the word points to is actually shifting. Whether looking at job postings or searching for people, if you don't keep this in mind, conversations won't align.

Insight Edge, the Sumitomo Corporation Group company responsible for digital promotion, organized this situation as follows when it established FDE positions in May 2026: FDE roles and definitions vary from company to company and are diverging from the original FDE image. The term "FDE" is already becoming a generic noun, used as a broader concept beyond the Palantir-originated definition. The company describes this as a "chaotic situation" and notes that, with expectations running ahead of reality, it resembles the state of data scientists a generation ago*1.

Five Types Called "FDE" in Japan

The company organizes the FDEs actually in use into five types*1.

TypeWork Performed
Platform typeConfigures, extends, and implements their own product to fit the customer's environment
Pre-sales typeHandles technical proposals, demos, and PoC design at the sales negotiation stage
Customer success typeSupports post-implementation adoption, improvement proposals, and expanded usage
SIer typeHandles everything from custom development to operations according to each customer's requirements
Value creation typeHandles everything end to end from strategy formulation to AI implementation and delivering results

The one corresponding to the original FDE is the first, the platform type. The second and third are traditional pre-sales engineer and customer success roles with the FDE name attached. The fourth is contract development that doesn't assume an in-house product, making it the furthest from the original definition. The fifth is a role that handles everything from AI utilization strategy to implementation; its scope is broader than the original FDE, but its ties to a specific product are weaker.

The reason for this expansion lies in the revenue structure. The original FDE model is sustained by massive platform businesses as its resource base, but most companies lack that resource*1. Nevertheless, demand remains strong for "people who go on-site and implement," so the FDE name has been attached to existing roles.

In Job Postings, Determine the Type from the Job Description

Looking at actual job postings, this range appears as is. Some SaaS companies with their own products recruit FDEs as product implementation specialists, while consulting firms that solve customer business challenges with generative AI list FDEs as roles handling everything from strategy formulation to implementation. Even with the same title, the former centers on depth of product knowledge, while the latter centers on business design and proposal capabilities.

In other words, the term "FDE" alone does not determine what a person has done or what they will be responsible for going forward. Both applicants and employers need to verify three points rather than relying on the job title: whether the company has its own product, the scope of problem definition, and how far implementation responsibility extends.

From this point on, this article explains FDE based on its original definition (the platform type). However, in sections that look at actual conditions through domestic job listings, such as salary and career paths, it also covers positions recruited in the broader sense.

What FDEs Do

An FDE's work flows as a single continuous process, from discovering problems to embedding solutions into daily operations. The biggest difference from other roles is that the person in charge does not change between phases.

PhaseWhat is done
1. Observing the fieldVisit the customer's office or factory and see the business processes and data flows firsthand. Identify what staff members are spending their time on
2. Defining the problemBridge the gap between the issues described by management and the problems actually occurring on the ground, and define the problem to be solved
3. Building a prototypeLoad the customer's data and business processes onto the company's own product and create a working prototype within days to weeks. Have on-site staff try it and fix issues immediately
4. Implementation into productionBuild the data integration connecting the company's product with existing core systems. Handle data formatting and permission settings personally, and fill gaps in the product's features with custom development
5. Embedding into operationsSee the transition through until the field has fully adopted the new business processes. Feed the custom features and insights developed on-site back to the company's product development team to make them standard features

In traditional system development, requirements definition, design, development, testing, and deployment are handled by separate teams. In the FDE model, the same person carries these through, so no information is lost between phases.

There is another reason for the speed: not building from scratch. Palantir describes its FDEs as "engineers who embed themselves with customers and configure the company's existing platform to solve the hardest problems"*2. Components such as data ingestion, screens, and permission management already exist on the product side; the FDE assembles them to fit the customer's business and develops only what is missing. Short cycles of working output are possible because of this foundation.

The Deliverable Is "Business Outcomes," Not "Proposals"

An FDE is evaluated not by whether a deliverable was completed, but by whether the customer's business actually changed. Even if the system works, the outcome is zero if the field does not use it. For this reason, an FDE's work includes dialogue with on-site resistance such as "the new way is a hassle." It is a role whose technical work cannot be isolated from the rest.

The fifth point, "feeding back to the product development team," is also easy to overlook. If a problem solved at one site is incorporated into the product's standard features, deployment to the next customer becomes faster. FDEs stand at the starting point of this feedback loop.

How FDE Differs from Other Roles

Because FDEs "work at the customer's site," they can appear to overlap with existing roles such as SES, SIer, and IT consultants. The differences can be organized into three points: whether the company has its own product, whether the person defines the problems, and whether they are responsible through implementation.

RoleOwn productProblem definitionImplementation responsibilityMain deliverable
FDEYesDefines them personallyResponsible through operational adoptionBusiness outcomes
On-site staffing (SES)NoReceives from the customerWithin instructed scopeProvision of labor
SIerNoFollows customer requestsSystem completion and deliveryCustom-developed system
IT consultantNoDefines them personallyBasically up to the proposalProposals and roadmaps
Systems engineer (SE)NoReceives requirementsDesign and developmentDesign documents and code
Solutions architect (SA)DependsDefines them personallyMainly designArchitecture diagrams and design policies
Software engineer (SWE)YesReceives from PdMProduct qualityProduct code

The working style looks most similar to SES, but the conditions under which the provider profits are reversed. With SES, compensation is based on hours worked, so the longer the engagement drags on, the higher the provider's revenue. FDE revenue comes from continued use of the company's own product, so contracts continue as long as the customer achieves results quickly. Even though both involve being "on-site," one wants the engagement to last while the other wants results fast.

Why FDE Is in Demand Now

The background is not a lack of AI model performance. It is a bottleneck on the implementation side: high-performing AI cannot be integrated into actual business operations.

AI Adoption Easily Stalls at the PoC Stage

As of 2024, the US research firm Gartner predicted that at least 30% of generative AI projects would be abandoned after pilot deployment (PoC) by the end of 2025, due to poor data quality, rising costs, and unclear business value*4.

Now that this deadline has passed, the situation of Japanese companies is reflected in the numbers. According to the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications' "Reiwa 8 White Paper on Information and Communications" (July 2026), 86.4% of companies use generative AI in some business activities. On the other hand, 27.0% of companies answered that they have "no organizational initiatives" for business transformation through generative AI, far exceeding the US at 1.4% and Germany at 4.9%*5. While many companies have started using it, a considerable number are stuck at the stage of restructuring how their business operates.

Meanwhile, the use cases for AI continue to grow. In August 2025, the firm announced its forecast that enterprise applications equipped with task-specific AI agents would reach 40% by 2026, up from less than 5% in 2025*6.

The opportunities to use AI keep increasing, yet companies cannot get it into a usable state. Interest in FDEs is growing as the role that bridges this gap.

Even the Companies Building AI Have Launched Specialized Implementation Organizations

In May 2026, OpenAI announced the establishment of a new company, "OpenAI Deployment Company," specializing in AI implementation for enterprises. Through the acquisition of an applied AI company, experienced FDEs joined from day one*7. In the same month, Anthropic also co-founded an enterprise AI services company with several financial and investment firms. Its applied AI engineers enter customer sites, identify high-impact areas, and build solutions*8.

Consulting firms are moving in the same direction. In April 2026, Accenture announced the establishment of a "Forward Deployed Engineering" specialist organization with thousands of engineers, in collaboration with Microsoft *9.

Companies that build models are all starting to have organizational functions for "making things usable in the field." This is the direct reason why the job title FDE spread so quickly.

What Japan lacks is not researchers, but people who can put AI to work in the field

Looking at data from Japan, the substance of the skills shortage becomes clear. The "DX Trends 2026" report published by IPA (Information-technology Promotion Agency) in July 2026 divides AI talent into seven categories and asks companies about their staffing levels. The categories with the highest percentage of companies answering "insufficient" were: personnel who can plan and drive data management (71.9%), employees who understand the field and can drive AI adoption (69.6%), and employees who can analyze data with AI and apply it to the business (66.5%) *10.

Meanwhile, only 39.1% of companies reported a shortage of AI researchers who develop cutting-edge algorithms, which, along with executives and managers who understand AI (39.0%), was the lowest level among the seven categories. Moreover, 56.7% of companies said AI researchers are "not necessary for our company" *10. What most companies are looking for is not people who build models, but people who can bring existing AI into their own operations and make it work. Demand for FDEs can be understood as part of this structure.

For an analysis of which companies are actually hiring FDEs and what roles they expect, see "Why Is Demand for FDEs Surging? An In-Depth Analysis of Job Postings from 73 Japanese Companies."

FDE Skills and Salaries

Skills fall into three areas: technical ability, field understanding, and business understanding

The skills required of an FDE can be organized into three areas.

AreaWhat is required
Technical abilityImplementation skills in Python and similar languages, full-stack development experience, data integration with existing systems. For generative AI use: prompt design, building RAG (retrieval-augmented generation), and AI agent design
Field understandingThe ability to observe business workflows and identify bottlenecks. The ability to structure issues that frontline staff cannot articulate themselves
Business understandingThe ability to demonstrate return on investment in numbers. The ability to explain to management how technical choices affect the business

Technical ability requires both depth and breadth. Looking at public job postings in Japan, deep system knowledge sufficient to judge the feasibility of unfamiliar technical challenges, and experience designing and implementing production-quality code, are listed as mandatory requirements *11*12. On top of that, since each engagement involves different customer technology stacks and business domains, the ability to adapt quickly to unknown areas is also tested.

Salary benchmarks and why FDEs command high salaries

The expected annual salaries offered in Japanese FDE job postings range widely, with lower bounds in the 5 to 8.1 million yen range and upper bounds of 15 to 25 million yen *11*12*13*14. Examples at the higher end include Loglass offering 10 to 20 million yen for an FDE role in new business development, and LayerX citing a benchmark of 12 million yen or more *15*16. This is a high level for engineering positions in Japan, but salaries vary considerably depending on experience and scope of responsibility.

Looking at a larger number of postings makes the levels clearer. Of the 73 Japanese companies recruiting FDEs as of September 2026, among the 50 that listed salaries in yen, the median lower bound was 7.5 million yen (49 companies listed a lower bound) and the median upper bound was 15 million yen (46 companies listed an upper bound) *17. The methodology and breakdown of hiring companies are explained in the earlier article "Why Is Demand for FDEs Surging? An In-Depth Analysis of Job Postings from 73 Japanese Companies."

The reason compensation is high is that the combination of required abilities is rare. There are engineers who can implement. There are consultants who can talk to clients. But people who can do both alone and take responsibility until results are delivered are limited.

Career Paths to FDE

Because FDE is a new profession, almost no one in the market has "practical FDE experience." In actual hiring, candidates are evaluated on how their experience in adjacent fields can be applied.

Previous roleExperience that is valued
Software engineerImplementation skills and full-stack development experience. Often also required: experience interacting directly with clients to organize issues
SE / SIer backgroundUnderstanding of core system architecture, and experience delivering working systems within security and infrastructure constraints
IT consultantExperience in problem definition and stakeholder alignment. Proof of implementation ability is separately required
Data scientist / ML engineerData preprocessing and model tuning. Strong if you have experience integrating analysis into business operations
Project managerThe drive to mobilize multiple departments. A technical background is assumed

Three things to check in job postings before applying

Which of the five types described above a role falls into can be read from how the job posting is written. Checking the following three points before applying will help you avoid mismatches after joining.

  • Whether it leans toward implementation or proposals: If "data pipeline construction" and "SDK/API integration" appear, it leans toward implementation; if "business process redesign" and "stakeholder alignment" are central, it is closer to the value-creation type
  • The role of the company's own product: If the work centers on implementing a specific product, it is platform-type; if no product is assumed, it approaches the SIer type or value-creation type
  • Contract type and definition of results: If billing is based on hours worked, the reality approaches SES regardless of the title

Exits from FDE Experience: PdM, CTO, VPoE

Because FDE experience involves standing at the intersection of technology and business, it leads to careers in multiple directions.

The closest exit is product manager (PdM). Because you understand both customers' raw requests and technical constraints, you can judge which features should be standardized. Founding an AI startup or becoming a CTO are also options. Experience running the full cycle from problem discovery through product application to delivering results is structurally similar to launching a business. On the VPoE (VP of Engineering) path, your ability to translate between management and engineers is valued.

Why Companies Struggle with FDE Hiring

The difficulty companies face when trying to hire FDEs isn't just fierce competition for talent. FDE is fundamentally a role at companies that own products; it was never designed as a position for client-side companies to fill in-house.

FDE revenue rests on product licenses

The condition "carrying their own product" in the FDE definition isn't decoration. The revenue structure itself depends on it. The main revenue source for companies that employ FDEs is product license fees and SaaS subscription fees. When an FDE gets a product to a "usable state" at the customer's site, ongoing contracts are secured. That's why companies pay FDEs high compensation and grant them the authority to make decisions in the field.

Conversely, in organizations without a product to sell, this structure doesn't work.

What happens when a company tries to "hire an FDE in-house"

With the above in mind, the distortion becomes visible when a company looking to advance AI adoption thinks "let's hire an FDE."

  • Most talent calling themselves FDEs has delivered results based on a specific product. If your company doesn't use that product, only part of their experience transfers
  • Once hired by a client company, that person is no longer "someone on the front lines carrying their own company's product." In reality they are an internal AI promotion officer, and the meaning of hiring under the FDE job title fades
  • Nevertheless, compensation gets pulled toward FDE market rates, so only the hiring cost rises

In other words, what companies really need is not "a person with the FDE title," but someone who can carry the process of adapting AI to frontline operations and making it stick. If you search by job title without deciding who owns this process, no amount of market searching will fill the gap.

Note that the problem of the implementation process being left blank is common to securing AI talent in general, not just FDEs. The respective limits of hiring, training, and outsourcing are organized in a separate article, "What Is AI Talent? Types, Skills, Training Points, and a New Option That Handles Implementation and Adoption in the Field."

How companies can secure FDE capabilities

So what should companies do? The conclusion: rather than "searching" for the FDE job title, determine the scope that can be delegated to a vendor's FDE, and then decide who will own the work that falls outside it.

A vendor's FDE covers only "the areas where their product is effective"

Engaging a vendor that employs FDEs is a valid option. If the product to be introduced is already decided and you want it usable in the field, having the person who knows the product best enter the field is the shortest path. However, the scope they can cover has limits.

Easy to delegate to an FDELikely outside an FDE's scope
Adapting that product to frontline operationsDeciding which product to choose in the first place
Connecting existing systems with that productRedesigning business processes spanning multiple tools
Starting operations in the adopting department and initial adoptionCompany-wide rollout across departments
Frontline training on how to use the productHandover until in-house staff can run things on their own

Work left outside that scope cannot be covered by a party selling the product

The work remaining outside the scope that can be delegated to an FDE includes the following.

  • Prioritizing which business processes to apply AI to
  • Designing business workflows spanning multiple tools
  • Horizontal expansion from the adopting department to other departments
  • Handover and training until in-house staff can run things themselves

These are hard for a product vendor to cover, yet if fully outsourced, no know-how remains in-house. The realistic approach is to bring in a provider who doesn't sell products as a member of your team and proceed together with in-house staff. An AI-savvy professional embeds in the organization, handling everything from business design to implementation and adoption, leaving the methodology behind in-house. Like a vendor's FDE, they work hands-on in the field and don't leave until results appear. The difference is that they have no stake in selling a particular product.

Four things to decide before bringing in an external provider

Whether it's an FDE or another arrangement, the things to decide before putting an external provider in the field are the same.

  • Scope of access to operations and data: without observing the work and touching the data, even defining the problems can't begin
  • How much decision-making to delegate: the value of this role lies in making judgments and acting in the field. If everything must be brought back for internal approval, speed suffers
  • How to measure results: agree before starting on whether to measure by hours saved, lead time, or usage rate
  • The handover goal: define which operations, owned by whom, will be self-sufficient by when

If you bring someone in while these four points remain vague, even an excellent provider won't deliver results. Conversely, if they're settled, the boundary between what to delegate to an FDE and what to keep in-house falls naturally into place. Contract forms when engaging an external FDE, criteria for deciding whether to outsource, and preparation before engagement are organized in a separate article, "What to Know Before Outsourcing an FDE | Decision Criteria and Preparation."

FDE-like roles outside engineering

FDE explanations are usually written assuming an engineering role, but the structure of "entering the field, reworking operations, and getting AI running" is also appearing outside engineering. The "GTM Engineer," who rebuilds GTM processes spanning sales, marketing, and customer success into systems using data integration and automation, is one example. In back-office functions like accounting and in customer support too, the nature of the work—reading the business and redrawing procedures—remains the same. GTM engineers are covered in a separate article, "What Is a GTM Engineer? Job Description, Differences from Sales Planning, and the Reality of 4 Domestic Employers."

What's needed is not aligning job titles, but a state where someone who knows the business deeply and someone who can implement it work on the same team. With only one of the two, you end up with either a system that doesn't fit the field or a vision that never runs.

Summary

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who enters the customer's field carrying their own company's product and consistently handles everything from problem discovery through implementation to operational adoption. As AI-building companies themselves began to own implementation organizations, the title spread rapidly in 2026. At the same time, in Japan the term has become genericized, with everything from presales to contract development and AI strategy planning being recruited under the FDE name. Rather than the title, you have no choice but to read the substance: whether they have their own product, the scope of problem definition, and how far implementation responsibility extends.

For those aiming to become an FDE, it is a rare position that combines technical skill, on-the-ground understanding, and business understanding in one person, offering wide options for both compensation and career exits. Since experienced professionals are not yet on the market, transitioning from an adjacent field is currently the shortest route.

For companies, the conclusion takes a slightly different form. FDE is a role at companies that own products, and trying to hire it as a job title creates structural distortion. What is needed is to determine the scope that can be entrusted to a vendor's FDE, and decide who will handle the work that falls outside it (business design spanning multiple products, company-wide rollout, and internal adoption).

What needs to be filled is not a vacant job title, but the gap in execution between vision and adoption. Once you decide who will take on that role, the requirements for the person you bring in from outside and the roles to keep in-house naturally fall into place. The rapid spread of the FDE role is also evidence that the decisive battleground for AI adoption has shifted from "building" to "operating in the field." Rather than outsourcing AI initiatives and waiting, bring someone in-house to move things forward together. That structure can be set up even by companies without their own products.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Do FDEs work on-site at customer locations?

In the original model, physically commuting to the customer's office was the norm. A former FDE who spent about eight years at Palantir recalls being expected to work at customer sites three to four days a week, with a great deal of travel*18. On the other hand, many domestic job postings allow remote or fully remote work and do not require on-site presence*11*13*15. Whether "being in the field" means physical secondment or deep involvement in the work varies by company.

Q. What kinds of companies have FDE roles?

They are mainly found at companies with their own products. Overseas, Palantir has established the role, and OpenAI and Anthropic have organizations responsible for implementing their own models*1*7*8. In Japan, in addition to SaaS companies like LayerX and Loglass, SoftBank, JDSC, Stockmark, and others are recruiting*11*12*13*15*16. There are also examples on the ordering side: Money Forward is recruiting for an internal AX promotion department under the title "Forward Deployed Engineer (Internal)"*14. This is an example of distinguishing the internal-facing nature in the title itself.

Q. Can you become an FDE without prior experience?

The reality is that it is difficult starting from a state with no practical engineering experience. Domestic public job postings set conditions such as "development experience as an engineer (2+ years)" and "5+ years of software engineering or technical implementation experience" as mandatory*12*13. On the other hand, few postings require prior experience under the FDE title, and experience in adjacent fields such as software development, AI and data utilization, IT consulting, system implementation at SIers, and technical project management is valued.

Q. What is the difference between FDE, RDE, and AI Operations Manager?

An FDE implements the company's own product in the field. An RDE (Reinvention Deployed Engineer) is a role created by Accenture, and the difference explained is that it is not tied to a specific product and supports transformation of business processes and organizations as well*19. An AI Operations Manager redesigns internal business processes with AI, and companies in Japan are beginning to establish it as a position*20. All share the aspect of "getting into the field and making things work," but differ in their distance from the product and the scope they cover.

Q. If we hire one FDE in-house, will AI adoption progress?

First, a company on the ordering side can hire an FDE in the broad sense (an internal AI promotion lead). An FDE in the original definition is a role at the company that owns the product. With that premise, progress can be made by one person for a single business process or department. However, business design spanning multiple tools, horizontal rollout to other departments, and handover to internal staff cannot be completed by a single person. You need to decide in advance who will own that process.

Q. Even without anyone knowledgeable about AI in-house, will adoption progress if we leave it to a vendor's FDE?

If the product to be adopted is decided, you can entrust the vendor up to making it usable in the field. However, deciding which product to choose, designing workflows that span multiple tools, rollout to other departments, and handover to internal staff remain outside that scope. If there is no one in-house to receive the handover, things will not progress beyond the initially adopted workflow. It is more reliable to first narrow down to a single workflow, decide who will own the out-of-scope work, and then consult.
