HIPAA

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 1 min read

Summary

This article explains HIPAA and how it applies to protected health information (PHI) held by covered entities and their business associates, noting that health data in consumer apps falls outside PHI. For FDE interviews, it stresses tracing where PHI flows: ensure every vendor touching PHI, including hosted models, logging services and support tools, is covered by a business associate agreement, share only the minimum necessary, and keep PHI out of logs, traces and eval sets unless properly de-identified. It warns that a BAA covers only named services, and that de-identification legally requires Safe Harbor or expert determination, not simply dropping names.

Key points

  • HIPAA's Privacy, Security and Breach Notification Rules govern PHI under HHS.
  • PHI applies only within covered entities and business associates; consumer app data is not PHI.
  • Business associates sign BAAs and are directly liable for the Security Rule.
  • HHS does not certify vendors; BAAs cover only listed services and settings.
  • De-identification requires Safe Harbor or expert determination, not dropping the name column.

Full text

HIPAA is the US law under which HHS’s Privacy, Security and Breach Notification Rules govern protected health information (PHI) held by covered entities (health plans, health care clearinghouses, and health care providers that conduct standard transactions, such as claims, electronically) and by their business associates, the vendors that handle PHI for them (HHS, HIPAA for professionals). PHI is individually identifiable health information that a covered entity or business associate holds or transmits, a narrower category than personal data: the same health data in a consumer app outside that chain is not PHI. A business associate signs a business associate agreement and is directly liable for meeting the Security Rule. HHS does not certify vendors as HIPAA compliant.

In an FDE interview

OpenAI’s healthcare postings (San Francisco, New York and Seattle), as of September 2026, list building with safeguards for PHI and HIPAA among the role’s responsibilities. The weak answer says “we are HIPAA compliant” and stops.

A BAA with a cloud or model vendor covers only the services and settings it names, so check each new managed service against the vendor’s HIPAA-eligible list before PHI reaches it. And de-identified has a legal meaning: the Safe Harbor method or an expert determination (HHS de-identification guidance), not “we dropped the name column”.
