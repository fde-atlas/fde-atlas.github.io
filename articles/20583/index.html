What Is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? | Job Description and How to Become One

Original title: FDE（Forward Deployed Engineer）とは｜仕事内容・なり方

BizCodeX-FDE（FDEスクール） · 2019-04-08 · 8 min read

Summary

This introductory article from BizCodeX-FDE, a Japanese FDE training school, defines the Forward Deployed Engineer as someone whose goal is customer outcomes rather than delivery: entering the client's operations, structuring undefined problems, validating quickly with small prototypes, and feeding learnings back into the company's products and standards. It traces the role's origin to Palantir, cites a16z on why many AI projects stall before production, and notes that model providers like OpenAI and Anthropic now hire FDEs. It contrasts FDEs with SIer/contract developers, IT consultants, and product managers by their end goals, and outlines four required competencies plus the school's curriculum structure.

Key points

  • FDE work ends when the client's business measurably changes, not at delivery.
  • The role originated at Palantir, where Deltas build lasting improvement mechanisms with clients.
  • a16z says many AI projects die before production due to data and integration issues.
  • OpenAI and Anthropic now hire FDEs for discovery through production deployment.
  • FDEs combine domain, technical, communication, and problem-solving skills in one person.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a job title that is still unfamiliar in Japan. Where did it come from, why is it spreading now, and how does it differ from conventional work? We have organized this in diagrams.

Definition

The goal is not "delivery" but "customer outcomes."

  • 01 Enter the customer's actual workplace
  • 02 Structure undefined problems
  • 03 Validate small-scale with technology
  • 04 Take responsibility for customer outcomes and adoption
  • 05 Feed the insights gained back into your company's products, standards, and knowledge

An engineer who carries out these five steps as one continuous process is called an FDE.

This is the definition used in the BizCodeX-FDE materials. Since the scope of an FDE's responsibilities differs from company to company, it is defined in terms of actions.

Where can the work be said to be "done"?

  • Receiving a request
  • Working according to specifications
  • Being used in the field
  • The business changes and results can be measured

Conventional goal / FDE goal

Getting what you built to work is only an intermediate milestone. A milestone is only reached when users' work and decisions actually change. However, "taking responsibility for outcomes" does not mean doing every step yourself. When you find a problem blocking results, identifying its owner and getting them into a state where they can act is also part of an FDE's job.

Origin

It originated at Palantir in the United States.

よくある誤解は、Deltaをコンサルタントだと考えること。コンサルタントは一度きりの分析や提言をつくることが多いが、Deltaは顧客と一緒に、顧客が自分で改善し続けられる長期の仕組みをつくる。そのために、実際に製品を展開して成果を出す ── Palantirの社員は、そう説明しています[1]。

Why FDEs are needed

  • AIは、本番の手前で止まりやすい a16zは、AIプロジェクトの多くがデータの乱れや連携の難しさ、社内の担い手の不在から、本番環境に届く前に止まっていると指摘しています。[3]
  • AIを業務につなぐ、深い統合 AIエージェントを業務で働かせるには、社内のデータベースやAPI、業務の流れと安全につなぎ、文脈を与える必要がある。その役割を担う人として、a16zはFDEを挙げています。[2]
  • AIの会社自身が、FDEを置き始めた a16zは、OpenAIやAnthropicといったモデルの提供企業もFDEを採用していると書いています。OpenAIの求人票は、FDEの役割を「発見・技術的な範囲決め・システム設計・構築・本番展開」までと記しています。[2][4]
  • Generative AI adoption tends to stall at the prototype stage. Even if the technology is proven to work in a prototype, no one goes as far as deciding which business decisions to embed it in and whose work it will change and how.
  • It is a poor fit for the "write requirements and hand them off" model. With generative AI, even customers themselves do not yet know where it will be effective. It is hard to start with a process of compiling requirements and placing an order.
  • Business people and technical people are separated. To repeatedly revise prototypes while observing the flow of business decisions, inserting documents in between is too slow. It is faster for the builders to enter the field themselves.

Items 04-06 are BizCodeX-FDE's assessment (from the materials).

If customers cannot write requirements and builders are distant from the field, the only option is for the builders to enter the field.

Companies both overseas and in Japan are now looking for people who can take AI beyond prototypes and deliver it into production operations.

Compare

How does it differ from SIers, consultants, and PdMs?

The clearest difference is where you can say your work is done — the goal of the work. Not the distance from the customer, nor the novelty of the technology.

The stages of work each role covers

PdMs also cover the stage of "making it used," but the substance differs. PdMs make the product's usability and onboarding procedures ready for all customers, while FDEs embed it into that company's business, existing systems, and operational rules.

Goal of the work

  • SIer, SES, contracted development
  • Delivery of the assigned work according to specifications
  • IT consultant
  • Having recommendations accepted
  • PdM (Product Manager)
  • The product is used by many customers and delivers business results
  • FDE
  • The business of the customer in front of you changes and results can be measured

What is changed

  • SIer, SES, contracted development
  • The system as instructed
  • IT consultant
  • The customer's policies and plans (as recommendations)
  • PdM (Product Manager)
  • Your own product (the parts common to many customers)
  • FDE
  • One company's business (combining the product with custom development)

Approach to requirements

  • SIer, SES, contracted development
  • Follow the fixed requirements and instructions
  • IT consultant
  • Research and depict
  • PdM (Product Manager)
  • Decide based on feedback from many customers
  • FDE
  • Discover together with the customer through observation and validation

Whether you build it yourself

  • SIer, SES, contracted development
  • Build it
  • IT consultant
  • In principle, do not build
  • PdM (Product Manager)
  • In principle, do not build (the development team builds)
  • FDE
  • Build small things yourself and verify by running them

Where the learning goes

  • SIer, SES, contracted development
  • Remains as individual experience
  • IT consultant
  • Remains in the company as methodology
  • PdM (Product Manager)
  • Feeds into product planning
  • FDE
  • Is fed back into your company's products, standards, and knowledge

This is not about superiority or inferiority. The goal of SES work is executing tasks because that is what the contract stipulates. For work where the tasks are fixed and there is a labor shortage, that is the most rational form. That field experience becomes the foundation of an FDE.

Being resident on-site is not a requirement. Sitting at the customer's site while only doing assigned tasks is the traditional way of working; working remotely while taking responsibility for the customer's outcomes and iterating on validation is the FDE way of working.

Work cycle

An FDE's work does not proceed in one direction.

  • 01 Learn the customer's reality
  • 02 Decide what to change
  • 03 Build it fully, from prototype to production
  • 04 Make it actually used
  • 05 Measure the results and feed them back into the next decision

Every time results are measured, the initial understanding is re-examined. And the lessons learned from each engagement are fed back into the company's products and standards, making the next customer's ramp-up faster. This is not work that gets used up one engagement at a time.

Same request, different first move

Even with the same request, the first move is different.

"Our sales team is spending too much time on daily reports. Could we use generative AI to create them automatically? We want to roll it out to the whole sales department within six months."

Sales director at a parts manufacturer (example)

  • What to check first — Traditional: the report's fields, screens, and output format / FDE: confirming with the director that "what would count as success" has not yet been defined
  • What to investigate next — Traditional: generative AI products and a structure for generating reports / FDE: accompanying sales reps on client visits to find out who reads the reports and why
  • What you have in hand before starting to build — Traditional: a requirements specification and a six-month development plan / FDE: the verified facts and the criteria for success
  • What you build first — Traditional: an auto-generation feature built exactly to the requirements spec / FDE: a small prototype of the option chosen based on the facts

* These are teaching examples. The companies and requests are fictional, created for illustration, and the traditional approach shown is not a strawman. The difference lies in which is investigated first: "how to build it" or "what it is for."

Skills

Combining four competencies, alone.

Each of these competencies exists in other professions too. What makes an FDE distinctive is running the whole sequence — listening to problems in the field, redefining the problem, and validating it with a prototype the same day — without dividing the roles among people. You don't need all four from the start.

  • DOMAIN Domain knowledge: understanding the industry's language and business flows, who makes which decisions, and getting closer to the real reason behind the problem
  • TECH Technical skill: combining data, APIs, and generative AI to build, with your own hands and in a short time, a prototype that can validate what needs validating
  • COMMUNICATION Communication skill: hearing the circumstances behind the words, explaining technical constraints in the other person's language, and presenting decisions in a form that makes them easy to make
  • PROBLEM SOLVING Problem-solving skill: stepping back from the requested solution to redefine the problem, forming hypotheses, and validating them in small steps
