Full text

Serving as the "bridge-builder" between large models and factories: Shanghai proposes a Forward Deployed Engineer training program to build a reserve of versatile talent

$4 billion could buy tens of thousands of NVIDIA compute cards or hire large numbers of the world's top AI scientists, yet OpenAI recently used it to acquire a company whose core asset is a team of 150 Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs).

Forward Deployed Engineers are also a strategic resource that US AI companies such as Anthropic and Google are betting on heavily. Data from LinkedIn, a well-known career-focused social platform, shows that global demand for Forward Deployed Engineer positions has surged more than 40-fold over the past two years.

This hottest job in Silicon Valley also exists in Shanghai.

According to the reporter's sources, Professor Li Jinjin, director of the Artificial Intelligence and Microstructure Laboratory at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, was the first to introduce the Forward Deployed Engineer concept to Shanghai and pushed for the launch of the nation's first training program. Meanwhile, the company she founded, Jinpeng Technology, currently has 30 Forward Deployed Engineers—the first team in China to have successfully run through dozens of scenarios in both process manufacturing and discrete manufacturing, achieving large-scale deployment of AI models in industry.

Bringing AI to the Industrial Front Line

The concept of the Forward Deployed Engineer was invented more than 20 years ago by the US data company Palantir. Back then, because clients' data was highly confidential yet they could not articulate their specific needs, Palantir simply "sent down" its most expensive engineers to the client's site.

More than 20 years later, the pain points of enterprises persist. The core reason this niche profession of Forward Deployed Engineer has come back into the spotlight is that AI models are getting stronger, yet model deployment is not increasing accordingly. A report from the MIT Media Lab, "The Generative AI Gap," shows that in 2025, 95% of companies worldwide investing in generative AI saw zero returns.

This creates a chasm that requires a "bridge-builder" rooted on-site—modifying code, running validation, relaying messages between both sides, and coordinating integration.

The Forward Deployed Engineer is exactly that role.

Ren Yifei, born after 2000, is a representative Forward Deployed Engineer at Jinpeng Technology. A graduate of the Electronic Information program at the University of Shanghai for Science and Technology, she joined in 2025 as Professor Li Jinjin's assistant, often grabbing her suitcase and heading off at a moment's notice to Xinjiang, Inner Mongolia, Ningxia, and other places.

The first project this 26-year-old deployed was for the listed company Chuanning Biotech: the world's largest single-unit fermentation tank for antibiotic intermediates, located in Xinjiang. Fermentation is an enormously complex process involving hundreds of variables and lasting nearly two weeks. In the past, it relied on veteran masters on 24-hour shifts, turning valves, adjusting instruments, and tuning parameters based on experience. Five tons of finished product was the output ceiling of the 500-ton fermentation tank.

One of her core tasks is to feed these industrial data and tacit experience to AI as much as possible, and to ensure that the semantics of the data are fully aligned with the AI's understanding—this is the key to whether the model can be deployed precisely.

When discussing the pH value at a certain stage of fermentation, the veteran master said, "Anywhere from 4 to 6 works," but she insisted on pressing further: what states do 4, 5, and 6 correspond to respectively? Worried the master might find her annoying, she proactively helped buy meals and assisted with tasks, all to extract a few more details.

These follow-up questions are crucial. Li Jinjin told the reporter, "The corpus determines the upper limit of a model's capability. Factories always complain that golden batches in fermentation (referring to a batch whose metrics are significantly better than other batches under the same process) are a matter of mystique. But our practice proves that the finer, more multi-dimensional, and more accurate the data you feed in, the better the AI can extract high-dimensional features and solutions, making every fermentation batch a golden batch."

After repeated feeding, alignment, and tuning, the "Biological Fermentation AI Engineer" successfully predicted the fermentation curve for the next several hundred hours with 99.9% accuracy. The chairman of Chuanning Biotech made the decision on the spot: let AI take over the fermentation tank.

A month later, production capacity increased by 5%—more than the total progress of the previous five years.

One Person Facing "Thousands of Troops"

To date, Ren Yifei has been deeply involved in and stationed at 7 projects, covering fields such as biological fermentation, water treatment, and petrochemicals, with clients that are either industry giants or listed companies.

Ren Yifei has many times held meetings and coordinated deployments with hundreds or even thousands of people in clients' vast conference rooms. Down on the actual production line, she also has to deal with all kinds of complex and unexpected situations.

Palantir has a classic description—an ordinary engineer uses one capability to serve all clients, while a Forward Deployed Engineer uses all capabilities for one client.

Indeed, the Forward Deployed Engineer role carries great value, condensing the roles of algorithm engineer, software engineer, pre-sales consultant, product manager, Solutions Architect, and more into a single person.

The reporter learned from the AI Division of the Shanghai Municipal Commission of Economy and Informatization that, at the city level, Forward Deployed Engineer talent is still in the cultivation stage.

A few companies have spontaneously "grown" the Forward Deployed Engineer paradigm. For example, at Shiyuan Technology, the team collects anomaly samples beside circuit board production lines during the day and returns to the lab to iterate on models at night, cutting circuit board quality inspection changeover and tuning time from several hours to as fast as 55 seconds. Meanwhile, digital business engineers at ZPMC have collected large amounts of multimodal data from gas-shielded welding sites and are developing an "in-welding defect AI recognition" model.

To accelerate the resolution of the model deployment bottleneck, Shanghai has clearly defined a "3+3+3" Forward Deployed Engineer talent training program, targeting three groups:

First, leaders of state-owned enterprises and government departments, aiming to enhance strategic awareness and decision-making capabilities; second, existing industry engineers, driving them to transform into Forward Deployed Engineers with the combined capabilities of "business insight + consulting diagnosis + engineering implementation"; third, universities, continuously integrating the Forward Deployed Engineer concept into university curricula to build a lasting talent reserve.
