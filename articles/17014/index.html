To answer the question "We want to adopt AI, but we don't know which company can be trusted through implementation," we organized 38 companies in Japan offering FDE-style support into 7 categories, and published them for free with standardized official information on launch dates, case studies, and pricing.

"AI Agent Navi" (https://aiagent-navi.com/), an AI agent-focused media outlet operated by Marketing Planner, Inc. (headquartered in Minato-ku, Tokyo), has released the "FDE Provider Companies Chaos Map," which organizes Japanese companies offering "FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)"-style support that handles AI implementation in the field. The map features 38 companies across 7 categories. For each company, three items — "launch date," "presence of case studies," and "pricing/contract terms" — are listed based solely on statements from official websites and press releases.

Background of the Creation

AI is no longer "install and done" — implementation in the field is becoming the norm

Generative AI and AI agents are not tools that deliver results simply by being introduced; outcomes depend on tailored customization and ongoing support until adoption takes hold. This gave rise to "FDE," a service model in which engineers embed themselves at the client's site and take responsibility for everything from design and implementation to adoption. In 2026, Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS, Microsoft, and Salesforce successively launched FDE-style organizations and programs, and the number of provider companies in Japan has grown rapidly.

From the buyer's perspective, the single word "FDE" is not enough to tell them apart

When we examined communications from Japanese companies using the term "FDE," we found the following three things all described with the same word:

  • Companies that can be engaged externally
  • Companies still at the stage of building internal FDE organizations or recruiting
  • Training services for developing FDE talent

It takes time for buyers to distinguish between these. Moreover, most companies do not publish pricing or on-site days, making side-by-side comparison impossible.

Therefore, we are releasing a map that includes only offerings actually available to external companies, with each company's official statements placed on the same yardstick.

Features of the "FDE Provider Companies Chaos Map"

1. Seven categories show "how each company enters the field"

Whether a company handles implementation without being tied to a specific product, or assumes adoption of its own product, greatly affects flexibility after engagement. So we organized the 38 companies into 7 categories based on how they enter the field.

  • Implementation-embedded type: 11 companies
  • Consulting-support type: 7 companies
  • Own-product-based type: 7 companies
  • Enterprise type: 4 companies
  • Industry-specialized type: 4 companies
  • Certified partner program type: 1 entry
  • Talent-matching type: 4 companies

2. Only offerings actually available to external companies are listed

Communications from companies using the term "FDE" also include "launching internal FDE organizations or recruiting" and "talent development programs." Our listing criterion is "actually available to external companies." We listed only the 38 entries for which this could be confirmed on official service pages or press releases. The 10 companies for which FDE-style service offerings could not be confirmed are introduced separately as "under observation," and 15 entries such as development and training programs are excluded from the listing.

3. Search each company's "official statements" by keywords like "embedded," "monthly fee," and "Claude"

The search covers company names, each company's characteristics, and quotations from official pages. You can start searching by engagement terms (embedded, weekly), cost terms (monthly fee, success-based fees), AI product names in use (Claude, Salesforce, etc.), or familiar company names (SoftBank, NTT, etc.). No tags have been added by us based on speculation.

4. Verify each company's "three items" with the original text from official pages

For all 38 companies, we added the following three items based solely on statements from official websites and press releases ("—" if not officially stated).

  • Launch date: the month the FDE-style service was announced or launched
  • Case studies: customer-facing implementation case studies; "case study available" links to the case study page
  • Pricing/contract terms: officially published pricing and contract terms as-is

The image below shows an example comparing a card for the implementation-embedded type not tied to a specific product (SHIFT) and the own-product-based type (LayerX). The highlighted portions show the difference between the two: whether continued use of that product is assumed after adoption. We do not judge which is better; that is left to the buyer.

5. Filter with one tap by "case study available" and "pricing published"

The chaos map includes two buttons, allowing you to instantly filter down to the 15 companies that officially publish case studies and the 11 companies that publish pricing/contract terms. Only 6 of the 38 companies meet both criteria.

Findings from the Survey (as of August 2026)

The launch of FDE-style services is concentrated in this summer.

  • 21 companies officially announced or launched services in the last three months (June–August 2026)
  • By month, August had the most with 8 companies; only 1 company had been confirmed before 2025
  • On the other hand, only 4 companies publish actual pricing, and only 6 have case studies with named customers

While launches are surging, publication of information to support purchasing decisions is still to come.
