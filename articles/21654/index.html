An IT strategist is an upstream-process specialist who plays the role of bridging business strategy and IT strategy. Specifically, the job involves designing and proposing what IT systems and digital strategies are needed to achieve a company's business goals.

It is often confused with IT architects and PMO, which are also called "upstream processes," but the focus of the role differs.

RoleMain focusExample deliverables
IT StrategistIntegrating business strategy and IT strategyMid-term IT plans, DX roadmaps
IT ArchitectOverall technical design of systemsArchitecture design documents, technical standards
PMOProject promotion and managementProgress management, governance development
IT ConsultantProblem solving and general proposalsProposals, business improvement reports

Among the above, IT strategists are deeply involved in strategic decision-making, particularly on "why that IT is needed." There is demand for professionals who can engage in direct dialogue with CEOs and CFOs and formulate 3-to-5-year mid-term IT plans.

In addition, the IT Strategist exam defined by the Information-technology Promotion Agency, Japan (IPA) is the only national certification that officially recognizes this profession. With a pass rate of around 15-16% each year, it is highly difficult, and simply holding the certification makes it easier to gain market trust.

To sum up the role of a freelance IT strategist in one phrase: "an interpreter who helps executives understand 'IT language,' and a designer who enables IT departments to act in 'business language.'" The number of people who can serve as this bridge is extremely small across Japan, and this scarcity translates into high rates.

The number of freelance IT strategist projects has been increasing rapidly in recent years. Aggregating published data from major freelance agencies, as of January 2026, an estimated 2,000 to 3,000 projects across the industry feature keywords such as "IT strategist," "IT strategy," and "DX roadmap," showing growth of roughly 30-40% compared to 2024.

Project types fall broadly into three categories.

① DX strategy formulation and roadmap creation These are projects where IT strategists are hired as the command center for digital transformation (DX) promotion, regardless of industry—manufacturing, retail, finance, healthcare, and more. Project durations typically run 3 to 12 months, with monthly rates of 1 to 1.5 million yen being the norm.

② Generative AI adoption and utilization strategy These are projects involving strategy formulation for how to leverage large language models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini in one's own business. They have surged since 2025, requiring not only AI literacy but also the ability to design business applications. Monthly rates remain in the high range of 1.2 to 1.8 million yen.

③ IT governance and information security strategy These are consulting projects to formulate company-wide IT governance policies from the perspective of strengthening corporate governance and addressing information leak risks. There is strong demand from listed companies and major group companies, and these projects tend to lead to long-term engagements.

The main clients of freelance IT strategists can be broadly divided into the following three types.

1. Mid-sized to large companies that have just established DX promotion offices These are companies that have established a CDO (Chief Digital Officer) or DX promotion office in-house but lack personnel who can handle the actual work. Particularly in manufacturing, retail, healthcare, and the public sector, IT hiring cannot keep up, and cases of hiring external IT strategists on contract are rapidly increasing.

2. Startups and growth companies preparing for IPO These are companies that need to develop IT systems and formulate information security policies ahead of listing. Beyond a CTO or VPoE, they seek specialists who support IT decision-making at the executive level. Project durations are relatively short, but rates tend to be high.

3. Consulting Firms and System Integrators (SIers) Major consulting firms and independent SIers sometimes hire IT strategists with specialized knowledge of specific industries on a contract basis. English proficiency is an advantage when joining projects at foreign consulting firms.

Reason 1: DX investment is shifting to the "strategy formulation phase" Many companies have moved from the "execution phase" of DX to a "phase of reviewing and advancing strategy," and demand is growing for high-level design based on lessons learned from failed PoCs (proofs of concept). There is strong demand for professionals who can support the shift from "digitalization for its own sake" to "DX that drives business results."

Reason 2: Demand for "integration of generative AI into business strategy" Beyond simply implementing AI tools, needs are rapidly expanding for strategic design on how to incorporate AI into a company's competitive advantage. IT strategists who understand both technology and management are frequently the top choice in this situation.

Reason 3: Response to the "2025 Cliff" issue continues The "2025 Cliff" (the aging of legacy systems) warned about by IPA (Information-technology Promotion Agency) remains an ongoing challenge for many companies as of 2026. Demand for IT strategists capable of leading core system renewal strategies is expected to continue for the foreseeable future.

Working as a company employee offers the advantage of a stable base salary, social insurance, and retirement benefits. On the other hand, raises tend to depend on internal evaluations, and even if your skills far exceed your market value, this is often not reflected in your compensation.

It is not uncommon for an IT strategist earning 8 million yen annually as a full-time employee to command a monthly rate of 1 million yen (equivalent to 11 million yen annually) after going independent. In particular, professionals with strengths in the DX × generative AI area continue to command high rates.

Here is an objective summary of the pros and cons of going independent.

What you gain

  • Higher income tied to market evaluation (skills directly determine your rate)
  • Freedom to choose your own projects and clients
  • Income diversification by working on multiple projects in parallel
  • Tax benefits through expense deductions and incorporation

What you lose

  • Monthly stable salary, bonuses, and retirement benefits
  • Company-provided training and certification support
  • The sense of belonging from working in a team within a large organization
  • Risk of income gaps during periods between projects (intervals)

Most cases where new freelancers feel "anxiety outweighs earning power" stem from insufficient preparation before going independent. In particular, establishing a pipeline for continuous project acquisition and securing working capital are the most critical challenges.

Analyzing high-rate projects of 1.3 million yen or more per month on major agents and platforms reveals the following common requirements.

1. Combining industry knowledge with IT strategy These projects require both deep understanding of a specific industry and IT strategy formulation, such as "designing a DX roadmap for the financial industry" or "renewal strategy for manufacturing production control systems." Industry-specialized experts command higher rates than generalist consultants.

2. Track record of direct communication with executives Projects requiring experience interacting directly with CxOs and board members tend to pay higher rates. It is important to clearly state on your skill sheet "experience making direct proposals to the CEO, CFO, and CDO."

3. Knowledge of generative AI and the latest cloud technologies In addition to the traditional IT strategist profile, having experience in strategy design using generative AI and cloud services such as ChatGPT/Claude/AWS/Azure increasingly adds a 20–40% premium to rates.

4. English proficiency (global projects) Projects at foreign companies and Japanese global companies require English communication and documentation skills. With a TOEIC score of 800 or higher or a track record of business-level English communication, the range of projects you can apply for expands significantly.

IT strategists are "strategists," not "engineers," but a certain level of technical understanding is essential to make proposals persuasive. The most important technical knowledge areas as of 2026 are as follows.

Technical areaRequired levelReason
Cloud (AWS/Azure/GCP)Conceptual understanding to design levelEssential for formulating cloud migration strategies
Generative AI/LLM utilizationPrompt design to understanding of API integrationRequests for AI strategy formulation are rapidly increasing
Data utilization and analytics platformsConceptual understanding of data lakes and BI toolsFormulating data-driven management strategies
Security and governanceBasic knowledge of ISMS and NIST CSFTo handle security strategy formulation projects
Agile and DevOpsConceptual understanding of Scrum and CI/CDDigital organization design and development structure development

"You don't need to be able to implement technical details, but you must be able to discuss them as an equal with the engineering team"—this is the benchmark for the technical understanding required of freelance IT strategists.

In particular, in the generative AI field, as of 2026, demand is rapidly growing for IT strategists who can answer the question of "how to strategically position AI-driven business transformation." Professionals who understand the mechanisms of LLMs and the basic concepts of APIs, and who can translate that knowledge for the business side, have become highly scarce and valuable.

Business skills are the core value-providing area for IT strategists. The following three points are especially important.

① Ability to leverage business strategy frameworks It is essential to master frameworks such as 3C analysis, SWOT, the Balanced Scorecard (BSC), and value chain analysis, and to be able to explain IT strategy in terms of its linkage to management goals. This is an area where those from consulting firms have an advantage, but it can also be acquired through self-study and practical exercises in applying frameworks.

② Ability to calculate finances and return on investment (ROI) IT investment decisions require financial explanations such as "if we spend X hundred million yen on this system, how many years will it take to recover the investment?" Being able to make proposals to executives using concepts such as NPV, IRR, and TCO greatly increases your credibility.

③ Industry-specific understanding of business models It is a prerequisite to understand the business models, regulatory environments, and competitive structures of the industries your client companies belong to, such as finance, manufacturing, retail, healthcare, and the public sector. Experience in multiple industries enables you to apply for higher-priced projects.

An IT strategist's job is not "writing proposals," but "moving the management team with IT." For this reason, the following communication skills greatly influence actual evaluations.

  • Executive presentations: The ability to structure a message that conveys the core points to management within 5 minutes
  • Workshop facilitation: The ability to design sessions that draw out issues from cross-departmental teams
  • Stakeholder management: The negotiation skills to align the interests of IT departments, business units, and executives
  • Writing skills: The ability to logically write management reports, IT strategy documents, and proposal materials

As a freelancer, you will need to clearly present these skills as self-promotional material. Including concrete achievements on your skill sheet, such as "served as facilitator for Company X's DX promotion committee and gained approval at the executive meeting," directly leads to winning projects.

High priority: IT Strategist Exam (IPA) This is Japan's only national qualification for IT strategists. With a pass rate of about 15-16%, it is a difficult exam, but it works greatly in your favor when negotiating rates as a freelancer. Since it conveys the trust of "holders need no further proof," the ideal is to obtain it before going independent.

High priority: PMP (Project Management Professional) When involved in the execution phase of IT strategy, project management qualifications are highly valued. It has international recognition and is advantageous for global projects.

Medium priority: AWS Certified Solutions Architect / Azure Solutions Architect These certifications prove your ability to plan and evaluate cloud strategies. They are effective when applying for the latest projects involving "cloud migration strategy" and "multi-cloud policies."

Medium priority: Small and Medium Enterprise Management Consultant This qualification is highly recognized as a general business strategy credential and provides differentiation in DX support projects for small and mid-sized enterprises. It also builds tax and finance knowledge, which is useful for running your own freelance business.

Before going independent as a freelancer, be sure to prepare the following three things. If these are insufficient, you face a high risk of falling into a situation after independence where you "can't win projects" or "your rates are low."

① Articulating your track record and completing your "skill sheet" Your greatest weapon when going freelance is your past work achievements. However, vague expressions like "I promoted DX at a major company" will not be evaluated. You need specificity at this level: "At Company A in the X industry (annual revenue on the scale of X billion yen), formulated a three-year IT medium-term plan and gained executive approval for the DX roadmap; secured an investment budget of X hundred million yen through ROI estimates."

② Securing 6 months of living and business expenses It can take anywhere from 1-2 weeks to 2-3 months to land your first project after going independent. Also, even while working, payment often arrives at the end of the following month or the month after that, temporarily tightening your cash flow. It is safe to go independent only after keeping at least 6 months' worth of living and business expenses (PC, communication costs, accounting software, etc.) in cash on hand.

③ Lining up your first 1-2 projects The safest patterns for going independent are either to continue receiving work from your current employer as a contracted contractor, or to receive informal project introductions from an agent before resigning. If you "resign first and then register with an agent," finding projects takes time and the mental and financial pressure becomes greater.

The main channels through which freelance IT strategists win projects are the following four.

① Freelance agents (recommendation: ★★★★★) They handle rate negotiation and contract procedures on your behalf, making them especially effective in the early stages of independence. Agents specializing in consultants and high-skill talent hold higher-quality projects compared to those for general engineers. Margin rates are typically around 10-20%.

For how to choose an agent, the following article is helpful. See: Comparison of Agents for Freelance Consultants

② Referrals and personal networks (recommendation: ★★★★★) Introductions from former colleagues, clients, and industry contacts are the project acquisition route most likely to lead to high rates and long-term continuity. It is strongly recommended to consciously build your personal network before going independent and to polish your LinkedIn profile.

③ Contract work matching platforms (recommendation: ★★★☆☆) These are platforms such as CrowdWorks, Lancers, Bizreach Freelance, and Finds, where you can apply directly to projects online. Compared to agents, margins are lower so your take-home rate is higher, but be aware that project quality varies.

④ Own Media and Social Media Publishing (Recommended: ★★★☆☆) Posting technical and strategy articles on LinkedIn and sharing IT strategy insights on a personal blog are effective for long-term brand building. As direct inquiries bring in more projects, the proportion of high-rate projects without going through agents increases.

To maintain high rates long-term as a freelance IT strategist, a position shift from "an IT strategist who can do everything" to "an expert in a specific industry × specific theme" is essential.

By defining yourself through combinations of "industry × theme" such as the following, differentiation from competitors becomes clear, leading to being directly requested for projects.

IndustryExamples of themes to strengthenExpected rate range
Finance and insuranceCore system renewal, digital banking strategy1.3–1.8 million yen/month
ManufacturingSmart factory, SCM optimization1.0–1.5 million yen/month
Retail and e-commerceOmnichannel, personalization strategy0.9–1.4 million yen/month
HealthcareElectronic medical record integration, PHR utilization strategy1.0–1.5 million yen/month
Public sector and local governmentDigital Agency collaboration, government DX strategy0.9–1.3 million yen/month

The optimal criteria for selecting a specialty area are choosing one that meets all three conditions: "large market size," "overlaps with your past experience," and "expected to grow over the next five years."

Once you accumulate track records on 3–4 projects in a single industry, word-of-mouth begins to spread within that industry as "the IT strategist for that field." Reaching this phase means requests start coming in directly without going through agents, leading to a significant increase in take-home rates.

Timing and Methods for Rate Negotiation For your first project, accepting a rate slightly below market to "build a track record first" is one option, but once you have accumulated achievements, it is important to proactively request rate revisions. The most effective timing for negotiation is "1–2 months before contract renewal." Negotiate by presenting concrete grounds, such as: "The current market rate is around X million yen, and considering my additional contributions (achievements in X), would you consider revising my rate to Y million yen?"

Keys to Turning Projects into Ongoing Engagements To minimize "project gaps," the biggest risk for freelancers, the following actions are effective.

  • Send monthly activity reports to management to continuously "make your work visible"
  • Propose additional themes mid-engagement to naturally expand your scope
  • Create opportunities such as internal study sessions and briefings so that internal stakeholders become aware of your performance

Obtaining Word-of-Mouth and Recommendations After a project ends, make it a habit to request LinkedIn Recommendations from the executive, CDO, or CIO you worked with. Evidence such as "delivered X results in a project at my previous company" has an enormous effect on rate negotiations and building trust for your next project.

A. There is no clear difference in industry definitions, but the focus of the role differs. An IT consultant's main role is "proposals and execution support for solving problems," centered on addressing specific projects or issues. On the other hand, an IT strategist's main role is "integrating mid- to long-term IT policies and investment plans into business strategy," involving more senior decision-making. In the context of freelance projects, both are often recruited interchangeably, and in practice, judgment is based on skill sets and track records.

A. Common failure patterns include the following three.

① Dilution of expertise: With an "I'll do anything" attitude, it is difficult to win high-rate projects, and your rate keeps declining. It is important to narrow down your industry and theme to establish your position as a specialist.

② High dependence on a single project: Relying on just one client carries the risk of your income instantly dropping to zero when the contract ends. Aim for a portfolio-style working approach with multiple clients and projects running in parallel.

③ Neglecting information dissemination and network building: If you remain passive, projects will dry up. Continuous information dissemination through LinkedIn, seminar speaking engagements, and regularly refreshing your personal network are the lifeline of a long-term freelance career.

The freelance market for IT strategists continues to generate high-rate projects, supported by strong demand drivers such as DX, generative AI, and cloud migration as of 2026. Here is a summary of the key points from this article.

  • Monthly rates for freelance IT strategists center on 0.8–1.6 million yen, with top earners achieving over 2 million yen
  • Demand for specialists in the DX × generative AI domain continues into 2026, with an upward trend in rates
  • Obtaining the IT Strategist Examination (IPA) certification provides a major advantage in rate negotiations
  • Preparation before going independent (articulating your track record, working capital, and securing your first project) is the key to a successful transition
  • Narrowing your specialty to a specific industry × theme helps you establish a position that leads to direct requests, high rates, and ongoing projects
