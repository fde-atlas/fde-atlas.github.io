FDE vs AI Engineer: Two Paths Between Customer Sites and Product Teams

Original title: FDE vs AI Engineer：客户现场与产品团队的两条路

FDE中国 · 2026-08-14 · 4 min read

Summary

This article compares the FDE and AI Engineer career paths using a comparison table. Both roles write AI code with overlapping tech stacks, but differ in where requirements come from: FDEs dig real business problems out of customer sites, while AI Engineers work from product plans and metrics. Success definitions diverge too—FDEs are accountable for customer business metrics, AI Engineers for product metrics like retention and performance—which changes technical choices, e.g. RAG work on-site must handle data staying inside private networks. Experience compounds differently: industry knowledge for FDEs versus technical depth for AI Engineers. The author advises choosing by situation: junior engineers benefit from the more standard AI Engineer path, while those with domain expertise or several years of engineering experience gain more by moving on-site.

Key points

  • FDEs answer for customer business metrics; AI Engineers answer for product metrics.
  • Requirements differ: on-site interviews versus product roadmaps and data.
  • Same RAG work involves different metrics and pitfalls in each role.
  • FDE experience compounds into industry knowledge; AI Engineer into technical depth.
  • Engineers with domain background or years of experience gain more from FDE roles.

Introduction

FDEs and AI Engineers both write AI-related code using highly overlapping tech stacks, yet they do two different kinds of work: one brings model capabilities into a customer's business, the other builds model capabilities into a product. This article uses a comparison table to break down the differences between the two paths, offering a reference for those standing at the fork. For an overview of how FDEs differ from other roles, see Section 4 of the Beginner's Guide.

Three Key Differences Explained

Difference 1: Where requirements come from determines what the work looks like

AI Engineers' requirements are relatively structured: requirement documents, metrics, iteration schedules, with reviews for changes and buffers in timelines. FDEs' requirements live inside the customer's business and must be unearthed through interviews and observation—"why is this approval step slow" is often closer to the real problem than "build us a large language model application." People who crave certainty will find on-site work agonizing, while people who love concrete problems may feel that product requirements are one step removed.

Difference 2: Different definitions of success lead to different technical choices

FDEs' technical choices revolve around customer constraints: whether data can leave the intranet, how to integrate legacy systems, how to align evals with acceptance criteria. AI Engineers' choices revolve around the product: balancing latency, cost, and effectiveness at scale. Even with the same RAG work, the metrics each side cares about and the pitfalls they commonly hit are different, and experience cannot be directly transferred.

Difference 3: Different directions of compounding experience

FDEs accumulate industry and scenario knowledge: the workflows of this type of customer, the quirks of that type of data—the compounding lands on "understanding the business"; AI Engineers accumulate engineering and model depth, and the compounding lands on "technical height." Three to five years later, the two paths lead to different destinations such as industry solutions lead and AI architect, and switching costs rise with seniority. To judge which path suits you, consider which kind of meeting you'd rather spend time in: a customer's business process review, or your own product's technical design review.

How to Choose: Advice by Situation

Advice by situation: if you're a fresh graduate or still shallow in engineering depth, the AI Engineer path is more standardized—build your foundation first, then go on-site; if you have several years of backend or full-stack experience and want to amplify business impact, FDE offers a larger scope of responsibility and greater scarcity; if you already have accumulated expertise in a particular industry (such as manufacturing, healthcare, or finance), FDE lets that industry knowledge directly become your competitive edge. The two paths are not one-way streets: on-site experience flowing back into product teams is a channel deliberately designed by many companies.

Summary and Further Reading

In one sentence: AI Engineers build models into products, FDEs build models into customers' business processes. Both paths exist for the long term; the difference lies in which kind of problem you want to fill your days.
