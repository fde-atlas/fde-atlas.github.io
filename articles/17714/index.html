The Truth About Remote FDE Roles: Visa Sponsorship and Relocation Support

Original title: FDE 远程岗位真相：签证担保与搬迁支持

FDE Times · 2026-10-08 · 5 min read

Summary

Comparing five FDE job postings, this article argues that the "Remote" label is the least reliable field in a listing. An Istari Digital posting marked Remote actually requires onsite work at customer teams, up to 50% travel, and US citizenship without dual nationality. Meanwhile, Anthropic, OpenAI, and Palantir require office presence and 25%-50% customer travel but offer visa sponsorship, relocation support, or clear paths in. For Vietnamese engineers, the practical entry point is labs that explicitly bring foreigners over, not postings tagged Remote.

Key points

  • Istari Digital's "Remote" FDE role actually demands onsite customer work and US citizenship without dual nationality.
  • All five postings require roughly 25%-50% customer travel; none allow fully remote work.
  • Anthropic sponsors visas for some roles; OpenAI's UAE role offers relocation support with 3 office days weekly.
  • Palantir's Oslo role requires onsite work, 25%-50% travel preference, and local language skills.
  • Advice: read postings bottom-up, screen for visa sponsorship and relocation, and highlight onsite deployment experience.

Key Takeaways

  • An FDE job posting from Istari Digital is labeled "Remote," yet requires working on-site at customer locations, up to 50% travel, and mandates being a US citizen without dual nationality.
  • Anthropic and OpenAI explicitly do not offer remote work: at least 25% of the time in the office or 3 days per week on-site, with 25%-50% customer travel. But Anthropic offers visa sponsorship, and OpenAI offers relocation support.
  • For Vietnamese engineers, the word "Remote" matters far less than "Am I even eligible to apply?" The entry point is the labs willing to bring people over.

Istari Digital has a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) job posting labeled "Remote," with "International" even in the job title. Read further, and the picture is completely different: the hire works on-site embedded within customer teams, travels up to 50% of the time, including to customers in Australia, and must be a US citizen without dual nationality.

For engineers in Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City, the "Remote" at the top of the posting means nothing. Many people overlook this when searching for FDE roles in Vietnam: in a job posting, the work-arrangement tag is the least reliable field. What actually matters is where you must be, how much you travel, and whether the law allows you to apply.

Put enough postings side by side and a somewhat counterintuitive pattern emerges: the biggest AI labs make no remote commitments at all, yet the opportunities for foreign engineers there are more realistic. So if you only click on jobs labeled "Remote" while scrolling LinkedIn, you may be looking in the wrong direction.

"Remote" is a label, not a condition

The Istari posting exposes the trap clearly. The "Remote" tag does not mean you can work from anywhere. The work still happens on-site at customers, and you spend nearly half your time on the road.

The biggest barrier is the citizenship line. Requiring a US citizen without dual nationality effectively excludes nearly all overseas engineers—no matter how strong your resume. No skill can compensate for that line.

So a "Remote" job can cost you several evenings writing cover letters, with no chance from start to finish. The tag at the top promises freedom; the conditions at the bottom shut you out.

The labs say it plainly: you must be there

Anthropic's postings take the opposite approach, candid to the point of being almost uncomfortable. The Munich FDE role posted in April 2026 requires frequent travel to customers, roughly 25%-50% of the time, to work directly with them, along with regular office attendance.

The roles at the San Francisco, New York, and Seattle offices posted in August 2026 estimate travel at roughly 25%, depending on location.

The US postings also spell out Anthropic's overall policy: all employees, including FDEs, must spend at least 25% of their time in one of the company's offices. That is hybrid work, not remote. But the same posting contains a line the Istari posting does not: "We sponsor visas!"

Read that sentence carefully: Anthropic provides visa sponsorship for some roles, not all roles, and not all candidates.

OpenAI's direction is the same. Its FDE role in the UAE is tied to a specific city, uses a hybrid model of 3 days per week in the office, involves up to 50% travel, and offers relocation support. In other words, OpenAI does not expect you to work from home. It wants you to move to where it is, and is willing to help you get there.

Palantir is a third case—the company that created the FDE role in the first place. Its Oslo FDE role for a Norwegian government project is marked on-site, meaning working in the office, with preference for candidates who can travel 25%-50%. It also requires proficiency in the local language. This is a different kind of barrier than citizenship, but it is just as effective at keeping Vietnamese engineers out.

Five postings, one pattern

Put the five postings together along three key pieces of information, and the picture looks very different from reading each one alone:

The middle column of these five postings is nearly identical: every one requires roughly a quarter to half of the time traveling to customers. What truly sets them apart is the last column. The one labeled "Remote" is the only role with a barrier that individual effort cannot overcome.

The labs require you to be there, but they also give you a path to being there.

Why do all these roles require on-site presence?

There is a reason the travel column is so consistent. Anthropic describes travel as a way to build side by side with customers, while the Istari posting says the hire sits inside customer teams to understand their problems.

DataCamp's FDE career guide, published in July 2026, acknowledges that part of the work can be done remotely. But it also notes that deployments in heavily regulated, high-risk industries typically require more on-site involvement. The on-site role recruiting for the Norwegian government fits this observation exactly.

DataCamp also points out that the FDE title means different jobs at PostHog, Palantir, and a 20-person AI startup. So you cannot infer working conditions from a title or a tag, and the five postings above do not represent all employers. Every company's posting must be read down to the last line.

How should Vietnamese engineers adjust their strategy?

Not one of the five postings allows fully remote work. If they reflect the broader market, then searching for a role you can do "entirely from Vietnam" is betting on something scarce.

A more sensible strategy is to target places willing to bring foreigners over: roles that mention visa sponsorship, offer relocation support, and are based in cities with large offices.

When screening postings, read from the bottom up. Watch for these words: "citizen," "security clearance," "visa sponsorship," "relocation," "onsite," "travel up to," and language requirements. If a "Remote" role has a citizenship requirement, skip it immediately and spend your time on the next one.

Your resume should also target what the labs actually want to buy. They explicitly state 25%-50% travel, so a resume that only talks about backend and architecture is not enough. Write specifically about your experience working on-site at customers, projects you pushed to production in someone else's environment, and state clearly that you are willing to travel and relocate.

To an employer that needs to sponsor your visa, that statement carries real weight.

Many Vietnamese engineers underestimate their outsourcing experience—but told the right way, it becomes an advantage. On-site experience in Japan, Singapore, or Europe, sitting with a client's ops team for weeks debugging integration issues—these are exactly the kind of work described in FDE job postings.

Finally, remember the limits of the good news: Anthropic offers visa sponsorship for some roles, but not all; OpenAI's relocation support targets specific cities. This door is real, but narrow, so every application should target the right role rather than being a mass submission.

The "Remote" label in FDE postings promises you a shortcut. The real entry point for Vietnamese engineers lies in postings that explicitly tell you to be on-site—and also give you a path to get there.
