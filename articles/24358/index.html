Agile development has three process methodologies: Scrum, Extreme Programming (XP), and Feature-Driven Development (FDD)

Each has its own characteristics, and these are methodologies that anyone considering agile development should definitely know about.

In this article, we explain the basics of agile development and the three process methodologies in an easy-to-understand way.

Agile development is a methodology that develops software in small cycles based on functional units.

Because development proceeds in the order of "planning → design → implementation → testing" starting from the highest-priority requirements, it is resilient to specification changes during a project and makes it easy to maximize product value.

Traditionally, with what is called waterfall development, project requirements and design were decided in detail upfront, and then development proceeded in large cycles.

This method has the advantage of being easy to manage progress and plan the overall project, but it has the drawback that specification changes during development increase development workload.

Also, because it is difficult to reflect user feedback resulting from specification changes, the finished product may differ from the original vision.

With agile development, however, work starts with the highest-priority features, so it is possible to release quickly and make adjustments along the way.

As a result, you can improve product quality while shortening the time to delivery.

There is also a term with a similar meaning to agile development: "lean development."

Both share the common goal of developing products, but while agile aims for "speed," lean emphasizes "user satisfaction."

Each is needed in different situations, but since they complement each other well, it is good to keep both in mind during development.

Scrum is the most well-known process methodology in agile development. It creates a framework for the team to work within, and projects proceed flexibly within it. Related article: What Are the Disadvantages of Scrum Development? 3 Keys to Success Derived from Failures

This methodology maintains the flexibility of agile development while building a structure where each person takes responsibility for their assigned role. It has two major characteristics.

In Scrum, three roles, called roles, are assigned to team members.

- Product Owner: The person responsible for product development. Handles prioritization, requests to members, and schedule management

- Development Team: The engineers who actually do the development. A wide range of skills is required, including requirements/functional design, coding, operations, and testing

- Manager: Acts as an intermediary between the product owner and the development team. Works with the owner to communicate Scrum rules to the development members and shares feedback from the field with the product owner.

These three roles work together smoothly, and the development team unites to develop the product.

In Scrum, goal setting, development, and feedback are carried out in cycles of one to four weeks.

In agile development, cycles are often two weeks to two months, but Scrum can be said to be a speed-focused process methodology even among these.

The development cycle flows as follows.

Step 1. Create the backlog

The backlog is a list of features needed by users.

At the start, items to be implemented are selected from it, broken down into more detailed items, and created as the backlog.

What is important here is to thoroughly confirm, by mobilizing all members' skills, whether the work can be completed with confidence.

Once the project starts, irregular situations will arise, so it is important for each person to have the mindset of working responsibly.

Step 2. Hold daily scrums

A daily scrum is a meeting held every day before work begins.

Every day, each member shares their progress, challenges they are facing, and what they have resolved, and progress is confirmed.

Since it lasts only 15 minutes, rules are established such as preparing in advance and holding separate meetings when new issues arise.

If you try to implement all the daily scrum rules at once, you will fail, so we recommend adopting the ones that are effective first.

Step 3. Conduct a sprint review

A sprint review is one of the meetings held at the end of a development period. Depending on the project, it may be one week to one month.

In the sprint view, a product demo is conducted and development progress can be thoroughly reviewed, making it an important meeting.

For the development team, showing the finished product and receiving direct feedback from users allows them to confirm whether there is any gap between what they developed and user needs, and to verify their direction.

Since the next development cycle is decided based on the backlog (task list) obtained here, it also becomes easier to see what should be improved and how.

Step 4: Conduct the Sprint Retrospective

The sprint retrospective is a review meeting held at the end of a sprint.

The purpose of the sprint retrospective is to carry out future development better and improve product quality.

The scrum master and development team spend roughly one to three hours identifying areas for improvement for the next sprint and beyond. Since scrum emphasizes speed, this retrospective is extremely important.

Extreme Programming (XP) is a process methodology that emphasizes flexibility, whereas scrum focuses on speed in building products.

Agile development repeats the flow from requirements definition to testing, and among these, Extreme Programming responds flexibly to user requests and specification changes.

To provide flexibility, Extreme Programming consists of four practice areas (initiatives for realizing agile): "collaboration," "development," "management," and "customer."

Collaboration practices are habits that both the development team and users should have.

Iteration: repeatedly carrying out development cycles that finish in one to two weeks

Common language: creating a glossary to facilitate communication

Open workspace: building an environment that makes it easy to focus on work and communicate

Retrospective: creating a system that applies lessons learned from failures to future work

Since communication and improvement are repeated many times over short periods, it is important that each individual follows the rules.

Development practices are practices (initiatives for realizing agile) targeted at the development team, such as engineers.

Pair programming: two people jointly develop a single program

Test-driven development: writing test code before implementation and implementing to satisfy it

Refactoring: changing the internal structure without changing specifications

YAGNI: avoiding unnecessary development and building only what is needed now

The most distinctive practice is pair programming: while one person writes code, the other thinks about the overall design.

By developing while alternating these roles, high-quality products can be developed in a short period.

Since there are various other programming methods, it is necessary to apply the appropriate development approach.

Management practices are initiatives targeted at managers.

Accepting responsibility: ensuring that development is not one-way but achievable from the user's perspective by the team

Protection: identifying obstacles arising from relationships with stakeholders and others

Quarterly review: checking and reviewing progress at regular intervals

Mirror: frequently sharing the state of the team

Sustainable pace: managing the working hours of those involved

Managers focus on encouraging team members to act autonomously and proactively, and on drawing out maximum results.

Customer practices are initiatives targeted at customers (users).

Story writing: concisely summarizing feature requirements and discussing based on them

Release planning: deciding as a team how long it will take to implement issues

Acceptance testing: verifying from the customer's perspective whether the stories have been realized

Short releases: releasing the product at short intervals of two to eight weeks

At DeFactory, we place great importance on the customer practice of "listening directly to the voices" of target users.

- Discovering fundamental desires and unseen needs

- Obtaining information through interviews about "user actions and the results of those actions"

- Using open-ended questions to explore objective rather than subjective behavior

Through these, we aim to develop products with high added value for users.

User Feature-Driven Development (FDD) is a process methodology that focuses on the "user's perspective" in development.

It clarifies the features users want, iterates on implementation, and develops products that are valuable to users.

In this methodology, communication with users is important from an early stage, so how members are assigned becomes crucial.

Since the focus is on user features, it is easier to develop higher quality than for other features.

In this article, we discussed process methodologies in agile development.

1. Scrum: emphasis on speed

2. Extreme Programming (XP): emphasis on flexibility

3. Feature-Driven Development (FDD): emphasis on users

Agile development runs fast cycles while communicating with users, but some people may not know which process methodology to use depending on the product's characteristics and delivery deadlines.

Choosing which process methodology to use at the early stage of product development is an important decision. If it is difficult to decide internally, outsourcing to an external vendor is one option.
