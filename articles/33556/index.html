FDE vs ML Engineer: Skills, Salary and Which to Pick in 2026

Varsity Blog · 2026-11-23 · 14 min read

Summary

This article compares the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) and Machine Learning Engineer (MLE) roles, arguing the core difference lies in deliverables: FDEs turn ambiguous customer needs into deployed solutions, while MLEs build, evaluate and operationalise ML systems. Their skills overlap (Python, APIs, cloud, system design) but differ in depth. India salary data is patchy: Glassdoor reports FDE median total pay around ₹14.6 LPA (75th percentile ~₹42 LPA on Levels.fyi), while Indeed lists MLE average base pay at ₹11.15 LPA. The author cautions these figures aren't directly comparable and FDE samples are small. Career paths diverge toward applied AI and solutions architecture for FDEs versus ML platforms and research engineering for MLEs.

Key points

  • FDEs solve customer-specific problems; MLEs build and operationalise ML systems.
  • Both roles share Python, APIs, cloud and system design foundations.
  • FDE India median total pay: ~₹14.6 LPA (Glassdoor); 75th percentile ~₹42 LPA (Levels.fyi).
  • MLE average base pay in India: ~₹11.15 LPA per Indeed.
  • FDE salary data is new and sparse; comparisons need context.

AI engineering now covers several specialised roles, each focused on a different part of building and deploying intelligent systems. Two such roles are the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) and the Machine Learning Engineer (MLE). While both work with AI and software technologies, their responsibilities differ.

An ML Engineer typically develops, trains and optimises machine learning systems. An FDE, on the other hand, works with customers or business teams to understand operational problems and build solutions that work in their actual environments.

This comparison looks at their responsibilities, technical skills, salaries in India, career paths and the kind of work each role involves.

What is the Difference Between a Forward Deployed Engineer and an ML Engineer?

The main difference between a Forward Deployed Engineer and an ML Engineer lies in what they are expected to deliver. An ML Engineer focuses on building and operationalising machine learning systems, while an FDE applies software engineering and AI to solve specific customer or organisational problems.

What does a Forward Deployed Engineer do?

A Forward Deployed Engineer works closely with customers and business teams to understand their requirements and turn them into working technical solutions. The role combines software development, AI application engineering, system integration and customer collaboration.

Typical responsibilities include:

  • Understanding customer requirements and identifying technical problems.
  • Translating ambiguous business needs into technical specifications.
  • Building prototypes, integrations and production-ready applications.
  • Working with APIs, databases, cloud infrastructure and AI models.
  • Testing, deploying and improving solutions in customer environments.
  • Supporting adoption and communicating feedback to product and engineering teams.

An FDE is involved beyond the development stage. The role often includes ensuring that a solution works reliably within the customer’s existing systems and workflows.

What does a Machine Learning Engineer do?

A Machine Learning Engineer develops and maintains systems that use data and algorithms to make predictions, identify patterns or automate decisions. The role involves model development, experimentation, evaluation and deployment.

Typical responsibilities include:

  • Preparing and processing data for model development.
  • Selecting, training and evaluating machine learning models.
  • Improving model accuracy, performance and inference efficiency.
  • Building ML pipelines and deployment systems.
  • Monitoring models and maintaining their performance in production.
  • Collaborating with data scientists, researchers and software engineers.

Google Cloud’s Professional Machine Learning Engineer role description similarly covers building, evaluating, productionising and optimising AI solutions, along with model architecture, data pipelines and MLOps.

FDE vs ML Engineer: Skills and Technical Requirements

Both roles require strong programming and engineering fundamentals. However, the depth of specialisation differs depending on whether the work centres on customer-facing implementation or machine learning development.

Skills Required to Become a Forward Deployed Engineer

The key forward deployed engineer skills combine software development with practical AI implementation and communication.

  • Programming: Python, JavaScript or other relevant languages.
  • Backend engineering: APIs, databases, authentication and system integrations.
  • AI application development: LLM APIs, RAG, AI agents and tool calling.
  • Cloud and deployment: Containers, CI/CD, cloud infrastructure and monitoring.
  • System design: Building scalable and reliable applications.
  • Customer-facing skills: Requirement gathering, communication and problem decomposition.
  • Adaptability: Working with ambiguous requirements and changing customer needs.

FDEs need to understand machine learning and AI systems, but their work does not necessarily centre on training models from scratch.

Skills Required to Become a Machine Learning Engineer

The skills required for an ML Engineer place greater emphasis on mathematics, statistics and model development.

  • Programming: Python and software engineering practices.
  • Mathematics and statistics: Linear algebra, probability, calculus and optimisation.
  • Machine learning fundamentals: Supervised and unsupervised learning, classification, regression and clustering.
  • Deep learning: Neural networks, transformers and relevant frameworks.
  • Data engineering: Data preprocessing, feature engineering and pipelines.
  • MLOps: Model deployment, versioning, monitoring and retraining.
  • Model evaluation: Metrics, validation, error analysis and performance optimisation.

FDE vs ML Engineer Salary in India in 2026

Both roles can offer competitive compensation, but published salary data is more established for ML Engineers than for FDEs. Salary figures also vary depending on experience, employer, location and whether the source reports base pay or total compensation.

Forward Deployed Engineer Salary in India

FDE salary estimates in India vary considerably because the role is relatively new and salary datasets are still developing. Glassdoor’s India salary page reported a median total pay of approximately ₹14 lakh per year, with a reported range of ₹9.88 lakh to ₹22 lakh, based on 51 salary submissions as of November 2026. The same page distinguishes base pay from additional compensation, which may include bonuses and other benefits.

These figures should be treated as indicative rather than guaranteed salary bands. FDE compensation can also depend on the complexity of deployments, customer-facing responsibilities, technical specialisation and the employer’s size and stage.

Machine Learning Engineer Salary in India

Machine Learning Engineers have a more established salary benchmark across Indian employers. According to Indeed India, the average base salary for an ML Engineer was ₹11,15,659 per year, based on 47 salary reports and updated on 31 October 2026. The platform also reported a typical range of approximately ₹6.15 lakh to ₹20.22 lakh per year.

Actual compensation can vary based on experience, model-development expertise, industry, location and the scale of systems an engineer works on.

What Factors Influence Salary in Both Roles?

Several factors can influence compensation in either career:

  • Relevant experience: Experience working on production systems can strengthen an engineer’s profile.
  • Technical specialisation: Expertise in AI deployment, model optimisation or ML infrastructure can affect pay.
  • Company size and stage: Compensation structures differ between startups, established technology companies and global organisations.
  • Location: Hiring markets and remote-work arrangements can influence salary.
  • Ownership: Responsibility for production systems, customer outcomes or critical ML infrastructure can affect the scope of a role.
  • Additional compensation: Equity, annual bonuses and other benefits can change the overall value of an offer.

A role involving AI does not automatically guarantee a higher salary. The responsibilities, employer and overall compensation structure matter.

Career Growth: FDE vs ML Engineer

Both career paths offer opportunities to specialise or move into leadership. The direction of growth, however, often reflects the type of engineering work involved.

Career Path After Becoming an FDE

An FDE can progress into roles that combine technical ownership, enterprise implementation and applied AI leadership. Possible career paths include:

  • Senior Forward Deployed Engineer
  • Staff or Principal FDE
  • FDE Team Lead or Engineering Manager
  • AI Solutions Architect
  • AI Deployment Lead
  • Product or Platform Engineering Leadership

These are possible progression routes, not fixed promotion stages. The opportunities depend on the employer’s organisational structure and the engineer’s interests.

Career Path After Becoming an ML Engineer

ML Engineers can develop deeper technical expertise or move towards engineering leadership. Possible roles include:

  • Senior Machine Learning Engineer
  • Staff or Principal ML Engineer
  • ML Architect
  • ML Platform or MLOps Lead
  • Engineering Manager
  • Research Engineer or Applied AI Specialist

Which Role Offers More Flexibility?

ML Engineering skills can transfer across AI product companies, research-oriented teams and technology platforms. FDE experience can lead towards applied AI, enterprise deployment, solutions architecture and technical leadership.

Neither path guarantees greater flexibility. Both offer transferable skills in programming, system design, cloud computing and AI application development.

FDE vs ML Engineer: Which Career Should You Choose in 2026?

The better choice depends on the kind of engineering problems you want to work on and the responsibilities you enjoy.

Choose FDE If You Enjoy Building Solutions for Real-World Problems

Consider an FDE career if you:

  • Enjoy working directly with customers and stakeholders.
  • Like solving ambiguous, real-world problems.
  • Prefer building and deploying end-to-end applications.
  • Want exposure to different industries and business workflows.
  • Enjoy combining software engineering, AI and system integration.
  • Are comfortable adapting to changing requirements.

Choose ML Engineering If You Enjoy Deep Technical Specialisation

Consider ML Engineering if you:

  • Enjoy mathematics, statistics and algorithms.
  • Want to work closely with model development and optimisation.
  • Prefer experimentation and technical problem-solving.
  • Are interested in data pipelines, model performance and MLOps.
  • Want to specialise in machine learning systems and infrastructure.

Can you Move from ML Engineering to FDE or Vice Versa?

Yes. Both roles share software engineering and AI foundations, which can make a transition possible with additional role-specific preparation.

An ML Engineer moving into FDE may need to develop customer discovery, system integration, communication and deployment skills. An FDE moving towards ML Engineering may need deeper knowledge of model training, evaluation, algorithms and ML infrastructure.

Practical projects can help demonstrate these capabilities.

How to Prepare for an FDE or ML Engineering Career

Whichever direction you choose, start with a strong engineering foundation and then build the skills specific to your target role.

Build a Strong Foundation in Software Engineering

Learn Python and core programming concepts, understand data structures and algorithms, and practise debugging. Build applications using APIs and databases, and become familiar with Git, testing and standard software development workflows.

Develop Role-Specific AI Skills

For an FDE career, build an LLM-powered application, learn RAG, tool calling and agent workflows, and practise integrating applications with external APIs and databases. Take a project through deployment and monitoring.

For ML Engineering, study supervised and unsupervised learning, practise data preparation and model evaluation, and build projects using machine learning and deep learning frameworks. Learn the basics of model deployment and MLOps.

Build a Portfolio That Demonstrates Practical Capability

Your portfolio should show how you approach and solve engineering problems, not just list the tools you know.

For FDE roles, demonstrate problem discovery, system integrations and production deployment. For ML Engineering roles, show dataset preparation, model selection, evaluation and deployment. Document your decisions, trade-offs and limitations alongside each project.

Conclusion

Forward Deployed Engineering and Machine Learning Engineering are both valuable career paths, but they focus on different parts of the AI development lifecycle. Both require strong engineering foundations, but their specialisations and working styles differ. When choosing between them, consider the kind of problems you want to own, the skills you want to develop and the environment in which you work best. Choose the role that aligns with the work you want to do, rather than relying only on its popularity or perceived salary potential.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the difference between a Forward Deployed Engineer and a Machine Learning Engineer?

An FDE builds and deploys solutions for specific customer or organisational problems. An ML Engineer focuses on developing, training, evaluating and maintaining machine learning models and systems.

2. Is an FDE the same as an ML Engineer?

No. Both roles may work with AI models and software systems, but an FDE is generally more focused on customer requirements, integrations and deployment, while an ML Engineer specialises in machine learning systems.

3. Which role requires more coding, FDE or ML Engineer?

Both require coding. ML Engineers may write more code related to data processing, model development and ML pipelines. FDEs use coding extensively for application development, APIs, integrations and deployment.

4. What skills are required to become a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Important FDE skills include Python, backend development, APIs, databases, cloud deployment, system design, AI application development, communication and problem-solving.

5. Does a Forward Deployed Engineer need machine learning knowledge?

Yes, an understanding of machine learning and AI fundamentals is useful. However, FDE work is not always centred on training models. It often involves integrating existing models into practical applications and workflows.

6. What is the salary of an FDE in India in 2026?

Glassdoor reported a median total pay of approximately ₹14 lakh per year in November 2026, based on 51 submissions. Actual compensation varies by employer, experience and role scope.

7. What is the salary of an ML Engineer in India in 2026?

Indeed reported an average base salary of ₹11.15 lakh per year in India, based on 47 salary reports updated on 31 October 2026. Individual offers can vary considerably.

8. Which role has better career growth, FDE or ML Engineering?

Neither role guarantees better career growth. FDEs can move towards applied AI, solutions architecture and deployment leadership, while ML Engineers can specialise in ML platforms, research engineering and technical leadership.

9. Can an ML Engineer transition into a Forward Deployed Engineer role?

Yes. An ML Engineer can build on their programming and AI knowledge by developing customer discovery, communication, system integration and production deployment skills.

10. Which is better for freshers, FDE or ML Engineering?

The choice depends on a fresher’s interests and existing skills. Those who enjoy model development and mathematics may prefer ML Engineering, while those interested in application development, integrations and real-world implementation may prefer FDE.
