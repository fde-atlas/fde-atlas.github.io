The axis that separates all of them

Question 1 — whose environment do you work in? Yours, or the customer’s.

Question 2 — do you own the outcome or a contribution to it? The outcome, or your slice.

An FDE is the corner where both answers are the harder one: their environment, your outcome. Every adjacent role differs on at least one axis, and that difference explains everything else about the job.

Reading a job ad, whatever it is called

Titles are unreliable — roughly 40% of postings using the FDE label are not builder roles. These four checks work regardless of the title.

Production accountability

Does the posting say you own what runs, or that you “support”, “enable”, “advise” or “demonstrate”? Ownership language is the single strongest signal.

Compensation structure

Quota, OTE, variable-on-bookings → pre-sales-shaped. Base plus equity, no variable → builder-shaped. About 30% of FDE-titled postings fall on the pre-sales side.1

The feedback loop

Real forward-deployed roles describe carrying learnings back into the product — roughly a third of the job at a frontier lab.10 No product-feedback language means services or pre-sales.

Worked example: placing an unfamiliar job ad, step by step

An invented posting is titled Customer AI Engineer. That title appears nowhere in the table, which is the point. Place it with the two questions and the four checks, one line of the ad at a time.

StepLine in the adWhat it answersReading
1“Deploy AI agents inside enterprise customers’ clouds”Question 1: environmentCustomer’s
2“Own go-live and the first 90 days in production”Question 2, and check 1Owns the outcome ✓
3“Base plus equity, no variable component”Check 2: comp structureBuilder-shaped ✓
4“Bring what you learn back to our product team”Check 3: feedback loopPresent ✓
5“Customer VPC and on-prem targets”Check 4: deployment realityNamed targets ✓
6All of the abovePlacementTop-right corner: an FDE job under another name
7Swap step 3 for “OTE with a quarterly bookings target”Check 2 flipsOwns the sale: bottom-right, a solutions engineer role

One changed line moves the role across one axis, and that is enough to change the job. Base rates help too. Using the page’s split over 1,000 FDE-titled postings, 600 are builder roles, 300 pre-sales-shaped and 100 internal. Before you read a word, a random FDE-titled ad is a builder role only 60% of the time. Read ten and expect about four to fail these checks. The title is the cheapest part of an ad to change.

Common mistakes

  • Sorting the roles by how technical they areA senior solutions architect can be deeply technical and still never own production. Technical depth varies inside every box of the grid, so it separates nothing.
  • Trusting the titleAbout 40% of FDE-titled postings are not builder roles. Run the four checks before you read the title.
  • Describing the job as “helping customers”That is the support row. In an interview, say what you would own and where it would run: “their environment, my outcome”.
  • Treating full-stack range as the jobIt is the largest single prerequisite. The job adds discovery, deployment into environments you do not control, and commercial context.
  • Thinking a PM and an FDE do the same jobBoth own the outcome. The PM moves it through other people; the FDE changes the system directly. If giving up the keyboard would make you miserable, that difference is the whole decision.

Which two questions place any of these roles?

The page reduces the whole family to two axes, with the FDE sitting in the corner where both answers are the harder one: their environment, your outcome. Travel and technical depth vary within every one of these roles, so neither separates them.

A role works in the customer's environment but owns only its own slice of the delivery.

Every adjacent role differs from the FDE on at least one of the two axes, and that difference explains the rest of the job. Owning a contribution rather than the outcome changes what you are measured on and when you are finished, even with identical day-to-day surroundings.

You are reading a job ad with an unfamiliar title.

Titles in this family are inconsistent across companies, which is why the page offers a test that works on any ad. Anchoring to a familiar title imports assumptions from a different company's naming, and a seniority band says how much you are trusted, not whose environment you work in or what you own.

Quick answers

What roles are similar to a forward deployed engineer?

The closest are solutions engineer, solutions architect, applied AI engineer, agent engineer and technology consultant. Slightly further out sit product manager, platform/DevOps engineer, data engineer, SRE, technical account manager and customer success engineer. The FDE is distinguished by working in the customer’s environment while owning the outcome — every adjacent role differs on one of those two axes.

Is a forward deployed engineer the same as a full-stack engineer?

No. Full-stack describes technical range; forward-deployed describes position relative to the customer. Most FDEs are full-stack, but the role adds discovery, deployment into environments you do not control, evals as acceptance criteria, and commercial context. Full-stack range is the largest single prerequisite, not the job itself.

Is FDE closer to product management or engineering?

Engineering, with product manager as the closest non-engineering neighbour. Both own outcomes and spend heavily on discovery and stakeholders, but a PM influences through other people while an FDE changes the system directly. PM is one of the most common FDE exits because the discovery and prioritisation skills transfer cleanly.

How do I tell whether a job ad is a real FDE role?

Four checks that work regardless of the title: does it say you own what runs in production; does compensation mention quota, OTE or variable-on-bookings; does it describe carrying learnings back into the product; and does it name real deployment targets such as customer VPC, on-prem or air-gapped rather than only demos. Roughly 60% of FDE-titled postings are builder roles, 30% pre-sales-shaped and 10% internal.
