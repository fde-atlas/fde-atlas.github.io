Why Snowflake's CRO says top engineers avoid the fast-growing "AI role" of FDE

Original title: 元Snowflake CROが語る、トップエンジニアが急成長中の“AI職”「FDE」を敬遠する理由

Business Insider Japan · 2026-05-29 · 3 min read

Summary

Business Insider Japan reports that Chris Degnan, Snowflake's former Chief Revenue Officer of over 11 years, criticized the forward deployed engineer role on the '20VC' podcast. He called FDEs 'professional services people in name only', arguing top engineers prefer working on core products. He noted FDEs build products for clients that may never benefit their employer and leave technical debt for customers to maintain. Despite his skepticism, demand is booming: Indeed data shows FDE job postings up 5230% since January 2025 and roughly 729% year over year. OpenAI launched OpenAI Deployment Company with over $4 billion in initial investment, and Google announced a new AI organization staffed with FDEs. Stripe even applied the model internally for its marketing team.

Full text

  • Snowflake's former chief revenue officer described Forward Deployed Engineers as "glorified professional services workers."
  • Chris Degnan said that talented engineers would rather work on the core product than become an FDE.
  • Google and OpenAI are stepping up hiring for the role as FDE positions spread rapidly.

Investor Chris Degnan is pouring cold water on the "Forward Deployed" Engineer, one of the most talked-about roles in tech right now.

The Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), a role popularized by Palantir, is embedded at client companies, building technology while helping customers make the most of it from the inside. The model has become especially popular in the AI era, as companies race to make their workforces "AI-native."

Degnan spent more than 11 years as Snowflake's chief revenue officer before retiring in 2025. He now works as an investor and startup advisor. Appearing on the "20VC" podcast, Degnan discussed the challenges of hiring FDEs and what the day-to-day work actually looks like.

He called these engineers "glorified professional services workers."

"If you're a really good engineer, you don't want to be a Forward Deployed Engineer," Degnan said. "You want to work on the core product."

These engineers, Degnan pointed out, build products for client companies that may never be returned to (or reused by) their employer. The problem is that the customer then has to maintain the technology afterward.

"Forward Deployed Engineers leave behind a lot of technical debt, and it's risky," he said.

The FDE role has expanded rapidly amid the AI boom. According to data from Indeed, FDE job postings in April 2026 surged 5,230% compared with January 2025, and rose about 729% year over year.

Big tech companies are also jumping on the trend, launching new organizations dedicated to FDEs. OpenAI established the OpenAI Deployment Company backed by more than $4 billion in initial investment. Google recently announced the creation of a new AI organization staffed with FDEs.

Some companies are also applying the "Forward Deployed" model internally, ensuring that every team fully adopts AI tools. In early May, Stripe posted a job listing for a "Forward Deployed AI Accelerator" for its marketing team.

Some call the FDE "the hottest job in AI." On the podcast, Degnan drew a clear line.

"Forward Deployed Engineers are not on the level of core engineers building the core product," he said.
