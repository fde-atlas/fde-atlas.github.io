The long-term value of enterprise AI depends on "assetizing rules, designing organizational entry points, and cost governance" rather than one-time tool training — 2026-08-09

Original title: 企业 AI 的长期价值取决于「规则资产化、组织入口设计和成本治理」，而不是一次性工具教学 — 2026-08-09

FDE百科 · 2026-08-09 · 3 min read

Summary

This piece argues that enterprise AI's long-term value rests on three pillars rather than one-off tool training: codifying expert judgment rules into AI-callable governing documents, redesigning training as a trust entry point with profit shifted to diagnosis and on-site deployment, and continuous cost governance. It cites an investor who turned a value-investing manual into a rule system, Chinese training-market feedback showing tool-teaching margins shrinking, and Databricks' August 7 post reporting its AI Gateway Smart Router cut average task cost by over 30% while matching the most expensive model's quality.

Key points

  • Knowledge bases should constrain AI actions, not just store documents.
  • Tool training margins are shrinking; profit moves to deployment services.
  • Databricks' dynamic model routing cut average task cost by 30%+.
  • Every FDE delivery should include cost tables and budget alerts.

Today's Judgment

Today, three threads converge on the same question: whether enterprise AI can run long-term depends on whether expert rules can be built into the system, whether organizational roles can be clearly divided, and whether the quality and cost of every task can be continuously managed.

  • A professional investor turned his value investing playbook into an AI-callable charter document, letting the AI handle data gathering, tracking, and preliminary reasoning—this reveals that the next stage of knowledge bases is not "storing more material," but "constraining the AI's judgment and actions"
  • Practical feedback from multiple Chinese enterprise AI training markets shows that the profit margin of pure tool training is being continuously squeezed by big tech companies' free training and increasingly foolproof products—the real value of training lies in becoming a "trust entry point," with backend profits coming from scenario diagnosis and on-site deployment
  • Databricks' engineering practice proves that after AI coding is used at scale, dynamic model routing can reduce average task cost by more than 30%—cost governance is an essential skill for FDEs

Key Signals

Experts write judgment rules into AI-callable charter documents: the next stage of knowledge bases

  • Source: FDE enterprise field practice
  • A professional investor uses his value investing playbook as the AI's core charter document, breaking it into five steps—company research, valuation, position sizing, tracking, and mindset—and designs 12 standard documents for each investment target. His experience-based judgment is that information gathering, preliminary reasoning, counterargument construction, and database maintenance account for 80%-90% of investment research work.
  • Why it matters: This reveals the evolution direction of enterprise knowledge bases—not "storing more material for AI to retrieve," but "writing expert judgment rules into the system, constraining what the AI should do, what to judge first, when to escalate to humans, and what conditions trigger action." The deliverable of a knowledge base should not be a catalog of materials, but a rule system that constrains actions. This method can be transferred to finance, HR, sales, and any area of business operations.

Enterprise AI training profits are shifting downstream: from the classroom to the field

  • Source: Practice from multiple Chinese enterprise AI training programs
  • Feedback from multiple AI training programs targeting small and medium business owners shows: course content is still positively reviewed, but the profit margin of pure tool training is being squeezed by three forces—big tech companies' free training, standardized tools lowering the learning barrier, and products becoming increasingly "foolproof." At the same time, training remains an effective entry point for building customer trust and discovering real scenarios.
  • Why it matters: For FDE practitioners, this means the product funnel needs to be redesigned—training is not a profit center, but a trust entry point. Real profits come from post-training scenario diagnosis, rule assetization, on-site deployment, and annual maintenance. The era of "teaching bosses to use tools" is ending, and the era of "helping enterprises run AI through their business processes" is beginning.

Databricks: After AI coding at scale, dynamic routing reduces average task cost by more than 30%

  • Source: Databricks official blog | Original article
  • On August 7, Databricks shared its internal AI coding cost governance practice, breaking cost control into four types of levers: quickly switching to cost-effective models, request/task-level dynamic routing, giving developers visibility and budget alert thresholds, and reducing token overhead. Its internal results show that the AI Gateway Smart Router continuously reduces average task cost by more than 30% while roughly matching the quality of the most expensive models.
  • Why it matters: Model routing is not an add-on for underlying technical teams, but the per-task unit economics for FDEs. When enterprises start using AI at scale, "using the most expensive model for everything" will quickly burn through the budget. Every FDE delivery package should include an internal task set, quality thresholds, the minimum viable model, escalation strategies, and budget alerts. Only when costs are controllable can projects be sustainable.

Takeaways for FDE Practitioners

  • Add a "decision charter document" to every client knowledge base: It should at least include goal definitions, evidence tiers, judgment order, prohibited actions, human escalation conditions, and review cycles. A knowledge base is not a place to store materials, but a rule system that constrains AI behavior.
  • Redesign the product funnel from "selling courses" to "selling outcomes": Public content/courses → paid diagnosis → scenario rule packages → on-site deployment → annual maintenance/incremental revenue sharing. Profit increases at each layer, and trust increases at each layer.
  • Add a "per-task cost table" to every AI agent project: Model costs, tool costs, token consumption, human review costs, failure rerun costs, and final business outcomes—only by recording them separately can you optimize.
