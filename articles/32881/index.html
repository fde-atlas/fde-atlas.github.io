Case

Forward deployed engineer hiring index: Q4 2026

Kernavique · 2026-11-20 · 13 min read

Summary

Kernavique counted public job feeds from 180 employers on November 20, 2026: 79 had at least one posting titled “forward deployed”, totaling 526 postings, 298 of them US-based. A like-for-like comparison across 62 feeds showed little change from October (312 to 308). Of US postings, 63 mention a security clearance, 36 at Palantir. A separate pay sample of 42 US ads shows base salary ranges from $88,970 to $376,000. The authors stress this is a convenience sample, not a census, and that data providers' growth figures (e.g., Lightcast's sixfold rise from 2024 to 2025) use differing methods and should not be combined with their counts.

Key points

  • 79 of 180 employers had open “forward deployed” roles; 526 postings total, 298 in the US.
  • Like-for-like count across 62 feeds was flat: 308 postings versus 312 in October.
  • 63 of 298 US postings mention security clearance; Palantir accounts for 36.
  • Base salary ranges in 24 US ads run from $88,970 to $376,000.
  • Provider growth figures (Lightcast, Bloomberry) lack stated methods and conflict; don't average them.

How many employers are hiring, and how the count moved

On November 20, 2026, 79 of the 180 employers we checked had at least one open role titled “forward deployed”. That is 43.9% of the list, from 79 and 180.

The 79 has two parts. On the 177 public job feeds, 76 employers had a matching posting and 101 had none. Amazon, ServiceNow and Deloitte were read through their own job search, and all three had matching roles. Their counts are not comparable with the feeds, so they stay out of the posting totals.

A zero means no posting matched the title rule on that feed at that moment. It does not mean the employer has no forward deployed team.

Since October 11, like-for-like. We first read 62 of these feeds on October 11, 2026. On those same 62 feeds, with the same title rule and all locations counted:

  • November 20, 2026: 308 postings on 26 feeds.
  • October 11, 2026: 312 postings on 27 feeds.
  • Excluding interns: 292 on November 20, against 291 on October 11.

So the count on a fixed set of employers barely moved between the two reads. Inside the total, employers moved in both directions. OpenAI went from 26 postings to 38. Databricks went from 101 to 94 and Palantir from 79 to 72.

Only the all-location figures can be compared. The October check did not record a US-only count. On November 20, 183 of the 308 postings on those 62 feeds listed a US location.

One change is worth noting. On October 11, Anthropic’s only US forward deployed posting was a manager role. Its feed now lists a Staff Forward Deployed Engineer in New York City, San Francisco and Seattle.

The other 115 feeds were added for this read. They have no October figure, so the 526 total cannot be compared with anything earlier.

Where the roles are, and how many mention clearance

United States and elsewhere. Of the 526 postings on the 177 feeds, 298 list at least one US location. Those 298 sit at 65 employers. The other 11 employers with a matching posting either list no US role or name no country in the feed.

  • Wonderful had 35 matching postings, none in the US.
  • Eleven postings at Cloudflare, Vannevar Labs and Tines give a location such as “Hybrid”, “Remote” or “North America (Remote)”. We do not count those as US.
  • Outside the feeds, Amazon’s job search showed 82 matching roles, 57 of them in the US. ServiceNow showed 13, 6 in the US, from a partial read. Deloitte’s US careers site showed 56.

US postings are concentrated. Databricks had 60, Palantir 53, OpenAI 21 and Anduril 13. That is 147 of the 298.

We did not count postings by city. The feeds of the three employers with the most US postings each name New York and Washington, D.C.

What the title covers. The rule matches any title with “forward deployed” in it. Of the 526 postings, 294 are a plain forward deployed engineer title, with or without a team, region or level added, at 63 employers. Another 76 are titled forward deployed software engineer. The rule also catches 41 postings for managers of these teams and 22 for people who are not engineers, such as bankers and lawyers.

Clearance. Of the 298 US postings, 63 mention a security clearance in the ad text, at 11 employers. Palantir accounts for 36, Anduril for 10 and Databricks for 6.

This is a keyword match, not a reading of each ad. “Active clearance required”, “preferred” and “eligible to obtain” are counted together. Amazon’s roles are outside this count; 15 of its 57 US ads mention a clearance. Our page on cleared forward deployed engineer roles covers this part of the market.

Contract or permanent. No matching posting on an Ashby or Lever feed carried a contract, temporary or part-time employment type. Greenhouse feeds have no such field, so we could not check those employers.

Pay in brief

Pay comes from a separate sample: 42 US job ads from 31 employers, read on November 20, 2026. It was found by search, not drawn from the 180 employers above.

  • Base. 24 ads label their range as base salary. The lowest figure is $88,970 and the highest is $376,000.
  • Salary, not stated as base. 16 ads give a range without saying base.
  • On-target earnings. 2 ads quote on-target earnings.
  • Any basis. Across all 42 ads, the lowest figure is $54,400 and the highest is $405,000, in a manager role.
  • Equity. 30 of the 42 ads mention equity, stock or restricted stock units.
  • Clearance. 6 of the 42 ads require a security clearance or eligibility for one.

Compare figures on the same pay basis. The ad-by-ad tables, with what each ad says about equity and bonus, are in forward deployed engineer pay by employer.

What data providers report on growth

Our counts are a snapshot of one list of employers. Data providers publish growth figures across far more postings. None of the pages we read states its method, and the figures do not agree with each other, because the providers, title definitions and periods differ. Quote each with its provider and period. Do not average them.

Lightcast, on its own page (September 15, 2026). Lightcast says US job postings for the role rose from approximately 200 in 2024 to around 1,200 in 2025, which it calls a sixfold increase. It also says more than 5,200 postings appeared in the first seven months of 2026, already more than four times the 2025 total. That second sentence does not repeat “US”. The post does not say which titles were counted or how duplicates were handled. It is the only provider page we read that gives posting counts and not just a growth rate.

Bloomberry, using Revealera data (updated January 25, 2026). Bloomberry reports that forward deployed engineer jobs grew 1,165% from January through October 2025 against the same months of 2024. The figure comes from a set of 1,000 postings supplied by Revealera, a jobs data provider. Bloomberry is the analyst and Revealera is the data source. No method is stated.

Lightcast data, as reported by Fortune (September 3, 2026). This one is second-hand. Fortune reports, citing Lightcast data, that postings for forward-deployed engineers rose more than 1000% between January and August 2026 against the same period a year earlier, and more than 4,600% against 2023. The article does not state the geography, and we did not find these two figures on a Lightcast page.

Indeed and the Financial Times, as reported by Salesforce (March 18, 2026). Also second-hand. A Salesforce newsroom story says postings rose 800% between January and September 2025, and attributes the figure to an analysis by Indeed and the Financial Times. We opened neither the Indeed page nor the Financial Times article.

In practice: the provider figures describe growth over one to three years across a wide set of postings. Our like-for-like figure describes 62 employers across two reads in the fall of 2026. Both can be true. Neither should be used to adjust the other.

What employers have said about building these teams

We include only statements read on the employer’s own page.

Amazon Web Services (June 30, 2026; page last updated November 19, 2026). In a post on About Amazon, Francessca Vasquez, a vice president at AWS, describes a new forward deployed engineering organization backed by a $1 billion investment. The subtitle says it will embed thousands of experts with customers. The post gives no hiring target and does not say how the $1 billion is split. On November 20, 2026, Amazon’s job search showed 82 roles with “forward deployed” in the title, 57 of them in the US.

Salesforce (March 18, 2026). A Salesforce newsroom story says the company tripled its forward deployed engineering team in six months. It says around 40% to 50% of movement into these roles at Salesforce is internal, and that a six-week onboarding program was built in September 2025. It gives no headcount. We could not count Salesforce’s own postings; see the last section.

Salesforce (April 15, 2026). Salesforce announced a Forward Deployed Engineering Partner Network. It names Accenture and Deloitte as launch partners, joined by over 30 firms that include PwC, Slalom and IBM Consulting. Salesforce says firms in the network have driven one-third of all successful Agentforce implementations. That is the company’s own claim.

The consulting side shows in our counts too. Deloitte’s US careers site listed 56 matching titles on November 20, 2026. They are named for the vendor platform, including Palantir, Databricks, AWS, ServiceNow, Google, Anthropic, OpenAI, Snowflake and Salesforce.

For a company, these statements mean more employers are competing for the same engineers. What to watch next quarter

These are questions we will re-measure with the same rules. They are not forecasts.

  • Does the like-for-like count move? The 62-feed figure was 312 on October 11 and 308 on November 20. A third read will show whether that is a plateau or a pause.
  • Does the 177-feed count move? November 20 is the first read of all 177 feeds, at 526 postings. The next read gives it a comparison.
  • Does the US share change? It was 298 of 526 on November 20. Wonderful alone had 35 postings outside the US.
  • Does the clearance count change? It was 63 of 298 US postings, with 36 at one employer.
  • Do more employers become readable? Employers on Workday are thin in this sample. Whether we can read them changes who is in the denominator.
  • Do published ranges move? Eleven ads we read on both October 11 and November 20 showed the same range.
  • Do providers publish a method or full-year figures? Lightcast’s 2026 figure covers seven months and its post states no method.

What we left out

  • The employer-by-employer table. It is in who is hiring forward deployed engineers, not repeated here.
  • The ad-by-ad pay tables. They are in forward deployed engineer pay by employer.
  • Employers on Workday. The public job search turned us away for rate limiting at Booz Allen Hamilton, Accenture, NVIDIA, Leidos, CACI and others, so they are not in the 180. For Salesforce we read only the first 20 of 118 search results, none of them a US title, and give no count.
  • Google. Its careers search returned no job list as text. A count published by a news outlet is not ours and is not used.
  • Third-hand and untraced growth figures. An Indeed figure that reached us through two outlets, and figures attributed to LinkUp and LinkedIn that we could not open on the provider’s own page.
  • Projections and estimates from firms that sell hiring services, and a growth figure that covers only one vendor’s own job marketplace.
  • A median salary attributed to Lightcast. It appears only in a news article, with no period or geography stated.
  • Employer statements we could not read on the employer’s own page. These include reported remarks from Google Cloud and Palantir, and reported joint ventures at OpenAI and Anthropic.
  • A US-only comparison with October 11. The October check did not record one.
  • Postings by city. The feeds were not tallied by city, and long location lists were shortened when captured.
  • Deployment strategist and applied AI engineer titles. We counted them separately. They are not in any figure here.
