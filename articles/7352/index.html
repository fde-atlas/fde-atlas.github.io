Start Here: How to Prepare for FDE Interviews (2026)

FDE Interviews · 2026 · 4 min read

Summary

This guide from FDEInterviews lays out a step-by-step way to prepare for forward deployed engineer interviews in 2026. It suggests starting with a free 25-minute Foundations lesson, then using a 15-minute readiness assessment to identify gaps. It offers tailored paths for six backgrounds: backend/full-stack engineers, ML engineers, solutions engineers, practitioners already doing deployed AI work, students, and agent builders. Resources include 5 courses, 658 interview questions, 200 paper breakdowns, company guides covering 111 companies, and browser labs. The recommended loop: answer before reading solutions, practice under time limits, and revisit weak spots.

Key points

  • Start with a free Foundations lesson, then assess gaps
  • Six tailored prep paths by background
  • 658 questions, 200 paper breakdowns, 111 company guides
  • Answer first, then compare with worked solutions
  • You don't need to finish the site—target your gaps

Start from what you already know.

Open the background closest to yours and follow its steps. These are suggested starting points; you can change direction whenever a different gap becomes clear.

Backend or full-stack engineerConnect your engineering skills to customer work.

  • Foundations of FDE → Start with the free opening lesson, then follow the syllabus.
  • One module knows their system → Free lesson: isolate what is specific to a customer.
  • Customer and behavioural questions → Practise explaining scope, trade-offs and delivery decisions.

ML engineer or data scientistTake a model through integration, serving and evaluation.

  • The weights are the easy part → Free lesson: work out the memory budget before choosing hardware.
  • The FDE Engagement → Follow a customer problem through integration and handover.
  • Serving and cost research → Compare the mechanisms behind inference performance. Summaries are free.

Solutions engineer or consultantBuild the engineering depth behind the customer conversation.

  • How FDE differs from adjacent roles → Read the role comparison before choosing a preparation plan.
  • Foundations of FDE → Start free; spend more time on the integration and production modules.
  • Coding and DSA practice → Work a problem before opening its answer. Access is labelled.

Already doing deployed AI workStudy harder failure modes and defend your design choices.

  • The first 48 hours of a rescue → Free lesson: recover a deployment with incomplete context.
  • Production AI Agents → Start with the free lessons on workflow, tools and control.
  • The research starting path → Read the evidence behind retrieval, agents, evaluation and serving.

Student or new graduateBuild a foundation and one piece of work you can explain.

  • What a forward deployed engineer does → A public introduction to the role and its responsibilities.
  • Foundations of FDE → Start with the free lessons and work through one module at a time.
  • Try a browser lab → Choose a free starter mission. Practise without setting up a machine.

Building coding or domain agentsGive agent work explicit authority, evidence and recovery rules.

  • Define the harness boundary → Free lesson: separate a model's proposal from permission and acceptance.
  • Harness Engineering for FDEs → Follow 38 lessons and nine projects. Every lesson labels its access.
  • Agent research → Compare the evidence behind agent planning, tools and evaluation.

Learn, try, explain. Then repeat.

You do not need to finish the whole site. Choose a skill, practise it, and use the result to choose your next step.

  • 01

    Choose a focus

    Find a gap or choose a target role. Use it to decide what to practise next.
  • 02

    Learn the mechanism

    Follow a course. Use concepts, videos and research when an idea needs a closer look.
  • 03

    Try it yourself

    Answer before reading the solution. Then test the same skill in a lab or work sample.
  • 04

    Rehearse and revisit

    Explain your decisions under a time limit. Return to the part you could not defend.

The right resource for the task.

Courses give you a sequence. Concepts explain an idea. Papers let you examine its evidence. Choose the format that helps with the work in front of you.
