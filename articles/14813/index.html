Forward Deployed Engineer Jobs in Europe (2026): It's a London Market With Satellites

fde.directory · 2026-08-28 · 4 min read

Summary

Analyzing 30 European listings out of 218 on its August 2026 board, fde.directory argues Europe's FDE market is effectively a London hub with thin satellites: London appears in 20 of 30 postings, Paris in 4, and other cities only one or two each. The concentration follows enterprise-buyer density rather than engineer supply — English as the operating language, financial-services buyers, and labs like Mistral, Cohere, and Cursor routing EMEA seats through London. Pay runs below US levels: London bands around £95k–£130k, with Palantir's London packages near £137k, roughly 30–40% under American equivalents, though still above local engineering medians.

Ask where Europe’s Forward Deployed Engineer jobs are and the instinct is to name a handful of tech capitals — London, Paris, Berlin, Amsterdam, Dublin — and assume they split the market. They don’t. On our board, Europe’s FDE market is really one city with a few satellites orbiting it. London isn’t leading Europe; London is Europe, to a degree that surprised me when I ran the numbers. That concentration is the story, and like most things about this role, it traces back to where the customers sit.

What the board actually shows

Start with the honest count. On the fde.directory board, August 2026, of 218 open listings, 30 touch Europe — any role that names a European city or an EMEA/UK location. That’s about 14% of the global board: not a rounding error, not a co-capital to the US, but a real and growing slice worth taking seriously if you want to build an FDE career on this side of the Atlantic.

London appears in two-thirds of every European FDE posting on the board. Paris, the second city, shows up in four. Everything else is ones and twos — Berlin, Dublin, Munich, Zurich, Barcelona, Stockholm, Copenhagen, a Black Forest Labs seat in Freiburg. This is not a continent of competing hubs. It’s a hub-and-spoke map with a single hub, and the spokes are thin.

The names hiring into London tell you what kind of market it is: Palantir, Mistral (Paris and London), Cohere, Cursor, Vercel, MongoDB, C3 AI, AssemblyAI, Intercom, Sierra, Lovable, plus a long tail of London-only startups — Matta, Seamflow, Edra, Diligent. Frontier labs, infra companies, and enterprise-AI vendors alike route their European deployment seats through London first.

The pay gap is the other headline

Europe pays for FDE work — just structurally less than the US does. Here’s the rough shape of the market, and I’d read these as bands rather than precise figures, since public salary aggregators run noisy on a role this specialized:

For a concrete anchor, Palantir — the company that invented the role — pays its London FDEs an average total package around £137k (roughly £103k base plus equity), per Levels.fyi. That’s a strong European number. It’s also well below what the same title clears in the US, where Palantir’s Forward Deployed Software Engineers run $171k–$295k with a median around $208k. Convert and the London package lands roughly 30–40% under its American equivalent for the same work.

That discount is not a Palantir quirk; it’s the European tech-comp baseline, and it holds across the labs and vendors. The upside: European FDE pay still sits comfortably above local software-engineering medians, because the role blends engineering with customer ownership and companies pay for that scarcity. The honest framing for a candidate is that you’re trading absolute comp for location, language, and the ability to work the role without relocating to San Francisco or New York — a trade a lot of people will happily make.

Why London, specifically

If FDE geography tracked engineer supply, Europe’s map would look like its tech-talent map — a real contest between London, Paris, Berlin, Amsterdam, and Stockholm. It doesn’t, and the reason is the same mechanism that made the US market bicoastal: an FDE’s job is to sit next to the customer, not the model. The role’s center of gravity is enterprise-buyer density, and in Europe that density has an accent.

London wins on three axes at once. First, language — it’s the default English-operating market, which matters enormously when a US-headquartered lab wants one European seat that can serve clients across the continent without a language barrier. Second, buyers — London is Europe’s financial-services capital, and banks, insurers, and asset managers are exactly the deep-pocketed, data-heavy, deployment-hungry enterprises FDEs are hired to embed inside. Third, the labs plant their European flags there: Mistral, a French company, lists London alongside Paris; Cohere, Cursor, Sierra, and others route their EMEA deployment seats through it. When both the buyers and the builders converge on one city, the FDE seats follow, and the rest of Europe becomes spillover.

Paris is the one genuine counterweight, propped up by a homegrown frontier lab in Mistral and a small cluster of French AI startups like H Company. Berlin, Munich, and Zurich pick up the German-speaking enterprise and industrial-AI deployments. But none of them is within striking distance of London, and nothing in the current data suggests that changes soon.

What it means if you’re the candidate

The practical read is blunt. If you want to work as an FDE in Europe and you’re mobile, London is the market — it has the volume, the range of employers, and the clearest ladder from startup seat to frontier-lab package. If you’re anchored to the continent proper, Paris is the strongest non-UK bet, with Berlin and the German-speaking hubs viable if you’re targeting industrial or enterprise deployments specifically. And everywhere, calibrate comp to the European baseline: strong relative to local engineering pay, a real discount relative to the US. The seats are here, they’re growing, and they concentrate far more tightly than the continent’s size would lead you to guess.
