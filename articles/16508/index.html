How to Build a Forward Deployed Engineering Team, by Stage

Forward Deploying · 2026-11-03 · 2 min read

Summary

This article lays out how to deliberately build a Forward Deployed Engineering team across three funding stages. At seed to Series A, hire one senior generalist FDE who owns named customers and stays close to founders. From Series B to D, formalize a hiring scorecard, career ladder, and operating model, measuring outcomes like time to first production use. At late stage, appoint a Head of FDE, standardize playbooks and eval templates, and plan for compliance including the EU AI Act. It closes with common mistakes, such as hiring solutions engineers into FDE roles or letting deployments become custom forks.

Key points

  • Stage 1 (seed to Series A): one senior generalist FDE owning named customers, not a junior or pre-sales hire.
  • Stage 2 (Series B to D): write down the hiring scorecard, build a career ladder, and pick pods versus shared pool.
  • Measure deployments, not activity: time to first production use and what still runs after six months.
  • Stage 3: add a Head of FDE, standardize playbooks, and plan compliance like data residency and the EU AI Act.
  • Common mistakes: hiring solutions engineers as FDEs, giving FDEs sales targets, and pay benchmarking errors.

Published · 2 min read · By Vishal Wadhwani

Forward Deployed teams rarely start as a plan. A founder ends up doing every deployment, then one engineer starts spending most of their time with customers, and one day the company realises it has a function without a design. Here is how to build it on purpose.

Stage 1: the first Forward Deployed Engineer (seed to Series A)

When: customers are signing, but getting each one live depends on a founder. Who: a senior generalist who has shipped inside someone else's environment, ideally with startup or consulting experience. Not a junior engineer, and not a pre-sales hire.

  • Give them ownership of named customers, not "help with deployments".
  • Keep them close to the founders and the product team: their notes are your roadmap.
  • Decide early what counts as product and what stays as custom work, or every deployment becomes a fork.

Stage 2: from one FDE to a team (Series B to D)

When: more deployments than one person can run, and the same problems are being solved twice. Who: a mix of senior FDEs, a first lead and, for large customers, an architect who designs what the engineers build.

  • Write the scorecard down. The bar drifts as soon as more than one person interviews. Ours is public.
  • Create a career ladder. FDEs leave when the only promotion path is into product engineering or sales.
  • Decide the operating model. Pods per customer or a shared pool? Who owns the relationship: the FDE or the account team?
  • Measure deployments, not activity. Time to first production use and what is still running after six months.

Stage 3: a function (late stage and enterprise)

When: deployments span regions, regulated industries or government. Who: a Head of Forward Deployed Engineering, team leads, architects and specialists (security, data, clearance-eligible engineers where needed).

  • Standardise deployment playbooks, reference architectures and eval templates.
  • Plan for compliance: data residency, security reviews and, if you deploy AI that touches hiring or other high-risk uses in the EU, the EU AI Act.
  • Hire for regions before you need them: senior FDEs take longest to find in markets where few employers advertise.

Mistakes we see at every stage

  • Hiring solutions engineers into FDE seats, or the reverse
  • Giving FDEs a commercial target
  • Letting every deployment become a custom fork of the product
  • Benchmarking pay against senior software engineering and losing candidates on base
