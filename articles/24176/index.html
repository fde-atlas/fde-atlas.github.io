How to Launch an In-House FDE Team | Comparing Hiring, Training, and External Support, with a 90-Day Plan

Original title: 社内FDEチームの立ち上げ方｜採用・育成・外部伴走の比較と90日計画

FDE総研 · 2026-11-18 · 11 min read

Summary

FDE总研 (operated by VACAN Technologies) explains how to build an in-house FDE team, comparing three approaches—hiring experienced engineers, developing internal candidates, and partnering with external FDEs—along four axes: speed to launch, knowledge retention, first-year cost structure, and ease of exit. It recommends the sequence of securing internal candidates first, using external partners to establish a working template, then hiring to fill gaps, since FDE value derives largely from internal business knowledge. The article provides a 0–90 day plan (define problems, prototype, decide on production or exit), phased KPIs shifting from validated problems to production deployments to handoffs, and guidance tailored to company size and existing IT/DX organizations. It also contrasts in-house FDEs with SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers, and offers countermeasures against three failure modes: stalled PoCs, talent attrition, and broken feedback loops into products.

Key points

  • Compares hiring, internal development, and external partnering on four launch axes.
  • Recommended order: internal candidates first, external partners for templates, hiring last.
  • Phased KPIs: validated problems, then production deployments, then handoffs.
  • In-house teams suit firms with recurring issues across 3+ departments and 3-year in-house plans.
  • Countermeasures for stalled PoCs, attrition, and broken product feedback loops.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineering role that works on-site with customers and business units, handling everything from problem discovery to implementation and operational adoption. It is known as a role popularized by Palantir. FDE Soken (operated by VACAN Technologies, Inc.) is aimed at companies that want to build an FDE team in-house rather than sourcing FDEs externally, and compares three approaches to launching one: "hiring," "internal development," and "external support." The comparison criteria are FDE Soken's original "four launch axes" (speed to get up and running, likelihood of know-how staying in-house, first-year cost structure, and ease of exit if things fail). The 0-90 day plan is also presented in a ready-to-use format. The evaluation date is November 18, 2026.

What does launching an in-house FDE team mean? How does it differ from outsourced FDEs?

An in-house FDE team is a permanent team of your own employees who work on-site in business departments, handling everything from defining problems to implementation and adoption. The difference from outsourced FDEs is that know-how never leaves the company. The definition of FDE itself is explained in detail in What is an FDE?.

What is the definition of an in-house FDE team, and how does it differ from outsourced FDEs?

The difference between an in-house FDE team and outsourced FDEs is largest on two of FDE Soken's four launch axes: "likelihood of know-how staying in-house" and "ease of exit."

How to choose the contract type for outsourced FDEs (quasi-mandate or contract for work) is summarized in the FDE Contracting Guide.

What are the conditions for a company to benefit from an in-house FDE team?

An in-house FDE team is worthwhile for companies where the same type of operational problem recurs across three or more departments. If there is only a single problem, outsourced FDEs are often the more rational choice.

FDE Soken judges that it is worth considering an in-house approach if at least two of the following three conditions are met.

  • Data infrastructure and business systems exist in-house, and permissions can be provided for FDEs to access them
  • The managers of frontline departments can secure time with FDEs at least once a week
  • There is a premise that deliverables will be operated in-house for three years or more

Should you launch with hiring, internal development, or external support? How do first-year costs and timeframes compare?

Compared using FDE Soken's four launch axes, the combination that best retains know-how is external support for the first 90 days and internal development from the second year onward. Hiring is an acceleration measure only when experienced candidates can be secured. Cost breakdowns can be checked in FDE Implementation Costs.

First-year costs accumulate mainly from hiring fees and salaries for hiring, staff time for internal development, and contracting fees for external support.

What is the priority order when combining hiring, internal development, and external support?

FDE Soken's recommended priority is "secure internal development candidates → establish the playbook with external support → hire to fill gaps." The reason hiring is not placed first is that much of an FDE's value comes from your company's own business knowledge.

When conditions conflict, make decisions as follows.

  • An experienced hire was secured, but in-house business knowledge is shallow: Rather than having the experienced hire implement alone, pair them with an internal development candidate and prioritize absorbing business knowledge first
  • There are internal candidates, but no mentor: Contract external support as a "mentor substitute" for a limited period (e.g., 90 days)
  • Budget is secured, but frontline departments cannot spare time: Results are unlikely with any approach, so postpone the launch itself

The internal development curriculum is explained in detail in How to Develop FDEs.

How do the structure, KPIs, and evaluation systems change across the 0-90 day, 90-180 day, and post-180 day phases?

The KPIs of the in-house FDE team shift from "the number of issues validated in the field" during days 0-90, to "the number of solutions running in production" during days 90-180, and then to "the number of solutions handed over to operations" after day 180. Example KPI designs can be reviewed in FDE ROI and KPIs.

What should be done in the 0-90 day plan?

The goal for days 0-90 is to run through the full cycle from issue definition to prototyping at a single site, and to document the FDE team's "template."

  • Days 0-14: Narrow down to one target department and set up weekly regular meetings with the person in charge. Also complete applications for data access permissions at this stage
  • Days 15-45: The FDE enters the field to observe operations and identifies around five candidate issues, then narrows them down to one
  • Days 46-75: Build a prototype and have field staff use it in their actual work for at least two weeks
  • Days 76-90: Hold a meeting to decide whether to move to production or withdraw. Summarize the approach in an internal procedure document

This corresponds to the overall picture of the implementation process in FDE Implementation Steps.

How should the structure and evaluation systems change in the 90-180 day and post-180 day phases?

After 90 days, shift the evaluation criteria of the in-house FDE team from "the number of things built" to "the number of things continuously used in the field." It is important not to simply reuse the evaluation system designed for SWEs.

PeriodStructureMain KPIsKey points of the evaluation system
0-90 days1-2 FDEs plus field manager (plus external support)Number of issues validated in the field, continued use of prototypesEvaluate learning and documentation over results
90-180 days2-3 FDEs, expanding to two target departmentsNumber of solutions running in production, changes in field work timeInclude the field department manager's evaluation
After 180 daysEstablish as a permanent team, hand over to operations staffNumber of solutions handed over to operations, number of contributions back to products and platformsEvaluate successful handover and letting go

Note: Headcounts and KPIs are presented by FDE Soken as design examples for a launch plan (evaluation date: November 18, 2026). They are not industry averages.

From a practical field perspective, if changes to the evaluation system are delayed, FDEs are more likely to keep solutions to themselves rather than hand them over. For handover design, see System Handover in Practice.

How should an in-house FDE team be started, depending on company size, existing organization, and years of experience?

How you start an in-house FDE team depends less on company size and more on whether an information systems or DX department already exists. FDE Soken distinguishes the first move based on each organizational premise.

How should years of experience and English proficiency of development candidates be considered?

For in-house FDE development candidates, we look for an attitude of seeking to understand field operations and experience of seeing an implementation through to the end, rather than long years of experience.

  • Less than 3 years of development experience: To solidify implementation fundamentals, start in pairs with experienced developers or external support
  • 3-10 years of development experience: This is the easiest group to develop. Start by assigning field observation
  • 10+ years of development experience or managers: Rather than implementing themselves, they may be better suited to defining issues and coordinating between departments

English proficiency is unlikely to be a mandatory requirement for in-house FDE teams focused on domestic operations. However, when using overseas platforms (such as Palantir's Foundry), the ability to read technical documentation in English becomes necessary. From an individual career perspective, English Proficiency Required for FDEs is also a useful reference.

How do in-house FDE teams differ from SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers, and what kinds of organizations are they unsuitable for?

The distinctiveness of in-house FDE teams lies in one of FDE Soken's comparison axes: "whether the same person takes responsibility from issue definition through to operational adoption." Differences by role are explained in detail in Differences Between FDEs and SWEs.

What kinds of organizations are in-house FDE teams unsuitable for?

In-house FDE teams are unsuitable for organizations where field department managers cannot dedicate time, and for organizations centered on development where requirements are fixed from the start.

  • Unsuitable: There is essentially only one business issue, which can be sufficiently resolved through outsourcing
  • Unsuitable: FDEs cannot be granted access permissions to production data
  • Unsuitable: The evaluation system, which measures results by the same "development volume" as SWEs, cannot be changed
  • Suitable: The same type of issue occurs repeatedly across multiple departments, and in-house development over three or more years can be assumed

How can you prevent projects from stalling at PoC, talent attrition, and broken feedback loops to products?

Looking at the three major failures of in-house FDE teams through the four axes of launching an FDE research institute, they occur when one of the exit criteria, evaluation system, or feedback loop is missing. The structure of these failures is common to How to Read FDE Implementation Case Studies.

How do you prevent getting stuck at the PoC stage?

To prevent getting stuck at the PoC stage, agree in writing with the heads of the business departments on the "conditions for going to production" and the "conditions for withdrawal" before starting prototyping.

  • Define one condition for going to production in terms of an operational metric of the field (e.g., time required for the target task)
  • Set the period for using the prototype in the field to at least two weeks
  • At the decision meeting on day 90, always choose one of: go to production, extend, or withdraw (extension allowed only once)

How to engage the field is explained in detail in Change Management.

How do you prevent talent attrition and broken feedback loops to the product?

Talent attrition is prevented through the evaluation system, and broken feedback loops are prevented by defining the feedback path in advance. Both must be institutionalized within 180 days of launch.

  • Talent attrition: Since FDE experience is easily valued outside the company, prepare a career path and evaluation criteria separate from SWEs (field integration, handover, and feedback). Avoid assigning a single owner; use a two-person structure so knowledge doesn't become person-dependent
  • Broken feedback loops: Establish a monthly meeting to pass common issues found in the field to the product and platform owners (SWEs and PdMs). Record the number of items submitted and the number adopted

From the perspective of field operations, the more FDEs continue handling each site individually, the more opportunities to solve common issues collectively are lost. The value of an in-house FDE team is determined by whether it can establish a mechanism to feed individual solutions back into the product or platform.
