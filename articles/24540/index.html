18 Commercial Delivery, ROI, Acceptance, and Change Control

Original title: 18 商业化交付、ROI、验收与变更管控

FDE中国社区 · 2026-07-11 · 3 min read

Summary

This chapter from the FDE China community explains how to commercialize FDE projects: scoping, effort estimation, pricing, ROI, acceptance criteria, and change control. It opens with a case where a 20-person-day knowledge base PoC lost money because the contract only said "build a Q&A system," inviting scope creep. The author argues AI projects need firmer commercial boundaries as uncertainty grows, outlines pricing tiers from PoC to ongoing operations, and stresses writing explicit non-goals, measurable acceptance metrics backed by golden datasets, a change pool for non-critical requests, and post-trial customer success metrics covering adoption, trust, efficiency, economics, and risk.

Key points

  • Per-person-day pricing without scope boundaries causes losses.
  • Consulting is shifting to co-building and outcome-based delivery.
  • Pricing spans PoC, MVP, production, and ongoing operations tiers.
  • ROI must start from a documented manual-work baseline.
  • Acceptance needs golden datasets; changes go into a change pool.

Chapter Objectives

Master commercial delivery methods for FDE projects: scope locking, effort estimation, pricing, ROI, acceptance criteria, and requirement change control.

Opening Case: Why "Quoting by Person-Days" Often Loses Money

A team quoted 20 person-days for an enterprise knowledge base PoC. Initially the scope was only policy Q&A, but after the client trial, requests kept coming: integrating the approval system, supporting multi-department permissions, adding an IM entry point, building a backend configuration page, and exporting usage reports. The team found it hard to refuse, because the contract only said "deliver an intelligent Q&A system" without specifying what would not be done, acceptance samples, or change rules.

In the end, the project was both overdue and hard to accept. The problem was not development capability, but the lack of designed commercial delivery: an FDE project must clearly define scope, milestones, acceptance, a change pool, and customer success metrics.

The more uncertain an AI project is, the clearer its commercial boundaries must be.

Changes in Consulting Delivery Models

Last verified: 2026-07-11

Enterprise AI consulting is shifting from "report delivery" to joint coding, joint launch, and joint operations:

Old modelNew model
Diagnostic report + roadmap PPTCo-writing PoCs and co-defining acceptance samples with the client team
One-off strategy projectFixed-cycle implementation packages + ongoing optimization retainer
IT takes over after consultants leaveProfessional Services embedded on-site until adoption
Selling hours by person-dayOutcome-based delivery (by milestones or business metrics)

The core selling point of FDE-style delivery is: a runnable system + verifiable metrics + a handover-ready operations mechanism, not a thicker consulting deck.

TierTypical scopePricing logic

MVP / BetaMulti-role trial, basic permissions, evals and TraceMilestone payments + change pool
ProductionGateway, audit, regression gates, operations handoverImplementation + operations retainer
Ongoing operationsKnowledge base updates, model upgrades, sample maintenanceMonthly service package or outcome-based revenue share

How to Write Pricing Notes

## 本期范围

- 支持 1 个业务场景：客服售后政策工单。
- 接入 2 类知识资料：FAQ、SOP。
- 支持 1 个查询型工具：订单状态查询。
- 输出：分类、引用、回复草稿、转人工建议。

## 不在本期范围

- 不自动退款或赔付。
- 不接入全部历史系统。
- 不承诺替代人工坐席。
- 不覆盖所有产品线。

## 验收方式

- 30 条 Golden Dataset。
- 坐席试用 2 周。
- 指标：分类准确率、引用准确率、采纳率、高风险转人工准确率。

Writing down "what we will not do" is not being conservative; it is what makes the project deliverable.

Scope Locking

The project scope must clearly state:

  • What will be done.
  • What will not be done.
  • What comes first.
  • What comes later.
  • Which assumptions must hold to proceed.

A PoC should not promise full automation, replacing expert judgment, or directly modifying core systems.

Effort Estimation

Effort can be broken down into:

  • Requirements interviews.
  • Material preparation.
  • ETL.
  • RAG.
  • Agent / Workflow.
  • Tool integration.
  • Evals.
  • Deployment.
  • Training.
  • Operations handover.

ROI Plan

ROI should start with a manual baseline:

  • How many people are currently involved.
  • How long each instance takes.
  • How often per month.
  • What costs are caused by errors, rework, or delays.
  • Which metrics management cares about most.

AI transformation benefits can come from saving labor hours, reducing rework, shortening cycle times, improving conversion, lowering risk, and increasing satisfaction.

Acceptance Metrics

Common metrics:

  • Accuracy.
  • Recall.
  • Citation accuracy.
  • Tool success rate.
  • Generation time.
  • Human adoption rate.
  • Cost per task.

Acceptance samples should be defined early in the project to avoid changing standards right before launch.

Change Control

Every change request should be assessed for whether it adds:

  • Data sources.
  • System interfaces.
  • User roles.
  • Execution actions.
  • Compliance risk.
  • Evals samples.

Non-critical requirements go into the change pool without interrupting the current PoC.

Customer Success Metrics

Commercial delivery should also define post-trial customer success metrics:

  • Adoption: active users, task frequency, self-service completion rate.
  • Trust: human edit rate, citation acceptance rate, complaint rate.
  • Efficiency: time per task, queue time, first response time.
  • Economics: cost per task, labor hours saved, ROI payback period.
  • Risk: interception rate of high-risk actions, audit pass rate, number of rollbacks.

These metrics should be written into contract annexes or acceptance appendices to avoid vague commitments like "the system works."

Change Pool Example

ChangeBusiness valueTechnical impactRiskHandling
Add IM entry pointMediumNew integration and permissionsMediumNext phase
Integrate refund systemHighExecution-type toolHighRequires re-quoting
Add 10 evals samplesHighLowLowAbsorbed this phase
Support English repliesMediumModel and knowledge baseMediumSchedule after evaluation

The change pool lets the client know their requests are recorded, without silently swallowing the current delivery scope.

Hands-on Exercise

Write a one-page commercial proposal for your scenario:

  • Manual baseline.
  • AI transformation benefits.
  • Scope boundaries.
  • Effort assumptions.
  • Acceptance Metrics
  • Change rules.

Deliverables

  • ROI analysis.
  • Pricing and effort assumptions.
  • Acceptance criteria.
  • Change control table.

Common Pitfalls

  • Quoting only by development person-days.
  • Writing acceptance metrics as "the system works."
  • Putting uncontrollable goals into commitments.
  • Not managing post-trial scope creep.

Checklist

  • Is "what we will not do" clearly stated?
  • Does the ROI start from a manual baseline?
  • Are acceptance criteria backed by samples?
  • Are changes assessed for data, interface, permission, and risk impact?
