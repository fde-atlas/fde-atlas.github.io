How to Build an FDE Portfolio | Role Division Across 5 Media and Choosing Materials by Career Background

Original title: FDEポートフォリオの作り方｜5媒体の役割分担と経歴別の題材選び

FDE総研 · 2026-11-18 · 11 min read

Summary

FDE总研, run by VACAN Technologies, explains how engineers aiming to become FDEs should build a portfolio. Its core argument: unlike a normal developer portfolio showing what you can build, an FDE portfolio must prove you can repeat a full deployment—problem definition, on-site implementation, and adoption—at another customer. The site breaks this reproducibility into three elements and assigns distinct roles to five media: top page, case pages, README, demo video, and interview. It advises presenting quantitative results with baseline, measurement method, period, and personal scope, abstracting confidential details, choosing material based on experience, current role, English ability, and target company size, and checking secrets, links, and reproducibility before publishing.

Key points

  • FDE portfolios must show deployment reproducibility, not technical breadth.
  • Reproducibility has three parts: problem definition, constrained implementation, adoption mechanisms.
  • Five media (top page, case page, README, demo video, interview) each answer one question.
  • Quantified results need baseline, measurement method, period, and personal scope.
  • Material selection prioritizes missing process stages over language or seniority.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role pioneered by Palantir that embeds at the customer's site and takes responsibility for everything from problem definition to implementation and adoption on the ground. The FDE Research Institute, operated by VACAN Technologies, compares FDE careers and practical work using the same yardsticks of "scope of responsibility" and "reproducibility." This article organizes the portfolios built by engineers aiming to become FDEs along two axes: "role division across 5 media" and "choosing projects by career background." Since how to write documents is covered in How to Write an FDE Resume, this article covers presentation methods beyond documents.

What Is an FDE Portfolio? What Does "Implementation Reproducibility" That Recruiters Look At Mean?

An FDE portfolio is a collection of evidence demonstrating that you can reproduce the three stages of the Palantir-style process: problem definition, on-site implementation, and adoption.

An ordinary engineer's portfolio shows "what you can build." In contrast, an FDE portfolio needs to demonstrate "whether you can carry out an implementation to completion at another customer's site as well." Palantir's official blog presents a day in the life of a Forward Deployed Software Engineer as a continuous stream of work, from dialogue with customers to data integration and implementation.

What Recruiters Want to Verify Is Not Breadth of Technology but Implementation Reproducibility

FDE recruiters try to verify whether the same implementation could be redone for a different customer, rather than counting the technologies you can use.

For FDEs, both the customer's industry and data change with each assignment. For this reason, a single success story is unlikely to be enough for evaluation. Gergely Orosz's newsletter The Pragmatic Engineer also covers FDEs as a role that does engineering right next to the customer. It is safe to assume your work will be read with the premise that conditions differ at each site.

The FDE Research Institute Divides Implementation Reproducibility into 3 Elements

The FDE Research Institute evaluates implementation reproducibility by dividing it into three elements: "the path to problem definition," "implementation decisions under constraints," and "the mechanism for adoption."

Table 1: The 3 Elements of Implementation Reproducibility and the Evidence to Show in a Portfolio (compiled by the FDE Research Institute editorial team, as of November 2026)

ElementEvidence to show in the portfolioWhat often gets left out
Path to problem definitionThe difference between what was requested and the problem actually solvedOnly describing the process of implementing the request as-is
Implementation decisions under constraintsBudget, data, and permission constraints, and the options not takenMerely listing the names of technologies adopted
Mechanism for adoptionHandover documents, operational procedures, and how usage is verifiedThe description ends at the point of release

From the perspective of someone who reviews implementation projects at VACAN Technologies, we would weight the presence of descriptions verifying whether on-site staff continued to use the system afterward more heavily than whether a demo works. The third element, "adoption," is hard to demonstrate in personal development. Even so, simply designing how you will view usage logs will set you apart from other applicants.

What Should an FDE Portfolio Contain, and Where? How Are the Roles Divided Among the Top Page, Project Pages, README, Demo Videos, and Interviews?

An FDE portfolio uses 5 media—top page, project pages, README, demo videos, and interviews—tailored to the time the reader can spare.

If you repeat the same content in all 5 places, readers will be unsure where to look. The FDE Research Institute recommends assigning each medium to answer only "one question."

Table 2: Role Division Across the 5 Media of an FDE Portfolio (compiled by the FDE Research Institute editorial team, as of November 2026. Reader time is only a rough guide)

MediumMain readers and time guideQuestion to answerWhat to includeWhat not to include
Top pageScreening staff / a quick glanceWhat kind of problems do you solveOne-line summaries of around 3 representative projectsJust a list of your tech stack
Project pageOn-site FDEs and EMs / careful readingWhat did you solve and howProblem, constraints, decisions, results, limitationsClient names or internal figures
READMEEngineers / hands-on verificationHow does it runArchitecture diagram, setup steps, design trade-offsSales-oriented marketing copy
Demo videoIncluding non-technical interviewersHow is it used in the fieldAn operation flow from the user's perspectiveEndless code walkthroughs
InterviewThe entire hiring teamWhy did you make that decisionFailures not written down and what you learned from themReading aloud what is on the page

The Top Page and Project Pages Show "What You Solved" First

On an FDE portfolio's top page and project pages, write the problem you solved and the customer's constraints before the technologies you used.

If a project page is written in the 4-stage structure of "situation → decision → result → limitations," the areas probed in interviews become the headings themselves. It is important not to omit the final "limitations." In FDE interviews, how you convey what did not go well is likely to be scrutinized.

The README and demo video handle "how it works," while the interview handles "why you made that decision"

The FDE portfolio README and demo video are media for showing working proof, with the reasoning behind decisions left to be discussed in the interview.

In the README, include a setup procedure that can be reproduced step by step, plus a brief note on design alternatives you considered but rejected. Structure the demo video around the actual user's operations on-site rather than screens of code. That way, the value comes across even to non-technical business-side interviewers.

What's the difference between good and bad examples of FDE portfolios? How should you write quantitative results and confidential information?

Good FDE portfolio examples show results as before-and-after comparisons with measurement methods, and confidential information is abstracted in line with the approach of the Personal Information Protection Commission.

Table 3: Good and bad examples of FDE portfolios (compiled by the FDE Research Institute editorial team, as of November 2026)

PerspectiveGood exampleBad example
ResultsDescribe changes along with baseline values, measurement methods, and time periodJust writing "significantly improved efficiency"
Scope of responsibilitySeparate your own decisions from the team's achievementsPresenting the whole team's achievements as your own
ChallengesPresent both the original request and the problem you ultimately solvedStarting the story from technology selection
ConfidentialityAbstract to the level of industry, scale, and problem typeLeaving in screens or company names that identify the client

Write quantitative results with four elements: baseline value, measurement method, time period, and your scope of responsibility

Quantitative results in an FDE portfolio only become evaluable material when all four elements are present: baseline value, measurement method, time period, and scope of responsibility.

If you don't have the numbers on hand, don't fabricate them. Instead, describe "what metrics you designed the project to be measured by." What hiring managers want to see is not the size of the numbers, but whether you can design a system for measuring results yourself.

Abstract confidential information to the level of industry, scale, and problem type, and get permission to publish first

When handling confidential information in an FDE portfolio, rewrite it to a level of abstraction where the client cannot be identified, and check your previous employer's policies and get permission to publish first.

A level of granularity like "retail industry, several hundred stores, fragmented inventory data" is enough to convey the type of problem. Use dummy recreations for screen captures and sample data. If the scope of your previous employer's work rules or NDAs is unclear, deciding not to publish is also a realistic option.

How does the subject matter of an FDE portfolio change based on years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size?

The subject matter of an FDE portfolio changes based on four conditions: years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size. The FDE Research Institute recommends prioritizing processes missing from your current role and choosing subject matter by cross-referencing the FDE job role guide.

Let years of experience and current role determine the "star" of your subject matter

For the centerpiece of your FDE portfolio, prioritize subject matter that compensates for processes you lack experience in at your current role.

Table 4: Choosing subject matter by years of experience and current role (compiled by the FDE Research Institute editorial team, as of November 2026)

PrerequisitesSubject matter to featureSupporting subject matter
SWE with less than 3 years of experienceA small implementation where you defined a problem from your own work yourselfRecords of interviews with users
SWE with 5+ years of experienceA case of improvement you handled through to production operationHow you got involved from requirements definition
IT consultantA prototype where you carried your own proposal through to implementationREADME and reproducible code
PdMA case where you validated hypotheses with a prototype you built yourselfData integration implementation
SIerA case where you reinterpreted the business problems behind the specificationsEfforts to ensure adoption after release

Change the language and granularity of your materials based on English ability and target company size

For an FDE portfolio, it's realistic to vary the language and granularity depending on whether your target is a large foreign company or a domestic startup.

If you're targeting foreign or globally hiring companies, prepare the README and project page summaries in English. Also anticipate situations where you'll explain in English during the interview. If you're targeting domestic startups, writing in Japanese down to the business context tends to be evaluated more favorably. For English preparation, English skills required for FDE is also a useful reference.

When conditions conflict, prioritize the "missing process"

When the conditions for an FDE portfolio conflict, the FDE Research Institute recommends prioritizing in the order "missing process → language requirements → years of experience."

For example, consider someone with strong English ability but limited years of experience. Rather than enriching English materials first, prioritize creating one small case that covers the full cycle from problem definition to adoption. English ability can be demonstrated in the interview, but experiencing the full sequence of processes cannot be shown without subject matter.

How do portfolios for SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer differ from FDE-oriented ones? Who is not suited for FDE?

What makes an FDE-oriented portfolio different is that it consolidates the strengths of SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer into a single Palantir-style implementation case.

Each role's portfolio differs in where it draws the boundary of "results"

The portfolios of SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers differ from FDE-oriented portfolios in where they mark the boundary of results.

People not suited for FDE become apparent at the portfolio-building stage

Tendencies toward being unsuited for FDE tend to show up in whether you find yourself avoiding problem definition or user interviews during portfolio building.

People who want to focus on development with fixed specifications, or who feel strong stress from repeated interactions with customers, may perform better in SWE or product development roles. The overall picture of suitability is organized in People suited for FDE.

What should you check before publishing and submitting an FDE portfolio?

Before publishing your FDE portfolio, check four points: confidentiality, credentials, broken links, and the requirements of each application target. Inspect credentials using tools such as GitHub secret scanning.

Verify the four points with a pre-publication checklist

Before publishing an FDE portfolio, check four points in order: confidential information, credentials, broken links, and reproduction steps.

  • Whether customer names, personal information, or internal figures remain in screenshots, code, or commit history
  • Whether API keys or tokens are included in the repository (including past commits)
  • Whether demo URLs or video links are broken
  • Whether the README instructions work in an environment other than your own

Complete applications through official recruiting pages, and reorder your projects for each application target

Since the application process for FDE roles can only be completed through each company's official recruiting page, reorder the projects in your FDE portfolio to match each application target.

Read the job posting to determine whether the company prioritizes data platforms or production LLM operations. Then reorder the featured projects on your top page accordingly.
