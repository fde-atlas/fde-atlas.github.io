Summary

This article from DeFactory's media site, written by CEO Shohei Tokumitsu, advises freelance engineers in Japan on moving into upstream processes (requirements definition, basic design, PM/PMO). It claims the average freelance engineer earned about 800,000 yen per month in 2026, while upstream-capable freelancers command 1.0–1.5 million yen, with external technical advisors exceeding 2 million yen. Three motivations are given: higher rate ceilings, rates that rise with age, and lower replacement risk from generative AI. The author outlines three core skills (elicitation and articulation, documentation, architecture and technology selection), a three-step transition path starting with absorbing upstream tasks at one's current job, resume-writing tactics, contract scope protections, and AI-assisted workflows. It ends by mentioning Forward Deployed Engineer as an emerging top-tier upstream role, alongside promotion of DeFactory's own placement services, so readers should note its marketing slant.

Conclusion: The three core skills companies seek in upstream freelancers are: (1) "listening and articulation skills," (2) "documentation skills," and (3) "architecture design and technology selection ability." Only when all three are in place can rates exceeding ¥1,000,000 per month be justified.

Conclusion: The most important skill for an upstream freelancer is the ability to "translate a client's vague requests into implementable system requirements." The ability to draw out latent needs that clients themselves cannot articulate and organize them into structured requirements is the starting point of evaluation.

Here is a summary of the listening and articulation techniques practiced by excellent upstream freelancers.

  • As-Is/To-Be analysis: Diagramming current and target business workflows to structure the gaps
  • Quantifying pain points: Translating "inconvenient" or "slow" into "X hours wasted per month" or "takes 5 minutes to process"
  • Using the 5 Whys method: Repeating "why is this necessary" five times to extract the essential purpose
  • KPI tree structuring: Prioritizing individual requests by their contribution to higher-level KPIs
  • Writing personas and user stories: Describing requirements in the format "When A does X, they can do Y"
  • Prioritization with the MoSCoW method: Clarifying scope using Must/Should/Could/Won't

Listening and articulation skills cannot be acquired through books or training alone. The experience gained through dozens to hundreds of conversations with executives, business owners, and frontline users ultimately determines the quality of the output. This "accumulation of experience" is precisely what creates the barrier to entry for upstream freelancers and is the source of the scarcity that supports their rates.

Conclusion: The second core skill for upstream freelancers is the ability to "create documentation that allows other members to work without hesitation." Excellent documentation clearly separates the designer's decisions from the implementer's work, raising the productivity of the entire project.

In particular, "clearly stating scope boundaries in the requirements definition document" is a critical element directly tied to later rate negotiations and trouble prevention. Explicitly documenting "what is covered in this project" and "what is not covered" becomes a powerful weapon for preventing the "scope creep rate dumping" discussed later.

Conclusion: The third core skill is architecture design and technology selection ability that "moves beyond dependence on specific technologies to derive the optimal solution for the system as a whole." The essential change for an upstream freelancer is moving up from the level of "I can write Java" or "I can write Python" to the level of "I can compare multiple options and choose the solution best suited to the business."

Here is a summary of the typical comparison criteria used in technology selection.

  • Language and framework selection: Productivity of Java/Python/TypeScript/Go, etc., and ease of securing talent
  • Cloud selection: Cost and service characteristics of AWS/Azure/GCP, and compatibility with existing company assets
  • Architecture patterns: Monolith vs. microservices vs. serverless
  • Data store selection: Choosing between RDB/NoSQL/DWH/vector databases by use case
  • Scope of SaaS adoption: Deciding the boundary between in-house development vs. off-the-shelf SaaS vs. hybrid
  • AI/LLM integration: Choosing between in-house models vs. external APIs vs. RAG
  • Security measures: Designing phased countermeasures according to scale, industry regulations, and budget

These selections are inherently "a matter of no single correct answer." Even with the same business requirements, the optimal solution varies depending on company size, existing assets, budget, and personnel. The "immediate judgment ability" to organize the pros and cons of each option on a whiteboard in two to three minutes is a practical skill highly valued in the field. For a systematic explanation of architecture design, also see What Is an IT Architect? Roles, Essential Skills, and How to Go Independent as a High-Rate Freelancer.

Conclusion: For downstream freelancers to gain upstream experience, the most realistic approach is the small-step method of "intentionally taking over leader or PM tasks in your current project." Build a track record of "upstream-like work" in 3 to 6 months so you can describe it concretely on your skills sheet.

Here are the upstream tasks that are easiest to take over.

  • Basic design reviews: Review other members' design documents and propose improvements
  • Leading code reviews: Serve as facilitator for team code reviews
  • Attending requirements confirmation meetings: Participate in customer requirements confirmation meetings and create minutes
  • Impact analysis: Lead analysis of the scope of impact when specifications change
  • Technology selection proposals: Create comparison materials for selecting new libraries and tools
  • PoC implementation: Independently carry out pre-implementation PoCs and provide material for technical decisions
  • OJT for new members: Take on the role of mentoring new members on design
  • Incident response reviews: Analyze root causes of incidents and formulate preventive measures

Most of these are tasks you can take over by your own initiative. Superiors and leaders often welcome them as "reducing my own workload," so the risk of being refused is low. If you continue for six months, you will accumulate multiple achievements you can describe concretely on your skills sheet.

Conclusion: Upstream-phase freelancers can boost their productivity 2-3x by using generative AI not as an "enemy" but as a "powerful partner." Upstream freelancers who master AI produce larger deliverables in the same number of working days, which in turn leads to higher rates.
