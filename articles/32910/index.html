Forward deployed engineer vs deployment strategist

Kernavique · 2026-11-21 · 9 min read

Summary

Kernavique compared US job ads for forward deployed engineers and deployment strategists across 7 employers, read from public feeds on November 21, 2026. The dividing line is coding: five engineer ads require it, while four strategist ads ask for none or say it is unnecessary. Pay is inconclusive: the engineer range is higher at Palantir ($135,000–$200,000 vs $110,000–$170,000) and Picogrid, but lower at Databricks, and three pairs cannot be compared. Strategist roles often require travel, and two ads describe revenue ownership. The author notes limits: a single-day snapshot, hand-picked pairs, and two mismatched pairs.

Key points

  • Seven same-employer ad pairs were compared from feeds read on November 21, 2026.
  • Coding separates the roles: 5 engineer ads require it, 4 strategist ads do not.
  • Pay is mixed: engineer higher at Palantir and Picogrid, strategist higher at Databricks.
  • Deployment strategist postings mostly come from Palantir (17) and Databricks (7).
  • No ad mentions a quota; two strategist ads describe revenue or deal ownership.

How did we compare the two roles?

We compared ads from the same employer, read from each employer’s own public job feed on November 21, 2026.

The feeds cover 76 employer boards, mostly AI and tech companies. Seven employers had a US deployment strategist ad and a US forward deployed ad. All seven were paired.

For neighboring comparisons, see forward deployed engineer vs sales engineer, vs customer success engineer, vs solutions engineer and vs software engineer.

Who advertises deployment strategists?

Mostly Palantir. On November 21, 2026, the 76 feeds held 31 US postings with “Deployment Strategist” in the title, at 8 employers.

  • Palantir: 17. Three of them also carry “Forward Deployed” in the title.
  • Databricks: 7. Six of the titles also carry the abbreviation for forward deployed engineer, and one is a plain “Deployment Strategist”.
  • Picogrid: 2.
  • Lovable, ElevenLabs, n8n, Eudia and PolyAI: 1 each.

Seven of the 8 also advertise a US forward deployed role. PolyAI does not. For scale, the same feeds held 298 US postings titled forward deployed, at 65 employers.

How does Palantir describe each role?

Palantir’s two New York ads, read November 21, 2026, draw the line at code.

The engineer. The Forward Deployed Software Engineer ad describes engineers embedded with customers who build custom applications and own projects end to end. It asks for a “Strong coder with shown proficiency in programming languages such as Python, Java, C++”. It asks for 1+ years of experience and travel of up to 25%.

The strategist. The Deployment Strategist ad centers the role on using data in operations. Duties include meeting customer analysts on site and delivering demos. One duty reads “Work with Forward Deployed Engineers to integrate the data into a stable and extensible pipeline”. It asks for experience with programming, scripting or statistical packages, and travel of 25 – 75%.

The pay each publishes. The engineer ad gives an estimated salary range of $135,000 - $200,000/year. The strategist ad gives $110,000 - $170,000/year on the same label. Both say total pay may also include stock units, a sign-on bonus and other incentives.

The engineer ad lists four requirements and the strategist ad two. The strategist ad names forward deployed engineers. The engineer ad does not mention strategists.

Is the forward deployed engineer paid more?

In half of the pairs that can be compared. The tally uses the first salary figure each ad gives.

  • Higher at both ends: 2 pairs (Palantir and Picogrid).
  • Lower at both ends: 1 pair (Databricks).
  • Mixed: 1 pair (Lovable, the same at the bottom and higher at the top against Region A).
  • The same: none.
  • Not comparable: 3 pairs. ElevenLabs and n8n give no pay in either ad. Eudia’s strategist ad gives none.

Kernavique reading of employers’ ads, November 21, 2026. A full bar is all 7 pairs.

Palantir and Picogrid use one label for both roles. The Databricks and Lovable ads all carry a sentence saying the range can mean on-target earnings for some roles. No ad puts a value on equity, so total pay cannot be compared.

What do the requirement lines show?

Counts are from the 7 pairs, read November 21, 2026.

Coding. Five forward deployed ads require it and Picogrid’s prefers it. Lovable’s says “you will design, write, and ship production code”. Eudia’s lawyer ad does not ask. Three strategist ads ask at a lighter level (Palantir, Databricks and ElevenLabs). n8n’s says “You do not need to write code”. The other three do not ask.

Customers and scope. Databricks tells its strategist “You are a product manager for the field”. Picogrid’s strategist ad says “This is not a traditional engineering role or a conventional customer-success position.”

Travel. Five ads on each side state it. Picogrid’s strategist ad asks for up to 50%, and its engineer ad states none.

Quota and revenue. The word “quota” is in none of the 14 ads. Two strategist ads describe revenue or deal ownership. Eudia’s says “you carry the number”, and ElevenLabs lists a duty to “Own flagship deals end-to-end”. No forward deployed ad does.

Years. Both ads give a number in 3 pairs. The strategist floor is higher at Databricks and at Eudia, where the other ad counts years of practicing law, and lower at Picogrid. Palantir’s strategist ad gives none.

Which should you choose?

This is our reading of the counts, not a finding.

Choose the engineer role if you want to ship code. Five of 7 forward deployed ads require it.

Choose the strategist role if you want to scope, prioritize and run the customer relationship. Four of 7 strategist ads ask for no code or say none is needed. Expect travel, stated in 5 of 7, and ask whether you would own revenue, as 2 of 7 ads describe.

Which should you hire?

Again, our reading.

Hire the engineer when the gap is building. Hire the strategist when the gap is deciding what to build and keeping the customer aligned. Palantir advertises both and has its strategists work with its engineers.

Do not assume the strategist costs less. The published range was lower at Palantir and Picogrid and higher at Databricks.

What are the limits of this comparison?

  • The pairs were picked by hand. Palantir has 17 strategist postings and Databricks 7. We read one of each.
  • Two pairs mismatch. Lovable’s forward deployed ad is Staff and its strategist ad has no level word. Eudia’s forward deployed ad is a lawyer role, not an engineer.
  • Three of 7 pairs have no comparable pay.
  • This is one day’s read. It is not a market measure and shows no trend.
  • The ads were not read end to end. We read headings, bullet lines and keyword lines. “Not stated” means none of those lines said it.

In practice

If you are an engineer. Read both ads at the same employer and ask who writes the code and who owns the customer. Browse open forward deployed engineer jobs, or send us your details and résumé.

If you are hiring. Write down which of the two jobs you need before you pick the title. If you want help defining the role, you can ask us about hiring forward deployed engineers.
