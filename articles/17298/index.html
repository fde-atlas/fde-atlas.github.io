Module 13: Offers, negotiation and the first months

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 2 min read

Summary

Using Scale AI's Forward Deployed Engineer pay as a case study, this article teaches how to cite salary figures correctly. It distinguishes three data sources: posted base bands on job listings, offered base wages in certified H-1B filings (October 2024–June 2026), and self-reported Levels.fyi data (a $351K median total from 6 submissions as of September 2026). Each source measures a different slice of compensation—filings exclude bonus, equity, and non-sponsored hires; self-reports suffer from selection bias. The article provides template sentences for quoting each source with date, basis, and sample size, and argues that conflicting figures can all be accurate if properly qualified.

Key points

  • Three pay data types: posted bands, H-1B filings, self-reports—each covers a different slice.
  • Scale AI example: Levels.fyi median total of $351K from only 6 submissions.
  • H-1B filings show offered base wage only, excluding bonus, equity, and non-sponsored staff.
  • Always state a figure's date, basis, level, and sample size before quoting it.
  • A self-reported total above a posted base band isn't a contradiction—they measure different things.

Three kinds of pay data

Name a figure’s source before you quote it: each sees a different slice of the same offer.

Source What it measures What it leaves out
Posted band The range one posting states, on the day you read it Bonus and equity unless it names them; it may omit the basis and the level
H-1B filing The offered base wage an employer filed for a sponsored position Bonus, equity, anyone not sponsored, other titles
Self-report What people chose to submit, usually labeled total or base Everyone who didn’t submit, and the level unless it’s given

Each has its own sentence to say aloud:

  • “As of [month and year], the posting lists [range] as [base, , or no stated basis].”
  • “In H-1B filings certified from [start] to [end], the median offered base wage, taking the lower bound of each, was [figure], from [n] filings.”
  • “As of [month and year], Levels.fyi showed a median total of [figure] from [n] submissions.”

A self-reported total above a posted base band makes neither wrong: they see different slices.
