What is a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)?

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a technical professional who brings their company's product directly into the customer's business environment, writing code on-site to support implementation and adoption. The model originated from the organizational structure established by Palantir Technologies in the early 2010s, with the name derived from the military metaphor of engineers being "deployed to the front lines (forward)." Its defining feature is that engineers who can write code—not consultants or salespeople—embed themselves inside the customer's operations.

※ Source: FDE Academy, "How Palantir Invented the Forward Deployed Engineer Model" (retrieved May 2026)

We also offer a Complete Guide to Forward Deployed Engineers that systematically traces how the FDE role emerged at Palantir and why OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google all adopted it en masse in 2025-2026, covering everything from its origins to the latest trends and a comparison of five related roles.

Why It Matters

In enterprise AI adoption, the challenge of "a great product that never takes root in the field" is universal. Under the traditional SI model, the waterfall process of requirements definition → design → development → delivery takes months, during which business requirements often change.

The FDE model fundamentally reverses this problem. Engineers are stationed on-site, show a working prototype the next day, and reflect user feedback directly into the code on the spot. Palantir describes this cycle as "turning a gravel road into a paved highway." The feedback loop in which field insights directly drive product improvement is the core of the FDE model.

As OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Scale AI, and Glean began large-scale hiring for FDE roles in 2025-2026, this role has been elevated to "standard equipment in AI companies' enterprise strategies."

※ Source: Anthropic "Forward Deployed Engineer" job posting (retrieved May 2026)

How FDE Works — The Echo × Delta Dual Structure

The FDE role as defined by Palantir can be organized along two axes: Echo and Delta.

AxisDescriptionConcrete Actions
Echo (capturing the voice of the field)Observes customer operations and accurately articulates pain points and needsUser interviews, business flow analysis, log analysis
Delta (driving change)Immediately implements solutions in code, creating a delta (difference)Prototyping, API integration, workflow automation

The decisive difference from the traditional model that separates "the person who gathers requirements (the consultant)" and "the person who writes code (the engineer)" is that a single engineer handles both axes.

Key Skill Sets Required of an FDE

  • Coding ability: Implementation skills to rapidly build prototypes in Python, TypeScript, etc.
  • Domain understanding: Business knowledge of the customer's industry, such as manufacturing, finance, healthcare, or government
  • Communication skills: The ability to translate technology for audiences ranging from executives to frontline staff
  • Product sense: The judgment to decide whether a feature should be incorporated into the product itself
  • Ability to leverage AI agents and LLMs: Since 2025, the ability to combine generative AI tools in the field has become essential

※ Source: Palantir Technologies "Forward Deployed Engineering" careers page (retrieved May 2026)

FDE vs. SE / SI / Consultant

FDEs are often confused with existing roles, but there are clear differences.

Solutions Engineer (SE)Implementation support, demosLimitedShort-termIndirect
SIer (SI)Requirements definition through deliveryYesProject durationAlmost none

The biggest difference from Solutions Engineering is "whether or not you enter the product's feedback loop." Issues discovered by FDEs in the field are designed to be reflected in the following week's product updates.

Hiring Trends in 2025-2026 and Significance for the Japanese Market

FDE hiring status at major AI companies (2025-2026):

  • OpenAI: Large-scale hiring under the title "Applied AI Engineer," focused primarily on supporting enterprise customers' API usage
  • Anthropic: Publicly recruiting "Forward Deployed Engineers" to support enterprise adoption of Claude from the field
  • Google DeepMind / Google Cloud: Expanding similar roles for Gemini's enterprise rollout
  • Scale AI: Established an FDE structure for field integration of data quality and AI pipelines
  • Glean: Maintains an FDE organization specialized in driving adoption of its workplace search AI

The FDE model carries particular significance for the Japanese market. Japanese companies have a culture that values "vendors who stay close to the field," so the FDE approach of "being on-site and building together" aligns well with existing business practices. On the other hand, due to challenges such as English requirements and salary levels for engineers, FDE-style startups originating in Japan are still rare.

The scarcity of professionals who understand MCP (Model Context Protocol) and prompt engineering while also understanding customer operations continues to raise the market value of FDEs.

Practical Use Cases

What SMEs, professional service firms, and e-commerce businesses can learn from the FDE model

While the FDE role may seem specific to major AI companies, the underlying approach can be applied directly to small and mid-sized AI implementations.

  • Professional practices (lawyers, tax accountants, labor consultants): When using Dify or n8n to automate routine document generation, the FDE mindset means not just "handing over the tool" but customizing it to fit actual workflows and embedding it into daily operations.
  • E-commerce businesses: When automating product descriptions and AI-powered customer support, it's important to keep iterating alongside frontline staff after implementation so they don't abandon the tool.
  • Retail and service businesses: Even after introducing reservation management or automated inquiries, the process of continuously capturing exception patterns from real-world operations is exactly the "gravel road → paved highway" that the FDE model describes.

Common Misconceptions and Cautions

Misconception 1: "FDE = advanced AI researcher" What an FDE needs is not cutting-edge research ability, but "the implementation skill to get a product running immediately in the context of a customer's business." They are evaluated not on writing papers, but on whether they can deliver a usable prototype on-site by the next day.

Misconception 2: "It's only relevant to big AI companies, not SMEs" The essence of the FDE model is not the job title but the philosophy of "supporting adoption by writing code in the field." For SMEs adopting AI tools, avoiding a "hand it over and walk away" approach is practicing the FDE mindset.

Misconception 3: "It's just a consultant rebranded as an FDE" Consultants deliver recommendations, while FDEs write the code themselves to implement change. The fundamental difference is that FDEs take technical execution responsibility. They also play a role in feeding discovered issues back into the core product.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What skills are needed to become an FDE?

The three fundamentals are: coding ability to actually implement things (Python or TypeScript as a benchmark), understanding of business processes in a specific industry, and communication skills to translate technology for everyone from executives to frontline staff. Since 2025, hands-on experience with generative AI and LLMs has become nearly a mandatory requirement. Anthropic's job postings explicitly state "the ability to actually write code that solves customers' technical challenges."

Q. How does an FDE differ from a Solutions Engineer?

A Solutions Engineer mainly handles pre-sales (technical demos and requirements gathering before deals) and often hands off to another team after the contract is signed. An FDE, by contrast, takes long-term responsibility for on-site adoption and implementation support after the contract, and feeds discovered issues directly back to the product team. The biggest difference is whether the role is integrated into the product feedback loop.

Q. For Japanese companies adopting the FDE model, where should they start?

The first step is a mindset shift: "don't just hand over AI tools and walk away." Concretely, design a process where, for the first 2–4 weeks after implementation, weekly improvement meetings are held with frontline staff and exception patterns that emerge are continuously reflected in the workflow. When working with external partners, it's important to choose a contract model that "supports adoption while working in the field" rather than "delivering and finishing."
