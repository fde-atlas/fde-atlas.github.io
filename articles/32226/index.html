FDE Firm vs Hiring Your Own Forward Deployed Engineers

Reveneau · 2026-10-21 · 14 min read

Summary

This Reveneau guide compares hiring forward deployed engineers as employees versus contracting an FDE firm, using public data only. Levels.fyi shows Palantir FDE pay of $201K–$590K (median $233K); Tenex lists $224K–$394K for its New York role, but no firm publishes client prices. Bloomberry found FDE job postings up 1,165% year over year, making hiring slow and competitive. An MIT Project NANDA report found externally built customized AI tools reached deployment 67% of the time versus 33% in-house, though figures are self-reported and preliminary. The article concludes: hire in-house for long-term, data-sensitive work; use a firm for speed, short engagements, or when no one can evaluate candidates; or combine both with a contractually planned handover so knowledge stays with the company.

Key points

  • Palantir FDE median pay is $233K; Tenex lists $224K–$394K in New York.
  • FDE job postings rose 1,165% year over year, per Bloomberry data.
  • MIT NANDA: external AI builds deployed 67% vs 33% in-house, preliminary figures.
  • In-house suits multi-year, data-sensitive work; firms suit fast, short projects.
  • A mixed path with a contractual handover keeps knowledge in-house.

This page sets out what public data says about each path: pay figures from Levels.fyi and from one firm's job listings, the rise in FDE job postings that Bloomberry measured, and an MIT report on outside partners. Key takeaways

  • Levels.fyi, from user-submitted data, gives a range of $201K to $590K a year for forward deployed engineers at Palantir in the United States, with a median of $233K.
  • Tenex lists pay of $224K to $394K for its own Forward Deployed Engineer role in New York. That is pay to the engineer, and no firm publishes its price to clients.
  • Bloomberry found FDE job postings from January to October 2025 were 1,165 percent higher than in the same months of 2024, so many employers are looking for the same people.
  • A firm can start sooner and replace people who leave. Your own team keeps the knowledge. A mixed path, with a firm first and a planned handover, uses both.

A company that wants engineers working inside its own teams has two ways to get them. It can hire forward deployed engineers as its own employees, or it can contract a firm that supplies them. This page compares the two, using only public figures, each with its source. The guide to forward deployed engineering explains the role itself, and its page on how to hire a forward deployed engineer covers recruiting in detail.

What the two paths mean

Your own forward deployed engineers are employees. You find them, interview them, pay them a salary and benefits, and manage them. They stay when the first project ends, and what they learn stays with your company.

An FDE firm employs the engineers, or in some cases works with contractors, and places them inside your team under a contract. You pay the firm. The firm finds the people, replaces anyone who leaves, and brings its own method. When the contract ends, the engineers go back to the firm.

A third path, staff augmentation, adds engineers who take tasks from your own leads. The FDE guide compares it in forward deployed engineer vs staff augmentation.

What public pay data shows

Two public sources give pay figures for the role.

Source What it covers Figure
Levels.fyi [1] Forward deployed engineers at Palantir in the United States, from 226 salary reports that users submitted, updated 15 October 2026 $201K to $590K a year; median $233K
Tenex careers page [2] Pay for Tenex's own Forward Deployed Engineer role, on site in New York City, read 20 October 2026 $224K to $394K

Both figures are pay to an engineer, and a firm's price to a client is a separate number that no firm in this guide publishes. The Levels.fyi range covers one employer, and its figures come from people who chose to report them. The Tenex range covers one role at one firm in one city.

An employer pays more than the salary. It also pays for benefits, recruiting, equipment, managers' time and the weeks before a new hire is fully working. A firm's price has to cover the same costs for its own staff, plus its offices, its sales work, the time its engineers spend between projects, and a profit. So a firm's rate for an engineer will be higher than that engineer's pay. Whether the total cost is higher depends on how long you need the work and how fast you could hire.

Why hiring your own can take time

Henley Wing Chiu of Bloomberry, using job data from Revealera, found that postings for forward deployed engineers from January to October 2025 were 1,165 percent higher than in the same months of 2024. He found that October 2025 had the most FDE postings of any month in the data, and that 58 percent of the roles were at companies with 11 to 200 employees [3]. More postings mean more employers looking for the same people. Bloomberry describes most of those employers as growing startups, and a company hiring its first FDE competes with them for the same candidates.

Joe Schmidt of a16z wrote in June 2025 that, at the best companies that sell complex AI applications, the person who makes the product work for each customer is often a professional services employee, "sometimes rebranded as a forward deployed engineer" [4]. He counted 22 of the 311 open roles on OpenAI's careers page in those categories at the time. His essay argues that software vendors accept lower margins, meaning a smaller share of each sale left as profit, to pay for this work. It is written for software vendors, so use it as evidence of the demand, and of why those vendors spend on the role.

What a firm adds

  • People sooner. A firm can place engineers it already employs. Your own search has to begin by finding candidates.
  • A replacement when someone leaves. The firm finds the next person, and the contract can say how fast.
  • A method. Each firm brings its own way of working. Reveneau, by its own account, has AI write all of its code and requires every change to pass a large eval suite, a set of automated tests written from the specification, before release.
  • Skills you need for one stage only. A firm can add a data engineer for a month and then remove the role.

What a firm takes away

  • The knowledge, unless it is handed over. When the engineers leave, what they learned about your systems leaves with them. A written handover plan in the contract is the fix.
  • A share of the price as margin. You pay for the firm's own costs and profit.
  • Some control over who does the work. The firm picks and moves its people. TechCrunch reported in 2024 that Tribe AI builds client projects with a network of over 500 contractors [12], which is one reason to ask who exactly will work on your project.

What one MIT report found about outside partners

MIT's Project NANDA report, The GenAI Divide, published in July 2025, found that in its sample, customized AI tools that can learn from use, built with external partners, reached deployment, meaning they were put into use, 67 percent of the time. Tools built inside the company did so 33 percent of the time. The report marks both figures as approximate [5]. It also says the figures come from what companies reported about themselves, may not account for every other cause, and are preliminary. The site that hosts the copy read for this guide summarizes its method as 52 interviews with executives, 153 surveys and more than 300 reviews of AI projects.

The report compares outside partners in general with in-house builds and gives no separate figure for FDE firms. A single report should not decide a large purchase. It is the only public study found for this guide that compares outside help with in-house builds for AI work.

What Amazon designs for the end of a deployment

Amazon said on 30 June 2026 that its own AWS forward deployed engineering model is designed so customers are self-sufficient when a deployment ends [6]. That is a goal any company can write into a contract with any firm: the engineers leave, and your own staff can run and change the system. If you hire a firm, ask how it will meet that goal. If you hire your own engineers, the knowledge stays in your company for as long as they do.

How much time your own FDEs spend with users

If you hire your own, decide how their time is split. First Round Review reported in February 2026 that at Serval, a software company, FDEs are expected to spend "around 20%" of their time with customers and to focus on building product features [7]. Inside a company that buys software, the users are your own staff, and the split may be different. Write it into the job description.

The two paths side by side

Question Your own FDEs An FDE firm
Who employs the engineers You The firm, or its contractors
What you pay Salary, benefits, recruiting and management The firm's rate, which covers those costs plus its margin
How soon work can start After a search you run yourself After the contract, with people the firm already has
Who replaces an engineer who leaves You The firm, under the contract
Who keeps the knowledge Your company The firm, unless the contract requires a handover
How the work ends It continues while the engineers stay At the end date, with a handover

When hiring your own makes sense

Hire your own forward deployed engineers when the work will last for years, when the systems hold data that must stay inside your company, and when you can wait for the hiring search to finish. You also need someone senior who can judge an FDE's work in an interview. The FDE guide's page on how to hire a forward deployed engineer covers that step.

When a firm makes sense

Hire a firm when you need people soon, when the work has a clear end, when you need several skills for a short time, or when nobody on your staff can judge an FDE's work yet. Contract for a handover from the first day, so the knowledge stays when the firm leaves.

The mixed path

The two paths can be combined. A firm starts the work while you hire. As your own engineers join, the firm's engineers pair with them and then leave on a set date. The FDE guide's page on handover and exit explains what a handover should contain.

What the firms on this list say about staying and leaving

Each firm describes the length of its work in its own words.

  • Reveneau says it takes responsibility for the whole project, through production and after release.
  • Fluxon says its builders "stay for as long as you need them", and that "What we leave behind should feel like your own" [8].
  • Factored says its more than 500 engineers let it "expand teams as your systems, priorities, and technical requirements evolve" [10].
  • 10Pearls offers one senior FDE, several engineers as one unit, or a team that stays in place [9].
  • Tenex says, "We become an extension of your team, building whatever you need" [14].
  • Artium says it brings forward deployed engineers to build AI agents and to change how a client's own engineering teams work [13].
  • HatchWorks AI says its FDEs build with Claude and "train your organization" to keep the new way of working [11].

Ask each firm the same three questions. How long does a typical engagement of this size run? What is the handover plan? And what will your own staff be able to do on the day after the firm leaves?

Questions to decide between the paths

  • How long will this work last: months, or years?
  • Can you wait for a hiring search, and who will run it?
  • Does anyone on your staff know how to judge an FDE's work?
  • Must the data and the knowledge stay inside your company?
  • What will your staff need to run on their own when outside help ends?

Common questions

Is it cheaper to hire forward deployed engineers or to use a firm?

No public data answers that, because no firm in this guide publishes a price. A firm's rate covers the engineer's pay plus its own costs and profit, so the rate is higher than a salary. Hiring your own adds recruiting, benefits and management, and takes time. Compare total cost over the length of the work, with each firm's quote.

What does Levels.fyi show for forward deployed engineer pay at Palantir?

Levels.fyi, from 226 salary reports that users submitted, gives a range of $201K to $590K a year for forward deployed engineers at Palantir in the United States, with a median package of $233K. The page was last updated on 15 October 2026. The figures cover one employer and come from people who chose to report them.

How much does Tenex pay its own forward deployed engineers?

Tenex's careers page, read on 20 October 2026, lists pay of $224K to $394K for its Forward Deployed Engineer role, which is full time and on site in New York City. That is what Tenex pays its engineer. Tenex says it charges clients by story points of work delivered, and it publishes no client price.

Why can forward deployed engineers be hard to hire?

Many employers want the same people. Bloomberry, using Revealera job data, found that FDE postings from January to October 2025 were 1,165 percent higher than in the same months of 2024, and that October 2025 had the most postings in its data. Bloomberry describes most of those employers as growing startups, so a company hiring its first FDE competes with them for the same people.

Does the MIT NANDA report show that outside partners do better than in-house teams?

It found that in its sample, customized AI tools that learn from use, built with external partners, were put into use 67 percent of the time, against 33 percent for tools built in-house, with both figures marked as approximate. The report says the results are self-reported and preliminary. It compares outside partners in general with in-house builds and gives no separate figure for FDE firms.

When should a company hire its own forward deployed engineers?

Hire your own when the work will last for years, when the data must stay inside the company, and when you can wait for a hiring search. You also need a senior person who can judge an FDE's work in an interview. Your own engineers keep what they learn about your systems, which a firm's engineers take with them.

When does an outside FDE firm make more sense than hiring?

A firm fits when you need people soon, when the work has a clear end, when you need several skills for a short time, or when nobody on staff can judge an FDE's work yet. Put a handover plan in the contract from the first day, so the knowledge stays with your company after the firm's engineers leave.

Can a company start with an FDE firm and build its own team later?

Yes. A firm can start the work while you hire. As your own engineers join, the firm's engineers work beside them and then leave on a set date. The contract should name that date and list what the handover includes, such as documents, tests, access and working sessions with your new staff.

What does Amazon say should happen when its FDEs leave a customer?

Amazon said on 30 June 2026 that its AWS forward deployed engineering model is designed so customers are self-sufficient when a deployment ends. In plain words, the customer's own staff should be able to run and change the system alone. A buyer can ask any firm to commit to the same goal in writing.

Which FDE firms on this list say they train the client's own staff?

HatchWorks AI says its FDEs train the client's organization. Artium says it changes how client engineering teams work. Tenex offers training and help for staff to adopt new ways of working. Fluxon says what it leaves behind should feel like the client's own. Each statement is the firm's own, so ask for the training plan in writing.

FDE firms that also sell their own platform

Some forward deployed engineering firms sell two things: engineers who work inside your team, and their own software that your new system then runs on. In this guide, Distyl AI, Grid Dynamics, Turing and Tredence describe their own platforms on their own pages. Other firms have ties to an AI model maker or a cloud provider, and two say they are neutral about which tools a client uses. This page shows what each firm states, who states it, what a platform changes in the contract, and which questions to ask before you sign.

FDE firms owned by larger companies and investors

Several forward deployed engineering firms in this guide belong to, or are part-owned by, a larger company or investor. Anthropic and its partners own Ode, General Catalyst owns Percepta, AlixPartners bought Artium, and Advent International took a minority stake in Tredence in 2022. Grid Dynamics is a public company with a Chinese minority shareholder. This page explains each tie in plain words, names who reported it, and lists the questions a buyer should ask, because an owner can affect what a firm recommends and who it works for.
