Forward deployed engineer demand: what the numbers mean

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 17 min read

Summary

This article audits the conflicting statistics on forward deployed engineer demand. It argues that while every dataset shows growth, the headline figures measure different units over different windows and cannot be compared directly. The author proposes five questions to ask of any figure and separates measurements from projections. Plank counted 1,333 live FDE postings across 565 companies in September 2026; the author's own count of 103 employer job boards found 806 postings, led by Databricks, Palantir and Salesforce. It also explains why Business Insider misreported Indeed index values as job counts, and why search-result counts overstate the market.

Key points

  • FDE demand is real, but headline growth figures use different units, windows and baselines.
  • Plank counted 1,333 live FDE postings across 565 companies in September 2026.
  • The author's count of 103 employer boards found 806 postings; Databricks led with 117.
  • Business Insider corrected its story after misreading Indeed index values as posting counts.
  • Search-result counts are not job counts; filter on exact job titles.

Every few weeks another headline says postings grew by some enormous percentage, and no two headlines quote the same figure. If you are deciding whether to point your job search at FDE roles, you want to know which of those numbers to believe. If the job itself is still fuzzy, start with our guide to what a forward deployed engineer is; this post is about the numbers.

Yes, FDE demand is real: every growth figure in our research points up, and in its September 2026 analysis, Plank, a staffing firm, counted 1,333 live FDE postings across 565 companies. Source 1FDE Job Market: 1333 Forward-Deployed Engineer RolesPublisherPlank (IDYIA, LLC), staffing firmSource typeindependent analysis But the headline growth figures measure different things, over different months, in different units, so you cannot set one beside another. Below, each figure gets its source, its window and its unit, then what the numbers mean for your search and what to say if the market comes up in an interview.

The short answer: demand grew, and the headlines still mislead

Postings for the role grew fast. In Bloomberry’s analysis of postings titled “forward deployed engineer” from January to October 2025, the count was up 1,165% on the same months of 2024. Source 2What I learned analyzing 1K forward deployed engineer jobsPublisherBloomberry (Henley Wing Chiu), published Nov 18 2025, updated Jan 25 2026Source typeindependent analysis It is one of the clearer figures: it names its dataset, window and unit.

So hold three things at once:

  • Postings grew. Every dataset that measures growth agrees on the direction.
  • The size of the growth depends on who measured, what, and when. That is why the figures disagree.
  • None of them tells you your odds. No figure here counts applicants, and a posting is a request to hire, not a hire.

For which employers built FDE teams and when, read the free lesson Why the role spread when it did. It makes the case this post expands on: in an interview, a company’s own dated decision is better evidence than any market multiple.

Every growth figure, with its source, window and method

Before you trust a figure, ask it five questions. This is our reading method, not an industry standard:

  • Who measured it, and do they sell something related?
  • What is the unit: postings, an index, “positions”, or answers to a survey?
  • What is the window, and what is it compared with?
  • Is it a measurement or a projection?
  • What does it cover: one job site, one marketplace, employers’ own boards?

What an index value is

An index picks one month as its base and expresses every later month relative to it. It does not tell you how many postings existed in either month, and a huge percentage on a small base can still be a modest number of jobs.

Check Business Insider’s arithmetic

The year-over-year figure reported by Business Insider follows from its own two figures, read as percent above the January 2025 base: Source 11Job postings for this tech role have grown more than 700% in the last yearPublisherBusiness Insider (syndicated on AOL), Cadie Thompson and Lakshmi Varanasi, updated 18 May 2026Source typenews reportSource 12The rise of the forward-deployed engineer (FDE)PublisherLeadDevSource typenews report

# Read as percent above the base
apr_2025 = 1 + 5.43   # 543% above base
apr_2026 = 1 + 52.30  # 5,230% above base
print(f"{apr_2026 / apr_2025 - 1:.0%}")  # 729%
# If they were raw index values on a base of 100
print(f"{5230 / 543 - 1:.0%}")  # 863%

Indeed’s public postings index sets its base to 100 (Indeed Hiring Lab’s job postings tracker). An index value is not the same as a percent above the base, so without the raw values you cannot tell which one Business Insider was given. That is another reason to quote an index only as an index: one dataset, one base, and never beside Bloomberry’s or Lightcast’s figures.

The rule: never compare growth from different datasets

This is our rule, and our research follows it: compare a figure only with another from the same dataset, measured the same way. Every pair of figures above fails at least one of the five questions.

Three mistakes, and the fix for each

  • Stacking figures as if they agree. They agree on direction and nothing else. Fix: quote one, with its source and window.
  • Reading an index as jobs, as the Business Insider story first did. Fix: call it an index, or leave it out.
  • Reading a snapshot as a trend. A one-day count, ours or anyone’s, cannot show growth. Fix: only two counts from the same method, on different dates, show change.

What to say if it comes up in an interview

When an interviewer asks why you want this role now, a growth statistic is a weak answer: anyone can quote it, and it says nothing about the company. The company’s own dated decision does.

If you do mention the market, mention one figure the way an analyst would, then move to the company. For an OpenAI interview:

“In Bloomberry’s analysis, postings titled forward deployed engineer from January to October 2025 were up 1,165% on the same months of 2024. What the numbers do not tell a job seeker

No dataset in this post measures any of these:

  • How many people apply. A posting count has no count of applicants beside it.
  • How many postings become hires. A posting can close unfilled or be reposted.
  • Your odds. No employer publishes an FDE pass rate, and the pass-rate figures that circulate come from prep sites and blogs that cite no data of their own, or cite each other.   • Whether the title means one job. The same title covers different work at different employers. The lesson Who hires FDEs, and under which titles sorts them.

Growth in postings tells you the door is wider than it was, not how many people are walking through it with you.

What to do with this in your search

Use the numbers to decide where to look, then switch to evidence you can check yourself.

Turn the demand numbers into a search plan

  • Start from the employers with the most postings in one dated count, and add the ones you care about.
  • Search job sites by title, not keyword, and read each posting in full.
  • Note the date you read each posting, because postings close and reopen.
  • Sort each claim you meet into published, reported or unknown with the evidence map.
  • For each target, find the company’s own dated FDE decision and build your “why now” on it.
  • Drop any figure you cannot trace to a source, a window and a unit.

If you can only work remotely, filtering on a job site’s remote flag is not enough, because a posting marked remote can still ask for travel: read what “remote” means in an FDE posting before you filter. And whether the forward-deployed model outlasts the current wave is a separate question from whether postings grew: Is the forward deployed model here to stay? weighs what people who run FDE teams say.

Questions people ask

How many FDE jobs are there, according to posting analyses?

Can you compare the FDE growth figures in news reports and analyses?

No. Each measures something different. In Bloomberry’s analysis, postings titled forward deployed engineer from January through October 2025 were up 1,165% on the same months of 2024. The Indeed figures reported by Business Insider are index values against a January 2025 baseline, not counts of posts. Does fast growth in FDE postings mean the jobs are easy to get?

No. None of these posting counts measures how many people apply or how many postings are filled, so growth in postings says nothing about your odds. Use the figures to decide where to look, and read each posting closely.
