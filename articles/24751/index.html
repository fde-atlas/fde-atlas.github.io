First, software refers to programs that run inside a computer. Specifically, familiar examples of software include word processing software, spreadsheet software, games, and email.

Software of many kinds is used in a wide variety of situations, from tools that improve business efficiency, to those that enhance services, to communication tools.

The opposite of software is hardware. Hardware exists in a tangible, visible form and can be physically touched. Examples include smartphones, tablets, and PCs.

When software is installed inside such hardware, users gain access to a variety of functions.

In other words, software development can be described as "the work of developing functional products by combining hardware and software."

Software development can be broadly divided into three types. Since the content being developed differs for each, it's important to understand the characteristics of each before proceeding with software development.

Web software development refers to developing software that runs in a web browser. For example, Google's Gmail and Dropbox, one of the cloud services, are widely used.

A major characteristic of web software is that it doesn't need to be installed on hardware and can be used with just an internet connection and a browser. Additionally, managing software on a server makes maintenance easier, which is another advantage.

Application development is the development of software programmed for purposes specific to user needs. Software for listening to music, creating videos, or writing documents—programs that are basically installed on hardware and used—are called applications (apps).

Once installed, applications can be used without an internet connection, which is a significant benefit for users in terms of ease of use. However, to resolve issues and update them into more functional versions, it's necessary to regularly release update programs and have users install them.

Embedded software development involves incorporating specific functions into home appliances, devices, automobiles, machinery, and so on. For example, embedding "heating functions" and "oven/grill functions" into a microwave.

In recent years, with the spread of IoT (Internet of Things), linking various things such as home appliances to the internet has enabled convenient functions. Features like "operating your home air conditioner even when you're not home" are made possible through embedded software development.

The suitable programming languages vary depending on the type of development. The characteristics of the main languages are summarized in Types of Programming Languages Used in Software Development.

Requirements definition is the process of interviewing the client to determine the specific direction of what functions and specifications the software should have. It also involves confirming the outlook for the overall cost, personnel, and development effort required.

Generally, requirements definition takes several days.

This is the process of actually designing the software based on the results of the requirements definition. In the design phase, various verifications are performed, such as data processing methods, the software's appearance, usability for users, and ensuring security.

Like requirements definition, design also takes several days.

This is the process of actually programming based on the detailed design document. Work proceeds with careful verification to ensure that functions operate as specified in the design document.

After the requirements definition and design phases, it generally takes about 10 to 14 days from contract signing to the actual start of development. The development effort itself varies depending on the scale of the project, but taking MVP development as an example, it typically takes an average of 2 to 3 months.

This is the phase where the developed software is actually run to check whether it operates according to the design specifications and whether there are any problems. Testing is divided into four types: unit testing, integration testing, system testing, and acceptance testing.

Verification is carefully performed at the module level and across the entire program, and if a defect is found at any stage, it must be fixed each time. Finally, the software is also run in the user's environment to confirm that there are no problems.

Once all tests are passed, the software is delivered (released) to the client. Before release, a final check is also necessary to confirm that everything is ready to hand over to the client, such as settings and files not being missing and data not being left undeleted.

Even after release, operation and maintenance are performed to keep the software running in its best condition at all times.

Even software that has passed testing may encounter unexpected problems once it is actually in operation. In such cases, fixes are applied each time, and the software is updated accordingly.

Software development involves a variety of roles. Specifically, these include engineers, programmers, and sales staff.

Engineers are the role that decides what functions to incorporate into software or applications and how to do so. They are mainly involved from requirements definition through design. When an engineer also serves as a programmer, they may be involved in the phases after development, such as programming based on the design and testing.

As the name suggests, this is the role that performs programming. Based on the design created by the engineer, development proceeds so that the functions operate according to the specifications. In terms of the phases described earlier, programmers are basically responsible from development through testing, although in some cases programmers may also handle requirements definition.

As you can see, the boundary between engineers and programmers is not very clear, and in practice many people fulfill both roles.

Software development involves not only the team that actually builds the product, such as engineers and programmers, but also sales representatives. For a software development company to win projects, it needs sales representatives who have knowledge of software development and programming.

Sales representatives listen to what challenges the client is facing and also act as an intermediary between engineers and programmers. Since they can be considered the point of contact for the project, working with a trustworthy sales representative allows requests to be accurately conveyed to the development team, enabling the creation of the ideal product.

From here, we will explain two of the main software development methods.

Waterfall development is a method in which the entire development process described earlier (requirements definition through release) is determined in detail from start to finish, and development proceeds along that flow.

In waterfall development, development proceeds according to the pre-determined procedures, and each phase is carefully verified. To move on to the next phase, approval from the entire team, including the client, must be obtained at each phase.

Because waterfall development defines the development process in detail, its greatest advantage is that it makes it easy to check progress and resources and to manage schedules.

However, the flip side is that because the development process is defined in such detail, it can be difficult to accommodate specification changes mid-development. If the client's requirements may change along the way, the inability to respond flexibly is a disadvantage.

This development method is well suited to large-scale projects that require strict schedule management, and to projects whose concept and direction are firmly established and where specification changes are unlikely to occur.

Agile development is a development method that has become mainstream in recent years, in which development cycles are repeated quickly on a feature-by-feature basis.

The process from requirements definition through design, testing, and release is divided into small units by feature, and the PDCA cycle is run at high speed, making it possible to develop high-quality products even in a short period.

In agile development, in contrast to waterfall development, schedules are not set in detail, and since development proceeds feature by feature, it can flexibly accommodate specification changes along the way.

It is ideal when the ideal form has not yet been determined at the early stages of development, or when you want to move closer to the ideal product as development progresses.

However, while it offers flexibility, development status tends to be fluid, making it unsuitable for projects that require strict schedule management.

To fully enjoy the benefits of agile development, it is essential to proceed with development using a team of experienced managers and engineers.

The fundamental and most important principle in software development is "to build products that are truly needed, together with people who have the appropriate skills." Creating products with no demand in society will not lead to sales, and without skilled personnel, a satisfactory product cannot be developed.

Also, when entering a new market, it is important to proceed with development from a comprehensive business perspective, not just within the scope of software development, such as identifying the right timing to enter the market before developing and operating.

In addition, terms that often come up in discussions of software development include "MVP development," "lean development," and "lab development." Let us briefly touch on these as well.

MVP stands for Minimum Viable Product and refers to a product equipped with only the minimum necessary features. With MVP development, you first develop a minimal product and then gradually increase its completeness while referring to feedback from users.

The greatest advantage of MVP development is that it enables development at low cost and in a short period. When launching a new business or new product, using MVP development allows you to proceed with development quickly while assessing market value and user needs.

"Lean" is a word meaning "slim, with no fat" or "waste-free." While lean itself is a term used in business in general, lean development in software development refers to "a development method that maximizes product value while eliminating waste."

In lean development, the basic flow is "hypothesis → build → measure → learn," and if the hypothesis proves incorrect, the build, measure, and learn cycle is repeated.

To eliminate as much waste as possible, the product built in lean development is kept to one with only minimal features. This is what MVP development is. MVP development can be said to be an indispensable part of lean development.

In addition, since lean development works well with agile development, which runs PDCA cycles on a per-feature basis, the two are often combined.

Lab-type development is a form of "offshore development," which secures development resources overseas. In lab-type development, you sign an exclusive contract for a set period with overseas companies or engineering teams, mainly in Southeast Asia such as Vietnam or Myanmar, and outsource software development to them.

Overseas, engineers with technical skills on par with Japan's can be hired at low cost, making it possible to develop high-quality products while keeping budgets down.

However, it takes several months to assemble members, build the development team, and actually begin development. Also, while under contract, labor costs accrue even when there is no work to order, so if the project does not proceed smoothly, the cost-effectiveness may end up suffering as a result.
