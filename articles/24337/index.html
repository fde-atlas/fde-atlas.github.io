Summary

Written by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, this article examines the drawbacks of Scrum development: quality varies with individual member skills, and frequent ceremonies like daily standups demand strong communication abilities. It lists common failure causes—insufficient preparation, lack of buy-in from executives and stakeholders, the mistaken belief that agile needs no planning, and organizational culture mismatch. Three success tips are offered: plan tasks at roughly one-hour granularity, secure understanding from management and stakeholders, and treat every role (product owner, scrum master, developers) as central while keeping the team aligned on goals. Scrum teams typically have 3–9 members working in 2–4 week cycles, and first-time adopters are advised to allow around six months of preparation.

Key points

  • Scrum drawbacks: uneven quality across members and heavy communication demands
  • Failure causes: poor preparation, no executive buy-in, assuming no planning is needed
  • Success tips: fine-grained planning, stakeholder alignment, every member as protagonist
  • Typical teams are 3–9 people working in 2–4 week cycles
  • First-time adopters should allow roughly six months of preparation

Scrum development is one of the "agile development" methods widely used in development settings.

Scrum development has two disadvantages.

"Development quality varies depending on team members" and "It requires strong communication skills"

Scrum development is a development method undertaken as a team. As such, there are many opportunities for reporting and information sharing, such as daily meetings, which demand strong communication skills.

In this article, we will help you understand the disadvantages of scrum development and introduce the secrets to success in overcoming them and achieving success.

"Agile development" is a development method that breaks work into compact units and repeatedly cycles through "design → development → testing" over short periods.

One of its advantages is that, even in small units, development progress is checked while actually running the product, making it easy to obtain user feedback and easy to change direction if the course turns out to be wrong.

One of these agile development methods is "scrum development." Its name comes from the rugby scrum, and it is characterized by small development teams of usually 3 to 9 members working over roughly 2 to 4 weeks.

Although scrum development is widely used in development settings, you need to deepen your understanding of it before adopting it.

Here, we explain the disadvantages of scrum development in detail.

Scrum development cycles are as short as one week. If member turnover or new hires overlap, tasks such as training new members and preparing onboarding systems become necessary.

However, even if there are skill gaps among members, there is often no room for a sufficient training period, and members with insufficient technical skills may see the period end before they can demonstrate their abilities.

Since individual skill levels can affect the rhythm of the development process and the quality of development, member selection is crucial.

To avoid disrupting the development rhythm, the key is whether you can build a team that keeps member composition as unchanged as possible and allows development to proceed smoothly.

Scrum development is characterized by many opportunities for communication within the team, such as daily scrums (daily meetings).

It is also characterized by flexible responses to changes such as specification changes, so there are many changes such as to the overall schedule, and communication is important for sharing progress within the team.

Even if a member has high skills, if their communication skills are low, it will affect the quality of the entire development.

The challenge is securing talent with both high skills and strong communication abilities.

Now that you understand the disadvantages of scrum development, let's also look at the factors that cause scrum development to fail.

In most product development, a preparation period of one to two months is required in advance.

- Clarifying product goals and value - Preparing the product backlog (*1) - Clarifying roles (*2)

- Making the skills of development team members visible

- Aligning the knowledge levels of the scrum team and stakeholders

- Documenting rules for ways of working

(*1) Product backlog: A list of prioritized features and technical improvement requests for the system under development, as well as issues and problems that currently need to be resolved

(*2) In scrum, three roles are defined: "Product Owner," "Development Team," and "Scrum Master"

As shown above, there are many considerations to address before starting development, including things to decide, clarify, and share information about.

One key to avoiding failure is to identify all of these and prepare as much as possible in advance.

In particular, since scrum development is done feature by feature, it can be difficult to grasp the overall picture. Also, specifications may change during development, so it is important to have a system in place that can respond flexibly each time.

After thoroughly understanding the scrum development method, you should ideally set a preparation period of about six months and take solid countermeasures.

Since it is a development method different from traditional waterfall development, it is important to gain the cooperation and understanding of team members, and above all, the understanding of the entire company, especially executives and stakeholders.

If you do not thoroughly explain to stakeholders the purpose and aims of using "scrum development," as well as the expected benefits, and obtain their understanding and approval, problems may arise later.

Do not dismiss it as "just one development method"; instead, make careful and thorough preparations.

Since agile development is a method of repeated trial and error in small units, some people believe that advance planning is unnecessary.

However, this can be a factor leading to failure. Even though the functional units are small, an overall plan with a bird's-eye view is still necessary.

If you are new to Scrum development, a thorough preparation period is extremely important. You need to plan meticulously, from explaining the process to stakeholders to selecting team members.

Don't be swayed by the word "speed"; instead, carefully prepare each and every step.

There are also cases where the organization's culture simply doesn't match Scrum development.

Since it is a development method completely different from traditional product development, it often suits relatively young organizations and startups.

However, if you have inherited a long-standing organization or already possess mature product development know-how, trying to adopt Scrum development too easily may clash with your organization's culture and lead to failure.

It is important to thoroughly examine in advance which development method is best for your company.

Scrum development involves roles such as "Product Owner," "Scrum Master," and "Development Team Members," and the "Scrum Master" serves as the so-called leader.

However, unlike the leader role generally imagined, the Scrum Master primarily supports members as a coordinator with users. They are expected to focus on maximizing the team's capabilities.

With the mindset of a traditional, management-style leader, it may be impossible to fully draw out members' abilities, and the team risks ceasing to function properly.

What key points should you keep in mind to make Scrum development successful?

As mentioned earlier, it is no exaggeration to say that correctly understanding Scrum development and executing it in a planned and reliable manner determines success or failure.

The smaller the granularity of a task, the easier it is to handle. Ideally, you should plan meticulously with tasks of about one hour in volume, clearly defining "what needs to be done for the task to be complete."

In addition to understanding within the development team, understanding from stakeholders and other related parties is also important. When introducing a method different from what has been used before, there will inevitably be some headwinds.

In particular, it is important for management to deepen their understanding and recognize how the method differs from previous development approaches, along with its advantages and disadvantages.

A development method that can respond flexibly and quickly to user needs also has relevance to corporate management.

In Scrum development, every member of the development team is a protagonist.

The "Product Owner," "Scrum Master," and "Development Team Members" each have their own roles, with corresponding responsibilities defined.

It is important to respect one another and approach development with mutual trust and the shared recognition that "each person is a protagonist."

In product development, the entire team must share and constantly confirm the purpose and goals—the "why" and "what for"—so they are never lost sight of.

A team can demonstrate its maximum power when everyone aims in the same direction and shares the same aspirations. Because Scrum development involves repeated trial and error in small development units, the overall picture can become hard to see and the purpose may drift.

The key to success is to proceed while deepening team collaboration based on the rules unique to Scrum development.
