Frequently Asked Questions in FDE Interviews and How to Approach Your Answers | Preparation to Improve Your Pass Rate

Summary

This Japanese-language article from FDE Jobs explains common FDE interview questions and how to structure answers. It argues that FDE interviews weigh technical skill and customer-facing ability equally. Technical questions probe production deployments, LLM/RAG design decisions and evals, and pragmatic integration with legacy systems. Customer questions dig into turning vague requests into requirements, managing expectations during conflicts, and driving adoption with quantified before/after results. The author recommends converting past projects into case studies following a problem-role-action-metric structure, trying the target company's product beforehand, and using reverse questions to clarify an FDE's autonomy, proximity to the product team, and how success is measured. Candidates without direct FDE experience can still pass by framing adjacent-role experience in FDE terms.

Key points

  • FDE interviews weigh technical and customer-facing skills equally.
  • Technical questions focus on production deployments, LLM/RAG design, and evals.
  • Customer questions probe requirement definition, conflict handling, and adoption outcomes.
  • Best preparation: structure projects as problem-role-action-quantified-result case studies.
  • Reverse questions should clarify autonomy, product-team distance, and success metrics.

Key Takeaways of This Article (30-Second Summary)

The FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) interview is characterized by the fact that technical skills and customer-facing skills are evaluated with equal weight. For technical questions, the key to passing is whether you can give concrete examples of "why you chose that design and what the results were"; for customer-related questions, it's whether you can describe, with specific examples, experience "turning vague requests into requirements, moving stakeholders, and taking responsibility through to adoption and results."

  • Technical: Track record of production deployments, design decisions and evaluation (Eval) for LLM/RAG, realistic solutions for integrating with existing systems
  • Customer-facing: Turning vague requests into requirements, managing expectations during conflicts, post-deployment adoption and results (Before/After)

Characteristics of the FDE Interview: Both Technical Skills and Customer-Facing Skills Are Assessed

The FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) interview differs from both purely technical roles and sales roles. In addition to technical skills such as coding and system design, customer-facing skills—structuring customer problems and translating technology for non-engineers—are evaluated with equal weight. The distinctive feature is that you are assessed not only on whether you can "build it," but on whether you can "drive it to adoption in the field."

Technical Questions and How to Approach Your Answers

For FDE roles in the generative AI space, you will be asked whether you can talk about implementation and integration skills with concrete examples.

Common QuestionWhat Your Answer Should Demonstrate
What systems have you deployed to production?A track record of something that was "continuously used," not just a PoC, and your scope of responsibility
How did you implement and evaluate LLM/RAG?The reasoning behind design decisions, operational accuracy management (Eval), and the improvement process
What challenges did you face integrating with existing systems?How you overcame data, authentication, and legacy integration issues with realistic solutions

You will be evaluated positively if you talk about "why you chose that design" and "what the results were," rather than just "what you built."

Customer-Facing and Problem-Solving Questions

This is the core of the FDE interview. You will be probed deeply on "experience identifying problems, moving stakeholders, and delivering results" rather than on technology.

"How did you turn vague requests into requirements?"

Describe, using a specific project, how you identified the underlying business problem behind the customer's words and defined what should be built. You will be evaluated on your attitude of facing the essential problem rather than retreating into technology.

"What did you do when you disagreed with a customer?"

Show the process by which you guided the situation to the best solution while maintaining trust, through expectation management, explaining trade-offs, and proposing alternatives. The key is balance—neither simply deferring nor forcing your way through.

"Do you have experience making something you delivered stick?"

If you can describe, with quantitative data (Before/After), experience where you took responsibility not just for building something but for usage rates and results, it will be a strong differentiator.

Preparation Points That Make a Difference

The most effective preparation is to turn your projects into case studies in the form of "problem → your role → actions taken → quantified results." In addition, if you actually try out the product of the company you're applying to and can articulate in your own words "how you would solve this customer's problem," you will be highly evaluated as an FDE with a customer perspective. In your reverse questions, confirming the FDE's scope of discretion, distance from the product team, and how results are measured will help prevent mismatches after joining. As a foundation for your preparation, also review the skills required for FDEs.
