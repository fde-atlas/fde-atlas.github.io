Why This Hottest AI Role is Failing to Take Off in India

Analytics India Magazine · 2026-05-20 · 4 min read

Summary

The article examines why Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) are in surging demand but short supply in India. TeamLease Digital reports demand rose nearly 800% in a year as enterprise AI moves from pilots to production. The role requires a rare mix of software engineering, AI/ML, cloud expertise, and client-facing skills. Full text

As artificial intelligence moves deeper into enterprise operations, a new job title is gaining currency in corporate boardrooms with the urgency that once surrounded the role of data scientist. The Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is emerging as one of the technology sector’s most coveted roles, as companies race to find talent that can bridge AI systems with real-world business operations.

Unlike a conventional software engineer sequestered behind a terminal, the FDE operates on the front lines, embedded directly within a client’s organisation. They write production code, untangle legacy integrations, and translate the abstract promise of artificial intelligence into working systems that survive contact with the real world.

Part engineer, part consultant, part translator—the FDE is, in the words of venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz, “the hottest job”.

According to data from staffing firm TeamLease Digital, demand for FDEs has risen nearly 800% over the past year alone.

“The demand for FDEs has been increasing day by day. This is due to the fact that AI implementation and adoption have shifted from research or use cases to production. Deploying frontier models at enterprise scale requires engineers embedded with customers who understand both systems and business problems,” says Neeti Sharma, CEO, TeamLease Digital.

She adds that frontier AI companies, enterprise software firms, Google Cloud, and consulting organisations are all hiring aggressively, shifting from selling products to co-building AI solutions with customers.

The Supply Problem

Organisations of every stripe have moved beyond experimenting with AI and are now deploying it. Frontier models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google now underpin customer service systems, credit risk engines, and supply chain logistics for some of the country’s largest enterprises. But a gap has emerged between the promise of these models and their operational reality. The FDE is the human layer that closes it.

“Forward-deployed engineers, or roles that do the equivalent motion, are about to become one of the most in-demand jobs in tech. And one of the most important functions for AI rollouts. Deploying agents is far more technical of a task than most people realize, often far more involved than deploying software,” Aaron Levie, CEO of enterprise cloud company Box, wrote in a LinkedIn post last week.

But the trouble is that there are not enough FDEs to go around. The role demands a rare and largely self-assembled combination of capabilities: deep software engineering, fluency in AI and machine learning, expertise across cloud platforms, and the interpersonal skill to navigate a sceptical enterprise buyer.

“Supply of skilled FDEs is way lower than its demand. There is a small pool of engineers that come with a combination of software engineering, AI, cloud platforms, and customer-facing skills,” says Sharma.

According to Paraform, an AI-powered recruiting platform and marketplace, FDEs earn a premium over standard software engineers, with median total compensation at $173,816 and top-tier roles at companies like Palantir reaching $238,000 to $486,000.

“The FDE role exists because great software alone isn’t enough. Someone has to close the gap between what a product can do and what a customer needs it to do, in their environment, on their timeline,” Paraform noted in a blog post.

In India, entry-level FDE positions are drawing packages in the range of ₹9-12 lakh per annum. Mid-level practitioners, those with demonstrated experience in production AI deployments and a track record with enterprise clients, are commanding ₹25-40 lakh.

Google Cloud’s CEO Thomas Kurian has also noted that the company is rapidly accelerating its FDE hiring to meet customer demand. “While having FDEs is not new for Google Cloud, the demand from customers and partners for Google enterprise AI products and Google engineers to help them embrace agent development is growing very rapidly,” he wrote in a LinkedIn post.

For India’s technology sector, the implications are significant. The country is becoming an increasingly important theatre for enterprise AI deployment, driven by the rapid digitisation of financial services, healthcare, logistics, and government operations.

“AI-native companies, enterprise platform players, as well as global capability centres (GCCs) are increasingly re-evaluating existing engineering talent to align with the growing FDE wave tied to large-scale enterprise AI adoption. In India especially, the pool of professionals with this integrated capability stack remains very limited relative to the demand pipeline,” says Saravanan Balasundaram, Founder and CEO of business consulting and talent solutions firm Han Digital Solution.

These salary bands are also expected to continue rising as long as the supply constraint persists, and there is little in the current talent pipeline to suggest that relief is imminent. Organisations that move quickly to identify, develop, and retain FDE talent will hold a significant advantage as enterprise AI competition intensifies.

Qualified FDEs are expected to command a 50-75% compensation premium over conventional AI engineering roles, Balasundaram notes.

“People have realised that there is a lot of customisation required for every customer, every business process; it is not the same as building one and reusing it for everyone. The front-end customisation for end-user requirements is very important,” says Srinivas Padmanabhuni, an AI expert and technologist who previously worked as Principal Research Scientist and Associate Vice President at Infosys Labs.
