AI's Forward-Deployed Moment Is Missing the Point

Tonic3Com · 2026-09-11 · 5 min read

Summary

The article argues that the wave of forward-deployed engineering investments—Microsoft's $2.5B Frontier Company with 6,000 experts, AWS's $1B program, and a 1,000-person Google Cloud–Accenture Gemini Enterprise group—reflects an admission that AI value requires implementation, not just model access. But the author contends proximity alone is insufficient: engineers must deeply understand workflows, human decisions, and business processes. Citing an S&P 500 adoption study (only 11% deeply integrated AI in 2025) and a Microsoft study on AI coding agents (24% more merged PRs, driven by social dynamics), the piece concludes that the winning model is forward-deployed understanding, not just forward-deployed engineers.

Key points

  • Microsoft, AWS, and Google Cloud–Accenture are investing billions in forward-deployed engineering teams.
  • Only 11% of S&P 500 firms had AI deeply integrated into business processes in 2025.
  • A Microsoft study found AI coding agents drove 24% more merged pull requests, but adoption depended on social dynamics.
  • Proximity to customers doesn't equal understanding of how work actually gets done.
  • The author's core claim: success requires forward-deployed understanding, not merely forward-deployed engineers.
