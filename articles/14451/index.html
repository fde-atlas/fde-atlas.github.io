What are your compensation expectations?

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 8 min read

Summary

A guide to answering the compensation-expectations question in FDE interviews. The core advice: get the recruiter to state the band and level first, and never name a single number early, since it becomes your ceiling. The article cites traceable sources: OpenAI's San Francisco posting lists $185K–$300K base plus equity, and H-1B filings (certified October 2024–June 2026) show OpenAI's forward-deployed base wages with a median of $225,500 and a middle half of $220,000–$243,250. It contrasts Palantir ($135K–$200K) and Databricks ($152,900–$210,155) postings, stresses distinguishing base, OTE and total compensation, and warns against inventing competing offers or inflating current pay. If the band is fixed, level becomes the lever to negotiate.

Key points

  • Ask for the band and level before naming any number.
  • A single early number becomes your ceiling for the whole process.
  • OpenAI's posted range is $185K–$300K base plus equity.
  • H-1B filings show OpenAI FDE median base of $225,500.
  • Never invent offers or inflate current pay; if fixed, negotiate level.

How to answer

Treat this as the recruiter checking that you and the band overlap before they spend interviewers on you. Your job is to confirm the overlap without setting your own ceiling. The question can come before any call: the application form for Palantir’s , US Government posting in Washington, D.C., as of September 2026, asks whether you are comfortable with the compensation range advertised on the posting. Do the research before the call. Write down the posted range with the date you read it and what it covers. Equity and bonus sit on top, and you raise them separately.

Then say these, in order.

  • Ask for the band and the level first: “Can you share the range for this role, and which level you are hiring at?”
  • If the band works, say so and stop. Don’t place yourself inside it until there is a level and an offer; any point you name becomes the ceiling. Move the conversation to how level is decided.
  • If they insist on your number, give a base range whose bottom you would accept, tied to a level, and say that equity and level matter as much as base.
  • Close the loop: “If that’s outside your band, tell me now and we can decide together whether it makes sense to continue.”

What to keep out. Don’t volunteer current pay; redirect to the role’s range. Don’t claim an offer you don’t have. One candidate in Europe reported, in June 2026, that the recruiter for Anthropic’s Applied AI Architect role described two base salaries with no option to negotiate base. Source 8Applied AI Architect AnthropicPublisherBlind (teamblind.com)Source typecandidate report on Blind If the recruiter says the band is fixed, believe them for now and ask how level is decided, because level is the lever that is left.

The trap is the confident single number said too early. It becomes the ceiling, and it answers a question about a level no one has set yet.

Reading posted bands, filings and self-reports, and what is negotiable in an offer, are taught in Offers, negotiation and the first months, in Pro. Level is where this conversation leads, so practice what level you think you are, and why, then what to ask when the recruiter says the equity number is final.

Follow-ups

What the interviewer may ask next, once your first answer is on the table.

  • What are you making now?
  • If we came in below that, would you still move forward?
  • Are you interviewing anywhere else, and where are you in those processes?

Where answers go wrong

  • Naming one number before knowing the level or the band, then spending the rest of the process negotiating up from your own floor.
  • Inventing a competing offer or inflating current pay. If either is checked, nothing else you said is believed.

Answer this in two minutes

Write the answer you would say out loud. The clock starts with your first word.

Illustrative answer about a fictional project

Written in the first person to show the structure. Tell yours from your own work.

In this example, the recruiter raises it about 10 minutes into the screen. I have the posting open, with the date I read it written next to the range.

“Thanks for raising it early. I’d rather know now than after four rounds. Before I give you a number, can you tell me the range budgeted for this role, and which level you’re hiring into? Your posting, when I read it last Tuesday, listed a base of $190K to $250K plus equity, and I want to check that’s the band for the level you have in mind.”

She confirms the band and says the role is at senior.

“Good, that band works for me. Where I land in it should follow from the level your loop supports, so I’d rather settle the number at offer stage with the whole package in front of us. Level matters more to me than where in the band I start. How will you decide it?”

She asks what I make now.

“I’d rather keep this anchored on this role’s range than on my current package, because the scope is different: I’m not customer-facing full time today. What I can tell you is that the range you’ve shared works for me.”

She asks about other processes.

“I’m in two other processes. One is at the onsite stage and one just finished the technical screen. I don’t have an offer from either yet. If that changes, I’ll tell you the same day so you’re not surprised.”

She asks whether I’d still move forward if the offer came in below that band.

“It depends why it’s lower. If it’s a lower level, I’d like to know what the loop would need to show for senior, because I’d rather earn the level than negotiate the number. If it’s a lower band at the same level, I’d want to see the equity and the level together before deciding, and if it doesn’t work, I’ll tell you straight away rather than let it drift.”

If the recruiter had refused to share a band, I would have said this instead:

“Understood. Here’s what I’m working from. Based on posted ranges for comparable senior roles, I’m targeting a base between $220K and $250K, at a senior level. Equity and level matter to me as much as base, so I’d want to see all three together. If your band for this level sits below that, please tell me now, and we can decide together whether it makes sense to keep going.”

Three things are doing the work here. I asked for their band before giving mine, and once I had it, I named no number inside it: the only point I pushed on was how level gets decided, and when she tested a lower offer, I turned it back into a question about level. The one range I gave, in the fallback, came with what it covers and a level. And nothing I said about my situation was untrue, so nothing I said can be checked and found wrong later.
