Forward Deployed Engineer vs Solutions Engineer vs Applied AI Engineer

FDE Notes · 2026-09-18 · 2 min read

Summary

This article untangles three frequently interchangeable job titles. A forward deployed engineer works inside the customer's environment, shipping and hardening integrations against real data and constraints; a solutions engineer is a pre-sales-leaning role judged by whether deals close; an applied AI/ML engineer owns the model and pipeline—training, retrieval, evaluation, inference. Using 241 US postings with disclosed annual base pay (snapshot 2026-11-16), it reports medians of range midpoints: applied AI/ML at $259,750, FDE at $242,500, and solutions engineer at $210,000. Figures exclude equity, bonus, and cost-of-living adjustments, and the full dataset of 937 live roles is available for download.

Key points

  • FDEs are judged on what ships in the customer's environment.
  • Solutions engineers are judged on whether the deal closes.
  • Applied AI engineers are judged on whether the model is right.
  • Applied AI/ML roles show the highest median pay at $259,750.

Full text

These three titles overlap so much that job boards, recruiters, and even the companies posting the roles use them interchangeably. Here is the honest difference — what each role actually does, and what the postings we track pay for each.

What each role actually does

Forward deployed engineer (FDE)

Sits close to the customer and turns the product into something that actually works in the customer’s real environment — their data, their constraints, their production systems. Ships a working integration, then hardens it for production. The pattern is most associated with Palantir and has since spread across AI teams of every size.

Solutions engineer

A technical, pre-sales-leaning role. Works with prospective customers to validate fit, build proofs-of-concept, and clear technical objections so a deal can close. Closer to the sales motion than to owning a production system in the customer’s environment.

Applied AI / ML engineer

Owns the model and the pipeline: training, retrieval, evaluation, inference. Focused on making the AI system itself correct and fast, less on the customer relationship and less on closing deals.

The short version: an FDE is judged on what ships in the customer’s environment; a solutions engineer is judged on whether the deal closes; an applied AI/ML engineer is judged on whether the model is right. The titles blur at the edges, but that’s where the pay gap shows up.
