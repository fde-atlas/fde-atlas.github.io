FDE vs Solutions Engineer: What's the Real Difference Between the Two Most Confused Roles

Original title: FDE vs Solutions Engineer：两个最容易混淆的岗位到底差在哪

FDE中国 · 2026-08-14 · 4 min read

Summary

This article distinguishes FDE from Solutions Engineer (SE), two roles frequently conflated in job postings. The core difference: SEs create value before contract signing by designing and pitching solutions, while FDEs deliver and operate systems after signing. The author argues job titles are unreliable—evaluation metrics and deliverables reveal a role's true nature—and offers three diagnostic questions: does the role require production code, on-site residency, and what metrics define success. It also notes a mismatch in the Chinese market where some SE-titled roles actually do delivery work, and advises candidates to probe these three points in interviews and avoid teams that cannot articulate their own role definition.

Key points

  • SEs are measured on deal contribution; FDEs on customer business metrics and adoption.
  • Judge a role by deliverables and metrics, not by its title.
  • Three questions: production code, on-site residency, evaluation criteria.
  • In China, many titles mismatch actual duties, causing post-hire disappointment.
  • Avoid teams that answer these questions vaguely in interviews.

Introduction

FDE and Solutions Engineer (often abbreviated as SE) are probably the two roles most easily conflated in job postings—the descriptions of the two positions frequently "borrow" from each other. The biggest difference in one sentence: SE focuses on clearly articulating the solution and closing the deal before signing, while FDE focuses on building the system and getting it running after signing. For an overview of how FDE differs from other roles, see Section 4 of the Beginner's Guide.

Three Key Differences Explained

Difference 1: Deliverables define the daily work

An FDE's daily work revolves around code and systems: debugging, integration, evals, launch. An SE's daily work revolves around solutions and persuasion: writing proposals, building demos, accompanying sales to meet clients. To judge which type a role actually is, check whether "writing production code" is an explicit requirement in the job posting—multiple companies' FDE job descriptions list it as a core evaluation item, while SE roles typically only require a "technical background."

Difference 2: Performance metrics reveal the true positioning

Ask a role "what counts as doing well this year"—the answer is the most honest. An FDE is accountable for client business metrics and system adoption—if nobody uses the system after delivery, it's a failure; an SE is accountable for solution quality and contribution to wins—whether the deal closes is the core variable. The pace at which value is realized also differs: an SE's value is realized before signing, while an FDE's value keeps accruing throughout the delivery period.

Difference 3: Different distances from the client

An FDE is stationed on-site, facing the details of the client's business processes, the realities of internal collaboration, and all kinds of imperfect data; an SE maintains a professional distance from the client, interacting with their architects and procurement decision chain. Both distances have value, but they imply completely different work intensity and interpersonal skill profiles. To judge a role's true on-site intensity, phrases like "permanently stationed at the client site" or "frequent travel" in the job description are more reliable than the title.

How to Choose: Ask Yourself Three Questions

If the comparison table isn't enough to make a decision, use three questions to probe yourself thoroughly:

  • Do you enjoy building the system more, or articulating the solution? The former points to FDE, the latter to SE;
  • Can you accept being stationed long-term at a client's site? SE's rhythm is mainly travel and project-based, with much lower on-site intensity;
  • What is your coding ability worth in the market? FDE's engineering bar is hard, while SE values solution and communication skills more.

If two of the three answers are "the former," FDE suits you better; if two are "the latter," SE is the smoother path. The worst mistake is wanting both—the conflicting rhythms of closing deals and delivering will drag each other down when borne by the same person long-term.

Summary and Further Reading

The two roles have no hierarchy—only differences in work objects and realization timelines. If you still can't tell which type your role is after reading the comparison table, ask the recruiter directly for a list of recent delivery cases—the answer is usually in there.
