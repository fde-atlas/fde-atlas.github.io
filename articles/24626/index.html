Why We Created the FDE Certification: The Origins of the System Design

Original title: なぜFDE検定を作ったのか｜制度設計の原点

日本Fde協会 · 2026-07-25 · 6 min read

Summary

This article by the Japan FDE Association explains, from the designers' perspective, why it created the FDE certification exam. The core problem: Forward Deployed Engineers work inside client sites end to end, but no common yardstick exists for judging how much a person can be trusted to handle. The certification is therefore designed mainly as a tool for those delegating work, not just credential holders. It uses five grades, from assistant to architect, with practical experience required at the top levels. Reliability measures include public scoring rubrics, at least two independent graders, and a hard cutoff on security, privacy, and ethics. Pass/fail is separated from training courses, and the association stresses it is a private certification with future national certification as a goal.

Key points

  • The exam exists because companies lack a standard for judging how much to delegate to an FDE.
  • Five grades visualize the scope of work a holder can be trusted with.
  • Practical tests use public rubrics and at least two independent graders.
  • Security, privacy, and ethics act as a cutoff: failing them means failing overall.
  • It is a private certification, separate from paid courses, with national certification as a future goal.

"We can't judge how much we can entrust to a person."—As AI adoption spreads, we at the Japan FDE Association keep hearing this from corporate decision-makers. In this article, we explain the philosophical origins of the FDE Certification—why we created it and how we designed it—from the perspective of the person who designed the system itself. By the time you finish reading, you will understand why mechanisms such as the five-level grading system, the public rubric, and the minimum-threshold requirements exist, and you will be able to judge the reliability of this certification with your own eyes. We also draw on the challenges that Representative Director Maekawa and Director Mitsunaga have witnessed in the field.

The Starting Point of the FDE Certification Philosophy: The Absence of Criteria for Delegation

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a professional who works on-site with customers, handling everything end to end—from organizing business processes to implementation and operational adoption. Yet there was no common yardstick for measuring from the outside "how far this person can go." That was the initial motivation for creating the certification.

Concrete Challenges Observed in the Field

The CEO of a SaaS company told us that even when they wanted to commission AI improvements, candidates' skills varied widely, and interviews alone couldn't reveal their true abilities. When Representative Director Maekawa built an organization of 60 people from scratch at a live-streaming agency, he likewise had to determine from zero, every time, "how much of the work can be entrusted to this person" for each hire and role assignment. Relying on individual, subjective judgment is unhealthy for both those delegating work and those doing it.

A System That Lets Users Judge by Grade Level

So we designed the certification not only "for the person who earns it" but also as a yardstick "for those deciding whom to trust with the work." Looking at the grade level tells you roughly how far you can safely delegate. That is the core of the FDE Certification philosophy.

Visualizing "What Can Be Delegated" Through Five Grade Levels

The FDE Certification consists of five grade levels. The design keeps the entry point broad and verifies practical experience at the higher levels. Below is an outline of the abilities certified at each level.

GradeTitleOutline of Certified Abilities
Grade 5FDE AssistantUnderstanding of basic knowledge and terminology
Grade 4FDE OperatorTool operation under instruction
Grade 3FDE PractitionerFrom problem analysis to improvement design
Grade 2Senior FDEEnd-to-end execution including implementation and client negotiation
Grade 1FDE ArchitectCross-organizational design and leadership

Broad Entry, Experience Verified at Higher Levels

We are currently considering the eligibility requirements for Grades 5 through 3, the handling of level skipping, and the years of practical experience required for Grades 2 and 1. This is because we distinguish between stages that can be discussed on knowledge alone and stages that cannot be fulfilled without field experience. Representative Director Maekawa's experience as a systems PM—personally handling everything from executive reporting to implementation and client negotiations—is reflected in the requirements for the higher grades.

Three Mechanisms That Support Reliability

As a private certification, we must demonstrate ourselves that "the grade levels can truly be trusted." At the system design stage, we decided to establish the following mechanisms.

Public Rubric

For the practical assessment, we plan to publish the scoring criteria (rubric). This allows candidates to know in advance "what they must be able to do, and to what extent, to be evaluated well." Keeping scoring out of a black box directly supports trust in the system.

Independent Scoring by Two or More Examiners and Minimum Thresholds

The practical assessment is scored independently by two or more examiners—a system that does not depend on any single person's subjectivity. In addition, we have set minimum thresholds for information security, personal data protection, and ethics. No matter how high the scores in other areas, candidates who fail to meet the standard here will not pass. FDEs work on-site with customers and handle confidential data. That is precisely why we decided this area allows no compromise.

Separating Certification from Courses

We keep pass/fail judgment separate from learning courses. The system is not structured so that taking a particular course leads to passing. The certification is purely a means of verifying ability, and candidates are free to choose how they learn. This separation preserves the neutrality of the certification.

Position as a Private Certification and the Road Ahead

This is a point we want to make clear to avoid misunderstanding. The FDE Certification is a private certification operated independently by the Japan FDE Association. It is not a national qualification, nor does it grant exclusive rights to a title or to professional practice. Whether or not one holds the certification does not legally restrict working as an FDE.

Grade Levels Are Self-Regulation, Not Legal Restrictions

Requiring practical experience for the higher grades is the Association's own self-regulation. It does not promise or imply any particular compensation. From our startup experience, what matters in the field is not job titles but "what you have actually accomplished." The certification is an auxiliary yardstick for measuring that ability, and we operate it on the premise that it is not a universal certificate.

Looking Toward Future National Certification

Director Mitsunaga runs multiple businesses in Kumamoto and continues activities that connect local people and places. From that perspective, a common yardstick raises trust across the entire industry. We have set future national certification as a goal, but at present we remain a private certification. That is precisely why we believe we must thoroughly ensure transparency in the design now.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Is the FDE Certification a national qualification?

A. No. The FDE Certification is a private certification operated independently by the Japan FDE Association. It does not grant exclusive rights to a title or to professional practice, and holding it does not legally restrict working as an FDE. Future national certification is a stated goal, but at present it is a private certification.

Q. Can I pass by taking a course?

A. Pass/fail judgment and learning courses are separate, and the system is not structured so that taking a particular course leads to passing. Candidates are free to choose how they learn. The certification is purely a means of verifying ability.

Q. Why are there minimum thresholds for security and ethics?

A. Because FDEs work on-site with customers and handle confidential data, we consider information security, personal data protection, and ethics to be areas that allow no compromise. Even with high scores in other areas, candidates who fail to meet this standard will not pass.
