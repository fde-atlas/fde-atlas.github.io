Insight 08

Why It's in Demand Now, How to Assess It, and Key Hiring Points

The FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a new role whose demand is rapidly growing in the AI era. For hiring managers, this article systematically covers everything from the definition of the role and how it differs from similar positions, to evaluation criteria for assessment, candidate sourcing, and compensation design.

Recently, you may have seen the role "FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)" appearing more and more in engineering hiring circles and on social media. Many hiring managers probably have a vague sense of having heard the term but can't quite picture what it involves.

From "writing code" to "contributing to the business"—demand for engineers who can take on "roles beyond building," such as management and leadership, is rising rapidly with the arrival of the AI era. This article explains what role an FDE plays and what to watch out for when hiring one.

Translated literally, Forward Deployed Engineer means "frontline-deployed engineer." The term originally referred to military units deployed on the front lines, and US-based Palantir adapted it into a business model. As the name suggests, an FDE is an engineer who embeds in the "front line" of the customer's site and, leveraging the company's own product, handles everything end to end, from problem discovery through implementation to operations.

In practice, an FDE can be described as a business transformation expert who combines four roles: software engineer, consultant, data scientist, and project manager. If you'd like a more detailed definition of the role itself, also see What Is an FDE? A New Engineer Profile That Completes AI Implementation in the Field.

The biggest driver is that the social implementation of generative AI has entered full swing. While LLMs and agent platforms are extremely versatile, getting them to work with a customer's proprietary business data, policies, and workflows requires a large amount of implementation and alignment work. Demand for FDEs is surging as the ones who fill this "last mile of AI implementation."

Especially at BtoB SaaS companies and AI startups, the FDE is positioned as a hybrid role that "spans sales, customer success, and engineering, and completes implementation on-site at enterprise customers." Rather than a one-off product sale model, FDEs embed deeply with customers and take responsibility through to value realization, making this a position that directly impacts a company's revenue, churn rate, and LTV.

When designing hiring requirements, it is important to understand how the role differs from existing positions.

Product development engineers need broad, bird's-eye domain knowledge of the industries they serve. FDEs, by contrast, are expected—on top of industry knowledge—to quickly pick up more specific, deeper domain knowledge, such as "the unique workflows of the particular company in front of them" and "the tacit knowledge of the people involved," and translate it into system implementation.

IT consultants' main role is to organize a customer's business challenges and propose solutions and IT strategy. An FDE, in contrast, understands that strategy and then goes as far as actually rolling up their sleeves to write code and adapt the system to the field. The differences between the two are compared in more detail in What's the Difference Between an FDE and an IT Consultant?

While on-site resident engineers are in the position of "implementing the features they are told to," FDEs are in the position of "taking ownership proactively from problem definition through decision-making." Their compensation structure, career path, and final deliverables all differ. Even though both are roles that "go into the field," their essence is different, and this must be clearly communicated in job postings and interviews.

For "full-stack software engineers who can write Python," the technical door is open. Skills in using LLM APIs, building AI agents, implementing RAG, and building data infrastructure are especially valued. What matters is "whether they can actually write production code with their own hands."

The second evaluation criterion for FDE hiring is "the ability to understand a customer's business and translate latent problems into system requirements." With technical skills alone, candidates risk being assessed as "mere implementation engineers," so the key to assessment is whether they can demonstrate consulting fundamentals through concrete episodes. Specifically, check the following elements.

  • Interviewing and problem-extraction skills: drawing out essential problems from a customer's vague requests
  • Requirements definition skills: translating extracted problems into implementable system requirements
  • Stakeholder coordination skills: aligning the interests of the customer's executives, field staff, and IT department
  • Prioritization skills: determining feature priorities that maximize impact with limited resources

If you'd like a deeper understanding of the fundamentals an FDE needs, The 3 Skills an FDE Needs is also a useful reference.

Because FDEs require the rare combination of "technical skill × customer-facing ability," rather than targeting a single job type, it is effective to search for candidates through multiple routes.

BackgroundStrengthsAreas to Fill
Backend/frontend engineerImplementation skillsBusiness and customer-facing skills
Strategy consultant/IT consultantProposal and presentation skillsTechnical implementation skills
Product managerCustomer understanding and requirements definition skillsTechnical implementation skills

With two or more years of software development experience, a transition from engineering is entirely feasible, and in 2026 career changes from consulting backgrounds are also increasing. PMs who can do both product thinking and engineering are especially prized.

To prevent hiring mismatches, assessing a candidate's orientation is also essential.

People who are a good fit include those who, even as IT consultants, have development experience and no aversion to hands-on work, or engineers with strong technical ability who don't shy away from dealing with business complexity.

On the other hand, consultants who want to focus on strategy or organizational change, and engineers who want to concentrate purely on internal product development, are unlikely to be a good fit for this role. Candidates motivated by statements like "technical skill alone is no longer satisfying" or "I want to go beyond proposals and get all the way to implementation" can be expected to have strong aptitude.

Because FDEs require the rare combination of technical skill and customer-facing ability, their salary levels tend to be higher than those of typical engineers or consultants.

Globally, according to Levels.fyi data, Palantir's FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer) has a total compensation range (base salary + equity) of $171,000 to over $415,000. In Japan as well, high-level offers are common, especially at foreign AI companies, so competitive compensation design is essential to secure hiring competitiveness. Specific salary ranges in Japan and abroad are summarized in A Thorough Guide to FDE Jobs (2026 Edition).

The keys to successfully hiring FDEs are the following.

  • Define the role correctly for your organization and candidates — Clearly articulate how it differs from "on-site client work" or "just an implementation engineer"
  • Assess on two axes — Verify both technical skills (implementation ability) and business skills (ability to translate business problems) through concrete examples
  • Broaden your sourcing channels — Hire from multiple routes including engineers, consultants, and PMs, keeping their complementary strengths in mind
  • Prioritize motivation fit — Target "consultants who want to build things" and "engineers who want to work in the field"
  • Offer competitive compensation — Since these are rare talents, design compensation levels based on the market

FDEs, who handle the last mile of AI implementation, will face increasingly fierce hiring competition going forward. Establishing role definitions and assessment criteria early is the first step to attracting top talent.
