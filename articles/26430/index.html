Today's Assessment

Two recent public signals come from ElevenLabs and Google: the former explicitly has its Forward Deployed Engineering team supporting enterprises in using synthetic voice detection, while the latter released AQuA for analyzing production agent trajectories. Together, they show that an FDE's work goes beyond wiring agents into enterprises—it also means turning enterprise boundaries and post-launch quality feedback into operational mechanisms.

Key Signals

1. ElevenLabs: Synthetic voice detection supported for enterprise customers by the FDE team

ElevenLabs released a synthetic voice detection capability that can determine within seconds of a call starting whether a voice is AI-generated, and allows enterprises to set rules for queuing, identity verification, blocking of sensitive operations, and human escalation. The company explicitly stated that the current capability is supported for enterprise customers by the Forward Deployed Engineering team, with broader access to follow later.

This forms a closed loop of "new capability—enterprise boundaries—on-site delivery." The FDE must translate detection results into routing, authorization, human takeover, and audit rules, rather than just delivering a model API.

Evidence boundary: The official disclosure covers product capability, enterprise support model, and protocol participation; no customer counts, detection accuracy, false positive rates, conversion, or cost results were provided, so this cannot be written up as production ROI.

Source: ElevenLabs: Defining how AI agents interact with your business

2. Google AQuA: Turning production agent quality into an outer loop of "sampling—verification—root cause—regression"

Google released and open-sourced AQuA (Ambient Quality Agent), which reads production trajectories alongside an enterprise's own Google Cloud project, samples up to 1,000 sessions, runs multiple checks, failure clustering, and full trajectory verification, then tracks NEW, RECURRING, and RESOLVED issues. In the official example, 32 multi-agent sessions produced 42 findings, which after verification were consolidated into 6 issue clusters; after fixes, full pass rates improved from 5/32 to 13/32.

This shows that launch is not the end of acceptance. The FDE needs to deliver an outer loop of "production observation—evidence sampling—issue attribution—replay regression," turning every failure into a reproducible test, rather than just watching for HTTP 200 or single-run success rates.

Evidence boundary: AQuA is Google's open-source reference implementation and example; the 32 sessions and fix results are not customer deployment data and cannot be extrapolated as universal enterprise outcomes.

Source: Google Developers Blog: AQuA

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Establish a "post-launch quality ledger" for enterprise agents: session samples, failure types, human takeovers, versions, fixes, and regression results.
  • Move governance of sensitive actions upstream to identity recognition, authorization boundaries, rule-based routing, and human escalation; don't treat detection results directly as business decisions.
  • Make trajectory sampling → failure classification → evidence verification → root cause identification → replay regression → continuous tracking the outer loop of production quality.
  • Distinguish vendor examples, product capabilities, and customer production results; don't substitute demo data for enterprise acceptance evidence.
