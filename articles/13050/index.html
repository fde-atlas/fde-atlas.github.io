Summary

This FDE Learning Center article traces AI history from Turing's 1950 paper to Claude Opus 4.7, arguing that understanding this history helps FDEs anticipate inference-optimization trends and judge today's hype rationally. It covers symbolic AI, two AI winters, expert systems like MYCIN and XCON, the deep learning revolution sparked by AlexNet, the Transformer's replacement of RNNs, Scaling Law, RLHF alignment behind ChatGPT, the open-source wave (Llama, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek), and the shift to reasoning models and agents. Each era is linked to evolving FDE skills, from model serving to agentic deployment.

Key points

  • Symbolic AI collapsed due to limited compute and combinatorial explosion.
  • AlexNet (2012) made GPUs the standard hardware for AI training.
  • Transformer (2017) replaced RNNs through fully parallel training.
  • Scaling Law and RLHF enabled GPT-3 and ChatGPT's success.
  • Reasoning models and agents create new optimization work for FDEs.

Why FDEs Need to Know AI History​

Interviews won't test history directly, but:

  • Understanding why Transformers replaced RNNs is essential to understanding the direction of inference optimization
  • Knowing how the Scaling Law evolved lets you anticipate trends in next-generation models
  • Knowing the causes of each "AI winter" helps you view the current bubble rationally

First Wave: Symbolic AI (1950s-1970s)​

Origins​

In 1950, Turing published the paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," proposing the Turing Test: if a machine can converse with a human such that the human cannot distinguish it from another human, then the machine possesses "intelligence."

In 1956, the Dartmouth Conference first coined the term Artificial Intelligence, marking the birth of AI as an independent discipline.

Core Ideas​

智能 = 符号操作
只要把知识用符号表示出来，用逻辑规则推理，就能实现智能

Representative Achievements​

YearAchievementSignificance
1956Logic Theorem ProverThe first AI program, proving 38 theorems from Russell's *Principia Mathematica*
1966ELIZAThe first chatbot, simulating a psychotherapist
1970SHRDLUUnderstanding natural language instructions in a constrained blocks world

First AI Winter (1974-1980)​

Causes:

  • Insufficient computing power: computers at the time had only a few KB of memory, unable to support practically useful systems
  • Combinatorial explosion: the complexity of symbolic reasoning grows exponentially with problem size
  • Lighthill Report (1973): a UK government report concluded that AI research had "largely failed"
  • Funding cliff: DARPA and the UK government drastically cut AI research funding

Second Wave: Expert Systems and the Seeds of Connectionism (1980s-1990s)​

Expert Systems​

专家系统 = 知识库（人工编写规则） + 推理引擎

Representative systems:

SystemDomainNumber of RulesResults
MYCINBlood infection diagnosis~600 rulesPerformed better than some physicians
XCONDEC computer configuration~10,000 rulesSaved DEC $40M per year

The Revival of Connectionism​

In 1986, Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams published the Backpropagation paper, proposing training multi-layer neural networks with backpropagation.

Second AI Winter (1987-1993)​

Causes:

  • Expert systems were extremely costly to maintain: adding a single rule could break existing rules
  • The rise of desktop computers: as general-purpose computers improved, specialized AI hardware lost its advantage
  • Neural networks questioned again: they could only handle very small-scale problems

Why AlexNet Matters​

AlexNet was the first deep learning model truly trained on GPUs:

CPU 训练：几周
GPU 训练：几天

GPU 的并行计算能力恰好匹配神经网络的海量矩阵运算
→ 从此 GPU 成为 AI 训练和推理的标准硬件

Relevance to FDEs: starting with AlexNet, the performance optimization of AI systems became deeply tied to GPU hardware.

The Dominance and Limitations of RNNs​

Before the Transformer appeared, RNNs (and their variants LSTM and GRU) were the mainstream architecture for NLP:

The core problem with RNNs: they cannot be trained in parallel. Sequential data must be processed step by step from left to right, which is a huge waste on GPUs.

The Transformer Era (2017-2020)​

The Turning Point: Attention Is All You Need (2017)​

Google proposed the Transformer architecture, completely abandoning RNNs in favor of Self-Attention only.

DimensionRNN/LSTMTransformer
Training parallelismLow (sequential dependency)High (global parallelism)
Long-range dependenciesPoor (even with LSTM)Good (direct Attention)
ComplexityO(n) stepsO(1) steps (but Attention is O(n^2))
PerformanceBLEU ~25BLEU ~28 (translation tasks)

BERT (2018)​

Google used the Transformer Encoder for pre-training, achieving SOTA on 11 NLP tasks.

预训练：在海量无标签文本上训练
    ↓
微调：在特定任务上用少量标注数据调整
    ↓
成为 NLP 的标准做法

GPT-3's In-context Learning was a paradigm shift: no fine-tuning needed—just give a few examples in the prompt, and the model can complete the task.

The Large Language Model Era (2020-2023)​

The Scaling Law (2020)​

OpenAI published a paper discovering a pattern:

模型性能 ∝ (计算量)^a × (数据量)^b × (参数量)^c

其中 a ≈ b ≈ c ≈ 0.05

结论：只要增加计算、数据、参数，性能就会稳定提升

This discovery directly fueled the subsequent "arms race":

ChatGPT and Alignment (2022) ​

GPT-3 → InstructGPT → ChatGPT

关键步骤：
1. 预训练（Pre-training）：海量文本上学习语言模型
2. 监督微调（SFT）：用人工标注的指令数据微调
3. RLHF：用人类反馈强化学习对齐输出
   a. 训练 Reward Model（人类标注偏好）
   b. PPO 优化策略（最大化 reward）

RLHF was the key to ChatGPT's success. GPT-3 itself was highly capable but "unruly"; RLHF enabled it to follow instructions and refuse harmful requests.

The Era of Multimodality, Reasoning, and Agents (2023-2026) ​

Multimodal Large Models ​

The Reasoning Model Revolution ​

Starting in 2025, models shifted from "answering directly" to "thinking before answering":

  • Chain-of-Thought (CoT): The model generates intermediate reasoning steps, greatly improving accuracy on complex tasks
  • GPT-5 (2025): Introduced long-chain reasoning, with significant improvements in code generation and mathematical reasoning
  • Claude Opus 4.7 (2026): A hybrid reasoning model, outstanding on complex tasks such as software engineering
  • Gemini 3.1 Pro (2026): Reasoning + multimodality + coding in one

FDE perspective: The thinking process of reasoning models generates a large number of hidden tokens (up to 5-10x the final answer), making TTFT and KV Cache a brand-new optimization battleground.

Agent Systems ​

Starting in 2023, research shifted from "the model itself" to "how to use the model":

  • ReAct: Alternating execution of reasoning and action
  • Tool Use: The model calls external tools (search, code execution, APIs)
  • Multi-Agent: Multiple agents collaborate to complete complex tasks
  • Agentic Coding (2025-2026): Models like Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5 Codex can autonomously complete long-cycle software development tasks

Interview Perspective ​

Q: "Tell me about a few key turning points in the history of AI development?"

Answer framework:

  • 2012 AlexNet — GPU + CNN, the start of the deep learning revolution
  • 2017 Transformer — Full parallelization, replacing RNNs
  • 2020 GPT-3 + Scaling Law — Proved "bigger is better", in-context learning
  • 2022 ChatGPT + RLHF — Alignment techniques made models "well-behaved"
  • 2023 Explosion of open-source LLMs — Llama, Mistral, Qwen, lowering the barrier to usage

Q: "Why did GPUs become the standard hardware for AI?"

  • AlexNet (2012) was the first to train a CNN on a GPU, achieving a 50x speedup
  • The GPU's SIMD architecture naturally matches matrix multiplication
  • Since then, AI and GPUs have been deeply intertwined, and FDEs must understand GPU architecture
