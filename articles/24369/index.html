FDE at Microsoft, Google, and Salesforce | Roles and Trends

Original title: Microsoft・Google・SalesforceのFDE｜職種と動き

FDE Consulting · 2026-10-22 · 6 min read

Summary

The article surveys how four major tech companies invested in Forward Deployed Engineers in 2025–2026, based on official announcements and Tokyo job postings checked in October 2026. Microsoft launched Microsoft Frontier Company with $2.5 billion and 6,000 experts, positioning it as beyond FDE. AWS created a $1 billion Forward Deployed Engineering organization. Google Cloud pairs its FDEs with consulting partners including Accenture, which is building a 1,000-person FDE team. Salesforce started an FDE team in April 2025, targeting 1,000 people. The author argues all four acknowledge a shortage of people who can embed AI in customer operations, highlights AWS's shift to outcome-based contracts as the biggest change, and cautions that vendor FDEs are tied to their own products.

Key points

  • Microsoft's Frontier Company invests $2.5B and deploys 6,000 industry and engineering experts to customers.
  • AWS launched a $1B FDE organization, contracting on shared goals and business outcomes rather than billable hours.
  • Google Cloud FDEs work with seven consulting partners; Accenture will build a 1,000-person FDE team.
  • Salesforce grew its FDE team threefold in six months, with 40–50% internal transfers and a six-week training program.
  • Tokyo job postings show differing requirements: bilingual strategy roles at Microsoft, Python/ML skills at Google, Agentforce expertise at Salesforce.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a technical role that goes into the customer's site and takes responsibility for building AI-powered systems and getting them running in production. The job title originated at Palantir and was popularized by OpenAI and Anthropic. In early 2026, major foreign tech companies such as Microsoft, Google, Salesforce, and AWS announced major investments in their FDE organizations.

Here, I will organize what is happening by looking at the four companies' official announcements and the Tokyo job postings on each company's recruiting site. The figures and dates were confirmed on each company's pages on October 21 and 22, 2026. The FDE role itself is covered in What is an FDE? The meaning and work of a Forward Deployed Engineer, the moves by OpenAI and Anthropic in OpenAI and Anthropic's FDEs, and the origin of the FDE in Palantir's FDE model.

What the four companies have in common is that they are trying to build, both in-house and through major consultancies, a system that not only sells products but also sends engineers into customers to get things running through to production. In my view, this is a move in which each company has acknowledged that, even as AI products become more powerful, there are not enough people who can get into customers' operations and make things work in production.

Microsoft — an organization that claims to go "beyond" the FDE

On July 2, 2026, Microsoft announced Microsoft Frontier Company on its official blog. It is a new business company that delivers AI-driven transformation for customers, and the announcement states that Microsoft is investing $2.5 billion and placing 6,000 industry and engineering experts inside customers. The same announcement positioned this as going beyond what has been called Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE).

Microsoft is building its own staff while also partnering with major consultancies. The announcement cites FDE partnerships with global SI partners such as Accenture, Capgemini, EY, KPMG, and PwC. On March 18, 2026, Accenture announced it would launch an FDE practice jointly with Microsoft, gathering thousands of engineers with AI skills to work directly with customers.

The recruiting site also has postings for Tokyo-based positions. A posting for Principal Forward Deployed Engineer - Software Engineer, published on July 29, 2026, requires using industry and customer context to create solutions that go beyond generic approaches, and defining a clear scope and technical direction to hand work to FDE crews (the operational FDE teams). The application requirements include fluency in Japanese and English.

Google Cloud — "builders" who work with partners

In his keynote text at Cloud Next '26 on April 22, 2026, Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian wrote that Google Cloud's FDE teams would be paired with teams from Accenture, Capgemini, Cognizant, Deloitte, HCLTech, PwC, and TCS to accelerate customers' AI agent initiatives. On September 8, 2026, Accenture announced it would form a Gemini Enterprise business group with Google Cloud and establish a 1,000-person FDE organization.

Google's recruiting site has a posting for Forward Deployed Engineer, Generative AI, Google Cloud (Japanese, English) based in Tokyo. The job description calls it an embedded builder who bridges the gap between cutting-edge AI products and the production realities of customers. It also describes the role of feeding insights gained in the field back into Google Cloud's product planning. The required qualifications include 5 years of experience with Python and machine learning libraries.

Salesforce — FDEs specialized in its own products

Salesforce launched its FDE team in April 2025. An official blog post on November 19 of that year stated that Salesforce alone had announced it would build a 1,000-person FDE team. Some FDEs have begun operating in small teams made up of one Deployment Strategist and two FDEs. The Deployment Strategist finds the best use cases for the customer and builds the AI strategy, while the FDEs design, build, and deploy agents.

According to an article dated March 18, 2026, Salesforce tripled its FDE team in six months, drawing from three internal teams: engineering, professional services, and customer success. About 40-50% of FDE transfers came from within the company, and in September 2025 it created a six-week onboarding training program. The executive in charge described the approach as "curiosity over credentials."

There are openings in Tokyo too. The Forward Deployed Engineer (Multiple Levels) posting published on August 5, 2026 requires designing and developing customer-facing solutions using the Agentforce platform, with 25–50% travel and fluency in Japanese and English as conditions.

AWS — a $1 billion FDE organization

On June 30, 2026, AWS announced the creation of a specialized organization called AWS Forward Deployed Engineering. Backed by a $1 billion investment, it will place thousands of experts inside customers' organizations to build and deploy agentic AI solutions together. The announcement states that work with customers will be structured around shared goals and business outcomes, rather than billable hours.

In my view, this single sentence is the biggest change among the four companies. A major foreign firm is shifting, at least on paper, from work sold by the hour to work accountable for results.

For those considering a career move, note that every company's FDE assumes its own products or cloud. It is easy to gain experience working deeply with a product, but challenges outside the product are harder to address. I have summarized how to find companies in Japan that employ FDEs and how to search for openings in FDE companies in Japan and FDE job and career change guide.

How to choose from the client's perspective

From the perspective of companies considering AI adoption, the FDEs of major foreign firms are strong partners once you have decided to use that company's products or cloud. The people who know the product best come inside the customer and build through to production. On the other hand, at the stage of deciding which product to use, or at the stage of rebuilding operations spanning multiple products, a partner not tied to a specific product is a better fit.

In my view, there are three things to verify: whether the person who made the proposal will actually join the field to build, whether they can show something running in production, and which numbers they take responsibility for. For partners who say, as in AWS's announcement, "we contract on outcomes," it is best to decide upfront which numbers count as results.
