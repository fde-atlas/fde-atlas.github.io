Buyer's guide6 questions

What does a healthcare AI consulting company do?

It decides with you where AI should do work in a clinic or health system, then builds it: agents that check eligibility and prior auth, coding assistants, denial appeals, documentation tools, patient access agents. The useful ones put it live inside your EHR and billing workflow and stay until your staff use it. The rest hand over a roadmap.

How much does healthcare AI consulting cost?

None of the ten ranked firms prints a dollar figure. Provectus prints the structure, a one week fixed fee Sprint then an 8 to 14 week build priced by milestone, and Tegria runs four to five week proofs of concept. Ask every firm for the price of the first phase in writing before a second meeting.

Where does AI pay first in a medical practice or health system?

In the revenue cycle, on the evidence of the pages read. Seven of the ten show revenue cycle work on the pages read: denials, prior authorization, coding, underpayments and appeals. Cognizant's MetroHealth result, 30% fewer denials, and Guidehouse's 27% lower revenue cycle cost at Hugh Chatham are both revenue cycle numbers.

Do I need an Epic specialist?

If you run Epic and pay for AI features you haven't switched on, yes, start there: Impact Advisors and Tegria both work inside Epic, and Provectus builds agents that sit above it. If you run athenahealth, eClinicalWorks or a dental system such as Dentrix, ask every firm for a case on your platform, because most of the cases read here are Epic.

Is the data safe with a consulting firm?

Ask for the business associate agreement before any data moves, and ask where the model runs. Huron says it builds HIPAA compliant AI solutions and Provectus says "HIPAA controls stay yours", but no firm here prints its own HITRUST certificate on the pages read.

Consulting firm or forward deployed engineer?

A consulting firm brings a team and a programme, which suits a health system with an Epic estate and a board that wants a known name. A forward deployed engineer is one senior builder inside your business, which suits a clinic or dental group of under a few hundred staff whose systems need joining up before any AI can work.

How to choose

  • 1This week, pull last month's denial rate and days in accounts receivable from your billing system, and write them down. That's the baseline every firm should be measured against, and a firm that won't take one is selling a deck.
  • 2List the systems a claim touches on its way to cash: the scheduling system, the EHR, the clearinghouse, the billing system and the spreadsheet someone keeps on the side. Check whether the same patient carries the same ID in each. If not, joining them up is the first job, and any AI built before that runs on bad data.
  • 3Pick one workflow, not a strategy: prior auth for one specialty, or denials from one payer. Tegria's four to five week proofs of concept and Provectus's one to two week read only baseline both start this narrow.
  • 4Ask each firm for the price of the first phase in writing, how long it runs and what you hold at the end of it: working software in your workflow or a document.
  • 5Ask who owns the code, the prompts, the agents and the cloud accounts when you leave, and get the business associate agreement before any patient data moves. Provectus commits to handing over every line of code at go live; get the same sentence from whoever you hire.
  • 6Ask who builds it, by name, and for a reference at a provider your size. Huron sells forward deployed engineers by name; check that the people on the sales call are the people on the build.
  • 7If your scheduling, EHR and billing still don't talk to each other and staff retype between them, that's when you bring in a forward deployed engineer rather than a consulting team.
