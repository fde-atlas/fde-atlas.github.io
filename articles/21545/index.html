An AI agent (AI Agent) is an AI system built around an LLM (large language model) that autonomously repeats planning, decision-making, action, and verification toward a given goal. By "thinking while acting" like a human, it can automatically execute complex business tasks that go beyond simple one-off question answering.

Traditional chatbots and AI agents are often confused, but there is an essential difference between the two.

The core technologies supporting AI agents consist of the following four elements.

  • LLM (reasoning engine): Models such as GPT-4o, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and Gemini 2.0 Flash function as the "brain," planning and deciding the next action
  • Tools (actuators): Functions that actually "take action," such as web search, code execution, database queries, and external API calls
  • Memory: Utilizes past action results through short-term memory (conversation context) and long-term memory (storage in vector databases, etc.)
  • Planning and reflection loop: Uses frameworks such as ReAct (reasoning → action → observation → re-reasoning) to approach the goal through trial and error

Behind the attention AI agents are receiving is a technical breakthrough: the ability to automate "tasks that previously could not be automated with AI."

Comparison with traditional RPA (Robotic Process Automation):

RPA is an automation tool that "repeats predefined procedures according to rules." It excels at automating routine, exception-free processes, such as recognizing screen coordinates and clicking, but it does not function when situations change, data is irregular, or judgment is required.

AI agents, thanks to the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, can "read the situation and make judgments," enabling the automation of "non-routine, complex tasks" that RPA could not handle. This is why some call AI agents "RPA 2.0."

Main business impacts:

  • Qualitative change in business automation: Flexible handling of situations outside the rules greatly expands automation coverage
  • 24/7 operation: Eliminates human resource bottlenecks and enables processing of tasks at night and on holidays
  • Shift of knowledge workers to higher-value work: Routine tasks are delegated to agents, allowing humans to focus on strategic decisions
  • Scalability: Processing volume can be increased during busy periods without additional costs

Customer support is the area where AI agents are most widely put into practical use. Unlike traditional FAQ bots, AI agents can deeply interpret inquiry content and, when necessary, operate internal systems to resolve problems.

Main tasks AI agents can perform in support operations:

  • Directly entering and executing order, return, and change procedures in systems
  • Generating individually optimized responses by referencing customers' inquiry history and purchase data
  • Transferring to human operators only when escalation is needed, automatically generating summaries and context at that time
  • Multilingual support (global support through real-time translation)

In overseas examples, there are reports that AI agents introduced in customer support automatically handle more than 2,300 support tickets per month, improving email response productivity by 10x. There are also cases where AI resolves about 30% of inquiries on its own without human agents.

Business automation in sales and marketing is one of the areas where AI agents deliver the greatest return on investment.

Typical workflow of sales automation with AI agents:

  • Lead discovery: Automatically research companies and individuals meeting specific criteria (industry, size, job title, etc.) from LinkedIn and corporate databases
  • List creation and enrichment: Supplement collected leads' contact details, company information, and recent news via APIs
  • Personalized email generation: Automatically generate individually optimized outreach emails based on lead information
  • Sending and follow-up management: Monitor open rates and reply status, and automatically send follow-up emails
  • Automatic CRM registration: Automatically enter deal information into Salesforce or HubSpot

By having AI agents handle these processes, sales representatives can focus on essential work that only humans can do, such as meetings, relationship building, and closing deals.

Challenges like "the information exists in-house but takes time to find" and "we manually collect data from various systems to create reports" are typical inefficiencies common to many companies. AI agents dramatically streamline these tasks.

Key functions of internal knowledge agents:

  • Cross-search internal wikis, Confluence, SharePoint, and Slack history to instantly retrieve relevant information
  • Execute natural language instructions such as "Create this month's weekly report based on last month's project progress"
  • Automatically aggregate data from multiple data sources (SFA, ERP, data warehouses) and generate reports in a specified format
  • Provide 24/7 automated onboarding Q&A for new employees (internal policies, procedures, FAQs)

Selecting the LLM that serves as the AI agent's "brain" is the most critical design decision, as it affects the agent's performance, cost, and speed.

Key criteria for LLM selection:

  • Task complexity: Use high-performance models for complex multi-step reasoning; use lightweight models for simple classification or summarization to reduce costs
  • Cost: Since cost is determined by API call frequency × token count, optimizing input/output token counts is essential in production environments
  • Latency: Response speed is critical for customer support requiring real-time responses
  • Data privacy: When handling sensitive information, choose open-source models that can be deployed on-premises or Azure OpenAI Service (with SLA)

In AI agent implementation, RAG, which compensates for the knowledge limits of LLMs, and Tool Calling, which operates external systems, are the two essential technologies.

The role of RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation):

RAG is a mechanism that retrieves company-specific information or up-to-date data not held by the LLM from external databases and incorporates it as the basis for answers. When building it into an agent, optimizing retrieval accuracy (chunking strategies and hybrid search) greatly affects quality.

ユーザー質問 → ベクトル検索（社内ドキュメント）→ 関連情報を取得 → LLMが総合判断して回答

The role of Tool Calling (Function Calling):

This is a feature that calls predefined tools (functions) when the LLM determines that "this tool should be used." It serves as the "hands" of modern AI agents.

Examples of main tools used with Tool Calling:

  • Web search tools: Search real-time web information using Tavily, SerpAPI, etc.
  • Code execution tools: Generate and run Python or JavaScript code for data processing and graph generation
  • Database query tools: Generate SQL from natural language to execute database searches
  • External API tools: Integration with business SaaS such as Salesforce, Slack, Google Calendar, and Weather API
  • File operation tools: Loading, generating, and saving PDFs, Excel files, and images

Design patterns for multi-agent configurations:

For complex business processes, a "multi-agent" configuration where multiple agents share roles is more effective than a single agent.

Example: Multi-agent configuration for sales automation

  • Research agent: Collecting and investigating information on lead candidates
  • Writer agent: Generating personalized outreach email copy
  • Review agent: Quality-checking generated emails and suggesting corrections
  • Sending agent: Registering in the CRM and executing email sends
  • Orchestrator: Managing and coordinating the progress of each agent

For AI agent development, a phased approach of "PoC (Proof of Concept) → pilot operation → full production rollout" is recommended in most cases.

Overview of the PoC phase:

A PoC is a phase in which, before full-scale development, you verify in a small-scale environment whether "AI agents are effective for this problem," "which technology stack is optimal," and "whether the expected ROI is achievable."

PoC phase itemsDetails
Typical cost1 million to 5 million yen (varies by scale and complexity)
Typical duration1 to 3 months
DeliverablesWorking prototype, technology selection report, ROI estimate, recommendations for production migration
Main verification itemsLLM selection, RAG accuracy, Tool Calling behavior verification, user acceptance testing

Keys to PoC success:

  • Narrow the verification scope to 1 or 2 business problems (if you broaden it too much, nothing can be verified)
  • Clearly define success/failure evaluation criteria (KPIs) before starting the PoC
  • Involve end users (business stakeholders) as testers from an early stage
  • Base the final decision on "whether it can be introduced into the business," not just technical feasibility

Once effectiveness is confirmed in the PoC, you move into the migration (scale-up) phase for the production environment. In this phase, you must meet production requirements such as quality, security, availability, and monitoring systems.

Ongoing running costs:

After going into production, the following running costs are incurred in addition to development costs.

  • LLM API costs: Usage-based billing according to processing volume (tens of thousands to millions of yen per month, depending on scale)
  • Cloud infrastructure costs: Vector DB, computing, storage (from tens of thousands of yen per month)
  • Maintenance and improvement costs: Prompt improvement, new feature additions, incident response (around 200,000 to 500,000 yen per month)

Situations where in-house development is advantageous:

  • When you want to continuously improve and expand your AI agent (for product-oriented cases)
  • When customization requiring knowledge of confidential internal data and business workflows is essential
  • When you want to strengthen internal AI capabilities as part of a long-term AI utilization strategy
  • When investment in an in-house team is expected to yield higher ROI than ongoing outsourcing costs

Steps for in-housing:

A. Generative AI like ChatGPT is fundamentally based on one-off conversations that "answer questions," generating a single response each time the user gives an instruction. In contrast, AI agents are essentially different in that they "are given a final goal and autonomously plan and execute multiple actions toward it." For example, given the instruction "create next week's sales report," ChatGPT would directly generate the answer, while an AI agent would autonomously execute a series of tasks: retrieve data from the SFA → aggregate it in Excel → calculate month-over-month changes → generate the text → save it to the specified folder.

Within Python, the fundamentals of asynchronous processing (asyncio), API integration (requests/httpx), and data processing (pandas) are frequently used in practice.

For PoCs and learning purposes, CrewAI is the fastest way to get something working, making it well suited for your first implementation.
