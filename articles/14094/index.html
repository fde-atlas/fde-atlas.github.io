Is the forward deployed engineer role here to stay?

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 16 min read

Summary

The article makes no prediction on whether the FDE role will last; instead it lays out evidence as of September 2026. On the pro side, OpenAI, AWS, Microsoft and Accenture have each committed between $1 billion and $4 billion to FDE organizations, and OpenAI's Colin Jarvis reported growing from 2 to 52 people in one year. On the con side, Decagon's CEO argues platforms beat custom builds, a16z frames the model as trading margin for moat, and writers suspect many FDE postings are rebranded implementation or pre-sales jobs. Palantir's history shows a better product shifts the mix without ending the role. The piece closes with transferable skills like scoping, shipping and measuring outcomes.

Key points

  • OpenAI, AWS, Microsoft and Accenture have all announced funded FDE organizations since 2026.
  • Decagon's CEO argues platform tooling makes custom agent building slow and expensive.
  • a16z's 'Trading Margin for Moat' frames FDEs as a costly but strategic investment.
  • Palantir's history shows better products shift engineers back to core roles without ending FDE work.
  • The article advises verifying the actual job and building transferable skills regardless of title.

You are about to bet a career move on a job title that, until recently, you mostly heard in connection with Palantir. So is the role here to stay, or is it a label that will fade once the current wave of AI deployments settles? If you are still working out what the job is, start with what a forward deployed engineer is.

Short answer: no one can promise it, and this post makes no prediction. What is on record, as of September 2026, is that OpenAI, AWS and Accenture have each announced FDE organizations. Below are both sides, from the companies betting on it and the people raising doubts, then the skills that last either way.

The bet you are making

The real question is not “will the title exist?” It is “if the title fades, will what I did in the job still be worth something?”

There are two ways to lose this bet:

  • The title fades and your work does not transfer. You spent your time configuring one vendor’s product and running meetings, and you have little engineering to show for it.
  • The title survives but your version of it is a different job. The posting said engineer; the work is implementation, pre-sales or account management.

Both risks are about the specific role you take, not the market, so you can check the role before you sign. Here is the scorecard; the evidence follows.

  • For: four large companies are building FDE organizations, and one OpenAI FDE leader reported fast growth in his own team.
  • Against: a platform CEO says products beat custom builds, an investor’s essay names the cost of the model, and writers suspect some FDE jobs are implementation roles with a new title.
  • Mixed: Palantir, where a better product moved people back into engineering but the role survived.

The case against: platforms, economics and focus

The cautions come from a competing CEO, an investor, writers and people who run FDE teams, and they are more specific than “it’s a fad”.

A platform instead of people

In a March 2026 memo published on Decagon’s blog, CEO Jesse Zhang describes the rest of the industry as taking “a Palantir-style approach, where forward-deployed engineers assemble custom agents for each company”, and positions Decagon’s platform tooling as the alternative. Source 8Build the future you wantPublisherDecagon blogSource typecompany blog His argument is that AI agents need constant iteration, and that the custom-build approach makes iteration “expensive and slow”. Source 8Build the future you wantPublisherDecagon blogSource typecompany blog

Read it for what it is: a CEO making the case for his own product. Still, if products absorb more of the custom work, the bespoke build part of the job shrinks.

There is evidence the other way too. Sierra, which builds customer agents with its own engineers, open-sourced a benchmark in September 2026, hyper-τ-bench, which tests how well models can build a customer-service . Source 9Hyper-τ-bench: Evaluating agents that build agentsPublisherSierraSource typecompany blog Its best model configuration, working alone, passed 23.9% of held-out tasks, against 82.2% when paired with an engineer with deep context. Source 9Hyper-τ-bench: Evaluating agents that build agentsPublisherSierraSource typecompany blog It is a vendor’s own benchmark, but it tests exactly the work a platform is supposed to absorb.

The economics

Andreessen Horowitz titled its June 2025 essay on the role “Trading Margin for Moat”. Teams that try to do everything

Jarvis said, in the same November 2025 interview, that the biggest mistake his FDE team made that year was generalizing too early, and his advice to founders building FDE teams was to be very clear about the team’s purpose. That is a lesson about running the model, from someone who runs it: an FDE team without a clear purpose drifts toward doing whatever the customer or the sales team asks.

It fits Jarvis’s point: the model strains when a company treats its FDEs as a resource for every sale. For the day-to-day side of that strain, see whether being an FDE is stressful.

One lesson for your decision, and it is our reading, not Palantir’s:

  • A better product changes the mix; it does not end the job. The people who moved went into software engineering, taking their field experience into the core product.

Implementation with a new label?

The sharper worry is not that the title disappears but that it spreads to jobs that are something else.

In an Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) essay from June 2025, Joe Schmidt wrote that at the best complex AI application companies the implementation role is “often filled by a professional services employee, sometimes rebranded as a forward deployed engineer or an implementation/solutions specialist”. The skills that transfer either way

Vinoo Ganesh wrote in Latent Space, in September 2026, that he led Project Frontline, Palantir’s rotation that turned software engineers into FDEs, and that about 250 people went through it. Source 19The Rise of the Forward Deployed Engineer — and How To Do the Job RightPublisherLatent SpaceSource typenews report Ganesh also wrote in Latent Space that the engineers who mattered were not the ones who shipped the most for customers, but the ones who came back and changed what was built. Source 19The Rise of the Forward Deployed Engineer — and How To Do the Job RightPublisherLatent SpaceSource typenews report

The list below is ours, not any employer’s: the skills an FDE role can build that hold their value whatever the title becomes.

Skill Where it carries
Scoping a vague problem Product, consulting, founding
Shipping inside someone else’s systems Any software team
Measuring whether it worked Product, data, leadership
Explaining trade-offs to non-engineers Every senior role
Turning field patterns into product Product engineering, product management

What each looks like as proof you can show later:

  • Scoping. A one-page write-up where you turned “we want AI for claims” into one workflow, one user and one number to move.
  • Shipping inside their systems. A first version running on the customer’s data, behind their login, through their change process, in weeks, not a demo on your laptop.
  • Measuring. A before-and-after number the customer agreed to in advance, not a number you picked afterward.
  • Explaining. An email or a short deck where an executive chose between two options because you made the trade-off clear.
  • Changing the product. A feature, a connector or a fix that exists in your company’s product because you saw the same problem at more than one customer.

The last item is the one Ganesh points to, and it is what two questions in our bank practice: a pattern you noticed across customers and field feedback that changed a roadmap. If you have a story for each, you have the core of an FDE résumé and a software engineering one.

How to pick a role that keeps its value

You cannot control where the market goes. You can control which version of the job you take. This checklist is our method, built from the cautions above, not something employers publish.

Questions to ask before you accept

  • “What is this team for, and what does it say no to?” A clear answer is a good sign; “whatever the customer needs” is the generalizing-too-early risk.
  • “How many customers does each FDE carry at once, and who decides?” You are listening for a limit and an owner.
  • “How much of a normal week is writing and shipping code?” Ask for a recent example, not a percentage.
  • “When an FDE finds the same problem at several customers, what happens next?” Ask for the last time it changed the product.
  • “Who maintains what an FDE builds after the engagement ends?” If the answer is “the FDE, forever”, the load only grows.
  • “Where have people who left this team gone?” Moves into engineering, product or founding suggest the skills travel.

A fictional illustration. Two offers, same title. Team A says: “We say no to anything that isn’t a production agent. Each FDE carries two customers, and the team lead decides. Last quarter, a connector one FDE built for a bank shipped to every customer.” Team B says: “Whatever the customer needs. FDEs support the account executive on every deal. Coding time varies.” Team A answers three of the six questions clearly: purpose, load and field work reaching the product. Team B answers none of them clearly. Same title, different bet.

Green flags: a named purpose, a limit on engagements, and a story about field work reaching the roadmap. Red flags: a vague answer about coding time, FDEs attached to every sales deal, and nobody who can name a product change that came from the field.

If the interviewer turns the question around

If an interviewer asks why you want a role that might not exist in the same form for long, or why FDE and not software engineering, do not predict the market and do not defend the hype. Try words like these:

“I don’t know what the title will look like later, and I don’t think anyone does. I’m choosing the work: scoping a problem with the customer, shipping inside their systems, and bringing what I learn back into the product. Those skills matter whether I’m called an FDE or a software engineer. What drew me to this team is that you’ve told me what you say no to, so I’d be building, not just configuring.”

Mistakes to avoid:

  • “FDE is the future of software.” It is a prediction you cannot back, and it sounds like you read the headlines, not the job.
  • “It’s basically consulting, but I’m fine with that.” It tells a team that wants engineers that you expect not to build.

Which FDE skills transfer if the title fades?

Scoping a vague problem, shipping a first version inside someone else’s systems, measuring whether it worked, and explaining trade-offs to people who do not write code. Those skills carry into software engineering, product and consulting roles under any title.
