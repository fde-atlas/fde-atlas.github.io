Palantir Forward Deployed Software Engineer Interview (No Offer): Code challenge

Taro · 2017-06-01 · 4 min read

Summary

A candidate recounts a code challenge for Palantir's Forward Deployed Software Engineer role in New York, resulting in no offer. The problem wasn't difficult, but the candidate chased an optimal time-complexity solution and ran out of time; their advice is to submit a working solution first, even if not optimal. The post aggregates 36 interview experiences for this role: a 14% pass rate, with 56% positive, 28% neutral, and 17% negative sentiment. Three employee reviews describe fast growth, big early responsibility, and high autonomy, but also high pressure, little guidance, and expectations of near-constant availability.

Key points

  • Code challenge was not hard, but optimizing cost the candidate the offer
  • Advice: get a working solution down before optimizing complexity
  • 36 interview experiences show a 14% pass rate; 56% positive sentiment
  • Employees cite fast growth, early responsibility, and high autonomy
  • Downsides: high pressure, little direction, and 24/7 expectations

Full text

June 1, 2017

Positive ExperienceNo Offer

Code challenge. Not difficult. Although the later specs were probably O(n) related, definitely make sure you have a solution down, even if it's not the optimal time complexity. Tried for an optimized solution and ended up running out of time. The person overseeing the process was nice and cordial, even allowing for an extension to take the challenge due to scheduling conflicts.

1.0

Be ready to be on your own

Forward Deployed Software Engineer • Former Employee

New York, New York, United States

April 6, 2018

Pros: Salary is great, benefits are amazing, and they pretty much pay for everything.

Cons: The counterpart to having everything paid for you is that Palantir acts as if they own you and expect you to be working 24/7. Also, very little direction is given, and you're expected to figure things out by yourself from the get-go. And god forbid you ask for help, as you will be seen as weak and inefficient. It's a soul-crushing place that does not see workers as humans but merely as code monkeys.

5.0

A great company to grow and learn

Forward Deployed Software Engineer • Current Employee

November 3, 2020

Pros: People are very smart. There is room to grow. It's not uncommon to see people who have been in the company for 1-2 years becoming Leads. Huge responsibilities from day 1. "Choose your own adventure" -> You can create your own growth path in the company if you see the need for it. For example, if you work on X product/customer but you see that there is a need to develop another product, you can start working on that without an issue (as long as you don't jeopardize your other role). D&I is big here. You can get as involved as you want with this, and that's awesome! Overall, this is an amazing company. I love it.

Cons: Too much autonomy at times. This is also a pro, but being independent and figuring out things on your own is not for everyone.

4.0

High Pressure and Lots of Growth in First Year

February 13, 2022

Pros: - Most rapid growth in a role in 5 years of software experience. - Most results and business-impacting work on my resume. - High stakes and high pressure means I've been required to grow. I now have many stories about difficult situations and how I handled them well and poorly and learned from them. This is great for job interviews, builds the skills of a team lead and manager, and just makes me a more effective worker.

Cons: High-pressure, results-driven satisfying client demands means intermittent waves of pressure, leading to overtime. (Managers often encourage taking time off after working overtime.)
