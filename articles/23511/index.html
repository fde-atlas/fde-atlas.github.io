Summary

This Japanese guide by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, analyzes freelance consultant compensation in Japan using data from roughly 500 projects. The average monthly rate is about 1.49 million yen, with the typical annual income range at 12–18 million yen; median incomes are around 12.1 million yen for senior consultants and 17.05 million yen for managers, often 1.3–2x salaried peers. The article breaks down rates by domain, experience, and contract position, offers roadmaps for reaching 10, 15, and 20 million yen income targets, and covers stabilizing utilization and rate negotiation. It also notes freelancers bear social insurance, gap-period, and tax costs, narrowing the net gap.

Key points

  • Average monthly rate is ~1.49M yen; typical annual income is 12–18M yen.
  • Top-paying domains: generative AI, strategy consulting, and SAP/ERP.
  • Reaching 20M yen requires manager-level skills, direct contracts, and upstream deal positioning.
  • Five rate drivers: scarcity, deal position, utilization, minimizing gaps, ongoing skill updates.
  • Negotiation tactics: quantified results, competing offers, and PREP-based rebuttals.

Rate differences between domains are determined by "the magnitude of value impact on the client" and "scarcity."

Why strategy consulting commands high rates: Because it directly informs executive decision-making and a single recommendation can influence investment decisions worth billions of yen, the value per unit is set extremely high. In addition, professionals with strategy project experience at major firms are limited in absolute numbers, so supply is constrained.

Why IT Consulting and Generative AI Have Reached the Same Level as Strategy Consulting: DX promotion and generative AI implementation are top-priority issues as of 2026, and the scarcity of "consultants who also understand technology" is rising rapidly. According to an analysis by Consulting Portal, "These days are sometimes called the DX era, and there is a shortage of IT talent, so DX projects handled by IT consultants tend to command higher rates than strategy projects."

Why PMO and Business Reform Rank in the Middle: There are plenty of projects and the work is stable, but because many people enter this field, rate competition tends to occur, and rates are hard to raise without specialization. However, differentiation such as "PMO with a PM perspective" or "PMO well-versed in a specific ERP" makes higher rates possible.

Required Monthly Rate Benchmark: ¥1.25 million/month (¥15 million/year)

This is a realistic target for years 2–4 after going independent. The following strategies are effective.

① Differentiate from Competitors Through Specialization Rather than "general consulting," narrow down your domain × industry, such as "system modernization consulting for financial institutions" or "DX promotion consulting for manufacturing," and establish an expert position in that area.

② Run Multiple Projects in Parallel By shifting from a single 5-day-per-week project to "a main project of 3 days per week plus a secondary project or advisory contract of 2 days per week," you can raise monthly income by 1.2–1.5x.

③ Move to Direct Contracts and Prime Projects By contracting directly with clients or using platforms with low intermediary costs instead of going through agents, your take-home amount increases by 10–30% for the same workload.

Required Monthly Rate Benchmark: ¥1.67 million/month (¥20 million/year)

Freelance consultants earning ¥20 million a year share common characteristics.

① Focus Only on Projects Equivalent to Manager to Senior Manager Narrow your focus to projects of ¥1.5 million/month or more, and do not take on "analyst-level support work." Focus only on projects you can lead yourself.

② Increase Scarcity with Combined "Technology × Business" Skills For example, an IT consultant adding finance knowledge, or a business consultant adding generative AI implementation skills—by building a position through multiplication, you can become an irreplaceable specialist.

③ Move Up the Project Supply Chain The higher up the supply chain a project is (fourth-tier → third-tier → second-tier → prime contractor), the higher the rate. According to data from Consult Free, even for the same project, differences in the supply chain can create a gap of ¥200,000–500,000 per month.

④ Prioritize "Proving Value" Over Rate Negotiation Many people who maintain a ¥20 million annual income are in a state where "their rates rise naturally without negotiating." This is the result of continuously demonstrating clear business impact (cost reduction, revenue contribution, project success) with numbers on the projects they handle.

A freelance consultant's annual income follows the structure of "monthly rate × months worked per year × number of parallel projects," and the factors that determine these numbers can be summarized into the following five.

This is the most influential factor. The more you specialize in "areas few people can enter × areas with high market demand," the higher your rate becomes.

  • Examples of High-Scarcity Areas: Strategy consulting, SAP implementation, financial regulatory compliance, generative AI implementation support
  • Examples of High-Demand Areas: DX promotion, PMO, generative AI strategy, organizational transformation
  • The Strongest Combinations: SAP × manufacturing DX, generative AI × financial regulation, strategy × M&A PMI, etc.

At Consult Free, over 90% of projects are direct or prime contracts, and one of the key factors behind our average monthly rate of 1.85 million yen is this deal-flow design.

There is a significant income difference between 100% utilization (5 days a week, one project) and 70-80% utilization (about 4 days a week). On the other hand, running multiple projects in parallel can raise utilization to 110-120%.

Examples of utilization rate combinations:

  • Project A (4 days/week, 1 million yen/month) + Project B (1 day/week advisory, 200,000 yen/month) = 1.2 million yen/month, annual income of 14.4 million yen
  • Project A (3 days/week, 800,000 yen/month) + Project B (2 days/week, 500,000 yen/month) = 1.3 million yen/month, annual income of 15.6 million yen

Gaps between projects hit your annual income directly. Your effective utilization rate (the proportion of months with projects) throughout the year determines your annual income.

  • Full utilization for 12 months: annual income = monthly rate × 12
  • One month of downtime: annual income = monthly rate × 11 (at a rate of 1.5 million yen, annual income drops from 18 million yen to 15 million yen)
  • Two months of downtime: annual income = monthly rate × 10 (at the same 1.5 million yen rate, it drops to 13.5 million yen)

To minimize downtime, it is important to develop the habit of "looking for your next project 2-3 months before your contract renewal."

Freelance consultant rates are tied to the "difficulty and scarcity" of the projects you can handle. To maintain and increase your market value, the following continuous self-investment is essential.

  • Updating industry knowledge (keeping up with industry trends, regulatory changes, and technology trends)
  • Acquiring adjacent skills (adding generative AI implementation, financial modeling, data analysis, etc.)
  • Participating in networks and communities (exchanging information with fellow consultants and a source of client referrals)
  • Public outreach (visualizing your expertise through LinkedIn and blogs)

High-rate projects paying 1.5 million yen or more per month share the following common characteristics.

① Projects directly connected to executives and CxOs The closer your stakeholders are to the executive level (CEO, CFO, CDO, etc.), the greater the impact of your deliverables and the higher the rate. Projects where you "present at board meetings" tend to command higher rates than those where you "report to department managers."

② Upstream phases involving transformation and decision-making Upstream projects such as strategy formulation, requirements definition, and concept design command higher rates than downstream operations and maintenance. "Work that decides what to do" is valued more highly in the market than "work that executes what has been decided."

③ High-scarcity specialized knowledge as a prerequisite Projects in high-barrier fields such as SAP/ERP, financial regulation, and healthcare DX have few competitors, so rates remain high.

④ Direct or prime contracts (favorable deal flow) As mentioned above, projects contracted directly with end companies or as prime contracts have no middleman margins, resulting in higher rates.

⑤ Projects with performance-based or variable compensation elements Projects with compensation structures tied to project outcomes or KPI achievement, rather than fixed hourly rates, achieve effectively high rates through added success fees.

Condition ①: Quantifying and visualizing your track record The most important thing is being able to speak in numbers about "which projects you worked on and what business impact you delivered." Concrete results like "reduced operational costs by 25%" or "led from PoC to production deployment in 6 months" are your ticket to high-rate projects.

Condition ②: Establishing recognition in your specialty area Once you become recognized as "an expert in XX field" through LinkedIn posts, conference speaking, and contributions to industry media, high-rate project offers start coming in inbound.

Condition ③: Building direct order channels Most high-rate projects come through direct referral routes from clients. Moving beyond dependence on agents and increasing the proportion of direct orders via former employers, side jobs, and communities is key to raising your rates.

Condition ④: Choosing the right agents When searching for projects through agents, check their prime contract ratio, average rates, and deal flow in advance. Consult Free (average 1.85 million yen, over 90% direct contracts), Hyperform Consultant (largest number of projects in the industry), and Freelance Consultant.jp (specialized in strategy, PMO, and IT) are agents strong in high-rate consulting projects.

The biggest risk for freelance consultants is downtime after a project ends. This section explains how to design your work to keep utilization stable while maintaining your rates.

① Build a portfolio combining multiple projects

Dependence on a single project maximizes income risk. An ideal project portfolio looks like the following.

  • Main project (3-5 days/week, fixed monthly fee): The base accounting for 60-70% of your income. A project expected to continue for 3-6 months
  • Sub-project (1-2 days/week, advisory type): Stable side income of around 200,000-400,000 yen per month. A safety net for when your main project ends
  • Spot project (one-off, performance-based): A project that doubles as skill development and networking. It doesn't need to contribute much to income

② Always keep "searching for the next project" in progress

Consulting contracts often renew on a 3-month cycle, with renewal notices typically coming 1-2 months in advance. The most important habit for keeping utilization close to 100% is to start negotiations for your next project in parallel 2-3 months before your current project ends.

③ Maintain "ongoing contact" with agents

If you stay registered with multiple agents but "only contact them when a project ends," you will be less likely to get priority project introductions. It is important to share status updates about once a month and keep warming your relationship with agents.

When you find yourself "panicking because a project is about to end," you can easily fall into a vicious cycle of accepting projects even at lower rates. Here are key points to prevent this.

Maintain a cash reserve of at least 6 months of living expenses Financial peace of mind is the strongest weapon for protecting your rates. If you are in a situation where "life becomes difficult without a next project," you will have no choice but to accept unfavorable rates and terms.

Present alternatives when asked to lower your rate If a client proposes a rate reduction, prepare alternatives that avoid lowering your rate, such as "reduce working hours in exchange for a lower rate (switch to 3 days/week)" or "maintain the current rate while proposing an added success fee."

The most effective rate negotiation is demonstrating numerically "how much value I can provide." If you have a track record like "I contributed to a cost reduction of 100 million yen in my previous project," you can make the argument that "a monthly fee of 1.5 million yen is an overwhelming bargain in terms of ROI."

Specific examples of quantification:

  • "Reduced workload by 120 hours per month, contributing to an annual cost reduction of 24 million yen in labor costs"
  • "Shortened the PoC period from the standard 9 months to 4 months, contributing to early ROI realization"
  • "Drove AI adoption across three business departments and secured approval for company-wide rollout"

By registering with multiple agents and platforms and running multiple project offers in parallel, the fact that "I have another offer at 1.5 million yen" strengthens your negotiating position.

This doesn't require lying; actually having multiple options becomes legitimate negotiating material that "objectively demonstrates your market value." In the freelance market, parallel registration with multiple agents is standard practice, and clients set their prices with this assumption in mind.

How to practice this:

  • Maintain a state of receiving project proposals from 2-3 agents at all times
  • Naturally disclose that you have offers at the same level or higher rates from other sources while negotiating
  • Proceed with negotiations with the stance that "I'm considering other options, but your project is the most attractive and my top choice"

It's important to prepare rebuttals in advance for when you're asked to lower your rate. The following framework makes it easier to organize your response.

Rebutting rate negotiations using the PREP method:

  • Point: The current rate is appropriate for the market, and I'm reluctant to accept a reduction
  • Reason: The phase I handle (upstream design and executive liaison) has a market standard of ¥X million per month
  • Example: In my previous project, I was contracted at XX million yen under the same conditions, and I'm proposing at that level again this time
  • Point (restated): I promise top performance in return for compensation that matches the value

Many people worry that "being assertive in rate negotiations will damage the relationship," but contracting at an appropriate price is important both for maintaining your self-worth as a consultant and for building long-term relationships.

According to surveys of multiple agents, the overall average monthly rate for freelance consultants is 1.49 million yen (approximately 17.88 million yen per year). However, this is the average for those with projects, and actual income factoring in utilization rates and gaps between projects realistically falls in the 12-15 million yen range. By position, the median annual income is approximately 12.1 million yen for senior consultant level and approximately 17.05 million yen for manager level.

Comparing the same positions, freelancers often earn 1.3 to 2 times the actual income of salaried employees. However, freelancers also face "full self-payment of social insurance," "risk of no income during gaps between projects," and "the burden of tax filing and expense management," so the difference in take-home pay is smaller than the headline difference suggests. Once you can reliably earn 12-15 million yen or more annually, freelancing is often advantageous overall.

There are cases of people transitioning to freelancing with only 1-2 years of consulting experience (analyst level) on projects paying around 700,000-1,000,000 yen per month. However, to consistently secure projects and keep raising your rate, having at least 3-5 years of practical experience is a realistic standard. Going independent earlier carries greater risk of project gaps and rate plateaus, so it's safer to try projects as a side job before making the switch.

Based on data as of 2026, the top three areas by monthly rate are: ①Generative AI/AI consulting (1.5-2.2 million yen), ②Strategy consulting (1.3-2.2 million yen), and ③SAP/ERP (1.2-2.5 million yen). SAP/ERP projects have particularly high ceilings, and annual incomes exceeding 20 million yen are not uncommon for experienced consultants in this area. However, the AI and strategy markets are larger with more abundant project availability.

The most effective timing is contract renewal timing (once every 3-6 months) and immediately after achieving a major result on a project. At renewal, it's natural to discuss compensation in the context of "reviewing your work status," and right after a result, it's easier to negotiate as "fair recognition of your achievements." Conversely, negotiating "when other projects have ended and income is uncertain" puts you at a disadvantage, so it's important to negotiate from a position of financial security.

The main channels for securing projects are the following three. ①Consulting-specialized agents (Consultant Free, High Performer Consultant, Free Consultant.jp, ProConnect, etc.) offer access to the largest number of projects and are especially effective right after going independent. ②Direct contracts (via previous employers and communities) are the channel with the highest rates. ③Inbound via social media (LinkedIn, X) becomes the most stable channel over the medium to long term. A combination of multiple channels is recommended.

You need to maintain a rate of 1.67 million yen or more per month for 12 months. Common conditions among those who achieve this include: ①skills at manager to senior manager level (7+ years of practical experience), ②deal flow centered on direct and prime contracts, ③recognition in a specialized field (industry specialization or rare skills), ④diversified income through multiple parallel projects or advisory contracts. SAP specialists, former strategy consultants, and those with generative AI implementation experience tend to reach 20 million yen relatively quickly.

It's true that monthly income fluctuates more than for salaried employees, but with the right measures it can be sufficiently stabilized. Specifically, the following are effective: ①securing a cash reserve of at least 6 months of living expenses, ②diversifying income through multiple parallel projects, ③maintaining ongoing relationships with agents to minimize project gaps, and ④lining up your next project before contract renewal. Once a project network is built after 2-3 years of independence, gap periods often become nearly zero.

Agents are highly effective for securing projects right after going independent. Margins (commissions) vary by agent, but generally 20-50% is considered the market rate. Agents like ProConnect and Consultant Free, which emphasize prime direct contracts, are designed with lower margins, and there are cases where "a project quoted at 1.2 million yen per month by another agency became 1.7 million yen." After 2-3 years of independence, the standard path to maximizing your rate is to increase the proportion of direct contracts and reduce dependence on agents.

It is highly advantageous. The "technical consultant"—an IT engineer background combined with business consulting skills—is one of the most in-demand profiles for upstream projects such as generative AI, DX, and system modernization. The ability to "understand technology" is in short supply even at major firms, and in the freelance market it serves as a differentiating factor that readily leads to high-rate projects paying 1.5–2 million yen per month. Engineers with these skills have very high market value, and going independent as a freelancer puts them in an environment where they can continuously secure high-rate projects.

The difference is that the role goes beyond consulting alone or engineering alone, crossing the boundary between the two: it is "deployed" directly into the client's site and drives product implementation, data integration, and business improvement end to end. It may be easier to picture it as a hybrid role combining consulting and engineering.
