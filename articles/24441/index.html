Example of How FDE Support Is Provided

Original title: FDEの支援の進め方の例

FDE Consulting · 2026 · 4 min read

Summary

This article from FDE Consulting presents three illustrative (non-client-specific) examples of how forward deployed engineers engage with AI adoption projects. In a large hospital, FDEs design department-specific AI use cases, clarify what data is safe to input, and follow up with low-usage departments. In insurance contract management, they decompose workflows to separate AI-suitable steps from human judgment, build ready-to-use scenarios with real data, and iterate weekly on usage data. In a medical data integration project stuck at the concept stage, they redefine requirements for production conditions, attend the go-live to handle issues, and hand over runbooks so the on-site team can operate independently. The common pattern: FDEs embed on-site, work in weekly cycles from design through adoption, and aim for measurable results reportable to management.

Key points

  • Three example scenarios: hospital AI adoption, insurance contract management, system go-live.
  • Hospital case: department-specific use cases plus clear data-safety boundaries.
  • Insurance case: split workflows into AI-suitable and human-judgment steps.
  • Integration case: production-grade design, go-live support, runbook handover.
  • Common model: weekly on-site cycles until teams run independently.

Full text

Professional services firm (shihōshi, etc.)

Support for automating procedural work (handling document copies, deadline alerts, onboarding/offboarding procedures), a knowledge AI to answer internal questions, and data migration to business systems (starting from item inventory).

How We Work

For three common situations, we show examples of where an FDE starts, what they do, and what end state they aim for. These are not the results of specific client projects.

These are support track records of our operating company (Aim Inc.). Firm names and effect figures are not disclosed.

Healthcare: Large medical institution — Establishing adoption

Common situation

Generative AI tools have been introduced, but the level of usage varies greatly between departments, and outside a few early-adopter departments, usage has hardly spread.

Challenges

  • Work content differs greatly by department, so a one-size-fits-all usage guide doesn't resonate with staff on the ground
  • "What should we use it for?" is unclear, and many staff hold back from using it due to security concerns
  • There are no metrics to measure the impact of adoption, and reporting to management stops at "we introduced it"

FDE approach

STEP 01

We inventory each department's work and design usage scenarios that are "high-impact and low-risk" for each department. Rather than applying the same approach to all departments, we tailor it to the actual work on the ground.

STEP 02

We clarify which categories of information may be entered and concretely show the "range of safe use," eliminating hesitation caused by anxiety.

STEP 03

We monitor usage, identify the causes of low adoption in departments that aren't using it, and follow up individually. We build mechanisms to share successful usage practices across departments.

Target state

  • Usage methods are defined for each department, and new departments can spread them on their own
  • Usage status and effects are visible, enabling reporting to management with numbers

Support structure: 1–2 FDEs embed themselves in the field, accompanying the team on a weekly cycle from usage design through adoption

Finance/Insurance: Contract management work — Driving usage

AI adoption for insurance contract management work is underway, but there aren't enough usage scenarios tailored to the work, so the tools go unused despite being available.

  • There is a gap between generic AI tools and actual contract management work, and staff on the ground can't find where to use them
  • Usage is concentrated among a few staff members and doesn't lead to improvement across the whole team
  • Work procedures are complex, and there's no clear organization of where AI should be incorporated

We break contract management work into steps and separate the processes where AI is effective (verification, drafting, checking, etc.) from those requiring human judgment.

We implement usage scenarios using actual work data and documents, creating a state where they can be used as-is in one's own work.

Alongside implementation, we provide usage support for the field. Based on usage data, we eliminate the causes of non-use and continuously improve the scenarios.

  • Usage that depended on specific staff members spreads across the whole team
  • AI is embedded in the workflow, so usage doesn't end as a one-off

Support structure: Implementation support and field-level usage promotion proceed in parallel. Usage data is reviewed weekly

Healthcare: Data integration with core systems — Production launch

Data integration between an AI-enabled business system and core systems is stuck at the concept stage, and with the design still vague, there's no prospect of production launch.

  • Data integration requirements are vague, and understanding isn't aligned between the departments involved
  • It works in the PoC environment, but production operating conditions (data volume, exception handling, failure response) haven't been considered
  • The post-launch operational structure and procedures haven't been designed

We interview the departments involved to redefine the data integration requirements and raise the design to production-grade, including exception handling and behavior during failures.

We attend the production cutover, isolating and addressing problems on the spot. We provide intensive support through the unstable period right after launch.

We prepare the operations Runbook and hand over in a state where the field team can run daily operations and failure response on their own.

  • The integration system that was stuck at the concept stage runs in production
  • Operational procedures are in place, and the field team alone can run it

Support structure: The FDE continuously handles everything from requirements definition through production cutover attendance and initial stabilization
