Forward Deployed Engineer at Anduril: Role, Salary, and Interview (2026)

fde.directory · 2026-09-11 · 4 min read

The author weighs equity upside and the career value of a clearance against austere field conditions and defense-work fit.

Here is the fact that separates an Anduril Forward Deployed Engineer from every other FDE this site tracks: the job posting is titled “Forward Deployed Engineer,” but internally the role is called Technical Operations Engineer, and the hard prerequisite is not a GitHub profile. It’s US citizenship and eligibility for a security clearance. At OpenAI, Anthropic or Harvey, “forward deployed” is a metaphor — you embed with an enterprise customer and the “field” is a Fortune 500 conference room. At Anduril, the customer is the Department of Defense and the field is a border installation, a forward operating base, or a ship at sea. Same two words, almost entirely different job. If you’re reading FDE listings as one interchangeable category, Anduril is where that assumption breaks.

What the role actually is

Anduril builds autonomous defense hardware — Ghost drones, Sentry surveillance towers, Dive-LD subs, counter-UAS systems — all stitched together by Lattice OS, the software layer that turns thousands of sensor feeds into a single command-and-control picture. The FDE sits at the seam between that hardware and the operator using it. Per Anduril’s own postings, you “deploy and support Anduril’s products all over the world, going forward with products early in their lifecycle and working with engineering teams to iteratively improve them over time.”

Read that carefully. The classic FDE loop — prototype at the customer site, feed the failures back to the product team, harden what works — is intact. What changes is the physics. Your prototype runs on edge compute in a place with intermittent connectivity, mesh radio links, and no ability to hotfix from a coffee shop. The postings ask for C++, Python, Rust, Linux, networking, sensor integration, and real-time systems — but the actual differentiator is temperament: the ability to own a technical problem in an austere environment where the nearest teammate is a time zone away and the user is a soldier, not a data scientist. Anduril splits these seats by product line — Air Defense, Mission Autonomy, Connected Warfare, Ghost — but the shape is consistent across all of them.

Compensation

Aggregated data puts the FDE base band at roughly $155,000–$250,000, and Anduril’s postings note that the listed figure is base salary only, with equity included in the majority of full-time offers. To read that band honestly, set it against Anduril’s software-engineer ladder on Levels.fyi:

Level Total comp (Levels.fyi, 2026)
L3 SWE ~$154K–$215K
L4 SWE (median TC) ~$291K
L5 SWE (median TC) ~$385K
L6 SWE (median TC) ~$415K
FDE / Tech Ops base (posted, base only) ~$155K–$250K

The pattern here is the one that recurs across the FDE market: the field seat’s base overlaps the SWE ladder’s lower-to-middle rungs, and equity is what closes the distance. But Anduril’s equity story is unusually loud right now. The company doubled its valuation to $61 billion in a $5B Series H led by Thrive Capital and Andreessen Horowitz in May 2026, up from $30.5B at its June 2025 Series G — and by July 2026 was reported to be in talks to raise at roughly $100 billion. A grant priced against a mark that has tripled in twelve months carries real, if illiquid, upside. Ask the recruiter for the total-comp target including equity and whether the band shifts with clearance level; a candidate arriving with an active Top Secret is worth more to Anduril than one who still needs sponsoring, and that difference is negotiable.

There’s a second, non-cash form of compensation worth naming: the clearance itself. A funded, active security clearance is a durable career asset that unlocks a whole cleared-defense labor market most engineers can’t touch. For the right person, that’s part of the package.

The interview loop

Anduril hasn’t published an FDE-specific script, so treat this as informed inference from candidate reports and the postings rather than a verified sequence. Expect the standard six-ish stages: recruiter screen, technical phone screen (45–60 min, systems-flavored coding in C++/Python — concurrency, latency, resource constraints, not just LeetCode), a work sample or take-home, and an onsite loop of four to six rounds mixing coding, system design, and behavioral.

Two rounds carry disproportionate weight. The system design round leans toward the real problem domain — design a distributed sensor network, reason about edge compute and degraded connectivity, handle a node that drops mid-mission — rather than the web-scale “design Twitter” prompt. And the mission-alignment behavioral round is not a formality. Anduril screens hard for whether you actually want to build defense technology and whether you’ll hold up when a deployment goes sideways in an uncomfortable place. Clearance eligibility and citizenship get verified early, often in the recruiter screen; if you’re not a US citizen, the loop ends there regardless of how strong your engineering is. That gate, more than any coding round, is what makes this FDE seat structurally different from its AI-lab cousins.

Should you take it?

The case for: you’d join one of the fastest-compounding private companies in the market — $61B and climbing, roughly 4,000 employees and hiring aggressively — in a role where “forward deployed” means what it says, with equity leverage that few companies can match and a clearance that pays dividends for the rest of your career. The case for caution: the posted base sits below Anduril’s mid-level SWE total comp, the work is genuinely field-based (travel, austere environments, hardware that fails in ways no stack trace explains), and it is defense work — a mission fit you either feel or you don’t. Unlike the enterprise-embed FDE, where you can talk your way in on software chops alone, here the citizenship-and-clearance gate is binary. If you clear it and the mission resonates, there are few seats in engineering where the phrase “own the last mile” is this literal.
