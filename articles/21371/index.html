C3 AI FDE Job Requirements Breakdown and Resume Customization

Original title: C3 AI FDE 岗位要求解读与简历定制

FDE Times · 2026-10-24 · 5 min read

Summary

Using four identically titled Forward Deployed Engineer postings at C3 AI as a case study, this guide teaches a three-layer method for reading job descriptions: hard eligibility conditions first (citizenship, security clearances, office attendance), then experience and education thresholds, then bonus skills. The London role needs a STEM degree plus 2 years of object-oriented programming; the UK Defence role requires 5 years and SC/NATO clearance; both US Federal roles require citizenship. The author recommends tailoring each resume to the posting's keywords to pass ATS screening, mapping every requirement to concrete evidence, and only claiming what you can discuss in interviews.

Key points

  • Four C3 AI FDE postings share one title but differ sharply in requirements.
  • London role: STEM degree, 2+ years OOP experience, five days in office.
  • Defence role: 5+ years experience plus SC/NATO security clearance.
  • Both Federal roles require US citizenship; Lexington Park needs active Secret clearance.
  • Tailor resumes per posting with keywords you can defend in interviews.

Key Takeaways

  • The London role requires a STEM bachelor's degree, 2+ years of object-oriented programming experience, and five days a week in the office; the Defence role raises the experience requirement to 5+ years and also requires SC/NATO security clearance.
  • Both Federal roles require U.S. citizenship; TS/SCI is only a plus for the Tysons role, while the Lexington Park role requires an active Secret clearance.
  • Tailor your resume to each job posting, matching its keywords to pass ATS screening, but only include things you can explain clearly in an interview.

Open C3 AI's careers page and you'll find at least four postings titled Forward Deployed Engineer: one in London, one Federal role in Tysons (Virginia), one on-site role in Lexington Park (Maryland), and one Defence role in the UK.

The job titles are identical, but the entry requirements differ greatly. Someone qualified for one role might be rejected for another before anyone even reads the skills section.

Skimming a posting, spotting familiar tech terms, and clicking apply is the most common way to waste effort on FDE roles, because postings often mix legal requirements, experience thresholds, and skill requirements in the same list.

Aced (formerly Exponent) points out that FDE resumes are often screened by applicant tracking systems (ATS) before anyone reads them. A resume not tailored to a specific posting may stall right there.

This article uses C3 AI as an example to practice a skill applicable at any company: reading job postings in layers, then matching each requirement to evidence in your resume.

One Job Title, Four Different Doors

The London posting describes the core work as building full-stack, AI-driven enterprise applications on the C3 AI Platform. The Lexington Park on-site role is clearly more operations-focused: keeping the platform stable and maximizing uptime in an on-premises environment isolated from customer networks.

The work differs, and who qualifies for the job differs even more.

The "not listed" entries for the two Federal roles do not mean those postings have no experience requirements. Re-read the original posting or ask the recruiter before applying; don't assume anyone can get in.

As the table shows, skills only decide the outcome once the other conditions are met. A brilliant JavaScript engineer without U.S. citizenship has no chance at either Federal role, no matter how good the code is.

Reading a Job Posting in Three Layers

The first layer is hard requirements: nationality, security clearance, residency duration, and in-office requirements. These are non-negotiable and should be read first. The sentence "This role requires U.S. citizenship" in a Federal posting has only two answers: yes or no. It is not a skill you can learn.

Read this layer slowly too. The Defence role requires SC clearance. According to Security Cleared Jobs, SC clearance usually requires 5 years of continuous UK residency before applying, but not necessarily British citizenship. Those who read too quickly can easily assume clearance equals citizenship and disqualify themselves.

The second layer is minimum thresholds: education and years of experience. The same person may qualify for the London role but fall short of the Defence role, because 2 years and 5 years are entirely different thresholds. Note the wording: what's required is professional software development experience using object-oriented languages, not total years of work.

The third layer is preferred skills, such as JavaScript and Python in the London posting. Missing a few here doesn't prevent you from applying. But after passing the first two layers, this is the only part you can still improve on your own, and it's worth the effort.

Example: A Backend Engineer with Three Years of Experience Targeting the London Role

Imagine an engineer named Minh: three years writing Java for backend systems, occasionally fixing React frontends, with an engineering degree, the right to work in the UK, and happy to be in the office all week. Minh passes the first and second layers of the London posting; what remains is the third layer and the job description.

The method is to lay out two columns side by side: on the left, each requirement from the posting; on the right, a specific piece of evidence. For "2+ years of experience with object-oriented languages," instead of writing "3 years of Java experience," Minh writes: "Designed and ran three Java services for an internal operations team, owning everything from API to deployment."

For JavaScript, a vague bullet like "familiar with React" is far weaker than this sentence: "Built an order-tracking page in React that connected directly to an API I wrote myself." This sentence also proves the "full-stack" part of the job description, something a list of tech terms cannot do.

Aced recommends tailoring your resume for every FDE application, matching the posting's keywords, but only writing things you can explain clearly in an interview. So Minh uses the posting's exact words: "full-stack," "JavaScript," "object-oriented," each backed by a work experience Minh can discuss in detail.

After this exercise, two cells in Minh's right column remain empty: Python and knowledge of the C3 AI Platform. These are two concrete tasks for the coming month, far clearer than vague advice like "learn more AI."

How to Prepare for a Model-Driven Platform

Since the FDE's main job here is building applications on the C3 AI Platform, it would be a shame to walk into an interview without knowing what it is. The official documentation describes it as a model-driven environment for developing and running enterprise AI applications.

The documentation also notes that applications are built starting from a common modeling language called the C3 AI Type System. For engineers used to writing everything from scratch, this means a different way of working: read the overview and figure out for yourself what the platform handles and what you need to write.

You don't need to memorize the docs. An FDE builds applications for enterprises and can explain the platform in plain language, which is more worth practicing than remembering details. Practice explaining in a few sentences what the Type System is, and why starting from the data model changes how applications are written.

Mistakes that get your resume rejected early

The first mistake is sending the same resume to every role. The Defence role requires 5+ years of experience and an SC/NATO security clearance, while the London role requires 2+ years of experience and five days a week in the office. Sending a resume with only three years of experience to the Defence role wastes everyone's time.

The second mistake is stuffing keywords to get past the ATS without a story behind them. Matching the wording of the job posting can get your resume in front of a human, but every line you write may become an interview question.

The third mistake is reading "nice to have" as "required," or the other way around. TS/SCI is only a preference for the Tysons role, while an active Secret clearance is required for the Lexington Park role. Both postings contain the word Federal, and skimming over that line is the costliest mistake.

Layered reading of job postings isn't just for C3 AI. Every FDE posting you encounter from now on has the same three layers, and anyone who can distinguish them within ten minutes knows which role to apply for and what their resume is still missing.
