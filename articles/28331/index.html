Today's Judgment

Today, the FDE role received triple confirmation: OpenAI packaged years of customer deployment experience into Presence, explicitly delivered by FDEs and system integrators; four Beijing departments wrote "accelerating the adoption of AI agents through model innovations such as Forward Deployed Engineers" into official policy; and monday.com published data on the identity, permissions, evals, interception, and rollback of production-grade agents.

Taken together:

  • The deliverable of enterprise AI is no longer a chat interface, but a specific role + controlled actions + continuous evals + production feedback + on-site deployment.
  • The value of an FDE is not just helping customers build agents, but fitting unstable model capabilities into business systems that are acceptance-testable, accountable, and continuously improvable.

Key Signals

OpenAI Presence: The first enterprise agent product explicitly delivered by FDEs

  • Source: OpenAI | Original link
  • OpenAI released Presence on July 22, 2026, covering customer service, outbound sales, and high-risk internal processes. Each deployment starts from a specific role, opening only the knowledge and system permissions needed for that role, accompanied by policies, SOPs, guardrails, approval actions, simulations, evals, and a Codex-driven continuous improvement loop.
  • Presence is already used for OpenAI's own English-language phone support, reaching or exceeding frontline human quality benchmarks within weeks and currently resolving 75% of calls without human assistance; the Codex improvement loop reduced human handoff rates by 15 percentage points within 10 days. The product is available only to eligible enterprise customers, with no self-service purchase; deployments are led by FDEs and designated global system integrators.
  • Why it matters: This is the most direct evidence yet of "FDE productization." What OpenAI sells is not model calls, but role definitions, system connections, permission policies, evals, launch, and continuous optimization bundled into a deployed product; the FDE is part of the product, not an after-sales add-on.

Beijing's new AI agent policy: China's first provincial-level policy to include "Forward Deployed Engineers"

  • Source: Beijing Municipal Development and Reform Commission and three other departments | Original link
  • On July 21, 2026, the Beijing Municipal Development and Reform Commission, Cyberspace Administration, Science and Technology Commission, and Bureau of Economy and Information Technology jointly issued the "Beijing Measures for Accelerating AI Agent-Led Development" (Jing Fa Gai [2026] No. 1185), which took effect on July 23. The document explicitly states: "Support accelerating the adoption of AI agents through model innovations such as Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), improving agent capabilities through on-site co-creation and continuous iteration."
  • The same policy also covers: Harness Engineering, Agent Interconnection Protocol (AIP), AI OS, OPC one-person companies, Token economy (TaaS/AaaS/RaaS), tiered and classified regulation, and compute vouchers and Token vouchers.
  • Why it matters: This is the first time FDE has moved from an enterprise methodology into a Chinese provincial-level policy document. The policy distills the essence of FDE into three keywords—model innovation, on-site co-creation, and continuous iteration. This is not just a job title being mentioned, but a delivery model being incorporated into the industrial policy toolkit.

monday.com's production-grade agents: True autonomy comes from identity, evals, and interception systems

  • Source: AWS | Original link
  • AWS and monday.com publicly disclosed their production-grade AI Teammates architecture on July 22, 2026. Each agent has a stable identity, a manager, a scope of responsibility, and a performance score, and can be assigned, reviewed, or deactivated in Slack, GitHub, and monday.
  • Of Morphex, the first fully autonomous engineering agent's 20 PRs, about 19 could be merged automatically; in a broader sample of high-output agents, about 30% of PRs were ultimately merged, roughly three-quarters of which required no human modification, with rollback rates remaining in the low single digits. Automated guardrails review tens of thousands of PRs per month, with about 20% intercepted at least once, and human overrides in the low single digits.
  • Why it matters: Agent autonomy is not simply removing humans; it requires first establishing stable identities, shared state, real sandboxes, deterministic standards, eval trajectories, historical rollback rates, and confidence-based routing. "How many errors were intercepted" should be listed alongside "how many tasks were completed autonomously" as an acceptance metric.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Redefine what you are selling: Not "building agents," but "deploying a specific role or process into production, and being accountable for outcomes, permissions, and continuous improvement." OpenAI Presence already defines its product this way.
  • Use the Beijing policy as a trust entry point: China's first provincial-level policy to include FDE is one of the most persuasive endorsements when facing enterprise customers. You can use the policy as a conversation starter—"Beijing has already written FDE into its AI agent adoption policy. Is your enterprise ready?"
  • Add reverse numbers to acceptance metrics: Every project should report not only autonomous completion rates, but also rule interception rates, human handoff rates, and post-launch improvement cycles. monday.com's data proves that "how many errors were intercepted" is just as important as "how many tasks were completed."
  • Pay attention to policy support tools: Compute vouchers, Token vouchers, and agent service vouchers—these subsidy tools can help SMEs lower the barrier to purchasing FDE services for the first time.
