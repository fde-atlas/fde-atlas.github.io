FDE Position for New Graduates: Entry Requirements for Forward Deployed Engineer Campus Recruiting

Original title: 应届生 FDE 岗位：前线部署工程师校招门槛

FDE Times · 2026-10-08 · 5 min read

Summary

The article examines new-grad FDE openings at Palantir and Applied Intuition, concluding that the door exists but is narrow: both companies only accept candidates graduating in a fixed window (December 2026 to spring/summer 2027). Screening hinges less on tech stacks than on communication skills, on-site requirements, and for Palantir's UK government role, security clearance. Addressing Paraform's claim that FDE work demands senior judgment, the author argues small-team structures can bridge that gap, so the two positions aren't contradictory. For most Vietnamese engineers, the realistic route is 12-18 months of real deployment experience first, ideally work involving direct customer contact, with applications prepared about a year before graduation.

New-grad FDE roles do exist, but the entry point is narrow and opens only in fixed graduation windows; for most engineers, the realistic path is a detour you can start preparing for right now.

Key takeaways

  • Palantir and Applied Intuition are both hiring new-grad FDEs, but only accept candidates graduating within a fixed window: December 2026 to spring or summer 2027.
  • Paraform warns that FDE work requires senior-level judgment; Applied Intuition places new hires in small teams, which can be understood as a way to bridge that gap.
  • For most engineers in Vietnam, the more realistic path is to first accumulate 12-18 months of deployment experience, then move into an FDE role.

Palantir, which calls itself a pioneer in embedding engineers inside customer organizations, is currently hiring Forward Deployed Software Engineers who have not yet graduated. The job posting promises new grads that they will be given responsibility from day one.

Also this year, the recruiting platform Paraform advised companies not to post Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roles at junior levels. The reasoning was brief: FDE work requires senior-level judgment.

These two positions seem to conflict, and they raise the question that many final-year students and engineers with one or two years of experience in Vietnam are asking: do junior FDE roles actually exist?

The answer is yes, but the door is narrow, opens only seasonally, and appears designed precisely to compensate for the judgment that new grads still lack. For most engineers in Vietnam, the realistic route is a detour, and that detour can start being prepared for now.

The new-grad door is real, but it opens on a calendar

Palantir's Commercial role, based in Chicago, describes work nearly identical to that of a senior FDE. New hires sit with customers and design and build software and LLM workflows end to end. The explicit standard is production-grade quality: software that customers actually use.

Applied Intuition has also opened a Forward Deployed Engineer New Grad role. The company says new hires will work in small teams, own high-impact projects from start to finish, and work from the Sunnyvale office five days a week.

The most critical commonality between the two postings is timing. Palantir only accepts people graduating in December 2026 or spring 2027; Applied Intuition accepts those graduating in December 2026 or summer 2027. If you graduated in 2025 and have already been working for a year, you are not in this candidate pool, no matter how strong your skills.

The real filter is not the tech stack

Reading the requirements closely reveals something surprising. Palantir lists a computer science degree only as "preferred." The hard requirement is proficiency in at least one programming language, such as Python, Java, C++, TypeScript/JavaScript, or a similar language.

Applied Intuition puts communication first. Instead of listing frameworks, the posting requires candidates to be strong communicators who genuinely enjoy solving technical problems directly with customers.

Imagine two candidates. The first has a resume packed with technical buzzwords, but every project is coursework. The second only uses Python, but built a tool for their school's registrar's office, sat down to listen to staff describe their workflows, and revised it three times based on feedback.

For a role like Applied Intuition's, the second candidate has a story to tell in the interview; the first does not.

If senior judgment is required, why hire new grads at all?

Paraform's warning is not without merit. FDE work forces you to make decisions that no ticket will spell out: when to tell a customer "no," which parts must be solid, and which only need to work during a demo.

Paraform also points out that salary gaps between FDEs are largely determined by level and scope of responsibility, meaning the market pays precisely for this kind of judgment.

So why is Palantir willing to hand over responsibility on day one? One reasonable interpretation: taking ownership does not necessarily mean working alone. The small-team model described by Applied Intuition suggests that new hires fully own a piece of work, but within a team, rather than facing customers alone.

Viewed this way, the two sides are not actually in conflict. Paraform says FDE work requires senior judgment, which is true, and the new-grad programs do not deny it.

The strict graduation-window conditions show that these companies open their doors to only a specific class of students each cycle. For everyone else, Paraform's warning still more accurately reflects the market.

Barriers unrelated to skills

Putting the job postings side by side reveals that most of the filters have nothing to do with how well you write code.

Palantir's UK Government role requires candidates to hold or be able to obtain security clearance, which is a major barrier for foreigners that even the strongest resume can hardly overcome. Applied Intuition requires working from the Sunnyvale office five days a week.

Given these conditions, if you graduate within the window, the two postings worth reading closely are Palantir's Commercial role and Applied Intuition's role; the UK Government role adds an additional security clearance hurdle.

For engineers studying and working in Vietnam, the graduation window and the requirement to work on-site overseas are two conditions worth weighing carefully before investing effort in applying.

The 12-18 month detour is not a loser's path

FDE Academy is a training organization that would have every reason to portray the entry barrier as wide open, yet it acknowledges that the direct path for new grads is narrow.

In its view, new grads can still enter the FDE field, but usually need to work for 12 to 18 months first to accumulate real deployment experience. It believes the gap lies mainly in preparation, not talent.

The 12-18 month figure should not be taken as a rule. What is worth remembering is the word "deployment": what matters is experience putting software into real environments and into the hands of real users, not how many years of tickets you closed.

In Vietnam, this kind of experience often hides in places few would call FDE work: outsourcing projects with direct client contact, on-site experience deploying systems for banks, or a small startup where you both write code and listen to user complaints.

Between a glamorous job that only assigns you tickets and a less prestigious one that puts you in front of customers, anyone targeting an FDE role should lean toward the latter.

How to prepare so you don't miss the next cycle

If you're still in school, the first thing to do is check the calendar. New-grad recruiting explicitly states graduation date windows, so you should start following the careers pages of companies with FDE programs about a year before graduation, rather than waiting until you have your degree.

When reading job postings, watch for phrases like "embed with customers," "end-to-end," and "work directly with customers." These indicate whether the role is a true FDE or a software engineering job with a new name. Before spending an evening polishing your resume, read the lines about work location and security clearance carefully.

Don't let your resume be just a list of technical buzzwords. Walk through a project in order: who the users were, what decisions they needed to make, what you built end-to-end, and what you changed after hearing feedback.

This kind of description shows recruiters early signs of judgment, which is exactly what Paraform worries new grads lack.

The door for new grads will remain narrow. But those who are entrusted with major responsibilities on day one are often the ones who practiced taking responsibility before anyone handed it to them.
