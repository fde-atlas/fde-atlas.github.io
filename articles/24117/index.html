Case Studies of Palantir Foundry Implementation by Industry | Results and the Role of FDEs

Original title: Palantir Foundry導入事例を業界別に解説｜成果とFDEの役割

FDE総研 · 2026-10-20 · 10 min read

Summary

This article surveys publicly documented Palantir Foundry deployments across industries—Airbus (Skywise), BP, NHS England's Federated Data Platform, and Japan's SOMPO Group joint venture—and extracts a common pattern: rather than stopping at data integration, Foundry projects model business objects via an Ontology and build operational apps that feed decisions back into workflows. It also explains Palantir's FDE structure, with Delta engineers handling implementation and Echo Deployment Strategists handling business design, describes how FDE work shifts across deployment phases, and offers guidance for Japanese companies on in-house capability, data residency, cost, and choosing a first use case with a clear decision-maker and measurable outcomes.

Key points

  • Key public cases: Airbus Skywise (2017), BP contract extension (2024), NHS England FDP (2023), SOMPO joint venture (2019).
  • Foundry is chosen as a platform for operational apps, not just BI dashboards.
  • Palantir pairs Delta (Forward Deployed Software Engineer) with Echo (Deployment Strategist) on site.
  • FDE phases: scoping, data connection, app building, handover to customer teams, then expansion.
  • Recommended approach: start small with measurable use cases; failures usually stem from missing business owners and delayed operational handover.

Palantir Foundry case studies span a wide range of industries, including aviation, energy, healthcare and the public sector, manufacturing and supply chain, and insurance and elder care. What they have in common is that they connect disparate data using an "Ontology (a model of business objects)" and translate it into on-the-ground decision-making. Based solely on publicly available announcements and reports, this article organizes how Foundry is used by industry and the role of FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers).

Which companies have adopted Palantir Foundry?

Foundry is used by large organizations such as Airbus in aviation, BP in energy, and NHS England in the UK. We will focus on cases that can be confirmed from public information.

What patterns are common across case studies?

The common thread is "not stopping at data integration, but building out operational apps for the field." This is what distinguishes these projects from BI tool implementations.

  • Integration of distributed data: Collect data from ERP, sensors, business systems, Excel, and more via pipelines
  • Modeling with Ontology: Redefine data as business objects such as "aircraft," "equipment," "patients," and "orders"
  • Building operational apps: Using Workshop and similar tools, create screens and actions that field staff use daily
  • Writing decisions back: Reflect decision outcomes in the original systems and business workflows

Reading the public case studies, the results being highlighted are not "dashboards were built," but rather "planning, maintenance, and allocation decisions became faster." Foundry appears to be chosen less as a BI product and more as a platform for building operational applications.

What does Foundry solve, by industry?

Challenges differ by industry, but what Foundry addresses is "slow decision-making across departments and systems." Here is a breakdown by industry.

How is it used in manufacturing, aviation, and energy?

In heavy industries, the focus is on linking equipment and aircraft operational data with maintenance and planning work. Predictive maintenance and supply chain visibility are representative examples.

IndustryTypical challengesHow Foundry solves them (common patterns)
Aviation (Airbus, etc.)Operational and maintenance data for each aircraft is scattered across airlines, manufacturers, and parts suppliersShare data across the industry, as with Skywise, and use it for maintenance planning and parts procurement
Energy (BP, etc.)Sensor data from oil fields and plants is disconnected from operational decision-makingModel equipment as digital twins and use them for operational optimization and safety management
Manufacturing/AutomotiveSlow to notice parts shortages or demand fluctuationsConnect procurement, inventory, and production planning via Ontology to quickly identify the scope of impact

From the perspective of field practitioners, what matters in this domain is not advanced algorithms but a state where "every department can see the same parts and equipment under the same IDs." Stories about master data maintenance taking more effort than expected are common in data integration projects, not just with Foundry.

How is it used in healthcare, the public sector, and finance?

In healthcare and the public sector, use cases for making decisions about the allocation of hospital beds, staff, and supplies while maintaining strict access control stand out. In finance and insurance, the focus is on risk analysis and fraud detection.

  • Healthcare (NHS England, etc.): The NHS FDP is an initiative to connect data across hospitals to support the management of surgical waiting lists and hospital beds. Palantir's adoption has also been debated in the UK regarding the handling of patient data.
  • Public sector/Government: Palantir was originally known for Gotham, built for governments. Foundry is also used for government data integration, and its fine-grained permission management and audit logs are often highly rated.
  • Finance/Insurance: By connecting customer, transaction, and insurance claim data, it is used for fraud detection and underwriting risk analysis.

The more heavily regulated the industry, the more data lineage and permission design come into question. At the technology selection stage, it is advisable to involve legal and information security departments early.

What are the key issues when Japanese companies adopt it?

For Japanese companies, vendor-dependent structures and constraints on data location are common points of discussion. The effectiveness of Foundry depends heavily on whether there are people in-house who can operate it.

  • In-house development ratio: If everything is outsourced to a SIer, the design intent of the Ontology does not remain within the company
  • Data location: Confirm upfront whether storage in a domestic region is required and whether personal information is included
  • Cost outlook: Estimate the cost structure of licenses and computing resources from the PoC stage onward
  • Engaging the front line: Adoption tends to stick when business departments can modify their own screens

There are still not many publicly disclosed cases in Japan. If you are considering adoption, it is realistic to consult Palantir Technologies Japan or implementation partners about whether they can show you non-public reference cases.

What role does an FDE play in Foundry adoption?

An FDE is a role that embeds in the customer's operations and consistently handles everything from identifying business issues to data integration, application building, and adoption. It effectively determines whether a Foundry implementation can deliver results.

How are "Delta" and "Echo" divided at Palantir?

At Palantir, the role corresponding to an FDE is internally called "Delta" and is mainly responsible for technical implementation. Customer dialogue and business design are handled by "Echo" (Deployment Strategist). These two roles work together in the field.

RoleInternal Palantir nameMain responsibilitiesRequired skills
Forward Deployed Software EngineerDeltaPipeline building, Ontology design, application implementationPython/SQL/TypeScript, data modeling
Deployment StrategistEchoProblem definition, stakeholder coordination, defining outcomesBusiness understanding, facilitation, hypothesis building
Product Engineer(Headquarters side)Developing product features, incorporating field feedbackSoftware design, platform development

A distinctive feature of the FDE is moving back and forth between the product team and the customer, judging everything by the standard of "can it be used in the field?" Since such role names and team structures differ by company, for FDE job postings outside Palantir you need to verify the scope of the role individually.

What does an FDE do in each phase of adoption?

An FDE's work changes significantly depending on the adoption phase. Early on, the focus is on exploring issues and connecting data; later, on transferring operations and expanding horizontally.

  • Discovery (days to weeks): Interview the front line and narrow down to a single business area that delivers value
  • Connection: Connect to source systems and build minimal pipelines and an Ontology
  • Building: Create applications for front-line staff and iterate in short cycles while monitoring usage
  • Handover: Transfer operation and modification skills to the customer's engineers and business staff
  • Expansion: Extend use cases to adjacent processes and departments

From a practitioner's perspective, an FDE needs, beyond technical skill, the ability to articulate "whose decisions, and how quickly, will be made." In career terms, it is a role where experience in both data engineering and consulting tends to be valued.

What is the right approach to a successful Foundry adoption?

An approach that tends to succeed is "start small, demonstrate that front-line decisions have changed, then expand." Building a company-wide data platform first is said to be a sequence prone to failure.

How do you choose the first use case?

The standard approach for the first use case is to choose a process with a clear decision-maker and results that can be measured numerically. Prioritize whether the business pain is large, rather than whether the data is readily available.

Selection criterionGood exampleExample to avoid
Decision-makerA responsible decision-maker can be identified, such as a plant manager or logistics headRequires consensus across multiple departments
How results are measuredNumber of stockouts, time spent planning, utilization rateAbstract metrics like "degree of data utilization"
FrequencyDecisions made daily to weeklyPlanning done once a year
DataKey data already exists in existing systemsRequires installing new sensors

With AIP, Palantir introduced a method of building use cases on customer data in a short time through multi-day "AIP Bootcamps." The idea of running initial validation in short cycles applies to Foundry adoption as a whole.

What are the checkpoints for avoiding failure?

Most failures stem not from technical problems but from a missing owner and delays in operational handover. It is important to decide the structure and exit plan before adoption.

  • Appoint a business owner: Place the responsible person on the business side, not in IT
  • Secure in-house FDE talent: Develop people who can make modifications even after Palantir or partners withdraw
  • Design governance: Build permissions, auditing, and data lineage into the design from the start
  • Cost-benefit review: Every quarter, review which use cases are delivering value
  • Lock-in assessment: Check how much of the Ontology and business logic can be taken outside

For engineers interested in FDE, these checkpoints also serve as a list of "the work required in the field." For companies considering adoption, they will be a clue for defining the kind of talent to develop in-house.
