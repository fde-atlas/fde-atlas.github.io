How to Securely Grant Forward Deployed Engineers Access

FWD Deployment · 2026-10-19 · 8 min read

Summary

This guide explains how logistics companies should grant system access to forward deployed engineers (FDEs). Drawing on NIST SP 800-53 AC-20, CISA zero-trust and mobility guidance, and FedRAMP examples, it prescribes a strict sequence: sign an approved connection or processing agreement before issuing any credentials, build task-based least-privilege roles in SAP, TMS and WMS (including narrow service accounts), set an expiry date on every grant, and keep evaluating access after login, including device health. It also covers the VDI-versus-laptop decision, contract topics such as NDAs and IP ownership, a seven-step onboarding protocol, and how to document everything as SOC 2 and ISO 27001 audit evidence.

Key points

  • Sign an approved connection or processing agreement before issuing any credentials, per NIST AC-20.
  • Build task-based least-privilege roles; service accounts and API tokens need their own narrow scope.
  • Set an end date on every grant and keep evaluating access and device health after login.
  • VDI versus company laptops is an implementation choice, not a mandated rule.
  • A seven-step onboarding sequence doubles as SOC 2 and ISO 27001 audit evidence.

Grant forward deployed engineers (FDEs) access only after an approved connection or processing agreement is in place, then give them the least privilege their assigned tasks need, for a defined period, with access re-evaluated continuously. That order follows NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5.1, AC-20, which permits external-system access only after required controls are verified or such an agreement exists. The rest of this article turns that rule into roles, device choices, contract topics and an onboarding sequence.

If you are still deciding what an FDE does day to day, start with our explainer on what the forward deployed engineer job is and who hires. This piece assumes you have already chosen to bring a team in.

What must be in place before an FDE gets any access?

An agreement and verified controls must come first. NIST's external-system control permits access only after required controls are verified or an approved connection or processing agreement is in place.

The earlier NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 4 AC-20 text supports the same practice: require an approved connection or processing agreement before an external engineer uses an external system to handle your organizational information.

In practice, this means no sandbox login, no VPN profile and no test credentials until your legal and security teams have signed off. A kickoff date is not a reason to skip the gate.

How do you build least-privilege roles for FDEs in SAP, a TMS or a WMS?

Create dedicated FDE roles instead of copying an employee's role. NIST requires that users receive only the access necessary to perform assigned organizational tasks, and that rule applies to every platform you run.

SAP authorization names, role behavior and available settings vary by product edition, release, installed components and your own configuration. Check each role design below against the documentation for your deployed release before you build it.

These are implementation choices, not platform-mandated settings:

  • One role per task. Separate reading shipment and inventory data from changing configuration or moving changes between systems.
  • Start read-only. Add write access to a named transaction or function only when a written task needs it.
  • No borrowed roles. Do not clone a power user's role and trim it afterward.
  • Named accounts only. No shared logins, so every action traces to one person.
  • Sponsor approval. One of your managers signs off on each role, in writing, with the task it supports.

NIST also states that least privilege applies to processes acting for users, not only to people. FDEs often build integrations, so service accounts, API credentials and scheduled jobs need their own narrow scope.

How do you keep access limited after the engineer logs in?

Keep evaluating it. CISA's zero-trust guidance says authorization must continue evaluating and monitoring access requests after authentication, and that every user and device should be treated as a potential threat.

CISA's Zero Trust Maturity Model Version 2.0 adds that access should be granular enough to prevent unauthorized access to data and services. For a logistics operation, that means an FDE working on dock scheduling should not be able to open carrier rate tables.

Set up alerts for the cases that matter most to you: a role used outside the assigned module, a login from an unexpected device, or access that continues past the end date.

Should FDEs use company-issued laptops or VDI?

Either can work, and the choice is yours to make. We found no verified rule that requires one over the other, so treat it as an implementation decision based on your risk and your engineers' workflow.

What the guidance does support is the test your choice must pass. CISA's enterprise mobility guidance says device security posture should be continuously monitored and validated, and that authorization decisions should continuously evaluate user privileges, device health and contextual information.

Ask these questions of each option:

  • Can you see the device's health at every access request?
  • Can you revoke access to the device or session the same day an engagement ends?
  • Does the setup let the engineer do the work, or will they route around it?

If an option fails the first two, do not use it, whichever label it carries.

Which contract terms belong in the DPA, NDA and IP assignment?

Have your counsel draft the wording; this article is general information, not legal advice. We can say what the agreements must connect to: the NIST principle that an approved connection or processing agreement exists before access begins.

Use this as a checklist of topics for your lawyer, not as model clauses:

  • Processing scope. Which systems and categories of data the firm may handle, matching the roles you built.
  • Named personnel and end dates. Who may hold access and until when.
  • Device and session conditions. The posture requirements you decided on above.
  • Incident support. A named contact who helps users handle and report incidents. FedRAMP's incident-response guidance calls for an incident-response support resource with exactly that job, which is a useful model.
  • Ownership of work product. Who owns code, configurations and integrations the FDE builds, settled in writing before the first login.
  • Confidentiality. Covering your shipment, customer and carrier information.

Whether the firm's individual engineers sign these terms directly or through the firm depends on your deal structure and your counsel's advice.

What is the step-by-step onboarding protocol?

Use this sequence and do not skip ahead. Each step depends on the one before it.

  • Define the task list. Write what each engineer will do, which systems they touch and who sponsors them.
  • Sign the agreements. Complete the connection or processing agreement and related contract terms before any credential exists.
  • Verify controls. Confirm the firm's required controls and the device setup you chose, as NIST AC-20 contemplates.
  • Build the roles. Create task-based roles in SAP, your TMS and your WMS, plus narrow service accounts, with expiry dates.
  • Provision identity and device. Issue named accounts with strong authentication, and enroll the laptop or VDI session.
  • Start in a non-production environment. Move to production roles only when a task requires them.
  • Review and remove. At the end date, remove access unless a sponsor renews it in writing.

CISA's mobility guidance calls for the least possible privileges for a specified period. FedRAMP's playbook shows one example of time limits in practice: it permits temporary repository access for authorization-package review for up to 60 days. That figure applies to FedRAMP reviews, not to logistics engagements, so set your own period by task.

How does this fit with SOC 2 and ISO 27001?

Record each step inside the control set you already operate. Written task lists, approvals, expiry dates and access reviews give your auditor evidence of who had access, to what, and why.

NIST SP 800-53 is a control framework that your own controls can reference. How FDE access maps to your SOC 2 scope or ISO 27001 controls is a question for your auditor or certification body, so raise it before the engagement starts, not at audit time.

How do you choose a firm you can onboard safely?

Ask each candidate how they would handle the steps above, then compare answers. Our profiles of forward deployed engineering firms show who does this work, and how we pick the companies on this site explains our criteria.

If you are weighing an FDE against an in-house developer for system access, see forward deployed engineer vs software engineer. The access model differs because the FDE sits closer to your live operations.

Common questions

How do you restrict forward deployed engineers from accessing sensitive customer PII in shipping manifests?

Apply least privilege: if an engineer's assigned task does not need consignee or customer fields, their role should not include them, and the same goes for their service accounts. The exact masking method depends on your SAP release and how your TMS or WMS is configured, so have your platform administrator confirm it.

What specific NDA and IP assignment clauses are required when hiring an external forward deployed engineering team?

We cannot give you a verified required wording, and your counsel should draft it. Make sure the agreements cover confidentiality over shipment and customer data, who owns the code and configurations the team builds, and are signed before any access begins.

Should forward deployed engineers use company-issued hardware or secure virtual desktop infrastructure (VDI)?

No verified rule requires one over the other, so it is your decision. Whichever you pick, it must let you continuously monitor and validate device security posture, as CISA's zero-trust mobility guidance describes, and let you cut off access quickly.

How does granting system access to forward deployed engineers impact SOC 2 compliance for logistics providers?

It adds access you must be able to explain: who holds it, what it covers, who approved it and when it ends. How that maps to your SOC 2 scope is a question for your auditor.

What is the standard onboarding timeline for provisioning system access to a forward deployed engineering firm?

It depends on how quickly your gates close: signed agreements, verified controls, role build and device setup. Do not shorten the timeline by issuing credentials before the agreement is in place.
