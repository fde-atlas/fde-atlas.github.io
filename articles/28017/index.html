I'm an ex-Googler who became a forward deployed engineer. It feels like the right career move.

Business Insider · 2026-11-21 · 3 min read

Summary

Paden Gayle, previously a software engineer at Accenture, Bloomberg, and Google, moved into a forward deployed engineer role at Notion in April 2026 after worrying about AI's impact on coding jobs. He describes FDE work as a blend of engineering and consulting: implementing Notion for clients, building custom agentic workflows, and answering technical questions live. He notes FDE interviews add a decomposition round testing reasoning and communication, and suggests the role suits technically strong people who enjoy working directly with customers.

Courtesy of Paden Gayle

Paden Gayle says a forward deployed engineer has to be technical and also communicate with customers.

This as-told-to essay is based on a conversation with Paden Gayle, a forward deployed engineer at Notion, and the author of "The Forward Deployed Engineer." He is 30 years old and lives in New York. The following has been edited for length and clarity.

Before getting into tech, I wanted to pursue music. Cello was a passion of mine from a young age.

Worried about whether I could make a stable income in music, I studied computer information systems. I went on to work at Accenture as a technology analyst, and then at Bloomberg and Google as a software engineer.

In the years following the release of ChatGPT in 2022, I grew fearful of how AI would affect software engineering jobs.

It wasn't about "if" you use AI, but "when," and I wanted to get as much of an advance on that "when" as I could.

That's how I became a forward deployed engineer, or FDE.

A mix of software engineering and consulting

When I learned more about the role, I felt it was a mixture of my role in software engineering and consulting. I liked that the role involved an interpersonal aspect but was still technical.

In late 2025, I started looking for FDE roles. One day, while scrolling on LinkedIn, I saw a hiring manager's post saying Notion, an AI workspace company, was hiring. I DM'd them, got through to the interview process, and was offered the job in April 2026.

My job as an FDE is to help clients implement Notion's products in their workspace, often building on top of the company's platform with custom code to meet their needs. For example, one task could be building out an agentic workflow for a business on top of the company's technology.

I have to stay up to date on new developments to our product. If a customer on a call asks me technical questions about what our company can do for them, I could say, "Let me check on that and get back to you," but I should really be able to answer those questions live.

I do a lot of context switching when working with different clients. I have to understand what each customer wants and what I can do that's valuable for them.

In my experience, the FDE role involves more out-of-the-box thinking than a traditional software engineer. If I were hired to make a client's workflow easier, I'd review their existing workflow, suggest parts that could be automated, and then I could use AI to find solutions, design, and code them. It feels like a subset of AI engineering, as I'm deploying AI into workflows.

FDE interviews place more emphasis on your communication skills

Of the FDE interviews I've done across multiple companies, there are similarities to software engineering interview loops that I've done, such as coding interviews and behavioral and system design rounds.

One key difference is FDE interviews often also include something called a decomposition round. An interviewer gives you a hypothetical situation where you have to meet a client's needs, and you talk through your approach live. Some companies also use a take-home task, which tests similar skills. I've been asked to spend three to four hours building a working solution and present it. In both cases, it feels like they're assessing your reasoning and communication skills.

If you're technical and love talking to people, forward-deployed engineering could be for you

FDEs have to be technically skilled, but they also have to communicate with customers. By contrast, in my experience, software engineers can be siloed in their work. It's about being given a task and delivering it. I've realized that I don't love traditional software engineering. What I really like is working directly with the people the software is for.

I think people who are amazing at software engineering interviews could pass FDE interviews, but the question is more about whether this is a role they want to do. Because I've done a lot of tutoring, I've spent a lot of time helping people. It's something I genuinely love.
