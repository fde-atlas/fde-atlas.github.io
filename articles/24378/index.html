Freelance FDE | How to Think About Rates, How to Find Work, and Whether It Suits You

Original title: FDEのフリーランス｜単価の考え方・仕事の探し方・向き不向き

FDE Consulting · 2026-10-20 · 7 min read

Summary

This Japanese article covers practical issues for working as a freelance Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) in Japan. The author argues the most important contract term is defining in words when the work is considered done; otherwise an FDE engagement becomes disguised on-site staffing. It explains how rates are determined, four channels for finding work, obligations under Japan's Freelance Act (enacted November 2024), and who is suited to going independent. Examples include SB OAI Japan salaries of ¥8.123–20.346 million and FDE Consulting's contracting rate from ¥1 million per month.

Key points

  • Defining the endpoint of responsibility in the contract is the key to avoiding de facto on-site work.
  • Rates depend on five factors: responsibility endpoint, hands-on implementation, hours, languages, and industry knowledge.
  • Reference points: SB OAI Japan lists ¥8.123–20.346 million salaries; FDE Consulting contracts from ¥1 million/month.
  • Japan's Freelance Act (November 2024) requires written terms, payment within 60 days, and other protections.
  • The author recommends gaining 2–3 production experiences as a salaried FDE before going independent.

Job openings for FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) are mostly full-time positions. However, freelance job boards now have an FDE category, and some companies have started hiring FDEs on a contracting basis. FDE Consulting's careers page also lists contracting terms (from 1 million yen per month) alongside full-time positions.

When taking on FDE work as a freelancer, I think there are three things to consider: rates, how to find work, and contracts. In my view, the most important thing for a freelance FDE is to put into words, within the contract, "what counts as completion of the work." If this is vague, the job becomes client-site embedded work with the FDE label attached.

The FDE role itself is covered in What is an FDE? The meaning and work of a Forward Deployed Engineer, and full-time job openings and hiring companies are summarized in FDE Job and Career Change Guide. Here, I will only cover the freelance case.

What Does a Freelance FDE Take On?

An FDE's job is to go into the client's site, break down the problems, implement AI-based systems themselves, and carry the work through to actual use in business operations. The substance doesn't change for freelancers. What changes is the form of the contract and who you are accountable to.

PerspectiveFull-time FDEFreelance FDE
Contracting partyYour own companyThe client, or an intermediary support company
End of workDecided by the companyEnds within the scope written in the contract
Direction on how to proceedReceived from a supervisor at the companyYou commit to deliverables and deadlines, and decide how to proceed yourself
CompensationAnnual salary (fixed pay and bonuses)Monthly or daily rate
Continuity of projectsThe company finds the next clientYou find the next job yourself

The row most easily overlooked in this table is "direction on how to proceed." According to materials from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, even if a contract is formally a service contract, if the actual situation means the person is judged to be a worker under the Labor Standards Act, labor standards laws apply and the Freelance Act does not. A working style where you sit at the client's desk and receive daily work instructions from the client's employees is hard to call a service contract, no matter the FDE label.

The difference between FDE and SES is written up in What is the difference between FDE and SES?. Freelance FDEs should also decide to commit to deliverables rather than working hours, so as not to end up working like an SES.

How to Think About Freelance FDE Rates

Within the scope of my research, I could not find any official statistics summarizing freelance FDE rates. The amounts listed on job boards are figures for the projects posted at that moment, not the level for the profession as a whole. So let's look at two clues separately.

The first is the annual salaries in full-time job postings. As of job postings on October 20, 2026, SB OAI Japan (seconded from SoftBank) lists an FDE with a theoretical annual salary of 8.123 to 20.346 million yen, and JDSC lists an FDE at 5 to 25 million yen. The range of salaries and what determines it is written in How much does an FDE earn?. A full-time salary does not include the company's share of social insurance or pay for periods without projects. When comparing freelance rates to annual salaries, assume you will cover those costs yourself.

The second is the amounts offered by companies hiring on a contracting basis. FDE Consulting's careers page lists contracting work from 1 million yen per month. Since the assumptions (percentage of availability, scope of responsibility) differ by company, simply comparing the amounts side by side has little meaning.

In my view, rates are determined by the following five factors.

FactorDirection that raises the rateQuestions to verify
Endpoint of responsibilityNot just up to PoC, but through production operation and business metricsWhat has to happen for the work to be done?
Whether you implementNot just organizing requirements, but writing the code yourself and deploying to productionWho writes the code, and who deploys it to production?
Percentage of availabilityNot just part of the week, but nearly all of itHow many days a week, and which meetings do you attend?
LanguageNot just Japanese, but Japanese and EnglishWhat languages are used with the client and internally?
Industry knowledgeNot general business, but business in heavily regulated industriesWhat are the industry regulations and constraints on data handling?

What I recommend in rate negotiations is not to state a monthly figure first, but to align the five factors above first. A contract where the endpoint of responsibility is "until business metrics move in production" but the rate is set on the assumption of "up to PoC" becomes more painful the longer it continues.

How to Find Work

In my view, there are four entry points for freelance FDE work.

Entry pointWho it suitsWhat to watch out for
Freelance job boardsPeople who want to see what the market actually containsEven with the "FDE" name, the substance may be only development work
Contracting openings at support companiesPeople who want to join a team rather than contract with a client aloneCheck how the endpoint of responsibility is written in the contract between the support company and the client
Directly from clientsPeople with many industry contactsYou handle everything yourself: contract templates, invoicing, and payment management
Referrals from publishing your workPeople who publicly share what they write and buildWhen building a track record, be careful not to violate client confidentiality

Whichever entry point you use, what to look for in the posting is not the job title. Check whether the three points are stated: "Will you speak directly with the client?", "Will you implement it yourself?", and "Will you carry it through to production?" The five points in the FDE Job and Career Change Guide can be used as-is for reading job postings.

What to Verify in the Contract

On November 1, 2024, the Act on Appropriate Transactions between Freelancers and Business Operators (Freelance Act) came into effect. Business operators that commission work from freelancers (individuals without employees, or corporations with no employees other than a single representative) have the following obligations (from materials of the Miyazaki Labour Bureau, Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare).

ObligationContentScope
取引条件の明示（第3条） 業務を委託したら、直ちに取引の条件を書面か電磁的な方法で明示する 業務委託のすべて
Setting of payment due date (Article 4)The payment due date shall be set within 60 days of receiving the benefits, as short a period as possible, and payment shall be madeSame as above
Prohibited acts (Article 5)Seven acts: refusal of receipt, reduction of compensation, returns, driving down prices, forcing purchases or use, demanding undue economic benefits, and undue changes to or redoing of deliverablesService contracts of one month or longer
Accurate representation of recruitment information (Article 12)When recruiting widely, do not make false or misleading representations, and keep the content accurate and up to dateRecruitment aimed at two or more people
Advance notice of mid-term termination, etc. (Article 16)When terminating a contract mid-term or not renewing it, in principle give notice at least 30 days in advanceService contracts of six months or longer

The law protects only the minimum of a transaction. In an FDE's work, I want to put the following five things in writing beyond what the law requires.

  • The endpoint of responsibility. Whether it ends at PoC, at production operation, or when business metrics actually move
  • How results are measured. Which numbers, at what point in time, and how they are measured
  • Rights to deliverables. Who owns the code, prompts, and evaluation data
  • AI tools that may be used. Whether customer data may be entered into external AI, and under what settings
  • How instructions are received. From whom, in which meetings, and about what

The fourth item is specific to FDEs who use AI as a tool. If the customer's internal rules restrict the use of generative AI, the way work is done changes after the contract is signed. Ask to see the customer's policies in advance.

Suitability

A freelance FDE's work adds "getting work on your own" and "managing your own contracts" to the work of a salaried FDE. In my view, suitability splits along the following table.

SuitedNot suited
Has experience hearing requirements on-site at the customer, implementing them personally, and shipping to productionHas only ever done either implementation or requirements gathering, but not both
Can verbally agree with the customer on "what counts as done"Ends up expanding and taking on whatever is asked
Can manage money with periods of no projects in mindFeels strong anxiety without a steady monthly income
Can explain their own work within the bounds of confidentiality obligationsHas not turned their track record into a form that can be shown to others

If the left side of the table is not yet complete, I recommend going independent after gaining experience with 2-3 production projects as a salaried FDE. The path to becoming an FDE is summarized in How to Become an FDE.

Freelance FDEs from the Client's Perspective

From the perspective of the commissioning company, a freelance FDE is an option that lets you bring someone on-site only for the period you need. In exchange, relying on a single person means knowledge is lost when that person leaves. In my view, the client should decide at the start of the contract "whether the code, procedure documents, and evaluation data will remain in-house when the contract ends."

Whether you are the FDE or the client, I believe deciding in advance how the contract ends is the best way to keep FDE work from becoming permanent on-site staffing. The process and fees for FDE Consulting to work with a company are summarized in Services.
