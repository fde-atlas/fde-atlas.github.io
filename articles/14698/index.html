A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who works directly with a company's customers, working out what their real problem is and then building and deploying the system, these days often an AI system, that solves it inside their business. Unlike a solutions architect or a consultant, an FDE writes the production code and owns the outcome when it runs on the customer's real data.

What the job involves day to day, how it differs from neighbouring roles, who is hiring right now and how to get there: one page, built on the FDE Times hiring data and our guides.

What an FDE does day to day

The title says "forward deployed" because the engineer works where the customer is, not behind the product team. In practice the week looks like this:

  • The day runs on the customer's schedule. Customers report problems asynchronously, so strong FDEs triage whatever arrived overnight and then settle the day's priorities at the customer's standup, not only their own company's. How an FDE's day runs.
  • Finding the real problem. The request a client brings is a hypothesis: "automate PR review" can turn out to mean two overloaded reviewers. Discovery comes before code. How FDEs clarify requirements.
  • Wiring into messy systems. Enterprise AI pilots often stall because data sits in many separate systems. Inventorying, mapping and reconciling the customer's data is the core of the job, not a chore before it. Customer data onboarding.
  • Owning production. There is no handoff to an implementation team: if the customer's pipeline breaks at 2am, the FDE is the one who fixes it. FDE vs software engineer.
  • Being on site. The usual range is 25–50% of time at the customer: about 25% at Palantir, up to 50% at OpenAI and Istari Digital. Why most FDE roles cannot be done from home.
  • Feeding the product. A patch written at one customer can die with the project or become a feature for everyone; which one happens depends on how the FDE records it and hands it back. From one customer's patch to a shared feature.

FDE vs software engineer, solutions architect and consultant

The four roles overlap, and many job posts blur them. The cleanest test is who writes the final code and who answers when production breaks.

Frequently asked questions

Is Forward Deployed Engineer a good career?

Demand is strong right now: FDE Times counts 838 open FDE roles at 169 companies, 256 of them posted in the last 30 days. In US posts that state pay, the median base salary is $200k (salary data). The costs are travel, often 25–50% of time at customers, and owning a customer's production system when it breaks. Later moves include principal FDE, product manager, engineering manager and founder (career paths after FDE).

Do Forward Deployed Engineers code?

Yes. Coding is the entry bar, with Python and full-stack work the most common requirements (what recruiters look for). Writing production code is also what separates an FDE from a solutions architect, who delivers architecture documents and often stops at a proof of concept (FDE vs solutions architect).

How is an FDE different from a solutions engineer?

Solutions engineers and solutions architects mostly work before the sale as technical advisers and often stop at a demo or proof of concept. An FDE comes in after the sale, works deep in rollout and operations, and owns the production code at the customer. Moving from solutions engineer to FDE means moving from demo code to production code and taking responsibility for how the deployment turns out (from solutions engineer to FDE).

What companies hire Forward Deployed Engineers?

Right now the most open FDE roles are at Palantir (95), Databricks (94), Amazon (AWS) (68), OpenAI (39), Salesforce (21), ByteDance (20), Rogo (19), Scale AI (12), out of 169 companies in the FDE Hiring Tracker, which is updated from official careers pages.

How do I become a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Show that you have shipped working systems in messy, real environments: FDE job posts ask what you have shipped, not for AI certificates (what FDE job posts ask for). Recruiters screen on coding and rank on customer communication; the FDE Times roadmap covers the eight stages from foundations to career. New-grad FDE roles exist but the door is narrow; for most engineers in Vietnam, building 12–18 months of deployment experience before switching is the more realistic path (new-grad FDE roles).

Is Forward Deployed Engineer a remote job?

Mostly not. Only 24% of the 838 open roles FDE Times tracks are open to remote work. The job is built around working alongside customers, typically 25–50% of time on site, and some air-gapped systems cannot be reached remotely (why FDEs travel). Even a listing labelled "Remote" can require on-site work and travel (reading the "Remote" label).

How much does a Forward Deployed Engineer make?

In 168 US FDE job posts that state a range, the median base salary is $200k, and the typical posted range is $155k–$238k. That is base pay only; equity and bonus are not included (FDE salary data).

What does FDE stand for, and where did the role come from?

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer. The model is most closely associated with Palantir, which splits deployment between two roles: Echo, an analyst who knows the business domain, and Delta, an engineer who builds prototypes fast (Delta and Echo). Companies now use several names for similar work (six companies, six titles).
