New grad forward deployed engineer: who hires, what they ask

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 18 min read

Summary

This article maps which employers hire new graduates into forward deployed engineer roles and what those postings require. Palantir and C3 AI run dedicated new-grad FDE postings for December 2026 or 2027 graduates, while Databricks' AI FDE posting explicitly excludes new grads, though one candidate reported an entry-level L3 FDE offer there. It catalogs junior and early-career routes (Accenture Federal, EY Prague, LangChain, Salesforce's AI Builder program), lists common requirements such as graduation windows, coding fundamentals, AI fluency and 25–50% travel, and suggests ways to demonstrate customer-facing ability through teaching, clubs or side projects.

Key points

  • Palantir and C3 AI post FDE roles specifically for December 2026 or 2027 graduates.
  • Databricks' AI FDE posting excludes new grads, but an L3 FDE offer was reported.
  • LangChain's 'early career' roles still require 1–3 years of experience and 40% travel.
  • Common requirements: graduation windows, coding fundamentals, AI tooling, 25–50% travel.
  • Ramp and ElevenLabs accept teaching or club experience as evidence of customer skill.

How to show customer skill without a job history

Start with what hiring teams have said in public:

  • Leo Mehr, Ramp’s Director of Engineering, wrote on Ramp’s engineering blog in August 2025 that previous customer-facing experience “helps, it’s actually not necessary”, and that people who have been instructors or teaching assistants, and enjoyed it, have the ability and interest to do this well. Source 23Forward Deployed Engineering (Leo Mehr, Director, Engineering)PublisherRamp Builders (engineering blog)Source typecompany blog
  • On a June 2026 episode of dearCC, he named a public technical project combined with people-facing work, such as a cashier or sales job, as a great example of someone who might be good for FDE. Source 24What is a Forward Deployed Engineer? (FDE Explained) feat. Leo Mehr of Ramp (YouTube auto-generated English captions)PublisherdearCC (Clara Shih), YouTubeSource typerecorded talk or interview
  • ElevenLabs’ FDE postings say it is fine if you only worked with customers “in student clubs or side projects.” Source 25Forward Deployed Engineer - Software Engineer - North America @ ElevenLabsPublisherElevenLabs (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job posting

So the evidence can come from anywhere people depended on you. What follows is our method for presenting it; no employer has said it uses this frame.

Proof one: a people-facing story told as customer work

Pick the time you served someone who was not an engineer: tutoring, a teaching assistant job, a campus club’s tools, a retail shift, a family business. Tell it in four beats:

  • Who you served, by role: “the students in my section”, “the club treasurer”.
  • What they needed, in their words, and what they actually needed once you asked.
  • What you built or changed for them, and one tradeoff you made.
  • What happened, with a measured result if you have one.

Here is the difference on a résumé. These lines are invented to show the shape:

  • Before: “Teaching assistant for Data Structures. Strong communication skills.”
  • After: “Teaching assistant for Data Structures. Found that most office-hours questions came from one confusing autograder message, rewrote the messages, and wrote a short guide the instructor adopted for the course.”

The second line shows discovery, a fix, and adoption. Add your real number where you have one, then compare your spoken version with the model answer to the customer-facing experience question. If this is your main gap, our post on showing customer skill with no customer job goes deeper.

Proof two: a project with a named user

A project someone outside your team used proves you can find out what a person needs and ship it. Build something small for a real user: the club treasurer’s dues tracker, a lab’s data-cleaning script, a tool for a local shop. Log what they asked for, what you built first, and what you changed after they used it; that log is your interview material. Our post on the portfolio project that gets FDE interviews walks through the build.

Proof three: the answer to “why FDE?”

Mehr also said, on the same dearCC episode, that software engineers who say they want to be closer to customers or users are a great predictor of FDE fit. Source 24What is a Forward Deployed Engineer? (FDE Explained) feat. Leo Mehr of Ramp (YouTube auto-generated English captions)PublisherdearCC (Clara Shih), YouTubeSource typerecorded talk or interview Your answer should show that pull with evidence, not claim it. Something like:

“In my TA job I liked the moment a student’s problem stopped being vague more than I liked writing the fix. I want a job where that’s the work: sit with the person, find the real problem, and ship.”

Mistakes that sink new-grad applications

  • Adjectives instead of stories. “Excellent communicator” with no example tells a reviewer nothing.
  • Calling a group project customer work. If no one outside the team used it, say what it taught you and do not stretch it.
  • Hiding the non-technical job. A cashier or sales job is the kind of people-facing work Mehr named. Source 24What is a Forward Deployed Engineer? (FDE Explained) feat. “I believed our club’s sign-up form was failing because it was too long. When I read the drop-off data, most people quit at the payment step, not the questions. I moved payment to after sign-up, and completed sign-ups rose that term. Now I check where people leave before I decide why.”

The first five minutes, played through

The other Blind candidate, a new grad who did not name the role, reported 8,000 London taxi records with 8 columns and a request for a first-cut solution for drivers that could be built and deployed in a week. Source 27Palantir Learning & Decomposition Interview (Blind)PublisherBlindSource typecandidate report on BlindSource 28Update: interview experience - Palantir new grad FDSE interview (Blind)PublisherBlindSource typecandidate report on Blind Here is how our method would open it. This is our approach, not a script any interviewer uses.

  • Clarify the decision. The user is given: drivers. So ask about their day. “Which decision in a driver’s shift are we improving: where to wait, when to stop, or which fares to take?”
  • Check the columns. “Do we have pickup time, pickup location and fare? Is the data clean enough to rank areas, or are there gaps I should know about?”
  • Propose a one-week first cut. “A ‘best zone to wait in for the next hour’ lookup, built from historical pickups by zone and hour. The metric is time to next fare.”
  • Say what you are not building yet. “No live demand model, no routing, no app redesign. If the lookup cuts waiting time, those come next.”

That opening accepts the given user, checks the data first, and scopes to the week. Our free lesson on the open-ended round and the method on one page sets out the whole sequence, and the fitness-app data question gives you another prompt to run it on.

A plan for the weeks before you apply

Before you apply

  • List every new-grad, junior and early-career posting you could apply to, with its graduation window, travel line and citizenship line.
  • Drop the ones that exclude you in writing, and note the ones that ask for experience you can argue around.
  • Write your people-facing story in the four beats above, and say it aloud until it takes about a minute.
  • Build one small thing for a named user outside your team, and keep the log.
  • Rewrite your résumé bullets from adjectives into actions, using the FDE resume guide.
  • Keep up coding practice on data structures and algorithms, since the postings still ask for them.
  • Draft your “why FDE” answer and one “changed my mind” story.
  • Practice one open-ended problem aloud against someone who answers back.
