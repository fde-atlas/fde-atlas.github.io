We would put the most preparation time on the outcomes row, because it is the hardest one to back with evidence. Tying usage to faster iteration assumes somebody measured iteration speed before the rollout, and that baseline often does not exist. An honest answer names a proxy and says what the proxy misses. That holds up better than a clean number nobody in the room can rebuild.

The posting tells you which skills to build, and any interview format only samples them. A screen, a panel, a live exercise and a working session all draw on the same skills, so prepare the capability instead of a script.

This posting puts hands-on enablement in the Solutions Architect's charter. Cursor has also posted an AI Adoption Engineer role, since delisted, whose posting described designing and running hackathons and Customer Developer Days for customers. At a smaller vendor, one person might do both kinds of work. Say which kind you have done, since a team enablement session and a company-wide event sound the same on a CV.

What interview questions should I expect?

Each question below maps to a line in the posting. They are question shapes the responsibilities imply, not questions Cursor is known to ask. Answer each with a specific story or a live walk-through instead of a generic framework, and rehearse the gap between the strong and weak answers.

  • "Roll Cursor out to a 500-developer org that uses it in pockets today. Where do you start?" Strong answers sequence teams by readiness and name an adoption signal to track; weak answers list features and skip how usage spreads. (expansion, rollout design)
  • "A platform team calls an AI coding tool a governance and security risk. How do you run that architecture review?" Strong answers treat governance as a design problem with concrete controls; weak answers reassure without a mechanism. (trusted technical partner, trust boundaries)
  • "A finance team wants an agent to take over part of a monthly process. How do you scope the Grok BotAn early AI teammate product from SpaceXAI and Cursor that works across apps on a persistent cloud computer and asks for approval on selected actions; launch materials describe it as beta, but Cursor says Beta Services terms do not apply. Press Enter for the full definition. workflow, and where does a person approve the output?" Strong answers start from the real job friction and keep human judgment where an error costs most; weak answers automate the whole process at once. (Grok Bot journey, workflow design)
  • "How would you measure whether an enterprise adoption is working, and what tells you it's slowing?" Strong answers tie usage to an outcome like faster iteration; weak answers stop at seat counts. (outcomes, recovery plans)
  • "Explain how Cursor fits a customer's CI pipeline and code review to a skeptical staff engineer." Strong answers stay concrete and admit limits; weak answers drift into a pitch. (technical depth, communication)
  • "Tell me about a time you drove a workflow change across an engineering team that resisted it." Strong answers show the friction and how you earned adoption; weak answers claim easy wins. (ownership, adoption)
  • "A strategic account's usage dropped last quarter, and a competing tool is gaining ground. Walk me through your recovery plan." Strong answers diagnose the cause before acting; weak answers jump straight to another demo. (outcomes, competitive risk)

The application form asks a version of the resistance question in writing: "Tell us about a time you convinced a technical audience to adopt a new solution or tool. What was the resistance and how did you handle it?" Write that answer about a real rollout, and be ready to defend it in an interview.

Take the first question. Sequencing teams by readiness sounds weaker than sequencing by importance, so it is the harder answer to defend in an interview. The team an executive sponsor cares about most is often the team with the least slack in its quarter. Start there and the pilot competes with a release date and loses. Ask about each team's next ship date before you ask for volunteers.

The governance question wants a mechanism the customer's architects can keep using after you leave. The posting asks you to set up a governance and security framework that lets leadership adopt the platform top down. The practice track here builds that review around a short written recommendation that stakeholders can forward to their CISOChief Information Security Officer. The executive who owns security; usually the hardest and most important person to win over. Press Enter for the full definition., plus a risk register that gives each risk an owner. If a review goes badly, check first whether the security team sat in it or was briefed afterward.

Bring proof: a rollout you ran, a metric you moved, a workflow you designed or a demo you can give live. The posting asks for extreme ownership and measurable impact, so show the work.

Six prior titles appear in the fit list, from Solutions Architect to integration consultant, and they cover a wide spread of technical depth. So expect the interview to test that depth directly rather than infer it from your title. What the six share, in our reading, is accountability for a customer's outcome. Pick the account where the outcome was yours to answer for, and prepare what you would change if you ran it again.

Several fit lines are new since our first read, among them workflows beyond the IDE, and a working grasp of security and compliance, or a clear path to one. If your experience is all developer tooling, prepare one business process you automated, and be clear about where your security knowledge stops.

The fit list also asks for a passion for how AI will reshape software engineering and knowledge work. Anyone can claim that, so bring an actual opinion, including one thing you think current tools still get wrong.

Frequently asked questions

Does Cursor publish its Solutions Architect interview questions or stages?

No. The posting describes the role's responsibilities and the profile it wants, but no stages, question list or round counts. Its application form has one open-ended question: a time you convinced a technical audience to adopt a new solution or tool, and how you handled the resistance. Prepare the skills the posting implies rather than a rumored loop.

Is the Cursor Solutions Architect role remote?

The posting lists the role as remote and full-time in Customer Success, and it asks for a willingness to travel to customers as needed. Confirm the location on the posting you apply to, since Cursor updates roles as it hires.

Is this a pre-sale or post-sale role?

The posting uses neither term. It places the role in Customer Success, working with strategic customers on rollouts, expansion and recovery plans alongside AI Deployment Managers, whose own posting has them "own the post-sale journey for a portfolio of enterprise customers." In our reading, that makes it post-sale work.

How is Solutions Architect different from AI Deployment Manager at Cursor?

The two roles are paired. The Solutions Architect posting has SAs partner with AI Deployment Managers to own the technical track across architecture, workflow redesign and enterprise security. The AI Deployment Manager posting gives that role "a book of business with our top enterprise customers" and says success is measured by "customer retention, product adoption, and revenue expansion."

What's the best way to practice for it?

Use Cursor daily on real work, learn the controls a security team will ask about, and rehearse both an enablement session and an executive value conversation. The free Solutions Architect practice track here runs those reps and ends in a mock loop, though it does not cover Grok Bot.
