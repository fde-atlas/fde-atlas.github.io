Where to Consult About Generative AI | How to Choose Between Public Help Desks and Private Support Companies

Original title: 生成AIの相談先｜公的な窓口と民間の支援会社の使い分け

FDE Consulting · 2026-11-05 · 8 min read

Summary

This Japanese-language article helps small businesses decide where to seek advice on generative AI. Citing a Tokyo Chamber of Commerce survey (June 2026), it notes 83.9% of surveyed companies use generative AI, yet only 7.7% have guidelines, with security cited as the top challenge (46.7%). The author maps topics to public, free consultation channels—yorozu support centers, the Personal Information Protection Commission, the Agency for Cultural Affairs, and IPA—and explains their limits. Custom design, integration, and implementation should go to private firms, including FDEs. The article recommends preparing a one-page summary of tools, tasks, data, and questions before consulting, and comparing vendors by deliverables rather than price.

Key points

  • 83.9% of surveyed firms use generative AI; only 7.7% have guidelines.
  • Public windows handle legal interpretation but not custom implementation.
  • Privacy, copyright, and security issues each have dedicated public contacts.
  • Building and integrating systems belongs to private firms or FDEs.

When looking for someone to consult about generative AI, the first thing people struggle with is "who should I ask." According to the "Survey on Digital Shift and DX among Small and Medium Enterprises" published by the Tokyo Chamber of Commerce and Industry on June 17, 2026, 83.9% of companies at digital shift/DX levels 2 through 4 reported using generative AI. On the other hand, only 7.7% of companies have established guidelines for generative AI, and among challenges, "ensuring security" was the most common at 46.7%. I read these numbers as indicating a state where companies have started using generative AI, but their rules and safeguards have not caught up.

Who you should consult about generative AI depends on the content of your question. The right person to ask differs depending on whether it concerns handling personal information, copyright, or which business processes to use it in. Below, I organize this by separating what can be consulted about for free at public offices and what should be entrusted to private support companies. The names, phone numbers, and reception hours of the offices were confirmed on each organization's official website on November 5, 2026, and may change.

Consultation Destinations by Type of Question

First, here is an overview of the types of questions and the corresponding consultation destinations. The table is my own organization of the information.

Type of questionPublic officeWhen relying on the private sector
Which business processes to use it in, organizing operationsYorozu Support Centers, Productivity Improvement Support CentersHave someone inventory your internal operations and decide which processes to use it in and in what order
Whether personal information can be enteredPersonal Information Protection Commission consultation hotlineHave your retained lawyer check it in the context of your company's contracts and operations
Generative AI and copyrightAgency for Cultural Affairs legal consultation officeAsk a lawyer knowledgeable in intellectual property to handle it as an individual case
Damage from viruses or unauthorized accessIPA Information Security Consultation DeskAsk a security company to investigate and take countermeasures
Building internal rules and frameworks(No office available. Read public guidelines)Create regulations together with an external expert
Connecting and running it with internal systems(No office available)Ask a development company or engineers to handle everything from design to operation

Public offices are strong at interpreting regulations and sorting out entry points. Once you reach the stage of building something tailored to your own operations, you fall outside the scope of these offices. Knowing where this line lies reduces the effort of searching for someone to consult.

What Can Be Consulted at Public Offices

Yorozu Support Centers and Productivity Improvement Support Centers

For small and medium-sized enterprises and small businesses, the first place to consult is the Yorozu Support Center. These are business consultation offices established by the national government in all 47 prefectures, offering free consultations on all kinds of management issues as many times as needed. In addition to SMEs and small businesses, eligible parties include NPO corporations and those planning to start a business.

From April 2026, Productivity Improvement Support Centers were established within the Yorozu Support Centers. According to a Mirasapo plus article based on an interview with a METI official, experts provide hands-on support with visualizing operations, standardizing work and improving processes, and considering the introduction of digital tools and labor-saving equipment. Experts are dispatched free of charge multiple times, and include personnel in the fields of DX and AI utilization. The consultation window is the Yorozu Support Center.

In my view, these centers are best used at the stage before deciding "which business processes to use generative AI in." The order of visualizing operations first and then choosing tools remains the same when introducing AI. The contact points and phone numbers for each prefecture are listed on the Yorozu Support Center locations page.

Personal Information Protection Commission Consultation Hotline

Questions about whether customer information can be entered into generative AI become a matter of interpreting the Act on the Protection of Personal Information. The Personal Information Protection Commission's Personal Information Protection Act Consultation Hotline can be reached at 03-6457-9849, and accepts calls from 9:30 to 17:30 excluding weekends, holidays, and the year-end/New Year period. It handles complaints regarding the handling of personal information by businesses and administrative organs, as well as questions about the Personal Information Protection Act and related systems. Basic questions can also be asked via the 24-hour PPC Question Chat.

The Personal Information Protection Commission published "Cautionary Notes on the Use of Generative AI Services" on June 2, 2023. Reading this before calling will help you narrow down what to ask. I have summarized the boundaries of what information can be entered in Generative AI and Personal Information.

Agency for Cultural Affairs Legal Consultation Office

Regarding the copyright of text and images created with generative AI, there is the Agency for Cultural Affairs' Legal Consultation Office for Artistic and Cultural Activities. "Matters concerning AI and copyright" is included among the topics covered, and the office is handled by Lawyer IP Net, which has been commissioned by the Agency for Cultural Affairs. It is not an office exclusively for artists. AI users other than artists, such as businesses engaged in AI development or service provision, can also consult about matters related to the Agency for Cultural Affairs' guidelines and issues related to their own activities or contracts.

Consultations are submitted via the consultation form on the Agency for Cultural Affairs' website, and responses arrive by email within 10 days in principle, excluding weekends and holidays. However, exercising rights such as negotiations or litigation with the other party, and the investigations or evidence preservation required for such actions, are out of scope. I have written about what should be decided in internal rules in Generative AI and Copyright.

IPA Information Security Consultation Desk

The IPA (Information-technology Promotion Agency) Information Security Consultation Desk is an office that provides advice on technical consultations regarding general information security (mainly viruses and unauthorized access). The phone number is 03-5978-7509, and it accepts calls from 10:00 to 17:00 excluding weekends, holidays, and the year-end/New Year period. Consultations are free, but the caller bears the phone charges. It does not accept consultations on legal interpretations or on contracts and payment methods. Rather than for how to use generative AI itself, it is best thought of as an office to use when you have suffered damage from suspicious emails or apps impersonating AI services.

What to Entrust to Private Support Companies

What public offices do not handle is design tailored to your own operations and the actual building and running of systems. From here on, you rely on the private sector. Who you ask depends on what you want done.

What you wantExamples of providersArticle to read first
Someone to consult with a few times a monthExperts under retainer contractsWhat Is an AI Advisor
Someone to create policies and plansConsulting firmsHow to Choose an AI Consultant
Someone to work on-site and help until adoption takes holdHands-on support companiesWhat Is Hands-on AI Support
Someone to build it connected to internal systemsDevelopment companies, FDEsHow to Choose an FDE Support Company

In my view, you do not need to settle on a single provider at the initial consultation stage. When the company that puts the policy on paper and the company that builds and runs the system are different, discrepancies tend to arise between planning and implementation. If there is a prospect of entrusting the building as well, check at the initial consultation stage whether the provider can handle implementation too. The role of working on-site and building both the operations and the systems is filled by a job type called FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer). I have written about what this role entails in What Is an FDE.

When comparing quotes, don't just compare the amounts; compare side by side what will actually be delivered as deliverables (a list of tasks, internal rules, a working system).

What to prepare before consulting

Whether you consult a public office or a private firm, the quality of the consultation depends on the materials you bring. I believe it is best to consolidate the following four items onto a single sheet before consulting.

What to prepareContentsWhy it is needed
Tools in useThe generative AI services employees are using, and whether they are personal accounts or company contractsAnswers to questions about personal information and security vary depending on the contract structure
Tasks you want to use AI forThe name of the task, the number of people in charge, and the monthly volumeConsultations on organizing tasks cannot proceed without knowing the volume
Data to be inputWhether customer information, confidential documents, or other companies' copyrighted material will be includedThis serves as a clue for choosing where to consult
What you want to decide"Whether it is okay to use it," "how far you can go with inputting data," and "who should build it"If it is unclear what answers you want, you will get generic responses

The procedure for checking in advance which AI is being used in-house is described in Shadow AI Inventory. The order for small and medium-sized enterprises to start small is summarized in AI Adoption for SMEs.

The order for combining public offices and private firms

In my view, using them in the following order minimizes waste.

  • Consolidate the tools used in-house and the tasks you want to use AI for onto a single sheet in the form of the table above
  • Among personal information, copyright, and task organization, confirm any sticking points with a public office
  • Entrust the design tailored to your company's tasks and the building and operation of the system to a private firm

Public offices are free and provide neutral answers on the interpretation of regulations. However, they do not get deeply involved in building systems for your specific tasks. Private firms cost money, but they do the hands-on work. Since their roles differ, it is better not to try to rely on just one of them.
