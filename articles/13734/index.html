What Is Forward-Deployed Engineering?

IDC · 2026-11-10 · 4 min read

Insights from IDC’s The Role of Forward Deployed Engineers in Enterprise Intelligence Services

Forward-deployed engineering is a delivery model in which provider-employed, customer-facing technical specialists work as an extension of client teams to design, build, integrate and deploy AI solutions in production.

What is Forward-Deployed Engineering?

Forward-deployed engineering places provider-employed engineers inside client teams to design, build, customize, integrate and deploy solutions in production. According to IDC, the model originated at Palantir as a problem-solving methodology in which engineers work backward from a defined business outcome, and it has become a marketwide label as providers respond to the gap between AI pilots and production results. This matters because the label alone no longer tells a buyer what they are getting.

According to IDC’s The Role of Forward Deployed Engineers in Enterprise Intelligence Services (IDC #US54332126, June 2026), the FDE label “has outrun a single working definition.” IDC’s market note The Forward-Deployed Engineer Race (IDC #US54918426, October 2026) reports that AWS, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Cloud and Salesforce each committed capital within one year to embed engineers with customers.

Forward-Deployed Engineering Details

What it is. A forward-deployed engineer (FDE) is a provider-employed, customer-facing technical specialist who works as an extension of the client team. Palantir developed the approach as a methodology rather than a service category, and IDC notes that the designation reflects how engineers approach work, not where they sit during an engagement. The term now spans vendors, systems integrators and new deployment companies.

How it works. IDC identifies three non-interchangeable forms: vendor FDEs employed and funded by the vendor, GSI FDEs who are typically retrained systems integrator staff, and deployment company FDEs working in a separate venture partly financed by outside investors. Vendors build, buy or fund the capacity, and each route trades speed against cost differently. All three lean on the same few global systems integrators.

Why it matters. FDEs are the industry’s attempt to close the execution gap between AI pilots and production. IDC finds that client operating-model readiness, not provider capability, is the binding constraint on FDE value. Buyers who understand their own skill needs first can source help that fits, whatever the label.

Where did forward-deployed engineering come from?

IDC traces the model to Palantir, which developed it as a problem-solving methodology rather than a service category. Engineers work backward from a defined business outcome to build software against the actual problem.

Supporting points

  • Palantir is explicit that the FDE designation reflects how its engineers work, not where they sit.
  • IDC describes the original model as deploying data analytics platforms inside intelligence and defense agencies.
  • IDC says the term spread across the whole market in under two years.

Why are AI vendors investing in FDEs now?

IDC links the investment to a production gap that better models and bigger marketing budgets have not closed. Enterprises have stopped paying for pilots and are demanding delivery.

Supporting points

  • Only 15% of organizations report that more than 75% of their AI projects have delivered measurable outcomes.
  • IDC’s May 2026 benchmark of about 1,900 organizations found 50% remain Opportunistic in AI maturity.
  • In April 2026 Salesforce launched an FDE Partner Network, and AWS announced a $1 billion FDE organization on June 30.

What forms does forward-deployed engineering take?

IDC separates vendor FDEs, GSI FDEs and deployment company FDEs. None is definitively better, and each handles customer feedback and cost differently.

Supporting points

  • Vendor FDEs (AWS, Microsoft) send customer feedback directly to the company but are costly to hire.
  • GSI FDEs (Accenture, Deloitte, PwC) offer cross-platform scale, though feedback passes through the GSI’s delivery methodology.
  • Deployment company FDEs (OpenAI’s DeployCo, Anthropic’s Ode) provide immediate scale, but feedback must be deliberately routed to the lab.

How should buyers evaluate an engagement that includes FDEs?

IDC says the FDE label is now insufficient as a procurement signal. What matters is the underlying delivery model, the scope of outcome accountability and where the boundaries of the role are drawn.

Supporting points

  • Two firms in IDC’s interviews named “FDE washing,” the practice of applying the term to roles that do not influence product development or drive measurable outcomes.
  • IDC finds client operating-model readiness, not provider capability, is the main constraint on FDE value.
  • In the past 12 months, 45% of organizations worked with service-provider FDEs, 30% with software or platform-provider FDEs and 21% with both.
