AI tools allowed in interviews? FDE rules by company

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 16 min read

Summary

This article compiles each employer's official rules on AI tool use in interviews, stage by stage, as of September 2026. Meta expects candidates for select roles to use the built-in CoderPad assistant, and Canva requires AI in engineering technical interviews; Notion, Sierra, GitLab, Salesforce and Palantir allow or encourage AI in specific rounds. By contrast, Google, Snowflake, Microsoft, IBM, Adobe, Accenture and Deloitte ban AI by default, and Anthropic permits it only for first-draft applications, not live interviews. The author stresses that the right question is which tools, in which round, and per what source, since rules change quickly and press reports about internal documents are not official policy.

Key points

  • Meta and Canva explicitly expect AI use in some technical interviews.
  • Google, Snowflake, Microsoft and others ban AI by default in interviews.
  • Anthropic allows AI for application drafts but not live interviews.
  • Rules differ by stage within the same company, so check each round.
  • Press reports on internal documents are not official policy; trust employer pages.

The part people miss is the stage. One company can let an AI polish your application, then rule it out by default on the take-home and again in the live round. Source 5Guidance on Candidates' AI UsagePublisherAnthropicSource typecompany website So the useful question is never “does this company allow AI?” It is “which tools, in which round, and who said so?”

Three things to know before the tables:

  • Most of these rules are company-wide. They cover every candidate, not only forward deployed engineers, and they still apply to you. Where a rule sits on a specific role or posting, the table names it.
  • Rules change. OpenAI’s sentence about AI tools is not in a July 2026 archived copy of its interview guide and is in an August 2026 copy, so it was added in between.   • A rule is only as good as its source. An employer’s own page is a rule. A news story about an internal document is a report. This post keeps the two apart.

Employers that expect or allow AI in some rounds

Two say they expect it (Meta for select roles, Canva for engineering); the rest allow it in a named round.

Meta. Its hiring-process page says select roles now include an authorized AI assistant within CoderPad during technical interviews, and that candidates are expected to use it. Source 1Meta Jobs Hiring ProcessPublisherMetaSource typecompany hiring page The assistant includes Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Meta’s models, and only the tools inside the interview environment may be used. Source 1Meta Jobs Hiring ProcessPublisherMetaSource typecompany hiring page “Expected” does not mean “any tool you like”: your own Cursor window is still an outside tool.

Canva. Its engineering blog said in June 2025 that Canva expects Backend, Machine Learning and Frontend engineering candidates to use AI tools such as Copilot, Cursor and Claude in its technical interviews. Source 3Yes, You Can Use AI in Our Interviews. In fact, we insistPublisherCanvaSource typecompany blog That names engineering tracks, not an role.

Notion. Its public AI Applications Engineer interview guide, for the role on this list closest to FDE, has one AI-enabled coding interview where AI coding tools are “available and strongly encouraged”, and asks you not to use tools like ChatGPT or GitHub Copilot to write code in the others. Source 11AI Applications Engineer InterviewsPublisherNotion (notion.notion.site)Source typecompany website That is two rules inside one loop.

Sierra, GitLab and Salesforce. Sierra’s engineering onsite has a Build stage done with the AI tooling of your choice. Source 12The AI-native interview (Vijay Iyengar, Arya Asemanfar, Angie Wang)PublisherSierraSource typecompany blog GitLab welcomes transparent AI use in technical assessments and asks you to disclose which tools you used, and a Salesforce careers post from May 2026 says technical candidates may complete an assessment demonstrating how they use AI. Source 13Using AI in the interview processPublisherGitLabSource typecompany hiring pageSource 14Ask a Recruiter: How Do I Get AI-Ready Without a Technical Background?PublisherSalesforceSource typecompany blog Allowed is not required: skipping AI in these rounds breaks no rule. Sierra’s AI-native interview walks through that format.

A round about AI, not necessarily with it

Glean’s postings, the Founding Forward Deployed Engineer posting included, describe a brief AI-focused exercise or discussion, and say you may reference any AI tools you use without prior Glean experience. Source 21Founding Forward Deployed EngineerPublisherGlean (Greenhouse job board)Source typecompany job posting Be ready to talk through the tools you use, and ask whether you will use one live.

What “using AI well” looks like out loud

GitLab expects you to explain your prompts and technical decisions, and Sierra digs into how you used AI along the way. Source 12The AI-native interview (Vijay Iyengar, Arya Asemanfar, Angie Wang)PublisherSierraSource typecompany blogSource 13Using AI in the interview processPublisherGitLabSource typecompany hiring page So the tool’s output is only half of what they see; your narration is the other half.

Here is our suggested narration for a round where you must parse a log file and retry failed uploads. It is our method, not a script any interviewer uses:

  • “I’ll ask it for the parser only. I’ll write the retry logic myself, because that’s where the judgment is.”
  • The prompt: “Write a Python function that reads this log format and returns one dict per line. Here are three sample lines.”
  • “Before I keep this, I’ll run it on the empty-file case.”
  • “It drops the header row, so I’m fixing that myself.”
  • “Kept: the regex. Rejected: its error handling, because it swallows exceptions.”

Each line says what you asked for, how you checked it and what you decided. How to pass an AI-assisted coding interview goes deeper on each step, and the question on extending unfamiliar code with an AI assistant is a round-shaped drill for it.

“Unless we tell you otherwise” means they tell you

The exception belongs to the employer. Permission comes from the interviewer, the prep materials or the recruiter in writing, never from your own reading of the round. A round that “feels practical” is not an invitation.

Two cases where this bites:

  • An AI company is not an AI-allowed company. Anthropic’s live interviews are no-AI by default. Source 5Guidance on Candidates' AI UsagePublisherAnthropicSource typecompany website
  • Two dates, one company. Salesforce’s August 2025 tips say no AI in the interview unless prompted, and its May 2026 post says technical candidates may do an assessment that shows how they use AI. Source 14Ask a Recruiter: How Do I Get AI-Ready Without a Technical Background?PublisherSalesforceSource typecompany blogSource 174 Actionable Tips for a Successful Salesforce InterviewPublisherSalesforce Careers BlogSource typecompany blog Both can be true, because they cover different stages. Treat the newer post as an exception for that stage, not a new default.

If you are unsure mid-round, ask. It takes a moment and shows you read the rules:

“Before I start: which tools can I use in this round? Is it the editor only, or can I use the assistant?”

Recording, transcription and note-taking tools

This rule is easy to break without noticing if a meeting assistant joins your calls automatically.

  • Okta asks you not to use AI or any other technology to record, transcribe or take notes during interviews and technical screening without its explicit written consent. Source 20Okta Interview GuidePublisherOktaSource typecompany hiring page
  • Adobe covers “AI or recording tools” in the same sentence on its FDE postings. Source 19Senior Forward Deployed EngineerPublisherAdobe (Workday careers site)Source typecompany job posting
  • Salesforce asks you not to record or transcribe your interviews, regardless of AI features. Source 174 Actionable Tips for a Successful Salesforce InterviewPublisherSalesforce Careers BlogSource typecompany blog

The reverse also happens. In September 2026, 106 of the 110 open Palantir Forward Deployed and application forms asked about an AI notetaker, saying Palantir may use one to transcribe interviews and that opting in or out will not affect your candidacy. Source 27Apply: Forward Deployed Software Engineer (New York, NY) - Palantir on LeverPublisherPalantir Technologies (Lever)Source typecompany job posting That is Palantir’s tool, with your consent. It is not permission to run yours.

Before every video interview

  • In your notetaker’s own settings, turn off auto-join or disconnect it from your calendar for the interview, and switch off any AI summary your meeting app starts for you.
  • Close any browser tab with a chatbot in it.
  • Turn off inline completions in your editor if the round is no-AI.
  • Keep the written reply that says which tools you may use.

What to do when nothing is published

Some FDE employers publish nothing we could find on AI in interviews. We found no published Palantir rule for its live interviews, for example, though its Deployment Strategist Build to Apply postings for US Government say LLMs, AI and other tools are “permitted and encouraged” while you build the project you submit (how Build to Apply works). Source 15Deployment Strategist, Build to Apply - US GovernmentPublisherPalantir Technologies (Lever)Source typecompany job posting Silence is not permission. OpenAI’s own guide gives the right move: its preparation materials explain what is allowed, and if you are unsure, ask your recruiter before the interview. Source 10OpenAI interview guide (Wayback Machine capture)PublisherOpenAI (archived by Internet Archive)Source typearchived company pageSource 16Interview guidePublisherOpenAISource typecompany hiring page

Ask in writing, per stage, and keep the reply. Here is a note you can adapt:

Hi Priya, thanks for scheduling the next rounds. Before I prepare, could you confirm which tools I may use at each stage? Specifically: the take-home, the live coding round and the system design round. Is an AI assistant allowed in any of them, and if so, is it one you provide or my own? Thanks, Sam

Three reasons this works. It shows you read rules, which is part of the job. It gets you an answer per stage, not one blanket answer. And the reply is your record if an interviewer on the day assumes something different.

Then prepare for both versions of each round. If the answer is “no AI”, practice with your editor’s completions turned off; the free practice case puts you in front of an AI customer, a safe place to rehearse narrating your thinking out loud with no assistant open. If it is “AI allowed”, practice narrating your prompts and your checks. For the take-home, whether you can use ChatGPT or Claude on a take-home covers the published rules and how to disclose what you used.

Common mistakes, and the fix

Mistake Fix
Carrying one company’s rule into the next loop Read each company’s own page first
Assuming the take-home rule holds live Check the rule per stage
Treating a news story as the rule Go by the careers page
Letting your notetaker join Turn off auto-join in its settings
Hiding AI use where it was allowed Say what you used and how you checked it

An interviewer may also ask how you use AI tools at work, whatever the round’s rule. Have an honest answer ready, with one place you trust them and one place you don’t: how you use AI coding tools, and where you don’t trust them is that question with a model answer.
