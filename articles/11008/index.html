The "Division-of-Labor Trap" Seen Through by AI: Why the Forward Deployed Engineer Is Essentially a Salesperson?

Full text

Recently, I heard a very insightful point: today's Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is essentially the salesperson of the past.

In the business logic of the past, salespeople were supposed to understand technology, products, and delivery. It was only as the industry evolved that this role was artificially split apart. Now, the spread of AI is ruthlessly puncturing this "illusion of division of labor," forcing the sales role to return to its essence.

01 The Essence of the Term: Everything Is About Selling

We need to re-examine the definitions of "marketing" and "sales." Whether it's pre-sales consulting, in-sales follow-up, or after-sales maintenance, these actions are fundamentally one and the same, with a single core purpose: to sell the product and solve the customer's problems.

Why do we now need a versatile, technically savvy role like the FDE? A everyday analogy can illustrate this:

At an appliance store in a small town, you buy a fan. Before the purchase, the owner personally explains its features; after the purchase, if something goes wrong, you go straight to the owner to get it fixed. The owner would not—and could not—assign a "pre-sales attendant" and an "after-sales repairman" to handle your needs separately.

In scenarios like this, the business loop is highly unified. The owner is the purest form of salesperson, bearing full responsibility for explaining, closing the deal, and after-sales troubleshooting.

02 The Fragmented Sales Role and the "Laziness" Exposed by AI

If the role is essentially unified, why do companies today split sales into pre-sales, in-sales, and after-sales?

The core reason is organizational "laziness" in management. As system complexity increased, companies, in order to scale their teams quickly, chopped up a complete business process so that different people were only responsible for one link on the assembly line.

This model may have worked in an era of information asymmetry, but the arrival of AI has directly exposed this "laziness."

03 Next-Step Judgments and Recommendations

AI has greatly leveled information gaps and lowered the barriers to technical explanation and basic troubleshooting. When a fragmented division-of-labor system appears extremely bloated and inefficient, business logic will inevitably be forced to change.

The sales model of the future must, and can only, be carried by a single person taking on the complete role. That person is the technically savvy salesperson—or, in other words, a Forward Deployed Engineer with business acumen.

In the face of this trend, I offer two concrete recommendations:

  • For individual practitioners: If you are in a sales role, don't stop at "relationship maintenance"—you must start learning the underlying technical chain and deployment logic of the product; if you are a technical person or an FDE, you must develop end-to-end business thinking, oriented toward final sales delivery.
  • For organizations and managers: Stop being "lazy" with job definitions. Stop hiring cogs responsible only for one-way communication, and instead cultivate or recruit versatile talent capable of providing end-to-end service.

The essence of business has never changed. Returning to the essence of the term and letting technically savvy people take on full sales responsibility is the optimal answer to the impact of AI.
