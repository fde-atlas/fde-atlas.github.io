Palantir Forward Deployed Infrastructure Engineer Interview Experience (2026)

Aced (formerly Exponent) · 2026-11-05 · 1 min read

Summary

The author recounts interviewing at Palantir for a Forward Deployed Infrastructure Engineer role on the government side. The process included an engineer-led fit call with a tech lead, a LeetCode-style technical screen, and a three-hour virtual onsite with learning, decomposition, and Python debugging rounds. In the learning round, interviewers taught a Palantir product from scratch while progressively adding constraints, expecting the candidate to reason like a team member. Most rounds were highly verbal with rapid follow-up questions. Despite feeling the loop went well, the author was rejected a week later.

Key points

  • Role interviewed: Palantir Forward Deployed Infrastructure Engineer, government-facing.
  • First call was with a tech lead, favoring candidates with government experience.
  • Onsite was three hours: learning, decomposition, and Python debugging rounds.
  • Learning round taught a product from scratch with escalating constraints.
  • Author felt confident but was rejected one week later.

Forward Deployed Infrastructure Engineer Interview Experience

In the learning round they taught me one of their products from scratch, then kept adding constraints. They expected me to reason through it like I was already on the team.

Interview process

I had an intro fit call, then a LeetCode-style technical screen, then a 3-hour virtual onsite with three back-to-back rounds: learning, decomposition, and Python debugging. The biggest thing about the onsite was that two of the three rounds were super verbal, and they kept adding follow-up questions right after I answered. It was not the kind of loop where you just solve something once and move on. I walked out thinking I had done pretty well, including the last debugging round. Then I heard back a week later that I was rejected, which honestly confused me because the loop had felt pretty solid from my side.
