FDE vs Solutions engineer

Plank · 2026-09-11 · 3 min read

Summary

Plank compares forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) with solutions engineers. A solutions engineer supports the sale — demos, POCs, reference architectures — and is accountable up to signature; an FDE writes production code in the customer's environment after the deal closes and owns whether it delivers results. Drawing on Plank's census of 1,262 verified FDE openings across 555 companies, the article notes 35% of FDE job descriptions include pre-sales duties, and 1.8% fuse both titles outright (e.g., ServiceNow, MongoDB). Compensation differs too: solutions engineers get quota-tied variable pay, FDEs get base plus equity. The article ends with guidance on which role a company needs.

Key points

  • Solutions engineer proves it can work; FDE makes it work in production.
  • FDEs report into engineering, cover few customers deeply, and rarely carry quotas.
  • 35% of FDE postings in Plank's census mention pre-sales or solutions work.
  • Choose an FDE when deployments stall post-signature; a solutions engineer when deals stall on technical objections.
  • Solutions engineer is a common path into FDE, with production engineering depth as the gap.

A solutions engineer supports the sale — demos, proofs of concept, pre-sales architecture — and their accountability ends when the customer signs. A forward-deployed engineer's accountability begins there: they write production code inside the customer's environment after the deal closes, and they own whether it delivers the promised result. The shorthand is that a solutions engineer proves it can work and a forward-deployed engineer makes it work.

Side by side

Forward-deployed engineer Solutions engineer
Point in the deal cycle After signature, through production and adoption Before signature, through the technical win
Primary output Production code in the customer's systems Demos, POCs, reference architectures, RFP responses
Accountable for Whether the deployed system produces the promised outcome Whether the deal clears its technical bar
Codebase The customer's production repo, and the vendor's product Demo environments and sandboxes
Shape of attention Few customers, months to years deep Many prospects, weeks shallow
Usually reports into Engineering, or a dedicated delivery org Sales
Compensation shape Base plus equity, rarely a quota Base plus variable tied to quota

What a solutions engineer actually does

A solutions engineer (sometimes sales engineer or pre-sales engineer) is the technical half of a sales team. They scope the customer's problem, run demos and proofs of concept, design reference architectures, answer security and compliance questionnaires, and carry the technical argument through to signature.

Where they overlap

In practice the boundary is soft and getting softer. Plenty of forward-deployed engineers are pulled into late-stage deals as the technical closer, because the person who will own the build is the most credible voice on whether it can be built. And at smaller companies the same person often does both jobs under one title. The distinction that survives is accountability, not activity: if the role's success is measured at signature, it is a solutions engineer; if it is measured in production, it is an FDE.

What the hiring data shows

The overlap is measurable. Across the 904 machine-readable job descriptions in Plank's census, 35% mention pre-sales or solutions work as part of the FDE job — so a third of forward-deployed roles explicitly expect their engineers in the sales cycle. 20 postings (1.6% of the census) fuse the two titles outright, at companies including NODA, Aircall, MongoDB, ServiceNow. The titles are converging, and the postings show it.

From Plank’s first-party census of 1,276 verified open FDE roles across 547 companies, observed 2026-10-30. The census covers FDE-titled postings, so it describes the forward-deployed side of this comparison; it is not a survey of solutions engineer roles.

Which one do you need?

Forward-deployed engineer

  • The product genuinely does not work in a customer's environment without engineering effort.
  • Deals are closing but deployments are stalling, churning, or quietly never going live.
  • You need someone accountable for an outcome after the contract is signed.

Solutions engineer

  • Deals are stalling before signature on technical objections.
  • The product deploys cleanly and the gap is trust and proof, not integration.
  • You need to cover many prospects at once rather than go deep in a few.

Questions people ask

  • Is a forward-deployed engineer the same as a solutions engineer?
  • No. A solutions engineer is accountable for winning the technical argument before a contract is signed; a forward-deployed engineer is accountable for the system working in production after it is. They overlap in the late deal cycle, and some companies fuse the titles, but the point of measurement is different.
  • Do forward-deployed engineers do pre-sales work?
  • Often, yes. 35% of the FDE job descriptions in Plank's census mention pre-sales or solutions responsibilities. The difference from a solutions engineer is that pre-sales is a secondary duty rather than the job.
  • Which pays more, an FDE or a solutions engineer?
  • They are structured differently rather than simply ranked. Solutions engineers typically earn a base plus variable pay tied to quota attainment. Forward-deployed engineers are usually paid as engineers — base plus equity, no quota — which at frontier AI labs pushes total compensation well past what a comparable solutions engineer earns, mostly through equity.
  • Can a solutions engineer become a forward-deployed engineer?
  • It is one of the most common paths in, provided the engineering is real. The customer-facing instincts transfer directly. What usually needs building is depth in production code — owning a system on-call, integrating with a real system of record, and shipping against someone else's data model.
