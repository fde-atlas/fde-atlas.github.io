Forward deployed engineer bootcamp | 12 weeks in person

Data Science Dojo · 2026 · 7 min read

Summary

Data Science Dojo is running a 12-week, in-person forward deployed engineer bootcamp starting January 18, 2027, with 30 seats per cohort. The first four weeks cover LLM fundamentals, retrieval, agents, and evaluation/deployment, followed by four weekly build sprints (document review, support assistant, enterprise integration, human-in-the-loop operations) and a three-week client capstone scoped, built, and deployed to production. Week 12 is a demo day with hiring-partner interviews. The program is free to members—funded by hiring partners who pre-pay hiring credits—with offers starting at $125,000, though students cover their own living costs in the Redmond/Seattle area. Admission requires a technical assessment and interview; applications close December 21, 2026. As a first cohort, no placement statistics exist yet.

Key points

  • 12-week full-time, in-person residency in Redmond/Seattle, 30 seats per cohort.
  • Four weeks of foundations, then four weekly build sprints with code reviews and demos.
  • Client capstone runs three weeks: scope with stakeholders, build, deploy to production.
  • Free to members; hiring partners fund it, with offers starting at $125,000.
  • First cohort, so no placement numbers have been published yet.

What a forward deployed engineer does

  • The job

    You work directly with a customer. You scope a messy real-world problem, build the AI system that solves it, and ship it into production.

  • Why now

    AI companies, and the enterprises adopting AI, are hiring engineers who can do this.

  • Why it lasts

    The job combines engineering, judgment and client communication. That mix is harder to commoditize than writing code alone.

  • Why a residency

    The role is learned by doing it, under real deadlines and alongside other people. Online courses alone rarely give you that.

Who should apply

  • Recent engineering graduates

    Computer science and engineering graduates looking for a first role You have the degree. Now get the offer. Turn coursework into shipped, deployed work, and graduate with a portfolio of client-style projects that gets interviews.

  • Experienced IT professionals

    Enterprise, infrastructure and consulting professionals, including those reskilling after a layoff Your experience is an asset. Make it AI-ready. Client-facing and systems experience is an advantage in this role, and the residency turns it into the FDE profile.

  • Who it is not for

    People who cannot commit full time and in person for all 12 weeks. This is a full-time commitment, like a job.

Four weeks of foundations, then a project every week

Four weeks of foundations, taken from the curriculum of our LLM and agentic AI bootcamps, then four build sprints with code reviews and demos, a client capstone, and demo day. It is a full-time commitment, like a job: in-class learning, projects, homework and talks from industry experts.

Foundations

Week 01Mon, Jan 18How LLMs workTokens, embeddings and attention

How large language models work

The map of the stack you will deploy for clients, then what happens inside the model. Tokenization, embeddings and how models generate text; transformers from the ground up, with positional encoding and self-attention; where models are strong and why they hallucinate; and how pre-training, fine-tuning and in-context learning each give a model your own knowledge.

Week 02Mon, Jan 25Retrieval and contextVector search, agentic RAG and fine-tuning

Retrieval, context and fine-tuning

Retrieval sets the ceiling on everything a client sees. Embeddings and vector databases; semantic, keyword and hybrid search; HNSW indexes and re-ranking; agentic RAG with multi-step retrieval; the problems production adds, from PII-safe ingestion to latency and cost; and when fine-tuning with LoRA beats better retrieval, and when it cannot help.

Week 03Mon, Feb 1Building agentsLangChain, LangGraph, design patterns and MCP

Building agents

From chains to agents that hold state. LangChain components, tools and LCEL; LangGraph graphs, typed state and memory; the reflection, planning, tool-use and multi-agent patterns, and what each costs in control; and Model Context Protocol, so a tool is exposed once and called by any agent, with A2A and ACP compared.

Week 04Mon, Feb 8Evaluation and productionEvals, observability, guardrails and deployment

Evaluation, observability and deployment

Prove a system works, then keep it working. Benchmarks, RAGAs and G-Eval; traces and online evaluation once it is live; the reference architectures that recur in production, from a single agent with tools to supervisor and workers and human-in-the-loop review; agents in Docker sandboxes with policy enforcement and guardrails; and deploying to the cloud.

Build sprints

Week 05Mon, Feb 15Sprint 1A governed document-review agent

Build sprint 1: a governed document-review agent

Your first client-style engagement: a document-review agent for a public-sector client, with the governance that client needs. Scope it, build it and ship it inside the week, then demo it and take a code review.

Week 06Mon, Feb 22Sprint 2A customer support assistant

Build sprint 2: a customer support assistant

A customer support assistant with evals, guardrails and monitoring. Ship it inside the week, demo it, and take a code review.

Week 07Mon, Mar 1Sprint 3An agent inside an enterprise system

Build sprint 3: an agent inside an enterprise system

A data pipeline and an agent workflow, integrated into an existing enterprise system rather than built on a clean slate. Ship it inside the week, demo it, and take a code review.

Week 08Mon, Mar 8Sprint 4A human-in-the-loop operations agent

Build sprint 4: a human-in-the-loop operations agent

A multi-agent workflow that works incoming requests, such as invoices, claims or orders, across several systems over MCP, and routes the decisions that matter to a person for approval. Ship it inside the week, demo it, and take a code review.

Client capstone

Week 09Mon, Mar 15Capstone: scopeStakeholders, constraints and a plan

Client capstone: scope the problem

The capstone is a real problem with stakeholders and constraints. Week one is the part of the job courses skip: meet the stakeholders, pin down what they actually need, and agree on what you will ship.

Week 10Mon, Mar 22Capstone: buildBuild it and evaluate it

Client capstone: build and evaluate

Build the system you scoped, measure it against what the stakeholders agreed to, and keep them informed as the constraints push back.

Week 11Mon, Mar 29Capstone: deployInto production

Client capstone: deploy to production

Ship the capstone into production, with the evaluation, security and governance the client signs off on.

Demo day and hiring

Present your capstone at demo day, then interview with hiring partners, with mock interviews and support through your offers.

Every project is framed as work for a client, with a brief, constraints and someone to answer to. You finish with four shipped sprint projects and a capstone in production, and you can explain the decisions behind each one.

What happens in week 12 and after

This is the first cohort, so there are no placement numbers yet. When there are, we will publish them with the method behind them: cohort size, how many graduates were job-seeking, the timeframe, and the roles counted.

  • Demo day

    Present your capstone to hiring partners and the cohort.

  • Partner interviews

    Interview with hiring partners in week 12.

  • Interview and offer support

    Mock interviews before, and help negotiating your offer after.

  • Offers start at $125,000

    Every offer from a hiring partner starts at $125,000 a year or more. It is a floor, not a promise of an offer.

Free to cohort members. Hiring partners pay.

The program is funded by the companies that hire its graduates, not by you.

  • Free to cohort members

    What you pay You pay nothing to attend, and cover only your own travel, housing and living costs. No fees, clawbacks or repayments, whether you finish, leave or turn down an offer.

  • Hiring partners fund it

    How it works Companies that need forward deployed engineers fund the program. They pre-pay for hiring credits, and we are paid only when they hire and keep one of our graduates.

  • Why it works for you

    Aligned incentives We only succeed when you get hired, and the companies you interview with in week 12 are the ones funding your seat.

Three months in Redmond/Seattle

The program is free, but you cover your own travel, housing and living costs in the Redmond/Seattle area, so budget for three months.

  • Housing

    We connect admitted students who want to share housing in the Redmond/Seattle area.

  • Getting there

    Classes are at 5010 148th Ave NE, Suite 200A, Redmond, WA 98052, in the Seattle area.

  • Work authorization

    Open to applicants authorized to work in the US. Sponsorship for later cohorts depends on our hiring partners.

A selective, competitive admissions process

Admission is highly selective and competitive: 30 seats a cohort, each filled through a technical assessment and an interview. We look for problem-solving, communication, grit and commitment.

  • 1. Apply online

    Tell us about your background and why you want this role. Applications close December 21, 2026, and shortlisted applicants hear from us within two weeks.

  • 2. Technical assessment

    A take-home task that looks like the job: from a short client brief, build a small working AI application, then write a one-page note to the client explaining your choices and what you would do next.

  • 3. Interview

    A technical and motivation interview.

  • 4. Offer and enrollment

    Offers follow the interview, and the cohort starts January 18, 2027.
