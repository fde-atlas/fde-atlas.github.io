FDE vs solutions engineer: which role fits

Mayur Patil · 2026-11-02 · 4 min read

Summary

Mayur Patil distinguishes two roles: a solutions engineer helps a buyer decide before purchase, judged by whether the deal closes, while a forward-deployed engineer builds inside the client's systems and is judged by whether the system runs after handover. The article compares duration, outcomes, risks and trade-offs, gives week-by-week shapes for each, and lists decision criteria. Common mistakes include buying a demo expecting production, hiring an FDE before the use case is chosen, and treating handover as an afterthought. A hybrid works well: prove fit first, then deploy and hand over with a named owner.

Key points

  • Solutions engineer answers whether a product fits; FDE answers whether your system works.
  • FDE engagements run weeks to months and end with a running system, runbook and handover.
  • Solutions engineer work is fast and low-commitment but demos may fail on real data.
  • Key mistakes: expecting production from a demo, hiring an FDE before choosing the use case.
  • A hybrid sequence, fit proof then deployment, often works best.

The short answer

A solutions engineer helps a buyer decide. A forward-deployed engineer helps a team deliver. The first one is judged by whether the deal closes. The second is judged by whether the system runs after they leave.

Decision table

Question Forward-deployed engineer Solutions engineer
What it is An engineer who works inside the client's systems and writes code there A technical seller who shows the product and answers questions before purchase
Who owns the outcome The engineer and the client's named owner, together The sales team, with the vendor's product
Typical duration Weeks to months, on one bounded use case Days to weeks, around one sales cycle
What you get at the end A working system, its runbook and a handover A proof of concept, a technical answer and a recommendation
Main risk Dependence on one engineer if handover is weak A demo that does not survive contact with real data
Best for Turning a chosen use case into production Qualifying a vendor and proving fit before buying

Use cases for a forward-deployed engineer

  • An agent that must act in a CRM. The engineer builds the write steps and the approval for each change, inside the client's own CRM.
  • Moving approvals from email into a workflow. The engineer builds the workflow in the client's system and hands it to the process owner.
  • A pilot that passed its demo and now needs live data. The engineer connects it to the real records and runs the evaluation on them.

Use cases for a solutions engineer

  • A buyer comparing two platforms for summarising documents. The solutions engineer runs both on the buyer's sample documents in a short proof of concept.
  • A team asking whether a product can read its PDFs. A quick technical answer, tested on a sample file the team provides.
  • A shortlist before procurement. Technical questions, integration checks and a written list of the product's limits.
  • A product already chosen and needing set-up. Configuration support and training for the team's administrators.

Trade-offs

Forward-deployed engineer

Advantages. The system is built in your environment and tested on your data. The engagement ends with a running system, a runbook and a named owner.

Disadvantages. It takes longer to reach a first answer than a demo does. It needs access to your systems, which needs approval. If the handover is weak, the system depends on the engineer who built it.

Solutions engineer

Advantages. It is fast, and it surfaces a product's limits early, before any contract. It carries little commitment before a purchase decision.

Disadvantages. A demo on sample data can mislead about how the product behaves on your records. The work after the sale belongs to someone else. It does not build your workflow.

How each option runs

Forward-deployed engineer, week by week

A typical shape, not a fixed plan:

  • Week 1. Agree the workflow, the data the agent needs and the owner who signs off results.
  • Weeks 2 and 3. Connect the agent to real records in a test environment and build the approval steps.
  • Week 4 onward. Run the evaluation on live-shaped data, fix the failures and hand over.

Solutions engineer, week by week

  • Day 1. Ask the buyer's questions and run the product on a sample the buyer provides.
  • Week 1. Write up what worked, what did not, and the limits the buyer should expect.
  • Week 2. Support the commercial decision with integration notes and a security questionnaire.

What drives the effort

  • How much of your own data the proof of concept needs to touch.
  • How many integrations the chosen product must reach.
  • How many people must be trained to run the product afterwards.
  • Whether a security review is needed before any real record is shared.

This site does not publish prices. The engagements page describes what each shape includes.

A checklist before you choose

  • Are you still choosing a product, or has it already been chosen?
  • Does the product need to run on your records to answer the question?
  • Who will run the system after the sale?
  • Is there a named owner who can accept the limits the demo reveals?

If the product is still open and you only need technical fit, start with a solutions engineer. If the product is chosen and it has to run on your records, the engineer is the usual next step.

What people get wrong

  • They buy a demo and expect a production system. A demo is evidence of a possibility, not of a working system.
  • They hire a forward-deployed engineer before the use case is chosen. The first two weeks are then spent deciding what to build.
  • They treat handover as a final step. It is part of the plan from the first day.

A hybrid that often works

A solutions engineer proves that a product fits a narrow task. A forward-deployed engineer then takes the chosen task into your systems and hands it over. The two roles can come from the same vendor or from different ones, as long as one person is accountable for the result.

Common questions

Can one person do both roles?

Sometimes, on a small project. On a larger one, the selling and the building pull in different directions, and a team usually separates them.

Does a forward-deployed engineer replace a solutions engineer?

No. They answer different questions. A solutions engineer answers whether a product fits. A forward-deployed engineer answers whether your system works.

Which role costs more?

Cost depends on scope and duration, not on the title. Compare the engagement shapes on the engagements page; this site does not publish prices.

What should I ask a candidate for either role?

For a forward-deployed engineer, ask for an example of a system they handed over and how the owner ran it afterwards. For a solutions engineer, ask how they test a claim on your data before they make it.
