Palantir Forward Deployed Software Engineer Interview (No Offer): After the OA, I had a call with

Taro · 2024-04-05 · 3 min read

Summary

This Taro post documents a candidate's failed interview for Palantir's Forward Deployed Software Engineer role in New York. After passing the online assessment, the candidate had a call with the HR hiring manager, who seemed disengaged and asked few questions; the call ended quickly and no further round followed. Aggregated data from 36 interview experiences show a 14% pass rate, indicating a highly selective process. Candidate sentiment was mostly positive: 56% positive, 28% neutral, 17% negative. The post also includes reviews from current employees, who praise smart colleagues, rapid growth, early leadership opportunities, and large autonomy, while citing high pressure, overtime, suboptimal hours, and a steep learning curve as drawbacks.

Key points

  • Candidate was cut after an HR call following the online assessment
  • Only 14% pass rate across 36 interview experiences in New York
  • Sentiment: 56% positive, 28% neutral, 17% negative
  • Employees praise fast growth and early leadership opportunities
  • Common complaints: high pressure, overtime, steep learning curve

Forward Deployed Software Engineer • Current Employee

New York, New York, United States

November 3, 2020

Pros: People are very smart. There is room to grow. It's not uncommon to see people who have been in the company for 1-2 years becoming Leads. Huge responsibilities from day 1. "Choose your own adventure" -> You can create your own growth path in the company if you see the need for it. For example, if you work on X product/customer but you see that there is a need to develop another product, you can start working on that without an issue (as long as you don't jeopardize your other role). D&I is big here. You can get as involved as you want with this, and that's awesome! Overall, this is an amazing company. I love it.

Cons: Too much autonomy at times. This is also a pro, but being independent and figuring out things on your own is not for everyone.
