FDE | On-site problem solving: fast, affordable, and reliable AI implementation

Original title: FDE | 現場に来て解決する、速く・安く・安心のAI導入

TIMEWELL · 2026-08-13 · 18 min read

Summary

TIMEWELL, a Japanese vendor of the export-control AI product TRAFEED, describes its forward deployed engineer (FDE) service. Engineers work on-site, show a working prototype built on masked real data on day one, release a new version daily, and hand over a system the client can run independently within 45–90 days. Pricing targets half or less of large-vendor contracted development, based on product fees plus a fixed-period engagement rather than accumulated hours. The company holds ISO/IEC 27001 certification and has deployed to 20+ organizations including universities. The article contrasts FDE with contracted development, SES, and consulting, stresses data preparation (digitizing paper documents, building knowledge graphs), and lists four conditions—permissions, simulation, human approval, audit logs—before AI writes to core systems.

Key points

  • Day-one prototype from masked real data; daily releases for 45–90 days
  • Pricing is period-based, targeting half of large-vendor development costs
  • ISO/IEC 27001 certified; TRAFEED deployed at 20+ organizations including universities
  • Field knowledge loops back into product features; glossaries stay with the client
  • AI writes to core systems only after permissions, simulation, human approval, and audit logging

95% of AI adoption stalls at the workplace. That is why you need a provider who comes to the site

With job postings up 50x and 38 companies lined up, many of them have simply renamed traditional outsourced development "FDE." You can tell the real thing by whether know-how gained on-site becomes product features and whether the next project gets faster and cheaper.

FDE means engineers who come to your workplace and turn problems into working software

To use a medical analogy, it is a "house call." Instead of waiting at the hospital, the doctor visits the patient's home, examines them on the spot, and treats them there. FDE works the same way: we do not wait for specifications to be written. On day one, we show a working prototype—even if incomplete—and ask "where does this differ from reality?", then fix it the next day. Through this repetition, the real requirements that never surface in meeting rooms become visible.

  • 01 Listen We hear from three perspectives—management, business divisions, and the front line—sit beside actual work, and capture unspoken procedures and exceptions.
  • 02 Build On top of components from our products, we assemble in a few hours a prototype running on real data with personal information masked, and show it to your team.
  • 03 Return to the product Mechanisms useful to other companies become standard features of our product, while procedures unique to you remain as your dedicated configuration. That is why the next project gets even faster.

We thoroughly convert paper drawings, forms, and coarse-grained data into databases

Most causes of stalled AI adoption lie before the model—in the data itself. Still on paper, still in PDF, still only in a staff member's head. We do not skip this step. We sit on-site and prepare the data ourselves until it is in a form AI can use.

Digitization of paper, PDFs, and images

Paper drawings, handwritten daily reports, scanned PDFs, photos. We use reading components to structure them, and verify and fill in anything unreadable on-site.

Organizing and reconciling Excel files and forms

Tables written differently by each person, part numbers that differ by department, notation inconsistencies. We set rules to unify them and record them in your business glossary.

Completing coarse-grained data

Operations running on "roughly" and "as usual" are converted into numbers and categories through interviews and observation of staff, to a granularity AI can use for decisions.

Into a database in a form AI can use

We turn the prepared data into a database (knowledge graph) that AI can traverse, organized around your terminology and business relationships. Only after this step can AI truly be used in the field.

Prototype on Monday, organize on Friday. Repeat this week for 45–90 days until your team can operate it on its own

Rather than lining up phases in sequence, we run "listen, build, have them use it, return to the product" simultaneously every week.

  • Free 30-minute consultation to verify your challenges and conditions We hear what management, business divisions, and the front line each want to change, and verify the conditions for speed: whether we can access the data and whether staff time can be secured. If the project is not a good fit, we tell you honestly on the spot.
  • Monday Listen to the three perspectives, identify the data owners, and show a prototype by evening We build in a few hours, on top of components from our products, a prototype running on real data with personal information masked, and show it to your team.
  • Tuesday–Thursday Observe the work, gather exceptions, and release a new version every day We sit beside the work, capture procedures that were never explained, and release a new version daily. On Thursday, we tentatively measure the effect.
  • Friday Organize the week's insights, update the business glossary, and return shared mechanisms to the product We organize the fragments gathered over the week and update your business glossary. Mechanisms useful to other companies become product features; procedures unique to you become your dedicated configuration.
  • Weeks 4–12 Gradually add write capabilities and hand over a system your team can operate on its own From around week four, your staff update the business glossary themselves. The engagement ends when the cycle keeps running even on days when our engineers are not present.

Writes to core systems and connections requiring security review cannot go to production in one week. Reviews proceed in parallel, and during the review period development continues with masked data.

Contract development means "build to spec"; FDE means "fix it on-site until results appear"

This is not a matter of FDE being better or worse. For work with fixed requirements that ends once built, contract development is cheaper and more reliable. FDE is suited to work where requirements are buried in the field and can only be understood by trying things out.

Four conditions before AI writes anything

Permission settings, pre-execution simulation, human confirmation for important decisions, and audit records. We do not write to core systems until all four are in place. Actual data is handled only after masking personal information and similar items.

We decline poor-fit requests, leave a business glossary with you, and define exit conditions upfront

As more companies call themselves FDEs, failure patterns have become visible. Here are three failures and how we prevent each.

Becoming a handyman

Accepting every request from the field, getting bogged down in one-off responses, and accumulating no results.

We decline poor-fit requests with a reason. Additional requests incur no extra cost if they use components usable by other companies. Company-specific procedures are handled via configuration or declined.

Build and leave

Deliverables remain company-specific, and when the person in charge changes, no one can update them.

We update your business glossary weekly, and from around week 4 your team takes over the updates. The contract explicitly states that the glossary's contents belong to you.

Dependence on specific individuals

Nothing works without a specific engineer, and the on-site engagement never ends.

We define exit conditions upfront. The engagement ends if there is a week where the cycle runs without our engineers present. If it does not run, we revise the design rather than extend.

Projects we welcome

We state upfront the projects where we excel as an FDE, and, honestly, those that do not suit us.

  • Introducing AI into manufacturing floor operations (drawings, quotations, processes, quality records)
  • Organizing coarse-grained data such as paper drawings, documents, and Excel files into databases
  • Building foundations that turn IoT and robot data into forms usable for on-site decisions
  • Business AI with strict security requirements where data cannot leave the country or the company
  • Automating operations involving export control and economic security
  • Embedding AI into existing internal systems and SaaS
  • Consultation on the design (architecture) of products under development
  • One-off contract development with fixed requirements and acceptance testing (a contract development firm is a better fit)
  • Sites that cannot secure staff time or data access
  • Projects consisting solely of company-specific procedures with no prospect of shared components usable by others

Frequently asked questions about FDEs

How is this different from contract development?

We do not line up phases sequentially; results are measured by the person in charge's daily working time rather than acceptance testing; know-how gained on-site becomes features of our products; and our contracts never embed a structure where the longer we stay, the more we profit. In contract development, revenue grows the longer you stay; in FDE work, value comes from finishing early.

Who owns the business knowledge we learn on-site?

We divide it into three. Shared mechanisms that exist at other companies too (e.g., approvals can have multiple stages) become standard features of our products. Company-specific procedures remain as configuration exclusive to you. The contents of your business glossary belong to you. This is explicitly stated in the contract.

Is it true that you show something working on day one?

We will show you an incomplete prototype running on real data with personal information and other sensitive details masked. It is not a finished product; it is meant to draw out answers from your staff on "what is different here." Writing to your core systems will only be added once four conditions are met: permissions, pre-execution simulation, human review, and audit logs.

A security review is required. Can you handle that?

Yes. We proceed with the review in parallel, and at the first meeting we set up a plan to continue development with masked data during the review. We are ISO/IEC 27001 certified, and operation in domestic regions or configurations within your own environment are also possible.

How much does it cost?

It consists of two parts: product usage fees and an implementation fee determined over a 45–90 day period. We design with a guideline of half or less compared to major outsourced development, though it varies with scope and conditions. We will provide a quote after the free consultation.

Can we request advisory services only?

Yes. We also accept engagements such as design reviews of products under development or monthly technical advisory. Separate from on-site development, please consult us via the free consultation.

We have our own engineers. Can we develop together?

Yes—in fact, we welcome it. The more your engineers learn to use our components and update the business glossary, the sooner your company will be able to operate it on its own. The design calls for your side to take over updates from around week four.

Can you work remotely?

Being on-site is a condition for speed, so the first week and observation days are basically in person. Other development and weekly meetings can proceed remotely. Location is not a restriction.

What form does the contract take?

Based on a quasi-mandate contract (Article 656 of the Civil Code), we clearly specify the period (45–90 days), success metrics, and ownership of deliverables (shared mechanisms go into our product; your company-specific configurations and business glossary belong to you). We do not make it a contract that simply ends with acceptance of a fixed deliverable.

Which industries do you mostly serve?

Mainly manufacturing (drawings, quotes, processes, quality records), sites handling IoT and robotics data, and work related to export control and economic security. The more fields where our product's components can be used as-is, the faster we can move.

Write your challenge in just one line and send it

Something like "I want to speed up creating quotes from drawings" or "I want to use sensor data for on-site decisions" is enough. We will suggest dates for a free consultation (30 minutes). Even if your consultation topic is not yet settled, you can start from that stage.

First, start with a 30-minute free consultation

We listen to your on-site challenges and frankly tell you whether this is a project we can solve quickly, affordably, and with peace of mind. Design consultations and requests for technical advisory are also handled through the same contact point.
