Summary

Written by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, this Japanese article analyzes freelance IT consultant rates in Japan using January 2026 Freelance Board data: average monthly rate of about 1.081 million yen, or roughly 12.98 million yen annualized, the highest among IT professions. The author argues that exceeding 10 million yen requires three conditions: a specialized positioning (DX, SAP, AI/data analysis), multiple deal-sourcing channels, and minimizing gaps between projects. Rates depend heavily on supply-chain depth—direct end-client deals can pay 400,000 yen more per month than third-tier subcontracting. The article compares Big Four salaried salaries (8.98–9.68 million yen), suggests 1.3–1.5x salary as the break-even point for going independent, and highlights FDE as an emerging 1.2–1.5 million yen/month niche. Note the piece promotes the author's own BizDev Tech agency.

Key points

  • Freelance IT consultants average about 1.081 million yen/month (12.98 million yen/year), top of all IT professions.
  • Exceeding 10 million yen requires specialization, multiple sourcing channels, and short gaps between projects.
  • Supply-chain depth matters: end-direct deals can pay ~400,000 yen more monthly than third-tier subcontracting.
  • Author suggests 1.3–1.5x salaried pay as the break-even point for independence.
  • The article promotes DeFactory's BizDev Tech service, so read with that bias in mind.

"How much will my annual income increase if I go independent as an IT consultant?"—many engineers and consultants likely have this question.

To give you the answer right away: the average annual income of freelance IT consultants is 12.98 million yen—the highest level among all IT professions. However, not everyone can achieve this figure. Realizing high rates requires three conditions: "specialization," "sales channels," and "the ability to secure projects."

In this article, based on the latest 2026 rate data, we explain the specific conditions for exceeding an annual income of 10 million yen and the reality of securing projects from an agent's perspective. An IT consultant is a professional who solves corporate management challenges through the power of IT. Rather than technical implementation itself, their main role is to define "what to do" and "why to do it" from a management perspective and guide IT investment in the right direction.

Going independent as a freelancer gives you the freedom to choose your own projects, but in exchange, it demands sales skills and self-management abilities. Here, we will organize the basic definition of an IT consultant, the differences from full-time employment, and the types of projects involved.

High-rate, direct-contract project information

An IT consultant is "a professional who solves corporate management challenges through the power of IT."

Specific responsibilities include analyzing the client company's current state, structuring challenges, developing solutions, creating roadmaps, and supporting vendor selection. Unlike engineers who simply build systems, IT consultants must be able to convincingly propose and advise management on "why this IT investment is necessary."

What companies expect from IT consultants is their function as a "bridge" who is versed in both technical and business matters. Even when a company has IT talent in-house, few people can connect business strategy with IT strategy. That is precisely why demand for IT consultants as external experts is growing.

The biggest differences between freelance IT consultants and full-time consultants lie in "flexibility of work style" and "compensation structure."

Full-time employees receive a fixed monthly salary, along with social insurance and benefits. Freelancers, on the other hand, are compensated per project, signing contracts for each project.

As a freelancer, you can choose your own projects and even take long breaks between projects. If you secure high-rate projects, you can earn more than a full-time employee.

However, if projects run out, your income drops to zero. You must handle sales, self-management, and building the network needed to secure your next project all by yourself.

Projects handled by freelance IT consultants can be broadly divided into six categories. We have organized the characteristics and rate trends of each.

In particular, professionals with DX support or SAP implementation experience tend to command high rates. PMO projects are numerous and easy to enter, but rates vary greatly depending on the sales channel, so whether you can secure end-client direct projects is crucial.

Now let's look at the income of freelance IT consultants based on the latest survey data. According to the Freelance Board survey (January 2026), the average monthly rate is 1.081 million yen and the estimated annual income is 12.98 million yen—the highest level among all IT professions.

Source: Freelance Board, "Freelance Jobs and Projects for IT Consultants"

The highest rates are in the DX/IT strategy domain, where the market rate is 1.5 to 2 million yen per month. Since this work involves dialogue with executives and strategy development, talent with experience at consulting firms or as CIO/CTO at operating companies is highly valued.

Factors that influence rates include project scale, specialization, years of experience, and the depth of the sales channel. Even with the same skill set, these factors can create a difference of 200,000 to 400,000 yen per month.

To exceed an annual income of 10 million yen, you need to sustain a monthly rate of 840,000 yen or more for 12 months. At an average monthly rate of 1.08 million yen, you would reach an annual income of 12.98 million yen, but achieving this requires meeting three conditions.

It is important to establish a position where people say, "If it's XX field, this is the person to go to." In particular, areas such as DX promotion, SAP implementation, and AI/data analysis have high demand and tend to command higher rates.

Currently, specialists with deep expertise in a specific field can more easily command high rates than generalists with broad but shallow skills. As direct requests for your services increase, you also gain leverage in rate negotiations.

No matter how strong your skills are, your income is zero without projects. It is important to have multiple channels for securing projects, such as using agents, getting referrals through your network, and building a presence on social media.

Securing projects through your network in particular has the advantage of tending to command high rates, since no middleman margin is involved. Valuing your relationships on a daily basis expands the possibilities for referral projects.

For freelancers, there can be gaps between projects. How short you keep these gaps directly affects your annual income.

It is common to start looking for the next project two to three months before the current one ends. Once the end of a project comes into view, consult with agents early or tap your network to secure your next project.

By meeting these three conditions, an annual income exceeding 10 million yen becomes a fully realistic goal.

Beyond the 10-million-yen barrier for freelance IT consultants, there is another role attracting attention: the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer).

The FDE is a role that combines the "proposals and upstream support" of IT consultants with the "implementation and technical execution skills" of engineers, with its core value proposition being direct commitment to clients' business outcomes (KPI improvement, reduced churn, increased sales).

The biggest difference from freelance IT consultants is that FDEs don't stop at proposals—they consistently handle everything through implementation and adoption. Because of this characteristic, freelance FDE projects in the 1.2 to 1.5 million yen per month range are beginning to emerge in Japan, and a growing number of IT consultants are choosing this as the next stage of their careers.

Comparing gross figures alone, the freelance average annual income of 12.98 million yen looks higher. However, since full-time employees receive benefits and company-paid portions of social insurance premiums, their "effective annual income" is somewhat higher.

On the other hand, freelancers can significantly increase their take-home pay by incorporating a company and using tax-saving schemes. For example, if a freelancer earning 12 million yen per year files taxes as a sole proprietor, about 3.5 million yen is deducted for income tax, resident tax, national health insurance premiums, and so on, leaving a take-home of about 8.5 million yen. However, by establishing a micro-corporation and optimizing executive compensation, some cases can increase take-home pay to about 9.5 million yen through reduced social insurance premiums and the use of corporate tax.

That said, it is important not to forget that freelancing carries the "risk of income interruption." Full-time employees continue to receive salary for a certain period even if they take time off due to illness or injury, but freelancers earn nothing if they are not working.

With this in mind, "1.3 to 1.5 times a full-time employee's annual income" can be considered the break-even point when switching to freelancing.

Since consultants' work centers on creating documents and performing analysis, it is well suited to remote work. The typical arrangement is to go into the office only for meetings with clients and work from home or a coworking space otherwise.

This flexibility is one of the great attractions of being a freelance IT consultant. Even those living in regional areas may be able to join high-rate projects in the Tokyo metropolitan area remotely.

Going freelance has both advantages and disadvantages. Beyond the appeal of "high income" and "freedom," you also need to honestly understand the risks, such as "unstable income," "reduced social credibility," and "no employee benefits."

Here, we provide a balanced, objective explanation.

Going independent as a freelance IT consultant offers five advantages that are not available to full-time employees.

The average annual income of freelance IT consultants is 12.98 million yen, the highest among all IT professions. It is not uncommon for people who earned 6 to 8 million yen as full-time employees to exceed 10 million yen after going independent.

The appeal of freelancing is that you can set your own income ceiling depending on your skills and track record.

Some projects only require working three to four days a week, and fully remote projects are increasing. Being able to work without constraints on time or location is a privilege unique to freelancers.

Eliminating commuting time gives many people two to three hours of free time per day.

While it is difficult for full-time employees to take more than two consecutive weeks off, freelancers can take one to two months of extended vacation between projects.

Many people also use it for overseas travel or self-improvement.

You have the freedom to turn down work you don't want to do. You can build your career by choosing projects that enhance your expertise or that are in industries that interest you.

You no longer need to worry about relationships with a boss you dislike or coworkers you don't get along with.

You can work in a way that suits your lifestyle, such as reducing commuting time, working at your own pace, and securing time with your family.

Many people also say it has become easier to balance work with childcare or caregiving.

These benefits are of great value to people who have strong self-management skills and the will to build their own careers.

On the other hand, freelancing comes with disadvantages and risks that cannot be avoided. Be sure to understand them before going independent.

If projects dry up, your income drops to zero. Depending on economic conditions and industry trends, there may be periods when it is difficult to secure projects.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, there were cases where many freelancers lost their projects and saw their income plummet.

You need to handle tasks yourself, such as filing tax returns, doing accounting, and reviewing contracts, which the company used to take care of when you were a full-time employee.

If you hire a tax accountant, it will also cost around 200,000 to 500,000 yen per year.

You may be at a disadvantage when applying for a mortgage or credit card. Especially right after going independent, you have no track record, so you need to be prepared for the possibility that applications may be rejected.

If you are considering going independent, it is wise to take out loans and get credit cards while you are still a salaried employee.

For health insurance, you will enroll in the National Health Insurance, and you bear the full cost of premiums. Your employee pension also switches to the National Pension, so your future pension benefits may decrease.

You also need to make your own arrangements for things like health checkups, paid leave, and childcare leave.

Even if you become unable to work due to illness or injury, there is no paid leave. It is recommended that you take out income compensation insurance or similar coverage on your own.

If trouble arises, the company will not protect you, and you must deal with it yourself.

Understanding these disadvantages and still wanting to go independent is the first step toward succeeding as a freelancer.

Success as a freelance IT consultant depends on whether it suits you. Calmly judge which type you are closer to.

Traits of people who are well suited

  • High self-management skills, able to deliver results without being managed by others
  • Not bothered by sales and negotiations, or willing to learn them
  • Confident in a specific area of expertise
  • Able to accept the risk of unstable income
  • Motivated by a desire to "work more freely" or "earn more on my own terms"

Traits of people who are not well suited

  • Feel anxious without a stable monthly income
  • Dislike or want to avoid administrative work and tax filings
  • Not good at building connections and networking
  • Feel anxious without a company's name behind them
  • Uncomfortable making decisions on their own

If many of the "well suited" traits apply to you, becoming a freelancer is a strong option. Conversely, if many of the "not well suited" traits apply, it is recommended that you first gain experience as a full-time employee while gradually preparing for the transition.

To succeed as a freelance IT consultant, the three pillars of "experience, expertise, and connections" are essential. Going independent directly with no prior experience is not realistic, but by allowing a sufficient preparation period, you can minimize risk and get your career on track.

It may seem mundane, but consultants who excel here never see their rates drop. People who can create clear and accurate documents are highly valued by clients.

Becoming a freelance IT consultant directly with no prior experience is realistically difficult.

The recommended amount of experience is at least 3 to 5 years. For those from consulting firms, that experience is a major strength.

If you have no consulting firm experience, a realistic path is to work as an SE or programmer at an SIer or IT vendor, then gain PM or PMO experience in-house, and then transition to freelancing in stages.

Without achievements and skills that make clients think "I can trust this person," it will be difficult to secure high-rate projects. It is important to build a solid track record during your preparation period before going independent.

To keep securing high rates as a freelancer, it is important to establish a strength that lets people say "this is the person for that field."

In particular, fields such as AI and data analytics, DX promotion, and SAP implementation tend to have high demand and rising rates. Take stock of your experience and skills, and clarify which field you will compete in.

A "cloud consultant" specializing in cloud migration and utilization strategy is also one option for specialization. For details, see What Is a Cloud Consultant? Explaining Roles, Salary Benchmarks, and How to Leverage Freelancing.

Building connections is equally important. Many freelancers secure new projects through their connections or past clients. You may often receive offers from former colleagues or clients, and in such cases there is no middleman margin, so terms tend to be favorable.

Before going independent, it is recommended to expand your network by attending industry study sessions and seminars, and by sharing information on social media.

Since income may be unstable right after going independent, it is strongly recommended to save 6 months to 1 year of living expenses before becoming independent.

Since it can take time to land projects, in addition to living expenses, you should aim to secure around 1 to 2 million yen as working capital for your business.

Administrative procedures required for starting a business include submitting a notification of business commencement (to the tax office), filing the application for approval of blue tax return status, enrolling in National Health Insurance, switching to the National Pension, opening a business bank account, and introducing cloud accounting software.

If you are considering incorporation, incorporation costs of around 200,000 to 300,000 yen are required. It is said that tax-saving benefits from incorporation start to appear once annual income exceeds around 7 to 8 million yen.

It is also reassuring to learn how to read contracts and acquire basic legal knowledge (key points of outsourcing agreements, the Subcontract Act, etc.).

To steadily obtain work after going independent, it is important to have multiple means of acquiring projects. This section explains specific methods, focusing on the two main pillars of using agents and leveraging your network.

We will also consider the future prospects of freelance IT consultants in light of two tailwinds: growing demand driven by DX promotion and the shortage of IT professionals.

There are several ways for freelance IT consultants to acquire projects. We have organized the characteristics and priority of each.

A realistic strategy is to register with agents to get project referrals while simultaneously increasing referrals through your network. Having multiple channels reduces the risk of projects drying up.

Freelance rates vary greatly depending on "how deep the transaction chain is." Understanding this point is extremely important when aiming to raise your rates.

Transaction ChainExample Rate for the Same PMO ProjectTake-Home Guide
End-direct (primary contractor)1.2 million yen/monthHigh
Secondary contractor1 million yen/monthMedium
Third-tier contractor and below800,000 yen/monthLow

Even with the same skill set and the same project content, the deeper the transaction chain, the more middleman margins are taken and the lower the rate becomes.

For example, in a project ordered by the end client at 1.5 million yen per month, if it passes through a primary-contractor major consulting firm, a secondary-contractor SIer, and a third-tier agent, the freelancer may be left with only 800,000 yen per month.

Conversely, this means that choosing agents that handle end-direct and prime contractor projects is the shortest route to raising your rates. When offered a project, make a habit of asking, "What is the transaction chain?"

When choosing an agent strong in IT consulting projects, check the following points.

  • Whether the ratio of end-direct and prime contractor projects is high
  • Whether they handle IT consulting projects in the 700,000 to 1.5 million yen per month range
  • Whether they have plenty of remote projects
  • Whether the account manager knows the IT industry well and can discuss technical matters

Choosing an agent with many projects with shallow transaction chains makes it easier to land high-rate projects. Also, if you value flexibility in how you work, it is best to choose an agent with a high proportion of remote projects.

Demand for freelance IT consultants is expected to continue rising. Behind this are two major tailwinds.

Corporate DX (digital transformation) is no longer a passing trend; it has become an essential management issue.

Areas requiring IT consultant support, such as digitizing business processes, leveraging data, and creating new digital businesses, continue to expand. In particular, mid-sized and small companies lack DX promotion know-how, and their reliance on external consultants is growing.

According to a survey by IPA (Information-technology Promotion Agency), 43% of IT personnel capable of handling upstream processes are in short supply.

Source: IPA "DX Trends 2024 – The Deepening IT Talent Shortage and Challenges in Promoting DX"

In particular, professionals who can link business strategy with IT strategy are rare, and this supply-demand gap is not expected to be resolved anytime soon. Even as companies strengthen their recruiting efforts, securing excellent talent remains difficult.

Defining upstream business issues and formulating strategy require sophisticated human judgment, making them areas where AI substitution is difficult. Rather, as professionals who promote the use of AI, the value of IT consultants will only increase.

Due to the supply-demand gap, IT consultant compensation is also trending upward. This trend is expected to continue, and a favorable environment for freelance IT consultants is predicted to persist.
