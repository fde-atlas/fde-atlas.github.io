FDE | We come to your floor and solve it. Fast, affordable, secure AI adoption

TIMEWELL · 2026-08-13 · 14 min read

Summary

TIMEWELL describes its forward deployed engineering service: engineers work on-site, deliver a running prototype on day one using anonymized real data, and hand over a system the client's team can run within 45 to 90 days. Pricing is roughly half or less of large-firm contract development, based on a time-boxed fee plus product usage rather than accumulated hours. The company holds ISO/IEC 27001 certification, and its TRAFEED export-control AI service is used by over 20 organizations including universities. The article stresses data cleaning, knowledge graph construction, weekly iteration, returning shared patterns to its products (ZEROCK, TRAFEED), and distinguishing FDE work from contract development, staff augmentation, and consulting.

Key points

  • Day-one prototype built on anonymized real data from existing product components.
  • 45–90 day engagements ending when the client's team runs the loop independently.
  • Time-boxed pricing at roughly half or less of large-firm contract development.
  • ISO/IEC 27001 certified; TRAFEED used by 20+ universities and public institutions.
  • Heavy focus on digitizing paper, forms, and coarse data into AI-usable knowledge graphs.

A prototype on day one, half or less of large-firm pricing, and an ISO/IEC 27001 certified system

What a workplace stuck on AI adoption needs is not documents or proposals but people who come, build, and fix. We promise three things.

Fast

A running prototype on day one and a new version every day. We set the end, 45 to 90 days, before we start. We build on components already running in ZEROCK and TRAFEED, so nothing starts from zero.

Affordable

95% of AI adoption stalls on the floor. That is why you need a team that comes to the floor

When postings grow fiftyfold and 38 companies line up, most are contract development renamed 'FDE'. The real thing is told by one question: is learning returning to the product so that hours per customer fall?

An FDE is an engineer who comes to your workplace and turns the problem into running software

The medical analogy is a house call. Instead of waiting for the patient at the hospital, go to the home, examine on the spot, treat on the spot. Instead of waiting for a specification, put an incomplete working artifact down on day one, ask what is different, fix it the next day. That loop draws out the requirements that never surface in a meeting room.

  • 01AskAsk executives, the division, and the floor; sit at the workstation and catch the steps and exceptions nobody explained.
  • 02BuildAssemble a first version on our product components in a few hours, running on your anonymized real data, and put it in front of the person on site.
  • 03Return to the productPatterns other companies also have go into the product as standard features; your own branches stay as your extensions. The next site gets faster.

We turn paper drawings, forms, and coarse-grained data into a proper database, thoroughly

Most AI adoption stalls not at the model but before the data: still on paper, still in PDFs, still in someone's head. We do not skip this part. We sit on the floor and shape the data with our own hands until AI can use it.

Digitizing paper, PDFs, and images

Paper drawings, handwritten daily reports, scanned PDFs, photos. We structure them with our reading components and fill in what could not be read by checking on site.

Cleaning and reconciling spreadsheets and forms

Tables written differently by each person, part numbers that differ by department, inconsistent spellings. We set the rules, unify them, and record them in your business glossary.

Filling in coarse-grained data

Work that runs on 'roughly' and 'the usual' gets turned into numbers and categories through interviews and observation, at a granularity AI can use for decisions.

A database AI can actually use

The cleaned data, together with your terms and how your work connects, becomes a database AI can traverse (a knowledge graph). Only then does AI work on the floor.

Prototype on Monday, sort on Friday. Repeat this week for 45 to 90 days until your people run it themselves

We do not run phases in series. Asking, building, reading the reaction, and returning to the product happen in parallel every week.

  • Free consultation

    30 minutes to confirm the problem and the conditions

    We ask what executives, division heads, and the shop floor want to change, and confirm the conditions for speed: access to data and the person's time. If it is not a fit, we say so on the spot.
  • Monday

    Ask the three layers, find the data owner, put down the first version in the evening

    A first version running on anonymized real data is built on our product components in a few hours and put in front of the person on site.
  • Tue–Thu

    Collect exceptions by observation, one version a day

    Sit at the workstation, catch the unexplained steps, ship one spec-driven version a day. Take a provisional measurement on Thursday.
  • Friday

    Make groups, update the dictionary, return patterns to the product

    Turn the week's fragments into structure, update your dictionary of nouns and verbs, send shared patterns to the product and your own branches to your extensions.
  • Weeks 4–12

    Introduce writes gradually and leave with your team running it

    From around week four the person on site edits the dictionary. When the loop turns on days our engineer is not there, we are done.

Write integrations with core systems and connections that need security review do not reach production in a week. The review runs in parallel while we work on anonymized data.

Contract development builds to the spec. FDE stays on the floor and fixes it until results show

This is not about FDE being better or worse. Work with fixed requirements that needs building once is cheaper and more certain as contract development. FDE suits work whose requirements are buried on the floor and only surface while something is running.

We decline requests that do not fit, leave the glossary with you, and set the end condition first

The more companies call themselves FDE providers, the clearer the failure patterns become. Three failures, and how we prevent each.

Becoming the odd-job person

Every request from the floor gets a yes, the team drowns in one-off fixes, and nothing accumulates.

We decline the wrong request with reasons. Additional requests: patterns at no extra charge; your own branches absorbed by configuration or declined.

Shelfware

The deliverable stays specific to you, and when the person changes nobody can update it.

We update the dictionary of nouns and verbs weekly and have the person on site edit it from around week four. The contract says the dictionary is yours.

Key-person dependency

Nothing runs without one particular engineer, so the residency never ends.

We set the end condition first: a week when the loop ran without our engineer on site. If it does not, we revisit the design rather than extend.

Questions we are often asked about FDE

How is this different from contract development?

Phases are not run in series, outcomes are measured by the person's daily metrics rather than acceptance, learning returns to our products, and the incentive to stay is kept out of the contract. A contractor earns more the longer they stay; an FDE creates more value the sooner they leave.

Who owns the business knowledge you learn on site?

It is split three ways. Patterns shared by other companies (such as multi-stage approval) return to our products as standard features. Procedures unique to you stay as your extensions. The contents of the dictionary of your nouns and verbs belong to you. It is written into the contract.

Is it true you ship code on day one?

We put down an incomplete first version running on your anonymized real data. It is not a finished product but a device for drawing out the answer to 'what is different?' Anything that writes goes in only after permissions, prior simulation, human involvement, and audit records are in place.

We require a security review. Can you handle that?

Yes. We arrange at the first meeting to run the review in parallel and work on anonymized data while it is pending. We are ISO/IEC 27001 certified and can operate in a domestic region or inside your environment.

What does it cost?

Two parts: product usage and a deployment fee for a 45-to-90-day period. We design pricing at half or less of large-firm contract development as a guideline, but it varies with scope and conditions. A quote follows the free consultation.

Can we engage you for advisory only?

Yes. We take architecture design reviews and monthly retained technical advisory for products under development, separately from embedded development. Ask in the free consultation.

We have our own engineers. Can we build together?

Yes, and we prefer it. The sooner your engineers learn our components and how to update the dictionary, the sooner you run it yourselves. From around week four your side does the updating.

Can you work remotely?

Sitting on the floor is the condition for speed, so the first week and observation days are in person. The rest of the development and the weekly loop run remotely. Location is not a constraint.

What is the contract form?

A quasi-mandate (Civil Code Art. 656) as the base, stating the term (45 to 90 days), outcome metrics, and ownership (patterns to our product; branches and the dictionary to you). We do not sign contracts that end at acceptance alone.

Which industries do you work with most?

Manufacturing (drawings, quotes, processes, quality records), workplaces handling IoT and robotics data, and work involving export control and economic security. The more our product components apply as-is, the faster we move.

Write one line about the problem and send it

'We want quotes from drawings to go faster' or 'we want sensor data to reach the people making decisions' is enough. We will reply with times for a free 30-minute consultation. A rough topic is fine; we can start from there.
