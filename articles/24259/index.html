What are the best books for FDEs? How to choose by skill and reading order by career background [FDE Research Institute]

Summary

This Japanese article from FDE Soken organizes ten recommended books for forward deployed engineers (FDEs) along three axes the site defines: problem definition, data implementation, and customer trust. It recommends starting with Kazuo Ataka's issue-framing book and Martin Kleppmann's Designing Data-Intensive Applications, chosen because they apply across roles, age well, and can be tested immediately. Reading order varies by experience, current role, English ability, and client company size, prioritized in that order. The piece also contrasts FDE learning paths with SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer backgrounds, warns that books alone cannot make someone an FDE, and suggests supplementing with Palantir and OpenAI public materials.

Key points

  • Ten books organized by three axes: problem definition, data implementation, customer trust.
  • First picks: 'Ishue kara Hajimeyo' and 'Designing Data-Intensive Applications'.
  • Reading order prioritized as experience > role > company size > English ability.
  • Enterprise-facing FDEs should read trust books earlier; startup-facing ones data books.
  • Books alone are insufficient; practice plus Palantir/OpenAI public info recommended.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineering role that embeds itself in customer environments and handles everything consistently, from identifying problems to implementation and establishing operations. In Japanese, it is sometimes called "zenpo tenkai engineer" (forward deployment engineer), and the role is said to have been popularized by Palantir Technologies as a job category. Because FDE work spans multiple specialized fields, searching for books often leads to a state where "everything seems relevant, but I don't know where to start." In this article, FDE Research Institute organizes books along three original axes (problem definition, data implementation, and customer trust) and presents reading orders tailored to years of experience, current role, English proficiency, and company size.

Best books for FDEs: Which should you read first?

FDE Research Institute recommends two books as a starting combination: "Issue-Driven" and "Designing Data-Intensive Applications." FDE work is the product of "deciding which questions to solve" and "implementation that connects data and systems." This view aligns with Palantir's official engineering blog, which introduces the Forward Deployed Software Engineer as a role that engages with customer problems.

Three criteria for choosing the first two books

FDE Research Institute selected the first two books based on three points: "usable across roles," "unlikely to become outdated," and "testable from next week."

  • Usable across roles: FDEs move between customer business owners, executives, and their own company's development teams. A book that only helps one side is not suitable as a first book.
  • Unlikely to become outdated: Books covering specific cloud services or how to use LLMs (large language models) become outdated with each new edition. For the first two books, we chose books that deal with principles and ways of thinking.
  • Testable from next week: The approach of "Issue-Driven"—narrowing down questions before analyzing—can be tried immediately in your next customer interview. The ideas about data models and consistency in "Designing Data-Intensive Applications" serve as decision-making material when considering how to connect with existing systems.

Which books should you choose by the skills FDEs need?

Of FDE Research Institute's three axes (problem definition, data implementation, and customer trust), the shortcut is to pick one book at a time starting from your weakest axis. The three-axis framework was created by organizing the job descriptions on Palantir's careers page into customer-facing elements and technical elements.

Problem definition axis: Books that extract the questions to solve from customers' words

To strengthen the problem definition axis, the most efficient order is to learn how to narrow down questions with "Issue-Driven" and how to ask questions with "The Mom Test."

  • Issue-Driven: Based on the premise that the quality of questions determines results, it covers the flow of forming hypotheses before designing analyses.
  • The Mom Test: Based on the premise that customers are polite and don't speak their true thoughts, it presents question patterns for eliciting facts about past behavior. It's in English but short and easy to read.
  • 考える技術・書く技術 (The Art of Thinking and Writing): Complements the ability to structure hearing results into reports that decision-makers can read in a few minutes.

Like VACAN, which provides a service for visualizing vacancy and congestion information, in businesses where on-site facility operations are directly connected to data, it is common for the words that come out as requests to differ from the actual issues. Books on this axis help you acquire "question patterns" for noticing that gap.

Data implementation axis: Books that connect existing systems with AI

For the data implementation axis, use 『データ指向アプリケーションデザイン (Designing Data-Intensive Applications)』 as the foundation, and add 『AI Engineering』 if using generative AI in customers' business operations.

Customer trust axis: Books that establish adoption and sustain relationships

The customer trust axis is an area that tends to be neglected by people with long experience in technical roles. It is effective to start reading with 『The Trusted Advisor』.

FDE results are often measured not by "whether it was delivered" but by "whether it continues to be used in the customer's business." 『The Trusted Advisor』 presents a framework for thinking separately about the components of trust, and 『INSPIRED』 covers how to determine what to build together with the customer. 『チームトポロジー (Team Topologies)』 is useful when considering how to design the boundaries between the customer's teams and your own.

How does the reading order change based on years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size?

According to FDE Soken's recommendation, those with less than 3 years of experience start with the implementation axis, and those with 5 or more years start with the problem definition axis. As evidence, starting with OpenAI's careers page, many companies hiring FDEs post job descriptions that require both technical fundamentals and the ability to engage with customers.

How does the reading order change by company size?

According to FDE Soken's recommendation, FDEs serving enterprise customers read the customer trust axis early, while FDEs serving startup customers read the data implementation axis early.

  • When serving enterprise customers: Because there are many stakeholders and decisions proceed through multiple layers, include 『The Trusted Advisor』 and 『考える技術・書く技術 (The Art of Thinking and Writing)』 within the first two books.
  • When serving startup or SMB customers: Because you often handle everything from design to implementation alone, move 『Fundamentals of Data Engineering』 and 『AI Engineering』 up in the order.
  • For companies considering adopting FDEs in-house: Decision-makers will find it easier to define hiring requirements if they first use 『チームトポロジー (Team Topologies)』 to think about the boundary between the FDE team and the existing development organization.

Priority when conditions conflict

When conditions conflict, FDE Soken determines the reading order with the priority "years of experience > current role > company size > English ability."

For example, if someone is "good at English but has short experience," they solidify implementation fundamentals with 『データ指向アプリケーションデザイン (Designing Data-Intensive Applications)』, which has a Japanese translation, before reading English originals. English ability affects how books are chosen (original or translation), but what determines which axis to start with is the lack of experience. Conversely, if someone is "experienced but weak in English," they use 『イシューからはじめよ (Start from the Issue)』 as the first problem definition book instead of the English original 『The Mom Test』.

Can books alone make you an FDE? Differences in learning approaches compared to SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers, and who is not suited?

Compared to the four roles of SWE, IT consultant, PdM, and SIer, FDE places greater weight on hands-on implementation experience, making a transition through books alone difficult. Even at Palantir, considered the archetype of the FDE, their engineering blog repeatedly emphasizes growth through practical work at customer sites.

Differences in learning approaches compared to the four roles

The difference between FDE and the four roles is that FDE presupposes the experience of "building things yourself at the customer's site and seeing them through to actual use."

Characteristics of people not suited to FDE

FDE is not a good fit for people who want to build only after specs are finalized, and for those who strongly want to avoid traveling to or being stationed at customer sites.

  • People who cannot act without clear specifications: In FDE, much of the specification is decided through dialogue with the customer.
  • People who want to keep digging deep into a single technical area: FDE often involves covering a broad range of areas shallowly and quickly; if you want to deepen your specialty, SWE is a better fit.
  • People who find it hard to accept candid complaints from customers: In the early stages of deployment, this role often puts you in the position of hearing customer complaints directly.

Even if one of these applies to you, there is still value in learning the FDE way of thinking. However, before reading further, we recommend pausing to consider whether you actually want to change jobs.

Three steps to turn what you read into practical experience

To turn the book's content into practical experience, follow the three stages recommended by the FDE Research Institute: "read → try it internally → record it."

  • For each book, decide on "one pattern to use tomorrow": For example, with *The Mom Test*, drill down to a concrete action such as "prepare three questions that ask about past behavior for the next meeting."
  • Treat another internal department as your customer and practice: Listen to the challenges of sales or operations teams, and build small tools or dashboards for them to use.
  • Record four items: situation, decision, result, and limitations: These records become concrete examples for your resume and interviews when you change jobs.

In businesses like VACAN, where on-site operations and the service are closely connected, observing on-site operations before reading specifications often reveals key issues faster. Try applying the patterns you learn from books in such observational settings.

How to fill the gaps left by books with public information from Palantir and OpenAI?

Palantir's engineering blog and Palantir Learn, and OpenAI's careers page, let you verify the reality of FDE work from primary sources. Use books for the principles and companies' public information to supplement the latest picture of actual practice.

What you can verify from Palantir's public information

Palantir's public information lets you verify the thinking behind the role that became the prototype of FDE, and how to use its data platform products.

  • Palantir Engineering Blog: Articles are published introducing differences between engineering roles and ways of working at customer sites.
  • Palantir Learn: Official training for learning Palantir products, where you can learn hands-on the flow of data integration and application building.
  • Palantir Official Documentation: Product specifications can be referenced as primary sources.

What you can verify from OpenAI's public information

On OpenAI's careers page, you can verify the experience and skills required for FDE in the generative AI field from the wording of job postings.

Even for jobs with the same title, FDE job postings vary in the weight each company places on different skills. Since applications and selection processes can only be completed through official careers pages, always confirm final conditions on the official page. Job availability and content change frequently, so this article only organizes the thinking as of November 2026.

Four steps to combine books with public information

Combine books and public information in this order: "break the job posting into three axes → read books on the axes where you're lacking → practice with public training materials → cross-check again."

  • Choose one FDE job posting from a company you're interested in, and sort each requirement into the three axes: problem definition, data implementation, and customer trust.
  • For the axis where you have the most requirements you lack confidence in, read one of the 10 books in this article.
  • Try what you read in practice using public training materials like Palantir Learn or small internal projects.
  • After three months, reread the same job posting and check whether you can now explain more of the requirements.
