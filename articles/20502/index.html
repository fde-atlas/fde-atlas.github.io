London FDE Salaries: Breaking Down the Forward Deployed Engineer Pay Gap

Original title: 伦敦 FDE 薪资：前线部署工程师工资差距解析

FDE Times · 2026-10-21 · 5 min read

Summary

This article compares three London job postings all titled Forward Deployed Engineer, with base salaries ranging from £65,000 to £255,000 — nearly a fourfold gap. The author argues the difference tracks the nature of the work rather than the title: higher-paying roles involve building production systems inside customer environments, plus travel and office requirements. Anthropic pays £225,000–£255,000, well above Indeed's London average of £114,000, while Arva AI and Adverity sit below it. The piece also warns about equity trade-offs and conflicting salary figures on job aggregators.

Key points

  • Anthropic's London FDE base salary is £225,000–£255,000, exceeding Indeed's £183,665 upper bound.
  • Arva AI offers £80K–£110K plus 0.10%–0.30% equity; Adverity lists £65,000–£90,000 plus bonus.
  • Higher salaries correlate with building and deploying systems in customer environments, not advisory work.
  • Anthropic requires 25–50% travel; Arva AI requires 4–5 days per week in the office.
  • Adverity's posting shows conflicting figures (£25K–£35K vs £65K–£90K), so verify on the company's own page.

Key Takeaways

  • Anthropic's London FDE role has a base salary of £225,000–£255,000, even higher than Indeed's recorded London FDE salary ceiling of £183,665.
  • Arva AI offers £80K–£110K plus 0.10%–0.30% equity. Adverity's job description states £65,000–£90,000 plus bonus, but the top of the page shows a different figure. Both fall below Indeed's average of £114,000.
  • Among these three postings, the higher the salary, the more the work leans toward building systems in client environments. The highest-paying role also requires 25–50% travel.

London has three job postings, all titled Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), yet their base salaries differ dramatically. Anthropic offers £225,000–£255,000 per year. Adverity's job description states £65,000–£90,000 plus a performance bonus, though the top of the job aggregator page shows a different range.

At first glance, it's easy to conclude that AI labs simply pay more than everyone else. But reading each posting carefully reveals that each number corresponds to its own type of work and conditions: what you'll build, how long you'll spend at the client's site, how many days a week you'll be in the office, and a slice of equity that may never materialize.

If you're an FDE, or want to become one, this way of reading matters more than the numbers themselves. It can help you fairly compare two offers, and it can tell you what to practice to reach the highest salary tier.

Where does the market stand?

Start with the baseline. Indeed UK puts the average London FDE salary at £114,000 per year, with a common range of £70,759 to £183,665.

On its own, this range is so wide it's almost meaningless. The upper bound is about 2.6 times the lower bound, showing that the label "London FDE" mixes together very different jobs.

The figures from two other sources differ, mainly because they measure different things. Levels.fyi gives a median of £143,990, but that's total compensation: base salary, annualized equity, and bonus. FDE Academy gives a base salary range of £65,000–£110,000, but doesn't state its data source.

This table reveals a very common mistake: comparing Levels.fyi's £143,990 against a posting that lists only base salary, then concluding you're underpaid. These two numbers don't measure the same thing.

Place the three postings within Indeed's range, and each one's position becomes clear. Based on the figures in the job descriptions, Adverity's lower bound falls below the bottom of the range.

Arva AI's upper bound of £110,000 is still below the average. By contrast, Anthropic's lower bound exceeds the top of the range by £41,335; even counting base salary alone, it's £81,010 above Levels.fyi's median total compensation.

So AI lab salaries don't represent the market rate. They sit far above what most London FDEs actually earn.

Does salary track the amount of "building"?

The postings don't explain why each company pays what it does. But putting the three job descriptions side by side, one pattern is hard to ignore: the higher the salary, the more the work leans toward building systems in client environments.

At Adverity, the FDE is a domain expert who accompanies clients through onboarding with the company's latest AI product, helping them see value as quickly as possible. This is closer to solutions engineering or customer success than to building systems.

At Arva AI, the FDE owns the client's entire deployment: from first contact, through implementation and launch, to post-launch iteration. They also translate client problems into product requirements—in other words, feeding back into the product roadmap.

At Anthropic, the FDE goes directly into the client's systems and builds production applications using Claude models.

Three postings aren't enough to prove a rule. But they offer a practical way of reading: before looking at the salary, count the verbs. "Guide," "advise," and "help onboard" point to one kind of work; "build," "deploy," and "own" point to another—and the two seem to be paid very differently.

What else does £225,000 require beyond skills?

The rest of the compensation package is about how you live. Anthropic's posting states that this role requires frequent client visits, taking up 25–50% of your time, building products alongside clients, and it also requires office work.

A year has 52 weeks, which works out to roughly 13 to 26 weeks of living out of a suitcase. That cost is paid in time away from family, and it should go on the scale from the moment you start comparing two offers.

Arva AI requires a different kind of presence: on-site work in central London, 4–5 days a week in the office. In other words, nearly the entire workweek spent in the office.

Startups compensate with equity, but how much it's worth, nobody knows

Arva AI's base salary is below Indeed's average, but it comes with 0.10%–0.30% equity. This is a common startup trade-off: take less cash today in exchange for a stake that could be worth a lot in the future.

The equity range itself is worth noting. 0.30% is three times 0.10%. Imagine two people both earning £95,000: the one who negotiated 0.30% holds three times the equity of the other, with identical paychecks.

Arva also explicitly states it's willing to pay above the range for exceptional candidates. So £110,000 isn't quite a ceiling. If you can produce evidence of having owned a deployment end to end, you have leverage to negotiate both salary and equity.

Don't trust the first number you see

Adverity illustrates a very common trap. On the job aggregator site, the top of the posting shows £25,000–£35,000, while the body text states £65,000–£90,000 plus bonus. Both can't be right, and from the aggregator page alone, there's no way to tell which is the real offer.

The lesson is simple: read both the top and the body of a posting; when they conflict, verify on the company's own careers page, or ask the recruiter directly in the first round.

For roles with bonuses, ask what targets the bonus is based on, because an FDE measured on successful client onboarding will work very differently from an FDE measured on revenue.

The path to the highest salary tier runs through clients' systems

If the pattern in these three postings holds, what's most worth accumulating early isn't the FDE title, but experience building and running software in someone else's environment. You can start from your current job.

Actively participate in customer integration projects, take over the investigation of deployment incidents, or follow a feature from the moment a customer raises the issue all the way through to after it ships. Each time you do this, you add another concrete story to your resume.

On your resume, make these experiences easy for FDE recruiters to read. Don't write "collaborated with stakeholders"; instead, be specific: which customer you worked for, what their systems looked like, what you built and delivered to production, and what you fixed after launch.

In London, at least across these three postings, the gap between £65,000 and £255,000 isn't about the title. It's about whether you stand beside the customer as an advisor, or sit inside the customer's systems and build.
