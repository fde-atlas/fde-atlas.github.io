Practice questions for forward deployed engineer interviews: discovery role-play, ambiguous system design, live debugging, AI/RAG design and how to prepare.

Note: We wrote the questions below to show the kinds of skills FDE interviews test. They aren't questions from any specific company. Interview loops vary, so read each job description carefully.

Short answer: FDE interviews usually test four things:

  • Coding, often practical rather than puzzle-style.
  • Ambiguous problem solving and system design.
  • Customer skills, such as discovery, explaining trade-offs and handling pushback.
  • For AI FDE roles, applied AI design, such as RAG, agents, evaluation and security.

Prepare for all four. Strong coders often fall short on the customer rounds. That fits how the role is described publicly: The Pragmatic Engineer defines an FDE as a software engineer who alternates between being embedded with customer teams and core product engineering teams (The Pragmatic Engineer). If the role is new to you, start with our guide on what a forward deployed engineer is.

Practice questions by round

Discovery and customer role-play

  • "Our support team wants 'AI'. What do you ask in the first 30 minutes?"
  • "The CIO says the pilot must go live in two weeks, but security hasn't approved it. What do you do?"
  • "Explain to a non-technical operations head why the chatbot sometimes gets answers wrong, and what you'll change."
  • "The customer wants a feature that would make the product worse for everyone else. How do you handle it?"

Ambiguous problem and system design

  • "A bank wants an assistant that answers staff questions from policy PDFs and a legacy SQL database. Design it."
  • "Data arrives as nightly CSV drops with schema changes. Design a reliable ingestion pipeline."
  • "The customer's environment is a locked-down VPC with no internet egress. How does your design change?"

Applied AI (for AI FDE roles)

  • "Retrieval quality is poor on tables and scanned forms. Walk through how you'd diagnose and fix it."
  • "Design an agent that can create Jira tickets but must never close them without approval."
  • "How would you measure whether this assistant is good enough to launch?"
  • "How do you defend against prompt injection coming from retrieved documents?"
  • "Usage cost tripled this month. Where do you look first?"

For question 11, a strong answer treats retrieved text as untrusted input. It mentions separating instructions from data, limiting what tools the agent can call, requiring approval for risky actions and testing with adversarial documents. OWASP's prompt injection and excessive agency pages are a good checklist. If you want the agent-design background, read what is agentic AI.

Practical coding

  • "Write a function that calls a paginated API, handles rate limits and writes results to a table."
  • "Here's a failing integration test against a mocked customer API. Find and fix the bug."

A worked example: question 1

A weak answer jumps straight to "we'll build a chatbot". A stronger answer sounds like this:

  • "Which team and which task? Show me the last ten tickets your agents struggled with."
  • "What does success look like in numbers: resolution time, deflection, quality scores?"
  • "Where does the answer live today: PDFs, a knowledge base, a CRM? Who owns that data, and can we access it?"
  • "What can go wrong? Who needs to approve this from security, legal and operations?"
  • "What is the smallest pilot we can ship in a few weeks, and how will we decide whether to expand it?"

Notice that none of those questions mention a model. Interviewers are checking that you start from the customer's problem.

Behavioural

  • "Tell me about a time you owned an outcome you didn't fully control."
  • "Describe a time you had to say no to a customer."

How to answer well: a simple structure

  • Clarify: users, success metric, constraints (data, security, timeline).
  • Scope: what you'll do first, and what you'll deliberately leave out.
  • Design: components, data flow, failure modes.
  • Risks: security, data quality, adoption.
  • Measure: how you'll know it works, and what you'll report.
  • Hand over: documentation, runbooks, training the customer's team.

Interviewers often care more about steps 1, 4 and 5 than about the diagram.

A 4-week preparation plan

  • Week 1: Coding fluency. Do daily API and data tasks, and write tests.
  • Week 2: System design in a customer context. Do two designs a week and say your clarifying questions out loud.
  • Week 3: Applied AI. Build or polish one RAG + agent project with an evaluation report.
  • Week 4: Mock loops. Do two full mock interviews, including a customer role-play, and get written feedback.

FAQ

What is asked in a forward deployed engineer interview?

Usually practical coding, ambiguous problem-solving or system design, customer communication and behavioural questions. AI FDE roles add RAG, agent and evaluation design.

Is the FDE interview harder than a software engineer interview?

It's broader rather than harder. You're judged on customer skills and ownership as well as code.

Do FDE interviews include LeetCode-style questions?

Some companies include algorithmic rounds, but many focus on practical, real-world coding. Check the job description and ask the recruiter.

How do I prepare for the customer role-play round?

Practise discovery questions, summarise what you heard, state trade-offs plainly and agree next steps. Record a mock and review it.

Are these real interview questions from Palantir or OpenAI?

No. They're practice questions Datamarcos wrote to cover the skills FDE roles describe publicly.
