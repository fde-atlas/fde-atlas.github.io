Week 24: Complete the controlled pilot, handover, and graduation defense

Original title: 第 24 周：完成受控试点、交接与毕业答辩

FDE 成长手册 · 2026-09-12 · 20 min read

Summary

Week 24 of the FDE handbook closes the Capstone loop. Based on the week-23 decision, learners either run a genuinely authorized pilot or a course-controlled simulation, logging every task, failure, safe terminal state, and human burden. A peer who did not build the system must deploy, monitor, and roll back using only the handoff package. The same evidence base then backs three defenses aimed at executives, frontline users, and engineers. Simulations cannot claim adoption or ROI; hard technical NO-GOs cannot be waived by the graduation date. A synthetic example shows scoring: 80/100 overall with five sub-gates at 60% each and zero hard blockers. The article ends with a failure checklist and post-course paths.

Key points

  • Week 24 turns week-23 decisions into an honest delivery loop: pilot or simulation, handoff, defense.
  • Unauthorized work must use the simulation track, which cannot claim adoption, efficiency, or ROI.
  • A non-author must deploy, handle a fault drill, and roll back using only the handoff package.
  • Graduation requires 80/100, five sub-scores at 60% each, and zero hard blockers like leaks or unrepeatable effects.
  • All three audience defenses must cite one shared evidence chain, not three separate success stories.

Direct answer: Week 24 closes Capstone M6 and M7 into one honest delivery loop: first, based on the Week 23 decision, choose a "real authorized pilot" or a "course-controlled simulation," recording tasks, failures, safe end states, and human burden one by one; then have someone who did not participate in development complete deployment, random re-verification, on-call duty, and rollback using only the handover package; finally, defend with the same set of evidence to three audiences — the boss, frontline staff, and engineers. Without real authorization, take the simulation track and state clearly that it cannot prove user adoption or ROI; if there is a hard technical NO-GO, the author must rescue things on-site, or prohibited effects occur, the graduation date cannot serve as a green light.

Which segment of the evidence chain is completed this week

Heading "Which segment of the evidence chain is completed this week"

Week 24 is not about adding more features, but about checking whether the evidence from the previous 23 weeks can be consumed by a real or credibly simulated delivery process:

业务决定与价值机制  → 一线任务、例外、保护与替代路径  → 数据、权限、RAG 与 MCP 边界  → 第 23 周发布决定  → 真实授权试点 或 课程受控模拟  → 任务结果、失败、人工负担与产品反馈  → 非作者部署、值守、降级、回滚  → 老板 / 一线 / 工程三听众答辩  → 毕业、补证、缩小或停止

The earlier evidence must not be "polished into a nicer story" in this week. When new contradictions are found, keep the original records, update the judgment, and clearly explain why it changed.

Heading "What is the difference between a real authorized pilot and a course-controlled simulation?"

Both tracks can train an FDE in the full delivery process, but they support different conclusions:

Real authorization must be written as a decision

Heading "Real authorization must be written as a decision"

At minimum, record:

[D] 授权角色、日期与适用组织→ 候选版本和允许任务→ 参与人数上限、时间窗与环境→ 允许的数据、外部效果和人工支持→ 监测、停止、回滚与通知条件→ 谁可以暂停，谁可以重新批准

Verbally saying "you can give it a try," getting a system account, or receiving a dataset does not equal full pilot authorization.

Frontline participant protection remains a hard gate

Heading "Frontline participant protection remains a hard gate"

Whether it is a real pilot or a simulation with peer participants, you must explain in advance the purpose, recording method, scope of visibility, and use; allow participants to skip, correct, stop, and withdraw; direct supervisors must not observe research targeted at individuals; do not pass identifiable quotes to management without consent; records must not be used for personal performance reviews or disciplinary action. Without data authorization, use only synthetic, sandboxed, or anonymized replays, and do not ask participants to bypass permissions or take on extra work they did not consent to.

When conditions are not met, the correct record is "pilot not conducted due to insufficient authorization or protection," then move to the course simulation — rather than quietly lowering the standard.

Mental model: look at the task, the system, and the handover capability at the same time

Heading "Mental model: look at the task, the system, and the handover capability at the same time"

Week 24 must answer three different questions:

  • Task layer: Does the goal the person needs to accomplish reach the correct end state? Refusal, escalation, or manual substitution is sometimes a safe success, but it does not mean the business task is complete.
  • System layer: Are quality, safety, latency, cost, and recovery signals still within the approved boundaries? Stop immediately if prohibited effects occur.
  • Handover layer: Without the original author, can another operator deploy, judge, mitigate, recover, and roll back?

Therefore, do not just count "how many answers were given." An honest task ledger must at least separate:

任务目标是否完成≠ 系统是否进入正确安全终态≠ 用户是否选择采用≠ 业务是否产生因果改善

Can you enter a pilot after a NO-GO?

Heading "Can you enter a pilot after a NO-GO?"

No. Only candidates and scopes explicitly permitted in Week 23 may enter a real pilot; when evidence is insufficient or invalid, "failure not yet proven" must not be interpreted as a GO either. Isolation exercises can train shutdown and handover, but cannot substitute for pilot authorization or graduation evidence.

First triage by decision:

Week 23 resultPermitted Week 24 actions
GOEnter the corresponding track only within the scope authorized by the card
Limited GOKeep the restrictions; tasks, participants, data, tools and time must not be expanded on your own
Hard technical NO-GOCapstone Rubric still not passed; real pilots forbidden, shutdown or handover may only be practiced in an isolated environment, and it must not be passed off as M6 or graduation evidence
Invalid or insufficient evidence NO-GOWhen blind test leakage, missing denominators, non-reproducible evidence, or inability of a non-author to verify occurs, no pilot or graduation; supplement evidence first, and use a new independent sealed set if the blind test is compromised
Technical gate passed, but value, authorization or protection conditions NO-GOKeep the decision to stop a real pilot; you may complete the synthetic simulation and shutdown/handover capability, and finish the course within the simulated boundary

If what is being fixed is a hard technical NO-GO, you must return to Week 23 to generate a new candidate and a new final blind sealed by an independent custodian, then form release evidence. For an evidence-type NO-GO, if only the evidence index or independent verification is missing, fill it in and re-decide; if the blind test has leaked or the fix changed the candidate, a new sealed set is also required. The calendar moving from Week 24 to Week 25 does not change this requirement. If the technical gate has passed but the real pilot was stopped due to insufficient value, authorization or protection, do not fabricate a pilot; an honest simulation and shutdown handover can demonstrate course capability, but still cannot produce launch, adoption or ROI claims.

Once Week 24 substantially modifies code, model/prompts, corpus/index, ACL, policies, tools, MCP Server, schema or deployment package, the current activity is immediately paused: generate a new candidate and return to Week 23 to re-gather evidence. A real pilot also requires a role with authority to re-bind the authorization to the new candidate; authorization for the old version cannot be inherited automatically.

Denominator discipline for the real track

Heading "Denominator discipline for the real track"

If you need to describe usage behavior, record at least separately:

  • the number of people meeting pilot eligibility;
  • the number invited and giving informed consent;
  • the number who actually initiated at least one task;
  • the number of task attempts;
  • the number of tasks reaching the target final state;
  • the numbers of abandonments, refusals, escalations, failures and human takeovers;
  • the observation time window and data sources.

Even if someone keeps using the system in a real pilot, you may only describe observations within this sample and window. Without a control and causal design, do not attribute changes to the system; without a full-scope cost accounting, do not calculate or promote ROI.

Denominator discipline for the simulation track

Heading "Denominator discipline for the simulation track"

Use "scripted tasks," "role-play steps" and "drill operators"; do not write "users," "adoption rate" or "production tasks." Human time is simulated execution time, not the real burden of target employees.

Lower human burden is not always better. Some high-risk steps should inherently require human approval; what to judge is whether the burden is necessary, acceptable, visible, and who bears it — not deleting protections for the sake of automation rate.

Product feedback is not a license to change features mid-activity. Once code, model/prompts, corpus/index, ACLs, policies, tools, Server/schema, or the deployment package changes substantively, stop the current activity, generate a new candidate, and return to Week 23 to re-gather evidence; the real track must also re-bind authorization.

Step 5: Let a non-author take over using only the handoff package

Heading "Step 5: Let a non-author take over using only the handoff package"

The handoff recipient must not have participated in the implementation. You can ask a peer, mentor, or open-source reviewer to do this in a synthetic environment; if you can't find a second person, you may self-test first, but you must not record the self-test as a passed non-author handoff.

Week 18 already taught how to write runbooks and design rollbacks; this week is not about rewriting them, but about having a non-author consume the complete Capstone handoff package. The reviewer should draw normal, refusal, permission, and write tasks from the versioned handoff random re-verification pool, and randomly inject one fault into the model, index, or MCP, checking whether the old documentation can truly guide a new maintainer.

This re-verification pool is not the Week 21–23 final blind. The final blind was unblinded after its first run in Week 23 and can only continue to serve as regression; the handoff random draw only proves that a non-author can operate and re-verify, and does not produce a new blind-test conclusion. If a new blind test is still needed, it must be built and sealed by an independent custodian.

The handoff package must contain at least:

范围、非目标、负责人和升级路径→ 架构、数据、权限与版本 manifest→ 部署前检查和明确部署步骤→ 正常 / 拒答 / 升级 / 受控写入冒烟任务→ 仪表盘、告警、开关和 runbook→ 数据迁移、索引、策略、MCP 与外部状态回滚→ 结果不明时的对账→ 回滚后的任务级复验→ 当前 NO-GO、未知和已接受风险

Handoff test rules

Heading "Handoff test rules"

  • The author hands over the package first; the operator reads it independently first;
  • The operator deploys in a clean environment and records every blocking point;
  • The author must not use verbal hints to paper over documentation gaps before the record is made;
  • The operator draws cases from the versioned handoff random re-verification pool, then does one on-call session for an authorized fault drill whose type and timing they did not know in advance; this random pool does not masquerade as the final blind;
  • The operator performs a full versioned package rollback;
  • After the rollback, re-run normal, refusal, escalation, and controlled writes, and reconcile external state;
  • If it fails, fix the package and have a non-author re-execute; do not manually change the result to pass in the table.

"The non-author understood the documentation" is not evidence; the state changes they actually completed and the re-verification are.

Three framings of the Beichen completion example

Heading "Three framings of the Beichen completion example"

Boss version

What this course simulation supports is "whether it's worth applying for a restricted policy-confirmation pilot," not a company-wide rollout. Of the 12 scripted tasks, 8 completed the scripted role's goal, all 12 reached the expected safe terminal state, and all human steps totaled 36 minutes. Since there was no real authorization, these numbers cannot prove adoption, efficiency, or ROI; a real pilot next would require re-approving participants, data, systems, and observation metrics.

Frontline version

The system only answers based on current, visible, and sourced policies. When evidence is insufficient, outdated, or unauthorized, it explains why and hands off to a human; before creating a ticket it shows a preview, and after parameters change it requires re-confirmation. Participants in drills can skip, correct, or stop at any time, and records are not used for performance evaluation.

Engineering version

rc4 binds the corpus, retrieval, prompts, policies, MCP, evals, and deployment versions. In the isolated simulation, approval changes, cross-role actions, and unclear results all entered predefined states; a non-author, using the handoff package, completed a clean deployment, an on-call index fault, and a rollback from the faulty configuration to a known-good version, then re-ran the four task types after the rollback.

All three passages cite the same track card, 12 tasks, rc4 manifest, and handoff records. The boss version doesn't dress the simulation up as real value, the frontline version doesn't hide failures, and the engineering version doesn't pass the role card off as actual production.

Step 7: Build the graduation evidence index

Heading "Step 7: Build the graduation evidence index"

Graduation is not about file counts; it's about a stranger reviewer being able to trace from a decision back to the original evidence:

M0 问题与风险合同├─ 业务决定、价值机制、一线任务、保护与非目标M1 数据与访问├─ 语料、ACL、候选隔离与缓存M2 有引用问答├─ 回答、拒答、冲突、过期与失败分类M3 受控 MCP├─ 预览、逐次审批、写入、幂等、对账与审计M4–M5 发布证据├─ 冻结候选、盲测、安全、负载成本、故障与决定M6–M7 试点 / 模拟与交接└─ 任务、失败、人工负担、非作者运营、答辩与复盘

The final decision can only be one of:

  • Graduate: the technical hard gates and project Rubric have passed, all applicable thresholds for the current track have verifiable evidence, and the conclusion's boundaries are honestly stated;
  • Supplement evidence: the implementation may be correct, but non-author reproduction, denominator, authorization, or sources are incomplete;
  • Shrink: only a narrower task, data, tools, or population can pass;
  • Stop: the problem, protection conditions, or hard risks do not support continuing.

"Project Rubric passed" here follows the explicit thresholds of the Capstone contract:

  • Total score of at least 80 / 100;
  • Each of the five areas — RAG, MCP, security, evals, and operations — must reach at least 60% of that area's full score; high scores elsewhere cannot compensate;
  • If any one of the following exists — cross-tenant leakage, unauthorized writes, real secrets entering storage, duplicate external effects, or an unexecutable rollback — the Capstone fails outright.

When business value is insufficient, real authorization is missing, or protection conditions don't hold, a correct NO-GO or scope reduction can still demonstrate decision-making ability; as long as the technical hard gates and project Rubric have passed, the course can still be completed with an explicit simulation. A hard technical NO-GO caused by cross-scope leakage, unauthorized effects, duplicate effects, real secrets, or an impossible rollback means the Capstone has not passed and must be fixed and re-evidenced. Hiding blockers, tampering with thresholds, or faking a pilot is disqualifying on any track.

Common gates for all tracks

Heading "Common gates for all tracks"

  • The Week 23 decision permits the current activity; a NO-GO has not been bypassed by the calendar;
  • If Week 23 was a hard technical NO-GO, the current activity is only labeled as an isolated exercise or a shutdown handover, not masquerading as M6 or graduation;
  • If Week 23 evidence is invalid or insufficient, first complete re-review, denominators, or a new sealed blind test, without writing "unknown" as a pass;
  • Track, candidate, tasks, data, external effects, window, owner, and stop conditions are explicit;
  • Each task separately records the real-role goal or scripted-role goal and the safety end state, linked to verifiable evidence;
  • Failures, refusals, escalations, human takeovers, and abandonments have not been removed from the denominator;
  • Human burden states total minutes, task denominator, roles borne, and data sources;
  • Any prohibited fact or effect stops the activity and reopens the decision;
  • Any material change to the candidate pauses the activity, generates a new version, and returns to Week 23; a real pilot authorization re-binds the version;
  • A non-author completes deployment from a clean environment, and the author's ad-hoc prompts have not been hidden;
  • A non-author completes on-call, loss mitigation, recovery or degradation, full rollback, and task-level re-verification;
  • Rollback covers the applicable parts of code, configuration, corpus/index, policies, tool schemas, migrations, and external state;
  • The three defenses—boss, frontline, and engineering—cite the same evidence chain;
  • The graduation card distinguishes proven, unproven, unknown, limitations, and next review conditions;
  • The project total score is at least 80 / 100, and each of the five items—RAG, MCP, security, evals, and operations—reaches 60% of that item;
  • Cross-tenant leakage, unauthorized writes, real secrets entering the repository, duplicate external effects, and non-rollbackable states are all 0;
  • Public materials contain no real secrets, non-consented participant information, or customer data.

Additional gates for a real authorized pilot

Heading "Additional gates for a real authorized pilot"

  • An empowered role has explicitly authorized with dates, scope, and candidate version;
  • Participants know the purpose, recording, use, and visibility scope, and can skip, correct, stop, and withdraw;
  • The direct supervisor does not observe personal research, and records are not used for performance or disciplinary action;
  • Adoption or usage behavior has qualifying denominators such as conditions, invitations, consent, attempts, and tasks, plus a time window;
  • Conclusions are limited to the current sample and window, with no unfounded extrapolation or causal ROI.

Additional gates for the course-controlled simulation

Heading "Additional gates for the course-controlled simulation"

  • Only synthetic corpora, isolated services, and approved tools are connected;
  • Participating peers know this is a course exercise and are not described as customers or target employees;
  • Reports use accurate names such as "scripted tasks, role-play, simulated environment";
  • No claims of user adoption, customer satisfaction, production reliability, business improvement, or ROI.

Independent migration: supply chain material-shortage coordination

Heading "Independent migration: supply chain material-shortage coordination"

Migrate the Beichen policy confirmation to "a supply chain planner queries supplier lead times and substitute-material rules, and after per-instance approval creates a material-shortage escalation ticket." Independently complete:

  • Decide whether it is a real authorized procurement-team pilot or a synthetic supply chain role simulation;
  • Design 10–15 tasks covering normal queries, contract conflicts, expired lead times, unauthorized factories, parameter changes, unclear supplier API results, and stale indexes;
  • Separately record task completion, safety end states, human verification, escalation, and reconciliation burden;
  • Have someone not involved in the implementation deploy and run on-call once through a supplier API failure;
  • Roll back from the failed candidate to a known-good version and confirm no duplicate escalation tickets;
  • Defend separately to the supply chain lead, planners, and platform engineers.

Without real enterprise authorization, use synthetic suppliers, orders, factories, and escalation tickets. You may claim "completed a controlled supply chain simulation," but not claims of reducing line stoppages, improving supplier fulfillment, gaining adoption, or generating ROI.

Final check of the three-layer explanation

Heading "Final check of the three-layer explanation"

  • Boss version: Explain the supported decision, the value-realization mechanism, alternatives, current evidence, cost and human burden, hard risks, and continue-or-stop conditions; do not package simulations or short-term observations as ROI.
  • Frontline version: Explain which step of the work changes, how sources are verified, when it must refuse, re-approve, hand over to a human, or stop; how participants and feedback can exit, without treating guardrails as resistance.
  • Engineering version: Explain how a candidate version, data and permissions, evals, security, traces, load, failures, reconciliation, deployment, rollback, and non-author handover can be reproduced.

If any layer needs to hide facts from the other two to hold up, the evidence chain still hasn't graduated.
