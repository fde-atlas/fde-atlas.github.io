Training AI Talent at Financial Institutions | How to Develop Staff Who Understand Operations into In-House FDEs

Original title: 金融機関のAI人材育成｜事務を知る職員を社内FDEに育てる進め方

株式会社Aiworker · 2026-11-16 · 5 min read

Summary

This article by Aiworker argues that Japanese financial institutions, having largely adopted generative AI, now lack people who can build AI systems. It proposes training internal FDEs by pairing business-side staff who know operational procedures (from planning, operations centers, or branches) with system-department engineers who understand information governance. In a test with five fictional internal inquiries, AI produced a consolidated list in 128 seconds and draft answers with regulation citations for 4 of 7 items in 88 seconds. AI output remains material for human judgment: credit decisions and customer replies stay with staff. Recommended starting points are repetitive, rule-based internal tasks, with data-handling rules agreed in month one and a registry maintained for audits.

Key points

  • BOJ survey: 79.3% of institutions use generative AI; talent shortage is the next issue.
  • Pair business-savvy staff with system engineers as internal FDEs.
  • AI test: inquiries listed in 128s, draft answers with citations in 88s.
  • Credit decisions and customer responses remain with human staff.
  • Start with repetitive internal tasks; set data rules and keep a registry.

If you want to increase the number of people who build AI systems at financial institutions, the fastest path is to develop staff who know operations—such as operations planning, operations centers, and branch office operations—and pair them with staff from the systems department. What is delegated to AI extends only to draft responses to inquiries and draft credit approval documents. Credit decisions and responses to customers are made by staff even after the systems are in place. The first project should be chosen from internal operations that do not reach customers directly.

Why do financial institutions need AI builders in business departments?

Because the stage of simply using generative AI is nearly over, and the shortage of people who build systems has become the next challenge.

In a fiscal 2026 survey conducted by the Bank of Japan with 150 counterpart institutions, 79.3% of financial institutions were using generative AI, exceeding 90% including those in trial stages. Meanwhile, among the 136 institutions using or trialing it, 65.4% had DX or IT departments deciding on and executing company-wide adoption. Only 20.6% had a promotion organization directly under management overseeing the entire company.

However, the ones who know the detailed procedures and exceptions of operations are staff in headquarters operations departments and branch offices. When the same survey asked about issues requiring improvement going forward, "understanding and skills of business departments" was cited by 67 institutions and "development of promotion talent within DX/IT departments" by 66. Developing people on both the user side and the promotion side has become a challenge.

Who should be paired together for in-house FDEs at financial institutions?

Financial institutions are an industry with particularly strict requirements regarding the handling of customer information and accountability. Even if business department staff create a prototype, it cannot be used in production without passing information management review. Conversely, if only the systems department builds it, it tends not to match field procedures and ends up unused. Therefore, we develop two types in combination.

How far could AI get when tested with fictional operations inquiries?

We prepared five fictional operations inquiries from a fictional branch office and excerpts of fictional operations regulations, and asked an AI tool to process them. The formats varied: inquiry forms, phone memos, emails, and chat logs.

The AI organized the inquiries into a list in 128 seconds, splitting inquiries containing two questions into separate rows, turning 5 inquiries into 7 items. When we then asked it to attach draft responses based solely on the regulations, it attached draft responses and the relevant clause numbers to 4 items in 88 seconds. For the remaining 3 items, it did not write answers, stating that no applicable provisions existed. Since the names and account numbers in the inquiry texts appeared in the list, there was also a comment that they should be masked when sharing internally.

In other words, what AI creates is material for staff to make judgments. What matters is that staff who know the regulations and field circumstances review the content and can issue the next instructions. Note that this is the result of a single run using fictional data.

Which operations should you start with, and how should you continue?

For the first project, choose internal operations that occur repeatedly and have nearly fixed procedures. Candidates include draft responses to operations inquiries, drafting the standardized portions of credit approval documents, and classification of complaints and requests with monthly reporting. Credit decisions themselves are not chosen as a project.

Where things tend to stall is customer information and systems department review. In the first month, decide with the responsible department and the systems department the scope of information that may be entered and how to mask it. Also, since this is an industry with frequent personnel rotations, proceed with two people per department and record the name, creator, using departments, and data handled of each built system in a ledger. This ledger should serve as explanatory material when asked about usage during inspections or audits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can staff without programming experience be developed?

Yes, they can. Today AI supports the work of writing programs, so if you can explain in words the processing you want to build, you can create the initial system. In selecting people, tenure in the role and the ability to articulate specific problems matter more than experience.

Is there a risk of conveying AI errors to customers?

What AI creates is drafts and lists of points to verify. Responses to customers and credit decisions are made by staff verifying the content, just as before.

Shouldn't we just proceed with the systems department alone?

Urgent development can be outsourced to the systems department or external parties. However, our recommendation is to place staff in business departments who know the operations and can make fixes themselves, so you don't have to request help every time regulations change.
