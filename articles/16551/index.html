A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who works directly with a company's customers: first figuring out what the customer's real problem is, then personally building and deploying the system that solves it (often an AI system these days), and making it actually run within the customer's business. Unlike a Solutions Architect or a consultant, an FDE writes production code themselves and is accountable for the results when the system runs on the customer's real data.

What the job involves day to day, how it differs from similar roles, who is hiring now, and how to break in: all explained on one page, based on FDE Times hiring data and our guides.

What an FDE does every day

The role is called "forward deployed" because the engineer works where the customer is, rather than staying behind the product team. A typical week looks roughly like this:

  • The day's rhythm follows the customer. Customers often report issues asynchronously, so a good FDE starts by handling problems that arrived overnight, then sets the day's priorities at the customer's standup (not just their own company's standup). A day in the life of an FDE
  • Finding the real problem. What the customer asks for is only a hypothesis: "automate PR reviews" may ultimately be a problem of two reviewers being overloaded. Requirements discovery comes before writing code. How FDEs clarify requirements
  • Integrating messy systems. Enterprise AI pilots often stall because data is scattered across many disconnected systems. Inventorying, mapping, and reconciling the customer's data is core to the job, not chores to do before the real work begins. Customer data integration
  • Owning production. There is no handoff to an implementation team: if the customer's data pipeline breaks at 2 a.m., the FDE is the one who fixes it. FDE vs. software engineer
  • Being on-site with customers. Typically 25%–50% of the time is spent at the customer's site: Palantir is around 25%, while OpenAI and Istari Digital go up to 50%. Why most FDE roles can't be remote
  • Feeding lessons back into the product. A patch written for one customer may disappear when the project ends, or it may become a feature available to all customers; which outcome you get depends on how the FDE documents it and hands it back to the product team. From one customer's patch to a general feature

How the FDE differs from software engineers, Solutions Architects, and consultants

These four roles overlap, and many job postings are vague about it. The clearest test is: who writes the final code, and who is accountable when production breaks.

FAQ

Is being a Forward Deployed Engineer a good career?

Demand is strong right now: FDE Times has tracked 863 open FDE positions at 181 companies, of which 254 were posted in the last 30 days. Among US positions that list salaries, the median base salary is $200k (salary data). The trade-offs are travel (typically 25%–50% of your time at customer sites) and being accountable when the customer's production systems break. Career paths afterward include Principal FDE, product manager, engineering manager, and founding a startup (career paths after FDE).

Do FDEs need to write code?

Yes. Writing code is the entry barrier, with Python and full-stack development being the most common requirements (what hiring managers look for). Writing production code is also exactly what separates FDEs from Solutions Architects: the latter deliver architecture documents and often stop at the proof of concept (FDE vs. Solutions Architect).

What is the difference between an FDE and a Solutions Engineer?

Solutions Engineers and Solutions Architects mainly work pre-sale as technical consultants, usually stopping at demos or proofs of concept. FDEs come in after the deal is signed, deeply involved in launch and operations, and are accountable for production code at the customer site. Moving from Solutions Engineer to FDE means shifting from demo code to production code and taking responsibility for the final outcome of the deployment (From Solutions Engineer to FDE).

Which companies are hiring Forward Deployed Engineers?

The companies with the most open FDE positions are currently Palantir (95), Databricks (94), Amazon (AWS) (68), OpenAI (39), Salesforce (22), ByteDance (20), Rogo (19), and Scale AI (12). The FDE job tracker covers 181 companies, with data sourced from each company's official careers page.

How can I become a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Prove that you have delivered working systems in messy, real-world environments: FDE job postings ask what you have shipped, not about AI certificates (What FDE job postings look for). Recruiters use programming ability as a filter and customer communication skills for ranking; the FDE Times roadmap covers eight stages from fundamentals to career development. Entry-level FDE roles for new graduates do exist, but the entry bar is narrow (FDE roles for new graduates).

Can FDEs work remotely?

Mostly no. Of the 863 open positions tracked by FDE Times, only 24% support remote work. The job is designed around working side by side with customers, typically with 25%–50% of time on-site, and some air-gapped systems simply cannot be accessed remotely (Why FDEs travel).

How much do Forward Deployed Engineers earn?

Among 168 US FDE positions with listed salary ranges, the median base salary is $200k, with a common listed range of $154k–$236k. This is base salary only, excluding equity and bonuses (FDE salary data).

Are any companies in mainland China hiring FDEs?

Yes: of the 863 positions FDE Times tracks from the official careers pages of 181 companies, 31 are located in mainland China, from ByteDance (20), Tencent (10), and NVIDIA (1). Another city in the Asia-Pacific region with a notable concentration of positions is Singapore, with 17. See the FDE jobs page for the latest list.

What does FDE stand for? Where did the role come from?

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer; the Chinese version of this site translates it as "前线部署工程师". The model is most closely associated with Palantir: Palantir splits deployment work between two roles, Echo, an analyst familiar with the business domain, and Delta, an engineer who rapidly builds prototypes (Delta and Echo). Today, companies use several different titles for similar work (Six companies, six job titles).
