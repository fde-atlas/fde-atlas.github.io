How to Write an FDE Job Posting: Forward Deployed Engineer JD Template

Original title: FDE 招聘启事怎么写：前线部署工程师 JD 模板

FDE Times · 2026-11-04 · 6 min read

Summary

This guide dissects real FDE job postings from Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic to build a six-part template: mission, team goal, scope, concrete work, requirements, and logistics. Its core argument is specificity: name deliverables like MCP servers and sub-agents, identify customer stakeholders, and state numbers for travel (up to 25% at Palantir) and salary ($135,000–200,000 at Palantir; $185,000–325,000 for an OpenAI SF role). Team leads can use it to write postings; candidates can read postings backward to see what their resumes must prove, with each responsibility matched by a concrete deliverable.

Key points

  • Six-part template: mission, team goal, scope, concrete work, requirements, logistics.
  • Each responsibility line should pair a verb, a deliverable, and a customer stakeholder.
  • Separate hard requirements from nice-to-haves; Palantir asks only one year of experience.
  • Logistics must use numbers: travel caps, salary ranges, security clearances.
  • Candidates should map every posting responsibility to a resume line with a deliverable.

Key Takeaways

  • Palantir opens with its company mission, while OpenAI explains the FDE team's goal in a single sentence. Both approaches let candidates understand why the team exists.
  • A good job posting lists specific deliverables, such as MCP servers and subagents, and states exactly which people on the client side the engineer will work with.
  • The logistics section should include numbers: up to 25% travel time, security requirements specific to each business line, and salary ranges like $135,000–$200,000.

Anthropic's Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) job posting contains a line that spells out exactly what new hires will deliver to customers: MCP servers, subagents, and agent skills. The posting doesn't stop at vague phrases like "support integrations." Upon reading that line, candidates can immediately picture what a week in this job looks like.

Writing such a specific line isn't hard, but it requires a framework that tells you what question each section should answer. This guide builds a six-part framework, with each part modeled on a passage from a real job posting by Palantir, OpenAI, or Anthropic.

There are two moments when you'll use it. If you're an FDE about to become a team lead, sooner or later you'll need to write a job posting for your own team. If you want to transition into an FDE role, understanding the structure of job postings tells you what your resume needs to prove.

What will you build, and what do you need to prepare?

The final deliverable is a roughly one-page job posting divided into six parts: mission, team goal, scope of responsibility, specific work, qualifications, and logistics. The example running throughout this guide: suppose you work at a fictional startup that sells invoice-processing agents to banks.

All you need is a terminal and a text editor. Palantir's job posting is published on Lever and can be retrieved via the posting API. OpenAI's and Anthropic's job postings can be opened in a browser, on Built In, Greenhouse, and General Catalyst's job pages respectively.

Step 1: Read the originals before you write

Download Palantir's job posting and see how it divides its sections:

curl -s "https://api.lever.co/v0/postings/palantir/dab396d4-2f14-4796-aac0-0d82883dccf0" \
  | python3 -m json.tool > palantir_fdse.json

Check: Open the file and identify the section headings. The paragraph introducing the company mission appears before any role details. It's followed by "Core Responsibilities," "What We Require," "What We Value," and a separate salary section. For the other two job postings, just noting the section names is enough.

Step 2: Start with why the team exists

Palantir opens with a paragraph about what its software does: drug development, supply chains, finding missing children. OpenAI is more concise, using just one sentence in its "About the team" section: the FDE team works with customers to turn research breakthroughs into systems in production.

## 关于公司
[一段话：产品解决什么问题、为谁解决，附 2-3 个真实例子]

## 关于团队
FDE 团队与[客户类型]合作，把[产品能力]
变成[在生产环境中运行的成果]。

For the fictional startup, the team sentence could be: "The FDE team works with banks to take invoice-recognition agents from demos into banks' real reconciliation workflows." Check: This sentence must identify the customer, the product, and the end result. If any of the three is missing, rewrite it.

Step 3: State the scope of responsibility up front

Palantir states the autonomy in the very first sentence of its responsibilities section: you will work in a small team with end-to-end ownership of high-stakes projects. Candidates know their scope before reading the specific tasks. This ordering is worth copying.

Anthropic says in its role overview that it wants FDEs to work autonomously and thrive in ambiguous environments. Anthropic's Technical Deployment Lead posting goes further, defining the scope: from SOW all the way to production deployment.

## 岗位职责
你将在[规模]人的团队中工作，负责从[起点]
到[终点]的全部工作。遇到[模糊情形的类型]时，你需要自己做判断。

Check: The reader must be able to answer "When is my work done?" For the fictional startup, the work is done only when the agent is running on the bank's real reconciliation data, not when the demo passes.

Step 4: Write responsibilities as deliverables and people

This is the section most prone to vagueness. Palantir interweaves technical work with direct collaboration with client-side stakeholders, ranging from engineering teams all the way up to executives.

OpenAI's first list item reads: work closely with strategic customers to understand the details of their business problems and technical requirements. Anthropic calls out each type of deliverable one by one.

Vague phrasing

  • Support customers with integrations
  • Communicate well with stakeholders
  • Build AI solutions

Rewritten phrasing

  • Write MCP servers that connect agents to the bank's invoice system
  • Report reconciliation results to the head of operations every two weeks
  • Build subagents that triage problem invoices before they reach human review

The easiest technique to apply: every line should have a verb, a deliverable, and a person on the client side. Check: Cross out the company name. If the line would make sense on any engineering job posting, it isn't specific enough.

Step 5: Separate hard requirements from nice-to-haves

Palantir splits the two into separate sections, "What We Require" and "What We Value." Its experience bar is low: one or more years of relevant work experience after graduation.

OpenAI pairs its hard experience threshold with one behavioral trait: a preference for hands-on work and a willingness to iterate with customers. Anthropic's "You May Be a Good Fit If You Have" section states the experience threshold while explicitly encouraging former technical founders to apply.

## 必备要求
- [经验门槛，用数字写明]
- [与客户合作时可观察到的行为]

## 加分项
- [领域、工具]
- [希望吸引的非传统背景]

Check: For each line in the must-have requirements, ask whether it would unfairly filter out a class of excellent candidates. If so, move that line to the nice-to-haves.

Step 6: Write logistics with numbers

Palantir states the travel ratio in its qualifications section: up to 25% of your time on-site with customers, flexible depending on individual circumstances. Anthropic states travel as a range.

Salary is also given as numbers. Palantir's separate salary section estimates $135,000 to $200,000 per year. OpenAI's San Francisco posting states $185,000 to $325,000, and Anthropic's Technical Deployment Lead posting also gives a salary range.

Palantir's version for its US government business line shows how the same framework varies by domain. It adds one hard requirement: an active security clearance at the Top Secret level or above, and requires on-site work.

## 后勤安排
- 出差：最多 [X]% 时间在客户现场，[灵活程度]
- 领域特定要求：[安全许可、现场办公、语言]
- 薪资：[下限] – [上限] / 年

Check: Every line in this section must contain a number or a yes/no condition. With this information, candidates can judge for themselves whether they're a fit before applying.

Common Mistakes

The first mistake is letting behavioral standards devolve into adjectives like "energetic" or "proactive." OpenAI writes it as a verifiable behavior: iterating with customers. In interviews, you can then ask questions targeting that behavior.

The second mistake is using one job posting for all domains. A better approach is to keep the core content and add domain-specific hard requirements for each one, just as Palantir adds security clearance for its government version.

The third mistake is hiding the travel requirements. If an FDE only discovers after joining that they'll spend more time on-site with customers than expected, they most likely won't stay long.

Where is this skill actually used in practice?

Writing a job posting exercises exactly the ability needed at a customer kickoff meeting: turning vague requirements into scope, deliverables, and acceptance criteria. A team lead who can write "from SOW to production" in a job posting can write the same line in a project proposal.

If you're a job seeker, read the job posting backwards. Every specific responsibility should map to a line in your resume, with deliverables and the customer-side people you worked with.

For example: "Built an MCP server connecting internal systems and presented it to the head of operations." If you've been a founder, put that at the very top of your resume, because at least one company has explicitly said it's looking for that.

A job posting that describes a new hire's first week attracts the right people. A resume written the same way makes recruiters stop and read closely.
