Today's Judgment

Three kinds of infrastructure are emerging simultaneously around FDEs in China: enterprises are starting to build delivery teams through cross-role academies (Liulian Intelligence established an FDE Academy); local governments are starting to organize scenarios using three lists of "demand, capability, and demonstration" (Beijing released 240 application scenario projects); universities are starting to test students' abilities with real tasks (the Beijing university student OPC competition received 1,814 projects).

Together, these point to one judgment: FDE has moved from a job concept into the institutionalization stage of talent, projects, and public awareness. But stay clear-headed—training headcounts, project lists, and competition awards are not delivery outcomes; they must continue to pass through real tasks, customer acceptance, and asset reuse.

Key Signals

Liulian Intelligence establishes FDE Academy: cross-role closed bootcamp to build an internal delivery team

  • Source: Lei Tech
  • On September 12, Liulian Intelligence established its FDE Academy, with the first closed bootcamp lasting one week; the first cohort of 20 people came from roles including sales, R&D, after-sales, quality, supply chain, operations, IT, engineering, and production-sales. The curriculum structure: 30% workshops, 30% assessments, 40% hands-on projects; the company plans to incubate 30-50 hands-on FDE projects in the short term and build a thousand-person team in the long term.
  • Why it matters: Traditional hardware/ODM companies are beginning to transform forward deployed capability from "individual experience in pre-sales or R&D" into an internal talent program. The most instructive part is cross-role selection—closer to the real requirements of an FDE than hiring only programmers, because customer sites simultaneously involve business, product, data, systems, and organizational coordination. But the validation standard is equally clear: the eight-stage FDE talent program validation chain—cross-role selection → training workshops → stage assessments → controlled hands-on work → real customer projects → dual acceptance → reusable assets → cycle and revenue validation on the next project. Curriculum ratios and cohort sizes are inputs; sign-off rates and reuse rates are outcomes. Note: both the 30-50 projects and the thousand-person team are company plans.

Beijing releases 240 application scenarios in "three lists": demand, capability, and demonstration organized in tiers

  • Source: Beijing Municipal People's Government | Original link
  • On September 13, Beijing released its 2026 application scenario "three lists" totaling 240 projects: 40 demand projects (covering 13 domains, involving 9 government departments and 23 municipal state-owned enterprises), 160 capability projects (from 120 companies and 10 universities and research institutes), and 40 demonstration projects (emphasizing replicable solutions, standards, and norms).
  • Why it matters: This is the prototype of a city-level deployment funnel—the demand list defines problems, the capability list provides supply, and the demonstration list consolidates validated templates. But the missing middle layer is precisely where FDEs fit: completing each project with a task owner, data/system permissions, budgeted procurement, technical acceptance, business acceptance, and replication evidence. The full funnel from lists to delivery is: demand list → capability list → project matching → task/data/permission/budget freeze → minimal pilot → dual acceptance → demonstration list → cross-organization replication. Demonstration projects must retain original baselines and acceptance records; they cannot be promoted directly from event showcases.

University OPC competition and enterprise FDE hiring: both ends of the talent supply chain active at once

  • Source: The Beijing News; Zhaopin 360 job page
  • The Beijing university student OPC innovation competition received 1,814 projects, selected 30 "OPC Stars," and established a university student OPC community—the evaluation orientation explicitly states "real scenarios, real tasks, real delivery". At the same time, 360 NanoWork posted a "Forward Deployed Engineer" job listing, with responsibilities covering customer-side deployment, workflow/skill/knowledge base building, system integration, training, troubleshooting, and industry experience reuse.
  • Why it matters: The talent supply side (university competitions, communities) and the demand side (enterprise roles) are active simultaneously, and the evaluation language of both sides is converging—universities talk about "real delivery," while enterprise JDs talk about "experience reuse." This confirms an industry consensus: FDE development must pass through a seven-level validation of "courses → controlled tasks → real customers → professional review → customer sign-off → asset reuse → revenue/roles"—any academy, competition, or certificate can only prove entry. 1,814 projects are the hype; how many people can complete their first real delivery is the output.

Takeaways for FDE Practitioners

  • Learn "cross-role mixed teams" from enterprise internal training: Select business, IT, operations, and product staff from within the customer and mix them with external FDEs, running a 7-day controlled validation on one real task—closer to real-world practice than generic courses, and it also screens out the "rule owners" inside the enterprise.
  • Fill in the middle layer when engaging with government scenario lists: The lists provide entry points for supply and demand; the FDE's value-add lies in compressing projects into executable task packages of "owner + permissions + budget + acceptance + replication evidence"—if no one does this layer, the lists stay on paper.
  • Keep a "results ledger" for every training project: Task baselines, samples, quality, hours, anomalies, human takeovers, customer sign-offs, reused assets, savings on the next project—not just class hours, headcounts, and satisfaction. The differentiation of a training business lies in whether you dare to show your results ledger.
