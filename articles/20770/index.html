OpenAI's "AI Deployment Manager" Position Open in Tokyo — A Job That Gets People Using ChatGPT Enterprise | AI Time-Saving Lab

Original title: OpenAIが東京で募集する「AI Deployment Manager」──ChatGPT Enterpriseを使わせる職

Summary

The article examines OpenAI's job posting for an AI Deployment Manager (Builder) at its Tokyo office. The role sits in the AI Deployment Management (ADM) team and focuses on post-sales enablement: making sure ChatGPT Enterprise, Codex, Agents, and API are actually adopted and continuously used within customer organizations, mainly through designing and running training, workshops, and executive briefings. The author verified the full posting by fetching embedded JSON data and compared it with a similar San Francisco posting, which names a different team (Enablement Lead), adds a subject-matter-expert duty, and discloses a $197K–$278K salary range; Tokyo's range is unpublished. Tokyo also has a separate Technical Deployment Lead role under the Forward Deployed Engineering team, focused on delivery execution. The author reads this as evidence that the bottleneck in enterprise AI adoption has shifted from closing deals to sustained organizational use.

Key points

  • OpenAI's Tokyo AI Deployment Manager role focuses on post-sales enablement, not sales or engineering.
  • Core duties include designing onboarding, advanced training, executive briefings, hackathons, and workshops.
  • Requirements: 4+ years in customer-facing or teaching roles plus technical depth in RAG, evals, and fine-tuning.
  • Tokyo posting offers hybrid 3-days-per-week work and relocation support but no disclosed salary.
  • San Francisco's equivalent role discloses $197K–$278K and differs in team name and some duties.

3-Line Summary OpenAI is hiring an "AI Deployment Manager (Builder)" for its Tokyo office. The role belongs to the "AI Deployment Management (ADM)" team and is dedicated to driving adoption and sustained usage of ChatGPT Enterprise, Codex, Agents, and API at customer companies. The substance of the job is neither sales nor development, but "designing and delivering training, workshops, and executive briefings" — a hybrid role combining technical understanding, instructional design, and customer advisory. The work arrangement is a hybrid schedule of 3 days a week in the office, and the posting explicitly states that relocation assistance is available for new hires.

A job posting for a position titled "AI Deployment Manager" is open for OpenAI's Tokyo office. What does this role — neither sales nor engineering — actually do? We read the job posting to find out.

The Role of the Team

The job posting first describes the mission of the "AI Deployment Management (ADM)" team to which this role belongs as follows (quoted from the original).

The AI Deployment Management (ADM) team enables organizations to turn OpenAI products into real, sustained impact through world-class enablement and training execution. Our mission is to help customers successfully adopt and operationalize AI across their organizations.

In other words, the ADM team's mission is not simply "deploying" OpenAI products, but "ensuring that, after deployment, they are actually and continuously used within the organization." The posting goes on to state that the team "helps customers convert the potential of AI into sustained capability through structured training, technical enablement, and scalable deployment programs," and that it "accelerates time-to-value and deepens product adoption by helping customers move from experimentation to production."

What the Job Actually Involves

The posting explains that the AI Deployment Manager is a position dedicated to this "post-sales" enablement (supporting sustained usage). The products covered are the full lineup: "ChatGPT Enterprise, Codex, Agents, and API." The listed responsibilities are as follows.

  • Own technical enablement for ChatGPT Enterprise, Codex, Agents, and API, defining effective deployment patterns for each customer segment
  • Lead training and enablement across the entire customer lifecycle, from initial onboarding through expansion, optimization, and long-term adoption
  • Design and deliver high-impact training such as onboarding sessions, advanced feature training, executive briefings, hackathons, and hands-on workshops
  • Drive customer activation, continued usage, and measurable business value through structured enablement and deployment programs
  • Collaborate with the Sales, AI Success Engineers, Solutions Engineering, and Product teams to ensure a seamless handoff from pre-sales to post-sales
  • Develop and improve reusable training materials, playbooks, and best practices based on patterns observed across customers and regions

Exceptional presentation and communication skills (the ability to explain technical concepts clearly to executives)
Strong technical depth across coding, agents, and APIs, including a practical understanding of RAG, evaluation strategies, fine-tuning, and trade-offs
Experience leading structured technical training such as API bootcamps and workshops, with the ability to design learning experiences, handle live Q&A, and solve problems in real time
The ability to connect technical capabilities to concrete business outcomes such as productivity, efficiency, cost reduction, risk mitigation, and revenue impact
A humble attitude, a desire to help others, and a willingness to proactively fill in gaps in one's own knowledge for the success of the team and customers
The ability to think flexibly in live customer-facing situations, and a personal commitment to the safe and ethical development of AI

The technical requirements use specific terms such as "RAG, evaluation strategies, fine-tuning, and key tradeoffs," indicating that the role assumes not just the ability to explain products, but an understanding of the technical know-how involved in actually running AI systems in production.

Work Arrangements Are Explicitly Stated

What stands out in this posting is that the work arrangements are described in concrete terms.

This role is based in our Tokyo Office. We use a hybrid work model of 3 days in the office per week and offer relocation assistance to new employees.

It explicitly states that the role is based at the Tokyo office, with a hybrid schedule of three days per week in the office, and that relocation support is available for new hires. This suggests that applications from overseas are anticipated. Checking the metadata of the job posting page, the team affiliation is linked to two teams, "Go To Market" and "Technical Success," and the compensation tier information is private (the field is empty). The same job page also includes a note on application limits stating "Candidates may not apply more than 5 times within 180 days," which appears to be a measure against duplicate applications for this popular position.

The same role in San Francisco is posted under a different team name

OpenAI's job listings page (

jobs.ashbyhq.com/openai

), fetched this time with

curl

, confirmed that a similar posting is also live at the San Francisco headquarters alongside the Tokyo one. However, the title on the listing is nearly identical to the Tokyo version, "AI Deployment Manager - Builder," while the body refers to itself as "Enablement Lead, Builder" (AI Deployment Manager). When the actual job posting body was fetched and compared with the Tokyo version, several differences emerged.

  • The team name is listed as the "AI Deployment Management (ADM)" team in the Tokyo version, whereas the San Francisco version is listed as the "Enablement Lead (EL)" team
  • The San Francisco version explicitly includes the role of acting as a product-area expert, stating "Serve as a Subject Matter Expert (SME) for designated product areas," but this sentence is absent from the "In this role, you will" bullet points in the Tokyo version
  • The technical requirements in the San Francisco version use the phrasing "software engineering, the software development lifecycle," which leans more toward the software development process than the Tokyo version's "coding, agents, and APIs"

While both use the same parenthetical title of "AI Deployment Manager," the official team name and some of the job responsibilities differ by location. Whether this is merely a difference in how the job postings are written (localization) or whether the teams are genuinely operated under different names organizationally cannot be determined from this comparison of job postings alone.

There is one more piece of information on the San Francisco job page that is absent from the Tokyo version: the compensation range.

Whether the salary for the same type of position in Tokyo is at the same level as the San Francisco version cannot be directly determined from these figures.

There is one more "deployment"-related opening in Tokyo

Checking OpenAI's job listings this time with

curl

, another posting for the Tokyo location was found besides AI Deployment Manager: "Technical Deployment Lead - Tokyo." When the job posting was actually fetched, it showed that this role belongs to the "Forward Deployed Engineering" team and is explicitly described as a "founding role." The responsibilities are described as "defining how to deliver complex systems to customers and owning the building, shipping, and adoption of them" and "driving day-to-day execution across FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers), researchers, and customer engineers." It emphasizes that "this is not a management role," and explains that the position carries end-to-end ownership of delivery.

The same Tokyo office thus has two similarly named postings side by side: (1) the ADM team's "AI Deployment Manager" (focused on training and enablement) and (2) the Forward Deployed Engineering team's "Technical Deployment Lead" (the execution owner of technical delivery). How far the two roles are divided and how they collaborate in practice cannot be determined from the job postings alone.

The move to make "deployment" a specialized profession

What this role indicates is the recognition that the bottleneck in enterprises adopting AI is shifting from "signing the contract" to "actually being used continuously within the organization." The phrase repeatedly used in the job posting, "from experimentation to production," also supports this. A similar structure is commonly seen in roles such as Forward Deployed Engineer and Applied AI Engineer, which this media outlet has covered separately. However, how strictly the roles are divided is partly inferred from the job posting descriptions.
