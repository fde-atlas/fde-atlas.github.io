Palantir CTO Shyam Sankar: Why We Invented the FDE Model

Original title: Palantir CTO 夏姆·桑卡尔：为什么要发明FDE模式

cniteyes.com · 2026-07-01 · 8 min read

Summary

This article recounts how Shyam Sankar, Palantir's President and CTO and its 13th employee, created the FDE model around 2006. Early intelligence and defense clients posed three unsolvable problems: sensitive data could not leave their networks, staff could not articulate standardized requirements, and workflows kept changing, breaking conventional software delivery. Palantir responded by embedding engineers on-site for 6–12 months. The model splits FDEs into two tracks: Delta engineers who ship quick prototypes in days, and Echo engineers with domain backgrounds who distill reusable industry ontologies. Field learnings flow back to headquarters to improve Foundry, enabling cheaper, faster delivery to new clients. The author argues today's AI companies face the same constraints and that FDE's real value is turning customer sites into product labs, not outsourced customization.

Key points

  • Palantir invented FDEs because intelligence clients had locked-down data, vague needs, and shifting workflows.
  • Delta FDEs build temporary working solutions on-site within 1–3 days.
  • Echo FDEs, often former domain experts, abstract field knowledge into reusable ontologies.
  • Insights flow back to HQ, iterating Foundry so new client deployments need only light customization.
  • AI companies now copy the model because private data and hidden requirements demand on-site engineering.

Today, nearly everyone in the AI industry is talking about and building FDE teams, but the surface-level move of "embedding engineers on-site" makes it extremely easy to get this playbook wrong. Without understanding the underlying design logic of the FDE model, it is easy to fall into the trap of "on-site outsourcing, pure custom delivery, and no accumulation of reusable industry assets." Investing massive manpower not only fails to build a long-term competitive moat, but once you slide into an outsourcing model, it becomes very hard to reverse course. It is worth revisiting the original thinking and intent of Shyam Sankar, the creator of the FDE model. Shyam Sankar, currently President and CTO of Palantir and the company's 13th employee, is the architect of the entire global FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) system. With a dual background—a bachelor's in electrical engineering from Cornell and a master's in management science and engineering from Stanford—he spent his early career deep in classified digital delivery for government and enterprise. After joining Palantir in 2006, to solve the industry dead-end where traditional software delivery completely failed for intelligence agencies and defense customers, he created from scratch the Echo/Delta dual-FDE team architecture and the "gravel road–highway" closed-loop iteration theory, and defined the FDE talent screening standards, on-site delivery processes, and industry ontology accumulation flywheel that are still used worldwide today. Now OpenAI, Anthropic, and vertical AI companies at home and abroad are all replicating this delivery model, and Shyam Sankar is its original creator and first evangelist. The article "Why Palantir Invented the FDE Model" fully answers the thinking behind the FDE's inception. For readers who are building FDE teams, studying AI delivery, or working on industry ontologies/data flywheels, this article is an excellent source for setting the record straight and better understanding the essence of the FDE.

Summary

In the early 2000s, Palantir faced a fatal problem that most software companies never encounter. Our earliest customers were US intelligence agencies at all levels, and these organizations had three unsolvable pain points: staff could not clearly and completely articulate their business requirements; core sensitive data was strictly forbidden from leaving the customer's intranet; and internal business processes were constantly in dynamic adjustment and change. Traditional standardized software product development processes completely failed with these customers. There was no clear requirements list, no stable feedback loop, and no way to conduct conventional user interviews and research. So Palantir made a decision that, at the time, the industry considered heretical: instead of sitting in an office asking customers what they needed, it deployed engineers directly into customers' real working environments for long-term embedding. These on-site engineers solved customers' immediate data problems through field observation, on-site experimentation, and real-time coding. This decision ultimately gave rise to the entire Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) system. What began as a reluctant measure for special government and enterprise customers has grown into a business strategy with the capacity for scaled replication. Today OpenAI, Anthropic, and all kinds of AI startups are replicating this delivery model, with underlying logic identical to Palantir's back in the day.

I. The Core Reasons Palantir Had to Create an Entirely New Engineering Delivery Model

The birth of the FDE model is deeply tied to Palantir's early business foundation serving intelligence and defense customers. Intelligence agencies deal entirely with highly sensitive, extremely complex national security problems, and these business scenarios can never have a complete, fixed requirements specification produced in advance. The team structures of most software companies on the market are built on clear, fixed business problems: collect standardized requirements, design standardized solutions, and mass-produce standardized products. But this process simply does not work with our customers. We quickly realized a key truth: in complex government and enterprise scenarios, customers' real needs are hidden in the details of day-to-day business operations, not in verbal descriptions in meetings or written requirements documents. Traditional software vendors have only two delivery roles, and both have fatal shortcomings:

  • Consultants: fluent in business methodology and able to produce complete PowerPoint decks, but unable to write production-grade code that can actually run;
  • Sales Engineers: able to demo product features on-site and liaise with customer technical teams, but unable to deeply restructure the product's underlying capabilities or accumulate industry-specific data models.

Palantir created a third, entirely independent role—the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). We recruit engineers who pass equally rigorous algorithm interviews and have full production development capabilities, and after passing customer security clearances, we station them for 6–12 months at military bases, intelligence buildings, and enterprise production floors. An FDE's daily work: following customer business staff through every workflow end to end, identifying data silos and process bottlenecks on-site, and writing custom code adapted to the customer's scenario on the spot; at the same time, continuously feeding the industry-wide pain points, standardized business ontologies, and general data-processing logic discovered in the field back to the product R&D team at Silicon Valley headquarters.

II. The Three Core Pain Points of Government and Enterprise Customers

Pain Point 1: Classified/enterprise core data cannot leave the domain, making remote development completely infeasible

Intelligence agencies, defense companies, and large financial institutions enforce strict isolation and control over core data—no raw data may be transmitted externally, and no external personnel may remotely access intranet systems. Traditional remote development and online testing are entirely impossible; only engineers physically embedded in the customer's intranet environment can directly read and process local data.

Pain Point 2: Customers themselves cannot produce complete, clear, standardized requirements

Frontline business staff are steeped year-round in industry-specific business rules and internal confidential workflows. Many implicit operations, industry-internal jargon, and special exception processes are second nature to them and cannot be converted into standardized, written product requirements. Meeting-room interviews capture only surface-level, simplified requirements, and many key details that determine whether a product can be delivered are completely missed, so the resulting standardized product is entirely disconnected from the customer's real business.

Pain Point 3: Government and enterprise business processes change dynamically and continuously, and standardized product iteration cannot keep pace with changing scenarios

In defense, finance, and manufacturing, policies, regulatory rules, and business operating models are constantly adjusted; the traditional software product cycle of major version updates every six months or year simply cannot match customers' rapidly changing business needs. A remote headquarters team cannot sense changes in customer processes in real time, and by the time customers report requirement changes, the business scenario has already moved on—development work is hopelessly lagging.

III. The Underlying Logic of FDE Talent Screening

We divide the FDE team into two role tracks, with two completely independent talent screening standards, all established by Shyam Sankar and still in use today:

Track 1: Delta / rapid-prototyping FDE / on-site development engineer

Hard skill requirements: must pass a Palantir backend algorithm engineer interview of equal difficulty, be able to independently write stable, runnable production code, and be familiar with foundational development skills in data cleaning, data joining, and ontology modeling. Core personality screening criteria: reject perfectionist architecture engineers; prioritize pragmatic, delivery-oriented engineers. They must be able to accept writing "temporary lightweight code" to fit a single scenario rather than obsessing over a perfect architecture in one step; they must have extremely strong rapid trial-and-error ability, able to build a temporary deliverable solution for the customer within 1–3 days. Best-fit scenarios: short-term on-site deployments, quickly resolving customers' urgent data bottlenecks, and building single-customer "gravel road" temporary solutions.

Track 2: Echo / industry-deep-dive FDE / business ontology accumulation expert

Preferred hiring backgrounds: people with years of frontline experience in the relevant industry—hybrid talent such as retired intelligence analysts, defense process experts, manufacturing process engineers, and bank risk-control experts—while also having basic code-reading and simple scripting abilities. Core personality screening criteria: extremely strong observation, synthesis, and abstraction skills. They can distill industry-wide standardized business processes, entity relationships, and data schemas (ontology frameworks) from customers' scattered, fragmented business operations; they excel at connecting across levels with both senior business owners and frontline operators, syncing information in both directions. Core work goal: accumulate reusable industry know-how and standardized ontology models, abstracting single-customer temporary solutions into general-purpose assets for headquarters teams to build an industry-wide replicable standardized platform (the highway).

IV. The Core of the FDE Model: From the Customer Site to Platform Scale

The entire system forms a never-ending data and demand flywheel, structured as a complete three-layer chain: the front-end on-site layer (FDE in the field): Delta engineers quickly build temporary adaptation solutions, solving customers' immediate business pain points on the spot and rapidly delivering quantifiable business value; Echo engineers simultaneously organize and accumulate industry ontologies and general business rules; the middle-layer feedback layer (information synced back to headquarters): all common requirements, general data models, and high-frequency business scenarios accumulated by FDEs in the field are uniformly fed back to the product and R&D teams at Silicon Valley headquarters; the back-end platform iteration layer (standardized replication): engineers at headquarters use the industry-common assets fed back by FDEs to iteratively upgrade the Palantir Foundry underlying platform and general Ontology tools, building standardized product capabilities suited to the entire industry. When a new customer in the same industry signs a contract, the platform already has a large number of directly reusable industry-standardized modules; only a small number of FDEs need to be on site for minor adjustments, enabling rapid delivery of a complete solution, greatly reducing delivery costs for new customers and shortening implementation cycles.

V. Why Every AI Company Today Is Replicating the FDE Model

The difficulties facing the large language model and AI agent industry today closely mirror those Palantir faced with government and enterprise clients in 2006:

  • Enterprise customers' private data is strictly isolated and cannot be uploaded to public large model platforms;
  • Every industry and every company has completely different business processes, data formats, and business goals, so no single standardized AI product can fit all customers;
  • Frontline business staff at enterprises cannot clearly articulate their real needs for AI implementation; a large amount of implicit business rules and industry-specific judgment logic can only be uncovered in real business operation scenarios.

The core value of the FDE model is not simply sending people on site to provide delivery services, but turning the customer site into a forward exploration laboratory for the product. All industry knowledge and real customer needs accumulated in the field drive continuous iteration and optimization of the underlying platform and large model applications in reverse, forming industry data and knowledge assets that belong exclusively to the company and cannot be replicated by competitors.

VI. The Irreplaceable Core Competitive Moats of This Model

Knowledge asset moat: the industry ontologies, business schemas, and proprietary data processing workflows accumulated by long-term on-site FDEs are exclusive assets that purely standardized SaaS vendors cannot quickly build up; customer value moat: traditional software vendors can only deliver standardized software products, while the FDE model directly delivers quantifiable business outcomes for customers (such as reducing factory defect rates, shortening intelligence analysis time, and reducing financial risk control loopholes); the customer's core reason for paying shifts from "buying software" to "solving business problems," greatly increasing customer stickiness, renewal rates, and deal size; iteration speed moat: FDEs capture changes in customers' businesses in real time and synchronously iterate adaptation solutions, shortening iteration cycles to days or weeks, far exceeding the quarterly or annual iteration rhythm of traditional software.
