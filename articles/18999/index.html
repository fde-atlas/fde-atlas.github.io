Asia FDE Job Requirements: Hiring Bar in Tokyo, Seoul, and Singapore

Original title: 亚洲 FDE 岗位要求：东京首尔新加坡招聘门槛

FDE Times · 2026-11-04 · 6 min read

Summary

This guide examines closed FDE job postings from OpenAI, Anthropic, Notion, FieldAI, and Singtel across Tokyo, Seoul, and Singapore to extract each market's hard requirements. Tokyo and Seoul postings make bilingual fluency (Japanese/English, native Korean plus professional English) a mandatory filter, along with 4+ years of customer-facing technical experience. Singapore postings emphasize travel tolerance—OpenAI's ad mentioned up to 50% of time on customer sites—and demand extends beyond AI labs to robotics and telecom firms. The author recommends turning each posting into a matrix of language, years, experience type, and travel share, honestly comparing your own record, and treating closed postings as signals of where companies actually need FDEs.

Key points

  • Tokyo postings from OpenAI and Anthropic require fluent Japanese and English plus 4-5 years of customer-facing experience.
  • Notion's Seoul role demanded native-level Korean and professional working English.
  • Singapore's key filter is travel: OpenAI cited up to 50% of time at customer sites.
  • FieldAI and Singtel show robotics and telecom firms also hire FDEs in Singapore.
  • Readers should build a four-column matrix and distinguish engineering years from customer-facing years.

You open a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) job posting in Tokyo, read through the description, and feel like a great fit. Then, in the requirements, you see one line: fluency in spoken and written Japanese and English. You click on another company's posting, but the page shows the role has already been filled.

One thing to note upfront: most of the postings mentioned below have been taken down, stopped accepting applications, or been filled. Treat them as a snapshot of companies that have hired FDEs in the APAC region, not a list of roles you can apply to today.

The value of this snapshot lies in its requirements. The skill worth practicing is this: read job postings the way you'd read a customer requirements document, strip out the hard requirements from the description, and honestly compare yourself against them.

Who's hiring, and where?

Tokyo has the most prominent companies. OpenAI describes its Tokyo FDE role as leading complex end-to-end deployments, pushing frontier models into production. The role is hybrid, three days a week in the office, with relocation support and travel mainly within Japan.

OpenAI also posted a more senior role in Tokyo, "Forward Deployed Software Engineer," requiring 7+ years of full-stack experience and fluency in both languages; that posting has since been taken down.

Anthropic also hired in Tokyo for "Forward Deployed Engineer, Applied AI," working directly with its most strategic customers to build production-grade applications on Claude. That posting was taken down in April 2026.

Singapore is more varied. OpenAI posted an FDE role there requiring 4+ years of experience and willingness to travel up to 50% of the time, working on-site with customers; its careers page now shows it is no longer accepting applications.

A foundation model startup once hired an FDE based in Singapore, remote or in-office, responsible for covering customers and partners across APAC; that posting was taken down on February 24, 2026.

Demand in Singapore doesn't only come from foundation model companies either. Robotics company FieldAI hired an FDE to lead end-to-end deployments of autonomous systems in customers' own environments.

Singtel Group posted an FDE role on May 6, 2026, and its careers page now shows the role has been filled. Anthropic has also announced an office in Singapore, its fifth in APAC, with open roles including Applied AI.

Seoul has Notion. The company hired an FDE (GTM - Korea) in Seoul on a hybrid basis, responsible for driving technical projects with customers, requiring native-level Korean and professional working English (including spoken). That posting was taken down on July 3, 2026.

In Tokyo and Seoul, language is the first hurdle. The reason is easy to understand: an FDE sits with customers, reads their internal documents, and meets with their operations teams. If you can't work in the customer's language, the role is hard to do, so employers list it as a must-have, not a nice-to-have.

Singapore is different. The hurdle there is travel: an FDE based in Singapore may need to look after customers across multiple countries. A posting saying up to 50% of time on-site with customers means spending half the year away from your own desk.

Example: turning a job posting into a matrix

Imagine a backend engineer with 5 years of experience, excellent English, and intermediate Japanese. Of those 5 years, about 2 were spent doing integration work directly with enterprise customers, and the rest building internal services.

Compare this resume against OpenAI's Tokyo posting. The posting asks for 5+ years of customer-facing technical work experience, not 5 years of engineering experience.

So although this engineer has 5 years in the industry, only about 2 years match the posting's criteria. Add the bilingual requirement, and this resume has two gaps, both of them hard requirements.

Anthropic's Tokyo posting lowers the bar to 4+ years of customer-facing technical work experience, but still requires fluency in Japanese and English, plus 25-50% travel to meet customers. The gap is smaller, but it's still there.

OpenAI's Singapore posting highlights 4+ years of experience and willingness to spend up to 50% of time on-site with customers. For this engineer, the key question is no longer language, but whether they can accept that travel pace.

From this matrix, the path is fairly clear. In the short term, Singapore is the more realistic entry point for this resume. Tokyo requires longer preparation: raising Japanese to a working level and proactively seeking more customer-facing work in the current job to build up the years.

How to do the same for your target city

Start by listing companies by city, not job postings. Many of the postings in this article are closed or filled, but if a company has hired an FDE in a city, it usually means it has customers there. Watching companies' own careers pages is more reliable than waiting for postings to appear on job aggregators.

Break each posting into four columns: language, years of experience, type of experience (general engineering, full-stack, or customer-facing work), and travel percentage. Mark which columns are hard requirements and which are just preferences. Then write your own numbers next to them, without rounding up.

Finally, translate the matrix into your resume. If a posting values customer-facing years, put that number first and tie it to specific work: what systems, for which customers, and how they performed in production.

The phrase "end-to-end deployment" appears in both OpenAI's and FieldAI's postings, so every bullet point should cover the full cycle: from receiving a requirement to the system actually running. If your target is Tokyo or Seoul, state your language proficiency using standards employers can verify.

Which mistakes get applications rejected?

The most common mistake is miscounting the type of experience. Three years building internal APIs is not three years of customer-facing work, and interviewers will probe exactly this point.

Close behind is assuming "my Japanese is decent" is enough, when the posting explicitly requires fluency in both spoken and written. Applying when you don't meet the bar rarely pays off; better to spend the energy learning the language.

Many people are still only focused on large model companies. FieldAI and Singtel show that Singapore's robotics and telecom industries also need people to bring systems into customers' real environments. If you have a background in infrastructure or embedded systems, these companies are worth a try.

The last mistake is treating a closed job posting as a shut door. A filled position may mean the team is expanding, or it may just be backfilling—but either way, it shows the company needs an FDE in that city.

The most important thing is to figure out what kind of person the team will need for its next hire, and prepare your application materials against those requirements.
