The quality of material about FDEs varies widely: some of it consists of real job descriptions from companies that are actually hiring, while some is trend-driven narrative written by media outlets chasing traffic. Sorting these sources into tiers by "credibility" is the first step to systematically learning about the FDE role—otherwise it's easy to see only the hiring hype and miss the business model and organizational risks.

Below, the most worthwhile material is divided into four categories, with notes on what each should be read for and why their credibility differs.

Four tiers of material

What to read: FDE / FDSE / Applied AI Engineer job pages from companies like OpenAI, Google Cloud, Palantir, Mistral, Scale, and ByteDance.

Why it's credible: It directly tells you "who companies are actually hiring"—real skill requirements, salary ranges, and travel ratios—more accurate than any secondhand interpretation. Best suited for building a competency model.

What to read: Announcements about enterprise AI services companies such as OpenAI Deployment Company and the joint ventures formed by Anthropic and Blackstone.

Why it matters: It explains "why the FDE role is being institutionalized"—model companies are elevating "deployment" from a post-sales service activity to a core commercialization capability. What you're looking at is the strategic intent behind the trend.

What to read: Reports on FDE job growth from Business Insider, FT, WSJ, and similar outlets.

Why to be cautious: Different media outlets use different statistical methodologies (729% / 800% / 10x, each with its own figure), so these are useful as a trend signal but not as a precise market size. Read the narrative, not the absolute numbers.

What to read: a16z's Palantirization discussion, Pragmatic Engineer's critique, and Bob McGrew's in-depth interviews.

Why it's irreplaceable: It helps you see clearly the strengths, weaknesses, and pitfalls of the FDE model—for example, "if it can't generate product compounding, it will degenerate into a low-gross-margin services business." It keeps you from being swept along by hiring hype alone.

This site has turned thousands of sources into a searchable library

The biggest obstacle to understanding the four categories above is that "the material is scattered, the sources are mixed, and cross-verification is difficult." To address this, OpenFDE has done the painstaking work: deduplicating, categorizing, and tagging the sources it collected to build a searchable reference library.

No need to trawl the web yourself—the original links, sources, and topic tags for all four categories have been organized into the FDE Signal Library, searchable by company, topic, and credibility tier, so you can cross-check how many reliable sources back a given claim.

A reading order for systematically learning about FDEs

Once the material is tiered, reading it in the following order is the fastest way to go from "knowing the term" to "building a complete mental model":

  • First, read the official job descriptions—Palantir FDSE, OpenAI FDE, Google Cloud FDE—to establish the hard standard of what a "real FDE" is.
  • Next, read the origin material—Palantir Dev vs Delta—to understand the Echo / Delta division of labor and the origins of the platform foundation.
  • Then, read the deployment company announcements—OpenAI Deployment Company and Anthropic's enterprise services company—to understand the institutionalization trend.
  • After that, read the strategic analysis—a16z's Palantirization and the Bob McGrew interviews—to understand the core tension between "services vs. product compounding."
  • Finally, read the trend reports—treat job growth data as background narrative, not as evidence in itself.
