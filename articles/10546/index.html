Technology matters least; AI office tools have entered a tedious phase of labor-intensive service and price wars.

Contributing editor | Li Yuan, Editor | He Yifan

The AI office war is raging, and the big tech companies are competing to the point of bayonet fighting, from organization to marketing.

Led by Tencent, WorkBuddy—advanced internally as a strategic-level project with Pony Ma regularly hearing progress reports—has received various strategic priority resources at the group level.

China Entrepreneur has learned that recently, to drive WorkBuddy's enterprise-side adoption, Tencent has offered distributors rebate discounts of over 30%, on the condition that they can complete the rollout of tens of thousands of enterprise accounts. A tier-one distributor revealed that the official requirement is to sell 100 seats to qualify for the discount, and they are also negotiating discount room for tier-two or tier-three distributors.

But there are also thresholds for distributors who want to sell WorkBuddy: besides selling account licenses, they must have FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) capable of private deployment, providing pre-sales and after-sales support for customers. Problems the distributor cannot solve are then passed on to Tencent to resolve.

According to China Entrepreneur, Tencent is also conducting large-scale external training for WorkBuddy sales, down to every product detail, including differentiation advantages over competitors, compiled into sales scripts and uniformly disseminated on social media such as Xiaohongshu and Channels.

One industry insider estimates that WorkBuddy's sales network already has more than 2,000 distributors and continues to expand through fission-style growth.

By comparison, although Alibaba and ByteDance have each integrated "Qwen Office + DingTalk" and "Doubao Pro + Trae Work + Feishu" at the product and organizational levels, neither has yet planned a large-scale rollout of sales distributors.

Over the past 6 months, Volcano Engine and Alibaba Cloud have both stuck to a "Token-first" strategy, with marketing leaning more toward models than application products, and sales incentives tied to Token volume. A WorkBuddy distributor said: Alibaba Cloud has very high qualification requirements for distributors—you need to reach a certain volume in the previous year, otherwise it's hard to open the door to cooperation.

Tencent, which lagged a step behind in the AI model wars, urgently needs WorkBuddy to win back ground and quickly widen the gap with rivals in user scale, so its marketing strategy is simpler and more direct.

When distributors promote WorkBuddy, commissions are based on accounts: selling the 198-yuan enterprise edition directly earns a discount, and the more accounts promoted, the more they earn. Compared with Token consumption metrics, this more directly motivates the sales side.

Industry insiders told China Entrepreneur that technology is becoming the least important capability in the AI office war. Models or products can be aligned in a short time, and the industry is entering a tedious phase of labor-intensive service and price competition.

The Labor Race

A SaaS company founder once told China Entrepreneur, "I don't believe that in China, a company buying an enterprise software product worth around 20,000 yuan doesn't need a salesperson to visit."

The moat of the AI office battlefield is so thin that, beyond advanced weapons, coordinated strategy and tactics, and the will of commanders, it inevitably devolves into a "war of human waves."

An HR director at an industry-leading company has recently been intensively "besieged" by several big tech firms. He told China Entrepreneur: the company works with both DingTalk and Feishu. Previously, when the company planned to launch an internal AI innovation initiative, Feishu sent an employee with an SOP (standard operating procedure) plan. But when Tencent's WorkBuddy heard about it, it dispatched a cross-departmental team, and the company ultimately chose to work with Tencent.

The office software track has always been labor-intensive; at the peak of its business expansion in 2022, Feishu's team once swelled to 8,000 people. The competition in AI office tools likewise cannot escape the underlying logic of heavy staffing and heavy delivery.

In August this year, Changan Automobile and Tencent jointly formed a 34-person FDE team (including 24 Changan business and technical backbone members and 10 Tencent experts), with members taking two months off from regular duties to learn how to cultivate "silicon-based employees." To get WorkBuddy into Mengniu Group, the Tencent Cloud team also accompanied them throughout, cultivating 200 "seed players" at Mengniu to serve as AI pioneers.

It is understood that this year Volcano Engine has also built its own FDE team, especially targeting key industries such as automotive, healthcare, and finance.

In June, Volcano Engine President Tan Dai told media including China Entrepreneur: Volcano Engine hopes to use FDEs to go deep into every industry, starting with deep co-creation with benchmark customers in each industry. "An FDE is neither a salesperson nor pre-sales; they have the ability to deliver code, especially AI code."

But the CEO of another leading cloud vendor has a different view. He believes the "FDE" concept, which originated from the overseas company Palantir, is a rather idealized team model, and many companies have simply borrowed this fashionable name.

The prerequisite for FDEs to work smoothly is organizing a company's high-quality data assets, but many companies' data is messy today. For example, a company's enterprise IM (instant messaging) tool might be DingTalk or Feishu, its ERP software might be SAP, and business terminal data might be scattered across platforms like Meituan and JD.com.

By comparison, AI office tools trying to enter enterprises to assist with data asset decision-making are still at an early stage. They can only handle document processing or workflow optimization. Reaching a company's core business data and supply chain requires passing through the enterprise gateway—a deep-water zone.

Therefore, in fields like finance, Tencent actively seeks partnerships with professional software providers such as Wind.

Ge Zhengqi, Chief Strategy Officer (CSO) of Wind Information, told China Entrepreneur: high-level AI users now tend to hand complex tasks that once required a whole team over to agents. Within Wind's AI community, more than 1,600 Skills have been accumulated, including proprietary Skills like "Wind-developed Alice Financial Expert," which are open to WorkBuddy and can be invoked directly by users.

The Price Race: Enterprises Are Increasingly Counting Every Penny

On the flip side of big tech's rush to penetrate enterprise office scenarios, many enterprises have already begun calmly reflecting on the real business returns behind high Token consumption.

Recently, Wang Puzhong, CEO of Meituan's Core Local Commerce, mentioned in a speech: at the beginning of 2026, the company had all employees "raising shrimp," which resulted in a surge in Token bills—daily consumption exceeding 10 million yuan. The decision-making errors produced by shrimp-raising also interfered with real operations.

Wang Puzhong analyzed that, judging from the current situation, although AI supply is abundant, at this stage only a few enterprises can measure tangible returns. This problem stems from a fourfold mismatch: cognitively, enterprises either exaggerate or underestimate AI; in efficiency, large models are mostly used for trivial daily tasks and rarely touch decision-making cores; in scenarios, AI still lingers on the periphery and is hard to integrate into operations; in evaluation, it is easy to seek instant results while ignoring the enterprise adaptation cycle. In Wang Puzhong's view, the stacking of these four mismatches makes it difficult for AI investment to translate into measurable productivity growth.

This also reveals a reality: Token costs and returns remain a pressure enterprises cannot avoid. Models and products need to prioritize performance, and finding the cost-performance balance point is also a challenge.

Xia Junchen, technical lead of Jike 2.5 Lab (Jike's innovation lab), told China Entrepreneur that he found the industry expected models to get cheaper and cheaper, but recent trends show many models are getting more expensive. Some cheap models have even been deprecated by vendors, forcing customers to switch to more expensive models.

In his view, a viable approach is: for brand-new scenarios and needs, first run through the process with the most top-capability model, then set an effect benchmark, and then replace it with a more cost-effective model, doing engineering optimization and fine-tuning.

For AI office scenarios, Xia Junchen believes that as long as the cost of an employee using AI to handle tasks is lower than their hourly wage, the ROI can be calculated.

Take email processing as an example: the company has a photo translation app called kulikuli, and he needs to handle more than 2,000 customer emails a month, spending about 50 hours a month on it. "Interns can help process emails, but they can't achieve Scaling either."

Using an agent, for 2,000–3,000 yuan in token costs, this work can be completed, and the ROI fully pays off. So for tools like WorkBuddy, what they may replace first is the value of interns, not frontline employees.

To grab users, major AI office tools have also launched price wars in succession, using subsidies, membership discounts, and points campaigns to seize the market. Among them, high cost-performance and large points subsidies constitute WorkBuddy's key competitiveness. Many users say outright that what they like about WorkBuddy is that it's "cheap and plentiful."

After the Hy3 model launched, WorkBuddy rolled out limited-time free and nighttime discount campaigns: new users get 500 points upon registration; the personal edition comes in three tiers—Standard at 99 yuan, Premium at 199 yuan, and Flagship at 999 yuan—with 30% off for consecutive monthly subscriptions, and plans come with a large number of bonus points.

ByteDance's Trae Work has likewise ramped up subsidies: enterprise edition model calls enjoy 50% off until the end of 2026; the personal tier starts as low as 9.9 yuan for the first month, members get the Doubao Seed model at 25% off, plus 500 bonus points per month.

Alibaba's Qwen Office distributed 2,000 points directly to new users after opening its public beta in August. The new model Qwen3.8MAX is open for free trial; the personal edition gets 20% off for consecutive monthly subscriptions, along with login points that expire the same day.

By comparison, ByteDance's Trae Work has a lower monthly payment threshold, while Tencent's WorkBuddy is more generous with points.

Alibaba and ByteDance are brewing a counterattack

In terms of user numbers, WorkBuddy is already half a move ahead. According to third-party statistics, in June 2026, WorkBuddy's monthly PC visits exceeded 20 million, more than the second and third places combined. But Alibaba and ByteDance are brewing a bigger counterattack.

China Entrepreneur learned that after ByteDance integrated its Feishu and Doubao product teams on July 30, the intelligent assistant Aily inside Feishu was replaced by "Doubao Work Companion," which now supports access to the latest models including Kimi K3, Doubao Seed 2.1-Turbo, and GLM5.2. Trae Work has also been connected to Feishu—just @TraeWork in a Feishu chat to assign tasks.

After Qwen Office launched its public beta on August 3, it has also been improving its technical infrastructure, integrating with DingTalk, supporting third-party model calls such as GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek V4 Pro, and open-sourcing the enterprise-grade context infrastructure component "MyContext."

"We keep saying internally that most people still haven't seen a sufficiently powerful agent," said Chen Yusen, then head of MuleRun, in an interview with China Entrepreneur in May this year. In June, he was appointed CEO of DingTalk, becoming the youngest business unit CEO at Alibaba.

At the time, Chen Yusen's view was clear: the enterprise office market is still an early market, a wilderness. Top enterprises will definitely compare all products on the market before making a choice, and only players with real strength will win.

Back then, his role was merely that of a star project lead, able to polish the product in a relatively independent small environment. Now standing on a much bigger business stage, he needs to pick up the pace and build market momentum for Qwen Office.

While Alibaba, ByteDance, and WorkBuddy battle it out, Tencent is also plotting to seize rivals' AI video dividends.

Recently, building on WorkBuddy's success, Tencent launched two creation platform tools, WorkSolo and WorkRally. WorkRally's foundation incorporates ByteDance's Seedance 2.0 and 2.5 models, targeting AI comic-drama, professional film, and premium animation editions. Tencent disclosed in its Q2 2026 earnings call that Hy4, planned to launch soon, will further improve model performance and multimodal capabilities.

A short-drama industry insider told China Entrepreneur: Tencent's leadership initially refused to let WorkSolo integrate the Seedance series of models, because it required prepaying a very substantial fee. But because Hy's multimodal capabilities were limited, they ultimately had to compromise.

However, Tencent still holds a trump card—WeChat. In April this year, Wind Alice, an AI product under Wind Information, integrated with WeChat's ClawBot, allowing finance professionals to directly call its core functions such as data analysis and report generation within WeChat; the product's cloud resources were exhausted within 30 minutes of launch. Currently, Tencent is also exploring bringing WeChat Pay into Skill to complete the ecosystem loop.

But overall, the AI office market is, after all, an early market, where the tool attribute still far outweighs the platform attribute, and tools can be swapped out by enterprises at any time. Interviewees unanimously told China Entrepreneur: they would never put all their eggs in one basket and are happy to try multiple options.
