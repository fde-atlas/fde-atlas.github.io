Forward Deployed Engineer Salary by Tier

Vibe Engines · 2026-09-03 · 10 min read

Summary

Vibe Engines' FDE Comp Explorer is a transparent multiplier model that breaks forward deployed engineer pay into base, annualized equity and bonus by employer tier, level and market, instead of one headline number. Anchors for a senior FDE in SF/NYC range from $190K at defense integrators to $450K at frontier labs, each with a confidence label and band reflecting source quality. The article argues you should compare offers on the cash line first, judge equity separately, and ask six questions—on variable pay, equity terms, travel, customer load, utilization reviews and on-call—before comparing offers.

Key points

  • Tier anchors run from $190K (defense) to $450K (frontier labs)
  • Frontier-lab equity is 62% of the package and unliquidated
  • Tier matters more than level: senior startup FDE earns less than mid-level lab FDE
  • Defense packages can carry more annual cash than frontier-lab ones
  • Model is directional, not scraped data; posting ranges beat it

Six questions that change what these numbers mean

  • Is any of this variable, and on what? Quota or OTE means a pre-sales-shaped role, whatever the title says.
  • Units, strike price, latest preferred price, last round date, vesting, refresher policy.
  • Travel expectation — in writing. Travel is uncompensated cost.
  • How many customers at once? One deep engagement is a different job from six shallow ones.
  • Is any part of my review utilisation-based? The clearest early signal of the services trap.
  • Who is on-call, and for how long after handoff?

Forward deployed engineer pay is not one number, and this explorer refuses to pretend it is. Pick an employer tier, a level and a market, and the estimate splits into base salary, annualized equity and bonus, with a band whose width reflects how good the source behind that tier is. It is a transparent multiplier model with its anchors stated, not a scraped table.

What it computes

Each tier has an anchor: total compensation for a senior FDE in a major US market. Level and market scale it, and the tier's own shares split it.

total        = anchor × level_mult × market_mult
equity_share = min(0.75, equity × geo_damp × level_lift)
  geo_damp   = 0.75 if market_mult < 0.9, else 1
  level_lift = 1.08 if level_mult ≥ 1.5, else 1
equity       = total × equity_share
bonus        = total × bonus_share
base         = total − equity − bonus
band         = total × (1 − spread) to total × (1 + spread)
cash         = base + bonus

Level multipliers are Junior 0.5, Mid 0.72, Senior 1.0, Staff 1.55 and Principal 2.05. Market multipliers are SF / NYC 1.0, US other 0.85, London / Dublin / Berlin 0.62 and India 0.3. Amounts under $1M print in whole thousands and larger ones as $X.XXM. The base and equity percentages are rounded and bonus takes the remainder, so the three always add to 100.

Assumptions and limits

  • The sources differ in quality. Frontier labs rest on the Perspective 2026 FDE compensation report (about 1,200 respondents), Palantir on levels.fyi FDSE data ($171K to $295K, median near $211K), and the startup tiers on scattered reports. Wider spreads mark weaker data.
  • Equity is annualized grant value. For a private company it is a forecast about a liquidity event, not money.
  • The equity damp is a threshold. It applies to every market below 0.9, which includes US other as well as the non-US markets.
  • Shares are fixed per tier. Quota, OTE and commission plans are not modeled, so a pre-sales-shaped role will not look like one here.
  • It is a snapshot of the anchors and sources named above, not live data.

How to use the result

  • Compare offers on the cash line first. In the table, the $190K defense package carries $10K more cash this year than the $450K frontier-lab package: $181K against $171K.
  • Choose the tier before the level. A senior FDE at a vertical AI startup ($240K) earns less here than a mid-level FDE at a frontier lab ($324K).
  • Ask the six questions. Variable pay, equity terms, travel in writing, customers at once, utilization in reviews and on-call after handoff change what a number means more than the number does.
  • Prefer first-party ranges. Where pay transparency applies, the posting's own range beats every estimate on this page.

When to use it, and when not

Use it to get oriented before a recruiter call, to check whether an offer's shape is normal for its tier (a 62% equity share is typical of a frontier lab and strange at a defense integrator), or to see how much a move between tiers changes the cash you live on.

Do not use it to name a number in a negotiation, to value a specific company's private equity, or for a role paid on quota or OTE. For those, the posting's range, the full equity terms and people at that employer beat any multiplier model.

Three mechanics are worth knowing. The 75% cap on equity share never binds: the highest share any combination reaches is

0.62 × 1.08 = 0.6696

. The level lift applies to every tier, so even a staff defense role moves from 5% to 5.4% equity. And the band is a fixed ± spread around the point, not a percentile range from data.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing headline totals across equity types. $450K with 62% private equity and $260K with 30% public stock are different kinds of money. Compare cash lines, then judge the equity separately.
  • Annualizing a grant wrongly. Annual equity is the grant divided by its vesting years: a $1M grant over four years is $250K a year. Setting a full grant beside someone else's annual figure inflates one offer fourfold.
  • Reading the band as a confidence interval. ±22% is the model's judgment of source quality, not a range in which 90% of real offers fall.
  • Assuming your employer uses these market factors. A US-other offer from an employer that does not adjust for location will sit near the SF figure, not at 0.85 of it.
  • Mapping titles one to one. A senior title at a small startup may cover less scope than senior at a large lab. Pick the level by what you will own, using the description under each level.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a forward deployed engineer make?

It depends mostly on the kind of employer. For a senior FDE in San Francisco or New York, this model puts total compensation at about $450K at a frontier lab, $330K at an AI infrastructure company, $260K at big tech or consulting, $240K at a vertical AI startup, $225K at Palantir and $190K at a defense or government integrator. Each figure comes with a band, and the frontier-lab and startup anchors rest on survey and anecdotal data, so treat them as directional.

Why does equity matter so much in an FDE offer?

At the top tiers it is most of the package. In the default view, a senior frontier-lab FDE in SF or NYC, equity is 62% of the $450K total, or $279K a year, while the cash you can spend this year is $171K. For a private company that equity is an estimate about a liquidity event that has not happened, which is why the widget warns whenever equity reaches 45% of the package and why it prints a separate cash line.

Does tier or level matter more for FDE compensation?

Tier, by a wide margin. Level multipliers run from 0.5 for a junior hire to 2.05 for a principal, but tier anchors already differ by more than two times at the same level, from $190K to $450K. In this model a senior FDE at a vertical AI startup ($240K) earns less than a mid-level FDE at a frontier lab ($324K). Choosing the tier, and judging the quality of its equity, outweighs a level step early in the career.

How does location change FDE pay in this explorer?

Every total is multiplied by a market factor: 1.0 for SF or NYC, 0.85 for the rest of the US, 0.62 for London, Dublin or Berlin and 0.3 for India. Any market below 0.9 also shrinks the equity share by a quarter. A senior frontier-lab package therefore reads $383K in the rest of the US, $279K in London, Dublin or Berlin and $135K in India, with equity falling from 62% to 47% of the total in all three.

Where do the FDE compensation numbers come from?

Each tier names its anchor and a confidence label. Frontier labs rest on the Perspective 2026 FDE compensation report, a practitioner survey of about 1,200 respondents. Palantir uses self-reported levels.fyi data for FDSE roles, a $171K to $295K range with a median near $211K. AI infrastructure and vertical startups use aggregated or scattered reports, big tech uses public software engineer bands, and the band width grows as the source gets weaker. It is a model, not scraped live data.

What should I ask before comparing two FDE offers?

Six things. Is any of the package variable, and on what, since quota or OTE means a pre-sales-shaped role whatever the title says. What are the full equity terms: units, strike price, latest preferred price, last round date, vesting and refreshers. What is the travel expectation, in writing. How many customers will you carry at once. Is any part of your review utilization-based. And who is on call, and for how long after handoff.
