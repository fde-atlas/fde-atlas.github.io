Palantir Build to Apply: how to build and film the demo

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 18 min read

Summary

This article explains Palantir's Build to Apply hiring route for US Government Deployment Strategist roles: candidates build a project on Foundry and AIP, then submit a demo video under 5 minutes via an unlisted YouTube link within 7-10 days of starting. As of September 2026 there are six postings with estimated salaries of $110,000-$170,000; four locations require an active Top Secret clearance. The author offers a four-part problem filter (named user, recurring decision, legally obtainable data, genuine AIP use), a fire-inspection scheduling example scoped as a thin end-to-end workflow, a seven-day build plan, and guidance on scripting the video. The build offers potential expedited consideration, not a guaranteed interview.

Key points

  • Build to Apply requires a Foundry and AIP project plus a demo video under 5 minutes.
  • Submissions are due 7-10 days after building starts; AI tools are explicitly encouraged.
  • Six Deployment Strategist postings exist; four require active Top Secret clearance.
  • The problem filter: named user, recurring decision, legal data, and real AIP work.
  • Recommended plan: seven days building a thin, complete workflow, with two days on AIP.

Getting set up

The postings answer the first practical question, which is how to get Foundry at all: Source 1Deployment Strategist, Build to Apply - US Government (Washington, D.C.) - Palantir on LeverPublisherPalantir Technologies (Lever)Source typecompany job posting

  • Create a DevTier account with AIP, and build the project in it.
  • Take the crash course at learn.palantir.com if Foundry is new to you.
  • Ask questions in the Palantir Developer Community.
  • Bring data: publicly available data, or notional data you create yourself.

The posting counts the window from when you begin building, and doesn’t define that. So our advice is to do the thinking first: pick the user, find the data and write the one-sentence problem before you open your DevTier account.

Pick a problem a customer would pay to fix

Palantir’s own annual report describes AIP bootcamps that deliver real workflows on actual customer data in days, which is the same thing the postings ask you to show. Source 11Palantir Technologies Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025PublisherPalantir Technologies (SEC EDGAR)Source typecompany SEC filing A generic dashboard over a famous dataset shows much less.

Here is the filter we use to choose a problem. It is our method, not something Palantir publishes. Keep an idea only if it passes all four:

  • A named user. A job title, not “stakeholders”: a fire inspection scheduler, a grants officer, a warehouse planner.
  • A recurring decision. Something that person decides every day or week, such as which sites to inspect, which claims to review first, or which trucks to send.
  • Data you can get legally. Public data is safest: a city’s open data portal, federal spending data from USAspending.gov, or disaster declarations from OpenFEMA. Or use notional data you generate yourself, which the postings explicitly invite. Source 1Deployment Strategist, Build to Apply - US Government (Washington, D.C.) - Palantir on LeverPublisherPalantir Technologies (Lever)Source typecompany job posting Never use data from an employer or client.
  • A place for AIP to do real work. Something a language model can read, draft or recommend inside the decision, with a person checking it. If AIP only decorates the app, pick again.

The postings are for US Government roles, so a public-sector user fits naturally, though nothing we found requires one. Here is the filter applied to three first ideas:

First idea Problem Rewrite
A dashboard of all federal spending No user, no decision A grants officer ranks which awards to review first
An AI chatbot over city documents The decision is unclear A permit reviewer gets a drafted deficiency letter to approve
Fire inspections ranked by risk Passes Keep it, name the scheduler, add an AIP briefing

The rewrites are what a strategist does in a first customer meeting: turn “we want AI” into one person, one decision and one measure. Our lesson on stakeholders and success metrics covers choosing the measure the customer already trusts, and the free question on building one feature from an app’s event data is the same move in miniature.

Scope one working workflow

Take the fire inspection idea. The scheduler for a city fire marshal’s office picks, each week, which businesses to inspect. Today that means a spreadsheet and memory. Your build gives them a ranked list with reasons, an AI-drafted briefing for each visit, and a way to schedule from it.

Scope it as a : every layer, thin, joined end to end. In Foundry terms, that is roughly:

  • Data in. Load the city’s inspection and violation history and its business license list, and clean them in a pipeline. Keep a note of every cleaning rule.
  • Objects the user thinks in. Model a Business, an Inspection and a Violation as object types in the Ontology, with the links between them.
  • A screen they act in. One app view: the ranked list, and a detail panel showing why each business is on it.
  • One action that writes back. “Schedule inspection” records the decision, so the tool changes what happens next instead of only displaying data.
  • The AIP step, at the center. An AIP Logic function reads the Business, its Inspections and its Violations from the and drafts two things: a short briefing for the inspector and a suggested focus for the visit (“check the kitchen hood suppression; it failed twice”). Its edits are staged for the scheduler to review, not applied blindly. Palantir’s AIP Logic overview says Logic edits can be automatically applied or staged for human review.

That is the whole build. No login pages, no second use case, no model training. The walking skeletons lesson explains why thin and complete beats deep and partial, and what you may fake. In a demo, you may fake the volume of data and the number of users. You may not fake the workflow or the AI output: if the briefing is wrong on some inputs, show one and say why.

A plan for the window, which is ours to adapt, not Palantir’s:

  • Before you start. Pick the user, find or generate the data, write the one-sentence problem.
  • Day 1 and Day 2. Data in, cleaned, and modeled as objects.
  • Day 3. The app view and the write-back action.
  • Day 4 and Day 5. The AIP Logic function: what it reads, what it drafts, the prompt, and the staged review. Test it on ten businesses and note where it goes wrong.
  • Day 6. Break it on purpose: missing fields, a business with no history, a briefing that invents a violation.
  • Day 7. Script, record, watch it back, submit. Anything after that is spare time, not scope.

The AIP step gets two days because the postings say it is assessed. Using AI while you build

The postings are explicit: LLMs, AI and other technical tools are permitted and encouraged while you build. Source 2Deployment Strategist, Build to Apply - US GovernmentPublisherPalantir Technologies (Lever)Source typecompany job posting Use them where they save the most time and check everything they produce.

Good uses. Drafting pipeline transforms and cleaning code. Explaining an unfamiliar Foundry feature. Generating notional records and ugly test cases: a business with no address, an inspection dated in the future. Tightening your script after you have written it.

Where to stay in charge. Picking the problem, choosing the ranking rule, and deciding what the AIP step may do on its own. Those are the judgments the video exists to show, so they should be yours.

Say it in the video. One sentence is enough: “I used an assistant to draft the cleaning code and the test records, and I checked each rule against the raw data by hand.” That turns AI use from a question into a sign of good process.

Keep two kinds of AI apart when you talk. The coding assistant helped you build; AIP is part of the product. Only the second is the capability the postings grade.

The encouragement covers the build. For any later live round, ask your recruiter before you assume.

Script the video

The limit is under 5 minutes, so aim to finish with time to spare. Source 2Deployment Strategist, Build to Apply - US GovernmentPublisherPalantir Technologies (Lever)Source typecompany job posting Film it as a customer demo, not a tour of what you clicked. The times below are our suggestion.

0:00

The user, the decision, and why.

Open on the app, not on Foundry’s home screen. “This is for the scheduler in a city fire marshal’s office. Every week they choose which businesses to inspect, and today that’s a spreadsheet and memory. I picked this because the inspection data is public and the decision repeats every week, so a better tool pays off every Monday.”

0:30

The data, briefly.

“It runs on the city’s public inspection history and business licenses, cleaned in a pipeline and modeled in the Ontology as businesses, inspections and violations.” Show the object graph for a few seconds, then move on.

1:00

The live run, with AIP named.

Open the ranked list and pick the top business. Then say it: “This is the AIP Logic function. It reads this business’s violation history from the Ontology and drafts a briefing and a suggested focus for the inspector. Nothing is saved until the scheduler approves it.” Edit one line, approve it, and schedule the inspection. Show the record update.

2:45

One decision.

Name the choice a reviewer would question, the alternative and why. “I ranked by time since last inspection and the severity of past violations, not by a model score, because a scheduler has to explain the list to a business owner who asks why they were picked. I used AIP for the briefing, where a wrong word gets caught in review.”

3:30

Where it breaks, and what’s next.

Show one failure. “For a business with no history, the briefing has nothing to go on, so it says so instead of guessing. With the office’s own data I’d add license age and complaint calls.” End on the next step you would take with this customer.

4:15

Stop.

That leaves margin under the limit for a slow page load.

For recording mistakes and exact words for trade-offs, the take-home walkthrough video post has a shot-by-shot script. Three points apply to this video in particular:

  • Watch the link signed out. Open the unlisted YouTube link in a private window before you submit.
  • Zoom in. Foundry screens are dense. Zoom the browser until a phone viewer could read the list.
  • Hide what is not yours. Close tabs, notifications and anything not public or notional.

Practice the part the video can’t fake

The build takes days. The judgment behind it, turning a vague request into one user and one decision, is what every strategist and round comes back to, and you can practice it today. Work through improving a city’s bus reliability as a warm-up, and study the worked case on a city’s building permits for the full method on a public-sector problem.
