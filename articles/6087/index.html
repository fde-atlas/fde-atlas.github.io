The craft around the work

What decides whether a technically successful engagement continues: not being single-threaded on one person, refusing the wrong request without losing the room, and building something that keeps running after you leave.

Capstone

Assemble the freight engagement into one dossier: every artifact the modules had you draft, completed, made consistent, and defended in a mock review. The output is an engagement you can talk about for an hour.

About this course

Before you start: Foundations of Forward Deployed Engineering, or a year or two of relevant experience. You should be comfortable reading an unfamiliar system, scoping a thin slice, and shipping something small into someone else's environment.

Foundations covered the first week of an engagement. This course covers all of it: the arc from a sales call where nobody has agreed what success means, to a system running in production that somebody else operates, to the review meeting where you find out whether it mattered.

The difference from the first course is that nothing here is a clean exercise. Every module works against a system you did not build, inside constraints you did not choose, for people whose incentives are not identical to yours. That is the job at this level, and the technical content is inseparable from the judgment around it.

One customer runs through the whole course. The project lessons follow a single fictional engagement, a composite built from the shape these deployments usually take rather than any real company. You will scope it, integrate it, deploy it, get it through a security review, put a model in front of it, and argue for its value. By the end you have a set of artifacts that fit together, which is a far better thing to bring to an interview than a pile of unrelated exercises.

Read the modules in order. The sequence is the engagement, and later modules assume the decisions made in earlier ones.

When you finish, you can

  • Run technical discovery alongside a sales team and write a pilot proposal that de-risks the deal
  • Build an integration that survives a legacy system, real auth, and a network you do not control
  • Deploy inside a customer environment and pass a security review without stalling for a month
  • Take an AI feature to production behind an eval harness, a cost budget and real guardrails
  • Give an agent authority over a workflow without giving it a blast radius
  • Measure the value you created and defend it to the person who funded it
  • Hand the whole thing over so it survives a reorganisation and your departure
  • Leave the capstone with a complete engagement dossier: nine artifacts, reconciled, defended against a skeptic
