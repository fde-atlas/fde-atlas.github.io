Summary

This introductory article from DeFactory's media site explains the relationship between agile and Scrum development. Agile, established by the 2001 Agile Manifesto, is an umbrella concept for iterative, feature-based development; Scrum is one specific method alongside XP and FDD. The article contrasts agile with waterfall development, describes Scrum's three fixed roles (product owner, developers, scrum master) and sprint cycles of roughly two weeks, and lists benefits: flexibility to changing customer needs, avoiding big-bang testing through per-sprint reviews, and shorter time to market. It also outlines five Scrum steps—product backlog, sprint planning, daily scrum, sprint review, and retrospective—and cautions that success requires strong communication, skilled members, and someone to set development direction. The piece ends with promotion of DeFactory's development support services.

Key points

  • Agile is an umbrella concept; Scrum is one method within it.
  • Scrum fixes three roles and runs development in ~2-week sprints.
  • Benefits: flexibility, no big-bang testing, faster time to market.
  • Five steps: backlog, sprint planning, daily scrum, review, retrospective.
  • Success requires communication skills, expertise, and clear direction.

To win in competition with other companies, it is important not only to respond quickly to fast-moving markets but also to deliver products that match customer needs.

When developing products that meet customer needs, introducing Scrum development can be expected to maximize results.

In this article, we explain in detail the differences between agile and Scrum development, the benefits of introducing Scrum in agile development, and the flow of Scrum development.

Agile development, first of all, refers to "a development method that rapidly repeats development in functional units."

This development method was born in 2001, when 17 developers who experimented with software development methods to replace conventional ones issued the "Agile Software Development Manifesto."

In other words, agile development does not refer to a single development method but to all development methods sharing common characteristics, so it can be defined as "a concept in software development."

On the other hand, Scrum development is "one of the agile development methods" and enables software development that leverages agility.

There are mainly three agile development methods; besides Scrum development, the following two methods exist.

・Extreme Programming (XP)

・Feature-Driven Development (FDD)

What all agile development methods have in common is that "product development proceeds in order from the highest-priority, highest-risk processes."

Also related to these development methods is "MVP development." MVP development is a method that incorporates only the minimum necessary features.

Especially for new businesses, you want to proceed with development while keeping costs as low as possible. With MVP development, you can minimize development costs and shorten the time to market.

Another attraction is the ability to quickly grasp user needs. Like agile development, MVP development can be considered an important, highly flexible development method.

A major characteristic of Scrum development is its emphasis on teamwork.

This is because the term "Scrum development" comes from the word "Scrum" used in rugby, and the fact that it is "a development method focused on the team working as one" is reflected in its name.

During development, team members' roles are fixed as follows.

・Product Owner: Manages the product as the overall person in charge

・Developers: Design and build software as skilled members

・Scrum Master: Acts as a coordinator, solving product issues and explaining rules

Another characteristic of Scrum development is progressing the product in "sprints." A sprint is a concept of dividing time into small units and setting goals within each unit, and it is an important point for executing Scrum development smoothly.

The representative development method compared with agile development is waterfall development. As mentioned earlier, the characteristic of agile development is rapidly repeating development in functional units.

For example, in agile development, work is divided into Feature A, Feature B, and so on, and the cycle of "planning → design → implementation → testing" is run for each functional unit.

On the other hand, in waterfall development, as the name "Water Fall" suggests, the cycle of "planning → design → implementation → testing" is run for the entire set of features, which differs from agile development.

Waterfall development proceeds to the next phase only after each phase is reliably completed, so it is a method often used for large-scale development.

Its advantage is that rework is less likely to occur, but it also has the disadvantage that if specification changes occur in later phases, the workload tends to increase.

However, with agile development, since development is carried out in small functional units, it is possible to respond flexibly even if specification changes occur in later phases.

What benefits does agile development, which enables efficient development in functional units, offer? Below, we introduce the benefits of agile development, focusing on three points.

By introducing Scrum and proceeding with development, it becomes possible to create products that match customer needs.

This is because, among agile development methods, whose characteristic is "rapidly running PDCA cycles for each feature," Scrum development is a method with superior agility.

In a sprint in Scrum development, the features to be worked on within a single period are broken down into small pieces before development begins. This makes it possible to respond flexibly even if part of the customer's requirements changes midway.

If a modification arises in the late stages of a project, the cost of going back can be enormous. However, with Scrum development, you can stay firmly aligned with customer needs while smoothly making course corrections during development.

Being able to avoid big bang testing is another benefit of Scrum development. For example, when development proceeds as one large sequential unit, a big bang test must be carried out in the final stages of the project.

In a big bang test, all the components of the software or product are tested and verified all at once, so if a defect occurs, it takes a great deal of time to determine "which part is actually causing the problem."

However, in Scrum development, a review is conducted at the end of every sprint. This makes it possible to avoid big bang testing and proceed efficiently through the entire development process.

That said, in some cases, the fact that "customer evaluation is required at every review" can be considered a drawback. This is because in waterfall development and similar approaches, customer evaluation is generally only required at milestones (key stages of the process).

Therefore, to run Scrum development smoothly, it is important to build a relationship of solid cooperation with the customer.

Another benefit of Scrum development is the expected reduction in time to market. This is because Scrum development starts with the highest-priority features to be implemented.

As a result, the features a product should have can be implemented from an early stage, enabling development that eliminates wasteful processes.

Furthermore, since development effort can be estimated for each sprint, development hours can be calculated more accurately than with other development methods. Introducing Scrum development is an important point as a method for maintaining an advantage over competitors.

Scrum development can be carried out in five main steps. In the following sections, let's look at the flow.

A product backlog is a list that compiles, in one place, the features and requirement items prioritized for the product.

It is generally defined by the product owner and is constantly managed so that the highest-priority items appear at the top of the list.

In Scrum development, it can be called a "guideline" and is extremely important for gaining an overview of the project.

At the start of a sprint, the product owner, developers, and Scrum Master first gather to make a plan.

At that time, based on the product backlog created in Step 1, the features to be worked on in that sprint are selected and an execution plan is created. Note that the list of features to be worked on within a sprint is called the "sprint backlog."

The sprint backlog is basically held by the development side and is not shared with the client. Also, the sprint period is generally about two weeks, and even when long, it is considered to be about one month.

Every day, a meeting called the "daily scrum" is held at the same time. The key is to keep it short, around 5 to 15 minutes, and it is used to report progress and issues and confirm how work should proceed.

Since Scrum development places great emphasis on teamwork, the daily scrum is one of the important points for running development smoothly.

The purpose is not simply to report progress, but to communicate so that issues and problems are properly shared within the team.

After the daily scrum is finished, the day's content is shared with the product owner.

On the final day of the sprint, with the product owner in attendance, testing and evaluation of the deliverables, called the "sprint review," is conducted.

The purpose is to conduct a demonstration and confirm whether the sprint backlog requirements have been met.

If, by any chance, the requirements are not met, the release is judged to be postponed. Also, depending on the results, the specifications may be changed and the backlog may need to be created anew.

Ideally, the sprint review should also be attended by the client and stakeholders. Since opinions can be exchanged from each perspective, a more meaningful review can be achieved.

The sprint retrospective is a "review meeting" of the sprint. In Scrum development, development is never completed in a single sprint; sprints are repeated.

In the sprint retrospective, the following topics are mainly discussed.

- Evaluation of the current sprint - What went well and what the issues were - Examples of issue resolution - Improvements to address in subsequent sprints

After the above content is discussed and shared within the team, it is fed back into the next sprint, and work proceeds toward better product development.

While introducing Scrum into agile development has many benefits, there are also points to be aware of. The following three items are the main cautions in Scrum development.

- Communication skills across the entire team are important - Members must have a certain level of skill - Someone who defines the direction of development is necessary

In Scrum development, there are many opportunities for communication within the team, such as the daily scrum and the review on the final day of the sprint.

Therefore, it can be said that having a certain degree of communication skills across all team members is essential for proceeding with development smoothly.

Also, in Scrum development, someone who defines the direction of development is essential. This is because without someone who understands the overall specifications, the process cannot be properly managed.

In this way, it is no exaggeration to say that in Scrum development, deploying engineers with advanced skills and experience holds the key to a project's success.

Agile development is a "general term" for development methods that run cycles on a per-feature basis, and Scrum development is one of those methods.

Introducing Scrum development offers many benefits, such as "enabling product development that matches customer needs" and "shortening lead time to market."

However, to maximize the benefits of Scrum development, the experience and skills of team members are also important factors.
