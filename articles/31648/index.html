An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer embedded in a customer's team who deploys software or AI capabilities in the customer's real business environment. According to PostHog, this role originated at Palantir; in recent years, AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic have also been hiring for it. Its core characteristics: writing production code, working directly with customers, and being accountable for "making the business work," rather than just doing demos or pre-sales.

What is an FDE

FDE is a type of engineering role, and also a delivery model. Instead of sitting in a back-office R&D team waiting for requirements, the engineer is embedded in the customer's team, working within the customer's data, systems, and processes to close the gap between "what the product can do" and "what the customer actually needs." According to PostHog, this work can involve being on-site for weeks, joining the customer's shared communication channels, or developing directly within the customer's infrastructure.

The biggest difference between an FDE and a regular software engineer is where their time goes: software engineers spend most of their time building reusable, general-purpose features, while FDEs spend most of their time on the customer's side, focusing on one customer at a time and solving one specific problem thoroughly.

Origin: why Palantir, and why it's hot again now

According to PostHog, when Palantir faced intelligence-sector customers, it found that even a single demo could require weeks of NDAs and security clearances. Palantir's solution was to "forward deploy" engineers to customer sites, working side by side with customers under proper compliance and authorization.

Starting in 2025, this role has surged in the AI industry. According to an eWeek report from November 2025 (citing Financial Times research), OpenAI assembled an FDE team in early 2024 and planned to expand it to about 50 engineers in 2025; Anthropic planned to grow its applied AI team—including FDEs and product engineers—fivefold that year; Indeed data shows FDE job postings grew more than 800% from January to September 2025.

The reason is no mystery: large models are highly capable, but enterprises have scattered data, complex processes, and caution around sensitive data. Turning model capabilities into verifiable business results requires someone working on-site in the business. The FDE is the person who takes on that job.

What skills does an FDE need

  • Engineering skills: able to understand customer systems, write deployable code, and do integration and debugging; for AI work, also prompts, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), agent orchestration, and evals.
  • Business understanding: able to understand the problems frontline staff describe and break them into verifiable small goals.
  • Communication and drive: able to align expectations with management, work with frontline staff, and clearly communicate results.
  • Product sense: turning one-off solutions into reusable components rather than starting from scratch for every customer.
  • Judgment: knowing what to do, what not to do, and when human oversight is needed.

According to PostHog's summary of hiring requirements at companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Databricks, common requirements include customer empathy, communication and presentation skills, product sense, and a willingness to blend engineering, consulting, and delivery.

How to become an FDE

For those with an engineering background, fill in AI application skills: prompts and evals, RAG knowledge bases, agents and tool calling, and integration with common enterprise systems. For those without an engineering background but with deep business experience, start with AI tools and Vibe Coding (driving AI coding tools with natural language): first turn a small process in your own business into something that works, then gradually build engineering skills. Either way, the most convincing proof is real work you've built and a retrospective you can articulate clearly.

For a concrete 90-day path, competency model, and portfolio advice, see "How to Become an FDE."

How enterprises can use the FDE model to deploy AI

For enterprises, the value of the FDE model is having AI projects driven on-site in the business by people who know AI, rather than buying software no one uses or producing a proposal no one implements. The common approach: first diagnose and identify processes worth improving; pick a pilot scenario with clear pain points, well-defined rules, and high repetition; have on-site or semi-on-site engineers work with business staff to build a verifiable version; validate with business metrics, then expand to adjacent scenarios.

This model isn't right for every enterprise. For highly standardized needs where products work out of the box, standard products plus training are more cost-effective; the FDE model suits scenarios with complex processes, sensitive data, and custom integration needs. See "How Enterprises Can Deploy AI with the FDE Model" for details.

FDE FAQ

What does FDE mean?

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, commonly translated into Chinese as "Forward Deployed Engineer." It refers to an engineer embedded in a customer's team who deploys software or AI capabilities in the customer's real business environment and is accountable for the results. According to PostHog, the role originated at Palantir.

Is an FDE the same as a Solutions Engineer?

No. In the common division of labor, Solutions Engineers focus on proposals, demos, and technical Q&A before signing, while FDEs embed long-term with customers, write production code, and are accountable for deployment results. However, role definitions vary across companies, so refer to the specific job description.

Why are AI companies hiring FDEs?

Because model capabilities need to be converted into verifiable business results within enterprises, which requires someone to go into the customer's data, systems, and processes to do integration and debugging. According to research cited by eWeek, OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, and others are all expanding their FDE or similar teams, and Indeed data shows that job postings for related roles grew by more than 800% from January to September 2025.

Can you be an FDE without knowing how to code?

The FDE role itself requires writing production-ready code. But if you have deep business experience, you can first use AI tools and Vibe Coding to turn small workflows in your business into something runnable, then gradually build up your engineering skills; you can also pursue directions like AI implementation consultant, AI operations, or business-side AI lead.

Should enterprises adopt the FDE model?

It suits scenarios with complex processes, sensitive data, and a need for customized integration; for standardized needs and out-of-the-box products, standard products plus training are more cost-effective. We recommend doing a diagnostic first, then deciding.
