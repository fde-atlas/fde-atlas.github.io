What is a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)? Why are OpenAI and Anthropic both competing for this kind of talent?

In the 2026 AI industry job market, one job title is growing faster than any algorithm role: Forward Deployed Engineer, literally "Forward Deployed Engineer," known in the industry as FDE.

This role is not a new invention. It was born at Palantir—the company famous for "installing software into the world's most difficult organizations." Early on, Palantir discovered a harsh truth: when selling the platform to Walmart or the US Army, the real bottleneck was not the model, not the compute, but the messy, never-documented real business inside the customer's organization. So they moved their strongest engineers from back-office desks to the customer's site, sitting alongside the customer's frontline staff until the system actually ran. That is the origin of the FDE.

What does an FDE actually do

Broken down, an FDE's day is roughly allocated like this:

  • 30% understanding the business: interviewing frontline staff, observing real workflows, digging through historical data. The goal is singular—figure out "how the work is actually done," not "how the process manual says it's done."
  • 40% writing production-grade code: data pipelines, integration layers, model orchestration, user-facing interfaces. Note: production-grade—not a demo notebook, but a system with monitoring, tests, and alerting.
  • 30% on alignment and handover: confirming the approach with key customer stakeholders, training the customer's team, "handing over" the system rather than "leaving behind a black box only we know how to maintain."

This allocation explains why the FDE role is hard to replace with "pure remote, pure outsourcing"—understanding the business requires being on-site, and handover requires face-to-face trust.

Why FDEs suddenly exploded in 2026

Three forces converged at once:

First, AI capability is abundant; deployment capability is scarce. By 2026, foundation models are strong enough that for most enterprise problems the bottleneck is no longer the model, but "how to connect the model to a 20-year-old ERP, middleware written by 3 outsourced teams, and a workforce accustomed to Excel." The bottleneck has shifted to the "last mile," and the FDE is exactly who does the last mile.

Second, AI coding tools have multiplied the output of a single FDE. Agents like Claude Code and Codex make it possible for one engineer to command 3-6 parallel "squads." In the Palantir era, an FDE did one person's work; in 2026, an FDE leads a whole team. Per-person efficiency has improved by an order of magnitude, making the FDE model economically viable for enterprises for the first time.

Third, top companies are voting with real money. FDE hiring at OpenAI, Anthropic, and major domestic model vendors has doubled repeatedly, with salaries generally higher than pure R&D roles at the same level. The market voting with its feet says: this last mile is worth paying for.

What an FDE is not

Three common misconceptions:

1. An FDE is not a Sales Engineer. A Sales Engineer's KPI is signing deals; an FDE's KPI is the system running stably in the customer's production environment and real changes in business metrics. The former presents slides; the latter watches dashboards. 2. An FDE is not on-site outsourcing. Outsourcing sells person-days and stacks headcount on demand; an FDE is accountable for outcomes, and writing code is just one of the means. 3. An FDE is not "a product manager who can code." An FDE writes production-grade code and does their own on-call and bug fixes. A product manager defines problems; an FDE both defines problems and solves them hands-on.

Why enterprises should care

Because the FDE model has gone from "something only companies with million-dollar deal sizes like Palantir could afford" to a standard delivery method that ordinary enterprises can afford. A 50-person factory can hire an FDE with an AI squad on a project basis, and 8 weeks later have 6 fewer manual inspection stations on the quality line—unthinkable three years ago.

To judge whether your service provider is a "true FDE model," three questions suffice:

  • Will the engineers enter your workshop/group chat before starting work to observe real workflows?
  • Is the quote based on person-days, or on milestones + KPIs?
  • Does the system keep running after delivery? If business metrics don't change within 30 days, do they take responsibility?

If all three answers are yes, you're buying the FDE model; if any one is vague, you may just be buying an outsourcing contract dressed up with AI buzzwords.
