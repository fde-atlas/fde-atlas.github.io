Who Owns the Promise Between an FDE and Sales?

Falkster.AI · 2026-09-29 · 1 min read

Summary

This article argues that sales owns the bookings number while the FDE owns scope, and the two must align on a single page before a contract is signed. Before signature, the FDE sorts every promised outcome into three columns: ships today, needs a build with an estimate and named approver, or a clear no. Citing Henley Wing Chiu's Bloomberry analysis of 1,000 FDE job postings, the author notes zero percent of FDE roles carry quotas, and a16z's Joe Schmidt argues services should be sold at cost.   • Sales owns the number; the FDE owns scope, aligned pre-signature.
  • Promised outcomes are sorted into ships, builds, or no columns.

Full text

THE SHORT ANSWER

Sales owns the number and the FDE owns the scope, and the two meet on one page before the signature. The promise to a customer is made in the deck and on the second call, by someone whose number is bookings, and the forward deployed engineer inherits it the moment the contract is signed. So before any contract with an FDE attached is signed, the FDE sorts every outcome in the proposal into three columns: ships (the product does it today or it lives in the customer layer, with a date), builds (it needs a harness change, with an estimate and the name of the engineer who has to approve it), or no (it needs a core change, or the product will not do it this year). Both names go on the page and it is attached to the contract. The FDE does not carry a quota: Henley Wing Chiu's Bloomberry analysis of 1,000 FDE job postings found exactly zero percent are quota-carrying roles, and Joe Schmidt at a16z wrote that implementation leads rarely want one and that services should be sold at cost. Variable pay goes on the customer's outcome and the renewal or expansion it produces, never on bookings and never on an accelerator for deployments shipped, which is a quota with a lab coat on. Credit splits at the signature: the booking is the account executive's, the expansion is the FDE's. Ron Schmelzer's Forbes piece of September 19, 2026 quotes Antanas Bakšys of Ace Waves, whose FDEs are part of the sales team and participate before the contract is signed, with contracts that pay on resolutions; that is the strongest form of the handshake, and the one-page version is what a company does when it cannot send an engineer to every prospect.
