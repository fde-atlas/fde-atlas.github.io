FDE vs Sales engineer

Plank · 2026-09-11 · 3 min read

Summary

Drawing on Plank's census of 1262 verified open FDE roles across 555 companies, this article contrasts forward deployed engineers with sales engineers. The FDE sits in engineering, builds the customer's production system after the deal closes, and is measured on deployment outcomes; the sales engineer sits in sales, carries a quota, and is measured on bookings. Compensation, time per account, and coding duties differ accordingly. Only 0.6% of FDE postings blend sales language into the title, far below blends with solutions or applied AI engineering, showing the two roles rarely merge. The piece ends with guidance on which role a company needs.

Key points

  • FDEs belong to engineering and are measured on deployment outcomes, not bookings
  • Sales engineers carry quotas with 70/30 or 80/20 base-to-variable pay
  • FDEs spend months to years per account; sales engineers days to weeks
  • Only 0.6% of FDE postings mix in sales titles, versus 6.7% with applied AI
  • 54% of FDE job descriptions say customer-facing, but that is not revenue-carrying

A sales engineer is a quota-carrying member of a sales team who provides the technical credibility that closes a deal. A forward-deployed engineer is a member of an engineering organization who builds the customer's production system after the deal closes. The sales engineer is measured on bookings; the forward-deployed engineer is measured on whether the deployment works.

Side by side

Forward-deployed engineer Sales engineer
Organizational home Engineering or delivery Sales
Measured on Deployment outcome, adoption, retention Bookings and quota attainment
Compensation Base plus equity Base plus commission against quota
Time per account Months to years Days to weeks

What a sales engineer actually does

A sales engineer partners with an account executive to carry the technical side of a deal: discovery, demos, proofs of concept, objection handling, and security review. The role is attached to a quota, and the compensation reflects it — typically a 70/30 or 80/20 base-to-variable split against booked revenue.

Where they overlap

"Sales engineer" and "solutions engineer" are frequently the same job under different names, and where a company runs both, the sales engineer is the one closer to the quota. Against a forward-deployed engineer the distinction is cleaner than with a solutions engineer, because the compensation structure forces it: a quota pulls attention toward the next deal, while an outcome commitment pulls it toward the current deployment. Very few people do both well at once, and companies that ask for it usually get a sales engineer with extra steps.

What the hiring data shows

Plank's census contains 7 FDE postings that also carry sales language in the title — 0.5% of 1,276. The comparison that matters is with the neighbouring title: forward-deployed and solutions engineering fuse three times as often (20 postings, 1.6%), and forward-deployed and applied AI engineering 12 times as often (82, 6.4%). Employers merge forward deployment with almost every adjacent engineering title. They do not merge it with sales.

Which one do you need?

Forward-deployed engineer

  • Signed customers are not reaching production.
  • The integration work is genuinely bespoke per customer.
  • You need the same person accountable in month six as in week one.

Sales engineer

  • You need more technical coverage in the pipeline.
  • Deals die at security review, POC, or architecture objections.
  • Your product deploys without meaningful custom engineering.

Questions people ask

  • Is a forward-deployed engineer a sales role?
  • No. Forward-deployed engineers sit in engineering and are typically not on a quota. They are customer-facing — 54% of FDE job descriptions say so explicitly — but customer-facing is not the same as revenue-carrying.
  • Do forward-deployed engineers carry a quota?
  • Rarely. The standard package is an engineering one: base plus equity. Some companies attach a deployment or renewal bonus, but a booking quota is unusual, because it would compete with the outcome the role exists to deliver.
  • What is the difference between a sales engineer and a solutions engineer?
  • Usually nothing but the company's naming convention. Where a firm runs both titles, the sales engineer is normally the quota-attached role in the deal team and the solutions engineer is the broader technical resource, sometimes spanning post-sale support.
