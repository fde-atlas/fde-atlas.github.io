"Engineers go into the field, identify challenges, and build the systems right there." Bringing the philosophy and practice of "FDE," an approach spreading around the world, to Japan's small and medium-sized businesses. As the first case, support for the Murase Group in Sasebo has begun

Minna Systems Co., Ltd. (head office: Sasebo, Nagasaki Prefecture; Representative Director: Yuki Noda) is fully launching DX support for small and medium-sized businesses struggling with labor shortages, in which engineers experience actual operations firsthand, identify challenges, and consistently carry the work through to building AI-powered automation systems.

This approach is called "FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)" and is now spreading rapidly around the world. Minna Systems is bringing it to small and medium-sized businesses. As its first case, the company has begun supporting the Murase Group, based in Sasebo City.

1. A new initiative launched by Minna Systems amid deepening labor shortages

In regional areas, once people leave due to a declining birthrate and aging population, hiring replacements is difficult.

In response to this reality faced by many small and medium-sized businesses, Minna Systems proposes an alternative: "create systems that keep running even when people change, without relying on hiring."

The first case is the Murase Group, which operates a driving school, hotel, and car rental businesses in Sasebo, Nagasaki Prefecture.

IT engineers will go on-site and, starting with streamlining accounting operations, carry out a comprehensive initiative that also looks ahead to stronger customer acquisition and growth strategies through AI.

2. A worldwide trend: "engineers going into the field"

The approach Minna Systems practices is known globally as "FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)" and is spreading rapidly.

Originally created and popularized by the US technology company Palantir, the concept calls for engineers to go directly into customers' business operations and take end-to-end responsibility, from identifying challenges through system implementation and adoption. In recent years, major AI companies have successively established FDE organizations, and it has been reported that overseas job postings related to FDE surged 729% year over year in the past year. (According to US-based Indeed / Business Insider)

3. Why do answers emerge when "engineers experience the work firsthand"?

Minna Systems' support begins with engineers experiencing the actual work themselves.

As a system development company, we have long been involved in system development for many small and medium-sized businesses, starting from interviews and requirements definition. Along the way, engineers often notice things when they actually see the front lines: "This never came up in the interviews, but it would work much better if we did it this way."

There are countless ways to use AI. If you have implementation know-how and knowledge of how to streamline operations, the benefits can be realized quickly. However, as a prerequisite, you must understand the flow of the work. Where should AI be placed, and how should it connect with existing systems? Unless that is decided, introducing tools will not produce the desired results. What is missing is not tools, but people who understand how to use them and implement them accordingly. And since both workflows and existing systems differ from company to company, the answer cannot be found without going into each individual workplace. That is precisely why the FDE approach is necessary.

For both system features and AI, the way the work looks is completely different between someone who has "used it" and someone who has "implemented it."

Only once engineers grasp the full picture of the workflows and existing systems does it become clear "where and how AI should be integrated for maximum effect." That is why engineers experience the work on-site. We believe this is the shortest path to building systems that make effective use of AI.

4. The reality facing Japan's small and medium-sized businesses

These challenges are not exceptional cases.

As the working population shrinks due to aging, labor shortages have become a structural business risk for regional small and medium-sized businesses.

According to a Teikoku Databank survey (April 2026), "labor shortage bankruptcies," where labor shortages are the direct cause, reached 441 cases in fiscal 2025, updating the record high for the third consecutive year and exceeding 400 cases for the first time on a fiscal-year basis.

Meanwhile, a survey by the Organization for Small & Medium Enterprises and Regional Innovation (February 2026) found that only 39.1% of small and medium-sized businesses have been able to pursue DX. The top reasons for slow progress were "no IT/DX personnel" and "not knowing where to start."

Not enough people. Want to do something about it with DX. But don't know what to do.

These concerns are structural challenges common to small and medium-sized businesses across Japan.

5. Comment from the Representative

Yuki Noda, Representative Director, Minna Systems Co., Ltd.

AI is not just for large corporations. Like PCs and the internet, it will eventually reach every company. And I believe regional companies, where the workforce keeps shrinking, stand to benefit the most.

However, AI does not produce results on its own. What is needed is "AI × people." Results only emerge when there are people who understand the front lines and can integrate AI into operations. The engineers who take on that role are called FDEs around the world.

When we opened a branch in Sasebo in 2022, what struck me deeply was the severity of the regional labor shortage. People leave, and no one can be hired to replace them. Yet operations must keep running. Despite this, there were no realistic measures available, and no examples of how AI could actually be used. That is why I want to create a state where companies in every region can access the latest AI. First, we will deliver results on-site at the Murase Group, then bring them to regional companies facing the same concerns.

6. Comment from the Murase Group's Representative

MURASE GROUP: Representative of the Murase Group / Takahiro Murase, Representative Director, Honest Co., Ltd.

In continuing to run a business in a regional area, labor shortages are a challenge you cannot avoid.

With multiple businesses—a driving school, hotel, and car rentals—how to build "systems that keep running even when people change" has long been a concern for us. We hear the word AI every day, but which of our operations could it be used for, and how? We simply didn't know.

Minna Systems' engineers actually go on-site and keep finding opportunities for improvement in the everyday operations we had taken for granted. They don't dismiss long-standing practices; they first understand why things are done the way they are, and then work with us to select which parts can be replaced with AI. Rather than giving instructions from the outside, they stand alongside us in the field. That is why we were able to take the first step.

We see this not merely as improving operational efficiency, but as an investment in the company's future. Change starts on the front lines—that is what we truly feel now. We would be delighted if we could serve as a model for other regional companies facing the same challenges.
