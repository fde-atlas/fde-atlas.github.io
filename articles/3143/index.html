AWS Forward-Deployed Engineering: FDE Player Dossier

Plank · 2026-06-14 · 12 min read

Summary

AWS launched a forward deployed engineering unit on June 30, 2026, funded with $1B entirely from Amazon's balance sheet—unlike OpenAI's ~$4B and Anthropic's ~$1.5B deployment joint ventures, which required private-equity partners. Pods of 5-6 engineers embed in Fortune 500 customers' own AWS accounts, following a 45/45/45 cadence (45 minutes to ideate, 45 hours to validate, 45 days to ship) and leaving behind knowledge graphs, semantic layers, MCP servers, and trained internal champions. Named customers include the NFL, NBA, Southwest, BMW, and Lyft. A partner-led tier extends the methodology through Premier Partners. The unit started with hundreds of engineers and plans to grow into the thousands.

Key points

  • $1B funded entirely from Amazon's balance sheet; no PE partner, unlike OpenAI and Anthropic JVs
  • 5-6 engineer pods build production AI systems inside customers' own AWS accounts
  • 45/45/45 cadence: ideate in 45 minutes, validate in 45 hours, ship in 45 days
  • 9+ launch customers including NFL, NBA, Southwest, BMW, and Lyft
  • Partner-led tier via AWS Partner Network extends coverage without proportional headcount growth

Their role in FDE

  • The play
  • Embed, ship and leave. AWS says engagements are designed to leave the customer self-sufficient: the customer's engineers move from observers to co-builders to autonomous operators, and the systems, knowledge graphs and runbooks stay in the customer's own AWS account.
  • The economics
  • $1B of internal Amazon resources and no billable hours: AWS structures deployments around shared goals and business results. Partner Innovative Solutions sells a fixed-price POD for one defined production outcome, with an optional success bonus.
  • The edge
  • Speed by method: a 45-minute, 45-hour, 45-day cadence to ideate, validate and ship, with agents handling each phase while engineers verify and steer. NFL CIO Gary Brantley says AWS engineers got two products into production “in just weeks.”
  • The catch
  • Capacity. AWS is starting with hundreds of engineers, in pods of five or six per customer, and VP Asa Kalavade calls qualified FDE candidates “unicorns.” Customers without direct access go through partners such as Innovative Solutions.

FDE model · approach · differentiation

What they do

How engagements work

AWS engineers embed with enterprise customers, starting with a pod of roughly five or six engineers, and build production agentic AI systems that run in the customer's own AWS environment. AWS says FDE differs in three ways: it is agentic-first, it compresses timelines from months to days, and it is designed so customers are self-sufficient when a deployment ends. As projects advance, customer engineers move from observers to co-builders to autonomous operators. Engagements follow a 45-minute, 45-hour, 45-day cadence for ideating, validating and shipping.

Methodology: AI-DLC

Engagements use what AWS calls the AI-Driven Development Lifecycle (AI-DLC), a new approach to software development in which agents handle each phase while engineers verify and steer, and each project improves the next. AWS says FDE uses purpose-built agents to compress the development and launch of AI applications from months into days. diginomica reports that Vasquez lists AWS's spec-driven Kiro alongside Codex and Claude Code as coding tools.

What customers keep

FDE teams deploy a semantic layer into the customer's own AWS account that connects to enterprise data, enriches metadata and publishes a governed, versioned knowledge graph that agents reason over, so domain expertise stays in the customer's systems. Customers keep the deployed systems, knowledge graphs, runbooks, architectural documentation and trained internal champions ready to operate independently.

Pricing

AWS publishes no rate card for direct engagements and says deployments are structured around shared goals and business results, not billable hours. Innovative Solutions, an AWS Premier Tier Services Partner, prices its partner offering in three tiers: (1) AI Assessment, a fixed-price engagement in which an embedded engineer evaluates generative AI readiness, identifies use cases and builds a proof of concept on the customer's data; (2) POD (Product-Oriented Delivery), a fixed-price engagement to deliver one defined production outcome, with an optional success bonus; (3) FDS Retainer, a monthly retainer that keeps an engineer on the account, with optional bonuses tied to cost-efficiency goals.

Partner-led tier

AWS launched the partner-led tier through the AWS Partner Network on Jun 30, 2026, with a limited set of partners. Partners build ring-fenced, AWS-credentialed engineering teams, and their engineers must pass an AWS-defined production-engineering bar before working with any customer. Partners permanently own a reusable delivery harness covering domain ontologies, evaluation frameworks, MCP servers and agent-operations tooling. The harness also includes a context graph, an architecture agent, a governance layer and a capability registry. On Oct 1, 2026 AWS added three free partner training pathways on AWS Skill Builder (Ground, Orchestrate, Prove) and two credentials, FDE Validated and FDE Advanced.

  • Target customers
  • AWS job postings describe FDEs embedding directly inside a strategic enterprise customer. Named FDE customers include two sports leagues (the NFL and NBA), Cox Automotive and Southwest Airlines. The others are Ricoh (office technology), the Allen Institute (research) and Hoxton Wealth (wealth management). Constellation Research reports that regulated industries such as financial services, healthcare and public sector will be target areas, and AWS is hiring cleared FDEs for US government work. Partners such as Innovative Solutions serve mid-market and enterprise customers that do not have direct access to AWS FDE.
  • Pricing model
  • No public rate card for direct engagements; AWS says deployments are structured around shared goals and business results, not billable hours. Partner tiers at Innovative Solutions: a fixed-price AI Assessment; a fixed-price POD (Product-Oriented Delivery) for one defined production outcome, with an optional success bonus; and a monthly FDS Retainer with optional bonuses tied to cost-efficiency goals.
  • Tech stack
  • Amazon Bedrock, with OpenAI and Anthropic models among the options Vasquez cites; a semantic layer and governed, versioned knowledge graph deployed in the customer's own AWS account; AWS Context, a governed agentic services layer; purpose-built agents under the AI-Driven Development Lifecycle; and coding tools including AWS's Kiro alongside Codex and Claude Code. Partner teams also receive MCP servers and agent-operations tooling.

Backed by1

  • Amazon.com, Inc. Sole owner. AWS funds the unit with $1 billion of internal Amazon resources, with no joint venture or outside investment; OpenAI's and Anthropic's deployment ventures were formed with private-equity partners.

Scale & footprint

By the numbers

  • AWS is starting the unit with hundreds of engineers, drawing on experienced engineers within Amazon and AWS while also recruiting externally (Kalavade, CNBC, Oct 15, 2026).

  • AWS describes FDE as a global organization operating across AMER, EMEA, APJC and WWPS geographies. Open FDE roles are in the US, London and São Paulo. In Asia-Pacific, Tokyo, Seoul, Singapore, Sydney and Melbourne have open roles.
