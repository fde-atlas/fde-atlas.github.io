How to Prepare for FDE Coding Interviews: Practical Problem Examples, How to Solve Them, and Prep by Format

Original title: FDEのコーディング面接対策は？実務型の問題例と解き方・形式別の準備

FDE総研 · 2026-11-16 · 11 min read

Summary

This article from FDE Soken argues that FDE coding interviews weigh the process of turning vague requirements into working code more than speed at reaching a correct answer. It proposes four evaluation axes—requirement clarification, data handling, building a minimal working version, and explanation—and warns that standard SWE algorithm prep leaves the first two undertrained. It walks through three practice problem types (sensor CSV aggregation, paginated API fetching with failure handling, customer master data matching), gives format-specific advice for live coding, take-home, and algorithm rounds, a four-week study plan, and gap-filling priorities by background (SWE, IT consulting, PdM, SIer). It also covers AI tool etiquette, noting rules vary by company and should be confirmed via official pages like Palantir Careers. The framework reflects the editors' and VACAN practitioners' views, not official hiring criteria.

Key points

  • FDE coding interviews are judged on four axes: requirement clarification, data handling, minimal working version, and explanation.
  • Three practice problem types: CSV aggregation, API integration with retries and resume, and master data matching with human-review fallback.
  • Prep differs by format: verbalize questions in live coding, document decisions in READMEs for take-homes, drill basic algorithm patterns.
  • Gaps depend on background: SWEs need to ask before coding; consultants and PdMs need more hands-on implementation; SIer alumni need self-directed decisions.
  • Confirm AI tool rules beforehand and default to not using them; the key skill is validating AI output against real business data.

FDE interviews are less about quickly producing the correct answer and more about the process of turning ambiguous requirements into something that works while clarifying them—that is how FDE Soken frames it. Based on that focus of evaluation, this article covers practical problem examples, preparation for each interview format, and differences in preparation by prior job role, in that order.

Note that interview formats, evaluation criteria, and the handling of AI tools vary by company and over time. This article organizes how to prepare based on FDE Soken's comparison axes and the perspective of practitioners involved in customer deployments at our operating company, VACAN; it is not an official selection criterion of any particular company. Always check each company's official recruiting page (e.g., Palantir Careers) for application details.

How do FDE coding interviews differ from SWE interviews?

According to FDE Soken's analysis, FDE coding interviews are evaluated on four axes—"requirement clarification," "data handling," "working minimal version," and "explanation"—and the weight placed on algorithmic speed is lower. The role of an FDE can be confirmed in Palantir's official blog article introducing FDEs.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineering role that embeds with customers on-site and handles everything from problem definition to implementation and operations. Because FDEs build things on the spot in front of customers' business data and requirements, their coding interviews also tend to take forms closer to real-world work.

What does FDE Soken's "four axes of practical coding interviews" look at?

FDE Soken's four axes are a framework for evaluating candidates not only on correct code but also on four points: clarification, data processing, incremental implementation, and explanation.

Evaluation axis (FDE Soken's analysis)Behaviors likely to be observed in interviewsWeight in SWE interviews (general tendency)
1. Requirement clarificationChecking input formats, exceptions, and priorities before starting to write codeMedium (problem statements are often clearly defined)
2. Data handlingNoticing and handling missing values, duplicates, inconsistent types, and time discrepanciesLow to medium
3. Working minimal versionGetting the whole thing running first, then fixing itMedium
4. Explanation and handoffArticulating the reasons for decisions and the issues left unresolvedMedium

* The evaluation axes were defined by the FDE Soken editorial team based on Palantir's public information and our existing internal articles (Skills required for FDEs); they are not official scoring criteria of any company (as of November 2026).

What is missing if you use SWE interview preparation as-is?

If you approach FDE interviews with only SWE-oriented preparation, your practice on requirement clarification (axis 1) and data handling (axis 2) tends to be insufficient. SWE-oriented algorithm preparation is built on the assumption that problem statements are strictly defined. In FDE interviews, it is said that some formats deliberately leave gaps or ambiguities in the problem statement to see whether candidates ask questions.

From the perspective of practitioners involved in customer deployments at VACAN, on-site data almost never arrives exactly as specified. For this reason, whether you can first ask "does this data really come in this form?" tends to make a noticeable difference in how FDE-like you appear. The differences between SWEs and FDEs themselves are organized in the difference between FDEs and engineers.

What kinds of problems appear in FDE coding interviews, and how do you solve the examples?

The practical example problems FDE Soken envisions fall into three types: CSV aggregation, external API integration, and master data matching. The subject matter is close to the real work of FDEs handling customers' business data (Palantir's official blog).

The example problems presented here were created by FDE Soken for practice purposes. They are not past questions from any particular company.

Example 1: How to aggregate hourly congestion from sensor CSV data

FDE Soken's Example 1 is a problem of computing hourly average congestion from a store sensor CSV, where data cleaning accounts for half of the solution.

The problem statement is: "Read a CSV with columns for store ID, time, and headcount, and return the average headcount per store per hour." The steps to solve it are as follows.

  • Clarify: Ask the interviewer about the time zone of timestamps, how to handle rows with blank headcounts, and how to handle duplicate rows with the same timestamp
  • Build a minimal version: First aggregate after removing missing values and get the whole thing running
  • Add exceptions: Add duplicate removal, handling of invalid values (e.g., negative numbers), and time zone conversion
  • Explain: State the reasoning behind decisions, e.g., "I excluded missing values rather than filling them with 0, to distinguish zero people from missed measurements"
import pandas as pd

def hourly_avg(path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(path, parse_dates=["timestamp"])
    df = df.dropna(subset=["count"])            # 計測漏れは0と区別して除外
    df = df[df["count"] >= 0]                   # 不正な値を除く
    df = df.drop_duplicates(["store_id", "timestamp"])
    df["hour"] = df["timestamp"].dt.floor("h")
    return df.groupby(["store_id", "hour"], as_index=False)["count"].mean()

VACAN's business is visualizing congestion information. From the perspective of VACAN practitioners, being able to articulate the business impact as well—e.g., "treating missing values as 0 would produce a misleading display that makes the store look empty"—tends to contribute positively to your evaluation.

Examples 2 and 3: Where do API integration and master data matching differentiate candidates?

FDE Soken's Examples 2 and 3 are external API failure handling and matching customer master data across two systems. What differentiates candidates is whether they can decide the behavior for exceptional cases in advance.

ExampleSummary of the problemKey differentiating points
Example 2: API integrationRetrieve all data from an external API with pagination and save itHandling rate limits and retries on timeout, resuming after mid-run failures, deduplicating already-retrieved data
Example 3: Master data matchingCompare customer lists from the core system and CRM to identify differencesNotation inconsistencies (full-width/half-width, position of "Inc."), matching rules when keys are missing, extracting rows for human review

For Example 3, deciding "not to automatically match all records, but to route low-confidence rows to a list for human review" is also a valid answer. In FDE work, judgment that prioritizes a form the customer can operate from the next day over perfect automation is often required.

How should you prepare differently for live coding, take-home, and algorithm problems?

FDE Soken recommends shifting your preparation focus across the three formats: live coding, take-home, and algorithm problems. Check which format will be used via official announcements such as Palantir Careers or advance communication from interviewers.

What to prioritize preparing for each format

Per FDE Soken's recommendations, prioritize "verbalizing your checks" for the live format, "written documentation" for the take-home format, and "fundamental patterns" for the algorithm format.

FormatWhat tends to be evaluatedWhat to prioritize preparingPitfall
Live codingThe process of checking as you think and building step by stepPractice solving aloud in the order: questions → approach → minimal version → improvementContinuing to write silently so your approach isn't communicated
Take-homeCompleteness of the deliverable and documentation of decisionsWrite assumptions, trade-offs, unresolved issues, and execution steps in the READMECramming in too many features so explanations become thin
Algorithm problemsUnderstanding of basic data structures and computational complexityFundamental patterns of arrays, hashes, sorting, and graph traversalNeglecting preparation on the assumption that it matters less for FDE

* The preparation priorities are FDE Soken editorial recommendations (as of November 2026).

How to allocate four weeks of preparation

FDE Soken's basic allocation for four weeks of preparation is: week 1 for fundamentals, weeks 2-3 for practical example problems, and week 4 for mock interviews.

  • Week 1: Review fundamental algorithm patterns (about one problem per day)
  • Week 2: Solve practical problems like Examples 1-3 with a time limit
  • Week 3: Complete one take-home assignment, including writing the README
  • Week 4: Ask a friend or recruiter to play interviewer and do a full run-through in the live format

Since many companies hold case interviews alongside coding interviews, allocate practice time in combination with FDE case interview preparation.

How does FDE interview preparation change based on years of experience, current role, and English ability?

According to FDE Soken's analysis: if you have less than 3 years of experience, prioritize fundamental patterns; if you currently work at an SIer or consulting firm, prioritize implementation volume; if there is an English interview, prioritize verbal explanations. Confirm English requirements in the official job posting of the company you're applying to (e.g., Palantir Careers).

What to prioritize when conditions conflict

FDE Soken recommends that when conditions conflict, you solidify "whether you can build something that works on your own" before English ability.

For example, if your English is strong but your experience is short, even fluent English explanations may not improve your evaluation if your implementation stalls. Conversely, if your implementation skills are solid but you're anxious about English, preparing set phrases like "I'd like to confirm three things" will help you avoid getting stuck even in the live format. How to approach English learning is covered in Does FDE require English?.

How does FDE interview preparation differ from SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer? Who is a poor fit?

According to FDE Soken's comparison, among the four roles of SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer: those from SWE need to work on confirming requirements, those from IT consulting and PdM on implementation volume, and those from SIer on explaining experiences where they made decisions themselves. For differences in role definitions, Pragmatic Engineer's explanation is a helpful reference.

Who is a poor fit for FDE coding interviews?

In FDE Soken's view, people who don't start writing until specifications are fully fixed, or who can't take an interest in the client's business, tend to struggle both in FDE interviews and in actual work.

Specifically, people with the following tendencies:

  • Treating ambiguity in problem statements as "a flaw in the problem" and getting stuck without asking questions
  • Insisting on a highly polished design from the start rather than a minimal working version
  • Wanting to avoid writing code itself (FDE is a hands-on role)

However, all of these are habits that can be changed with practice. The full picture of fit is organized in Who is suited to FDE.

Are AI tools allowed in FDE coding interviews?

Whether AI tools can be used in FDE coding interviews varies by company and hiring stage (as of November 2026), so FDE Soken assumes that you will check the official guidance of the company you are applying to (e.g., Anthropic's candidate AI guidance and Palantir Careers).

How to check whether AI tools are allowed and how to behave

Check with the interviewer or recruiter in advance whether AI tools are allowed; if it is not explicitly stated, it is safest to prepare on the assumption that you will not use them.

SituationRecommended behavior
Explicitly prohibitedDo not use them. Follow the guidance, including autocomplete features
Explicitly allowedExplain verbally or in a README what you delegated to AI and what you verified yourself
Not explicitly statedCheck in advance. If you cannot confirm, proceed on the assumption that you will not use them

If AI tools are allowed, what is being evaluated?

In interviews where AI tools are allowed, you will likely be evaluated on your ability to notice and fix errors in generated code, and your ability to explain your judgment on how to use them.

In actual FDE work as well, running AI-generated code directly on customer data can leave business-level errors like the missing values in Example 1. From the perspective of practitioners involved in on-site deployment at VACAN, "whether you can validate AI output against the nature of the customer's business data" is considered a skill that is tested regardless of whether AI tools are allowed.
