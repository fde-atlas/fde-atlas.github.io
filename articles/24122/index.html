What is Palantir AIP? Components, Differences from Foundry, and Implementation Process

Original title: Palantir AIPとは？構成要素・Foundryとの違い・導入の流れ

FDE総研 · 2026-08-18 · 17 min read

Summary

This Japanese article explains Palantir AIP as an AI layer that runs LLMs on top of Foundry's ontology, connecting them to business data, permissions, and actions rather than serving as a standalone chat product. It covers AIP's components (AIP Logic, AIP Chatbot Studio, Automate, Evals), how it differs from and depends on Foundry, a five-stage implementation flow starting with AIP Bootcamp, and the skills FDEs need. The author stresses deciding upfront which decisions go to humans, AIP logic, or human approval, and notes failures usually stem from data quality and unclear operational ownership, not model choice.

Key points

  • AIP is an AI layer on Foundry's ontology, not a standalone product.
  • Key design task: split decisions among humans, AIP logic, and approvals.
  • Components include AIP Logic, Chatbot Studio, Automate, and Evals.
  • Implementation runs five stages; data connection is hardest to estimate.
  • Failures typically come from data issues and unclear operations ownership.

What is Palantir AIP?

Palantir AIP is an operational platform that connects LLMs to business data, permissions, and operations on top of Foundry's ontology.

Palantir AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform) is a suite of AI products announced by Palantir Technologies in 2023. It is designed on the premise of running LLMs (large language models) on top of the data structures held by the company's data platforms Foundry and Gotham (Source: Palantir Technologies official website "AIP" https://www.palantir.com/platforms/aip/ / retrieved August 19, 2026). The reason "Foundry/AIP" now appear together in FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) job postings and project descriptions is that this two-layer structure is a prerequisite for implementation work.

To translate "What is Palantir AIP?" into practical terms, it is best understood not as a chat interface, but as an execution environment for connecting LLMs to three things: business data, permissions, and operations. Compared to a typical chat-based AI implementation, the prerequisites differ as follows.

AspectTypical chat-based AI implementationPalantir AIP prerequisites
InputConversation text and attached filesObjects and links on the ontology
OutputTextExecution of actions on top of text
PermissionsPer accountPer object and action
ValidationPrimarily manual reviewPass criteria via evaluation (Evals)
Advance preparationMostly unnecessaryData preparation and ontology definition

This difference changes the very way implementation projects proceed. With chat-based AI, a "deploy first, figure out usage later" sequence works, but with Palantir AIP, you cannot begin design without first deciding "what to let it operate on."

What problems was Palantir AIP created to address?

Palantir AIP was created to tie LLM output to business data and permissions, and turn it into actionable decisions.

The first thing companies face after deploying a general-purpose chat-based AI internally is the disconnect of "getting answers, but being unable to reflect those answers in business systems." Operations such as reducing inventory, approving orders, or rearranging shifts involve permissions, audit logs, and data integrity, so text generation alone cannot complete them. Palantir AIP's design philosophy is not to place the LLM as a standalone chat interface, but to let it operate only within the bounds of business entities (objects) and the operations permitted on those objects (actions).

Working on Palantir AIP projects in the field, what customers initially expect is "smart answers," but what actually changes the evaluation is often "the moment an AI suggestion leads directly to an operation in a business system." This ordering is also where FDEs spend the most time explaining when managing expectations early in an implementation. Conversely, as long as prototyping stays within a scope that never reaches actual operations, the value of using Palantir AIP is hard to convey to customers.

Can Palantir AIP be used as a standalone product?

Palantir AIP is not a standalone product; it is an AI layer that runs on the ontology of Foundry or Gotham.

Palantir's product lineup consists of Foundry, the operational platform for private-sector companies; Gotham, for government and defense; Apollo, which handles software delivery; and Palantir AIP, which sits on top of them (Source: Palantir Technologies official website "Platforms" https://www.palantir.com/platforms/ / retrieved August 19, 2026).

ProductMain domain and roleRelationship to Palantir AIP
FoundryOperational platform for private-sector companiesHolds the ontology that Palantir AIP references
GothamFor government and defenseHolds the data structures that Palantir AIP references
ApolloSoftware deliveryHandles delivery of the entire platform
Palantir AIPAI layer handling LLMsSits on top of the above platforms

Therefore, in practice, talk of "implementing Palantir AIP" almost always comes paired with the question of "how far to build out Foundry's ontology." Listing "Palantir AIP" on its own in a product comparison table without understanding this structure skews the comparison. What should be compared is not chat products against each other, but the design headroom on the operational side—how far AI can be made to run on top of business data and permissions.

What does Palantir AIP mean for FDEs?

For FDEs, Palantir AIP is a tool for designing how much of business decision-making to delegate to LLMs.

The core of an FDE's work is not writing LLM prompts themselves. It is deciding together with the customer where the lines are drawn: which judgments are made by humans, which are delegated to Palantir AIP's logic, and which operations always require human approval. If prototyping proceeds while this boundary remains vague, discussions about accuracy and discussions about responsibility boundaries become entangled, and validation tends to drag on.

ClassificationDescriptionDesign treatment
Human judgmentJudgments not delegated to AIPalantir AIP is limited to presenting reference information
Delegated to Palantir AIPJudgments completed by AIP-side logicSet pass criteria in evaluation (Evals)
Human approval requiredOperations where Palantir AIP proposes and a human confirmsBuild the approval flow into the logic

In practice, simply writing out this three-way classification on a single sheet of paper at the first meeting makes it easier to design the downstream evaluation. Palantir AIP is a powerful execution environment, but its power only works on the safe side once you decide "what not to let it do."

What are the main components of Palantir AIP?

Palantir AIP is built on an ontology foundation, layered with AIP Logic, AIP Chatbot Studio (formerly AIP Agent Studio), Automate, and AIP Evals.

Because component names and functional scopes are updated with each release, exact specifications must always be confirmed in the official documentation (Source: Palantir Docs https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ / Retrieved: August 19, 2026). Here, we organize the representative components that FDEs frequently work with on projects.

ComponentMain roleHow the FDE engages
OntologyDefinitions of business entities, relationships, and operationsInterview the customer about their business and translate it into object types
AIP LogicBuilding business logic that includes LLM callsDesign input/output types and failure branching
AIP Chatbot Studio (formerly AIP Agent Studio)Building and publishing chatbots that use LLMs, the ontology, documents, and toolsNarrow down the available tools and permission scopes
AIP AssistBuilt-in assistant that supports platform operationsUse to shorten research time during development
AutomateAutomatic execution when conditions are met (can also trigger AIP Logic functions)Identify where human approval should be inserted
Evaluation (Evals)Verifying outputs of logic and agentsAgree on pass criteria with the customer and run continuously
Model integrationConnecting external and in-house modelsSelect models according to use case and cost

What does AIP Logic do?

AIP Logic is a feature for building business logic that combines LLM calls with ontology references, without code.

The practical key point of AIP Logic is that it allows you to structure logic by separating the areas where the LLM freely generates text from the areas that use definitive values retrieved from the ontology. For example, in a process like "extract shipments with high delay risk and summarize the reasons," you can finalize the extraction criteria with a query on the ontology and limit the LLM to generating summaries and explanatory text. A design that does not leave numerical calculations to the LLM tends to reduce validation costs.

Part of the processResponsible partyWhere to check when errors occur
Extraction of targets and finalization of numbersQuery on the ontologyData extraction criteria
Generation of summaries and explanatory textLLMGeneration instructions

This division of labor—"definitive values come from the data side, explanations from the generation side"—also pays off during review. When an output is wrong, you can determine whether the cause lies in the data extraction criteria or the generation instructions. Without this separation, you tend to end up rebuilding everything with each fix.

What are the roles of AIP Chatbot Studio and AIP Assist?

AIP Chatbot Studio (formerly AIP Agent Studio) is the place to build business chatbots, while AIP Assist is a built-in assistant that supports platform operations.

In AIP Chatbot Studio, you define together the scope of the ontology the chatbot can reference, the functions and actions it can call, and the permissions of its users. Deciding "what not to expose" here effectively becomes the security design. AIP Assist, on the other hand, supports Foundry/AIP operations and documentation searches, and can be expected to shorten the period during which customer-side staff get accustomed to the platform.

FeatureUsersDesign focus
AIP Chatbot StudioFDEs and developers building business chatbotsReference scope, callable functions and actions, permissions
AIP AssistCustomer-side staff and developers operating the platformSupport for operations and documentation searches

In FDE practice, the work of confirming with the customer "which objects may this business owner see" before configuring AIP Chatbot Studio takes more time than the configuration screen itself. This is because there is no correct answer for permission granularity other than aligning it with existing business responsibilities.

How far should Automate be allowed to execute automatically?

The scope of Automate's automatic execution should be determined first by the impact of errors, not by technical feasibility.

Reference-type aggregations and notifications can be automated since they are reversible, but operations with external impact, such as ordering, shipping, and credit decisions, are easier to get adoption agreement for when designed with human approval in the loop.

Type of operationExamplesRecommended treatment
Reference-typeAggregations, notificationsSafe to automate since reversible
Operations with external impactOrdering, shipping, credit decisionsInsert human approval

Articulating this distinction at the "problem identification" stage of adoption makes specifications less likely to waver in later phases. The scope of automation can be expanded gradually once operational track record accumulates.

How are safety and quality ensured in Palantir AIP?

Palantir AIP ensures quality by limiting the scope of LLM output application through ontology permission design and evaluation (Evals).

Palantir explains its approach to control in AIP as a configuration that enforces access scope and auditing across the data, logic, and action layers (Source: Palantir Technologies official website "AIP" https://www.palantir.com/platforms/aip/ / Retrieved: August 19, 2026). In practice, on top of this, whether or not pass criteria are quantified in evaluations (Evals) greatly changes the speed of decisions about moving to production. If you keep prototyping without setting criteria, you tend to remain stuck in a state of "looks good, but can't ship it."

The pass criteria do not need to be elaborate; even a level such as "how many of the prepared test cases count as correct answers" or "does it avoid proposing dangerous operations in edge cases" is sufficient as material for decision-making. What matters is agreeing on the criteria with the customer in advance.

What is the difference between Palantir AIP and Foundry?

Foundry is the foundation for data integration and business execution, while Palantir AIP is the layer that handles LLMs on top of it; the two do not conflict.

Palantir AIP and Foundry should be considered not as "which one to choose" but as "how to stack them." Below, we organize this along three points: the difference in scope, whether standalone deployment is possible, and the order of getting started.

How do the scopes of Foundry and Palantir AIP differ?

Foundry handles data integration, ontology, and application execution, while Palantir AIP handles reasoning and automated decision-making.

PerspectivePalantir FoundryPalantir AIP
Main roleData integration, modeling, business applicationsReasoning, generation, and automation using LLMs
Core conceptOntology (objects/links/actions)Logic and agents that reference the ontology
Representative featuresPipeline Builder, Ontology Manager, Workshop, ActionsAIP Logic, AIP Chatbot Studio, Automate, AIP Evals
PrerequisitesConnectable data sourcesA well-maintained ontology
How results are measuredCentralizing business data and executing operationsAutomation rate of decisions, reduction in processing time
FDE's main workData connections and business modelingLogic design, evaluation, and operational design

As the "Prerequisites" row in the table shows, the prerequisite for Palantir AIP is an ontology maintained on the Foundry side. This dependency determines the answers to the next two questions.

Can Palantir AIP be deployed on its own?

Since Palantir AIP's value presupposes an ontology, standalone deployment that skips data preparation is unlikely to succeed.

Requests to "just try AIP alone" are not uncommon. However, when the referenced data has not been organized into business vocabulary, all you can pass to the LLM are fragmented tables and files, making it hard to differentiate from a generic chatbot. Conversely, the more the existing data platform has advanced in defining objects and operations, the shorter the deployment validation of Palantir AIP tends to be. The reason FDEs seem to talk only about data preparation in the early phases is precisely this dependency.

Should you start with Foundry or Palantir AIP?

The realistic approach is to pick one target business process, prepare the ontology for that scope in Foundry, and then move on to Palantir AIP.

The order of "prepare all company data first, then start on AI" is correct in theory, but it takes a long time and evaluation stalls. In practice, an approach that makes validation easier to iterate is to first choose one decision you want to handle with AI, define only the objects and actions needed for that decision, and then build Palantir AIP on top of that.

ApproachCharacteristicsPractical assessment
Prepare all company data firstCorrect in theoryTakes a long time and evaluation tends to stall
Pick one decision and narrow the scopeDefine only the necessary objects and actionsEasier to iterate validation and can be extended later

Since a narrowly scoped ontology can be extended later, there is no need to aim for a complete design from the start.

How does a Palantir AIP deployment proceed?

A Palantir AIP deployment proceeds in five stages: issue identification, data connection, logic implementation, evaluation, and production operation.

In the early stages of deployment, Palantir's short-term intensive AIP Bootcamp often serves as the entry point; after that, the five stages are iterated for each target business process. Below, we organize the deployment format, the FDE's role at each stage, and common stumbling points in order.

What kind of deployment format is the AIP Bootcamp?

The AIP Bootcamp is a Palantir deployment program in which customers bring their own real data and complete prototyping in a short period.

Palantir offers a short-term intensive hands-on format (AIP Bootcamp) for the early stages of AIP deployment. In this format, the customer's engineers use their own data and push straight through to building something that actually works (Source: Palantir Technologies official website "AIP" https://www.palantir.com/platforms/aip/ / Retrieved: August 19, 2026). Behind the effectiveness of this format lies a rule of thumb shared by Palantir and similar FDE organizations: prototyping with real data leads to faster decision-making than explanations with slides.

What does the FDE do at each stage of deployment?

Across each stage, the FDE handles business interviews, ontology design, AIP logic implementation, and handover to operations.

StageFDE's main tasksEase of estimating effort
1. Issue identificationNarrow down to a single target business process and agree with the customer on the boundary between decisions delegated to AI and decisions kept by humansDepends on how well the target process is narrowed down
2. Data connectionIngest core systems and logs, and define object types and links aligned with business vocabularyHardest to estimate
3. Logic implementationBuild processing with AIP Logic or AIP Chatbot Studio, and design failure behavior and approval flows alongside itRelatively short if the scope is well defined
4. EvaluationSet pass criteria for expected and edge cases, and repeatedly validate with EvalsRelatively short if the scope is well defined
5. Production operation and handoverPrepare permissions, monitoring, and update procedures, and hand operations over to the customer's staffDepends on how far decisions about the operating owner have progressed

The stage where effort estimates become hard to read is step 2, and the more external system specifications or permission requests are involved, the more likely the timeline stretches. When producing estimates, sharing the uncertainty of step 2 with the customer upfront reduces the cost of explanations later.

Where do implementations of Palantir AIP tend to stumble?

Palantir AIP implementations tend to stumble more on data preparation and building consensus on the business side than on model selection.

A common case is one where the cause of accuracy falling short of expectations lies on the data side rather than the model side. Problems such as inconsistent master data notation, attributes that have not been updated, and differing category definitions across departments will not be solved by switching LLMs. Another pattern is one where the operating owner after the deliverable is up and running has not been decided, which is an organizational design issue rather than a technical one.

Type of stumbling blockConcrete examplePreventive measure
Data-side issuesInconsistent master data notation, attributes that have not been updated, differing category definitions across departmentsCheck the data before the model as the cause of insufficient accuracy
Organizational issuesThe operating owner after the deliverable is up and running has not been decidedConfirm the handover destination during the initial interview

From an FDE's perspective, I feel there is great value in confirming "who will take over this system" during the initial interview.

What skills are required for an FDE to work with Palantir AIP?

What an FDE needs falls into four categories: ontology design, implementation in Python and the like, LLM evaluation design, and business interviews.

Of these, the first three are technical skills, and the last one is the ability to converse with customers. Below, I will organize this into three points: the substance of technical skills, non-technical abilities, and the order in which to acquire them.

What technical skills are required for Palantir AIP?

On the technical side, the focus is on data modeling, implementation in Python and SQL, and LLM input/output design and evaluation design.

Skill areaSpecific contentHow it is used in projects
Data modelingExtraction of business entities, normalization, link definitionFoundation of ontology design
Python/SQLTransformation processing, function implementation, data validationImplementation of pipelines and logic
LLM designInput/output type definitions, instruction design, branching on failureBuilding AIP Logic / AIP Chatbot Studio
Evaluation designCreating test cases, quantifying pass criteriaContinuous verification with Evals
Permissions and securityObject-level permissions, reflecting audit requirementsPrerequisites for production migration
API integrationOntology operations from external applications (OSDK, etc.)Connecting with existing systems

From the top of the table down, the closer a skill is to the ontology that underpins Palantir AIP, the earlier in a project it is required.

What non-technical abilities are needed?

The ability to explain the customer's business in your own words, and the conversational skill to adjust expectations of AI, are just as important as technical skills.

In Palantir AIP projects, things often start with the customer's staff having either excessive or insufficient expectations of AI. Whether you can clearly present "what can be done, what cannot be done, and what can be done under conditions" at this point determines the speed of subsequent verification. When an engineer with only implementation skills enters the field, it tends to result in prototypes piling up while requirements remain unsettled.

Conversational situationWhat the FDE should presentWhat happens when it is missing
Adjusting expectationsWhat can be done, what cannot be done, and what can be done under conditionsVerification proceeds with excessive or insufficient expectations intact
Understanding the businessRestating the customer's business in your own wordsRequirements remain unsettled and prototypes pile up

In what order should Palantir AIP skills be acquired?

A realistic order is to first solidify data modeling and implementation, and then expand into LLM design and evaluation design.

Knowledge around LLMs changes rapidly, whereas the thinking behind ontology design and data preparation can be applied to other data platforms as well. If you reverse the order and start with prompt design, you will struggle to isolate data-side causes when accuracy falls short, and you are likely to get stuck. Palantir's official documentation is published as primary information on product specifications (Source: Palantir Docs https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ / Retrieved: August 19, 2026), so it is reliable to return here each time to confirm feature names.

Note that the practical commentary in this article summarizes trends generally observed in projects with an "ontology + LLM" configuration similar to Palantir AIP, and does not guarantee the results of individual projects. When making implementation decisions, it is recommended to first check the state of data preparation for the target business processes.
