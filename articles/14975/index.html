Profit participation unit

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 1 min read

Summary

This article explains profit participation units (PPUs): contractual rights to a share of future profit distributions, issued instead of stock, with no shareholder votes. Their value depends on terms such as return caps, payout schedules, and sale rules. The author notes that claims that Anthropic pays FDEs in PPUs are unsupported: Levels.fyi lists only stock or equity grants, and a Blind candidate reported double-trigger RSUs in an October 2025 offer. Candidates receiving PPUs or unfamiliar instruments should get written answers on payout triggers, caps, tender offer frequency, eligibility, sale limits, last price, and taxation before comparing offers.

Key points

  • PPUs are contractual profit-sharing rights without shareholder votes.
  • Value hinges on return caps, payout schedules, and sale rules.
  • Claims that Anthropic pays FDEs in PPUs lack supporting sources.
  • Levels.fyi and Blind reports point to stock or double-trigger RSUs instead.
  • Candidates should demand written answers on payouts, caps, tenders, and taxes.

Full text

A profit participation unit is a contractual right to a share of a company’s future profit distributions, granted in place of stock. It carries no shareholder vote, and it pays only when the company distributes profits or the units are bought back or sold, so its value rests on terms a stock grant does not have: any cap on returns, the payout schedule and the rules on selling the units.

In an offer

If you have read that Anthropic pays FDEs in PPUs, no source in our research supports it: Levels.fyi describes the equity on its Anthropic page only as stock or equity grants on a vesting schedule, and on Blind one candidate with an Anthropic software engineer offer reported, in October 2025, being told they would get double-trigger RSUs after the first year, and that the recruiter said not to price them like the stock options they had received. If an offer names PPUs or any instrument you do not recognize, a strong candidate asks what it pays out on, when payouts happen, whether returns are capped, how often tender offers run, who is eligible, what share of vested units you may sell and at what last price, and how the units are taxed, before comparing it with a stock grant. Ask for those answers in writing.
