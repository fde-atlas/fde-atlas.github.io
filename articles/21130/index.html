Why FDE Hiring Is Difficult and How to Write Job Postings That Attract Candidates [For Companies] | FDE board

Original title: FDEの採用が難しい理由と、採れる求人票の作り方【企業向け】｜FDE board

株式会社ウェステリア · 2026-11-16 · 10 min read

Summary

This Japanese article, published by Westeria (operator of the FDE board), argues that companies fail to hire forward deployed engineers mainly because the role is poorly defined internally, not because the talent pool is empty. It advises verifying that an FDE is actually needed, then writing job postings that specify coding ratio, on-site frequency, evaluation metrics, and six-month expectations. Salary ranges should not copy internal engineer grades, given competition with foreign AI firms like Anthropic. It also covers structured interviews and post-hire pitfalls such as assigning sales quotas.

Key points

  • Unclear internal role definition, not market scarcity, is the main hiring blocker.
  • Postings should state coding ratio, on-site work, metrics, and 6-month expectations.
  • Anthropic's US FDE role pays $280k–$320k, a benchmark candidates compare against.
  • Interviews should predefine three axes: technical, problem decomposition, client handling.
  • Placing FDEs under sales quotas or using them as support leads to early attrition.

As AI implementation projects increase, more companies want to hire "people who can go into customer sites and carry implementations through to completion." However, this role tends to suffer from a common problem: job postings attract few applicants, and those who do apply often don't meet the requirements. In most cases, the cause is not a depleted market but the job definition and the job posting itself. This article takes the company's perspective and organizes the reasons hiring gets stuck, along with practical steps to fix them.

Key Takeaways

  • The main reason companies can't hire FDEs is not the small talent pool but the fact that "the role's definition hasn't been settled internally." Postings that don't specify whether the role sits in the sales organization or the engineering organization are, from a candidate's perspective, impossible to understand.
  • In the job posting, clearly state the percentage of time spent writing code, the frequency of on-site work at customers, evaluation metrics, and expectations for the first six months after joining. Just including these four points changes the quality of applicants.
  • If you apply your internal software engineer salary bands as-is to set the compensation range, you will miss the mark. Design it based on the burden of customer-facing work and the reality that similar roles compete with foreign companies. Posting jobs on FDE board is free; you can list a job simply by sending the URL of your job posting.

Why FDEs Are Hard to Hire

The difficulty of hiring for this role lies in the combination of required skills. Most strong backend engineers don't want to deal with customers, and most consultants accustomed to customer work can't write production code. An FDE needs both, plus the self-driven ability to make decisions and move forward in situations where requirements aren't yet settled. It's true that the absolute number of people with all three is small.

That said, it's too early to blame the market for hiring difficulties. Three issues are commonly seen in practice. First, the role's definition isn't settled internally. If you post a job without deciding whether it's sales support, delivery, or part of product development, the posting becomes abstract and candidates can't judge what the job actually involves.

Second, writing the posting as if it were a sales engineer role. A posting filled with words like "technical support," "proposals," and "demos" filters out candidates who want to write code. Third, evaluation criteria vary from interviewer to interviewer, causing inconsistent pass/fail decisions. Engineer interviewers reject candidates for implementation skills, business-side interviewers reject them for customer-facing ability, and as a result, no one gets through.

Before That—Does Your Company Really Need an FDE?

Before starting the hiring process, verify that what you need is truly an FDE. Often the name is new, but what you actually need is a different role.

The way to tell is simple: ask, "Is this a situation where no value can be delivered unless someone from your company writes code in the customer's environment?" If the answer is yes, you need an FDE. If value can be delivered through explaining setup and usage, it's Customer Success or a Support Engineer; if the challenge is advancing sales conversations, it's a Sales Engineer; if the only issue is missing product features, adding product developers is the right answer.

Skipping this check leads to the new hire feeling "this isn't what I was told" and leaving early. People in FDE roles are in high demand in the market, so the cost of a mismatch-driven departure is higher than usual.

Four Items You Must Include in the Job Posting

Candidates try to read from the posting whether this role leans toward sales or development. If they can't tell, the better the candidate, the more likely they are to skip it. At minimum, write the following four items concretely.

In particular, expectations for the first six months are a differentiator because few companies include them. A single sentence like "Within six months of joining, lead one customer implementation through to production" is enough for candidates to grasp the shape of the job.

How to Set the Salary Range

A common mistake at Japanese companies is applying the existing software engineer salary bands as-is. FDEs bear the burden of customer-facing work and travel on top of implementation, and when results don't materialize, responsibility tends to fall on the individual. Paying the same as an internal engineer of the same grade will be judged as not worth the workload.

Another consideration is who your competitors are. Foreign AI companies hiring for this role offer high compensation levels even in Japan. For reference, Anthropic's publicly listed US-based Forward Deployed Engineer role shows a range of $280,000 to $320,000 per year as of the time of writing (2026-08). Japanese postings don't need to match this level, but you should assume candidates are using it as a comparison. Ranges actually offered in Japan can be checked on FDE board's job listings and salary pages.

As realistic design options, three approaches are effective: adding a customer-facing premium on top of the existing engineer grade, incorporating performance-linked elements (kept separate from sales incentives), and clearly stating the range in the posting to encourage self-selection. In particular, stating the range explicitly boosts application volume, since many companies keep it hidden.

Designing the Selection Process—Decide Evaluation Criteria First

The problem of criteria varying by interviewer can be prevented through selection process design. For FDE selection, first divide the axes to evaluate into three, and decide which interview assesses which before starting.

Then, for each axis, articulate in advance "this level passes" and share it with all interviewers. In particular, it's important not to apply the same standard as internal product development roles to technical skill. What an FDE needs is the ability to handle messy data and the judgment to ship something working within a deadline—not algorithm optimization ability.

Practically effective is including one interview where candidates are given an ambiguous problem and asked to structure it. For a question like "A customer is in this situation. What do you do in the first two weeks?", observe whether they start by asking clarifying questions about assumptions and whether they can deliberately narrow the scope. This one exercise is quite effective at determining fit for the role.

  • Technical skill: Can they implement? Can they ask questions when requirements are insufficient? Can they explain the weaknesses of their own code?
  • Problem decomposition: Can they structure ambiguous situations and decide what scope to tackle first?
  • Customer communication: Can they explain things in line with the other party's level of understanding? Can they admit when something can't be done?

After hiring — getting placement and evaluation right

Even if hiring succeeds, a wrong placement leads to resignation within a year. The most common mistake is putting an FDE in the sales organization with sales targets. When evaluated on deal value, spending time on hands-on work at customer sites counts against them, and the role loses its meaning.

Another mistake is burning them out as a customer-facing contact point. When their time fills up with handling inquiries and attending meetings, leaving no time for implementation, anxiety about losing their value as an engineer leads to turnover. Design mechanisms to protect implementation time from the start (caps on the number of assigned customers, separating out first-line inquiry handling).

For evaluation, we recommend explicitly including contributions where patterns found at customer sites are fed back into the product. Without this, FDEs will focus only on the customer in front of them, and learning won't accumulate across the organization.

About listing on FDE board

FDE board is a job board operated by Westeria Inc. that aggregates FDE job openings in Japan (https://westeria.jp/fde/). It was created as a place where candidates looking for FDE or similar roles can view all the jobs they can apply to in Japan in one place.

Listing is free. Simply send your company name, contact person, contact details, and the URL of your job posting via the listing application form, and we will review the content and publish it. There is no need to rewrite your job posting; you can simply send the URL of your own careers page or ATS (Greenhouse, Lever, HERP, etc.). Applications go directly to your company's primary information page.

After listing, if the job details change or the position closes, please contact us via the same form. For closed listings, we keep the page but switch it to a "recruitment closed" display and remove it from search results. We also accept consultations at stages where the job posting is not yet finalized.

Frequently asked questions

  • Is it realistic to have someone serve as both FDE and sales engineer?
  • It works in the short term but doesn't last. Time spent pursuing deals competes with time spent building solutions at customer sites, so one of the two gets sacrificed. If you must combine the roles when headcount is small, agree with the person in advance which one takes priority for evaluation purposes.
  • Can you develop an FDE from someone with no prior experience?
  • Yes, but the difficulty depends on where you set the starting point for development. Having an in-house engineer with implementation skills gradually take on customer-facing work tends to succeed relatively easily, while the reverse — someone with customer-facing experience acquiring implementation skills later — takes longer. It's often faster to find an existing engineer who is comfortable talking with customers.
  • Should you include a salary range in the job posting?
  • We recommend including it. Candidates for this role compare offers with foreign companies' postings, so if the range is not public, you won't even be in the running. Stating the range reduces applications from candidates whose conditions don't match and improves screening efficiency.
  • Does listing on FDE board cost anything?
  • No. Listing is free. Simply send the URL of your job posting and your contact details via the listing application form, and we will review the content and publish it. There is no need to create a new job posting.
  • What kinds of candidates will see my listing?
  • The core audience is candidates explicitly looking for FDE or AI implementation roles. FDE board also accepts candidate registrations and has a system to notify them when a matching job is posted. Listing companies therefore reach these registered candidates as well.
