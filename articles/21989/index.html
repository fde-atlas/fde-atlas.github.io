  • The driver is the implementation gap: PoCs stall before production and tools go unused on-site.

"I have engineers, but when it comes to decisions about AI adoption, I'm always the one making them as the business owner." Do you feel this kind of frustration?

"We want to adopt AI, but we don't know where to start." "We completed a PoC (proof of concept), but it never progressed to production." "We introduced a tool, but it ended up never being used in the field." Many companies are hitting this wall of "we adopted it, but it didn't produce results."

Recently, a role called "FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)" has been making headlines as a solution to bridge this gap. It is an engineering role that embeds itself in client companies' operations and handles everything end-to-end, from problem discovery to implementation and adoption.

This role, established by US-based Palantir in the 2010s, has been rapidly gaining attention as major AI companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, and Salesforce have successively moved to formalize FDE organizations in 2026, alongside the spread of generative AI.

An FDE is an engineering role that, while belonging to a vendor company, embeds deeply in the client's operations and drives everything firsthand, from problem discovery to AI implementation and business adoption. In 2026, global companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, and Salesforce have successively announced massive investments in FDE organizations, driven by a structural implementation gap: "AI gets adopted but never used in the field."

An FDE is defined as "an engineer deployed to a client company's operations who takes responsibility for both technical implementation and problem-solving."

While traditional software engineers build one feature for many clients, an FDE implements many features for one client (one company). The biggest difference from traditional IT roles is that FDEs sell problem-solving (outcomes) rather than technology (resources).

The FDE role originated at the US data analytics company Palantir. Its official title there is FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer), internally called "Delta," and it was established as a model of deploying engineers directly to the sites of clients such as government agencies and financial institutions where off-the-shelf software falls short. A detailed breakdown of the Palantir-style FDE role is covered in What Roles Are Required of Palantir-Style FDEs and FDRs?.

Since the start of 2026, major AI companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Salesforce, AWS, and Google have announced massive investments in FDE organizations one after another, almost in lockstep. Detailed data on each company's announcement dates and investment amounts is compiled in a list in What Is an FDE? Adoption Strategies for Forward Deployed Engineers, the New AI-Era Role, for Businesses.

What's noteworthy here is the pattern common to all these moves. Whether it's OpenAI FDE, Anthropic FDE, Salesforce FDE, or Google FDE, each has launched its FDE organization using the same approaches: (1) spinning it out as a specialized company or organization with external capital, and (2) acquiring or partnering with existing AI consulting firms or partner companies. This shows that each company positions FDE not as a casual headcount expansion but as a business area it is seriously pursuing as a core operation.

Rather than "which AI model is smartest," the new battleground for AI companies is becoming "which company can embed AI into clients' business systems."

The biggest reason FDEs are rapidly rising in prominence is the structural problem of the "implementation gap" in AI adoption. The background behind challenges like "stuck at the PoC stage" and "adopted but unused" mentioned at the start, along with job posting data confirming this trend, is explained in detail in What Is an FDE? Adoption Strategies for Forward Deployed Engineers, the New AI-Era Role, for Businesses. FDEs are no longer a role limited to a handful of tech companies; they are becoming the industry-standard position for turning AI adoption into real results.

Interviews with OpenAI's FDE team reveal that the pace of AI model evolution itself is transforming the nature of FDE work every six months or so. The essential characteristic of an FDE is not simply being an "AI adoption specialist," but a role that continuously changes how it works, with AI's evolution as a given.

In an interview article published by ITmedia on August 19, 2026, featuring two OpenAI FDEs (Hatsune Saito and Kane), the following concrete work examples were introduced.

  • Sales department transformation: After developing an AI agent system for a company's sales department, the proposal quality of new graduate employees improved to the level of employees in their 3rd to 4th year
  • Productization of a document creation support tool: An AI agent built by an FDE to reduce the man-hours required for creating documents was later officially productized as "ChatGPT for PowerPoint"
  • Modernization of legacy systems: Systems from 20 to 30 years ago whose specifications had been lost were rebuilt using AI, and in the process, security vulnerabilities were also fixed at the same time

What all of these cases have in common is that FDEs do not simply "build the features they are told to build"; they take problems discovered in the field and elevate them into product-level solutions on the spot.

The work of an FDE changes dramatically on a scale of months due to improvements in the performance of AI models themselves.

Colin Jarvis, Global Head of Forward Deployed Engineering at OpenAI, said of the changes that occurred in February 2026 that "about 70% of FDE work disappeared." The cause was the improved performance of the AI agent service "Codex."

Previously, the focus was on a development approach of individually building, evaluating, and combining workflows, but after Codex's performance improved, the approach shifted to one where business procedures and required knowledge are taught to AI agents as "skills," leaving much of the work to Codex itself. Jarvis stated, "You must be prepared for your role to completely change every six months." This change does not mean the FDE role will disappear; rather, it indicates that the focus continues to shift from routine tasks toward more advanced roles such as problem design and exception handling.

For executives, the important point is that it is not easy to maintain, within their own company alone, an organization capable of keeping pace with the speed of this change. Precisely because the optimal approach changes every six months in this field, there is value in bringing in the expertise of FDEs who constantly work with the latest implementation patterns from outside.

Hatsune Saito describes his role as the "Tip of the spear." This term refers to a person or group that takes on dangerous or difficult missions first.

Kane cites the structure for realizing "AI that works in production environments" as what distinguishes FDEs from traditional SIers and IT consultants. Leveraging the strength of direct access to OpenAI's own models, products, and APIs, the role involves working closely with executives and frontline employees to explore challenges and move AI adoption forward.

The faster AI evolves, the less useful pre-prepared manuals become. What is required of an FDE is an attitude of assuming change itself as a given and proactively identifying and pioneering new challenges.

The biggest reason FDE compensation levels have soared is that talent combining technical skill and business problem-solving ability at a high level is extremely scarce in the market.

According to a Nikkei Business report, the median annual income for FDEs in the US is approximately 32 million yen, about 50% higher than that of a typical software engineer. Nevertheless, it is said that only about 15% of engineers have the aptitude to succeed in this role.

A Nikkei Cross Trend report also states that the number of FDE-related job openings in the US increased sevenfold in one year, and multiple surveys confirm this surge in demand. The situation suggests that talent supply has not kept up.

In Japan, the typical annual income for full-time FDEs is 10 to 25 million yen, while freelance (contract-based) FDEs typically earn 1.3 to 2 million yen per month. Compared to US income levels (total compensation of $215,000 to over $1,200,000), the Japanese market is still developing, and there is room for compensation levels to rise further.

For reference, the typical annual income for SES and on-site resident SEs centers around 4 to 7 million yen, which is in a different league from FDE compensation. This pay gap directly reflects the difference in value provided: "providing man-hours" versus "providing problem-solving." The reason hiring FDEs is difficult lies in the fact that the required skills involve "multiplication" rather than "addition."

It is not rare to find people who have engineering skills or consulting skills individually, but the number of people who meet the following two requirements simultaneously at a high level is limited.

  • Requirement 1: The ability to identify problems by independently formulating questions from a business perspective (not just acting once given requirements, but being able to define the requirements themselves)
  • Requirement 2: The ability to actually solve problems through development technology including AI (implementation skills in Python, LLMs, RAG, and AI agents, and full-stack development experience)

Looking at it by professional background, former software engineers often need to strengthen their client-facing and problem-definition skills, while former IT consultants often need to strengthen their hands-on implementation skills; regardless of background, effort is required to fill a certain skills gap. For more detailed career strategies, see FDE Engineer Roles, Required Skills, and Career Strategies.

Hiring such rare talent as full-time employees is not a realistic option for most companies. In practice, the majority of companies use contract-based freelance FDEs or FDE-style support services delivered by teams of multiple people.

The organization of FDEs is not a movement limited to major US AI companies. In Japan as well, multiple companies across different industries, including SoftBank, CyberAgent, and Loglass, have begun hiring for FDE positions, and it is spreading rapidly in the Japanese market.

Specific initiatives by Japanese companies are as follows.

  • SoftBank: Hiring FDEs at "SB OAI Japan LLC," a joint venture with OpenAI. Responsible end-to-end for technical leadership in LLM and generative AI adoption for strategic customers, from identifying customer challenges to building prototypes and deploying to production environments
  • CyberAgent: Hiring FDEs as "frontline positions that solve customer challenges with technology." A role that leverages technical skill to directly address customers' business challenges
  • Loglass: The company, which provides management SaaS, is hiring FDEs to structure customer companies' management challenges and business workflows using the Loglass LLM platform and external AI solutions. Applicants are required to have 3 or more years of experience in mid- to large-scale system design and development at SIers, consulting firms, or similar organizations

What these companies have in common is that they position FDEs not merely as "engineers familiar with AI" but as strategic talent who engage directly with customers' business challenges. Notably, with LayerX publicly posting FDE positions offering annual salaries starting at 12 million yen, offers exceeding 10 million yen are becoming standardized among domestic startups as well.

That said, these are examples from large companies and startups with strong recruiting power. Even for small and mid-sized or growing companies where hiring engineers itself is difficult, there are options beyond full-time hiring, such as outsourcing work to freelance FDEs or using FDE-type support services. We will revisit the criteria for choosing the procurement method that fits your company in section 5-3.

FDEs at foreign AI companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Salesforce, etc.) focus primarily on applying globally standard implementation patterns to customers, based on the AI models and products the company itself develops. In contrast, FDEs at domestic companies tend to focus on integrating the company's own SaaS or LLM platform with customers' existing systems and business workflows.

There are also differences in the experience required. Foreign companies tend to emphasize global development experience in AI/ML and product orientation, while domestic companies (particularly the Loglass example) often require project management experience at SIers or consulting firms, reflecting a tendency to value understanding of Japanese business customs and processes.

If you would like a more detailed comparison of FDE companies in Japan and abroad, see Complete Comparison of Japanese and US FDE Companies (2026 Edition) | Salaries, Roles, Hiring Requirements, and a Third Option.

From the perspective of "FDE in Japan," the companies that have so far organized FDEs are mainly in the AI and SaaS fields, but the structural problem of the AI implementation gap is a challenge common to all industries.

The demand for FDE-type talent is likely to expand in industries such as finance, insurance, manufacturing, construction, and retail, where the need to leverage AI is growing while legacy systems and complex business workflows remain. In fact, in the retail industry, initiatives using FDEs have already begun, such as a proof-of-concept by Fujitsu and Aeon Food Style. In the recruiting market discussed later, there is also a growing number of positions seeking talent in "roles close to FDE" for LLM, RAG, and AI Agent projects in the financial industry, and demand for "FDE-style ways of working" is steadily spreading across industries.

As awareness of the term FDE spreads, the market is seeing more outsourced work labeled "FDE" even though the actual work is closer to SES, PMO, or progress management. When outsourcing FDE work externally, it is important to have the perspective to distinguish genuine FDEs.

There is also skepticism about the rapid expansion of FDEs. A ZDNet Japan column points out that some view FDE as a "buzzword," and raises concerns about the risk of "vendor lock-in" from depending on AI agent vendors for engineering capabilities as well. The argument is that while FDEs are already functioning actively in the US, it remains uncertain whether the same model will take hold in Japan.

These skeptical perspectives lead to the lesson that one should judge not by the FDE title alone, but by examining the actual contract terms and where accountability for results lies.

Looking at the actual content of work that is ordered and contracted as FDE, it becomes clear that there is a wide range in how the term "FDE" is used.

For example, some outsourced work actually recruited and ordered as FDE explicitly describes content such as "consistently driving everything from decomposing customer requirements in the growth phase after MVP validation, through PoC and production deployment, to self-service and operational handover" and "end-to-end support from requirements definition through specification design, schedule management, and cutover," where a single FDE handles everything from implementation to operational handover, with contract fees in the range of roughly 1.0 to 1.2 million yen per month.

On the other hand, among the outsourced work routinely circulating under the name "FDE," some are grouped under expressions like "in a role close to FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)," but the actual work centers on regular meetings with customers, organizing requests, progress management, and coordination among stakeholders, with technical implementation handled by a separate team, and the contract is dispatch-based with a workload of around 0.5 to 0.6 person-months. Required skills are also listed as "experience in PM, PMO, IT consulting, FDE, etc.," treated in parallel with other positions, and in many cases implementation ability itself is not a required condition.

Below is a summary of the main differences between genuine FDEs and FDEs in name only. When ordering FDE work externally, the perspective to discern this difference is essential.

For those ordering FDE work, the most reliable way to identify FDEs in name only is not to judge by the "FDE" title alone, but to concretely verify the contract type, success metrics, and degree of involvement in implementation. A more detailed explanation of how to distinguish them is also provided in What Is FDE Consulting? Fee Structures and Differences from SES and Traditional Consulting.

The criteria for judging whether your company needs an FDE lie in the nature of the problem: is it that "you lack headcount in development resources," or that "the PoC succeeded but production deployment and on-site adoption are not progressing"?

Companies that match any of the following will find FDEs a strong option.

  • You want to introduce AI but cannot judge where to start on your own
  • The PoC (proof of concept) succeeded, but things have stalled at the transition to production
  • AI tools were introduced, but the field could not use them effectively and adoption did not take hold
  • You have engineers, but no one who can proactively propose solutions to business problems, and executives must give instructions one by one

On the other hand, if any of the following applies, using SES (providing labor through on-site resident engineers) is more appropriate than an FDE.

  • Requirements are already fixed, and you want someone who will implement them as instructed
  • You are simply short on development headcount (manpower)

If your company's challenges are closer to the former, hiring an FDE as a full-time employee is a high hurdle, so a realistic approach is to start by considering outsourcing work to a freelance FDE or consulting an FDE-style support service.

The search term "FDE AI" is not a standalone technical term; it is generally used to refer to "the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) role that is attracting attention in the AI industry." Since AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic are making massive investments in this role, searches combining it with "AI" are increasing.

It is not only AI companies that value FDEs, but AI companies are especially direct parties to the "implementation gap." Because they bear the responsibility of making their own AI models actually work in customers' production environments, moves to invest heavily in FDE organizations themselves stand out, such as OpenAI's deployment company and Anthropic's establishment of a new company.

The FDE role originated at Palantir. Its official title there is FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer), with the internal name "Delta," and this model, cultivated through work for government agencies and financial institutions, became the prototype of today's FDE role.

The content of FDE work changes dramatically every six months due to AI's evolution, but it is not being replaced and eliminated. OpenAI's Colin Jarvis has said that "about 70% of FDE work has disappeared," yet the role itself continues to shift from simple tasks toward more advanced areas such as problem design and exception handling.

It is likely to take root in Japan as well, but whether it will spread as rapidly as in the US remains uncertain. FDE hiring has already begun at domestic companies such as SoftBank, CyberAgent, and Loglass, and since the challenge of the AI implementation gap is common to Japanese companies too, there is room for further expansion going forward.

As a guideline, outsourcing to a freelance FDE costs around ¥1.3–2 million per month, while team-based FDE support services run around ¥1.5–3 million per month. Hiring an FDE as a full-time employee requires an annual salary of ¥10–25 million, so most companies start by considering external outsourcing first.

Salesforce offers implementation support through its "FDE Partner Network," available in Japan from June 2026 via domestic partner companies. Google and Anthropic are also building systems to deploy FDEs for companies adopting their products, but note that in all these cases a contract for the vendor's own product is a prerequisite. If you want FDE-style support without depending on a specific vendor, an independent FDE-style support service such as DeFactory is also an option.

If your challenge is "not enough development headcount," SES is more appropriate than an FDE, but if your challenge is "the PoC succeeded but production rollout and on-site adoption are not progressing," an FDE is a strong option. The first step in deciding is to check whether the contract type is a quasi-mandate (jun-i) with accountability for results, and whether you can entrust everything end-to-end from implementation through operational handover.

Although the FDE is a role established by Palantir, with massive investments made one after another by AI companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Salesforce, and AWS starting in 2026, it is rapidly raising its presence as a new role that symbolizes the AI industry.

The faster AI models themselves evolve, the more dramatically the content of FDE work is rewritten every six months. As those on the ground at OpenAI describe, while simple tasks are being replaced by AI, the FDE's role continues to shift toward more advanced areas of "pioneering problems on one's own."

On the other hand, the market also contains projects that call themselves "FDE" but are in reality centered on progress management, so it is essential to have the perspective of concretely discerning the contract type and where accountability for results lies.

If you are a company stakeholder struggling with the AI implementation gap of "we deployed it, but no one uses it," please consider the FDE option.
