FDE vs Staff Augmentation and Outsourcing

Onyx Devs Lab · 2026-09-15 · 2 min read

Summary

Onyx Devs Lab distinguishes forward deployed engineering from staff augmentation and outsourcing. Staff augmentation supplies capacity under client direction; outsourcing delivers an agreed scope with milestones and acceptance criteria. FDE instead owns a bounded operating problem, learning requirements inside real workflows and producing outcome evidence. The article argues job titles and onsite presence don't define the model—what matters is the unit of accountability, learning cadence, and exit condition. It recommends choosing the lightest model that places accountability where decisions can actually be made, and notes FDE should transition to stable delivery once uncertainty drops.

Key points

  • Staff augmentation provides capacity; the client owns priorities and outcomes.
  • Outsourcing delivers a defined scope with agreed acceptance criteria.
  • FDE owns diagnosis-to-evidence for a bounded operating problem.
  • Key differentiators: accountability unit, learning cadence, exit condition.
  • FDE should hand over to product delivery or internal ownership once uncertainty falls.

Full text

Staff augmentation

The client owns prioritisation, architecture, coordination, acceptance, and outcomes; the provider supplies named skills and capacity.

AI delivery model decision

No. Staff augmentation supplies capacity under the client’s direction. Outsourcing delivers an agreed scope. Forward Deployed Engineering owns a bounded operating problem and repeatedly connects field learning, implementation, deployment, and outcome evidence.

Job titles and onsite presence do not determine the engagement model.

The provider owns delivery against a defined scope, milestones, and acceptance criteria; changes use an agreed control process.

The team owns diagnosis-to-evidence for a bounded operating problem where requirements must be learned inside the real workflow.

Use the lightest model that places accountability where the necessary decisions can actually be made.

The work is already decomposed, internal technical leadership is strong, and the main constraint is temporary capacity.

Users, workflow, interfaces, constraints, tests, and acceptance can be specified with limited field discovery.

The valuable problem is known, but data, workflow, adoption, and intervention boundaries must be discovered and tested with operators.

Write the business decision, current baseline, affected operators, constraints, and failure consequence before selecting a staffing label.

Decide whether the provider owns capacity, a specified deliverable, or the evidence loop for an operating outcome.

Set field access, decision rights, product and engineering cadence, evidence reviews, and how new learning changes scope.

State when uncertainty is considered resolved, who receives the system and knowledge, and which model follows after the FDE phase.

No. It requires close access to the operating environment and fast decision loops. The appropriate mix of onsite and remote work depends on the workflow, data, systems, and users.

A bounded phase can have a fixed commercial envelope, but uncertainty, evidence gates, change rules, and stop conditions must be explicit. A frozen feature list may defeat the purpose.

Consulting may end with a decision or roadmap. FDE continues through implementation and production evidence for the bounded problem.

Yes. Once uncertainty falls and ownership is clear, FDE should transition to stable product delivery, internal ownership, or managed operations.

We will first clarify the workflow, evidence, data boundary, and outcome standard before recommending the next step.
