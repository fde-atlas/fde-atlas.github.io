"FDE washing" refers to rebranding ordinary implementation, professional services, or AI consulting as "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)" work, charging an FDE premium without the corresponding delivery depth. The term comes from Gartner's September 2026 research.

What Gartner Says

On September 29, 2026, Gartner published a forecast: by 2028, 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic systems built with vendor assistance, due to rising maintenance costs and enterprises' inability to modify them themselves. Gartner also predicts that by 2028, fewer than 20% of FDE engagements will turn customers' recurring needs into product features.

Gartner Senior Director Analyst Mukul Saha warns against "FDE washing": some providers slap the FDE label on routine implementation or consulting, sometimes charging a premium. In his view, well-scoped FDE engagements spell out governance rules, business value delivery, intellectual property ownership, knowledge transfer, and exit plans from day one.

Gartner does not reject the FDE model, but recommends using it only for problems that require deep product expertise, rapid iteration, or tight integration with the customer's operating environment.

Six Questions Enterprises Can Ask Providers

  • Who comes on-site, for how long, and which business metric are they accountable for?
  • What is delivered in the first week? It should be an observation list and a one-page task brief, not just a demo.
  • Has validation been done on anonymized samples, and can the validation records be shown to the enterprise?
  • Who owns the rules, prompts, and configurations, and can they be fully handed over when the project ends?
  • What is the exit plan? After the provider leaves, who inside the enterprise will maintain and modify the system?
  • How are results measured, against what criteria, and compared to what baseline?

Our Approach

The value of an FDE lies not in how long they stay on-site, but in whether the enterprise can continue on its own after they leave. So we insist on "doing it together, teaching how to do it, leaving it behind": for the first scenario, we work alongside the enterprise's key staff, and hand over the rules and configurations to the enterprise.

Frequently Asked Questions

After the external FDE leaves, will the system still work?

It depends on how thorough the handover was. If the rules, configurations, and validation methods all remain inside the enterprise, and internal staff participated in building them, the system can continue to be used and modified; if everything depends on external construction, it often becomes impossible to modify later — exactly the risk Gartner warns about.

FDE services cost more than ordinary implementation — what is the extra cost for?

It pays for the on-site time, rules clarification, sample validation, and handover work required to be accountable for outcomes. If the quote doesn't show these activities and their corresponding deliverables, beware of FDE washing.
