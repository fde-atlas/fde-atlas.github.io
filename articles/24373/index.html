OpenAI and Anthropic's FDEs: Why AI Companies Created the "Implementation Company" Role

Original title: OpenAI・AnthropicのFDE｜AI企業が「導入会社」を作った理由

FDE Consulting · 2026-10-08 · 5 min read

Summary

Based on job postings and official announcements, this article explains why OpenAI and Anthropic turned their Forward Deployed Engineer teams into standalone deployment companies in 2026. OpenAI Deployment Company, launched May 2026 with over $4 billion in initial funding and the Tomoro acquisition, targets large enterprises and governments; Ode with Anthropic, backed by Blackstone and others, targets mid-sized firms. Citing a16z's "Trading Margin for Moat," the author argues deployment has become core business, not a side service. In Japan, both firms' FDE presence remains small and enterprise-focused, so domestic demand is being absorbed by Hitachi, Fujitsu, NTT Data and local AI companies instead.

Key points

  • OpenAI's FDE team grew from 2 people in early 2025 to a planned ~50 by year-end.
  • OpenAI Deployment Company holds majority OpenAI ownership with TPG-led funding including SoftBank Group.
  • Ode with Anthropic acquired Fractional AI and explicitly targets mid-market customers.
  • The author frames deployment as a moat: embedding models into workflows reduces switching.
  • In Japan, vendor FDEs are scarce; Hitachi plans 1,000 domestic FDEs by fiscal 2026.

The companies that build models have created companies that go into the field. OpenAI established the OpenAI Deployment Company on May 11, 2026, and Anthropic launched Ode with Anthropic on July 15, 2026. Both are companies that take on the full process of getting their own models running inside customers' operations.

The job title FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) was born at Palantir, but it was these two companies that made it "standard equipment for AI companies." This article uses primary sources—job postings and official announcements—to organize what OpenAI's and Anthropic's FDEs look like organizationally, what is inside the two deployment companies, and what it all means for Japanese companies. The Palantir story is covered in The Palantir-style FDE Model, and the definition of an FDE in What is an FDE?.

OpenAI's FDE

OpenAI's FDE team started small. According to reporting by Pragmatic Engineer, it launched with 2 members in early 2025 and had more than 10 as of August 2025. The person in charge is Colin Jarvis. It was subsequently reported that the team plans to grow to about 50 members by the end of the year (Nikkei Cross Tech, November 14, 2025).

It is also active in Tokyo. On September 16, 2025, a "Forward Deployed Engineer – Tokyo" job posting was published, with requirements of Japanese and English, 5+ years of professional experience, and 3 days a week in the office. On July 21, 2026, a "Manager, FDE – Tokyo" posting also appeared. Salaries are hidden for both. It has been reported that a dedicated project team is assigned to the Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group (MUFG) engagement, so for large Japanese enterprises, OpenAI uses a model of placing a small dedicated team on each engagement.

In my view, OpenAI's FDE is a "special forces unit attached to major client engagements." The headcount is in the dozens, and the counterparties are governments and large enterprises. It is not a place where mid-market companies' inquiries land.

Anthropic's FDE

Anthropic officially uses the FDE title. The job posting reads: "As part of the Applied AI team, you will be a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)." Hiring locations are the US, London, Paris, and Munich. It was also reported that in 2025 the company planned to expand the Applied AI team fivefold.

The Tokyo situation is a bit different. As of September 29, 2026, there are no FDE-titled openings in Tokyo. What is open in Tokyo is Applied AI Engineer and Applied AI Architect, both requiring native-level Japanese. There was previously a Tokyo posting for FDE, Applied AI, but it is now closed. Only the title changed; the substance of the work—going into customers' field sites and implementing—is the same.

Why the two companies created "deployment companies"

In 2026, both companies elevated FDE from an internal team to an independent company.

Why would a company that builds models own a low-margin business that sends people into the field? In his June 2025 essay "Trading Margin for Moat," a16z's Joe Schmidt called this service-led growth and explained it as "building a moat even at the cost of margins." As gaps in model performance narrow, the difference in what customers pay is decided by "whether it runs in their own operations." If you handle deployment yourself, the model gets embedded in the customer's business processes and becomes harder to switch away from.

In my view, this is a declaration that "deployment itself has become the business." Through 2024, AI companies sold models and left deployment to SIs and consultancies. In 2026, they concluded that without owning deployment, they cannot build a moat. OpenAI put over $4 billion of initial funding into a majority-owned company because deployment is a core business, not an ancillary service.

What it means for Japanese companies

Three things can be said from the primary sources.

First. The FDEs at both companies' Japanese entities are still few in number, and their counterparties are large enterprises and governments. There are only a handful of Tokyo postings, with requirements of native Japanese and 5+ years of experience. This is not a stage where the door for inquiries is wide open.

Second. The Japanese expansion of the two deployment companies has not been announced as of October 2026. OpenAI Deployment Company's partners include SoftBank Group, and in Japan SB OAI Japan is recruiting secondment-style FDEs, but there is no information that the deployment company itself is winning business in Japan. Ode with Anthropic targets mid-market companies, but it is a company launched in the US, and its availability in Japan has not been announced.

Third. So in Japan, a home for FDEs has been forming outside the model companies. Hitachi plans to build a 1,000-person domestic FDE structure during fiscal 2026, Fujitsu became a Palantir Global FDE Partner, and NTT Data Group launched FDE-style consulting. Domestic AI and SaaS companies like LayerX, Loglass, and AI Shift have also newly created FDE roles. A domestic list is compiled in List of Companies Deploying FDEs.

For those commissioning work, the question is not whether to wait for the model companies' FDEs. A product vendor's FDE aims to entrench its own products and does not handle the pre-product-selection question of "where in our operations should AI go." That is the job of vendors-neutral support firms; how to choose one is covered in How to Choose an FDE Support Company.

What commissioning parties should verify now

If you want to translate the news that model companies have created deployment companies into decisions for your own company, there are three things to verify.

  • Do you need to decide which model to use first? If so, check the job postings to see whether that vendor's FDEs are active in Japan.
  • If you can't decide, ask a vendor-neutral support firm to help with business process inventory and KPI design.
  • In either case, check at the end of the contract whether it includes "a state where things run on your own."

The choice of model can be changed later. The design of embedding AI into your business processes is hard to change later. What you should decide first is the latter.
