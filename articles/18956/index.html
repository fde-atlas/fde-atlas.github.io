FDE & DS Excellence Programme

Foundry Labs · 2026 · 1 min read

Summary

Foundry Labs offers a four-session, two-hour-per-session programme for forward deployed engineers, centred on judgement, customer dialogue and delivery responsibility rather than coding. Participants distinguish the FDE role from engineering, consulting and presales, practise uncovering real needs with Five Whys, learn when to decide, consult or escalate, and rehearse answering objections like "we already have Copilot". Each session produces concrete artefacts such as behaviour lists, templates, decision matrices and positioning guides.

Key points

  • Four two-hour sessions for engineers working directly with customers.
  • Covers the FDE role's origins, economics, limits and build-versus-configure judgement.
  • Teaches Five Whys questioning, three-level KPIs and steering-committee design.
  • Trains decision-making with scope guardrails and a shared business ontology.
  • Outputs include an escalation rule, decision matrix and team positioning guide.

Full text

FDE / 4 sessions · 2 hours each

Forward Deployed Engineer

For engineers who work directly with customers. Focused on judgement, customer dialogue and delivery responsibility, rather than coding instruction.

  • The FDE role & design challenge

    Understand the role’s origins, economics and limits; distinguish it from engineering, consulting and presales. Decide when to configure, extend or invent, then rehearse a design under challenge from colleagues before presenting it to a customer. What you make Your team’s eight FDE behaviours, a design-note template and a quality-gate checklist.

  • Customer dialogue

    Use a chain of “why” questions and the Five Whys to uncover the real need. Discover KPIs at three levels, design steering committees and learn when to change the level of a conversation or escalate internally. DS managers can also join this session. What you make A steering-committee design and an escalation rule.

  • Judgement & escalation

    Know when to decide, consult or stop. Give engineers responsibility with a safety net, set scope guardrails and use an ontology to build a shared understanding of the customer’s business. What you make A decision matrix and a reporting format.

  • Positioning

    Answer “we already have Copilot” in the customer’s language. Explain where your offering fits and why its value matters in the customer’s own work. What you make A shared positioning guide for the team.
