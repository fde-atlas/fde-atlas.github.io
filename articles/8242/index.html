Onyx Devs Lab · 2026 · 1 min read

Summary

This Onyx Devs Lab piece explains forward deployed engineering (FDE): when requirements stay vague and data, policies, legacy systems and human judgment interact, engineers embed with domain operators and own the loop from diagnosis to deployment. Success is measured by verifiable operational changes, not frozen feature lists. Teams establish auditable baselines, distinguish symptoms from changeable constraints, ship the smallest complete workflow that moves the target metric, then expand, adjust, or stop based on metrics and failure cases. The article also notes FDE suits high-value, hard-to-specify problems where business staff can join the evidence loop, and that a first cycle covers diagnosis, limited intervention, and enough observation time.

Key points

  • FDE fits vague, high-value problems learned from real operations
  • Engineers embed on-site and own diagnosis through deployment
  • Success means verifiable operational change, not feature completion
  • Build auditable baselines, then deploy minimal complete workflows
  • Expand, adjust, or stop based on metrics and failure cases

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineering) connects requirements discovery, software engineering, deployment, and outcome verification, making it well suited for complex enterprise AI problems that cannot be fully defined before a project begins.

Deep on-site engagement working alongside domain operators

End-to-end ownership from diagnosis to actual deployment

Evidence-driven baselines, interventions, and outcome verification

When is FDE a good fit

When the right solution must be learned from real business operations, FDE is a better fit than frozen requirements.

Requirements are still vague

The team knows the pain points but cannot yet accurately describe the right system intervention.

The environment is complex

Data, policies, legacy systems, and human judgment interact with one another.

Outcome accountability is needed

Success must be reflected in operational metrics, not just completed software features.

How FDE differs from other models

FDE shortens the feedback distance between consulting, specification, engineering, and adoption.

Unlike traditional consulting

The team that diagnoses the problem also implements and verifies the intervention.

Unlike project outsourcing

The unit of success is a verifiable operational change, not a frozen feature list.

Unlike staff augmentation

The team owns a bounded problem and a closed evidence loop.

FAQ

What does FDE mean?

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineering, where engineers work close to the customer's real operating environment, connecting discovery, implementation, and verification.

Is FDE only for large enterprises?

No. It may be a good fit whenever the problem is high-value, hard to define in advance, and business staff can participate in the evidence loop.

How long does an FDE project usually take?

It depends on the business process and the outcome verification cycle; the first cycle typically includes diagnosis, a limited intervention, and enough time for operational observation.
