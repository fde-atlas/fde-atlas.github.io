Forward Deployed Engineer jobs exist in India and demand is growing, but the title is still emerging here. Which Companies Hire Forward Deployed Engineers in India?

The FDE hiring landscape in India is real but early. Roles exist across product companies, Indian startups, and consulting firms, but they don't always carry the exact "Forward Deployed Engineer" title. Here's who's hiring and what to search for.

Product Companies and Big Tech

Palantir coined the FDE title and defines the role. Their India hiring is limited (the company is selective and most FDE roles are US/UK based), but the role definition is theirs, and understanding Palantir's model helps you understand what every other company means when they say "FDE." Check Palantir's careers page for current openings.
Google Cloud hires Customer Engineers and delivery-focused engineers in Bengaluru who do FDE-shaped work: deploying Google Cloud products at enterprise client sites, integrating with client systems, and owning the technical relationship. The title is "Customer Engineer," but the work is forward-deployed. Check Google's careers page for Bengaluru openings.

Salesforce hires into Professional Services and forward-deployed delivery roles in India. The work involves deploying Salesforce products at client sites, customising integrations, and managing the technical delivery. Check Salesforce careers for India roles.

Databricks, Snowflake, and similar data-platform firms hire field engineers and solutions engineers in India who do FDE-shaped work: deploying data platforms at enterprise clients, building integrations, and managing the technical relationship. The titles vary, but the skill set and day-to-day match the FDE profile. Check Databricks careers and Snowflake careers for current openings.

Indian Startups and Product Firms

Sarvam AI and similar well-funded Indian AI startups are building forward-deployed teams to ship AI products at enterprise client sites. The roles are early but growing, and the "AI FDE" variant is where the demand is heading.

Zoho, Freshworks, Chargebee, Postman, and other Indian enterprise-SaaS companies hire solutions engineers, implementation engineers, and field engineers who do FDE-shaped work. The title is rarely "Forward Deployed Engineer," but the job is the same: deploy the product at the client's site, customise it for their environment, and own the technical delivery.

Why FDEs are paid a premium: the role is a rare hybrid of strong engineering and client-facing ability. Most engineers can do one or the other. The few who can do both are scarce, and scarcity drives compensation. MNC and product-company FDE comp typically sits above the standard SWE band because of the client-facing scope, travel expectations, and delivery responsibility.

The FDE title is still emerging in India, and many companies pay for the skill set (engineering + client delivery) rather than the specific title. If you're evaluating an offer, look at the scope (travel percentage, client portfolio, technical depth required) rather than comparing titles across companies.

What Skills Do You Need to Get Shortlisted as an FDE?

FDE hiring managers screen for a hybrid skill set. Missing any one of the three legs below is the most common reason qualified engineers don't get shortlisted.

Engineering Depth (The Non-Negotiable Foundation)

Strong coding (commonly Python, Java, or JavaScript), data structures and problem-solving, APIs and integrations, SQL and databases, and cloud basics (AWS, GCP, or Azure). This is what separates an FDE from a solutions consultant. You're an engineer first. The client-facing part is built on top of real technical ability, not instead of it.
AI-product FDE roles (at companies like Sarvam AI or similar) increasingly expect familiarity with LLMs, GenAI, and the modern AI stack. For the modern AI and LLM stack, that roadmap covers the technical depth AI-first FDE roles demand.

System Design and Deployment Sense

Understanding how systems fit together, integration patterns, and deploying or customising software in a real customer environment. This is what separates an FDE from a pure feature developer. You're not building in a clean sandbox; you're deploying into a client's messy, existing stack.

The question that tests this in interviews: "Walk me through how you'd integrate our product with a client's existing CRM system." If you can't think in terms of APIs, data flows, authentication, and deployment constraints, you'll struggle.
For system design fundamentals every engineer should know, that guide covers the architecture and deployment skills FDEs lean on daily.

Client-Facing and Product Skills (What Hiring Managers Screen Hardest For)

Communication, requirement-gathering, translating a customer problem into a technical solution, and domain empathy. This is the skill competitors under-weight and the one FDE hiring managers screen hardest for.

The interview round that trips people up isn't the DSA question. It's the "walk me through a messy client requirement you shipped" question. If you've only ever worked inside an engineering team, this round is hard to fake.

What Portfolio and Projects Get an FDE Candidate Noticed?

"Build projects" is generic advice. Here's what FDE hiring managers actually look for.
An end-to-end app that's actually deployed. Not just a GitHub repo. A working application with a URL, deployed on a cloud platform, that someone can use. This proves you can ship, not just code.
An integration project. Connect two systems or APIs. Pull data from one source, transform it, push it to another. This mirrors the FDE daily reality of making products work inside a client's existing stack.

A "how I deployed and handed it over" write-up. A README or case study that explains: what the project does, how it's deployed, how someone else would maintain it, and what trade-offs you made. This is the FDE-specific signal most portfolios lack. FDEs ship code that a client's team inherits. If your portfolio doesn't show you can hand over work, you're missing the point.

Evidence of client or stakeholder communication. A demo video with voiceover, a README written for a non-technical user, or a blog post explaining a technical decision in plain language. This proves the second half of the job.

How Do You Stand Out and Clear the FDE Interview?

Getting shortlisted is step one. Converting the interview into an offer is step two. Here's how to do both.

Reposition Your Resume for FDE

If you're coming from a standard SWE role, your resume probably leads with technical projects and algorithm skills. For an FDE application, reframe it:

  • Lead with deployment, integration, and client-facing stories. "Deployed a reporting tool for 50 internal users across 3 teams" is stronger than "Built a REST API."
  • Highlight any work with non-technical stakeholders: requirement gathering, demos, presentations, cross-team collaboration.
  • Show shipped, maintained projects not just built-and-forgotten repos.

Understand the Interview Shape

FDE interviews typically have three components:

  • Coding round: standard DSA, medium difficulty. The bar is real but not competitive-programming hard. Readable, maintainable code matters more than clever tricks.
  • System design / architecture: designing a system that integrates with a client's existing stack. Think about data flow, APIs, deployment constraints, and handover.
  • Behavioural / client-scenario round: "Tell me about a time you turned an unclear requirement into a shipped feature." This is the round that separates FDE candidates from SWE candidates, and it's the one most engineers under-prepare for.

Common Mistakes

  • Over-indexing on pure DSA. The coding round matters, but it's not the differentiator. The client-scenario round is.
  • Ignoring the communication round. If you can't explain a technical decision to a non-engineer, you're not ready for the role.
  • No shipped-project story. FDE hiring managers want to hear about something you deployed for real users, not a side project that runs on localhost.

Is a Forward Deployed Engineer a Good Career Choice in India?

The honest answer: yes, for the right person, with caveats.

The case for FDE in India: high impact (you see your work deployed at real clients), strong compensation (premium over standard SWE), fast skill growth (you learn engineering, product, and business simultaneously), and growing demand (AI and data companies need engineers who can ship and talk to customers).
The case against: travel and on-site client work are real (even in India, domestic travel to client sites is common), delivery pressure is higher than in a product-engineering role, and the title is still maturing here (you may need to explain what you do to future employers who haven't heard of FDE).

The decision framework: you'll likely thrive as an FDE if you enjoy both building and talking to customers, you're comfortable with ambiguity and changing requirements, and you want breadth over depth for the next few years. You may not thrive if you want pure heads-down coding, minimal human interaction, and a well-defined career ladder that every employer recognises.

It's a genuinely good career for the right person. It's a genuinely bad fit for the wrong one. The honest self-assessment matters more than the salary numbers.

FAQs

Is Forward Deployed Engineer a good career in India?

Yes, with caveats. FDEs earn a premium over standard SWE roles, work on high-impact client deployments, and grow fast across engineering, product, and business skills. But the role involves travel, on-site client work, and delivery pressure. The title is still emerging in India, so future employers may not recognise it. Best for engineers who enjoy building and customer-facing work.

What is the salary of a Forward Deployed Engineer in India?

Based on Glassdoor India, AmbitionBox, and Levels.fyi (as of 2026): early career (0-2 years) ₹8-15 LPA, mid-level (3-5 years) ₹15-30 LPA, senior (6-10+ years) ₹30-60+ LPA. FDEs command a premium over pure SDE roles because the hybrid skill set is rare. Data is limited and varies by company and city; access these sources directly for the most current figures.

What skills do you need to become a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Three skill areas: (1) strong software engineering fundamentals (coding, DSA, APIs, databases, cloud), (2) system design and deployment sense (integration patterns, deploying in client environments), and (3) client-facing skills (communication, requirement-gathering, translating customer problems into technical solutions). Missing any one of these is the most common reason qualified engineers don't get shortlisted.

Which companies hire Forward Deployed Engineers in India?

Palantir (origin of the title), Google Cloud (Bengaluru), Salesforce (Professional Services), data-platform firms (Databricks, Snowflake), Indian AI startups (Sarvam AI and similar), enterprise SaaS companies (Zoho, Freshworks, Chargebee), and consulting/GSIs (TCS, Infosys, Deloitte). Roles are often advertised as Solutions Engineer, Implementation Engineer, Field Engineer, or Customer Engineer — search adjacent titles, not just "FDE."

Is Forward Deployed Engineer the same as a Software Engineer?

No. An FDE is a client-facing SWE who deploys and customises products at the customer's site. The engineering bar is similar, but the FDE adds deployment, integration, and communication scope that a standard SWE role doesn't require. The day-to-day includes stakeholder calls, on-site client work, and shipping code that a client's team inherits.

Do Forward Deployed Engineers write code?

Yes, genuinely, day to day. The client-facing part is added to real engineering, not a replacement for it. FDEs write integrations, data pipelines, custom features, and deployment scripts. The coding is real production work, but it's interrupted by client meetings and stakeholder conversations. Some days are 70% code; others are 70% meetings.

How do I switch to an FDE role from a normal software job?

Reposition your resume around deployment, integration, and client stories. Add a shipped, end-to-end project to your portfolio with a "how I deployed and handed it over" write-up. Search for adjacent titles (Solutions Engineer, Field Engineer, Customer Engineer) in addition to "Forward Deployed Engineer." Prepare for the client-scenario interview round, which is the differentiator most SWE candidates under-prepare for.
