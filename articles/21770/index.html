If you work as a freelance engineer, you can't avoid questions like "What hourly rate is fair for me?" and "What does it take to exceed 800,000 yen per month?"

According to a survey by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, Japan is projected to face a shortage of up to approximately 790,000 IT professionals by 2030, and demand for freelance engineers is expected to continue growing.

In this article, based on public data from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare and the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, the latest private surveys from 2025 to 2026, and performance data from our own BizDev Tech, we explain market rates, the conditions for high-rate projects, and the reality of take-home pay.

A freelance engineer's rate is mainly determined by three factors: "programming language and tech stack," "years of experience and skill level," and "role and position."

In this chapter, we explain how each factor affects rates with specific figures. Use it as a reference to determine your current fair rate by comparing it with your own skill set.

Even with limited experience, engineers with in-demand skills (AI, cloud, etc.) may be offered high rates. Conversely, even engineers with long careers may struggle to raise their rates if they only work with outdated technologies.

So what is the average rate level for freelance engineers overall?

Based on official statistics from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare and the latest private survey data, we provide a multifaceted explanation of the actual situation, covering average monthly rates, annual income equivalents, rate differences by work style, and approximate take-home pay.

According to the fixed-point survey by "Freelance Start" (operated by En Japan), the average monthly rate for freelance engineers as of December 2025 is 780,000 yen (783,000 yen).

Throughout 2025, rates showed an upward trend, rising from 746,000 yen in April to over 780,000 yen in December.

PeriodAverage Monthly Rate
April 2025746,000 yen
September 2025760,000 yen
December 2025783,000 yen

Source: En Japan "Freelance Start" Average Monthly Rate Report

Around 750,000 yen serves as a benchmark, and the slight upward trend continues due to economic conditions and growing IT demand.

However, this is the overall average. Actual rates vary significantly depending on skills, experience, and the type of project.

Converting an average monthly rate of 780,000 yen to an annual income gives approximately 9.36 million yen.

However, freelancers cannot always maintain a 100% utilization rate. Considering project transition periods and vacations, the reality is that "an average annual income of around 7 to 9 million yen" is common.

According to the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "2023 Basic Survey on Wage Structure," the average annual income of full-time employee engineers is 5.576 million yen.

The annual income of freelance engineers is roughly 1.3 to 1.6 times that of full-time employees, offering a significant advantage in terms of income.

In addition, an independent survey by IT Pro Partners shows that "the 8 to 15 million yen income bracket accounts for about 50%," indicating that many freelance engineers achieve high incomes.

As of 2025, the proportion of remote projects has surged, and remote projects tend to have higher average rates than on-site projects.

Work StyleAverage Rate (September 2025)Average Rate (December 2025)
On-site738,000 yen766,000 yen
Remote790,000 yen802,000 yen
Rate differenceAbout 50,000 yenAbout 40,000 yen

Remote projects tend to offer high compensation to secure immediately productive talent, providing benefits not only in location flexibility but also in income.

However, fully remote positions are highly popular and competitive, and recently rates have begun to decline.

Conversely, having the flexibility of "being able to come to the office one or two days a week" increasingly gives you an advantage in rate negotiations. The reality on the ground is that by not insisting on full remote and including hybrid arrangements as an option, you can aim for a rate increase of 50,000 to 100,000 yen.

When considering the income of a freelance engineer, it is important to understand the difference between gross revenue and take-home pay.

According to the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "FY2022 Freelancer Actual Conditions Survey," the median annual income of freelancers overall is under 3 to 4 million yen, and only about 3.4% achieve an annual income of 10 million yen or more.

Source: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare "FY2022 Freelancer Actual Conditions Survey"

On the other hand, Mynavi's "Freelancer Awareness and Employment Actual Conditions Survey 2024 Edition" found that the average annual income (profit excluding expenses) of IT engineer freelancers is 6.108 million yen, higher than the average for freelancers across all occupations.

Freelancers must bear the following costs themselves.

  • National health insurance premiums
  • National pension premiums
  • Income tax, resident tax, and individual business tax
  • Expenses (PC, communication costs, transportation, etc.)

The reality is that even with the same gross income as a company employee, take-home pay tends to be lower.

As a guideline for setting rates, "1.3 to 1.5 times your salary as a company employee" is recommended. If someone earning a monthly salary of 500,000 yen becomes a freelancer, they should aim for a monthly rate of around 650,000 to 750,000 yen.

What characteristics and conditions do "high-rate projects" paying 800,000 yen or more per month, especially those exceeding 1 million yen, have?

We will explain in detail the required years of experience, skill sets, and positions, and also introduce trends by field and programming language where high rates are easier to achieve.

Generally, projects paying 800,000 to 1 million yen or more per month are considered "high-rate projects." Achieving 1 million yen per month means an annual income of 12 million yen.

High-rate projects have the following tendencies.

  • Many are direct contracts with end clients, eliminating middleman margins
  • Five or more years of practical experience is the benchmark
  • Upstream process or management experience is required

So, what conditions do engineers earning over 800,000 yen per month have in common? Try diagnosing yourself with the following checklist.

[High-Rate Engineer Spec Sheet]

ItemBenchmark
Practical experience5 years or more
Upstream processesExperience leading requirements definition and basic design
Infrastructure knowledgeExperience building cloud environments such as AWS/GCP
CommunicationExperience negotiating directly with clients
Languages and technologiesIn-demand tech stacks (Go, Python, TypeScript, etc.)

You don't need to meet all of these, but if three or more apply, you likely have a good chance of targeting high-rate projects.

If the checklist reveals any gaps, filling them will open the path to higher rates.

The key is choosing projects with "direct end-client" or "prime contractor" arrangements that have minimal middleman margins. Even with the same skills, it's not uncommon for rates to differ by 100,000 to 200,000 yen depending on the project's sales channel.

Fields that tend to command high rates have the following characteristics.

  • AI and machine learning
  • Cloud infrastructure (AWS/GCP/Azure)
  • Security
  • SaaS product development

The average rate for machine learning engineers is about 798,000 yen, among the highest of all occupations.

By language, as of October 2025, "Rust" leads with an average of 937,000 yen, rising for six consecutive months, followed by Go (870,000 yen) and TypeScript (855,000 yen) maintaining high rates.

METI has also pointed out the shortage of advanced IT talent (AI, IoT, big data, etc.), and the background to high rates lies in a supply-demand imbalance of "high market demand but few available professionals."

Source: METI, "Survey Report on IT Talent Supply and Demand" (2019)

For PM/PMO-level roles, there are many cases on large-scale projects where rates easily exceed 1 million yen per month.

Engineers who can handle upstream processes (requirements definition and basic design) are always in short supply, and demand is strong.

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Research Survey Project on the Labor Market for IT and Digital Talent" (March 2024) also shows that the higher one's IT skill level, the higher the wage level, with wages being highest particularly for "planning and project management" roles.

Along with technical skill, honing management and client-facing abilities and broadening the range of roles you can take on is the shortcut to higher rates.

Stepping up from "just writing code" to "being able to design and manage" can dramatically change your rate.

What they all have in common is added value beyond simply "being able to write code"—they can "move a team" and "speak directly with clients." You don't have to aim for PM right away; start by participating in design reviews or client meetings. That experience will become a major asset in your next rate negotiation. For engineers aiming beyond the 800,000 yen/month mark, the role attracting particular attention is the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer). Because FDE compensation is determined not by "payment for hours worked" but by "contribution to client business outcomes," it is a position where 1.2–1.5 million yen/month projects can be structurally targeted. It's an option worth knowing about for the moment you start feeling a ceiling on your freelance rates.

What strategies are effective for landing high-rate projects and increasing your income?

Here are seven practical tips covering both skills and sales. Compare them with your own situation and start with the ones you can tackle.

The shortcut to raising your rate is acquiring a skill set that the market values highly.

METI's survey also indicates that the shortage of advanced IT talent in AI, IoT, big data, and related fields is particularly severe, and high-rate projects are concentrated in these areas.

Specifically, the following skills are considered "hot."

  • AI/machine learning (Python, TensorFlow, PyTorch)
  • Cloud (AWS, GCP, Azure)
  • Security
  • SaaS development (TypeScript, React, Next.js)

Rather than fixating on a specific language, flexibility in continuously catching up with new technologies is important. Broad skills as a full-stack engineer are also an effective weapon for raising your rate.

In addition, not limited to engineering roles, more and more freelancers are combining adjacent creative skills such as web design to broaden their range of projects. If you are looking for an environment where you can improve your skills practically, 5 Web Design Schools for Freelancers! Comparing Schools That Make It Easy to Land Projects (Campus Co., Ltd.) is also a useful reference.

Most rate-increasing factors depend on "accumulating specialized skills and a track record."

  • Deep knowledge of the languages and frameworks you use
  • Business knowledge of a specific industry
  • Experience delivering measurable results

These become your weapons.

However, skills mean nothing if they aren't communicated. Enrich your portfolio and resume, and make sure "who you did what for, and what results you achieved" is clear at a glance.

Publishing code on GitHub, writing technical blogs, and speaking engagements are also effective ways to make your value "visible."

If you can handle design, requirements definition, and project management, you are more likely to be offered high rates.

PL/PM experience is valuable, and it is a position with very high market value.

Even if jumping straight to PM is difficult, you can step up gradually as follows.

  • Actively take on a leadership role in your current project
  • Volunteer as a sub-leader
  • Take charge of creating design documents
  • Participate in meetings with clients

By consciously accumulating experience beyond "writing code," you can broaden your career.

High-rate projects are often recruited as non-public openings through agents.

The benefits of using an agent are as follows.

  • You can efficiently find projects that match your desired conditions
  • You can receive support and representation in rate negotiations
  • You can leave contract procedures and trouble handling to them

If you "want to find high-rate projects but aren't good at sales" or "lack confidence in your sense of market rates," consider using an agent.

The key is to register even if you aren't looking for a project right now.

Good projects are often filled through agents before being publicly posted, so if you register in advance and communicate your desired conditions, you will be prioritized for introductions when matching projects come up.

A "be ready and waiting" approach is the key to landing high-rate projects.

The strength of freelancing lies in "being able to have multiple income streams."

If you have spare time and energy, taking on multiple projects simultaneously is also a strategy for increasing income.

For example, consider combinations like the following.

  • Project A working 4 days a week (rate: 700,000 yen/month)
  • Project B working 1 day a week (rate: 150,000 yen/month)
  • Total: monthly income of 850,000 yen (equivalent to an annual income of 10.2 million yen)

With the spread of remote work, a "main + side" project structure has become easier to manage.

However, taking on too many projects and becoming overworked defeats the purpose. Consider your own capacity and proceed only within reasonable limits.

When it comes to raising your rate, people tend to focus on technical skills, but trust with clients and communication skills also greatly influence compensation.

Engineers who clients feel "I definitely want to entrust work to this person" or "I want to maintain a relationship with this person even at a somewhat higher rate" tend to ultimately earn higher compensation.

Specifically, the following points are important.

  • Speed of response
  • Accurate understanding of requests and careful proposals and reporting
  • Cooperativeness within the team
  • Professional business manners

Soft skills are never explicitly stated in rate formulas, but the personal qualities that make clients think "I'd like to work with them again" are the hidden driving force behind rate increases.

To raise your rate, sometimes negotiating yourself is necessary.

However, blindly demanding "pay me more" is counterproductive. It's said that negotiation is 90% preparation.

Organize the following in advance.

  • Your strengths and track record
  • The basis for "I have value that justifies this rate"
  • Past results and market rate data from other companies

It's important to be able to explain with objective figures and examples.

Also, the timing of negotiation matters.

  • Right after achieving results
  • At contract renewal
  • When a project is on track

Aiming for a period when the other party truly appreciates your value increases your success rate.

During negotiations, maintain a dialogue-oriented attitude and explain logically in a calm tone. By giving the impression that "this person can be trusted with responsibility," it becomes easier for them to agree to improved terms.

Lean development is a philosophy centered on eliminating waste, while agile development is a set of methods that implement that philosophy as concrete development processes. Agile is a "sub-concept" of Lean.

The seven principles are: ① eliminate waste, ② build quality in, ③ create knowledge, ④ defer commitment, ⑤ deliver fast, ⑥ respect people, and ⑦ optimize the whole. These correspond to the seven types of waste eliminated in the Toyota Production System.

It is particularly suited to new product development where requirement changes may occur frequently, and to the early phases of startups. On the other hand, it can be unsuitable for regulated industries or contracted development with completely fixed requirements.

An MVP is "a product with minimal functionality for validating hypotheses." It is designed in five steps: ① clarify hypotheses → ② set KPIs → ③ narrow down features → ④ release → ⑤ measure and learn.

Scrum is a concrete framework for practicing agile (defining roles, ceremonies, and artifacts), while lean development is a more abstract philosophy. Scrum can be said to be one means of implementing Lean.

The most effective approach is to start by visualizing current waste through value stream mapping. Rather than rolling out across the entire organization at once, we recommend limiting the trial to one or two pilot teams.
