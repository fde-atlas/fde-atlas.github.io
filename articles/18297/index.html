What's the difference between an FDE and a SIer? The reality in Japan as major SIers launch FDE initiatives

Original title: FDEとSIerの違いは？大手SIerがFDEを始めた国内の実態

AIエージェントナビ · 2026-09-03 · 10 min read

Summary

This Japanese article explains how FDEs differ from traditional SIer (system integrator) engagements across three dimensions: FDEs start from undefined problems rather than fixed requirements, their work feeds back into the vendor's product rather than ending at delivery, and success means on-site adoption rather than acceptance sign-off. Drawing on Palantir's official blogs, it describes the Dev/Delta split and how field engineers contribute code back to the core product. It then documents major Japanese SIers entering the FDE space, including Fujitsu's Anthropic partnership, NTT Data group's new FDE-style service, and Salesforce's nine certified Agentforce partners. Of 42 Japanese FDE companies surveyed, 8 assume a specific in-house product, so buyers should ask three questions about requirements, ownership, and completion criteria to detect vendor-neutral versus product-tied providers.

Key points

  • FDE and SIer differ on requirements, where deliverables go, and what counts as done.
  • Palantir splits engineers into Dev and Delta; Deltas contribute code back to the core product.
  • Fujitsu, NTT Data, and other major Japanese SIers now offer FDE-style services.
  • Salesforce certified nine Japanese partners for Agentforce implementations.
  • Of 42 Japanese FDE firms, 8 are product-tied; buyers should verify neutrality with three questions.

When looking for places to introduce AI agents or generative AI, you will come across companies that advertise "FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers)." For companies that have until now entrusted development to SIers (system integrators) and their SEs (system engineers), it can be hard to see what is different from the traditional approach. The differences come down to three points: "whether requirements are fixed," "where the finished product ends up," and "what counts as completion." Moreover, in Japan, major SIers such as Fujitsu and NTT Data Group have themselves started offering FDEs, so you can no longer choose based on a company's business category alone.

Three differences between FDEs and SIers

The FDE is a role established as an in-house job type by the US data analytics company Palantir. It was not originally a role born for AI. The reason it is suddenly being discussed in Japan now is the sticking point of AI adoption: companies can manage pilot introductions, but fail to reach the point where the AI is actually used in day-to-day operations. FDEs are being cited as a way to fill this final stretch on the ground. The differences from SIers (contract development companies) come down to the following three points.

Comparison itemFDESIer (system building)
Starting pointIssues not yet definedFixed requirements
Destination of deliverablesAlso flows back into the provider's productThe customer's system
Definition of completionThe solution is in actual use in the fieldDelivery and acceptance

Starting point: whether requirements exist

Ordering from a SIer presupposes that "what to build" has already been decided. Based on an RFP (request for proposal) or requirements specification, the SIer's SEs (system engineers) build according to the agreed specifications. With AI adoption, however, in most cases it is impossible to know before starting which business processes AI would be effective for. FDEs enter the field from this state of "not knowing where it will help," and identify issues and design solutions within the course of actual operations.

Destination of deliverables

SIers generally consider their work complete once they hand over the built system to the customer's environment. FDEs, by contrast, place importance on incorporating solutions created in the field into the core functionality of their own product (platform). Features built for a specific customer end up contributing to the evolution of the product as a whole. However, this describes the original FDE model established by Palantir; in Japan there are also FDE-type companies without their own products (discussed later).

Definition of completion: delivery or adoption

A SIer's goal is delivery and acceptance. An FDE's goal, by contrast, is to reach the state where employees on the ground are using the system in their daily work. With AI adoption, it is not uncommon for tools to be installed and then left unused. FDEs regard this as the greatest failure, and repeatedly rebuild in short cycles based on feedback from the field.

The mechanism as explained officially by Palantir

The substance of the FDE role is described in detail in information published by Palantir Technologies Inc. The two articles quoted here are from 2019 and 2020, written before generative AI became widespread. This shows that the FDE is not a title born of the AI boom, but a way of working that has existed for some time. The company broadly divides its engineers into two groups: "Dev (product development)" and "Delta (engineers who go into the field)."

The difference in focus between development and the field

Palantir's official blog explains the difference in focus between Devs (product development engineers) and Deltas (the role corresponding to FDEs).

You can think of a Dev's focus as "one capability, many customers," while a Delta's focus is "one customer, many capabilities."

(If you think of a developer's focus as "one capability for many customers," a Delta's focus is on providing "one customer with many capabilities.")

Deciding whether to productize field functionality

FDEs are also tasked with feeding code written in the field back into the core product. The author of the blog post (a recruiting manager at Palantir) summarizes what they heard from the company's Deltas as follows.

Several of the Deltas I spoke to also mentioned that they often contribute code back to the core product.

(Several of the Deltas I spoke with also mentioned that they often contribute code back to the core product.)

However, not every custom feature gets productized. Regarding how to decide where to spend their time, Elisa, a Delta based in New York, explains as follows.

For example, you'll frequently need to decide whether to invest time building a feature your customer needs into the core platform, or to create something custom that we can deliver faster.

(For example, you frequently need to decide, case by case, whether to invest time in building a feature your customer needs into the core platform, or to create something custom that can be delivered faster.)

Product value born in the field

FDEs also play the role of bringing technical insights back to the product development team. Brian, an FDSE at the company, explains as follows in an interview on the official blog.

FDSEs also have the key responsibility of sharing technical expertise from the field back to our business development and product development teams. While FDSEs typically configure software for a particular customer, we often find that our configuration solutions could prove beneficial for many other customers. In fact, some of our most valuable product additions originated in the field through this process!

(FDSEs also have the important responsibility of sharing technical insights gained in the field with the business development and product development teams. While FDSEs typically configure software for a particular customer, they often find that their configuration solutions prove useful for many other customers as well. In fact, some of our most valuable product additions originated in the field through this process.)

Major Japanese SIers entering the FDE space

In Japan, the number of companies calling themselves FDEs is growing, and there is active movement among former SIers to take on the FDE role.

Fujitsu's FDE practical knowledge

Fujitsu, one of Japan's largest IT services companies, stated in its announcement of collaboration with Anthropic released on May 27, 2026 that it possesses FDE practical knowledge of its own, as follows:

Our company possesses practical knowledge of FDE cultivated through strategic partnerships with advanced technology partners, including Palantir Technologies Inc.

In the same announcement, Fujitsu declared that it will "strengthen and expand" an AI-powered FDE model. Japan's flagship SIer is thus putting Palantir-derived experience on its own signboard.

Nine Salesforce-certified companies in Japan

Salesforce emphasizes that implementing its AI agent "Agentforce" requires skills different from traditional system building. In its official announcement of June 8, 2026, it described the reasons why AI adoption stalls at the pilot stage as follows:

The main causes lie not in AI model performance, but in a lack of enterprise-grade implementation know-how, such as data integration with complex existing systems, establishing governance frameworks, and embedding AI into business processes.

To address this challenge, the following nine companies were selected as initial partners in Japan based on their track record of Agentforce implementations.

  • Accenture Corporation
  • NTT Data Corporation
  • Globalway Corporation
  • TerraSky Corporation
  • Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu LLC
  • Dentsu Soken Inc. / Dentsu Digital Inc.
  • PwC Consulting LLC
  • Fujitsu Limited
  • Flect Corporation

These nine companies include both long-established system integrators and consulting firms.

NTT Data's FDE-style services

Within the NTT Data Group as well, on June 16, 2026, Fortience Consulting (formerly QUNIE) announced a new FDE-style service. The official release explains that "a small, elite team of consultants embeds directly into the customer organization and provides consistent support across everything from AI-based business design to application implementation, improvement, and adoption." A group company of NTT Data, Japan's flagship SIer, has thus entered this model from the consulting side.

Why you still need to tell them apart

As shown above, major SIers have begun calling themselves FDEs. So what should clients look at? The clue is "whether the company has a product it wants to sell."

FDEs whose goal is adoption of their own product

SHIFT, listed on the TSE Prime market, offers FDE-style services under the banner of "technology neutrality," not tied to any specific product. In its official release of January 21, 2026, it described conventional FDEs as follows:

Conventional FDEs generally aim to support the introduction and adoption of their own products.

Eight companies premised on their own products

Of the 42 companies in Japan we verified based on official information (as of August 31, 2026), 8 offered FDE-style services premised on a specific in-house product. That means roughly one in five companies bundles its services with a product. If you have already decided to use that product, the vendor knows it best, so these 8 companies are not inferior. The problem is when clients compare options without realizing this premise.

In addition, 4 enterprise-type providers were operated by major groups (SB OAI Japan, Fujitsu, Accenture, and Exaforward Kyushu). Of these, Exaforward Kyushu is scheduled to begin operations in November 2026 and cannot yet be engaged. The 42 companies represent the range we could verify from primary sources and do not cover every company in Japan. The categories are also our own classification.

How to judge neutrality

Whether a company wants to sell its product or genuinely support problem-solving cannot be determined from its name or the "FDE" title alone. Before signing a contract, try asking the following questions:

"Will your FDE also take responsibility for integrating with our existing systems and with tools from other vendors?"

If the answer is "as long as it stays within our own products," the company is product-based; if it is "we will make your field operations run smoothly regardless of the product," it is technology-neutral. Note that Palantir, the originator of the model, is itself product-based, so "is it the same as Palantir?" is not a useful criterion for judging neutrality.

Summary: how to tell them apart in three points

With major SIers entering the space, the boundary between FDEs and SIers is becoming blurred. Rather than relying on labels, check the following three points.

  • Is what to build already decided? If a specification document can be written, it's an SIer; if you want to think through it together in the field, it's an FDE.
  • Where do the deliverables go? If you want assets dedicated to your company, it's an SIer; if you want to benefit from a shared foundation, it's a product-based FDE.
  • What counts as "done": If on-time delivery is the priority, choose an SIer; if embedding and improvement in the field matter most, choose an FDE.

At your next sales meeting, ask these three questions exactly as they are: "Will the requirements be decided by us, or together with your company?" "Will the system you build belong to us?" "What counts as done?" If a company cannot answer all three, it may call itself an FDE but in practice be close to conventional contracted development.
