Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer Interview Experience (Rejected, May 2026)

Top Tech Transition · 2026-09-08 · 1 min read

Summary

A mid-level candidate shares a rejected Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer interview from May 2026. The three-round process included a recruiter behavioral screen, a technical round covering dependency-based job ordering (topological sort), debugging duplicate alerts, and an open-ended product design task using taxi-trip data, plus a final round on ambiguous projects and customer impact. The candidate found interviewers collaborative and the process well organized, but struggled with time management in the unfamiliar-codebase round. Tips: practice medium coding problems, reading unfamiliar code, debugging under pressure, and tying solutions to customer outcomes.

Key points

  • Three rounds: recruiter screen, technical, final onsite.
  • Technical round tested topological sorting, debugging, and product design.
  • Candidate felt time management slipped in the unfamiliar-codebase round.
  • Advice: practice medium problems, debugging under pressure, customer framing.
  • Culture came across as direct, fast-moving, and ownership-driven.

Interview process

The process was well organized and the interviewers were collaborative. I did well when thinking aloud and adapting to new information. I could have managed time better during the unfamiliar-codebase round.

Interview rounds · 3

  • 1

    Recruiter screen

    Behavioral
    • Q1. Why do you want to work at Palantir?
    • Q2. Why forward deployed engineering?
    • Q3. Tell me about a time you worked with unclear customer requirements.
  • 2

    Technical round

    CodingDecompositionDebugging
    • Q1. Given jobs with dependencies, return a valid execution order.
    • Q2. Find and fix the defect causing duplicate alerts.
    • Q3. How would you design a product using historical taxi-trip data to help drivers?
  • 3

    Final / onsite round

    • Q1. Tell me about the most ambiguous project you have owned.
    • Q2. Why does your solution need to be built, and what changes for the user if it works?

Tips from the candidate

Practice medium-level coding problems, reading unfamiliar code, debugging under time pressure, and open-ended system design. Be ready to explain every decision and connect your solution to the customer outcome.

Company culture

The culture came across as direct, fast-moving, and ownership-driven. The team seemed comfortable challenging assumptions while still making the interviews collaborative.
