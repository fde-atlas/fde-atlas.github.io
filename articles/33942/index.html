Forward Deployed Engineer Interview Questions (2026)

Top FDE Jobs · 2026-11-27 · 24 min read

Summary

This article analyzes 1238 live FDE job postings to identify what interviews actually test, then presents 36 questions each with a scoring answer, a failing answer, and likely follow-ups. It maps the typical three-to-six-week loop, from recruiter screen to case, coding, system/AI design, and behavioral rounds, noting candidates find the case round hardest. It compares Palantir's Forward Deployed Software Engineer loop (phone screens, three onsite rounds, a decomposition round in nearly every loop) with Databricks' FDE loop (four to six onsite interviews, reportedly modeled on Palantir's). Coding appears in 82% of postings, LLM/RAG/agent skills in 78%, integrations and cloud/security each in 76%, and 53% mention customer-site travel.

Key points

  • LLM, RAG and agent skills appear in 78%; evals are a named requirement
  • Case round is the most commonly reported hardest stage
  • Palantir loops almost always include a decomposition round

Start with Coding

The loopHow FDE interviews run

Most FDE loops take three to six weeks and test you as an engineer and as someone a customer will trust. The shape varies by company; this is the common one.

  • 01Recruiter screenWhy FDE, how much travel works for you, which level fits.
  • 02Technical screen or take-homeA practical build: clean data, call an API, a small AI feature.
  • 03Case roundA vague customer problem. Scoping, data and a first version.Hardest to pass
  • 04CodingProduct-style problems in stages, with tests and edge cases.
  • 05System or AI designDesign for one real customer: retrieval, evals, permissions, their cloud.
  • 06Behavioral and hiring managerOwnership, pushing back on customers, what broke and what you changed.

Candidates most often report the case round as the hardest.

How Palantir and Databricks differ

Palantir

  • Onsite: three 60-minute rounds from a pool of interview types candidate reports
  • Hiring manager round that re-tests a weaker area from the onsite candidate reports

Candidates report a decomposition round (break a big, vague problem into parts) in almost every loop. Palantir's own advice: deliver a working idea first, then expand it.Source

Forward Deployed Engineer

#1Coding

Most FDE loops include live coding, usually practical rather than puzzle-style: parse a file, call an API, fix a bug in an unfamiliar codebase. Python comes up most.

Live FDE postings that ask for it82%

  • Expert-level proficiency writing scalable production code in Python, Scala, Java, Go, or JavaScript/TypeScript, and architecting solutions using modern application frameworks. Sr. Staff Forward Deployed Engineer · Databricks
  • Proficiency in a programming language such as Python, Go, TypeScript, or Java, and experience writing, testing, and maintaining production code. Forward Deployed Engineer · depthfirst

See FDE jobs that ask for this

  • Question 1

    Here's a CSV export from a customer. Write a script that cleans it and loads it into our API.

    Very close to real FDE work. They watch how you handle bad rows and failures. How to answer
    • 1Read with a proper CSV parser; check the header first
    • 2Validate each row and collect errors, not crash on the first one
    • 3Send in batches with retries and a clear report at the end
    • 4Write one or two quick tests for the parsing
    TipTalk through edge cases out loud: encodings, missing fields, duplicates, rate limits. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 2

    You get a codebase you've never seen and one hour to fix a bug. Where do you start?

    FDEs work inside customer and product code they didn't write. How to answer
    • 1Reproduce the bug with the smallest example
    • 2Find where the data goes wrong (search, logs, debugger)
    • 3Make the smallest fix and add a test that fails without it
    • 4Note anything risky you saw but didn't change
    TipReproduce first. Then narrow down with logs or a debugger before changing anything. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 3

    How do you keep quality high when you ship fast at a customer site?

    Speed is the job, but broken code at a customer is costly. They want your guardrails. How to answer
    • 1Tests for the parts that touch customer data or money
    • 2Code review even when it's fast, ideally by someone on the product team
    • 3Feature flags and an easy way to roll back
    • 4Clean up the shortcuts in the next release, and write them down
    TipName the few checks you never skip, and what you skip on purpose. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 4

    Fetch every record from a paginated API that rate-limits you and sometimes fails.

    A very common practical exercise. It looks easy; the follow-ups are about failure. How to answer
    • 1Loop on the next-page token until there isn't one
    • 2Back off and retry on rate limits and temporary errors, with a limit
    • 3Save progress so a crash resumes from the last page
    • 4Test with a fake API that fails on purpose
    TipWrite the happy path quickly, then spend your time on retries and resuming. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 5

    Build a small in-memory cache. Then add expiry. Then a size limit.

    Staged exercises show how your code holds up when requirements change mid-interview, like at a customer. How to answer
    • 1Start with get and set on a dictionary, plus tests
    • 2Store an expiry time with each value and check it on read
    • 3Track use order and evict the least recently used entry when full
    • 4Say what you'd change for many threads or many machines
    TipKeep the first version simple and well tested so the next stage is a small change. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice

#2LLMs, RAG and agents

Most new FDE roles sit on top of an AI product. Interviewers check that you can ship an LLM feature a customer can trust, and measure it.

Live FDE postings that ask for it78%

  • Modern software delivery with AI/GenAI, LLMs, RAG, agentic workflows, AI evaluation, prompt/retrieval testing, or responsible AI governance. Forward Deployed Engineer · Wolters Kluwer
  • Hands-on experience designing and building AI/LLM-based applications, including prompt engineering, RAG, and agentic workflows. Forward Deployment AI Engineer III (Embedded Solutions) · Insperity

See FDE jobs that ask for this

  • Question 1

    A customer wants a chatbot over their internal documents. How do you build it?

    RAG is the most common first project. They want the whole path, from messy files to answers people trust. How to answer
    • 1Collect and clean the documents; handle permissions per user
    • 2Split them into chunks, embed, and store with source links
    • 3Retrieve, then answer with citations; say “I don't know” when retrieval is weak
    • 4Build a test set of real questions with expected answers before launch
    TipSpend more time on the data and on testing than on the model. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 2

    How do you know an LLM feature is good enough to ship?

    Evals are what separate a demo from production. Many roles list them by name. How to answer
    • 1Write 50–200 real examples with expected outcomes, with customer experts
    • 2Agree on a pass bar and the errors that are never acceptable
    • 3Score automatically where possible; review a sample by hand
    • 4Re-run on every prompt or model change, and watch live traffic after launch
    TipDescribe a test set built with the customer, a pass bar agreed up front, and checks that run on every change. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 3

    The model gives a confident wrong answer to an important customer. What now?

    Tests how you handle trust, on top of the technical fix. How to answer
    • 1Tell the customer the same day what you know and when they'll hear more
    • 2Reproduce it and find the cause (bad retrieval, missing data, prompt, model)
    • 3Add the case to the test set so it can't come back unnoticed
    • 4Add a guard where it matters: citations, confidence checks, human review
    TipOwn it with the customer first, then show the change that stops it happening again. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 4 Senior and up

    When would you use an agent that takes actions, and how do you keep it safe?

    Agent work is growing fast in FDE postings. Senior candidates are expected to know where agents fail. How to answer
    • 1Use agents for multi-step tasks with clear success checks; avoid them where one wrong action is costly
    • 2Give the agent narrow tools and permissions, not broad access
    • 3Require human approval for risky actions
    • 4Log every step so the customer can audit it
    TipStart from the cost of a wrong action. Limit what the agent can do, and log everything. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 5

    Support says the AI assistant “is wrong sometimes.” How do you make that something you can fix?

    Customers describe AI problems in feelings. They want to see you turn that into a number. How to answer
    • 1Collect real failures from logs and from the support team
    • 2Sort them: wrong document found, right document but wrong answer, or the question wasn't answerable
    • 3Build a test set from them and measure today's error rate
    • 4Fix the biggest group first and show the number moving
    TipAsk for ten real bad examples before you change anything. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 6 Senior and up

    When would you fine-tune a model, and when is prompting or retrieval enough?

    Customers often ask for fine-tuning first. They want to hear you pick the cheapest thing that works. How to answer
    • 1Try clear prompts and examples first; measure against a test set
    • 2If the model lacks the customer's facts, add retrieval
    • 3Fine-tune when you need a format or behaviour prompts can't hold, and you have good examples
    • 4Count the cost: training data, upkeep and re-testing on every model update
    TipStart from what's wrong: missing knowledge points to retrieval, wrong style or format points to fine-tuning. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice

#3Integrations and APIs

Customer systems are old, custom and full of surprises. Expect questions on connecting your product to them safely, and on what you do when their API breaks.

Live FDE postings that ask for it76%

  • Hands-on experience with API and enterprise system integration, including REST and GraphQL APIs, webhooks, authentication and identity (such as OAuth), and event-driven or messaging patterns. Enterprise AI Architecture Consultant (Forward Deployed Engineer) · Accenture
  • API and integration fluency: Experience working with REST APIs, webhooks, and third-party integrations. You've connected systems together and understand data flow across platforms. Forward Deployed Engineer · Avallon

See FDE jobs that ask for this

  • Question 1

    Walk me through connecting our product to a customer's internal system that has no documentation.

    This happens weekly in the job. They want a calm, step-by-step method. How to answer
    • 1Find who owns the system and get read access to a test environment
    • 2Watch real traffic or logs to learn the data shapes
    • 3Build a thin adapter with logging around every call
    • 4Agree on how failures get reported, and who fixes what
    TipTalk about finding the owner of the system first. The code is the easy part. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 2

    How would you set up single sign-on for a large customer?

    SSO (SAML or OAuth/OIDC) is a common first task in enterprise deals and a common source of delays. How to answer
    • 1Confirm their identity provider (Okta, Entra ID, Google) and protocol
    • 2Exchange metadata and test in a staging tenant first
    • 3Map user groups to roles in your product
    • 4Plan for user removal: what happens when someone leaves the company
    TipMention the people involved, and the protocol second. The customer's identity team usually controls the timeline. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 3

    A customer's API starts returning errors in production at 2am. What do you do?

    FDEs often own production for their customers. This tests incident handling and communication. How to answer
    • 1Check your side first: logs, recent deploys, retries piling up
    • 2Stop the damage (pause jobs, queue requests, turn off the feature)
    • 3Tell the customer what you see and what you've done, in plain words
    • 4Afterwards: add a check that would have caught it, and write a short report
    TipSplit the answer into the first hour and the week after. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 4

    The customer is on the call. Their integration is failing on live data, you've never seen their system, and they want it working before the call ends. What do you do?

    FDEs debug in front of the customer. This tests whether you stay methodical with someone watching. How to answer
    • 1Reproduce it on their data first, with them watching
    • 2Split your side from theirs: what you sent, what they received
    • 3Ship the smallest fix they can see working
    • 4Say what you won't touch on this call, and when you'll follow up
    TipSay what you're checking as you go. The customer should always know what you're doing and why. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 5 Senior and up

    When would you build a custom integration, and when would you push for a product feature?

    Senior FDEs turn repeated customer work into product. They want to hear how you decide. How to answer
    • 1Custom first, to unblock the customer and learn
    • 2Track how often the same need shows up across customers
    • 3Bring the product team a short write-up with real usage and effort saved
    • 4Help build the general version and move customers onto it
    TipUse a simple rule, such as “the third time we build it, it becomes product,” and show how you'd make the case to the product team. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice

#4Cloud, deployment and security

Enterprise and government customers often run your product in their own cloud or data center. Security reviews can decide the deal, so expect questions on both.

Live FDE postings that ask for it76%

  • Proficiency in Kubernetes, Docker, Terraform, AWS/GCP/Azure, OIDC/OAuth2, and zero-trust networking. Distinguished Forward Deployed Engineer, AI Security · Palo Alto Networks
  • Strong background in on-premise, private cloud, and multi-cloud environments (AWS, Azure, GCP), including experience with containerization and orchestration (Docker, Kubernetes). Forward Deployed Engineer - DevOps · Seekr

See FDE jobs that ask for this

  • Question 1

    A bank wants our product inside their own cloud account, with no data leaving it. How do you approach it?

    Self-hosted and private-cloud deployments are common in regulated industries. How to answer
    • 1Learn their rules early: network limits, approved services, review process
    • 2Package the product so it installs the same way each time (containers, Terraform)
    • 3Plan how you'll get logs and updates without touching their data
    • 4Run a test install with them before the real one
    TipTalk about the conversation with their security team as much as the setup. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 2

    The customer's security team sends a 200-question review. How do you handle it?

    Security reviews often block deals. FDEs help get through them. How to answer
    • 1Start from your company's standard answers (SOC 2 report, past reviews)
    • 2Answer clearly; don't oversell
    • 3Bring in your security team for anything you're unsure of
    • 4Keep the new answers for the next customer
    TipReuse past answers, flag honest gaps, and offer a call for the hard questions. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 3

    How would you debug a deployment that works in staging but fails at the customer's site?

    Customer environments differ in ways you can't see. This tests method under pressure. How to answer
    • 1Compare versions, config and environment variables
    • 2Check network rules, proxies and certificates (common in enterprise)
    • 3Look at resource limits and permissions
    • 4Add the missing check to your install steps afterwards
    TipList the usual suspects and how you'd check each without full access. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 4

    How do you make sure an AI search tool never shows people documents they aren't allowed to see?

    This is the first thing a customer's security team asks about. Getting it wrong ends the deal. How to answer
    • 1Carry each document's permissions from the source system into the index
    • 2Filter search results by the user's identity before the model sees them
    • 3Re-sync permissions when they change at the source
    • 4Log who saw what, and test with users who should be denied
    TipFilter at retrieval, before the model sees anything. A prompt rule is not access control. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 5 Senior and up

    The agent reads emails and web pages. How do you stop text in them from taking control of it?

    Prompt injection is the main security risk of agents that act. They want concrete defences. How to answer
    • 1Keep instructions separate from the content the agent reads
    • 2Give the agent only the tools and permissions the task needs
    • 3Require a person to approve actions that send, pay or delete
    • 4Check outputs and log every tool call for review
    TipAssume some content is hostile and limit what damage it can do. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice

#5Ownership and working with customers

Behavioral rounds matter more for FDEs than for most engineers. Postings ask for ownership, comfort with ambiguity and travel, so prepare real stories.

Live FDE postings that ask for it73%

  • Drive end-to-end ownership of AI initiatives in a fast-paced environment, navigating ambiguity, influencing cross-functional partners, and delivering practical solutions that support use cases across the enterprise. Forward Deployed Engineer Senior - AI · PNC Financial Services Group
  • Operate with genuine autonomy and end-to-end ownership across the agent lifecycle. Forward Deployed Engineer · NiCE
  • Question 1

    Tell me about a project where the requirements kept changing. What did you do?

    Ambiguity is the norm. They want proof you can keep moving without a clear spec. How to answer
    • 1The project and why requirements kept moving
    • 2What you did to pin them down (short demos, written decisions)
    • 3The trade-offs you made to keep shipping
    • 4The result, with a number if you have one
    TipShow how you reduced the change, beyond how you coped with it. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 2

    Describe a time you had to tell a customer bad news.

    FDEs are the face of the company on site. Trust is part of the job. How to answer
    • 1The situation and what went wrong
    • 2When and how you told them
    • 3The plan you offered
    • 4How the relationship looked afterwards
    TipPick a story where you told them early and came with a plan. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 3

    Why FDE and not a regular software engineering role?

    They want people who like customer work, not engineers who'll tolerate it. How to answer
    • 1A moment when working directly with users changed what you built
    • 2What you like about the mix of engineering and customer work
    • 3Why this company's customers or product interest you
    • 4The part you'll miss, and why it's worth it
    TipBe specific about what you enjoy: seeing your code used, solving messy real problems, travel. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 4

    How do you handle being on the road with customers for weeks at a time?

    Many FDE roles involve regular travel. They check you know what you're signing up for. How to answer
    • 1Your past travel or on-site experience
    • 2How you stay productive away from the team
    • 3What travel level works for you, said plainly
    TipBe honest about what works for you. A mismatch here hurts both sides later. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 5

    Tell me about something you shipped that broke in production.

    Everyone has broken production. They want to hear you own it and change something afterwards. How to answer
    • 1What broke and who it affected
    • 2How you found out and what you did in the first hour
    • 3The root cause, said plainly
    • 4What you changed so it can't happen the same way again
    TipPick a real one with a cost. Spend most of the answer on what you changed. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice

#6Discovery and scoping

FDEs often meet the customer before anyone has written a spec. Interviewers want to see you turn a vague request into a problem you can solve in weeks, and say no to the parts that don't matter.

Live FDE postings that ask for it70%

  • Lead discovery and use case sessions with client stakeholders, translating business needs into application scope, and own that scope as it evolves. Senior Forward Deployed Engineer - Databricks Apps · Lumenalta
  • Lead discovery and scoping from pre-sales through post-production, translating ambiguous business problems into hypothesis-driven problem framing, system requirements, and delivery plans with measurable outcomes. Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), Financial Services · OpenAI
  • Question 1

    A customer says “we want AI in our claims process.” What do you ask in the first meeting?

    Most FDE work starts this vague. They want to hear you find the real problem before you pick a tool. How to answer
    • 1Map the current process: who touches a claim, in what order, with which systems
    • 2Find the slowest or most error-prone step and ask what it costs them
    • 3Agree on one measurable goal (for example time per claim, or error rate)
    • 4Ask what data you can access, and who signs off on security
    TipAsk about the work, not the technology. Who does it today, how long it takes, what a mistake costs, and how they'd know it got better. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 2

    How do you decide what to build first when a customer wants ten things?

    FDEs have limited weeks per customer. This tests judgment about value, effort and risk. How to answer
    • 1Score each request by business value and by effort, with the customer in the room
    • 2Pick one item that proves value fast and unblocks later work
    • 3Write down what's out of scope so it doesn't creep back in
    • 4Revisit the list after the first release, with real usage data
    TipShow a simple way to rank the list and name what you'd leave out on purpose. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 3

    Tell me about a time the customer asked for one thing but needed another.

    Good FDEs spot when a request is a guess at a solution. They want proof you can redirect a customer without losing their trust. How to answer
    • 1What they asked for, and the detail that made you doubt it
    • 2How you tested the doubt (shadowed users, looked at logs, ran a quick prototype)
    • 3How you made the case to the customer
    • 4What shipped and what changed for them
    TipPick a story where you checked your hunch with data or users before pushing back. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 4 Senior and up

    A senior executive and the hands-on team want different things. How do you handle it?

    Enterprise deployments have several stakeholders. Senior FDEs are expected to align them, not pick a side quietly. How to answer
    • 1Meet each side to learn what they need and why
    • 2Find the shared goal (usually the business metric the executive owns)
    • 3Propose a plan that meets that goal and removes the team's biggest pain
    • 4Get a written decision so the project doesn't stall later
    TipShow that you get both views on the table early and tie the plan to the goal the executive owns. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 5

    A delivery company is losing money and hands you six months of order data. Where do you look first?

    This is the classic case round: a vague business problem and a pile of data. They watch how you narrow it down. How to answer
    • 1Ask how they measure “losing money” today and since when
    • 2Split cost per delivery by region, time, driver and order size to find where the loss sits
    • 3Check the data for gaps that could fake a pattern (refunds, cancelled orders)
    • 4Propose one fix to test in a single region, with the number that proves it worked
    TipSay what the data can and can't tell you before you pick an angle. Missing columns are part of the answer. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 6

    The customer's finance lead says your report “looks off” but can't point to a number. What do you do?

    Vague complaints are normal. They test whether you stay calm and turn it into something checkable. How to answer
    • 1Ask them to walk you through how they read the report and what they expected
    • 2Compare one number end to end against their own source
    • 3Write down each metric's definition and get them to agree it
    • 4Fix it, then add a check that would have caught it
    TipSit with the person and the report together. Usually the definitions differ, not the math. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice

#7Data and SQL

A large share of FDE work is getting messy customer data into shape. Some loops include a live SQL exercise; others ask you to design a pipeline on a whiteboard.

Live FDE postings that ask for it45%

  • Develop ETL/ELT pipelines, notebooks, and APIs using Python, SQL, and PySpark. Support analytical solutions using Microsoft Fabric, Azure Synapse, Data Factory, Power BI, and Azure Machine Learning. Forward Deployed Engineer (Data, AI and Cloud Solutions) · Jacobs
  • Data Engineering & Integration: Proficiency in complex SQL, ETL/ELT pipeline design, and experience building system integrations connecting AI solutions to enterprise and telecom data sources. Senior Forward Deployment Engineer · T-Mobile

See FDE jobs that ask for this

  • Question 1

    Write a query that finds each customer's first and most recent order.

    A quick check that you're comfortable with grouping, joins and window functions. How to answer
    • 1Clarify the tables and what counts as an order (cancelled ones?)
    • 2Use MIN/MAX with GROUP BY, or ROW_NUMBER() if you need the whole row
    • 3Handle ties on the same timestamp, and customers with no orders
    • 4Explain how you'd test it on a few known customers
    TipSay what you'd check in the data before writing the query: duplicates, time zones, nulls. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 2

    The customer's data is spread across five systems that disagree. How do you get one clean view?

    Real deployments rarely have one source of truth. They want a practical approach, not a perfect one. How to answer
    • 1List each system and what it's the best source for
    • 2Pick matching keys and decide how to handle records that don't match
    • 3Load into one place with clear rules, and keep the raw copies
    • 4Show the customer the mismatches; they often fix the source
    TipName the system the business trusts most for each field, and get the customer to agree. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 3

    How would you design a daily pipeline that feeds customer data into our product?

    Tests basic data engineering: scheduling, failures, re-runs and monitoring. How to answer
    • 1Extract with timestamps or change tracking, not full dumps, when possible
    • 2Transform in steps you can re-run without creating duplicates
    • 3Validate row counts and key fields before loading
    • 4Alert a person on failure, with enough detail to fix it
    TipMake re-running safe (same input, same result) and say how you'd know it failed. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice
  • Question 4

    Two HR systems list the same people under different IDs. How do you match them?

    Matching records across systems is routine FDE work and easy to get quietly wrong. How to answer
    • 1Find the fields both systems trust: work email, employee number, name plus start date
    • 2Match exact keys first, then fuzzy matches with a confidence score
    • 3Send low-confidence pairs to a person for review
    • 4Keep a mapping table and re-check it when either system changes
    TipTalk about the cases you'd send to a person instead of guessing. Full answer, the answer that fails, and practice

Watch outMistakes that sink FDE candidates

  • 01Designing before asking Drawing boxes in the first two minutes of a case round. Ask about the user and the goal first.
  • 02Solving everything at once A full platform when a narrow first version would show value in weeks.
  • 03“We'll tune the prompt” AI answers with no test set, no pass bar and no way to tell if a change helped.
  • 04No production instincts Nothing about logging, monitoring, rollback or what happens at 2am.
  • 05Stories where you are “we” Team wins where your own part is unclear. Say what you did and what changed because of it.
  • 06Treating the customer as a spec Ignoring the people, politics and trust that decide whether the work gets used.

    • Prepare five stories: a customer win, a pushback, a failure, unclear requirements, owning something end to end
  • Week 2Practise the case round
    • Do three case prompts out loud with the five-step method
    • Record yourself and check you asked questions before designing
    • Practise naming one user, one decision and one metric
  • Week 3Build one real thing
    • Build a small RAG tool or agent over real documents
    • Add a test set and a pass bar, and measure it
    • Write a one-page design note you can defend in a deep dive
  • Week 4Coding and company prep
    • Practise product-style coding problems with tests: pagination, CSV cleaning, a staged cache
    • Read what your target company's postings and product docs stress
    • Do one mock loop end to end with a friend

What changes with level

The questions stay similar; the bar moves. Entry candidates are judged mostly on coding and how they learn. From Senior up, expect the questions marked “Senior and up”: scoping with executives, turning custom work into product, and safe agent design.

Questions

  • What do forward deployed engineer interviews cover?
  • Most loops mix live coding, a customer scenario or case study, system design and behavioral questions. In 1238 live FDE postings, the areas that come up most are Coding (82%), LLMs, RAG and agents (78%), Integrations and APIs (76%).
  • How hard is the FDE interview?
  • It is broad more than deep. You are tested as an engineer and as someone a customer will trust, so weak communication can sink a strong coder. Typical mid-level FDE postings ask for 3+ years of experience, Senior roles 5+.
  • Do FDE interviews include coding?
  • Almost always. 82% of live FDE postings name a programming language or production coding, and Python comes up most. Exercises tend to be practical: parse data, call an API, debug unfamiliar code.
  • How long does the FDE interview process take?
  • Usually three to six weeks: a recruiter screen, a technical screen or take-home, then three to six rounds covering a customer case, coding, system or AI design and behavioral questions. Companies differ, so ask the recruiter for the exact loop.
  • Can I use AI tools in an FDE interview?
  • It depends on the company and the round. Some allow AI assistants in take-homes, others ban them in live rounds. Ask your recruiter before each round, and be ready to explain every line you submit.
  • Do forward deployed engineers travel?
  • Often. 53% of live FDE postings mention travel or working at customer sites. Ask the recruiter for the expected share of time on the road before the final round.
