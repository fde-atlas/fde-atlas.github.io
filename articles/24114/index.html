What's the difference between Cloud Foundry and Palantir Foundry?

Original title: Cloud FoundryとPalantir Foundryの違いは？

FDE総研 · 2026-11-02 · 11 min read

Summary

This article untangles the frequent confusion between Cloud Foundry and Palantir Foundry, two products that share only the word "Foundry". Cloud Foundry is an open-source PaaS (originated at VMware in 2011, now under the Cloud Foundry Foundation, Apache 2.0) covering the app deployment and runtime layer. Palantir Foundry is Palantir's closed-source commercial data platform spanning data integration, ontology modeling, and operational apps. The authors compare them across four dimensions, provide keyword checklists for identifying which product a job posting refers to, and advise aspiring Palantir-style FDEs to prioritize ontology and data-pipeline thinking over platform runtime skills.

Key points

  • The two products are not competitors; they occupy different system layers.
  • Cloud Foundry: open-source PaaS for deploying and running apps (cf push, Buildpacks, BOSH).
  • Palantir Foundry: commercial platform for data integration, Ontology, and operational apps (AIP, Workshop).
  • Job postings can be disambiguated via surrounding keywords like SAP BTP versus Ontology or AIP.
  • Aspiring FDEs should learn ontology and data modeling first; decide which layer of problems you want to solve.

What's the difference between Cloud Foundry and Palantir Foundry?

Cloud Foundry is an open-source application execution platform, while Palantir Foundry is a commercial platform for data utilization—they are completely different things.

Because they share the word "Foundry," the two are sometimes confused in job postings and technical articles. To help readers considering an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) career avoid making the wrong judgment, FDE Soken has organized the two products along four original axes (the layer they handle, main users, deliverables, and distance from FDE work). First, let's look at the fundamental differences in developer and delivery model.

Differences in developer, license, and delivery model

Cloud Foundry is an OSS managed by the Cloud Foundry Foundation, while Palantir Foundry is a proprietary product of Palantir.

Cloud Foundry is a PaaS (Platform as a Service) that originated at VMware and was announced in 2011. Since then, it has continued to be developed as open source under the neutral Cloud Foundry Foundation. Well-known commercial distributions include the Cloud Foundry environment of SAP Business Technology Platform (SAP BTP) and VMware Tanzu-related products. Palantir Foundry, on the other hand, is a commercial data platform developed and provided by Palantir Technologies. Its source code is not publicly available, and it is used by companies and government agencies that contract with Palantir.

Are there any commonalities besides the name "Foundry"?

The only commonality is that both are platform software that runs in the cloud; there is no direct connection in terms of technical lineage or job role context.

Both are named "Foundry" (a place where things are cast) in the sense of "a foundation on which to build something," but the problems they solve are different. Cloud Foundry addresses "how to run written code in production," while Palantir Foundry addresses "how to connect scattered data to business decisions." As the editorial team of FDE Soken, we believe that comparing the two as "competing products in the same category" is itself a misunderstanding. What should be compared is "which experience is useful for which career path."

Which layer of the system do Cloud Foundry and Palantir Foundry handle?

Cloud Foundry handles the application deployment and execution layer, while Palantir Foundry handles the higher layers from data integration to business decision-making.

When the system is divided into layers, the difference in scope between the two products becomes clear. Cloud Foundry sits between infrastructure and applications, while Palantir Foundry sits between data and business operations.

The layer Cloud Foundry handles: application deployment and execution

cf push

This is a developer-oriented execution layer where, once you hand over your code, everything from build to launch and scaling is taken care of.

Cloud Foundry's key feature is that developers can push applications to production without worrying about the fine-grained configuration of containers or servers. Buildpacks automate the build process for each language, while the platform side handles runtime environment management and scaling. Traditionally, VM management via BOSH formed the foundation, but there is also a project called "Korifi" that reproduces the Cloud Foundry API experience on Kubernetes. People with Cloud Foundry experience are thought to have mostly worked on business application development on SAP BTP or internal PaaS operations at large enterprises.

The layer Palantir Foundry covers: data integration, ontology, and business applications

This is the layer that ingests data, translates it into business terms via ontology, and connects it all the way to the applications and decision-making used in the field.

Palantir Foundry ingests multiple data sources such as ERP systems, sensors, and spreadsheet files, and transforms them through pipelines. On top of that, it defines business concepts (objects) such as "customer," "equipment," and "order," along with their relationships, as an Ontology. Field staff use business applications built with tools like Workshop to not only view data but also execute "actions" such as approvals and assignments. With AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform), announced in 2023, the use of large language models on top of this ontology for business purposes is also expanding. It is best to understand that what Palantir's FDEs build at customer sites is mainly this layer.

How to distinguish between the two similarly named products in articles and job postings?

In job postings and articles, the most reliable way to tell them apart is to look at the surrounding terms (cf push and BOSH, or Ontology and AIP).

Job postings that simply say "Foundry experience preferred" or technical articles full of abbreviations can make it unclear which product is meant. FDE Soken recommends using the following checklist to decide.

How to verify when only "Foundry" is mentioned

Check the company name, industry, and required skills; if it is still unclear, the surest way is to ask directly about the product name during an interview.

There are other products named "Foundry" as well. For example, Microsoft's generative AI development platform (Azure AI Foundry family) and The Foundry, which makes visual effects software. The verification steps are as follows.

  • Look at the hiring company's industry: For manufacturing or distribution companies that have adopted SAP, Cloud Foundry is more likely; for companies that have announced partnerships with Palantir, Palantir Foundry is more likely.
  • Look at the languages in the required skills: If it centers on Java and Node.js with many infrastructure terms, it is Cloud Foundry; if PySpark, SQL, TypeScript, and data modeling are listed together, Palantir Foundry is more likely.
  • Look at the subject of the job duties: If it is "keep applications running stably," Cloud Foundry is more likely; if it is "solve customers' business challenges with data," Palantir Foundry is more likely.
  • Confirm the product's official name in a casual interview: This is the most reliable way to prevent mismatches after joining.

If you aim to become an FDE, should you learn Cloud Foundry or Palantir Foundry?

If you aim to become a Palantir-style FDE, you should prioritize learning the concepts behind Palantir Foundry, especially ontology and data pipelines.

The core of an FDE's work is to embed themselves in the customer's field and solve business challenges with data and software. Rather than knowledge of the platform that runs applications, the ability to "model the business" is what is tested. That said, Cloud Foundry experience is not wasted; the order in which you learn depends on your current situation.

From the FDE Research Institute's editorial perspective, the strength of people from Cloud Foundry is that they have a sense of "keeping things running in production." This is because whether something built at a customer's site goes beyond a one-time demo and actually enters operation is a factor that can easily determine the success of an FDE's work.

How to learn when you have no access to Palantir Foundry

Even if you cannot touch the product itself, you can acquire most of the underlying way of thinking through official documentation and general-purpose data technologies.

Since Palantir Foundry is a product that assumes a contract, it is not easy for individuals to use a business-level environment. The official documentation (palantir.com/docs) is publicly available, so we recommend first reading about the concepts of Ontology and Pipeline Builder. Personal trial environments and training availability and conditions may change, so check the official website as of November 2026. Even outside the product, you can practice the same way of thinking through approaches like the following.

  • Take a familiar business task (e.g., store inventory management) and write out a data model on paper in the form of "objects, relations, and actions"
  • Build pipelines with dbt or Spark to integrate and transform data sources
  • On top of the integrated data, build screens that "enable judgment and action" using TypeScript or low-code tools
  • Have a third party use what you built and check whether you can explain it in business terms

What kinds of engineers are suited to Palantir Foundry experience? Who is not?

It suits people interested in both data and business who enjoy working on-site with customers, and does not suit those who want to go deep into infrastructure technologies.

While Palantir Foundry experience is a shortcut to becoming a Palantir-style FDE, the nature of the skills gained is quite distinct. Comparing it with other roles makes it easier to judge whether it suits you.

Who it suits: comparing FDE with SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer

FDE differs from every adjacent role in that it takes on both "implementing things yourself" and "working on-site with customers."

People suited to this role include, for example, the following.

  • Those who do not mind digging into "why these numbers don't match" together with the business owners
  • Those who would rather show something working within a few days and get reactions than pursue perfect design
  • Those who can work hands-on across their area of responsibility, from organizing data to building screens

Who it does not suit, and career options for those with Cloud Foundry experience

For people who want to master a specific infrastructure technology deeply or focus on development with fixed specifications, a career centered on Palantir Foundry is a poor fit.

Because Palantir Foundry abstracts many functions on the product side, opportunities to work deeply with infrastructure or runtime internals are limited. For those who want to master Kubernetes internals or distributed system design, it may feel unsatisfying. Also, Palantir Foundry operational skills themselves can be hard to convey in value outside companies that have adopted Palantir products. For this reason, it is important to be able to explain not just product knowledge but also transferable strengths such as data modeling and decomposing business problems.

Even if those with Cloud Foundry experience do not aim to become FDEs, there are paths such as platform engineering, SRE, and internal development platform teams. Conversely, if you feel you want to work close to customers' business, it is realistic to build on your deployment and operations experience and expand into the data field. The FDE Research Institute recommends deciding "which layer of problems you want to solve" before "which product to learn."
