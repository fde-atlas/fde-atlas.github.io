What Is a Sales Engineer? | Job Description and How It Differs from an FDE

Original title: セールスエンジニアとは｜仕事内容と、FDEとの違い

FDE Consulting · 2026-10-23 · 7 min read

Summary

Drawing on public job postings from Datadog, GitLab, MongoDB, AWS, and OpenAI in Tokyo, this article explains what Sales Engineers do and how they differ from adjacent roles. The author organizes the comparison along two axes: when the role engages the customer and what it builds. Sales Engineers run demos and proofs of concept before a deal closes; Customer Success Engineers and Technical Account Managers support customers afterward, differing in breadth versus depth. FDEs, by contrast, own problems from scoping through production and write code themselves. The piece also covers skills for Sales Engineers, a path toward becoming an FDE, and how buyers should choose among these roles.

Key points

  • Sales Engineers center on pre-sale technical evaluation (POC) and demos.
  • Customer Success Engineers serve many clients; TAMs go deep with a few large ones.
  • FDEs work from problem definition to production, measured by business impact.
  • The author compares roles by timing of involvement and what each builds.
  • Moving from SE to FDE usually requires production coding and operations experience.

A Sales Engineer is a technical role at software and cloud companies who works alongside sales to help customers choose products from a technical standpoint. They listen to the customer's challenges, explain whether the product can solve them, and demonstrate and verify this through demos and technical evaluations.

Roles that sit between customers and technology have similar names and can be confusing. Besides Sales Engineer, there are Customer Success Engineer, Technical Account Manager (TAM), Solution Architect, and FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer). In my view, the differences are easiest to organize by looking at two things: "when they get involved" and "what they build." This article compares the job descriptions of each role based on job postings and descriptions published by Datadog, GitLab, MongoDB, and AWS for Tokyo and Japan. The FDE role itself is explained in What is an FDE? The meaning and job of a Forward Deployed Engineer.

What a Sales Engineer Does

A job posting that gives a clear picture of the Sales Engineer role is Datadog's Sales Engineer (Tokyo) listing. As of October 23, 2026, this posting describes the Sales Engineer as a role that identifies and drives customer and partner deals to closure, and states that the role contributes technical expertise through sales presentations, product demos, and support for technical evaluations (POCs).

The responsibilities listed in the same posting, translated into English and arranged, look like this.

Responsibilities from the job postingSupplementary details
Work with sales and customer success teams to explain the product's value and direction to customersAttend sales meetings as the technical representative
Present the product and technology to prospects and existing customersAnswer technical questions during proposals
Interact directly with product teams and relay feature requestsFeed customer feedback back into product improvements
Help customers try out the product and get started with itResolve blockers during technical evaluation and early adoption
Solve technical issues that arise across the entire customer lifecycleContinue to handle inquiries even after the deal closes

The application requirements include 2+ years of experience in sales engineering or solution engineering, along with experience in DevOps, SRE, or platform engineering. Since the role involves speaking directly with customers, native-level Japanese is required, and travel is up to 25% of working hours.

In my view, the core of this role is the technical evaluation. Customers want to verify, before signing a contract, whether the product will work in their environment and truly solve their challenges. The Sales Engineer helps design the validation, explains the results, and eliminates concerns one by one. Being good at demos alone is not enough; the role requires the ability to draw out the constraints of the customer's environment and scope the validation correctly.

Customer Success Engineer and Technical Account Manager

If the Sales Engineer is primarily a pre-sale role, there are also roles that support customers technically after the contract. Naming conventions vary by company, but two patterns can be seen in public job postings.

Customer Success Engineer

GitLab's Senior Customer Success Engineer (Japan) posting describes the job as supporting customers so they can realize measurable value from GitLab, through technical guidance and driving adoption. Working with customers via Zoom and email, the role expands usage and resolves adoption blockers. It also includes working with the Account Executive and Renewals Manager to support renewals and expansion. The position is based in Japan and requires fluency in both Japanese and English.

In other words, a Customer Success Engineer is a technical role that ensures the product is actually used after it is sold. They handle many customers in parallel and grow how customers' teams use the product through workshops and demos.

Technical Account Manager (TAM)

MongoDB's Technical Account Manager (Tokyo) posting describes the job as working deeply with a small number of large accounts, building relationships with customers' technical leaders while advising on configuration, performance, recovery, and security. The role also involves creating shared goal roadmaps with customers and relaying customer requests to product teams. The required experience is 7+ years.

At AWS, enterprise support plans come with a dedicated TAM. According to AWS's description, the dedicated TAM leads Well-Architected Reviews, provides design guidance on security, reliability, performance, cost, and operations, and brings in AWS specialist engineers when needed.

The difference between a Customer Success Engineer and a TAM, in my view, lies in the number and depth of the customers they handle. The former expands usage across many customers, while the latter accompanies the critical systems of a small number of large accounts.

How the Four Roles Differ from the FDE

Let me line up what we've covered so far, adding Solution Architect and the FDE. Since every company defines these roles differently, this is my own way of organizing them.

RoleWhen mainly involvedWhat they buildHow the work ends
Sales EngineerBefore the deal closesDemo and technical evaluation environmentsDeal closes
Solution ArchitectFrom pre-sale through post-deploymentConfiguration design and validationDeployment succeeds and usage expands
Customer Success EngineerAfter the contractUsage guides and workshopsUsage expands and the contract renews
Technical Account ManagerAfter the contract, long-termDesign reviews and operational adviceThe large account's critical systems stay stable
FDEFrom defining the problem through productionThe system that runs in the customer's business itselfIt is used in production and business metrics move

All four of the above aim to get their company's product used within the customer. What they build stops at demos, validation, guides, and advice; building the customer's business systems is the job of the customer or a partner.

The FDE takes that on themselves. OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting states that the role works with strategically important customers and owns discovery, technical scoping, design, building, and production deployment, and requires getting hands-on with code directly when progress depends on it. Success is measured by production adoption and measurable impact on the business.

In my view, a Sales Engineer is the person who answers "can this product solve it?", while an FDE is the person who answers "how should this business change?" and builds it. The background behind OpenAI and Anthropic introducing FDEs is summarized in FDEs at OpenAI and Anthropic, and the difference from GTM Engineers, who build the sales machinery, is summarized in What is a GTM Engineer?.

Who suits the Sales Engineer role, and the path to becoming an FDE

In my view, someone suited to being a sales engineer is a person who enjoys translating technical topics into the other party's language and doesn't tire of meeting many customers in short periods. Datadog's job postings also look for experience handling many projects of different sizes and complexity simultaneously.

There are three things to acquire.

  • The products you handle and the technologies around them. For a monitoring product, that means cloud architecture up through DevOps.
  • The ability to design technical evaluations. Decide in advance what needs to be verified for the customer to make a decision.
  • The ability to explain. Switching between value-oriented discussions for executives and detailed discussions for engineers.

There is also a path from sales engineer to FDE. The skills of listening to customer problems and explaining technology can be used as-is. What tends to be lacking is experience writing production code, operating it, and continuously fixing it. The path to becoming an FDE is summarized in How to become an FDE?, and what is asked in interviews is summarized in FDE interview questions.

How to choose from the customer's perspective

From the perspective of a company introducing AI or cloud products, a product vendor's sales engineer is the person who helps design the technical evaluation together before the contract. After the contract, customer success engineers or TAMs support usage and design. However, all of these roles operate on the assumption of their own company's products. Work involving problems that can't be solved with the product, or deciding where in business operations to introduce AI, tends to fall outside their scope.

In my view, if the products to use are already decided and you have people in-house to build with them, the support of these vendor roles is sufficient. If you have no one to build, or want to decide what to build from scratch, you need a partner who takes responsibility through to production.
