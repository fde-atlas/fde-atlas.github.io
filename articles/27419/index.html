Today's Judgment

Today's core story is not "a few more agents," but that enterprise AI has begun filling in two layers of truly reusable intermediate assets:

The screening layer—ChinaSoft International disclosed that its FDE team went on-site with customers and screened 30 "implementable" scenarios out of 235 AI idea scenarios—what enterprises lack is not ideas, but an elimination mechanism; the control layer—Baidu released its Agent Security Center (ASC), AgentOS, and Cloud Skills Center on the same day, and the competition over the "control plane" of domestic agent platforms is now in full swing.

The overseas engineering community also provided a deep sample of high-risk tasks: security firm Trail of Bits used agents to build an audit toolchain for a zero-knowledge virtual machine and discovered high-severity vulnerabilities—the value of high-risk tasks comes from toolchains and machine-verifiable evidence, not one-off answers.

Key Signals

ChinaSoft International: FDE team on-site screened 235 AI ideas down to 30 implementable scenarios

  • Source: China.com Finance | Report link; ChinaSoft International AI Consulting
  • Materials from ChinaSoft International's allmeta conference disclosed: the team went on-site with customers and screened 30 "implementable" scenarios out of 235 AI idea scenarios for project initiation and value validation; the company combines FDE with leading consulting, BootCamp, and the allmeta enterprise intelligence operating system into a complete pipeline from scenario identification to production deployment.
  • Why it matters: The 235→30 ratio reveals an undervalued value of the FDE role—eliminating early the ideas that lack value, data, an accountable owner, or acceptance criteria is worth more than delivering more prototypes. Enterprises never lack AI ideas; what they lack is a mechanism for "saying no in time." A reusable screening standard is the "five-gate screening": business value → data/interface availability → a clear owner → a measurable baseline → production acceptance and exit criteria—if any gate is missing, gather the evidence first before scheduling development. Elimination records are themselves an asset: recording the reasons for elimination weekly builds the organization's own scenario screening knowledge base. Note: the figures 235 and 30 come from conference materials, and the customer is unnamed; "implementable" does not mean "already in production."

Baidu releases ASC, AgentOS, and Cloud Skills: control plane competition in full swing

  • Source: Baidu Intelligent Cloud product updates | Original link
  • On September 20, Baidu Intelligent Cloud officially released the Agent Security Center (ASC), AgentOS, and the Cloud Skills Center; together with the agent sandbox AX released in August, this forms a continuous product line spanning runtime environments, security controls, and skill supply.
  • Why it matters: Within one week, Huawei Cloud's AgentArts and Baidu's ASC/AgentOS successively completed their control planes—the focus of competition among domestic agent platforms has shifted from "building an agent" to "managing where it runs, what it can call, and how it is constrained and reused." This precisely matches real needs in the enterprise field (BOE's "agent silos" the day before). Advice for service providers: don't treat writing a product list into a proposal as having the capability—pick a real workflow and run a minimal comparison test of "skill registration → permission declaration → sandboxed execution → trace logging → failure replay → version rollback" before deciding on a tech stack. Note: the official release focuses on product entry points and does not disclose named customers or production metrics.

Trail of Bits: the value of high-risk audit tasks comes from the toolchain, not one-off answers

  • Source: Trail of Bits | Original link
  • Security firm Trail of Bits reviewed its practice of using agents to build LSPs, decompilers, static analysis, and Lean formal proof tools for the Miden zkVM (zero-knowledge virtual machine)—discovering high-severity vulnerabilities and numerous type-validation defects. The core method: instead of letting AI directly "answer whether code is safe," have AI build reusable analysis tools and speak through machine-verifiable evidence.
  • Why it matters: This provides a methodological reference for all high-risk AI tasks—"AI gives conclusions directly" and "AI builds tools, and the tools produce verifiable evidence" are two ways of working with completely different levels of trustworthiness. Scenarios such as auditing, compliance, and financial verification should prefer the latter: AI's outputs are tools and evidence that can be run repeatedly and independently verified, not an untraceable judgment. This aligns with the logic of assetizing experience: one-off answers evaporate, while tools and evidence chains accumulate.

Implications for FDE practitioners

  • Run the demand pool uniformly through the "five-gate screening": business value, data/interfaces, an accountable owner, a measurable baseline, production acceptance—requirements that fail the gates do not enter the development schedule. Mistaking relationships and enthusiasm for project readiness is the number one cause of losses for service providers.
  • The completion marker of assetizing experience is "reuse by a second person": on-site records → events → evidence → insights → recommendations → applicable conditions/rules → a second person reuses and validates them in a real task. Without that last step, it can only be called knowledge structuring, not organizational capability—"writing the veteran's words into a knowledge base" is not the same as assetizing experience.
  • Label demo data with four evidence tiers: real materials, customer statements, inference, fiction—any statistics and conclusions must inherit the lowest evidence tier; screenshots of demo states spreading out of context are a common source of trust incidents.
  • For high-risk tasks, have AI build tools rather than give conclusions: learn from Trail of Bits—audit and compliance deliverables should be repeatable analysis tools and machine-verifiable evidence, which is also the best answer for explaining to customers "why our solution costs more."
