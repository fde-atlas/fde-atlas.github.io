What is the FDE Certification Level 3? What You Can Do with It and Its Benefits

Original title: FDE検定3級とは？取得でできること・メリット

日本Fde協会 · 2026-07-18 · 7 min read

Summary

The Japan FDE Association explains its FDE Certification Level 3 (Practitioner), the core tier of its five-level scheme. Level 3 certifies whether someone can independently handle a small-scale engagement—entering a client site, identifying problems, and designing and implementing solutions with AI or code. The article outlines what holders can do alone versus with supervision, the four skill areas tested, benefits for individuals and hiring companies, and preparation advice emphasizing real-world experience over memorization.

Key points

  • Level 3 measures end-to-end capability, not project scale.
  • Independently allowed: single-department improvements; cross-department or large data projects need supervision.
  • Exam tests problem discovery, technical implementation, client negotiation, and adoption support.
  • Preparation relies on practical experience; moving from Level 4 requires discovery and negotiation skills.
  • Certification gives freelancers objective credibility and companies a hiring benchmark.

FDE Certification Level 3 (Practitioner) is the core grade among the five FDE certification levels. It was designed as a standard for companies to judge "whether this person can be entrusted with small-scale projects." While Levels 5 and 4 measure foundational FDE knowledge and AI tool operation, Level 3 certifies practical capability to "enter the customer's workplace, identify issues, and design and implement solutions using AI and technology."

As the representative director of the Japan FDE Association, I designed this system, and my own practical experience is strongly reflected in the definition of Level 3. Entering a department of a small or mid-sized company, collaborating with dozens of stakeholders, and carrying out everything from requirements definition to operational design—that is the benchmark of the abilities certified at Level 3. If you want to first understand the overall picture of the FDE certification, please see "What is the FDE Certification? Explaining the Five Levels and Exam Content".

The Scope of What You Can Do at FDE Level 3

The benchmark of abilities certified at Level 3 is organized into three tiers: "what can be handled independently," "what should be done under the supervision of a senior," and "what should be refrained from."

CategoryDescription
Can be handled independentlyBusiness improvement projects for a department of a small or mid-sized company. Can complete workplace observation → issue identification → prototyping → implementation → adoption support alone. Includes AI tool selection, no-code/code implementation, and customer negotiation
Supervision of a senior recommendedProjects spanning multiple departments. Design of large-scale data platforms. Projects centered on negotiation with management. Project management with 20 or more stakeholders
Should be refrained fromOverseeing company-wide transformation programs. Institutional design of FDE organizations. Business-strategy-level architecture design (→ the domain of Levels 2 and 1)

This demarcation is not a restriction saying "you must not go beyond this at Level 3." It is an indicator for accurately understanding where you currently stand and knowing where you have room to grow.

What "Can Be Handled Independently" at Level 3 Means in Practice

When I worked with a manufacturing client, workplace observation revealed that a factory staff member was spending two hours every day manually transcribing data, and I built a simple dashboard prototype and showed it to them that same day—this sequence of actions is the practical image of Level 3. Finding issues yourself, shaping solutions yourself, and showing something working to the customer. This "ability to do everything from issue identification to implementation alone" is the core of Level 3.

The key point is not "scale" but "whether you can do it end to end." Even if the scale is small, if you can handle everything from issue discovery to adoption support alone, that is Level 3-level work. For more on the FDE way of working, see "What is an FDE? A Practitioner Explains the Difference from SEs and SIers".

Benefits of Obtaining Level 3

Benefit 1: It provides objective grounds for "calling yourself an FDE"

When you want to work independently as an FDE or win contracts for FDE-like work, the biggest hurdle is the customer's anxiety: "Can we trust you with this?" With Level 3 certification, you can say "I hold a benchmark of practical capability certified by the Japan FDE Association." This is not a sales pitch but an objective indicator of ability. Speaking from my experience struggling with personnel selection on financial institution projects, having such a common standard alone greatly reduces the risk of mismatches.

Benefit 2: You can see the "gaps" in your own skills

The Level 3 exam scope systematizes the skills needed for FDE practice. The areas where you did not pass are your room for growth. "Strong technically but weak at interviewing" or "high business understanding but little prototyping experience"—the exam makes your skill balance visible.

Benefit 3: It serves as a company-side "standard for finding FDE talent"

For companies wanting to advance DX initiatives, "we don't know what level of external talent to bring in" is a common concern. With a standard like "FDE Level 3 or above," talent selection becomes far easier. A director of our association, who runs multiple businesses, also points out: "Whether someone knows the actual workplace matters most, but there was no way to judge that in advance." Level 3 also functions as that means of judgment.

What Is Tested in the Level 3 Exam

The Four Ability Areas the Exam Measures

The Level 3 exam consists of four broad areas. First, issue discovery: the ability to observe the customer's operations and identify essential issues. Second, technical implementation: the ability to shape solutions into prototypes using AI, no-code, and coding. Third, customer negotiation: the ability to translate between customer requests and technical constraints and build consensus. Fourth, adoption support: the ability to embed what was built into the workplace and run operations.

These are not independent skills; in FDE practice they are required simultaneously. On a SaaS company project, asking a single question—"Would customers really use this feature?"—saved three weeks of effort. In that moment, issue discovery and customer negotiation were working at the same time.

For Those Aiming for Level 3—How to Think About Preparation

"Practical Experience" Is Your Greatest Weapon, More Than Exam Prep

The FDE Level 3 certification is not an exam you pass by memorizing a textbook. For people with experience solving problems using AI and technology in the field, the work they do every day is itself exam preparation. Conversely, trying to pass the exam alone without practical experience will not give you the essential value of Level 3.

I myself have failed many times in developing AI SaaS products and launching e-commerce businesses. I once believed "if you build something good with technology, it will sell," developed without customer feedback, and had zero sales for six months. Both the successes and failures of such practical work form the foundation of the abilities tested at Level 3.

The Image of Stepping Up from Level 4 to Level 3

Level 4 (Operator) is the level at which you can perform routine business improvements using AI tools. The difference from Level 3 is "whether you can enter the customer's workplace, find issues yourself, and solve them end to end." In other words, the step from Level 4 to Level 3 is less about improving technical skill and more about acquiring "issue discovery" and "customer negotiation." If you have 3 to 5 years of practical experience at an SES company, moving to Level 3 level is entirely possible by focusing on strengthening your business understanding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. When can I take the Level 3 exam?

A. The exam schedule is currently being prepared. The latest information will be published on the FDE Certification page. If you register as a member, we will notify you of the exam launch on a priority basis.

Q. Can I skip Level 4 and take the Level 3 exam directly?

A. We are considering allowing exam skipping. We are designing flexible exam routes so that candidates with extensive practical experience do not get stuck at the entry point.

Q. Will passing Level 3 increase my workload?

A. Level 3 certifies a benchmark of ability and does not guarantee the acquisition of specific projects or increased income. However, we believe it helps build trust with clients by allowing you to objectively demonstrate your practical capabilities as an FDE.

Q. If I fail, can I retake the exam?

A. Yes, retakes are allowed. Even if you fail, we plan to provide feedback on which ability areas need improvement. We aim for a design that helps you identify skill gaps and understand what to develop next.
