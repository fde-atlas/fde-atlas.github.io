When the "3E" Forum Meets FDE: The "Shanghai Solution" for Forward Deployed Engineers Is Unveiled_Pengpai Shanghai

Original title: 当“3E”讲坛遇上FDE，前沿部署工程师的“上海方案”出炉

The Paper · 2026-05-31 · 6 min read

Summary

On May 30, 2026, Shanghai Innovation Institute hosted the "3E" forum and launched the Shanghai (Yangtze Delta) competition for young and mid-career engineers, alongside a new advanced training program for Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs). Officials argued Shanghai's 3.28 million technical professionals still lack hands-on FDE talent to bridge AI from lab to market. The competition introduces a dedicated FDE track covering AI agents and embodied intelligence, plus a challenge-based track where companies pose real problems. The training program devotes over a third of its hours to practice on real industry scenarios. Speakers from StepFun and Luobo Intelligence stressed technical, product and business skills, while Fudan researcher Zhu Siyu presented work on world models, visual generation and AI for cultural heritage.

Key points

  • The "3E" forum and engineer competition launch were held May 30 at Shanghai Innovation Institute
  • A new FDE advanced training program was launched alongside the competition
  • Shanghai has 3.28 million technical professionals but lacks FDE talent for AI's last mile
  • The competition adds a dedicated FDE track on AI agents and embodied intelligence
  • Over one-third of training hours use real industry scenarios, not simulated exercises

Full text

On May 30, the 2026 "3E" Forum and the launch ceremony of the Shanghai (Yangtze River Delta) Innovation and Entrepreneurship Competition for Young and Middle-Aged Engineers was successfully held at Shanghai Innovation Institute. The event also launched the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) Advanced Training Program, innovatively creating a trinity cultivation model of "forum empowerment + competition-driven + training for talent," closely aligned with the development needs of new quality productive forces and focused on the key challenge of the "last mile" of AI deployment, delivering practical, versatile engineering talent for the AI and key industries of the Yangtze River Delta.

As artificial intelligence moves from technological iteration to deep industrial application, from the laboratory to the production line, the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)—who bridges technology R&D and scenario application, connecting algorithm innovation with market demand—has become the most scarce and core talent type in the industry. Shanghai has 3.28 million professional and technical personnel. How to convert this talent scale advantage into industrial competitive advantage, and how to cultivate FDE talent with both technical capability, industry insight, and deployment experience, became the core topics of this event.

Breaking Through the "Last Mile" of AI Deployment: Three Platforms Working in Concert to Forge an FDE Talent Force

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) refers to a versatile engineer who can go deep into the industrial frontline, face customers' real scenarios, deeply integrate cutting-edge AI technology, algorithm models, and industry needs, and transform technical solutions into deployable, deliverable, and value-generating products and services. They are both understanders of technology and translators of needs; both extensions of R&D and vanguards of deployment, shouldering the key mission of bringing AI technology from the laboratory to the production line and to the market. As AI technology continues to develop, FDEs have become the most scarce and core talent type in the integration of the digital and traditional industries.

Roundtable dialogue scene. All photos in this article are provided by the Shanghai Municipal Human Resources and Social Security Bureau

During the roundtable dialogue, Ding Feng, Party Secretary and Director of the Shanghai Human Resources Public Service Center, explained Shanghai's talent development approach of leveraging three platforms in synergy: the 3E Forum, advanced training programs, and the engineers competition. He stated frankly that as the AI industry has developed, many companies are still stuck at the "last mile": algorithms struggle to leave the laboratory, technology cannot be converted into products, and innovation fails to generate real value. FDE talent who can turn technology into products and solutions into scenarios is the key to breaking this impasse.

"Shanghai has 3.28 million professional and technical personnel. We don't lack people; we lack people who can fight tough battles and break through barriers. Today, we aim to fill exactly that gap!" Ding Feng said. Facing rapidly expanding market demand, the 3E Forum, as a high-level exchange platform for professional and technical personnel, will accelerate the transformation of traditional engineers into FDE talent, letting more engineers learn about FDE, understand FDE, and be willing to join the FDE ranks.

The innovation and entrepreneurship competition is the touchstone of value. This year, the competition has for the first time established a dedicated track for FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), focusing on hot areas such as AI agents and embodied intelligence. It has also launched a special "Challenge-Based Problem Solving" track, adopting the model of "companies pose problems, the government formalizes them, teams solve them jointly, the competition validates them, and the market evaluates them." "Last year we certified 315 mid-to-senior level engineers, and post-90s engineers are already taking on major responsibilities in the low-altitude economy and artificial intelligence. This year, we're waiting for you!" Ding Feng's words resonated powerfully.

Ding Feng, Party Secretary and Director of the Shanghai Human Resources Public Service Center, giving a presentation.

Ye Feng, Deputy Director of the Industrial Cooperation and Innovation Department at Shanghai Innovation Institute, revealed the underlying logic behind the training program's design, saying: "We will select mentors who are closer to the market, inviting sci-tech innovation companies in Shanghai and our entrepreneurship mentors to provide support from the market and enterprise side. We have set aside more than one-third of the training for practical training, and all scenarios come from the frontline and real industry needs."

Making Industry-Education Integration Real Through Scenario-Based Training: Companies Pose Problems, Talent Answers, Platforms Empower

How do you cultivate "the strongest brains" into battle-ready practitioners? In the roundtable dialogue, guests from government departments and sci-tech innovation companies engaged in in-depth exchanges around "real scenarios, real problems, real capabilities," jointly outlining a deployable, replicable path for industry-education integration.

Ye Feng, Deputy Director of the Industrial Cooperation and Innovation Department at Shanghai Innovation Institute, stated frankly that practice and deployment are the most core and most difficult part of FDE training. He said the institute insists on "training students on real battlefields," rejecting simulated, paper-based training; instead, starting from industry pain points and ending with delivered results, it forms a complete cultivation loop, so that trainees truly learn with problems in mind, tackle difficult challenges head-on, and graduate with real results.

Ye Feng, Deputy Director of the Industrial Cooperation and Innovation Department at Shanghai Innovation Institute, giving a presentation.

As one of the first trainees of the program, Xu Qi, Party Branch Secretary and Deputy Secretary-General of the Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Standardization Technical Committee, believes that FDE talent must have deep industry backgrounds, be proficient with AI tools, and possess the ability to solve problems in context. As more and more companies establish FDE-related positions, the specialization, industry focus, and scenario orientation of talent are becoming increasingly evident. Going forward, efforts will continue to promote scenario opening, standards improvement, and factor aggregation, creating a more complete industrial ecosystem for FDE talent development.

Xu Qi, Party Branch Secretary and Deputy Secretary-General of the Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Standardization Technical Committee, giving a presentation.

Sci-tech innovation companies understand frontline needs best. Zhao Haitao, Vice President of Shanghai StepFun Intelligent Technology Co., Ltd., talked about the biggest challenge companies face in their development: "whether they can always maintain their faith in AI." An engineer who can truly deliver needs to possess "advanced technical capabilities, business collaboration and deployment capabilities, and business thinking"—and these are precisely the goals of the FDE training system.

Zhao Haitao, Vice President of Shanghai StepFun Intelligent Technology Co., Ltd., giving a presentation.

Pan Yunan, Co-founder & CTO of Shanghai Luobo Intelligent Technology Co., Ltd., used the development of the hit AI companion product "Fuzai" as an example, saying that smart hardware like "Fuzai" requires engineers to be deeply involved throughout the entire product lifecycle, combining technical foundation, product thinking, and business acumen to transform vague user needs into clear product solutions, and to turn large model technology into deployable, perceivable, and commercializable real experiences.

Pan Yunan, Co-founder & CTO of Shanghai Luobo Intelligent Technology Co., Ltd., giving a presentation.

"You could say that every employee at our company, in getting to where they are today, is an FDE engineer." In her view, team members must deeply understand the technical boundaries of large models, know what product needs general and vertical models can support, and, even more importantly, precisely capture the value customers truly want during deployment, ultimately delivering hit products that users are willing to pay for, happy to share, and use long-term.

World Models Leading the Technical Direction: From Visual Generation to Social Simulation, Solidifying the FDE Technical Foundation

If the roundtable dialogue answered the question of "how to do it," then the personal practice of Zhu Siyu, a full-time mentor at Shanghai Innovation Institute and researcher at Fudan University, opened up grand imagination about "what can be done."

Zhu Siyu said that the AI field is changing extremely fast: AI has rapidly moved from text large models to multimodal generation, and from conversational interaction and code writing, it is gradually advancing toward world models and embodied intelligence that are deeply integrated with the real world. He stated frankly, "For AI to upgrade further, it must physically interact with the real world, acquiring real-time data in scenarios such as autonomous driving, robot operation, and on-site deployment, using data and computing power as dual drivers to achieve a higher-level leap in intelligence."

Zhu Siyu, full-time mentor at Shanghai Innovation Institute and researcher at Fudan University, giving a keynote presentation.

In the direction of micro visual generation, Zhu Siyu's team achieved breakthroughs in real-time digital human interaction, 4K ultra-high-definition video generation, and gesture-controlled visual content through model architecture iteration and reinforcement learning optimization, providing a technical foundation for industrial deployment. "We want generated content to better conform to physical laws, support more real-time interaction, and run stably for longer periods, so that the technology truly leaves the laboratory."

In addition, Zhu Siyu's team is also using AI to empower cultural heritage. "We have integrated scarce professional data such as archaeological reports, ancient scripts, and historical geography to build a multimodal model and agent application platform for early Chinese civilization." The team collaborated with the Hunan Provincial Museum on the digitization research of Mawangdui artifacts, and completed a simulation of prehistoric social evolution using Liangzhu culture as a sample, making AI a new tool and new method for historical research and cultural dissemination.

A "3E" forum showed government officials, entrepreneurs, and engineers in attendance the unlimited possibilities of "new technologies, new scenarios, and new value." As Zhu Yinghua, Director of the Professional and Technical Personnel Management Division (Public Institution Personnel Management Division) of the Shanghai Municipal Human Resources and Social Security Bureau, said: "In Shanghai, toward the world, creating unlimited possibilities. This forum and this competition are not just an event, but a signal that the government hopes to leverage expert resources to empower Shanghai's industrial and talent development."

The imagination about Forward Deployed Engineers opened up by this "3E" forum and the Shanghai (Yangtze River Delta) Young and Middle-aged Engineers Innovation and Entrepreneurship Competition has only just begun.
