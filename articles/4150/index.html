AI is said to be taking away jobs… yet this role sees an 800% surge in hiring [Tech Talk]

Big tech prepares for large-scale AI deployment, racing to recruit FDEs stationed at customer sites to rebuild and optimize enterprise information networks for AI

Because artificial intelligence (AI) excels at coding, big tech companies' demand for software engineers is shrinking. Yet Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic—the three major AI companies—are doing the opposite, waging a talent war over "Forward Deployed Engineers (FDE)." The FDE is an entirely new coding role born in the AI era: these experts are stationed inside client companies' buildings, dedicated to solving AI-related problems.

AI cools demand for programmers… FDE hiring jumps 8-fold

A Google engineer. Image provided by Google YouTube screenshot

View original image

On the 12th of this month (local time), Google announced it will create a new FDE team within its cloud division. Going forward, they will be responsible for connecting Google's AI products with various enterprises. Two days before Google's announcement, OpenAI also announced the establishment of a new subsidiary called "OpenAI Deployment Company." Its staff are OpenAI's FDEs, providing other companies with conversational bot systems including ChatGPT. Anthropic, meanwhile, had already unveiled an aggressive investment plan last November, announcing it would expand its FDE headcount fivefold.

Known as forward deployed engineers or on-site engineers, FDEs are currently among the hottest roles in the IT industry. According to data from the US job platform "Indeed," from January to September last year, FDE job postings in the US surged 800% year over year. This stands in stark contrast to the trend of IT companies cutting programmer hiring amid aggressive AI investment and operational efficiency strategies.

Stationed at customer sites, building the computing environments best suited to AI

Computer network equipment in a server room. Image provided by Pixabay

View original image

An FDE is an engineer stationed directly inside a client company's building, responsible for solving client needs. Just as troops deployed near the front lines in the military are called "forward deployed forces," FDEs are personnel dispatched to the front lines of business operations. OpenAI's blog describes the FDE this way: "You must be able to work closely with customer teams on-site, identify the areas where AI can have the greatest impact, and redesign the organization's infrastructure and core workflows to be AI-ready accordingly."

The FDE role reflects the maturity of the AI industry. AI is currently moving from the research and development stage to the large-scale deployment stage. "Agent AI" will be embedded inside computer networks to automate various tasks. However, most companies' computer network environments remain unsuitable for AI: it is common for data to be disorganized, cybersecurity to be weak, or server infrastructure to be ill-suited for AI operation. FDEs go directly into client companies and "tear down and rebuild" their information systems to ensure that these AI technologies, which cost astronomical sums, can deliver maximum performance.

Palantir is the originator… but some question whether it's "just consulting"

Palantir was the first company to adopt the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) business model. Photo shows Palantir CEO Alex Karp. Image provided by Palantir

View original image

The originator of the FDE model is the US defense tech company Palantir. Because the company works with intelligence agencies and the military, its business nature requires extremely strict security measures. However, FDEs did not stop at mere security considerations—they also earned high commercial acclaim, driving Palantir's growth. Today, this business model has spread from Palantir to big tech and the entire AI industry. In South Korea, LG CNS also stated at its April earnings call that it would cultivate FDE talent through its partnership with Palantir.

However, some are skeptical about the sustainability of the FDE boom. Deploying unfamiliar IT systems according to client needs has traditionally been the main business of consulting firms and system integration (SI) providers, and questions are being raised about whether big tech companies are now taking away their jobs.

Maria Gina Kuna, a US IT consultant and author, noted in her newsletter: "An FDE is simply a consultant under a different name. AI platforms may still be unfamiliar for now, but as the technology advances further, anyone will be able to easily adopt such platforms." She added: "At that point, engineers will no longer be treated as all-purpose 'jacks-of-all-trades,' and the huge bonuses currently being thrown at them will return to normal levels."
