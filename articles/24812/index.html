Why AI PoCs Fail in Production

Original title: なぜAIのPoCは本番で失敗するのか

PoC Success, Production Failure—Why Does the "Valley of Death" Occur?

In the adoption of generative AI and LLMs, cases abound worldwide where good results are achieved at the validation stage (PoC), but progress stalls when it comes to moving into production and delivering business outcomes. The demo works, yet the solution never reaches a state where it is "continuously used in the field and improves the numbers"—this is the so-called "PoC valley of death."

The essence of the problem is not technical capability itself, but the "last one mile" that lies between the product and the customer's field. The dedicated role that bridges this gap is the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE).

Five Typical Patterns of PoC Failure at Production

Failures are almost always caused by factors other than technology. Here are the five most common ones.

1. Integration with Data and Existing Systems Is Heavier Than Expected

PoCs run on clean sample data, but in production you hit walls of authentication, permissions, data quality, and legacy system integration. Misjudging integration effort leads to indefinite postponement of production deployment.

2. It Doesn't Fit the Field's Business Workflows

Even if technically correct, a solution won't be used if it doesn't fit the field's procedures, exception handling, and existing tools. The design work of adapting it to "a form that works in the field" is often missing.

3. No Operational Design for Accuracy and Evaluation

Since LLMs behave probabilistically, without mechanisms for evaluation (Eval), monitoring, and continuous improvement, quality fluctuates in production and trust is lost. What matters is not one-time accuracy, but reproducibility in operations.

4. It Doesn't Take Hold in the Organization

Even after deployment, usage won't grow without education, onboarding, and the design of success experiences. Placing a tool in place and having it used to produce results are separate issues.

5. Builders and the Field Are Divided

Without a translator between the product development team and the customer's field, gaps in requirements persist until the end. The absence of someone who spans both sides and takes responsibility is the biggest structural cause.

How FDEs Bridge the Last One Mile

FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) dive deep into the customer's field and take end-to-end responsibility, from requirements definition through implementation, integration, evaluation design, and operational adoption. By spanning everything from pre-sales to customer success and serving as both the "builder" and the "deliverer," a single person (or an FDE team) closes the five gaps above.

Another important point is that insights gained in the field can be fed back into the product. FDEs are not merely implementation support; they can be described as a role responsible for making the product "win" in the customer's field. A concrete picture of FDEs is explained in What is an FDE, and the required skills in Skills Needed for FDEs.

How Companies Can Cross the PoC Valley of Death

The key is to bring in talent with "production operations and adoption" in mind from the PoC stage. Specifically, three points: (1) validate data integration and operational design early, (2) evaluate based on reproducibility in operations rather than accuracy, and (3) assign FDE-type talent who spans the field and the builders. If you don't have FDE talent in-house, external hiring or partnering support is a realistic option.
