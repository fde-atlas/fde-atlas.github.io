Zhidao Chuangyu Enters the Enterprise AI Agent Space: Can Cybersecurity Vendors Carve Out a Differentiated Track with Data Security and AI Offense/Defense?

Full text

2026-06-18 22:53:03

On June 17, Zhidao Chuangyu (Knownsec) launched AiPy Enterprise Edition and AiPy Cybersecurity Edition, entering the enterprise-grade AI agent arena. Its products address enterprise data security pain points through multi-layered security protection mechanisms, and it has built an FDE system to go deep into business scenarios. AI is disrupting traditional cybersecurity, and facing the AI wave, traditional cybersecurity vendors such as Qianxin and 360 are also transforming. Whether Zhidao Chuangyu can build its own unique competitiveness remains to be seen.

NBD reporter | Zheng Xinwei, NBD editor | Yu Tingting

At present, enterprise-grade AI agents are at a critical juncture of moving from proof of concept to large-scale deployment, and new players keep emerging.

On June 17, cybersecurity company Zhidao Chuangyu released two strategic products, AiPy Enterprise Edition and AiPy Cybersecurity Edition, officially announcing its entry into the enterprise AI agent arena.

According to statistics from International Data Corporation (IDC), the number of active enterprise agents in China approached 2 million in 2025; a report by Kezhi Consulting shows that the market size of enterprise-grade AI agents in China jumped from 21.2 billion yuan in 2025 to 44.9 billion yuan in 2026.

Now this veteran company, which has been deeply engaged in cybersecurity for nearly two decades, has joined the competition. It must carve out a niche in the To B AI (business-facing artificial intelligence) track where internet giants have already deployed intensively, while also competing with a host of cybersecurity vendors on AI defense capabilities. Under this dual pressure, how can it chart its own development path in the AI era?

The enterprise-grade AI agent track in 2026 is experiencing an unprecedented "arms race."

In March, Alibaba released its enterprise-grade AI-native work platform "Wukong"; ByteDance's Feishu launched several enterprise-grade agent products and simultaneously upgraded its agent platform "Feishu Aily." In the same month, Tencent launched the AI agent desktop workbench WorkBuddy Personal Edition, and three months later, WorkBuddy Enterprise Edition and the office agent suite Agent Suite were officially released.

Amid the internet giants' intensive deployment, the entry of a traditional cybersecurity company is surprising, but this is not a spur-of-the-moment decision for Zhidao Chuangyu.

At the launch event, NBD reporters learned that the agent AiPy was developed internally at Zhidao Chuangyu as early as 2023, incubated into an application in 2024, and officially open-sourced in April 2025, nearly a year before OpenClaw (an AI agent framework, commonly known as "Lobster"), which exploded in popularity earlier this year. After three years of refinement, AiPy Enterprise Edition was finally officially launched.

Yang Jilong, co-founder and CTO of Zhidao Chuangyu. Image source: provided by the company

Speaking about why a cybersecurity company would start doing AI, Yang Jilong, co-founder and CTO (Chief Technology Officer) of Zhidao Chuangyu, said in a group media interview including NBD reporters that it is simply a natural extension of two decades of accumulation. Recounting the company's development, he said that Zhidao Chuangyu, founded in 2007, was already using big data and algorithms to solve security problems back in an era when most enterprises had not even heard of AI.

"In its early days, the company did web-based drive-by malware detection. Facing over a billion web pages worldwide, it was simply impossible to do manually; we had to use algorithms and big data," Yang said.

Zhidao Chuangyu has been deepening this technical route for more than a decade. Through long-term work defending users against cyberattacks and building cloud defense systems, it has accumulated massive amounts of real-world security data assets and mature algorithmic recognition capabilities. "We also entered the big data annotation track quite by accident," Yang said. He explained that Zhidao Chuangyu began building its big data annotation business in 2013 and has continued data training collaborations with multiple enterprises. With the arrival of the large model industry wave, Zhidao Chuangyu's years of accumulation have not only secured it a position in the upstream of the industry chain but also given it a complete foundation for AI technology iteration, making its comprehensive upgrade into the AI field a natural progression.

"The biggest pain point for enterprises advancing AI applications is not that models aren't strong enough, but that they dare not use their data and cannot retain experience. No matter how powerful public cloud AI capabilities are, enterprises dare not upload their core data, trade secrets, or customer information—data security is the sword of Damocles hanging over the heads of customers in every industry." This judgment by Zhidao Chuangyu on the pain points of enterprise-grade AI agent deployment highlights the cybersecurity DNA the company has been rooted in for years.

Luo Li, deputy general manager of products and AI technology director at Zhidao Chuangyu, said that AiPy Enterprise Edition builds a full-chain AI application security governance system through multi-layered security protection mechanisms—unified control via an AI gateway, agent permission isolation, runtime behavior auditing, and system-level hardening.

When asked about the difference between AiPy Enterprise Edition and the recently popular Tencent WorkBuddy Enterprise Edition, Luo Li said that WorkBuddy is a standardized product that connects Tencent's entire ecosystem, such as WeChat, QQ, and Tencent Docs, which is its core advantage. But many problems in enterprises cannot be solved by standard products; often it requires going deep into business scenarios, custom development, and direct integration with customers' business.

This ability to "go deep into business scenarios" is precisely the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) system that Zhidao Chuangyu is working hard to build.

FDE is a new role that has emerged in the AI era. These engineers are stationed at customer sites, understanding customers' data, processes, and pain points, and polishing general models and intelligent tools into concrete solutions that can run the business. They are engineers as well as product managers, solutions architects, and even half consultants. Today, this group of people has become hotly contested by Silicon Valley AI giants such as OpenAI and Anthropic.

Domestic enterprises also face a shortage of such talent, and Zhidao Chuangyu is recruiting FDE engineers, but there are "nowhere near enough." "NIO badly needs this kind of talent," Yang analyzed. Talent with strong communication skills, high emotional intelligence, and familiarity with AI agents is more favored by enterprises. He revealed that one of the key next steps is to cultivate AiPy certified training engineers, using a certification system to enable localized FDEs to serve customers nearby.

Zhidao Chuangyu, with its cybersecurity roots, has not forgotten its old trade—released alongside AiPy Enterprise Edition is AiPy Cybersecurity Edition.

Jin Hao, vice president of Zhidao Chuangyu, who presented the product, shared a story on stage: earlier this year, the water supply system of Mexico's third-largest city, home to 5 million people, was breached by a cyberattack. The attacker was not a professional hacker, had no industrial control knowledge, and no team collaboration. Using only a commercial AI account costing $20 a month, they had the AI autonomously scan intranet assets, retrieve equipment manuals, research attack plans, write code, iterate, and fix bugs, ultimately succeeding in the attack.

Attacks that once required professional hacker organizations can now be carried out with just an AI account. AI is disrupting traditional cybersecurity.

Jin Hao, vice president of Zhidao Chuangyu. Image source: provided by the company

In April this year, Anthropic released a preview of its latest model, Claude Mythos, a model hailed in the industry as the "strongest model," which was later restricted from open access for security reasons. At the recently held BCS 2026 (the 8th Beijing Cybersecurity Conference), Qi Xiangdong, chairman of cybersecurity company Qianxin, pointed out that Mythos can independently complete vulnerability probing, weaponized code generation, and full-chain penetration, "completely freeing attackers from the traditional constraints of manpower in terms of quantity, energy, and efficiency," compressing vulnerability discovery from "weeks" down to "minutes" or even tens of seconds. A report from 360's AI Security Research Institute released around the same time also noted that the attack side is using AI to accelerate vulnerability analysis and attack chain simulation, while many enterprises still rely on manual verification and scheduled remediation—the traditional remediation pace is becoming ineffective.

"The walls of the old era can never withstand the nuclear bombs of the new era." Jin Hao described the failure crisis of traditional security defense models this way at the launch event, proposing that the only way to fight back is "using AI to counter AI." "When attackers use AI to generate attacks, our defenses must also use AI at the same level for real-time identification and response." This became the original motivation for Zhidao Chuangyu to launch AiPy Cybersecurity Edition.

Jin Hao said that Zhidao Chuangyu's development of AI security operations tools is not about riding the AI hype, nor about layering AI features on top of a traditional cybersecurity product architecture—"in that case, AI is merely an auxiliary tool, and its capabilities would be limited by the original security products." Unlike this retrofit-style development approach, Zhidao Chuangyu chose to start from AI itself, with security being just one of its application scenarios.

This line of thinking also reveals the transformation logic of traditional cybersecurity vendors amid the wave of industrial AI. In an era where AI is disrupting industries, traditional cybersecurity companies face a choice: either be reshaped by AI or be eliminated by it. Zhidao Chuangyu has chosen the former, and it is not alone on this path.

At the BCS 2026 conference, Qi An Xin proposed a "trinity" defense-in-depth system, dividing security capabilities into three coordinated layers: the low layer (fully AI-powered security products responsible for execution), the middle layer (AI agents responsible for operations and scheduling), and the high layer (large model foundation responsible for decision-making). 360 has taken an even more aggressive approach, with founder Zhou Hongyi previously declaring "All in Agent" outright, and successively releasing products such as an agent factory and security agents. According to our reporter, Zhidao Chuangyu has also launched a five-in-one trusted AI full-industry-chain layout covering AI data services, domestic computing power adaptation, AI middle-platform governance, AI security protection, and scenario-based AI applications.

Qi An Xin uses AI for collaborative security operations, while 360 uses AI to redefine the security company. Whether Zhidao Chuangyu can build unique competitiveness with its new strategic positioning as a "trusted AI partner" remains to be seen. Of course, Yang Jilong's judgment is very firm: "A new industrial revolution has arrived. Whoever fails to embrace it will be 'done for.'"
