by FDE New World

$9

FDE Decomposition Drills — Timed Practice Pack

This is not information. It is repetitions. Ten decomposition drills in the format FDE interviewers actually score: a vague customer problem lands, and what gets graded is the order of the questions you ask before you design anything.

Each drill gives you the prompt, the worked answer (the questions that score, in order), and the self-score: numbers first, real examples second, constraints third, a one-sentence definition of week-one success fourth, one thing you would refuse to build, fifth. Run each one out loud, timed at 20 minutes, cover the answer first.

One full drill, free

Sample — Drill 2: "Build us an AI search over all our documents."

Ask: (1) Who searches, how often, and what do they do with a result? (2) Show me 20 real queries and the documents people actually opened. (3) What must never surface — HR files, other clients' data, drafts? (4) Week-one success: "for 30 test questions, the right document is in the top 3 results." (5) Refuse: indexing everything on day one; start with one team's one folder.

Good looks like: you build an eval set before you build the search.

What you get

10 worked decomposition drills (PDF + Markdown): support automation, AI document search, sales reporting, missed patient appointments, onboarding, returns agents, codebase search, churn prediction, meeting summaries, and the "just connect the AI to our database" trap

The 7-Day Sprint Plan (1 page, PDF): one drill a day in the order that builds the habit fastest, plus the re-run and mock-loop days

Straight about the overlap

These are the same 10 drills that appear in the full guide's Appendix A (the guide has 15, plus the loops, compensation data, roadmap, negotiation, and portfolio projects). Buy this pack if your loop is close and all you need is timed practice. Buy the guide instead if you have two or more weeks — and if you start here, the $9 comes off the guide when you upgrade.

Who this is NOT for

Beginners who do not yet write production code — the drills assume you can scope a technical build.

Anyone looking for leaked or real interview questions. These are original practice prompts modelled on publicly described round types.

Anyone looking for a guaranteed outcome. Practice changes preparation, not decisions.
