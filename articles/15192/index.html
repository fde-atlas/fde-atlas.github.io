Evals for FDEs: LLM, RAG and Agent Evaluation Guide

FDE Interviews · 2026-11-11 · 7 min read

Summary

FDEInterviews.com has published a 74-page, 32-chapter field guide teaching forward deployed engineers how to build trustworthy evaluations for LLMs, RAG systems and agents. It follows one fictional support-and-refund workflow through six parts: defining acceptance criteria and datasets, validating LLM judges, measuring repeated-run reliability, testing agent behavior, gating releases, and production monitoring. A key lesson is that high agreement does not imply failure detection: a judge with 94% agreement still misses four of ten real failures, so confusion matrices matter. The package includes a Python/SQLite companion (44 tests, 8 mutation checks), eight editable worksheets, and ten decision clinics.

Key points

  • 74-page, 32-chapter guide covering LLM, RAG and agent evals.
  • A 94%-agreement judge still misses 4 of 10 real failures; read confusion matrices.
  • Distinguishes pass@k from pass^k and repeated-run reliability.
  • Release gates must keep hard constraints, invalid trials and paired evidence visible.
  • Includes a tested Python/SQLite companion and 8 editable worksheets.

  • AI evaluation
  • A repeatable procedure for judging a defined AI system under stated conditions, using specified inputs, evidence, grading and a decision rule.
  • LLM-as-a-judge
  • A model used to apply a written evaluation criterion to supplied evidence. Its error rates and decision coverage must be validated against reviewed labels.
  • Agent evaluation
  • An assessment of an agent's outcomes, action history and operating constraints, including authoritative state and attempted behavior.
  • pass@k and pass^k
  • At-least-one success and all-run success across k attempts, respectively. They answer different reliability questions and require explicit sampling assumptions.
  • Evaluation release gate
  • A predeclared decision rule that combines evidence validity, hard constraints, quality comparisons and operating limits before a change reaches users.

A repeatable procedure for judging a defined AI system under stated conditions, using specified inputs, evidence, grading and a decision rule. They can diagnose failures, detect regressions, compare capabilities or support a release decision. Those uses need different case mixes and evidence.

No. Its candidates are controlled scripts so the measurement behavior is reproducible. It does not demonstrate production authentication, a live judge, a durable handoff queue or cloud deployment. The guide explains the additional adapters and evidence needed for those uses.

It defines narrow rubrics, protects evidence boundaries, measures pass and failure recall separately, retains unknown outcomes and checks position bias. Overall agreement is never treated as proof that a judge catches costly failures.
