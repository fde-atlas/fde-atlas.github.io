FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) overlaps significantly with customer-facing technical roles at product companies, such as Solution Architect (SA), Solution Engineer, Customer Engineer, and Applied AI Engineer. The differences can be summarized in three points: "whether they are involved before or after the sales deal," "whether they own production code in the customer's environment themselves," and "whether they carry a sales quota." The difference from AI Engineers and ML Engineers is whether they build models or help customers use existing models.

This article compares the differences between FDEs and five adjacent roles in comparison tables, based on job postings published by OpenAI, Anthropic, Databricks, Cloudflare, Google Cloud, and Snowflake as of October 2026, as well as articles from The Pragmatic Engineer and The New Stack. It also covers how to read the role titles in job postings, what to add when moving to an FDE role from each adjacent role, and differences in salary ranges listed in public job postings. Differences from SES, SIer, SE, and IT consultants are covered in a separate article.

Key Takeaways of This Article

  • The biggest differences between FDEs and Solution Architects are the timing of involvement and the depth of implementation. Colin Jarvis of OpenAI explains that Solution Architects are closer to advisors and rarely write code on customer infrastructure, while FDEs write code directly on customer infrastructure.
  • Solution Engineers and Customer Engineers are roles responsible for pre-sale technical proposals. Cloudflare's job posting for Customer Engineers in Japan defines the role as "a technical professional with a quota (sales target)" and includes commission in compensation. In contrast, FDE job postings contain no mention of sales targets, and Databricks' FDE job posting states that the role is "billable."
  • The line between Applied AI Engineer and FDE varies by company. Anthropic's Tokyo job posting for Applied AI Engineer defines the role as a technical advisor spanning pre- and post-sale, while OpenAI's Tokyo job posting for Applied AI Engineer defines it as a role that builds production systems with their own code, just like FDEs.
  • While AI Engineers and ML Engineers build models and training infrastructure, FDEs help customers use existing models in their business operations. What job postings require is not training or research experience, but experience implementing production systems involving customer interaction.
  • In public job postings as of October 2026, Anthropic London's base salary ranges are the same for FDE and Applied AI Engineer (£225,000–£255,000), higher than the pre-sales Applied AI Architect (£165,000–£190,000), and Databricks places FDE and Solution Architect at the same level.

Differences Between FDE and Solution Architect (SA)

The clearest explanation of the difference between FDEs and Solution Architects comes from Colin Jarvis, who leads the FDE organization at OpenAI. In an interview with The Pragmatic Engineer (August 12, 2025, by Gergely Orosz), Jarvis's team distinguishes the two roles as follows.

"A Solutions Architect plays a role closer to advisory, rarely writing code on a customer's infrastructure, and typically builds MVPs (minimum viable products) or PoCs (proofs of concept) using anonymized or offline data. In contrast, an FDE writes code directly on the customer's infrastructure, using the customer's tools to do so. FDEs are more hands-on and deal with more ambiguous situations than traditional cloud Solutions Architects."

Timing of involvement: before or after the deal

The Solutions Architect is the pre-sales point of contact for technical matters. Databricks' job posting for "Solutions Architect (Pre-sales)" in Tokyo (as of October 2026) explicitly labels the role as pre-sales in the job title, and states that the person will partner with Account Executives to design how to engage with customers, lead customer engagement from discovery through implementation planning, and create demos, prototypes, reference architectures, and migration plans. The US Solutions Architect job posting lists experience handling global enterprise customers with over $1 million in annual contract value as a requirement, and describes the role as "the primary technical owner of Databricks for multiple customers."

The FDE is the main driver of post-sale implementation. Databricks' Tokyo FDE job posting lists as responsibilities "building and productionizing" solutions to data and AI challenges together with customers, and "embedding within customer teams and engaging with stakeholders from frontline engineers to executives." The US FDE job posting includes the sentence "FDEs are billable and know how to complete projects to spec," showing that FDE work is tied to customer billing—essentially a position close to professional services.

OpenAI's "AI Architect" is "the CTO of your assigned customer portfolio"

OpenAI's job posting for "Applied AI Architect" in Tokyo (published May 2026) shows how broad the SA role can be. This position serves as "the CTO of your assigned customer portfolio," owning the technical strategy from pre-sales discovery and evaluation through implementation, adoption, and outcome measurement. However, commercial strategy is owned by the Account Director, and implementation execution is explicitly stated as "once an engagement begins, the delivery team owns implementation execution." The required background spans solution architecture, solution engineering, technical account leadership, AI deployment, and technical customer success.

OpenAI's Tokyo "Forward Deployed Engineer" job posting (published September 2025) defines the FDE as a role that "works alongside the most strategic customers to lead complex, end-to-end production deployments of frontier models," owning discovery, technical scoping, system design, building, and production deployment themselves. The measure of success is "production adoption, measurable business impact, and evaluation-driven feedback that changes the product and model roadmap." The departments also differ: FDEs belong to "Forward Deployed Engineering," while Applied AI Architects belong to "Go To Market."

In short, the SA decides "what to build and how to build it" together with the customer's technical leadership, while the FDE's role is to "build it until it actually runs in the customer's environment." The two split responsibility for the same customer before and after the deal, and at OpenAI, the AI Architect brings in Deployment Engineering as needed.

"Delivery Solutions Architect" is close to an FDE

Even with the SA title, some roles do work closer to that of an FDE. Databricks' Tokyo job posting for "Delivery Solutions Architect" states that the role owns technical execution "from technical win through production and adoption," building working solutions, prototypes, and reusable technical assets. It requires 5+ years of experience and up to 30% travel. Unlike pre-sales SAs, the focus is on post-sale execution. Don't assume a role is pre-sales just because it's titled SA—check the "timing of involvement" in the job posting.

The difference between FDEs and Solutions Engineers / pre-sales

The Solutions Engineer (also called Sales Engineer or Pre-sales Engineer) works alongside sales to handle pre-sales technical evaluation. The Pragmatic Engineer explains that "Agent Engineers," "Solutions Architects," "Sales Engineers," and "Technical Delivery Engineers" are all quite close to FDE work, and that what distinguishes FDEs is that they carry both "working with customers" and "contributing to the development of their own company's core product."

Google Cloud's Tokyo job posting for "AI Customer Engineer, AI Platform" (as of October 2026) describes itself at the top as a "Sales Engineer," and lists responsibilities including "identifying business opportunities and evaluating prospects, identifying customer technical objections, creating demos and presentations, and developing strategies to overcome technical obstacles," "owning the technical side of Go-to-Market strategy," and "creating technical sales assets to support frontline pre-sales engineers." The required experience is 6 years of cloud-native architecture experience in a customer-facing or technical support role.

Summarizing the differences from FDEs, there are three key points:

  • Milestones of deliverables: For Solutions Engineers, the work reaches a natural endpoint at the deal close (the technical win). For FDEs, the job extends until the system runs in production and is actually used in the business—OpenAI's FDE job posting lists success measures as "production adoption" and "measurable business impact."
  • Data and environment: Solutions Engineers' demos and PoCs are often built with anonymized or offline data, as Mr. Jarvis explained. FDEs build within the constraints of the customer's production data and production environment.
  • Number of customers: Solutions Engineers look after multiple customers within the sales territory in parallel. FDEs go deep into a small number of strategic customers—Databricks' Tokyo FDE job posting anticipates about 20% travel, and Anthropic's London posting 25–50%.

The New Stack (May 28, 2026) also explains that FDE success is measured not by hours spent, but by how much the system is used and how well it works. This is what sets FDEs apart from roles whose work ends at the deal close or at go-live.

Note that this does not mean solution engineers lack implementation skills. Databricks' Tokyo pre-sales SA job posting includes a "Vibe Coding Interview" and a "Build, Demo, Pitch!" interview in its selection process, requiring the ability to build prototypes yourself. The difference is not whether one has implementation skills, but whether one continues to own what was built in the customer's production environment.

FDE vs. Customer Engineer (CE)

Customer Engineer is a job title used at infrastructure and platform companies such as Google Cloud and Cloudflare. It is the role most easily confused with FDE, but reading the job postings reveals a clear difference: whether or not there is a sales quota.

Cloudflare's definition: "a technical person with a quota"

Cloudflare's job posting for "Senior Customer Engineer, Japan" in Tokyo (as of October 2026) defines the Customer Engineer as "a trusted advisor who owns the technical relationship from initial discovery through long-term adoption and ongoing account expansion, serving as the core of the technical Go-to-Market strategy." The role sits in the GTM (sales and marketing) organization, and the posting explicitly states that the engineer "does not hand off the customer after initial agreement, working as a technical person with a quota."

  • Commercial responsibility: "carries a variable quota tied directly to new customer acquisition and/or growth of existing accounts." Identifies cross-sell and upsell opportunities based on product usage data, and leads quarterly business reviews (QBRs) for executive audiences
  • Technical validation: Partners with Account Executives to lead technical discovery, design architectures, and deliver demos and PoCs
  • Compensation: "a competitive base salary plus variable commission tied directly to achieving full-lifecycle commercial goals"
  • Desired experience: Experience in customer-facing technical roles such as solution engineering, customer engineering, sales engineering, or FDE. Coding (Python, JavaScript) is a "strongly preferred" requirement

Cloudflare's job posting includes former FDEs among its target applicants. In other words, companies themselves view FDE and Customer Engineer as adjacent roles. The difference is that Customer Engineers carry responsibility for revenue, and their compensation includes commission.

FDE job postings have no sales targets

By contrast, the FDE job postings we reviewed from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Databricks contain no mention of quotas or commissions. Anthropic's London posting describes the FDE as a role that "works closely with post-sales, product, and engineering teams," and Databricks, as noted above, describes it as "billable." FDEs are evaluated not on sales, but on whether the customer's production system actually runs and is used.

That said, some companies place commercial roles within their FDE organizations. Snowflake's job posting for "AI Deployment Strategist" in Tokyo (published September 2026) defines this role as one that "sits within the FDE product organization," turns executive conversations into actual engagements, writes statements of work (SOWs), and screens incoming deals from the sales organization for FDE suitability. The division of labor with Account Executives is described as: "You own the scope of the engagement, product pricing, and delivery commitments, while they own the primary commercial relationship." The posting also states "we don't do pilots that don't ship," showing that the FDE organization is structured on the assumption of taking things all the way to production.

Anthropic's Tokyo job postings, as of October 2026, do not include a role called FDE; their customer-facing technical roles are two: "Applied AI Engineer" and the presales "Applied AI Architect." The Applied AI Architect posting explicitly describes itself as a "presales architect" and asks for experience as a solution architect or sales engineer. Applied AI Engineer is a role that spans from pre-sales evaluation to post-deployment advisory, through designing evaluation sets and pilots and advising on architecture. The FDE roles Anthropic recruits for in the US and Europe are defined in their postings as roles that "build production applications using Claude models within the customer's systems" and "deliver technical artifacts such as MCP servers, subagents, and agent skills for production workflows"; within the company, FDEs go further into implementation ownership than Applied AI Engineers.

At OpenAI, both Applied AI Engineers and FDEs write production code

What makes this confusing is that even the same "Applied AI Engineer" title has a different definition at OpenAI. OpenAI's Tokyo "Applied AI Engineer" posting (published March 2026) says you will "write code, debug, build evaluation systems, and solve complex integrations" and that "success is measured not by successful demos or activity volume, but by production systems, sustained adoption, and meaningful impact for customers"—essentially the same definition as FDE. The requirements also state that you must "demonstrate substantial individual contribution in code, architecture, evaluation, debugging, and production engineering, not just program or stakeholder management." The FDE role belongs to "Forward Deployed Engineering" while Applied AI Engineer belongs to "Go To Market," suggesting the difference lies in the department and the size of customers (FDEs handle "the most strategic customers").

Since the same job title means different things at different companies, when reading job postings, don't rely on the title—check these three points: "whether writing code is part of the responsibilities," "whether success is measured by production adoption or by winning deals," and "whether the role belongs to a sales organization or an implementation organization."

AI Engineer / ML Engineer: those who build models and those who enable their use

AI Engineers and ML Engineers (machine learning engineers) build the substance of the product, such as model training, evaluation, and inference infrastructure. FDEs make finished models usable in customers' business operations, and experience in model training or research is not a mandatory requirement in their postings. What OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting asks for is engineering experience including customer-facing work, production-quality full-stack code, and experience building systems using LLMs; Anthropic's London and US FDE postings likewise ask for "production experience with LLMs, including advanced prompt engineering, agent development, evaluation frameworks, and large-scale deployments."

There are also roles in between. Anthropic's US "Applied AI, Research Engineer" posting defines the role as one that tests new models early to understand their strengths and limitations, creates demos and evaluations, prepares field teams, and gets hands-on when strategic customers face new technical challenges. The posting itself describes it as a "connective tissue" organization linking the research side and the customer side, and lists 6+ years of experience and a salary of $300,000–$400,000.

Some companies, like Databricks, further subdivide FDE roles. The Tokyo "Senior AI Engineer - FDE" posting describes the AI FDE team as one that "provides professional services engagements helping customers build and productionize first-of-their-kind AI applications," and asks for experience with RAG, multi-agent systems, Text2SQL, and fine-tuning, plus years of hands-on data science experience. Meanwhile, the company's Tokyo "Forward Deployed Engineer" posting asks for deep experience with distributed processing using Apache Spark and 8+ years of data engineering experience—so even within FDE, expectations differ between data-infrastructure-oriented and AI-application-oriented roles.

There are four things to check when reading a job posting. First, does the Responsibilities section mention "writing code," "running things in production," or "building within the customer's systems"? Second, is success measured by production adoption and business impact, or by deals won and revenue? Third, is the role part of a sales (GTM/Sales) organization or an implementation (FDE/Deployment/Professional Services) organization? Fourth, does the compensation section mention commission or OTE (on-target earnings)? With these four points, you can determine whether a posting is for an FDE or for an SA, Solutions Engineer, or Customer Engineer, regardless of the job title.

What to add when moving to FDE from each role

Each company's job postings explicitly include experience in adjacent roles among FDE application requirements. Anthropic's Tokyo Applied AI Engineer posting lists FDE experience, Cloudflare's Customer Engineer posting lists FDE experience, and OpenAI's Tokyo Applied AI Engineer posting lists experience in FDE, customer engineering, solutions architecture, and technical consulting as application requirements. The reverse path—moving from an adjacent role to FDE—is realistic for the same reason. For each role, here is what to add, checked against the posting requirements.

From Solutions Architect / Solutions Engineer

  • What to add: Experience writing and continuously running code in customers' production environments. Because SAs' PoCs are often built with anonymized or offline data (per Mr. Jarvis), build a track record of handling production data, production permissions, monitoring, and incident response
  • What to add: Production experience with LLM-based systems. Both OpenAI's and Anthropic's FDE postings list experience building and deploying LLM-based systems as a requirement
  • What you can leverage: Experience with discovery and scoping. OpenAI's FDE posting places "discovery, technical scoping" at the top of FDE responsibilities, so an SA's strengths can be used as-is

From Customer Engineer

  • What to add: Ownership of post-sale implementation. Customer Engineers often stop at PoCs and integration scripts (in Cloudflare's posting, coding is a "preferred" requirement), so you need a track record of building production systems end to end
  • What to add: Designing evaluations (evals). OpenAI's FDE posting lists "feedback based on evaluations" as a measure of success, and Anthropic asks for experience with "evaluation frameworks"
  • What you can leverage: Owning customer relationships from deal-making through expansion, and conversations with executives. There is also a path to the commercial side of an FDE organization (Deployment Strategist), as at Snowflake

From Applied AI Engineer

  • For the Anthropic type (advisory-leaning), what to add: Experience building and owning production applications within customers' systems. The company's FDE posting lists delivering artifacts such as MCP servers, subagents, and agent skills among its responsibilities
  • For the OpenAI type (implementation-leaning): Since the definition is nearly identical to FDE, what to add is experience owning multiple deployments in parallel on engagements at the scale of "the most strategic customers"
  • What you can leverage: Production LLM experience and evaluation set design. This matches the core technical requirements of FDE postings

From AI Engineer / ML Engineer

  • What to add: Customer-facing experience. OpenAI's FDE posting requires 5+ years of experience "including customer-facing work," and Anthropic requires 4+ years in "technical roles involving customer interaction." You need a track record of handling external customers' discovery and scoping, not internal stakeholder management
  • What to add: Full-stack production code. OpenAI requires "writing and reviewing production-quality code in both frontend and backend," so model training alone is not enough
  • What you can leverage: Understanding model behavior and designing evaluations. OpenAI's FDE posting lists "understanding how model behavior affects the product experience" as a requirement

What is asked of everyone, regardless of prior role, is: "Have you owned your own code in a customer's production environment until it was actually used?" The next article explains how to run your job search and write your resume.

Summary

The differences between FDEs and Solutions Architects, Solutions Engineers, and Customer Engineers come down to three points: when they are involved (before or after the sales deal), whether they own production code in the customer's environment, and whether they carry sales targets. SAs and Solutions Engineers handle pre-sale technical proposals and PoCs, Customer Engineers additionally carry quotas and expansion responsibilities, and FDEs build production systems in the customer's environment after the deal closes and are evaluated on production adoption. Applied AI Engineer can be synonymous with FDE at some companies (OpenAI) or more advisory-focused at others (Anthropic). AI Engineers and ML Engineers build the models, while FDEs get the models used by customers.

When reading job postings, don't judge by the job title alone; check four things: whether the responsibilities include code and production, whether success is measured by production adoption or deal closure, whether the role belongs to a sales or implementation organization, and whether the compensation mentions commission or OTE. When moving to an FDE role from an adjacent role, the common additions are experience owning your own code until it is used in a customer's production environment, and production experience building systems using LLMs.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the difference between an FDE and a Solutions Architect?

It comes down to when they are involved and how deep the implementation goes. Colin Jarvis, who leads the FDE organization at OpenAI, explains that Solutions Architects play a role closer to advisory, rarely write code on the customer's infrastructure, and build MVPs or PoCs using anonymized or offline data. FDEs write code directly on the customer's infrastructure and take it to production using the customer's tools. Databricks' job postings also distinguish the two: Solutions Architects handle pre-sale work, while FDEs handle post-sale building and productionization.

Q: How does an FDE differ from a Solutions Engineer (pre-sales)?

Solutions Engineers work alongside sales to handle pre-sale technical evaluations, demos, and PoCs, and their work concludes with the deal closing. FDEs are responsible until the system runs in production and is used in actual business operations; OpenAI's FDE job posting states that success is measured by production adoption and measurable business impact. While Solutions Engineers handle multiple customers in parallel, FDEs go deep with a small number of strategic customers.

Q: What is the difference between an FDE and a Customer Engineer?

It is the presence or absence of sales targets (quotas). Cloudflare's Tokyo Customer Engineer job posting defines the role as a "quota-carrying technologist" and explicitly mentions variable quotas and commission tied to new customer acquisition and account growth. In contrast, the FDE postings from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Databricks contain no mention of quotas or commission, and Databricks describes the role as "billable." Cloudflare's posting includes candidates with FDE experience among its targets, showing that companies view the two as adjacent roles.

Q: Is an Applied AI Engineer the same as an FDE?

It depends on the company. OpenAI's Tokyo Applied AI Engineer posting defines the role as one that writes code, solves evaluation and integration problems, and measures success in production systems—essentially the same as an FDE. Anthropic's Tokyo Applied AI Engineer posting defines the role as a technical advisor to customers evaluating Claude, designing pilots and evaluation sets, which is more advisory-focused than the company's overseas FDE posting (building production applications within the customer's systems).

Q: What is the difference between an FDE and an AI Engineer or ML Engineer?

It is whether you build the models or get them used. AI Engineers and ML Engineers build the internals of the product, such as model training, evaluation, and inference infrastructure. FDEs make finished models usable in customers' business operations; the job postings seek not model training experience, but engineering experience including customer-facing work, and production implementation experience with systems using LLMs.

Q: Can you transition to an FDE role from a Solutions Architect or Customer Engineer position?

Yes, you can. OpenAI's Tokyo Applied AI Engineer job posting lists solution architecture and customer engineering experience among its requirements, and companies view these roles as adjacent to FDE. What you would need to add is experience writing code in customers' production environments and owning it until it's actually used, plus production experience with LLM-based systems. Your experience with discovery and scoping, as well as engaging with executives, can be leveraged as-is.

Which pays more, QFDE or Solution Architect?

It depends on the company. In public job postings as of October 2026, Anthropic's London FDE and Applied AI Engineer roles pay the same at £225,000–£255,000, while the pre-sales Applied AI Architect role is lower at £165,000–£190,000. At Databricks in the US, a Senior FDE earns $182,000–$250,208 and a Solution Architect $180,000–$247,500, roughly the same level. Roles with sales targets, such as Customer Engineer, are structured with variable commission on top of base salary.
