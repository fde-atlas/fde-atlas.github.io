Anthropic Forward Deployed Engineer: The Role and How to Prepare for the Interview

Prepare.sh · 2026-11-04 · 5 min read

Summary

This guide explains Anthropic's forward deployed engineer role, which embeds with strategic customers to ship production AI systems on Claude, including MCP servers, subagents, agent skills and evals. Since Anthropic publishes no FDE-specific interview loop, the author builds preparation advice from job postings, application questions and Anthropic's AI usage rules, plus original practice questions. Postings span London, Munich and US Staff roles, with pay from £225,000–£255,000 (London) to $280,000–$320,000 (Staff) and 25–50% travel. The 'Why Anthropic?' application answer is heavily weighted, and AI assistance rules differ by stage: allowed for refining drafts and interview prep, banned for take-homes and live interviews.

Key points

  • FDEs deliver production artifacts for customers: MCP servers, subagents, agent skills and evals.
  • London requires 4+ years' experience and 25–50% travel; Staff roles pay $280,000–$320,000.
  • The 200–400 word 'Why Anthropic?' answer is explicitly highly valued.
  • AI rules vary by stage: Claude allowed for drafts and prep, banned in take-homes and live interviews.
  • Practice areas include discovery conversations, MCP server design, evals and practical coding.

Anthropic's forward deployed engineers (FDEs) embed with large customers and build production AI systems on Claude. Its London posting says the role "embeds directly with our most strategic customers". On November 4, 2026, its board listed 9 titles with Forward Deployed or FDE in them: 4 for engineers, 4 for managers and 1 in strategy and operations.

Anthropic doesn't publish an FDE-specific interview loop, and we found no first-hand report we could read. So this guide covers what's documented, namely the postings, the application questions and the company's AI rules, plus practice built from those.

What you'd build

The London posting is specific about the output: "Deliver technical artifacts for customers like MCP servers, sub-agents, and agent skills that will be used in production workflows."

You should know what these are before the first call:

  • MCP is, per its docs, "an open-source standard for connecting AI applications to external systems."
  • Subagents, in Claude Code's docs, "are specialized AI assistants that handle specific types of tasks."
  • Agent Skills, in Anthropic's docs, "are modular capabilities that extend Claude's functionality." Our Claude Code skills guide builds one step by step.
  • Evals, in Anthropic's words: "An evaluation ('eval') is a test for an AI system: give an AI an input, then apply grading logic to its output to measure success."

The London posting's application form asks "Do you have 8+ years of experience", which doesn't match the 4+ in its description. If that matters to you, check with the recruiter.

The Staff posting is worth reading closely, since it says what seniority means here: "you can walk into a discovery conversation with only a business problem and leave with a defensible technical strategy". You'd also "Review designs and unblock other FDEs on hard technical calls."

The US Staff posting is more open on agent experience than the London one, so don't assume one posting describes them all.

The application is part of the interview

All three postings ask: "Why do you want to work at Anthropic? (We value this response highly - great answers are often 200-400 words.)" Treat this seriously. A specific answer about the work beats general enthusiasm for AI.

London and Munich also ask whether you've "built or deployed an application using LLMs in a enterprise production environment", and London asks if you've "been embedded onsite with an enterprise customer" while doing so. If yes, be ready to talk through it. If no, know what your closest real experience is.

The AI rules

Anthropic publishes its guidance on using AI when applying, and you should read it, since it differs by stage:

Stage Guidance
Application "Please create your first draft yourself, then use Claude to refine it."
Take-home "Complete these without Claude unless we indicate otherwise."
Preparing for interviews "Use Claude to research Anthropic, practice your answers, and prepare questions for us."
Live interview No AI assistance unless they tell you otherwise. "We're curious to see how you think through problems in real time."

The guidance is dated July 2025, and the application form asks you to confirm you've read the AI policy, so check for updates. The Staff posting also values "everyday use of AI coding tools with the judgment to review and steer", so being unfamiliar with these tools isn't an advantage either, even though interviews limit them.

The careers page adds: "All interviews are conducted over Google Meet", "we use live coding tools like Colab and CodeSignal. You can look things up", and "We're not able to provide feedback on resumes or interviews." Our Anthropic interview guide covers the general process and values round.

Practice questions

We wrote these from the postings. They're practice, not reported questions.

"A bank's operations team says they want 'an agent'. You have an hour with them. What do you do?"

This is the Staff posting's discovery conversation. A strong answer treats "agent" as a guess at a solution, and looks for the problem underneath.

  • Find the work. "Walk me through the last time this went wrong. Who did what, in which systems, and how long did it take?"
  • Find the cost. Volume, time per case, cost of a mistake. Is an error annoying or a regulatory problem?
  • Find the constraints. Data access, audit needs, who signs off.
  • Challenge the shape. Anthropic's guidance on building agents separates fully autonomous agents from simpler workflows. If the steps are fixed, a workflow with a model at one or two points may be better. Saying "you may not need an agent" is a strong signal.
  • Leave with a plan. "Here's what I'd build first, what I'd measure, and what I need from you."

Follow-ups to expect: What if they insist on full autonomy? What would you not automate? How do you handle a wrong action that's already been taken?

"Design an MCP server for this customer's internal ticketing system."

Cover these points:

  • Tools to expose. Start read-only: search tickets, read one ticket. Add writes later, with confirmation.
  • Tool design. Clear names and descriptions, since a model chooses tools from them, and small, well-scoped results.
  • Identity. Whose permissions does this run under? Acting as the user is different from a shared service account, and a customer's security team will ask.
  • Untrusted content. Ticket text can contain instructions. Treat it as data, and keep sensitive actions behind a separate check.
  • Audit. Log every call and who it was for.

"How do you know this agent works?"

Use the eval definition above. Build a test set from real cases with graded outcomes. Grade the end result and the path it took, including whether it called the right tools. Run it on every change, and read failures by hand. Agree on a threshold for launch with the customer before you see results, and keep a human reviewing until you have evidence to reduce that.

"Why Anthropic?"

The application question tells you this matters. Be specific about something in its published work that changed how you think or build, and connect it to customer deployments. Anthropic's careers page lists its values, so read those and don't recite them back.

Coding

Prepare practical Python or TypeScript. Our Anthropic question hub has hands-on practice, such as counting log levels in a log file. The AI engineer questions cover LLM APIs, RAG and evals.

How to stand out

Build an MCP server and an eval for a real problem, and be able to explain what broke. Bring a story of working directly with a customer. For Staff, show that you've made a technical call that others followed. And write the 200 to 400 words yourself first, as the guidance asks.
