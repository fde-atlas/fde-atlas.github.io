Forward Deployed AI Engineering Glossary

Nasser Ghanemzadeh · 2026-10-14 · 2 min read

Summary

This article is a glossary of forward deployed AI engineering terms. It defines the FDE as an engineer embedded in a customer's team who ships AI directly into their codebase, a model originating at Palantir, and notes that OpenAI and Anthropic each launched companies for this work in May 2026. It explains Palantir's two-role model (Echo for domain expertise and customer relationships, Delta for shipping code like a startup CTO) and Eupraxic's delivery model: a ten-business-day, fixed-price FDE Sprint preceded by a $500 Discovery Day, with deliverables including a Sprint Brief, an output quality rubric, and a workflow playbook. It also covers MCP, Claude Code, AI-native products, and Vectig, the author's own product used to test workflow patterns.

Key points

  • FDEs embed in customer teams and ship AI into their codebase, a model from Palantir.
  • Palantir splits the role: Echo owns domain expertise, Delta ships code like a startup CTO.
  • Eupraxic's FDE Sprint delivers one AI workflow in ten business days at a fixed price.
  • A $500 Discovery Day produces a go/no-go memo, credited toward a Sprint if the customer proceeds.
  • MCP and Claude Code are key tools for connecting AI workflows to customer systems.

Full text

  • Forward Deployed AI Engineer (FDE)
  • An engineer who embeds inside a customer's team and ships AI capabilities directly into the customer's product and codebase, rather than delivering a deck, a dashboard, or a demo. The delivery model comes from Palantir. In May 2026, OpenAI and Anthropic each launched a new company to do this work for their customers. See the full explainer: what is a Forward Deployed Engineer?
  • FDE as a service
  • Buying a forward deployed engineer's time or output from an outside provider instead of hiring one. Sold by model and platform vendors' deployment arms, consultancies, talent marketplaces, and independent engineers, usually by the hour or the month. See the FDE-as-a-service market map for 34 providers and their published prices.
  • Echo (Palantir)
  • At Palantir, the embedded analyst with deep domain expertise who owns the customer relationship, one half of its two-role FDE model. In Forward Deployed AI Engineering, Echo also names one half of the craft: the deployment that is working now.
  • Delta (Palantir)
  • At Palantir, the deployed engineer who ships code inside the customer's environment; the company describes the responsibilities as similar to a startup CTO's. In Forward Deployed AI Engineering, Delta also names the other half of the craft: the change that makes the next deployment work better.
  • FDE Sprint
  • Eupraxic's ten-business-day engagement in which a forward deployed engineer (FDE) ships one narrow AI workflow into a customer's product. Fixed scope, fixed price, end-to-end delivery and handoff. See the FDE Sprint.
  • Discovery Day
  • A two-hour paid working session, priced at $500, to assess whether an AI use case is useful, buildable, and worth a Sprint. The output is a go/no-go memo written on the call, credited toward a Sprint if the customer continues. See Discovery Day.
  • Workflow
  • A specific process a user runs, with a clear input and a clear output, that produces a measurable improvement. It is the unit of work shipped in a Sprint. Not a feature, and not a platform.
  • Output Quality Rubric
  • A practical checklist, usually on a 1-to-5 scale, that defines what good output from an AI workflow looks like, how the customer's team reviews it, and how the workflow improves over time. It ships as a deliverable with every Sprint.
  • Sprint Brief
  • The one-to-two-page document written on Day 1 of a Sprint and signed off by the customer's decision-maker, defining scope, success criteria, deliverables, and what is explicitly out of scope. It is part of the contract.
  • Workflow Playbook
  • A short handoff document covering a workflow's purpose, inputs, expected outputs, review steps, edge cases, and maintenance guidance, so the customer's team can run and improve it without the engineer.
  • Model Context Protocol (MCP)
  • The open standard Anthropic introduced for letting AI models talk to external systems and data. Many AI workflows connect to a customer's tools and data through MCP servers.
  • Claude Code
  • Anthropic's command-line tool for agentic coding. A primary delivery tool for shipping AI workflows directly into a codebase.
  • AI-native product
  • A product built around AI workflows from the start, rather than a traditional product with an AI feature added on later.
  • Vectig
  • Nasser Ghanemzadeh's AI-native startup finance product: cash-flow forecasting, runway scenarios, and investor update drafting. It serves as the live R&D lab where workflow patterns are proven before they ship to customers. See vectig.com.
