Today's Judgment

Today's three signals, spanning organizational, adoption, and career dimensions, together depict a "responsibility upgrade" for the FDE role:

Organizational dimension—Shanghai's State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission (SASAC) summarized AI advancement at the enterprises it supervises as "four transitions": from key pilots to full coverage, from compute supply to real-world scenario impact, from Q&A assistance to intelligent execution, and from fragmented efforts to ecosystem collaboration; Adoption dimension—OpenAI disclosed that every 1 human workday in its research organization now corresponds to 3.1 agent workdays, giving organization-level AI agent adoption a new unit of measurement; Career dimension—EY posted a Forward Deployed AI Engineer Manager role, and AWS's senior FDE job page defines the responsibilities as "end-to-end ownership from prototype to enterprise-grade production operations"—the FDE has formally entered the talent models of major professional services firms and cloud providers.

Key Signals

Shanghai's state-owned assets system's "four transitions": an organization-level roadmap for AI advancement in state-owned enterprises

  • Source: Economic Daily | Original link
  • On September 5, Shanghai SASAC launched an AI-focused training seminar, with 6 training days scheduled for September–October. More valuable is its summary of AI advancement at supervised enterprises—the four transitions: from key pilots to full coverage; from a focus on compute supply to balancing networking, technical breakthroughs, and real-world scenario impact; from Q&A assistance to intelligent execution; and from fragmented efforts to ecosystem collaboration.
  • Why it matters: These four transitions can be directly converted into a diagnostic framework for state-owned enterprise clients: coverage answers "who is using it," scenario impact answers "whether it creates results," intelligent execution answers "whether it can enter workflows," and ecosystem collaboration answers "how data, models, scenarios, and talent are delivered together". Score each of the four items with verifiable evidence attached, and you have a ready-made AI maturity assessment for state-owned enterprises. Note: "full coverage of supervised enterprises" is a coverage metric, not proof that all employees are deeply using AI or that business results have been produced.

OpenAI: every 1 human workday in the research organization corresponds to 3.1 agent workdays

  • Source: OpenAI official | Original link
  • On September 6, OpenAI released an internal research acceleration report: it has reached the "automated research intern" goal—systems can complete research tasks that would originally take skilled researchers several days, under human guidance. The organizational adoption figure: as of mid-August, converted into 8-hour workdays, every 1 human workday in the research organization corresponds to 3.1 agent workdays; the median researcher consumes over $600 per day in inference resources at API prices. OpenAI also emphasized: humans remain responsible for research priorities, judging and scaling results, and decisions to pause or deploy.
  • Why it matters: "Agent workdays per human workday" is an organization-level measurement unit worth borrowing—it is closer to "how much work AI is taking on" than call counts. But a complete scoreboard should also record: concurrent agent hours, human decision points, task success rates, experiment output, costs, safety pauses, and substitution flows. Equally important is OpenAI's candor: it explicitly stated that "the relationship between increased code and experiments and overall research progress remains hard to explain"—measuring input is easy; attributing output is hard—a reminder for every company's AI ROI reporting. Note: 3.1 is a runtime conversion, not equivalent to headcount replacement or productivity.

EY and AWS successively define FDE roles: end-to-end responsibility from prototype to production operations

  • Sources: EY Careers; Amazon Jobs
  • On September 4, EY posted a Forward Deployed AI Engineer Manager role: it requires embedding with clients, compressing ambiguous problems into workflows, constraints, success metrics, and incentives; jointly defining MVPs, data, and evals; and being responsible for deployment, monitoring, knowledge transfer, and reusable assets. AWS's current senior FDE job page defines the responsibilities as: embedding with strategic clients, end-to-end ownership from prototype to enterprise-grade production operations, establishing evals, observability, incident response, and production-readiness standards, and accumulating architectures and accelerators.
  • Why it matters: Big Four accounting firms and leading cloud providers have successively written the FDE into their formal talent models, with job descriptions highly consistently pointing to three keywords: client embedding, end-to-end responsibility, and reusable assets. This echoes, across borders, China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology "encouraging service providers to build FDE teams" and China Youth Daily's career survey—the FDE role definition is converging globally. Most instructive for practitioners is what both JDs emphasize: "knowledge transfer" and "accumulating accelerators": FDE delivery doesn't end at system launch—it also includes enabling client teams to take over and making the next project faster. Note: job pages prove role definition and organizational demand, not team size or business revenue.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Test every project with "six levels of closing evidence": process evidence (days/departments/interviews) → engineering evidence (features/tests/deployment) → business evidence (real users/sign-off) → operational evidence (sustained adoption/maintenance) → business-results evidence (work hours/costs/losses avoided) → public evidence (client confirmation/authorized scope). No level can automatically substitute for the next—"built it, tested it, launched it, someone keeps using it, the client formally signed off" are five different things.
  • Delivery is complete only when the client can take over: an eleven-item client takeover checklist—asset inventory, dual owners, installation permissions, operations manual, backup and recovery, test samples and thresholds, exception escalation, version updates, training reruns, support period, and acceptance signature. It's not about whether the FDE can run it, but whether designated client personnel can independently rerun it in the real environment and know whom to call when something goes wrong.
  • Use the "four transitions" for large-client diagnostics: score coverage, scenario impact, intelligent execution, and ecosystem collaboration separately, each with one piece of verifiable evidence—especially suited to state-owned and group-type clients.
  • Align with international JDs to calibrate your own capability matrix: treat the EY/AWS job requirements (eval design, observability, incident response, knowledge transfer, asset accumulation) as a self-assessment checklist—this is the FDE skill stack the market is currently pricing.
