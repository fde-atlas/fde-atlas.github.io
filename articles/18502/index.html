Forward-deployed engineering market data

FDE Notes · 2026-09-18 · 8 min read

Summary

FDE Notes publishes a citable snapshot (dated 2026-11-21) of the forward-deployed engineering job market, rebuilt on every deploy from employer ATS feeds. Of 943 live roles, 99.6% come directly from employers' own applicant-tracking systems (Greenhouse, Ashby, Workable), each linking to the original posting. Only 28% of postings disclose pay; across 239 USD ranges, the median midpoint is $225,000, spanning $90,000 to $850,000. Applied AI/ML roles pay the most, while solutions engineering/architecture is the largest family (314 roles). The page states its method and limits: medians are withheld below 10 disclosed ranges, data is not survey-based, and no forecasts are made. Third-party figures cited include a 729% year-over-year rise in Indeed postings.

Key points

  • Applied AI/ML roles pay most; solutions engineering is largest family.

What is in this dataset

939 of the 943 live roles (99.6%) are pulled straight from the employer's own applicant-tracking system. The remaining 4 come from public remote job boards (Jobicy), and we count them separately rather than folding them into the same claim. Nothing here is re-hosted — every row links to the original posting. A posting enters the dataset when its title or description shows customer-facing AI or deployment work, and leaves it within ten days of disappearing from the source.

Pay

260 of 943 live roles (28%) publish a pay range in the posting itself. Only those are counted here — nothing on this page is estimated, benchmarked, or backfilled from comparable roles.

Across the 239 roles posted with annual pay in US dollars, the median published range is $185,000–$250,000; half of the ranges sit between $160,000 and $300,000. The median of each range's midpoint is $225,000. The full span runs from $90,000 to $850,000. A further 21 disclosed ranges could not be pooled (5 in GBP, 7 in CAD, 3 in AUD, 1 in KRW, 3 in SGD, 2 in EUR) and are listed on the salary page rather than dropped.

Equity, bonus and benefits are excluded — we only ever see base pay, so that is all we report. Every figure is itemised by employer on the salary data page.

Pay by role family

The pooled ranges split by the role family in the title. Each group is the same records as the total above — the group counts add back up to 239 — but a group only gets a published median once at least 10 employers have disclosed pay in it. That rule is a product decision, not a statistical law: with fewer than 10 ranges, one unusual employer moves the number enough that quoting it would mislead the person reading it.

How this is collected

  • Source. 939 of 943 live roles (99.6%) come from employers' own ATS endpoints — Greenhouse, Ashby, Workable and similar — fetched from their public APIs. The other 4 come from public remote job boards (Jobicy). No aggregator page is scraped and no copy is re-hosted: every row links back to the original posting.
  • Normalisation. Titles such as "Forward Deployed Engineer", "Forward Deployed AI Engineer", "Applied AI Engineer" and "Deployment Solutions Engineer" are merged into one comparable dataset — the layer that makes the counts mean anything.
  • Pay. Structured fields where the ATS provides them, otherwise parsed from the posting text; each row records how it was parsed. Only employer-disclosed ranges are published.
  • Removal. A role that stops appearing in the source is dropped from all counts within ten days. Closed postings never contribute to a statistic.
  • Reproducibility. Every count on this page is recomputed on each build and machine-checked against the raw job data before the site ships; a mismatch fails the build.

What this page does not claim

  • Small samples are withheld, not smoothed. A group needs at least 10 disclosed ranges before its median is published; groups between 5 and 9 are labelled as small samples; below 5 the group is not broken out at all. That is why this page has no country-level pay split — 203 of the 239 pooled ranges sit in one country.
  • It is not a survey. Nothing here comes from asking companies what they pay; every pay figure is one an employer published themselves.
  • It is not the whole market. It is the forward-deployed and deployment-adjacent slice we track, and only roles whose source we could reach.
  • It is not a forecast. We publish counts as of a date, not projections.
  • It is not an endorsement of any employer, and nothing here was paid for by one.
