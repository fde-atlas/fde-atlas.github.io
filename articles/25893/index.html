Teams and roles

Forward-deployed engineer

A forward-deployed engineer (FDE) is a senior engineer who builds and ships software or AI systems directly in a client's environment, working alongside the client's own team.

AI pod

An AI pod is a dedicated, cross-functional team of senior engineers that designs, builds and ships an AI system as one unit, working in the client's cloud and repositories.

Tech lead

A tech lead is a senior engineer who guides the technical direction of a team, owning architecture decisions, code quality and delivery while still writing code, usually without formal people-management duties.

Solutions architect

A solutions architect designs how software systems, cloud services and integrations fit together to meet a business need, translating requirements into a technical blueprint that engineering teams can build.

MLOps engineer

An MLOps engineer builds and runs the infrastructure that takes machine learning models from experiment to production, including training pipelines, deployment and monitoring.

LLM evaluation engineer

An LLM evaluation engineer designs the tests, datasets and metrics that measure whether a large language model application gives accurate, safe and useful results.

AI engineering

AI engineering is the discipline of building production software around AI models, combining software engineering, data work and model integration so that AI features are reliable, measurable and maintainable.

Engagement models

Staff augmentation

Staff augmentation is a way of adding outside engineers to an existing team, where they work under the client's own managers, processes and priorities.

Team extension

Team extension is an engagement model in which a company adds outside engineers to its existing team, where they work in the company's processes and tools under its own technical leadership.

Dedicated development team

A dedicated development team is a group of engineers, and often related roles such as QA or design, assigned exclusively to one client's product or roadmap for an extended period, working as an extension of that client's organization.

Nearshore software development

Nearshore software development is the practice of working with engineers based in nearby countries that share or closely overlap the client's time zone, such as Latin America for US companies.

Offshore software development

Offshore software development is the practice of working with engineers in distant countries, typically with large time-zone differences from the client, usually to reduce cost or reach a larger talent pool.

Managed services

Managed services is an engagement model in which an outside provider takes ongoing responsibility for running an IT or software function, such as infrastructure, application support or testing, against agreed service levels.

Time and materials (T&M)

Time and materials (T&M) is a pricing model in which the client pays for the actual time worked, at agreed hourly, daily or monthly rates, plus any materials or expenses, rather than a fixed price for a defined scope.

Statement of work (SOW)

A statement of work (SOW) is a document that defines what a provider will do for a client on a specific engagement, including scope, deliverables, roles, timeline, acceptance criteria and pricing.

AI building blocks

Large language model (LLM)

A large language model (LLM) is a neural network trained on very large amounts of text to predict the next token, which lets it understand instructions and generate, summarize, translate and reason over language and code.

Foundation model

A foundation model is a large AI model trained on broad data at scale that can be adapted to many downstream tasks, such as chat, coding, search or image analysis, through prompting or fine-tuning.

Open-weight model

An open-weight model is an AI model whose trained weights are published for download, so organizations can run, inspect and fine-tune it on their own infrastructure, subject to its license.

Multimodal AI

Multimodal AI refers to models and systems that can take in or produce more than one type of data, such as text, images, audio, video and documents, and reason across them together.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a technique where a system first retrieves relevant information from a knowledge source and then passes it to a language model to generate an answer.

AI agent

An AI agent is software that uses a language model to decide which steps to take and which tools to call in order to complete a task, rather than only producing a single reply.

Agentic AI

Agentic AI is an approach to building software in which language models plan steps, call tools and take actions toward a goal with limited human supervision, instead of only answering a prompt.

Multi-agent system

A multi-agent system is a design in which several AI agents, each with its own role, tools and instructions, coordinate to complete a task that would be hard for a single agent to handle reliably.

AI agent memory

AI agent memory is the set of techniques that let an agent keep and reuse information across steps and sessions, such as conversation history, user preferences and facts learned while completing tasks.

Vector database

A vector database stores numerical representations of data, called embeddings, and quickly finds the items most similar to a query, which makes it useful for semantic search and RAG.

Embeddings

Embeddings are numerical vectors that represent the meaning of text, images or other data, placed so that similar items sit close together, which lets software compare things by meaning rather than exact words.

Semantic search

Semantic search is a search method that finds results based on the meaning and intent of a query rather than exact keyword matches, usually by comparing embeddings of the query and the content.

Reranking

Reranking is a second retrieval step that takes the top results from a fast search and reorders them with a more accurate model, so the most relevant passages reach the user or the language model first.

Document chunking

Chunking is the process of splitting documents into passages before they are converted to embeddings and indexed, so a retrieval system can find and return the specific part of a document that answers a question.

Knowledge graph

A knowledge graph is a structured representation of entities, such as people, companies, products or documents, and the relationships between them, stored so software can query and reason over connected facts.

Tokenization

Tokenization is the step that splits text into tokens, the word pieces a language model actually reads and writes, and it determines how much text fits in a context window and how usage is billed.

Context window

A context window is the maximum amount of input and output, measured in tokens, that a language model can consider in a single request, including instructions, conversation history and any documents supplied.

LLM temperature

Temperature is a sampling setting that controls how random a language model's output is: low values make answers more focused and repeatable, while high values make them more varied.

Prompt engineering

Prompt engineering is the practice of designing, testing and versioning the instructions, examples and context given to a language model so that it produces accurate, consistent and useful outputs.

Chain-of-thought prompting

Chain-of-thought prompting asks a language model to work through intermediate reasoning steps before giving a final answer, which tends to improve accuracy on multi-step problems.

Function calling

Function calling, also called tool use, is a capability that lets a language model choose a predefined function and return structured arguments for it, so an application can run that function and pass the result back.

Model Context Protocol (MCP)

The Model Context Protocol (MCP) is an open standard for connecting AI applications to external data sources and tools through a common interface.

LLM fine-tuning

LLM fine-tuning is the process of further training a pretrained large language model on a curated set of examples so it performs a specific task, format or style more reliably.

LoRA (low-rank adaptation)

LoRA, or low-rank adaptation, is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that freezes a model's original weights and trains compact add-on matrices, making it far cheaper to adapt a large model to a specific task.

RLHF

RLHF, or reinforcement learning from human feedback, is a training method that uses human preference ratings to steer a language model toward answers people judge more helpful, accurate and safe.

Model distillation

Model distillation trains a compact student model to reproduce the behavior of a larger teacher model, keeping much of its quality on a target task while cutting inference cost and latency.

Model quantization

Quantization stores a model's weights, and sometimes its activations, at lower numerical precision, such as 8-bit or 4-bit instead of 16-bit, so the model needs less memory and runs faster with a modest loss in quality.

Synthetic data

Synthetic data is artificially generated data that mimics the patterns and structure of real data, used to train, test or evaluate software and AI models when real data is scarce, sensitive or hard to label.

Computer vision

Computer vision is the field of AI that enables software to interpret images and video, including classifying what is shown, detecting and locating objects, reading text and tracking movement.

Speech-to-text

Speech-to-text, also called automatic speech recognition (ASR), converts spoken audio into written text, either in real time as someone speaks or in batches from recorded files.

Intelligent document processing

Intelligent document processing (IDP) uses AI to classify documents, extract data from them and validate the results, turning unstructured files such as invoices, contracts and forms into structured data for business systems.

AI orchestration

AI orchestration is the coordination of models, prompts, data sources, tools and agents into a single workflow, managing the order of steps, the data passed between them, retries, errors and handoffs to people.

Generative AI

Generative AI is a class of machine learning models that create new content, such as text, images, code or audio, by learning patterns from large training datasets and producing outputs in response to a prompt.

Conversational AI

Conversational AI is technology that lets software hold natural-language conversations with people over chat or voice, understanding what they mean, keeping track of context and responding or taking action.

Natural language processing

Natural language processing (NLP) is the field of AI and linguistics that enables computers to read, interpret, analyze and generate human language in text or speech.

AI copilot

An AI copilot is an assistant built into a software tool or workflow that suggests, drafts, summarizes and explains in context, while the person using it reviews the output and makes the final call.

Hybrid search

Hybrid search combines keyword search, such as BM25, with vector or semantic search and merges the two result lists, so a system finds both exact term matches and passages with similar meaning.

Running AI in production

AI inference

Inference is the stage where a trained model is used to produce outputs, such as a prediction, classification or generated text, from new inputs, as opposed to training, where the model learns its weights.

Model routing

Model routing sends each AI request to the most suitable model, based on task, difficulty, cost or latency, instead of sending every request to one model.

LLM gateway

An LLM gateway is a proxy layer between applications and model providers that centralizes access, routing, authentication, logging, rate limits and cost controls for every AI call.

LLMOps

LLMOps is the set of engineering practices and tools for deploying, evaluating, monitoring and improving applications built on large language models once they are in production.

MLOps

MLOps, short for machine learning operations, is the set of practices and tools for building, deploying, monitoring and updating machine learning models in production reliably and repeatably.

Feature store

A feature store is a central system for defining, computing, storing and serving machine learning features, so the same feature values are used consistently in training and in production predictions.

LLM evals

LLM evals are repeatable tests that measure how well a language model or AI application performs on the tasks it is meant to handle, using test cases, scoring methods and pass thresholds.

AI observability

AI observability is the ability to see what an AI system is doing in production, by tracing each request through prompts, retrieval, model calls and tool actions, and measuring quality, cost and latency over time.

Model drift

Model drift is the gradual decline in a deployed model's performance because the data it sees, or the relationship between inputs and outcomes, has changed since it was built or last evaluated.

AI guardrails

AI guardrails are the checks and controls placed around an AI model that keep its inputs, outputs and actions within safe, accurate and permitted limits.

Prompt injection

Prompt injection is an attack in which text crafted by an attacker, typed directly or hidden in content the model reads, overrides an AI system's instructions and makes it reveal data or take unintended actions.

PII redaction

PII redaction detects and removes or masks personally identifiable information, such as names, emails, account numbers and addresses, from text, documents or logs before they are stored, shared or sent to an AI model.

AI red teaming

AI red teaming is structured adversarial testing in which people or automated tools try to make an AI system fail, leak data or behave unsafely, so weaknesses are found and fixed before attackers or users find them.

AI hallucination

An AI hallucination is an output from a language model that sounds confident and plausible but is false, unsupported by its sources, or invented, such as a made-up citation or number.

Human-in-the-loop

Human-in-the-loop (HITL) is a design approach in which people review, approve, correct or label an AI system's work at defined points, so automation handles volume while humans keep control of important decisions.

AI governance

AI governance is the set of policies, roles, processes and technical controls an organization uses to make sure its AI systems are lawful, safe, fair, secure and aligned with business goals.

Model serving

Model serving is the infrastructure and process of hosting a trained machine learning model behind an API so applications can send it inputs and receive predictions reliably, quickly and at scale.

Responsible AI

Responsible AI is the set of principles and engineering practices for building AI systems that are fair, safe, transparent, private and accountable, so that their benefits are delivered without unacceptable harm.

Explainable AI

Explainable AI (XAI) is the set of methods that make a model's outputs understandable to people, showing which inputs drove a prediction or decision so it can be trusted, debugged and audited.

Data and platform

Data pipeline

A data pipeline is an automated series of steps that collects data from one or more sources, transforms and validates it, and delivers it to a destination such as a data warehouse, an application or an AI model.

ETL vs ELT

ETL (extract, transform, load) transforms data before loading it into a target system, while ELT (extract, load, transform) loads raw data first and transforms it inside the warehouse or lakehouse.

Data warehouse

A data warehouse is a central database designed for analytics that stores cleaned, structured data from many source systems so teams can run fast SQL queries, reports and dashboards.

Data lakehouse

A data lakehouse is a data architecture that combines low-cost data lake storage with warehouse features such as ACID transactions, schemas and fast SQL, so analytics and machine learning run on the same data.

Data mesh

Data mesh is a decentralized approach to data architecture and ownership in which business domains own and publish their data as products, supported by a shared self-service platform and common governance.

Event-driven architecture

Event-driven architecture is a software design in which services communicate by producing and reacting to events, such as "order placed" or "payment failed", usually through a message broker or streaming platform rather than direct calls.

Microservices

Microservices is an architecture style that builds an application as a set of independently deployable services, each owning one business capability and its data, communicating over APIs or events.

API gateway

An API gateway is a single entry point that sits in front of backend services and handles request routing, authentication, rate limiting, request transformation and monitoring for client applications.

Infrastructure as code

Infrastructure as code (IaC) is the practice of defining servers, networks, databases and other cloud resources in version-controlled files, so environments are created and changed through automated, repeatable deployments instead of manual console clicks.

CI/CD

CI/CD stands for continuous integration and continuous delivery or deployment: automated practices that build and test every code change, then release it to production through a repeatable, low-risk pipeline.

Observability

Observability is the ability to understand what is happening inside a software system from the data it emits, mainly logs, metrics and traces, so teams can detect, investigate and fix problems quickly.

Site reliability engineering

Site reliability engineering (SRE) is a discipline, started at Google, that applies software engineering to operations, using automation, measurable reliability targets and blameless learning to keep production systems available and fast.

Data governance

Data governance is the framework of policies, roles, standards and controls that determines how an organization's data is owned, defined, secured, accessed and used throughout its life.

Data quality

Data quality is the degree to which data is fit for its intended use, usually judged on accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, validity and uniqueness.

Platform engineering

Platform engineering is the discipline of building and running an internal developer platform: self-service tools, infrastructure and paved paths that let product teams build, deploy and operate software without reinventing the plumbing.

DevOps

DevOps is a set of cultural practices and automation that unites software development and IT operations, so teams share ownership of building, releasing and running software and can ship changes quickly and reliably.

Kubernetes

Kubernetes is an open-source container orchestration platform that automates deploying, scaling, networking and healing containerized applications across a cluster of machines.

Cloud migration

Cloud migration is the process of moving applications, data and infrastructure from on-premises data centers, or from one cloud provider to another, onto a cloud platform such as AWS, Azure or Google Cloud.

Planning and delivery

AI readiness

AI readiness is a measure of how prepared an organization is to build, deploy and operate AI systems, based on the state of its data, infrastructure, engineering skills, governance and leadership support.

Discovery phase

A discovery phase is the initial stage of a software or AI project in which a team clarifies goals, users, requirements, risks and technical approach before committing to a full build.

AI proof of concept (PoC)

An AI proof of concept (PoC) is a short, scoped project that tests whether an AI approach can solve a specific business problem with real data, against success criteria agreed before the work starts.

Minimum viable product

A minimum viable product (MVP) is the simplest version of a product that real users can use, built to test its core value and gather feedback with the least effort before investing in a full build.

Technical debt

Technical debt is the extra future work created when software is built with shortcuts, outdated designs or missing tests, so that every later change takes longer and carries more risk until the underlying problems are fixed.

Legacy modernization

Legacy modernization is the process of updating or replacing outdated software, infrastructure or mainframe systems so they are easier to change, secure, integrate and run, while keeping the business running during the transition.

AI transformation

AI transformation is the organization-wide shift from isolated AI experiments to AI built into core workflows, systems and decisions, changing how work gets done rather than adding a tool on the side.

Enterprise AI

Enterprise AI is artificial intelligence designed and operated to meet the needs of large organizations, including security, compliance, integration with existing systems, reliability and scale.

AI workflow automation

AI workflow automation uses AI models to complete steps in a business process that need judgment, such as reading documents, classifying requests or drafting replies, alongside traditional rules-based automation.

AI ROI

AI ROI (return on investment) is the measurable business value an AI system produces, such as cost saved, revenue gained or risk reduced, compared with the total cost to build, run and maintain it.

AI center of excellence

An AI center of excellence (CoE) is a central team that sets standards, builds shared platforms and expertise, and coordinates governance so that teams across an organization can adopt AI consistently and safely.
