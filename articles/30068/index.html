What to Do If You Fail the FDE Exam: No Retakes, and Only 1 of 5 Failure Types Offers a Second Chance

Original title: FDE 考试没通过怎么办：没有补考，5 种「挂了」只有 1 种有转机

FDE 指南 · 2026-11-03 · 7 min read

Summary

This article explains what happens after failing the Tencent Cloud ADP Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) certification exam. Its core finding: each purchase includes exactly one exam attempt with no free retake, and absence, lateness, or violations all count as using up the attempt. Scores are machine-graded with no appeal or review channel. The author debunks the widespread claim of three attempts, which comes from a different guide covering practitioner certifications. The piece reconstructs the exam structure (60 questions, 70-point pass mark, 1.5 points per single-choice, 2 per multiple-choice), flags multiple-choice as the main scoring risk, and offers practical advice: allocate study time by module weight, check Prometric seat availability before paying, and note the 24-hour rescheduling deadline.

Key points

  • FDE allows one attempt per purchase; no retakes, and absence counts as used.
  • Machine grading only; no official appeal or score-review channel exists.
  • The three-attempt rule applies to practitioner certifications, not FDE.
  • Exam is roughly 40 single-choice plus 20 multiple-choice; multiple-choice is the key risk.
  • No cooling-off period; the real constraint is Prometric seat availability.

Bottom line first

This is about the Tencent Cloud ADP FDE certificate. It is easy to confuse with several other FDE certification tracks, so before registering, first read the differences between the four systems and confirm which certificate you are actually taking.

The Tencent Cloud ADP Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) certification has no retake. This is not speculation; it is official wording, written in two places:

  • The "Retake Policy" section on the certification details page: Each purchase includes 1 exam attempt; if you fail or miss the exam, you must purchase again
  • The "Individual Registration and Exam Guide" (658/107930): 1 registration includes only 1 exam attempt; free retakes are not supported (this sentence appears twice in that document)

So the answer to "what if I fail" starts not with "how to apply for a retake" but with figuring out which kind of failure yours is. Because the five scenarios have different consequences, and most people assume they are in the first one.

Your situationExam attemptCan it be savedWhat to do
① Score below 70Used upNoPurchase again
② Missed the exam (didn't show up)Used upNoPurchase again
③ Arrived too late to enterUsed upNoPurchase again
④ Exam terminated for a violationUsed upNoPurchase again
⑤ You believe the score was graded incorrectlyUsed upNo channel existsSee Section 3

The first four are no different—the attempt is simply gone. The fifth looks the most like it "has a way out," but it is actually the only one with no application channel at all.

1. First confirm whether you actually failed

This step sounds redundant, but the FDE score release timing is inconsistent across the two official pages, so you need to know how long to wait.

SourceOriginal wording
"Score Inquiry" section on the certification details pageScore release time varies by exam type: theory exams within 3 days, hands-on exams within 14 days
"Score Inquiry" section of the "Individual Registration and Exam Guide"You can go to Personal Center - Certification Exams 30 minutes after the theory exam ends to check your certification score and certificate (if passed)

FDE only has a theory exam (the details page lists the exam type as theory exam, with the format being offline), so by the former it is within 3 days, and by the latter, 30 minutes after. Both are official pages, and I cannot judge for you which is more accurate.

Practical advice: Go to Personal Center - Certification Exams and check once 30 minutes after finishing. If the score is there, you'll know the result on the spot; if not, don't panic—anything within 3 days is normal. Don't plan your day around "results in 30 minutes"—some people get them immediately, others have to wait; this is something the documents currently don't explain.

There is only one entry point for checking scores: Personal Center → Certification Exams. Scores and certificates (if passed) are on the same page.

2. No retake, but many people think there is

The claims circulating online about "whether you can retake" mostly come from a different document.

Tencent Cloud has two registration guides with different rules:

DocumentWhich certifications it coversExam attempts
"Individual Registration and Exam Guide" (658/107930)Specialized technical certifications, FDE belongs to this category1 attempt, no free retake
"Practitioner Certification Registration and Exam Guide" (658/101119)Tencent Cloud Practitioner, AI Practitioner, WorkBuddy OPC Practitioner3 attempts

658/107930 states at the beginning that "practitioner certifications are excluded." So the rule of "3 attempts per registration" does not apply to FDE.

Budgeting based on 3 attempts means undercounting two exam fees. This is one of the most common misconceptions in this topic area, and I've also clarified it separately in the full registration process article.

3. The fifth kind of "failure": no appeal channel

This is the most easily misunderstood point, and the main reason I wrote this article.

Original text of Article 3 of the "Certification Exam Discipline Rules" (658/45313):

Regarding results: The system determines results through automated computer grading, and results are provided only to the exam initiator; the system does not intervene in results in any form.

"Does not intervene in results in any form"—this sentence is the official answer to "can you appeal to change a score." No.

After going through both documents thoroughly, there is no mention anywhere of score review, appeals, or manual re-grading entry points. The discipline rules also contain no "exam ban" clause; they only state that "violating security protocols may result in confiscation of prohibited devices and termination of your exam"—termination means ending it on the spot, not a later penalty.

So if you believe a multiple-choice question was graded incorrectly, or suspect the machine missed part of your answers, there is currently no official channel to pursue. It sounds unreasonable, but it is written in black and white. I'm stating this here not to make you give up, but to keep you from wasting time looking for an appeal entry point.

By the way: for machine-graded multiple-choice questions, it is usually "partial selections score zero, extra selections score zero"—only exact matches count. The official documents don't go into detail on this, but it is where multiple-choice questions lose the most points—2 points per question, a very real cost.

4. Re-registering: same as the first time, but with three differences

The process itself is unchanged: purchase → schedule → exam. The entry point is still the Tencent Cloud Training and Certification Center → FDE certification card → details page.

But three things differ from the first time, all related to timing:

First: the refund window is still 5 days, but you probably won't be able to use it. The refund rule allows a request "within 5 days of purchase and before scheduling the exam"; after that, no refund. Since you've already taken the exam once, the new order becomes non-refundable as soon as you schedule. Confirm exam seat availability before buying—don't pay first and then discover there are no slots within a month.

Second: the new order again has a 1-year validity period. Orders are valid for 1 year and expire afterward, with no scheduling possible. Reminders will be sent via in-site messages, email, and SMS 90 / 60 / 30 / 15 days before expiration—provided you have bound and verified your contact email and phone. If you haven't verified them, you won't receive the reminders; this is a common reason orders expire unused.

Third: the rescheduling window is only the 24-hour tier. The Guide's original text says free changes or cancellations are allowed "more than 24 hours before the theory exam, or more than 72 hours before the hands-on exam." FDE only has a theory exam, so the applicable window is 24 hours, not 72 hours. Changes within 24 hours before the exam are not supported.

5. The 70-point passing line: exactly how far off are you

This section addresses a specific question: how many more questions you need to get right next time.

FDE exam structure (original text from the details page):

ItemOfficial wording
Number of questions60
Exam duration90 minutes
Passing score / Total score70 / 100
Question typesSingle-choice + multiple-choice
Question valuesSingle-choice 1.5 points + multiple-choice 2 points

Back-calculating from 60 questions, 1.5 points for single-choice, 2 points for multiple-choice, and a total of 100: 40 single-choice + 20 multiple-choice (1.5×40 + 2×20 = 100). This step is arithmetic, not official wording, but all four numbers check out, so you can rely on it.

This gives you your margin for error: 70 points to pass, so you can lose at most 30 points.

If the lost points all come fromMaximum number of questions you can get wrong
Single-choice questions (1.5 points each)20 questions
Multiple-choice questions (2 points each)15 questions
Mixed (half wrong on each)About 10 single-choice + 7 multiple-choice

The most important row to remember in this table is the multiple-choice row. Getting 15 of 20 multiple-choice questions wrong costs 30 points, hitting the line directly; you'd need to get 20 single-choice questions wrong to reach the line. Multiple-choice questions are worth 33% more per question and there are half as many of them—it's the real battleground for lost points.

If you scored in the 60s last time, that usually means you were 5–10 points short, which translates to getting 3–5 more multiple-choice questions right, or 4–7 more single-choice questions right. That gap can be closed with targeted review; no need to start over from scratch.

VI. How to prepare next time: allocate time by module weight

The details page gives the knowledge distribution across 7 modules. This is the only officially published weight information, so allocating your review time by it is the most efficient approach.

ModuleShareConverted to 100 points
Scenario understanding and solution design25%25 points
ADP core features and application building22%22 points
Industry solutions and case implementation15%15 points
Delivery, evals, and operations11%11 points
AI and generative AI fundamentals10%10 points
Security, compliance, and responsible AI10%10 points
ADP product positioning and capability boundaries7%7 points

The first two add up to 47 points, nearly half. If you failed last time, the areas to review most are these two—not "AI fundamentals," which feels like it most needs work but is actually only worth 10 points.

The supporting resources are all on the details page; no need to buy anything extra: the exam guide, online practice questions, and the exam sandbox video, plus a full set of online courses (split into 4 major chapters, from AI fundamentals to Multi-Agent tuning). These come bundled with the certification and are not separately charged items.

VII. Timeline: from checking your score to re-entering the exam room

The official documentation does not specify a required interval between exams. There's no cooling-off period and no clause like "banned after failing several times"—neither appears in 658/107930 or 658/45313.

But "no interval requirement" doesn't mean "you can take it anytime." The real bottleneck is seat availability: the FDE is an in-person exam taken at a Prometric test center, with seats released by city and date.

So the timeline looks like this:

  • Check your result: failed (30 minutes to 3 days after the exam)
  • Check seat availability first, then pay—the details page has "real-time in-person seat availability lookup"; first check whether your city has openings in the next two weeks
  • Buy a new order (certification exam fee, subject to what's actually shown on the order page; at the time of writing, the details page listed ¥1200, with the accompanying online courses free for a limited time)
  • Book (choose city, date, and time; fill in your ID information)
  • 24 hours before the exam is the rescheduling deadline; after that, no changes are allowed
  • Arrive 30 minutes before the exam with your original ID card

There are official channels for seat coordination: call 400-613-7050 on weekdays, or email chinareg@prometric.com. These two contact details are written next to the "real-time in-person seat availability lookup" on the FDE details page—they're officially provided, not copied from elsewhere.

VIII. Four common misconceptions

"You can request a score review after the exam" — No. Article 3 of the Exam Discipline Rules explicitly states that "the system does not intervene in results in any form."

"Being absent doesn't count as using up an attempt" — It does. The details page states verbatim: "If you fail or are absent, you must purchase again." Arriving late beyond the allowed time and being denied entry to the exam room is likewise treated as an absence.

"You have to wait months after failing to retake the exam" — There's no such rule in the documentation. The interval depends on seat availability, not on regulations.

"The 2-year certificate validity counts from registration" — No. The certificate is valid for 2 years from the date the most recent passing exam result is released. This starting point matters: if you pass in November 2026, it expires in November 2028; if you retake and pass before expiration, the validity period restarts from the new passing date.
