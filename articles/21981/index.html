Summary

This Japanese article maps Japan's fast-growing FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) services market: 48 companies claim to offer FDE as of October 2026, and the author profiles 23 whose services are verifiable from public sources. Companies are grouped into 8 types (implementation-resident, consulting-accompaniment, own-product-based, major SI/consulting, big AI vendor, industry-specialized, staffing, and training), each suited to different buyers. Six selection criteria are proposed, covering on-site presence, scope, success definition, pricing, staffing, and exit/knowledge-transfer design. Published prices range from ¥300,000/month for a two-person setup to ¥1.5-1.65 million for full-time resident engineers. The operator DeFactory also sells FDE services, which readers should note.

Key points

  • 48 Japanese companies claim FDE services as of October 2026; 23 are compared using official public information.
  • Providers are classified into 8 types, each matched to a specific buyer situation.
  • Six pre-contract criteria: entry mode, scope, success definition, pricing, staffing, and exit design.
  • Published pricing spans ¥300k/month (2-person) to ¥1.5-1.65M (full-time resident).
  • Most failures stem from undefined success criteria; the author suggests concrete questions to ask vendors.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer—or a form of support—who is stationed on-site alongside customers, helps organize business challenges together with them, and takes responsibility for embedding AI and ensuring it takes root in the field.

Since 2025, the number of companies offering FDE services in Japan has grown rapidly, with 48 companies confirmed as of October 2026. This article lists 23 companies that can be engaged externally and whose details have been verified through official announcements (Source: AI Agent Navi "FDE Provider Companies Chaos Map").

However, "how far they will go" varies greatly from company to company.

Even among FDEs, the substance varies widely, including contracted development, consulting, and implementation support for their own SaaS.

In this article, we first present 6 criteria that clients should compare, and then organize the providers into 8 types.

An FDE takes business challenges with undefined requirements and works with the field to define them while carrying them through to implementation.

If specifications are already decided, contracted development is cheaper and faster.

Rather than "what can be done," check "who is responsible, to what extent, and for what outcomes."

It is a good idea to evaluate the items on each company card against these six criteria.

  • How They Engage On-Site: Full-time on-site, a few days a week, or remote. Whether they are present with key stakeholders
  • Scope of Responsibility: How far they go, from problem definition to design, implementation, and operational adoption
  • Definition of Outcomes: Whether acceptance criteria and KPIs can be set before signing the contract (to prevent projects from stalling at PoC)
  • Pricing & Contract Type: Quasi-mandate, fixed-scope contract, or outcome-linked. Whether a monthly estimate is available
  • Team & People: Whether the assigned staff's track record and background are disclosed in advance. Whether there are rules for staff changes
  • Internalization & Exit Planning: Whether knowledge transfer and handover materials are provided upon completion

  • "We know what to build, but have no builders" → Type ① implementation staffing, or Type ⑦ talent provision/matching
  • "Company-wide rollout with strict security review" → Type ④ major SI/general consulting, or Type ⑤ major AI company (Accenture's Japan delivery structure is unconfirmed, so please verify by inquiry)
  • "We want to internalize in the future" → Type ⑧ training/development, or Type ⑦ with knowledge transfer conditions
  • "Industry-specific operations (contact centers, manufacturing, HR, etc.)" → Type ⑥ industry-specialized

If multiple apply, first determine whether you can define success criteria on your own.

If not, start with ②; if yes, start with ① or ⑦.

Finally, ask the same questions (criteria 1-6) to 2-3 candidates and compare the specificity of their answers.

Most failures can be prevented before signing a contract.

Here we organize the three most common pitfalls into warning signs and questions to ask.

Why AI investments often fail to produce results is also discussed in What is FDE? Why AI Companies Are Growing Fast and the Reality of "70% Changing in Six Months".

  • Warning Sign: The contract or proposal does not specify "what constitutes success"
  • Question to Ask: "Can we set the PoC pass criteria and the criteria for proceeding to full development, in numbers, before signing?"
  • Warning Sign: No one actually doing the work is present at requirements definition
  • Question to Ask: "At what stages and how many times will you interview the on-site staff?"
  • Warning Sign: Only one specific engineer understands the whole picture
  • Question to Ask: "If the assigned person changes or withdraws, what will be handed over?"

These three correspond to the criteria mentioned above: Criterion 3 (definition of outcomes), Criterion 5 (team & people), and Criterion 6 (internalization & exit planning).
