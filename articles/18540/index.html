Forward Deployed Engineer Job Description Template

MarsLab · 2026-11-01 · 9 min read

Summary

This MarsLab article explains how to write a job description for forward deployed engineers, with a fill-in template covering duties, qualifications, success measures, and pay. It analyzes four real postings from OpenAI and Databricks (November 2026): required experience ranges from 2+ years (associate) to 10+ years (manager), all demand production engineering and customer-facing work, and travel runs roughly 20-50%. It contrasts AI companies, where FDEs build on models and feed signal back to research, with data platform companies, where FDEs drive platform adoption and may be billable. It advises keeping coding requirements even for managers, disclosing salary ranges where legally required (e.g., California), and lists warning signs that a posting is actually pre-sales or support, such as quotas, ticket duties, or no production deliverable.

Key points

  • Postings should state customer-time share, travel percentage, ownership, and success measures.
  • Four sampled postings require 2+ to 10+ years depending on level, all with production experience.
  • OpenAI emphasizes full stack and databases; Databricks emphasizes Spark and data engineering.
  • Manager postings should retain coding requirements and define scope, not just years.
  • Quotas, ticket language, or missing production work signal a pre-sales or support role.

A forward deployed engineer job description should describe a software engineer who works inside customers' environments, builds and ships production systems there, and reports what they learn back to the company. It should state the share of time with customers, the travel, the level, the experience required and what the engineer owns when the work ends. A template follows below.

This page goes deeper on the posting itself. For the role in general, read our forward deployed engineer guide. It is written for two readers: a hiring manager drafting a posting, and a candidate reading one. Every statement about a specific posting comes from a page we opened in November 2026. Postings change, and some of these may be gone.

What are the responsibilities of a forward deployed engineer?

A forward deployed engineer learns the customer's problem from inside their operation, builds and integrates a working system there, and stays until people use it. The job description should list these as duties with outcomes, not as a list of tools.

  • Learn the customer's operation by working beside the people who run it, then write the problem down in terms they agree with.
  • Scope the work. OpenAI's posting asks for "detailed scopes of work and project plans for both proof-of-concept prototypes and full production deployments".
  • Build and integrate production software, often on the customer's own infrastructure and next to their engineers.
  • Stay through adoption and handover, so the customer's team can run and change the system without you.
  • Report what the field teaches to product or engineering, and turn repeated work into reusable tools.

We describe how those duties play out across an engagement in what a forward deployed engineer does, week by week.

What did forward deployed engineer postings ask for in November 2026?

In the four postings we read, required experience ran from 2+ years at an associate level to 10+ years for a manager. All four asked for production engineering experience and comfort working with customers.

  • OpenAI, Forward Deployed Software Engineer, San Francisco. It asks for 7+ years of professional full stack engineering, with customer-facing experience "highly desirable", and experience with relational databases such as Postgres or MySQL. A former founder or early startup engineer is listed as a plus. It says travel of up to 50% is required.
  • OpenAI, Manager, Forward Deployed Engineering, New York City. It asks for 10+ years of engineering or technical delivery experience, including 2+ years managing FDEs or customer-facing engineers, and the ability to write and review production code in JavaScript or Python. Travel is up to 25%.
  • Databricks, Associate Forward Deployed Engineer. It asks for 2+ years in software engineering, FDE or AI engineering work, a bachelor's degree in a related field, Python and JavaScript or TypeScript, and experience with one major cloud or a strong wish to build it. Apache Spark and a Databricks certification are plus points. Travel is about 20%.
  • Databricks, Staff Forward Deployed Engineer, working with government customers. It asks for 7+ years in data engineering, data platforms or software engineering, two or more cloud ecosystems, deep Spark knowledge, and project delivery experience at director level or above. It also requires an active Secret or Top Secret clearance, or willingness and eligibility to obtain one.

Four postings are a sample of what we saw on two companies' own careers boards, not a survey of the market. They do show the pattern: required experience scales with level, and preferred items are a platform skill, a startup background or a certification.

How does the role differ at an AI company and a data platform company?

At an AI company the engineer builds on models and APIs and sends field signal back to research and product. At a data platform company the engineer builds on the vendor's platform and is often tied to adoption of it. The postings we read show both patterns.

  • What the engineer builds on: OpenAI's posting asks for software that uses OpenAI's APIs. Databricks' associate posting asks for data and AI solutions on the Databricks platform.
  • Where feedback goes: OpenAI's manager posting says success is measured partly by "how clearly you deliver signal to Research and Product". Databricks' staff posting asks the engineer to surface platform gaps to product and engineering.
  • How the work is framed commercially: the Databricks staff posting says FDEs "are billable" and asks for help to "expand platform adoption". The OpenAI postings describe partnering with customers and sales teams on the same account.
  • Skills emphasized: general full stack work and databases in the OpenAI posting. Spark, data engineering and MLOps in the Databricks staff posting.

Name the stack the engineer will really touch. Titles such as Applied AI Engineer or AI forward deployed engineer can describe the same job, so read the duties and not the title.

How do responsibilities change at senior, lead and manager level?

Responsibility moves from delivering your own workstream to shaping several, and then to running the team that delivers them. The postings we read show the start and the end of that path clearly, and are less consistent about the middle.

  • Forward deployed engineer: delivers a defined workstream and contributes to design. The Databricks associate posting says the engineer manages scope and timelines for their workstreams, "growing toward end-to-end delivery ownership".
  • Senior: owns a whole deployment from scoping to handover and works with customer executives as well as their engineers. This is our reading, since postings define this level in different ways.
  • Lead or staff: leads cross-team execution and mentors others. The Databricks staff posting asks the engineer to develop "frameworks and tools adopted beyond the immediate team".
  • Manager: builds and runs the team. OpenAI's manager posting asks the manager to lead and grow a team, own delivery outcomes end to end, and define how field teams are staffed. It still asks for production code to be written and reviewed.

A manager posting should keep a coding requirement, as the OpenAI one does. When you write a level into a posting, state the scope it carries, such as the number of concurrent customer workstreams or direct reports, and not just a number of years.

What should a forward deployed engineer job description template include?

It needs a summary, the duties, qualifications, how the work is measured and a pay range. Adapt the template below: fill in the square brackets and delete lines that do not apply.

Template, part 1: the basics.

  • Title: Forward Deployed Engineer, [level], [your product or domain]
  • Location: [city or remote region], with [number] days a week in the office if hybrid
  • Travel: about [percent] of the time, to [customer sites or regions]
  • Reports to: [title]
  • Pay range: [add your own range, see the note below]

Template, part 2: about the role.

  • [Your company] builds [your product]. Our forward deployed engineers work inside customer environments to turn [your product] into working systems that [customer outcome].
  • You will spend about [percent] of your time with customers and the rest building, reviewing and writing up what you learn.
  • You will own [what the engineer owns, for example one customer deployment from scoping to handover].

Template, part 3: what you will do.

  • Learn the customer's operation by working beside the people who run it, and write down the problem in terms they agree with.
  • Scope the work in writing, with a success measure, the systems involved and the access you need.
  • Build and integrate production software on [your stack], inside the customer's environment where needed.
  • Ship to real users and stay until the customer's team can run the system.
  • Write the handover: runbook, documentation, and ownership of accounts and credentials.
  • Report what you learn to [product or engineering team], and turn repeated work into [reusable tools].
  • [Senior and above] Mentor [number] engineers and lead [number] customer workstreams at once.

Template, part 4: what you bring.

  • [Number]+ years building and running production software in [languages].
  • Experience with [cloud platform] and [data store or integration type].
  • Experience working directly with customers or users who are not engineers.
  • Clear written and spoken communication, including the judgment to say no to scope that will not work.
  • Willingness to travel about [percent] of the time.
  • Nice to have: [your platform or domain], experience as a founder or early engineer, [a certification you value and whether it can be earned after joining].

Template, part 5: how we work.

  • Success is measured by [metric, for example deployments live with real users and the outcome the customer agreed].
  • Performance is reviewed by [process].
  • Code, data and credentials built for customers are owned by [policy].
  • The interview process is [steps and expected time].
  • Benefits: [list].

Do I have to put a salary range in a forward deployed engineer job description?

The template has no figure because the range should be yours. In some US states you must include one. California's Labor Commissioner says an employer with 15 or more employees must include the pay scale in any job posting.

The same page says this applies if the position may ever be filled in California, in person or remotely. See California's equal pay guidance. Other states have their own rules, so check the labor department of each state where you might hire. For market context before you set a range, see our forward deployed engineer salary page.

What should you leave out of a forward deployed engineer job description?

Leave out anything that makes the role sound like a different job or hides what it is. A crowded posting attracts the wrong applicants.

  • A long list of every tool in your company. Name the stack the engineer will really use.
  • Sales targets, if the engineer is not meant to carry a quota.
  • Support queue duties, if another team owns tickets.
  • A degree requirement you would waive for strong experience. Add "or equivalent experience".
  • Open-ended travel. State a percentage, as the postings above do.

What are the warning signs that a posting is really pre-sales or support?

The clearest sign is that no production system is named. Working with sales is normal, but sales should not be the job.

  • The duties are demos and proofs of concept for prospects, with no mention of shipping to production.
  • Quota, pipeline or bookings targets for an individual engineer.
  • Ticket language: response times, queues, tiers or on-call for customer issues.
  • Coding appears under "nice to have" and not under requirements.
  • No statement of who owns the result after handover, and no loop back to product.
  • No success measure at all, or one that counts meetings and not outcomes.

OpenAI's posting names Sales, Solutions Engineering and Customer Success among the teams on the same account, next to Research and product teams, and still asks for production full stack work. That is the healthy version. Our forward deployed engineer vs software engineer page explains where the lines sit.

How should a candidate read a forward deployed engineer posting?

Read for the share of customer time, the travel, what you will own and how your work is measured.

  • Check the stated travel percentage and whether the role is hybrid or remote.
  • Check what the engineer owns: a deployment, a workstream or a queue.
  • Ask how performance is reviewed and by whom.
