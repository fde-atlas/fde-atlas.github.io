FDE Pay: What Job Postings Leave Out (2026 Study)

Christian & Timbers · 2026-11-03 · 7 min read

Summary

A Christian & Timbers study argues that job postings badly understate elite Forward Deployed Engineer pay. Elite FDEs earn $586,000 to $1.45 million annually, but base salary is under 40% of the package at the midpoint, with equity and bonus making up 52% to 66%. Roughly 80% of elite FDEs come from Palantir and rarely appear on public salary platforms. Only about 2,000 US engineers have measurable agentic AI results, and OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft and AWS are spending heavily to compete for them. The author advises setting total compensation first, pricing what candidates forfeit by leaving, and paying for deployment track records rather than titles.

Key points

  • Elite FDEs earn $586,000 to $1.45 million; postings show only base salary.
  • Equity and bonus are 52% to 66% of pay; none of ten postings disclosed equity value.
  • About 80% of elite FDEs are current or former Palantir engineers.
  • Only ~2,000 US engineers have measurable agentic AI delivery records.
  • Advice: set total comp first, price exit costs, pay for track record not title.

An elite Forward Deployed Engineer earns $586,000 to $1.45 million a year, and the job posting shows only the base salary. At the midpoint of that range, base is under 40% of the package, so a board that prices the role from postings starts with an offer that's already too low.

Christian & Timbers' Elite FDE Compensation Study breaks the elite band into three parts, and only one of them shows up in a job posting.

[Table 1]

The Wall Street Journal first reported the study's findings, describing companies that now want FDEs on their own payroll and are competing with AI labs and consulting firms to hire them.

What Counts as an Elite FDE?

The study sets a high bar for who counts as elite. An elite FDE has 7 to 15 years of experience, including at least two years building and deploying agentic workflows with documented enterprise impact of $10 million or more. They bring consultative gravitas and have stood up and led a team of FDEs. They've typically built 10 to 15 agentic workflows, with ROI measured in dollars and reportable to investors or the board.

None of that shows up in a job title, which breaks most pay benchmarks. Engineers who were promoted into deployment work often keep titles like staff software engineer or principal AI engineer. Salary surveys built around the FDE title skip these engineers entirely, and staff engineer benchmarks put their pay far too low.

Palantir makes the gap wider. About 80% of elite FDEs are current or former Palantir engineers, and the alumni with proven deployment records rarely surface on job boards, LinkedIn job posts or Levels.fyi. That market runs on relationships, so the people a board most wants to price are the ones missing from public data.

Leadership roles sit above the engineer band. The compensation study puts a VP or Head of AI Deployment at up to $2.25 million, close to the $2.3 million to $2.6 million median for Russell 3000 named executive officers in The Conference Board's analysis of 2025 proxy data. At companies worth $50 billion or more, the emerging Chief Deployment Officer role pays $1.51 million to $5.98 million. A deployment leader who has never held a C-suite title can now cost close to what a public-company executive officer earns.

Why Don't Job Postings Show the Full Package?

Companies publish the number that's easiest to state and compare, and that's base salary. Christian & Timbers reviewed ten public FDE and AI deployment postings from Palantir, Google, Anthropic, OpenAI and Cerberus. None disclosed an equity dollar value, and eight disclosed no bonus percentage. Google's two postings were the only ones to list a bonus target.

Palantir's own numbers show the gap. Its posting for a Forward Deployed AI Engineer lists a base of $135,000 to $200,000 and says only that pay may also include restricted stock units and a sign-on bonus. On Levels.fyi, the Palantir FDE title market runs $175,000 to $315,000 across every level of the title. At the midpoints, the elite band is about four times that figure, $1.02 million against $245,000.

What Goes Wrong When Boards Price From Postings?

The first problem shows up at the offer stage. Strong deployment engineers are usually employed when you reach them, and they measure your number against what they'd leave behind. Frontier labs pay their own senior and principal FDEs $630,000 to $1.53 million, slightly more than the enterprise elite band and mostly through equity, and that's the bid an enterprise is up against. An offer built from posting data can look generous to a comp committee and thin to the person reading it.

Supply makes it worse. Christian & Timbers estimates that only about 2,000 engineers in the U.S. have a track record of delivering a measurable return from agentic AI.

Competition for that pool is growing. OpenAI and Anthropic each launched enterprise deployment companies in May. Microsoft has committed $2.5 billion to Frontier Company, a unit of 6,000 industry and engineering experts who embed with customers on AI deployment. Those roles cover more than FDEs, and most of the staff come from Microsoft's existing teams. AWS has committed $1 billion to its own forward deployed initiative. With that much money aimed at so few elite engineers, a low first offer tends to end the conversation.

There's a cost boards rarely count. When a company can't close an in-house hire, it may turn to an outside firm to run deployment. That puts a vendor inside the business processes the AI is meant to improve, and those processes are the part of the company most worth keeping proprietary. Every company I talk to now says the same thing: we have to do this ourselves.

How Should a Company Build an FDE Offer Instead?

My advice is to set the total compensation target first and let base fall out of it. At the low end of the elite band, a posting would show only the $280,000 base, while the full package comes to $586,000.

Then price the exit. Find out what the candidate forfeits by leaving, including unvested equity and any bonus they'd miss by resigning mid-cycle. Decide whether a sign-on grant or cash buyout will cover it before the candidate has to raise it.

Finally, pay for the record. When Christian & Timbers released the compensation study, I put it this way:

A title alone won't tell you whether someone can do this work. You need to understand what they have deployed, the results they delivered and whether they can lead the team you need. Then you can build an offer that will attract them.

A principal engineer who meets the study's deployment and leadership criteria belongs in the elite band, whatever their title says. Christian & Timbers' guide on how to hire elite Forward Deployed Engineers covers how to test for that record.

Where Posting Data Still Helps

Postings still give you a reasonable floor for base pay, and Google's bonus targets show what a disclosed incentive looks like. They also fit roles below the elite tier better. The study prices market-proven FDEs at hyperscalers at $251,000 to $610,000, much closer to what postings suggest.

The study's bands come with caveats of their own. Each band is a range. Its low end combines the lowest base, bonus and equity, and its high end the highest, so few packages sit at either extreme. The midpoint is the better single reference. The bands are C&T calibrations from closed offers and live mandates, including offers candidates turned down, cross-checked against more than 550 documented interviews with engineers, deployment leaders and executives from January through June 2026. They aren't statistical samples, and Christian & Timbers is a retained search firm paid for placing executives in roles like these.

Questions to Settle Before Approving the Offer

  • What total compensation are we targeting, and how much of it is equity?
  • What does the candidate give up by leaving, and does our offer cover it?
  • Has this person shipped agentic workflows with a measurable financial result, and have they led an FDE team?
  • Who will they report to (CTO, VP of AI or COO), and does that reporting line fit the pay?

Frequently Asked Questions About Elite FDEs

What's the Difference Between an FDE and an Elite FDE?

A Forward Deployed Engineer works inside a business to find high-value workflows, build AI systems and deploy them into production. Christian & Timbers defines an elite FDE as a senior builder, typically with 7 to 15 years of experience, who has deployed agentic AI into production with documented enterprise impact of $10 million or more, has consultative gravitas, and has stood up and led a team of FDEs.

How Much of an Elite FDE's Pay Is Equity?

Equity and bonus make up 52% to 66% of elite FDE pay, according to Christian & Timbers' Elite FDE Compensation Study. Annualized equity alone runs $250,000 to $700,000. Of the ten public FDE and AI deployment postings the study reviewed, none disclosed an equity dollar value.

Where Do Most Elite FDEs Come From?

About 80% of elite FDEs are current or former Palantir engineers, according to Christian & Timbers' Elite FDE Compensation Study. Frontier labs and hyperscalers also train strong deployment engineers, and frontier labs pay their own senior FDEs $630,000 to $1.53 million, slightly more than the enterprise elite band.

Christian & Timbers recruits elite Forward Deployed Engineers and the leaders who run them, including the VP of AI Deployment, Head of AI, VP of AI Transformation, VP of Engineering and CTO, for enterprises building deployment capability in-house. The firm has evolved into a full executive search firm with global reach into passive AI talent at frontier labs and top technology companies, and its AI Talent Intelligence practice tracks compensation and offer data across this market.
