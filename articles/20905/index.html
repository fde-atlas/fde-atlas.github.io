How FDEs Work: Comparing the Frequency of Remote Work, Business Trips, and On-Site Assignments in Japanese and Overseas Job Postings

Summary

This article compares FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) working conditions in Japanese and overseas job postings as of October 2026, arguing that FDEs are not typically stationed full-time at client sites. Instead, postings specify travel percentages or hybrid arrangements: Palantir's Japan Government FDSE requires 25–75% travel, Databricks' Tokyo FDE 20%, and OpenAI's Tokyo FDE a 3-days-per-week hybrid with mainly domestic travel. Japanese startups are more remote-friendly, with RevComm offering full remote work. Citing The Pragmatic Engineer, the article notes Palantir expects about 25% of FDE time onsite (Commure up to 50%), and OpenAI's three-phase process varies onsite days by phase. It advises candidates to verify onsite frequency and security constraints in interviews.

Key points

  • FDE postings specify travel ratios or hybrid schedules, not full-time client-site residency.
  • Palantir Japan Government FDSE: 25–75% travel; Databricks Tokyo: 20%; OpenAI Tokyo: 3 office days weekly.
  • Japanese startups are remote-friendly: RevComm full remote, Andpad mostly remote, JDSC remote allowed.
  • Palantir expects ~25% of FDE time onsite; Commure estimates up to 50%.
  • Onsite days vary by project phase; government and manufacturing clients require higher onsite ratios.

Written and reviewed by: FDE Talent Editorial Team / Primary sources such as official job postings and official announcements from each company are cited

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is often described as a role that "embeds with customers," leading many to imagine a work style of being stationed at client sites every day. However, actually reading job postings from Japan and overseas, the working conditions are written not as "on-site stationing" but in the form of "travel percentage" or "hybrid (X days per week in office)," and fully remote postings also exist. The frequency of visiting client sites varies greatly depending on the company and the phase of the project.

In this article, we compile a comparison table of the work arrangements, remote work availability, and travel descriptions written in Palantir, OpenAI, and Databricks job postings for Tokyo, as well as postings from SoftBank, JDSC, RevComm, Andpad, Money Forward, and Sakana AI (all originals confirmed as of October 2026). We also draw on Palantir's official blog and an interview article from The Pragmatic Engineer to outline an FDE's day and week, and the reality of time spent at client sites by project phase.

Key Takeaways of This Article

  • FDE job postings often describe working conditions not as "on-site at client" but as "travel percentage" or "hybrid": Palantir's FDSE for the Japanese government lists 25–75% travel, Databricks' Tokyo FDE lists 20% travel to client sites, and OpenAI's Tokyo FDE lists a hybrid arrangement of 3 days per week in office plus primarily domestic travel (as of October 2026).
  • FDE job postings at Japanese companies are more tolerant of remote work than overseas ones: RevComm offers full remote work, Andpad offers remote work as a rule (full remote negotiable), JDSC allows remote work, and SoftBank (seconded to SB OAI Japan) lists remote work allowed alongside client visits and travel (as of October 2026).
  • According to The Pragmatic Engineer, Palantir assumes FDEs spend about 25% of their time at client sites, while healthcare AI company Commure estimates up to 50% — "at the client every day" is not the standard.
  • The Pragmatic Engineer explains that OpenAI's FDE work progresses through three phases — initial scoping, validation, and delivery — and that being at the client site typically means a few days during scoping and a few days per week during the delivery phase.
  • In the daily schedule published on Palantir's official blog, a person at a European site works on a US client project, switching within a single day between a morning stand-up, implementing data pipelines, afternoon iterations with the client, and creating materials for stakeholders.

Conclusion: FDE working conditions are written as "travel percentage" or "hybrid," not "on-site stationing"

The terms used to describe working conditions in FDE job postings fall broadly into three categories. The first is "travel percentage": Palantir's FDSE for the Japanese government states "25–75%," and Databricks' Tokyo FDE states "20%," expressing the proportion of time spent at client sites numerically. The second is "hybrid": OpenAI's Tokyo FDE specifies "3 days per week in office," and Money Forward's internal FDE specifies "in principle, 2 days per week in office required, 3 or more days recommended," setting the number of days in their own office. The third is "remote," such as RevComm's "full remote work," Andpad's "remote work as a rule," and JDSC's "remote work allowed," which are commonly seen among Japanese startups.

Common to all of these styles is that expressions like "stationed at Company X," as seen in SES job postings, never appear. Since FDEs work with their own company's product and move between their own company's development team and the customer's site, their affiliation is always their own company, and they "visit client sites when needed." The structural differences between FDEs and SES (on-site stationing) are covered in detail in the following article.

Related article: Differences Between FDEs and SES, SIers, SEs, and Consultants | Explaining Their Positions with a Comparison Table

However, "low frequency of visiting client sites" is not the same as "weak contact with customers." As described below, every company requires FDEs to "embed" with the customer team, and interacting with customers almost daily, even online, is part of an FDE's daily routine. The proportion of time physically present at the client site and the proportion of time focused on the customer need to be considered separately.

Working Conditions Written in Overseas Companies' Job Postings (For Tokyo, as of October 2026)

Overseas AI and data platform companies clearly state working conditions for their Tokyo-based FDE postings using the same format as in their home countries. Because travel percentages and in-office days are written numerically, these are the most useful references for comparing work styles.

Palantir: Hybrid work + 25–75% travel (FDSE for the Japanese government)

Palantir's job posting for "Forward Deployed Software Engineer - Japan Government" (as of October 2026) lists the location as Tokyo and the work arrangement as "Hybrid," and regarding travel states: "Willingness and interest to travel as needed to client sites. Ability to travel 25 – 75% required." The job description also says "We also work externally with our customers, often on-site," making clear that work at client sites is assumed.

The wide 25–75% range indicates that time spent at client sites varies greatly depending on the project. The same posting also requires "Security clearance or the ability to obtain clearance" and proficiency in both Japanese and English, suggesting that for government and public sector projects, the proportion of work performed inside customer facilities is higher.

OpenAI: Hybrid with 3 days per week in office + primarily domestic travel (Tokyo FDE)

OpenAI's job posting for "Forward Deployed Engineer - Tokyo" (as of October 2026) states: "This role is based in Tokyo. We use a hybrid work model of 3 days in the office per week and offer relocation assistance to new employees. Travel is expected in this role, mainly within Japan." The responsibilities include "Embed closely with customer teams," making it clear that while the role involves deep engagement with customers, the base location is the company's own office.

The "Manager, Forward Deployed Engineer - Tokyo" posting on the same job board (as of October 2026) quantifies the travel cap: "This role also will require travel up to 40%, although mainly local." Since even the manager role allows up to 40% travel, field FDEs can be expected to travel at a similar level, or more depending on the project. OpenAI's FDE approach is also covered in the following case study article.

Databricks: 20% travel to customer sites (Tokyo FDE)

Databricks' job posting for "Forward Deployed Engineer" (Tokyo, as of October 2026) explicitly states: "Travel to customers 20% of the time." The responsibilities include "Customer Immersion: Embed with customer teams," and the role involves leading production-level data engineering and AI/ML integrations on the Databricks platform. The posting does not specify office or remote work; only the travel percentage is given as a number.

Customer onsite-time ratios reported by The Pragmatic Engineer

The Pragmatic Engineer (by Gergely Orosz, published August 12, 2025) interviewed OpenAI's FDE lead Colin Jarvis, Ramp's FDE lead Leo Mehr, and former Palantir FDE Anjor Kanekar, summarizing how FDEs work. The article states that "Palantir expects around 25% of FDEs' time to be spent onsite with customers," and notes that the healthcare AI company Commure estimates "up to 50%."

In other words, even among overseas FDEs, being at the customer site every day is not the standard; based on publicly available information, a rough guideline is that time spent at customer sites is about a quarter to half of the total. Compared with the "25-75%" figure in Palantir's job posting, positions with a high proportion of on-site work at customer facilities, such as government projects, are likely to have higher upper limits.

Working conditions stated in Japanese companies' job postings (as of October 2026)

Compared with overseas postings, Japanese FDE job postings tend to explicitly allow remote work. Few postings state travel percentages as numbers; instead, conditions are presented in forms such as "remote work allowed," "fully remote," and "travel allowances." Here, we extract the working-condition details from the job postings of six companies whose original texts we were able to verify.

SoftBank (seconded to SB OAI Japan): Remote work allowed + customer visits and travel (mainly domestic)

SoftBank's job posting for "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)/Secondment to SB OAI Japan LLC" (as of October 2026) lists the work location as "WeWork Takeshiba (Kaigan, Minato-ku, Tokyo) or a Tokyo-area office," and states "customer visits and travel (mainly domestic)" and "possibility of travel to OpenAI headquarters in San Francisco, USA." The work style is "remote work also allowed (working from home, satellite offices, etc.)."

The responsibilities include "designing/implementing generative AI systems in customer environments (cloud/on-premises)," "providing technical explanations to customer executives/department heads and supporting implementation," and "working closely with customer engineers/business departments," showing that even though remote work is allowed, engaging with customer environments and on-site settings is at the core of the job. The mention of possible travel to the overseas headquarters is a distinctive feature of a joint venture with OpenAI.

JDSC: Remote work allowed + flextime (core hours 10:30-16:30)

JDSC's job posting for "【Dev】FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)【Full-time】" (as of October 2026) lists the work location as the company headquarters in Koishikawa, Bunkyo-ku, Tokyo, and states the work system as "flextime, 8 working hours per day, core hours 10:30-16:30," "fully two-day weekends (Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays)," and "remote work allowed." The responsibilities require deeply embedding in customer companies' business operations and leading everything from problem identification through PoC development, production implementation, and post-deployment improvements with a high degree of autonomy, with an emphasis on "quickly bringing prototypes to the field" to drive implementation.

RevComm: Fully remote work + flextime with no core hours

RevComm's job posting for "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)" (as of October 2026) lists the work location as the Marunouchi Building, a one-minute walk from Tokyo Station, and states: "Fully remote work (you may work anywhere, including your home, as long as information security is ensured. However, some positions are excluded)." Working hours follow a "flextime system (no core hours)." Among the postings reviewed, this is the most remote-friendly condition.

The responsibilities include "organizing customer business requirements and structuring them into technical requirements," "designing using API integrations/data integration/Webhooks, etc.," "designing solutions leveraging MCP and third-party integrations," "clarifying technical issues and building consensus with stakeholders," and "abstracting knowledge from individual projects and feeding it back into the product." While the role involves customer engagement, it places a heavy emphasis on design and integration. The posting contains no mention of travel or customer visits.

Andpad: Principally remote work (full remote negotiable) + travel allowance

Andpad's job posting for "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)" (as of October 2026) lists the work location as the office in Mita, Minato-ku, Tokyo, and states, "We currently operate on a principally remote work basis to prevent the spread of COVID-19. Full remote work is negotiable." Working hours are "10:00-19:00, flextime (core time 11:00-15:00)," and the benefits section includes a "travel allowance."

The job description begins with, "You are an engineer, a consultant, and a businessperson. Standing at the front line with customers, you will use Andpad's AI technology to help solve their specific management challenges," and requires that you "feed back the 'firsthand information' gained at the front line to the development team to evolve Andpad's next-generation products." Since the product is for the construction industry, while work is principally remote, the presence of a travel allowance suggests there are opportunities to visit job sites.

Money Forward (Internal FDE): Hybrid with 2 office days required per week and 3+ days recommended

Money Forward's job posting for "【Forward Deployed Engineer (Internal)】 AX Promotion Division_Tokyo" (as of October 2026) lists the work location as the headquarters in Shibaura, Minato-ku, Tokyo, and describes a hybrid work style with "in principle, 2 office days required per week, with 3 or more office days recommended." Working hours are "basically 9:30-18:30 (60-minute break), left to the employee's discretion," with either the professional discretionary labor system or flextime applied.

As the "Internal" in the title indicates, this is an FDE that works with the company's own business divisions rather than external customers, handling "discovery, proposal, verification, and prototype development of problem-solving methods using AI" end-to-end, from interviews through requirements definition to PoC development. There is no mention of travel to or being stationed at customer sites. Even with the same FDE title, internal-facing roles can be an example of a working style with almost no travel.

Sakana AI: Hybrid work (office + remote)

Sakana AI's job posting for "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)" (published August 18, 2026, still listed as of October 2026) lists the work location as Mori JP Tower at Azabudai Hills in Minato-ku, Tokyo, with a work style of "hybrid work (office + remote)" and working hours of "flextime / discretionary labor system (depending on role)." There is no mention of the number of office days or travel ratio. The job description begins with, "The FDE dives into the customer, understanding their business more deeply than anyone else," and close communication with customers' operational staff and experts is a core part of the role.

A day and a week in the life of an FDE (from public information)

After working conditions, the next thing people want to know is what an actual day looks like. While there is little primary information publicly detailing an FDE's day with timestamps, a concrete picture can be drawn from "A Day in the Life of a Palantir Deployment Strategist" (published March 9, 2022) on Palantir's official blog and an interview article by The Pragmatic Engineer.

The "day in the life" featured on Palantir's official blog

The author of this article, Sabine, joined Palantir in December 2019 as a new graduate Deployment Strategist (a role internally called "Echo"). Palantir engagements typically involve a mixed team of Deployment Strategists and Forward Deployed Engineers ("Delta"), and the article explains that "in theory, Echo is more product-manager-oriented and Delta is more technical, but in practice both are a blend of product manager, software engineer, and strategist, and the line between the two roles is often quite blurry." It is also useful reading for understanding how FDEs work.

What stands out is the description of the work location. The author writes, "Although I am based in Europe, I am currently working on a pilot engagement with a major US drugstore company." In other words, rather than being stationed at the customer's site, this is an example of advancing a customer engagement remotely across continents. A typical day as the "pilot lead" described in the article is as follows:

  • 09:00 — Refresh the big picture of the engagement: what the customer wants, where we currently stand (while running or with coffee)
  • 10:00 — Daily stand-up. Everyone shares the same understanding of what they are working on and why, and confirms there are no blockers
  • 10:15 — Continue implementing a data pipeline that integrates the customer's ERP system with an application that automatically resolves shipping discrepancies
  • 12:30 — Lunch. Also an opportunity to learn what engagements colleagues are working on
  • 13:30 — Iteration session with the customer. Show progress from the morning, understand real-world problems, hear feedback, and spark ideas for new features that were previously thought impossible
  • 14:30 — Casual sync with another internal member (to get to know people working on different things)
  • 14:50 — Spend time on an internal initiative outside the engagement (Foundry for Builders activities for startups)
  • 16:30 — Prepare materials for a milestone presentation to senior stakeholders, set up an internal rehearsal, and share progress via Slack with colleagues waking up on the other side of the world

This day includes three types of work: implementation in the morning, customer interaction in the afternoon, and stakeholder material preparation in the evening. The customer touchpoint is the 13:30 iteration session; the rest of the time is spent on in-house development work and internal communication. The same article also features the day of an "enterprise lead" who runs an existing large-scale engagement (pre-sales meetings, 1-on-1s, weekly team meetings, writing one-page documents proposing features to product development teams, etc.) and the day of an "Internal Echo" who does more product-development-oriented work internally (PR reviews, time for writing code, design reviews, etc.), showing that even within the same role, the nature of the work can differ greatly.

The author also states, "I enjoy taking on multiple 4-12 week pilot engagements and meeting new industries and colleagues, but there are also many Echos who stay on a single engagement for a long time, going deep and building long-term customer relationships." Engagement length is not fixed either; there are both ways of working—moving between short-term pilots and staying with one company for a long time.

A week in the life of a Palantir FDE, as organized by The Pragmatic Engineer

The Pragmatic Engineer, based on interviews with former Palantir FDEs, breaks down a typical FDE week into three types of work. The first is work on customer projects, which is the main focus. The second is platform improvement, such as configuring new data models and committing stability improvements and fixes. The third is internal tasks, including internal communication, email, meetings, and stand-ups. One of the interviewed FDEs says that to keep meeting time down, they ask themselves, "Does this discussion need to be a meeting, and do I need to attend?"

OpenAI's three phases and the number of days spent at the customer's site

Colin Jarvis, head of FDE at OpenAI, explained in an interview with The Pragmatic Engineer that FDE engagements progress through three phases. According to the article, the number of days spent at the customer's site varies by phase.

  • 01 Initial scoping: Spend "a couple of days onsite" at the customer, sitting alongside users, diagramming processes, and identifying the areas of greatest value
  • 02 Validation: The FDE visits the customer to verify that the scoped work is truly the most valuable for the customer. Includes building evaluation criteria (evals)
  • 03 Delivery: Be at the customer's site "typically for a few days per week," precisely understanding the details of the problem, receiving data from the customer, and building the solution

The article also cites a case where Jarvis worked directly with farmers alongside FDEs on an engagement with John Deere in Iowa, which required completing the integration before the next growing season. It is a quintessential FDE way of working: going on-site in line with the customer's business cycle. OpenAI's Tokyo FDE job posting's "3 days in office per week + mainly domestic travel" can be read as the three-phase approach applied to engagements in Tokyo.

The reality of commuting back and forth to customer offices, and cases of isolated environments due to security requirements

Behind the travel ratios and in-office days in job postings lie the customer's industry and security requirements. What determines how often you go to the customer is not personal preference but the constraint of "whether the work can be done outside the customer's environment."

In manufacturing and public institutions, you may work on-site or in isolated environments

In The Pragmatic Engineer's interview, Anjor Kanekar, a former Palantir FDE, said he was stationed at Airbus's final assembly line, an aircraft manufacturer, and that "many of my fellow FDEs also worked in similarly unconventional environments, including air-gapped (physically isolated from external networks) environments." The fact that Palantir's FDSE job posting for the Japanese government requires security clearance and caps travel at 75% is presumably because there are data and systems that can only be handled within the customer's facilities.

Working in isolated environments comes with constraints such as being unable to use your company's tools or cloud services, having no free access to the internet, and restrictions on the equipment you can bring in. Experience building systems that run in such environments is one reason engineers who have worked on constraint-heavy SIer or enterprise projects are said to be well suited to FDE roles.

"Customer environments" and "security" as seen in Japanese job postings

Japanese job postings also mention customer environments. SoftBank's job posting (secondment to SB OAI Japan) lists "designing/implementing generative AI systems in customer environments (cloud/on-premises)" and "implementation design that meets security/governance requirements" among its duties. When on-premises implementation is required, the frequency of visiting customer sites increases. As RevComm's job posting states as a condition for full remote work, "anywhere as long as information security is ensured," even remote work comes with security conditions on where you work.

Time spent away from the customer is also time "facing the customer"

As the flow of a day shown in Palantir's blog illustrates, even when not at the customer's site, an FDE's daily routine involves iterating with customers online in the afternoon and working on implementations that handle customer data in the morning. The fact that the job postings of OpenAI, Databricks, and Sakana AI all say "embed into the customer team" and "dive into the customer" refers not to physical on-site presence, but to being deeply involved in the customer's business and data. Even in postings with a 20-25% travel ratio, it is safe to assume that the majority of working hours are spent on customer issues.

Criteria for choosing a job based on work style, and questions to ask in interviews

Since FDE work styles vary widely by company and project, it is important not to judge based on the job posting alone but to confirm specifics in the interview. From a lifestyle planning perspective, it is a good idea to evaluate job postings using the following criteria.

Three criteria for reading job postings

  • Whether "numbers" for travel and office attendance are stated: Postings with numbers, such as Palantir's 25-75%, Databricks' 20%, and OpenAI's three days a week in the office, allow you to concretely imagine your life after joining. For postings without numbers, confirm in the interview
  • Customer industries and environments: Companies with many government, financial, or manufacturing customers tend to have a higher ratio of working at customer sites or in on-premises environments due to security requirements. Companies focused on SaaS implementation support tend to have a higher ratio of work completed online
  • External vs. internal customers: Positions like Money Forward's "Internal" FDE, which work with the company's own business divisions, involve almost no travel. Choose based on whether you want a work style that involves visiting customer sites

Questions to confirm in the interview

  • In your most recent project, how many days per week were you at the customer's site? How does this change depending on the project phase (scoping, validation, delivery)?
  • When working at the customer's site, can you use your company's development environment and cloud services? How common are projects with isolated environments or restrictions on brought-in equipment?
  • Is travel mainly domestic? Is there overseas travel (to headquarters or the customer's overseas offices)?
  • How long do you typically stay involved with a single project? Do you ever handle multiple projects simultaneously?
  • How often does work aligned with the customer's business hours occur (early morning, nighttime, or holiday support)? Is there collaboration with customers or teams in different time zones?
  • For remote work, what situations require coming into the office (kickoffs, milestone presentations, incident response, etc.)?

Summary: FDE is not a "job stationed at the client" but a "job that embeds into the customer"

Comparing Japanese and overseas job postings, FDE working conditions are described not as "on-site presence" but in terms of "travel ratio," "hybrid," and "remote allowed." Overseas companies state conditions with numbers (Palantir 25-75%, Databricks 20%, OpenAI three days a week in the office plus domestic travel), while Japanese startups are lenient about remote work (RevComm full remote, Andpad remote by default, JDSC remote allowed). The customer-site presence guidelines reported by The Pragmatic Engineer are about 25% for Palantir and up to 50% for Commure.

On the other hand, every company requires "embedding into the customer team," and an FDE's daily routine involves facing customer issues even when not at the customer's site. Depending on the customer's industry and security requirements, you may work in isolated environments. If lifestyle planning is important to you, the surest approach is to look at the numbers in the job posting and the customer's industry, and then confirm the number of days spent at customer sites per project phase in the interview. FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do FDEs work on-site at customers every day?

As far as job postings show, daily on-site presence is not the standard. Palantir's FDSE for the Japanese government involves 25-75% travel, Databricks' Tokyo FDE involves 20% travel to customer sites, and OpenAI's Tokyo FDE is a hybrid of three days a week in the office plus mainly domestic travel (as of October 2026). The Pragmatic Engineer reports that Palantir assumes FDEs spend about 25% of their time at customer sites.

Q: Can FDEs work remotely?

Many Japanese companies offer postings that allow remote work. RevComm lists full remote work, Andpad lists remote work by default (full remote negotiable), JDSC lists remote allowed, and SoftBank (secondment to SB OAI Japan) lists remote work allowed (as of October 2026). However, some postings also mention customer visits and travel, so the job may not be entirely completed from home.

Q: How much travel do FDEs do?

It varies by company. Palantir's FDSE for the Japanese government lists 25-75%, Databricks' Tokyo FDE 20%, and OpenAI's FDE Manager (Tokyo) up to 40% as figures. Japanese companies mostly provide qualitative descriptions such as "customer visits and business trips (mainly domestic)" and "travel allowances available." There are also postings with almost no travel, such as in-house FDE roles (Money Forward's Internal FDE).

Q: What does a typical day for an FDE look like?

In a day-in-the-life example published on Palantir's official blog, after a 10 a.m. standup, the engineer implements a data pipeline integrating a customer's ERP, holds an iteration session with the customer at 1:30 p.m., and in the evening creates materials for senior stakeholders. A combination of implementation in the morning, customer interaction in the afternoon, and internal sharing in the evening is typical. Even within the same role, the content of a day differs greatly between someone leading engagements and someone more focused on internal development.

Q: Does an FDE's working style change depending on the phase of an engagement?

Yes, it does. In an interview with The Pragmatic Engineer, OpenAI's head of FDE, Colin Jarvis, explained that engagements are divided into three phases—initial scoping, validation, and delivery—and that during scoping he spends several days at the customer's site, while during the delivery phase he typically spends a few days per week there. The number of days spent at the customer's site increases or decreases by phase.

Q: Do FDEs sometimes work in secure, isolated environments?

Yes. In the interview with The Pragmatic Engineer, a former Palantir FDE said he was stationed on Airbus's final assembly line at the aircraft manufacturer, and that many of his colleagues also worked in unconventional environments, including air-gapped ones. Palantir's FDSE job posting for the Japanese government requires security clearance. When customers are in government, finance, or manufacturing, you should assume there are systems that can only be handled within the customer's facilities.

Q: Do FDEs have flexible working hours?

Flexible time systems are common in Japanese job postings. JDSC has core hours of 10:30-16:30, ANDPAD 11:00-15:00, RevComm has no core hours, Sakana AI offers a flexible time or discretionary labor system, and Money Forward offers a professional discretionary labor or flexible time system (as of October 2026). However, there are situations where you need to align with the customer's business hours.

Q: When choosing an FDE job with emphasis on working style, what should you check?

Check three things: whether the job posting includes figures for travel ratio and days in the office, the customer's industry (government, finance, and manufacturing tend to have high on-site ratios), and whether the role is customer-facing or internal. In interviews, asking specifically how many days per week you were at the customer's site in your most recent engagement, how many engagements involve isolated environments, whether there is overseas travel, and the duration of a single engagement will make it easier to imagine your life after joining.
