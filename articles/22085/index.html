Forward Deployed Engineer at Lovable: Role, Salary, and Interview (2026)

fde.directory · 2026-11-18 · 6 min read

Summary

This fde.directory piece profiles Lovable's Forward Deployed Engineer role as of late 2026. Despite building an AI product meant to remove the need for engineers, Lovable hires senior full-stack engineers to embed with enterprise customers and harden weekend-built apps into production systems. The company grew from $200M ARR in November 2025 to $500M by June 2026, reaching a $13.3B valuation. The FDE function only launched in July 2026, so hires help design the team itself. Requirements center on React and TypeScript; only the Lead role's pay is public ($241K–$390.5K US base). No verified FDE interview loop exists yet.

Lovable’s whole product is an argument that you don’t need an engineer to build software. Describe the app, the agent writes it, you ship it. CEO Anton Osika put it plainly at Slush last year: “Everyone becomes a developer in the future.”

So it’s worth noticing that Lovable is now hiring senior engineers to sit with customers and build for them. That’s not a contradiction. A product that spreads one employee at a time eventually becomes a multimillion-dollar company-wide contract, and somebody has to make it pass a bank’s security review, connect it to Salesforce, and turn twenty scattered internal apps into something IT will sign. At Lovable, that’s the Forward Deployed Engineer.

The company you’d be joining

Lovable’s growth numbers are among the steepest in software, and nearly all are company-reported. The Stockholm company crossed $200 million in ARR in November 2025, a year after launch. It passed $400 million in February 2026 with 146 full-time staff, per TechCrunch. That’s about $2.7 million of run-rate revenue per employee. It reached $500 million by June. One caveat for anyone valuing equity: Lovable computes ARR as the prior month’s revenue times twelve, a generous measure for a usage-heavy product.

Funding kept pace: $330 million at $6.6 billion in December 2025, then $400 million at $13.3 billion on August 12, 2026, led by Menlo Ventures and EQT’s Scaleup Europe Fund.

The enterprise story is the part that matters for this job. Osika said at Slush that about half of Lovable’s customers have enterprise accounts. At Dreamforce in September, the company said employees at nearly two-thirds of the Fortune 500 build with it. It has named Klarna, HubSpot, Adidas, Zendesk, and Nvidia as customers. It has also added what procurement asks for: security scanning on every change, admin visibility into every app, permission-scoped connectors, and AI-risk insurance through Lloyd’s.

That list previews the FDE work. In 2025, researchers found many Lovable-built apps had misconfigured database access rules that exposed data. Enterprise buyers remember, and much of the deployed engineer’s value is making a CISO comfortable.

What the role actually is

Lovable’s FDE function is new. Our board first tracked the founding FDE seat in July 2026. That posting asked for 5+ years of full-stack experience in React and TypeScript and promised to embed with the company’s “most ambitious customers.” A Forward Deployed Engineering Lead role followed in September. It was framed as a player-coach who builds the team in one location while still writing production code.

The current postings describe the work in consistent terms:

  • work directly with customers to understand their workflows and problems
  • turn vague requirements into technical designs and production systems
  • build, deploy, and operate both frontend and backend solutions that integrate with Lovable
  • debug issues that span the generated app, the infrastructure, and the AI
  • feed what you learn from real usage back into the core platform
  • help write the team’s playbooks, rituals, and org design

The Staff version, posted October 28, asks for 8+ years plus distributed-systems architecture.

Two things stand out. The stack is concrete: React and TypeScript, with web apps as the output, so the skill set is closer to senior product engineering than ML infrastructure. And nearly every posting asks you to help design the function itself at a company planning to reach roughly 450 people by the end of 2026. You write the playbook, and you live with whatever process doesn’t exist yet.

The contrarian read: at Fin, FDEs make an agent resolve more tickets. At Lovable, the FDE’s real customer is often the employee who already built an app over a weekend, and the job is making that app production-grade. That’s less “deploy our AI” and more “rescue and scale what our AI already built.” Engineers who enjoy hardening someone else’s prototype will find a lot of it here.

Where the seats are

Lovable hires FDEs on both sides of the Atlantic. US postings cover New York, Boston, and San Francisco, matching the East Coast pull in our NYC vs. SF analysis. The Lead role was posted for New York or Stockholm.

London is the newest hub. Lovable opened its London office with about 70 people and says it wants 100 there by December 31, 2026. FDE is one of its named teams, which makes Lovable one of the few high-growth AI companies running deployed engineering from Europe rather than from California. Our Europe guide covers the rest of that market. Postings are on-site or hybrid; don’t expect remote.

Compensation

Lovable publishes very little pay data. Here’s what’s verifiable:

Role Location Base range Source
Forward Deployed Engineering Lead US $241,000–$390,500 Posting text, Oct 2026 (via Indeed/FDE Pulse)
Staff Forward Deployed Engineer US / London Not published Lovable Ashby board
Forward Deployed Engineer Boston / NYC / SF Not published Lovable Ashby board

The lead band is wide, and the top of it is above most frontier-lab IC ranges in our salary guide. Postings mention equity but no figures. Since the IC bands aren’t public, an inference has to stay an inference: a reasonable expectation for a US staff IC sits below the lead band’s midpoint of about $316K, with equity on top. Get the number in writing early.

On equity: the stock is priced at $13.3 billion against roughly half a billion of run-rate revenue, so a grant only holds its paper value if Lovable keeps compounding. Ask for the share count, the latest preferred price, and the tender-offer history.

The interview loop

There are no credible public reports of Lovable’s FDE-specific interview loop yet, and we won’t make one up. The only first-hand engineering report we could find is old. It covers a Product Engineer candidate in Sweden, written up on Aced (formerly Exponent). It describes a founder conversation about zero-to-one experience and AI interest, then a live TypeScript code-reading screen the candidate wasn’t warned about. Treat that as a hint about the company’s style, not the current FDE process.

Prepare for what the postings emphasize: live TypeScript and React fluency, system design that starts from a customer’s messy requirements, and a story about taking a fragile prototype you didn’t write to production. Build something real on Lovable before the first call; knowing where the generated code breaks is the most relevant preparation you can do. Our interview guide covers the general FDE format.

Who this role is for

Lovable’s FDE seat suits a senior full-stack engineer who wants founding-team scope at a company with real revenue, and who enjoys the irony of being the human engineer at a company built on not needing one. The upside: a team you’d help design and a product that already spreads through enterprises on its own. The risks: a valuation that assumes growth continues, a security history buyers still raise, and almost no published pay data to negotiate against.
