For FDE-type projects, what should you decide first? Deliverables from business process organization through production operations

Original title: FDE型案件、最初に何を決める？業務整理から本番運用までの成果物

Inovie株式会社（イノビー） · 2026-09-08 · 7 min read

Summary

Inovie proposes structuring FDE-type engagements around three agreement points—before starting, after piloting, and before handover—rather than adding process stages. The article lists concrete deliverables for each phase: a one-page scoping sheet covering target tasks, exclusions, data permissions and evaluation criteria; evaluation records separating a working demo from business-ready judgment, with continue/revise/stop decisions; and operational handover documents including procedures, permissions, and shutdown/recovery steps. It also distinguishes client-specific deliverables from reusable internal assets, which should carry usage conditions and verification plans.

Key points

  • Agree on scope, exclusions, data permissions and evaluators before any prototyping.
  • A successful demo is a test record, not agreement that the system is business-ready.
  • Evaluation meetings decide to continue, rescope, or stop the engagement.
  • Handover requires the client to run shutdown and recovery without developer help.
  • Separate client deliverables from reusable assets, documenting reuse conditions.

"Let's first see what AI can do." Even if you start there, you'll want to decide whose work you're changing and who will judge the results of the trial. Even if you have something that works, if the customer and the provider are looking at different goals, you can't move forward.

What a provider of FDE-type support needs is not more process names, but documented agreements at each milestone. This article organizes the journey from business analysis to operational handover into three agreements: "before starting," "after trialing," and "before handover."

The processes and deliverables below are an example of project design proposed by inovie. They do not represent any specific company's standard processes or actual customer projects or support results.

First, what counts as FDE-type

At inovie, we consider applying our own repeatable products and platforms to customer sites and feeding that knowledge back into the product to be an essential condition of true FDE. Palantir also describes this as a method where field-adjacent engineers collaborate with the core development team to connect feedback to product development. Source: Palantir Architecture Center

On the other hand, what we cover here is the FDE-type project in a broader sense, connecting problem analysis through implementation and operations. This approach can be used by companies without their own products, but adopting it alone does not make it true FDE. We detail the differences in definition in Does FDE require your own product?

Before starting: align on the target business and the conditions for calling it done

"Make inquiry handling more efficient" alone doesn't define a scope. Receiving, investigating, drafting responses, approval, sending. Which of these you change determines what data and permissions you need.

The GOV.UK service design guide also emphasizes understanding the problem and constraints before starting to build. However, there's no need to apply the government service processes shown there directly to FDE-type projects. Source: How the discovery phase works

First, fill out a one-pager like the following together with the customer. Here, we assume a system for drafting responses to inquiries.

Filled-in example: targeting only the drafting of responses

This is a fictional example for illustration. It does not represent actual project conditions or results.

Users and pain points Support staff spend effort searching internal FAQs for the basis of responses.

Scope of change this time From the inquiry text, find relevant FAQs and display a draft response with cited sources.

Out of scope this time Automatic sending to customers, refund decisions, or commitments on conditions not in the FAQ.

Available information FAQs with verified usage permission and view access. Past inquiries are used only after confirming availability and required processing.

Evaluation criteria Consistency with cited sources, sections the staff modified, work time including verification, and hold behavior when no answer is available.

Who decides to continue The customer's business owner. Technical constraints are confirmed with the provider's lead, and information management conditions with the customer's data management lead.

"Improve accuracy" means pass/fail changes depending on who evaluates. Specify which question sets, which errors are unacceptable, and what must be verified before moving to the next step. Even when setting numbers, base them on current measurements and business impact. Don't fill in completion criteria with a borrowed accuracy rate alone.

Deliverables at this stage: the business flow, in-scope and out-of-scope items, data and permissions, and evaluation commitments. None of this needs to be a long document. For unconfirmed fields, attach who will confirm and by when.

After trialing: separate what worked from what's usable in the business

The demo produced a good answer once. That's a record confirming operation, not an agreement that it's usable in the business. Test not only everyday questions but also cases where documentation is outdated, where no answer exists, and where multiple rules conflict.

In the evaluation record, keep at least the input, expected response, actual output, cited sources, staff modifications, and reasons for unresolved items. Recording model and configuration versions also makes before/after comparisons easier. Don't evaluate only on the examples used for tuning; also test with separately held verification examples.

The deliverable here is not a "successful demo" but evaluation results showing under which conditions it can be used and under which it should not be.

What to decide at the evaluation meeting

  • Continue: proceed to production preparation, limited to the scope that met the agreed conditions.
  • Revise: if FAQ maintenance or narrowing the target business is needed, restructure the scope first.
  • Stop: if required information can't be used, or verification burden exceeds the value, and reasons to continue are weak, stop.

Avoid writing off remaining problems as "handled in operations." Who judges what, on which screen? If you place a verifier, evaluate whether the process works including that work.

Before handover: hand over the owner and how to stop

Being deployable to production and being operable by the customer are different things. Who re-evaluates after the FAQ is updated? If generation fails, how do you return to normal handling? If the contact point exists only in one developer's memory, the handover isn't finished.

Example deliverables to leave for operational handover

DeliverableWhat to recordWho verifies
Operations manualNormal operations, updates, contact points, handling when the owner is absentCustomer's operations staff
Permissions listWho can view or change what, and the procedure for revoking permissionsCustomer's management staff and the provider
Stop and recovery proceduresStop conditions, who operates, switching to manual handling, checks before resumingBusiness owner and operations staff
Evaluation and change historyAdopted versions, known constraints, examples to re-verify when changes are madeProvider and customer evaluation staff

Don't just hand over documents and finish. Verify whether the customer's operations staff can test stopping and switching without verbal explanations from the developer. The places where they can't become the places to fix the procedures.

Separate what you leave with the customer and what you grow in-house

After the project ends, will only the customer's code and documents remain? Or will evaluation methods and connection components that can be carried into the next project increase? This difference shapes how you grow an FDE-type business.

For example, an evaluation criterion like "put the response on hold when no matching FAQ is found" can be considered in other projects as well. On the other hand, this is not about moving the customer's actual data, internal rules, or specific connection details into shared assets. Whether something can be reused is determined by checking the agreed rights, confidentiality, and usage conditions, and generalizable insights are managed separately from them.

For reuse candidates, attach the conditions under which they can and cannot be used, how to verify them, and who will maintain them. Simply saving a component is not treated as "having built a foundation"; you also record what to check in the next project.

What to bring to the first meeting

There is no need to define the entire process precisely from the start. Pick one business process, and make sure you don't leave "what we won't do this time," "who will evaluate it," or "how to roll back if it doesn't work" blank. That alone will clarify what you should discuss after the prototype.

Rather than completing the project schedule first, create a state where you can make the next decisions together. As deliverables for that purpose, we design the business process organization, evaluation, and operational procedures.
