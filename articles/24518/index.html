FDE Series Column Learning Path

Original title: FDE 系列专栏学习路线

FDE中国社区 · 2026 · 4 min read

Summary

This article from the FDE China Community lays out a learning roadmap for a 20-chapter column series on forward deployed engineering. It frames the FDE as a role bridging business operations and AI engineering, covering customer discovery, process analysis, RAG/Workflow/Agent architecture choices, MCP and tool governance, evaluation infrastructure, deployment governance, ROI, and career transition. Each chapter follows a fixed structure ending in a concrete deliverable, and readers are advised to progress by producing one reusable artifact per chapter. Five reading paths suit different backgrounds, culminating in a complete FDE PoC proposal package.

Key points

  • FDE's core skill is translating model capability into systems businesses can adopt, verify, and operate.
  • The 20-chapter series spans role definition, business interviews, architecture, governance, and commercialization.
  • Five reading paths target different readers: roles, business co-creation, engineering, production governance, and case/commercial.
  • Each chapter ends with a deliverable for portfolios or client proposals, not just conceptual knowledge.
  • The final output is a full FDE PoC package including architecture, evals, governance, and ROI.

The FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) sits between the business frontline and AI engineering: you must be able to understand customers' real workflows, and also organize RAG, Workflow, Agent, MCP, tool governance, evals, and launch governance into a runnable system.

The goal of this series is not to cover every AI buzzword, but to help you build a set of enterprise AI deployment capabilities: discovering problems, scoping technically, designing solutions, implementing systems, evaluating results, launching and operating, and distilling your experience into a portfolio or client proposal.

How to Read This Series

This content is not a tool encyclopedia, nor a set of scattered tutorials. Think of it as the written version of an "enterprise AI deployment bootcamp": each lesson revolves around a real delivery problem—first explaining why this problem arises in the field, then providing the FDE's judgment framework, operational steps, deliverables, and common pitfalls.

We recommend progressing at the pace of "read one lesson, produce one small deliverable":

  • Read the problem first: whether this lesson addresses a role, process, architecture, evals, governance, or commercialization problem.
  • Then study the case: replace the scenarios in the text with an industry or project you know well.
  • Finally, produce output: don't just bookmark concepts—leave at least one reusable artifact from each lesson.

If you come from an engineering background, don't skip business interviews and ROI; if you come from a consulting or solutions background, don't skip RAG, Agent, MCP, evals, and deployment. The value of an FDE lies precisely in crossing these boundaries.

Five Learning Paths

Roles and Opportunities Path

Reading order: 01 → 02 → 03 → 04.

For readers learning about the FDE for the first time. You'll understand why enterprise AI competition is shifting from "who has the strongest model" to "who can deploy into workflows," and the real boundaries between FDE and development, algorithms, pre-sales, implementation, and consulting.

Business Co-Creation Path

Reading order: 05 → 06 → 07 → 08 → 09.

For readers preparing their first enterprise AI PoC. You'll learn how to interview customers, break down processes, locate pain points, write scenario cards, choose RAG / Workflow / Agent, and describe business objects with a lightweight Ontology.

Engineering Delivery Path

Reading order: 10 → 11 → 12 → 13 → 14.

For readers with an engineering background. You'll learn to design a minimal runnable system from the perspectives of knowledge bases, state machines, tool calling, MCP, ETL, AI Gateway, and private deployment.

Production Governance Path

Reading order: 15 → 16.

For readers focused on enterprise launches. You'll understand evaluation as infrastructure, Trace, Secure RAG, permission filtering, privacy interception, audit logs, launch gates, and rollback mechanisms.

Cases and Commercialization Path

Reading order: 17 → 18 → 19.

For readers working on client proposals, internal project approval, portfolios, or career transitions. You'll break cases into solution packages and fill in ROI, pricing, acceptance, change control, and portfolio presentation.

The Final Solution Package

After completing the full learning path, we recommend producing an FDE PoC solution package:

  • Business scenario and user roles.
  • Current flowchart and pain point list.
  • AI intervention points and architecture selection rationale.
  • FDE scenario card.
  • Data inventory, tool inventory, and permissions inventory.
  • Minimal RAG / Workflow / Agent engineering plan.
  • Knowledge base ETL, MCP tools, and AI Gateway design.
  • Golden Dataset, Trace, eval metrics, and failure samples.
  • Data classification, privacy interception, launch gates, and rollback plan.
  • ROI, customer acceptance, Demo script, and 30-day action plan.

Learning Method

Study each chapter with the same problem framework:

  • What real delivery problem does this chapter solve?
  • What input materials does it require?
  • What checkable deliverable does it output?
  • Which steps can use AI, and which should not?
  • How to evaluate results, control risks, and move into the next round?

If you remember only one sentence: the core FDE capability is translating model capability into system capability that the business frontline can adopt, verify, and operate.
