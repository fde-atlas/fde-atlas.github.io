2027 FDE Internship and New Graduate Position Application Requirements

Original title: 2027 FDE 实习和应届生岗位申请条件

FDE Times · 2026-10-24 · 5 min read

Summary

This guide to the 2027 FDE recruiting season argues that graduation dates, not skills, are the first filter. Palantir's FDSE internship accepts December 2027 or spring 2028 graduates, exactly one academic year after its new-grad role. Google London's summer internship opened October 2, 2026 and closed November 13—about six weeks—with no visa sponsorship and a 13-week full-time requirement. Applied Intuition requires March 2027 graduation and five days a week onsite. Job descriptions share common verbs—deploying, integrating into real environments, working with stakeholders—and require proficiency in only one language. The author advises rewriting resumes around environment, integration, users, and outcomes.

Key points

  • Palantir's internship and new-grad roles target graduation dates one year apart.
  • Google London's internship window lasted roughly six weeks with no visa sponsorship.
  • Applied Intuition requires March 2027 graduation and full-time onsite attendance.
  • Job descriptions emphasize deployment, integration, and stakeholder collaboration over language breadth.
  • Resumes should show real environments, users, and measurable outcomes, not frameworks.

Key Takeaways

  • Palantir alone: the FDSE internship role accepts students graduating in December 2027 or Spring 2028, while the new grad role accepts students graduating in December 2026 or Spring 2027.
  • Google London's FDE internship was posted on October 2, 2026, closes on November 13, 2026, does not offer visa sponsorship, and requires full-time work for at least 13 weeks.
  • The job descriptions focus on deployment, integration into customers' real environments, and working with multiple stakeholders; Palantir and Applied Intuition require proficiency in at least one programming language.

For FDE internships and new grad roles, the first thing to check is usually the dates, not the skills.

Palantir's Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE, Commercial) internship role in New York only accepts students graduating in December 2027 or Spring 2028. The new grad role, also in the Commercial track, accepts students graduating in December 2026 or Spring 2027.

The two roles at the same company are exactly one academic year apart. Apply to the wrong one, and your application will likely be rejected before anyone even looks at the skills section, no matter how well written it is.

The 2027 recruiting season has already begun. Applied Intuition posted its 2027 FDE new grad role on October 8, 2026; Google London posted its Summer 2027 FDE internship on October 2, 2026, with an application deadline of November 13, 2026.

If you are a current student or about to graduate and want to become a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), the most important skill right now is reading job postings like contracts: figure out which roles fit you, rule out the ones that don't as early as possible, and then write an application that matches the work described in the posting.

When reading a job posting, look for the graduation date first, then the company name and skill requirements. The table below lists five 2027 FDE roles using each posting's original wording.

As the table shows, the students Palantir's internship role accepts graduate exactly one academic year later than those its own new grad role accepts; Google London accepts students in both their penultimate and final years. The graduation dates accepted by the various new grad roles range from December 2026 to Fall 2027.

So to determine whether you qualify, don't look at the year in the title—look at the line in each posting that specifies the graduation window.

Putting Two Candidates into the Table

Imagine two students. The first expects to graduate in December 2027, meaning they are currently in their penultimate year.

This student qualifies for Palantir's internship role; if they meet the other requirements, they also qualify for Google London's internship. New grad roles like those at C3 AI and Applied Intuition are not yet open to them—applying now would be a waste of effort.

The second expects to graduate in December 2026 and qualifies for the new grad roles at Palantir and C3 AI. Applied Intuition specifies March 2027, which doesn't match this student's timeline. For gaps of a few months like this, rather than guessing, email the recruiting team to ask: an official answer is far more reliable than your own inference.

Students graduating in mid-2027 should read each posting's "Spring" and "Summer" carefully. C3 AI explicitly states Summer/Fall 2027, which is relatively safe; Palantir's "Spring 2027" cutoff is worth confirming with the company.

Beyond Dates, Roles Have Hard Requirements

Matching the graduation date doesn't mean you're through: applications can still get stuck on those few lines at the end of the posting. Google London states clearly that visa sponsorship is not provided for internship candidates. If you need work authorization to work in the UK, this role is not for you, no matter how good your resume is.

The second requirement is scheduling. Google requires interns to work full-time for at least 13 weeks during Summer 2027, so your summer courses or exam schedule must leave that window free. Applied Intuition requires on-site presence 5 days per week. Palantir states upfront that, depending on team and location, travel accounts for approximately 25–50% of the time.

These are not minor details. FDE work is tied to customer sites and customer schedules. If you can't be away for half a month at a time, you should know that before applying, not discover it in the final round of interviews.

What Work Do These Roles Describe?

Read the responsibilities carefully and you'll find these postings use the same set of verbs. Applied Intuition wants FDEs to work directly with customers to deploy and integrate products into real environments.

C3 AI requires designing, developing, and maintaining applications on the company's Agentic AI Platform. Google wants interns to identify recurring patterns and friction points in the field as customers use Google's AI technology stack.

The programming language requirements for these roles are fairly basic. Palantir requires proficiency in at least one language, such as Python, Java, C++, or TypeScript/JavaScript; Applied Intuition requires strong programming skills, such as C++ or Python. The difficulty lies elsewhere: Palantir requires interns to collaborate with stakeholders ranging from end users to executives.

Therefore, the most effective preparation is to pick one language and master it, then build a project with real users using it. This is far more useful than learning several languages and spreading your effort thin.

A small tool built for a club, a lab, or a corner shop—something someone complained about and you had to fix—carries more weight than three independently completed course assignments.

Rewriting a Resume Bullet the FDE Way

Consider a typical student resume bullet: "Built a chatbot using Python and LangChain with high accuracy." This talks about the technology but doesn't say who uses it, what environment it runs in, or what obstacles you overcame.

Rewrite it around four points: whose environment, what was integrated, who uses it, and what change it brought.

For example, if it really was you who did it: "Integrated a Python chatbot into the department's academic affairs office's existing Q&A system; worked with two staff members to identify the 30 most common questions; fixed a PDF parsing bug after the first week of student use." The numbers here are just examples—replace them with your own real data.

This new version maps almost line by line to the job description: integrating into a real environment, collaborating with users, uncovering friction points. In the interview, be ready to walk through this experience in depth: where you initially misunderstood the requirements, and how you later went back to clarify them.

Mistakes that get your application rejected before the interview

One easy mistake is to only look at the year in the title and submit without checking the graduation date. Also, don't wait until spring to start your search: Google London's application deadline was November 13, 2026, only about six weeks after posting. And don't skip the lines about visa status, days on-site, and travel percentage.

A subtler mistake is writing an FDE resume like an SWE resume, listing only frameworks and features. From the verbs used in the job posting—deploy, integrate into real environments, work with customers—you can infer that the resume reader will look for signs that you have put code into someone else's system and followed it through to smooth operation.

If your resume doesn't yet contain such an experience, what you need to do this semester is create one.

Tonight, cross-check your graduation date against every position before even thinking about your resume.
