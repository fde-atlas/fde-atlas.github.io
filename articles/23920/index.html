What is FDSE? Explaining the Differences from FDE, Job Duties, Required Skills, and How to Become One

Original title: FDSEとは？FDEとの違い・仕事内容・必要スキルと目指し方を解説

FDE総研 · 2026-09-03 · 13 min read

Summary

This article from FDE Soken explains FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer), Palantir's official job title for engineers embedded at customer sites who deliver production-quality software themselves. It distinguishes FDSE from the generic term FDE, from Deployment Strategist (who defines business problems), and from product SWEs, SIer upstream SEs, and consultants, arguing FDSE's essence is holding both requirements authority and implementation responsibility. It describes a typical project flow (on-site observation, data integration, early working prototypes, handover), the required breadth across data, frontend, and business communication skills, who thrives or struggles, and practical advice for building relevant experience and preparing for interviews, while cautioning against over-specializing in one platform.

Key points

  • FDSE is Palantir's official title; FDE is the industry-wide generic term.
  • At Palantir, FDSE handles implementation while Deployment Strategists define problems.
  • FDSE's defining trait: sets requirements on-site and writes the code too.
  • Projects run weeks to months, starting with field observation and early prototypes.
  • Success requires breadth from data pipelines to UI plus business translation skills.

What does FDSE stand for, and what kind of role is it?

FDSE is an official job title at Palantir, referring to engineers who embed themselves at customer sites and take responsibility all the way through to implementing production-quality software.

FDSE stands for Forward Deployed Software Engineer. This way of working—an engineer who gathers requirements at the customer's site and writes code on the spot—has long been discussed in Japan's IT industry in terms overlapping with upstream SIer processes or consultant work. FDSE is a role that adds to that way of working "the responsibility of writing production-quality software yourself." The name FDSE spread after Palantir Technologies (founded in 2003, currently headquartered in Denver, Colorado, USA) listed it as one of its main job roles on its careers page.

What is FDSE's official name, and how does Palantir define its role?

FDSE literally translates to "software engineer deployed to the front line," implementing customer-specific solutions on top of Palantir products.

As the name Forward (deployed to the front line) Deployed (positioned) Software Engineer suggests, FDSEs are stationed at customer sites. Palantir's recruiting information describes FDSEs as directly engaging with customers' business challenges and designing and implementing solutions on Palantir's platform. The four Palantir products FDSEs work with are as follows.

ProductPositioning
GothamPlatform centered on government and defense sectors
FoundryPlatform for commercial enterprises
ApolloDeployment infrastructure
AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform)AI platform introduced in 2023

FDSEs build customer-specific data pipelines, ontologies (models of business concepts), and business applications on top of these products.

Note that an FDSE is not "an engineer who accompanies sales." The FDSE as defined by Palantir is not someone who just gives demos. They are someone who delivers something that actually works and brings it to a state where it is used in the field.

Why was FDSE created?

FDSE is a role designed to fill business-specific gaps that generic products cannot cover, with people filling them on-site.

Manufacturing line yield rates, hospital bed management, supply chain parts supply. In these domains, the shape of data and the order of decision-making differ from company to company. Trying to solve everything with standard features alone inevitably leaves business-specific gaps at the end. Filling that remainder with consultant documents does not turn it into software. Putting it on the product team's roadmap means waiting several quarters.

FDSE is the answer to this structural gap. Cover most of it with the platform, and write the rest on-site. For this division of labor to work, the platform must be designed so it "can be extended on the spot." Palantir's Foundry and AIP are built on exactly that premise.

In recent years, AI companies such as OpenAI and Sierra have been recruiting the same type of role under the name "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)." The concept of the role born at Palantir is becoming a common industry term.

How does FDSE differ from FDE and ordinary software engineers?

FDE is a general term for the role type, while FDSE is Palantir's official job title. The biggest difference from other roles lies in the high proportion of implementation work.

Since the terminology is mixed, let's clarify it first. Most of what is written as "FDE" in Japanese job postings and articles uses it as a generic term derived from Palantir's job title. Meanwhile, Palantir's internal job structure has two tracks: FDSE handles implementation, and Deployment Strategist (DS) handles defining business challenges and managing customer relationships.

What is the relationship between FDE and FDSE?

FDE is an industry-wide conceptual term, and FDSE corresponds to the specific job title Palantir defined for the implementation side of that concept.

TermPositioningTypical usage
FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)General term/concept for the roleCross-industry. Used in job postings at AI and SaaS companies as well
FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer)Palantir's official job titleExplicitly carries implementation responsibility. Centered on development on the platform

From a practitioner's perspective, there is little need to worry about the distinction between FDE and FDSE. In the job market, searching for "FDE" will surface both. FDE-type positions in Japan often do not have roles divided as clearly as Palantir's. It is more useful to check the job description for how much implementation the position actually requires.

How does FDSE differ from SWEs, SIers, and consultants?

It is the same as an SWE in that the deliverable is code, but it differs decisively in who finalizes the requirements and where you work.

What makes FDSE unique comes down to being high on both "source of requirements" and "proportion of coding." You hold the authority to decide requirements, yet you cannot escape implementation. This tension is the essence of the FDSE role.

How do Deployment Strategists (DS) and FDSEs divide their work?

The basic model is that the DS poses the questions and the FDSE implements the solutions. However, the actual boundary shifts depending on the scale of the project.

On small projects, it is not uncommon for the FDSE to also take on the DS role. Conversely, on large deployments, multiple FDSEs split up modules, while the DS focuses on aligning outcomes and metrics with the customer's decision-makers.

Practitioners in the field often say that when Japanese companies create FDE-like positions, they sometimes burden a single person with both roles and burn them out. During the launch phase, wearing both hats may be unavoidable. But whether to keep that arrangement as a permanent structure deserves careful consideration.

What does an FDSE do, and how does a single project proceed?

An FDSE handles everything alone, from requirements definition through implementation to adoption support. A single project typically runs for a few weeks to a few months.

What phases does an FDSE go through on a project?

Start with on-site observation, show something working early, and refine it while watching how it is used. This is the basic FDSE approach.

  • Enter the field (day 1 to week 1): Observe the actual work and grasp who operates which systems and how many times. Do not settle for interviews alone.
  • Connect the data (weeks 1 to 3): Bring in scattered data sources such as core systems, Excel files, and sensor logs, and consolidate them into an integrated model. In the Palantir context, this corresponds to defining business concepts as an ontology.
  • Produce a minimal working version (weeks 2 to 6): Prioritize letting on-site staff actually touch it in their own work over polish. The reactions gathered here become the greatest source of information about the requirements.
  • Refinement and handover to operations (phase 3 onward, often spanning several months): Nail down exception handling, permissions, audit logs, and integration with existing business workflows. This is the most unglamorous step, and the one with the most pitfalls.
  • Adoption and withdrawal: Hand operations over to the customer's staff. Continue tracking whether it keeps being used for a period afterward, and if needed, expand to the next challenge.

Note that this flow is not linear. It can actually happen that the moment you show something in phase 3, you realize "the problem we should be solving was different all along." The whole point of showing things early is precisely to discover that as soon as possible.

What code does an FDSE actually write, and what technologies do they work with?

An FDSE moves back and forth alone across both ends: data processing and frontend. Breadth of coverage matters more than depth in a specialty.

Transformation and modelingNormalization into business concepts, quality assurancePySpark, SQL, ontology definitions

Operations and permissionsAccess control, monitoring, CI/CDVarious deployment platforms

A common concern is "Don't you need Palantir-platform-specific knowledge?" However, what is assessed in the selection process is general programming ability and problem-solving skills, on the assumption that Palantir-product-specific practices are learned after joining. The same applies to FDE positions at other companies.

What is the actual workload of an FDSE like? How much travel and on-site work is involved?

An FDSE's main burdens are two: travel to customer sites, and rebuilding context from scratch every time a project changes.

Since the pandemic, combining remote work has advanced, but the on-site share remains high in the initial phases. Moreover, industry knowledge must be rebuilt from scratch with every new project. Moving from manufacturing to healthcare, for example, is intellectually stimulating, but makes it hard to accumulate knowledge.

Multiple practitioners agree that whether you can enjoy this switching cost is the single strongest determinant of FDSE aptitude.

What skills and experience does an FDSE need?

An FDSE needs a three-layer skill set: data processing and application implementation, plus the ability to articulate business processes in words.

What technical skills does an FDSE need?

What is valued in an FDSE is not depth in a single point, but breadth of coverage that lets you carry things through on your own from data all the way to UI.

SkillRequired levelBenchmark for judgment
Programming (Python, etc.)Essential, highCan fully implement business logic on their own
SQL and data modelingEssential, highCan turn messy data into a usable form
Web/UI implementationEssential, medium to highCan build the screens the field uses themselves
Cloud and infrastructureMediumUnderstands deployment and permission design
Distributed processing (Spark, etc.)A strong plusCan be entrusted with large-scale data processing
LLM/AI utilizationRising in importance in recent yearsCan design how to embed it into business processes

This does not mean "shallow but broad." The premise is being able to write production-grade work in any of these areas, and on top of that, covering a wide range. That is the required standard.

What non-technical skills does an FDSE need?

The ability to translate the customer's words into specifications, and to make decisions that move things forward even when decision-makers are not in the room, greatly shape an FDSE's results.

Specifically, there are three:

  • Uncovering constraints: Find the constraints behind tasks that on-site staff dismiss as "a hassle."
  • Building consensus: When conflicting requests arise, propose priorities and secure agreement.
  • Making provisional judgments: Make provisional decisions before all the information is in, structuring things so they can be corrected later.

When these three come together, results differ greatly even with the same technical skill. English proficiency needs to be at a practical level if you aim for Palantir itself or foreign AI companies. On the other hand, many job postings for FDE-like positions at domestic companies do not require it.

Who is suited to FDSE, and who will struggle?

People who find value in the customer's front lines are suited to FDSE. Those who want to prioritize deep technical work will face significant friction.

Suited peoplePeople likely to struggle
Want to directly see how and by whom what they built is usedWant to nurture an architecture over the long term
Can enjoy learning business knowledge for its own sakeWant to hone their skills within a code review culture
Are comfortable making decisions amid uncertaintyWant to settle into and engage deeply with a single system

For people who fit the right column, the FDSE cycle of building per project and then handing off may feel unsatisfying. This is not a matter of superiority or inferiority; it is a difference in what you want to receive from your work as a reward.

What should you start with to become an FDSE?

The shortest route is to first take on work at your current job that involves talking directly with customers, and consolidate implementation and business understanding into a single track record.

What should you do to create FDE-like work at your current job?

Even before changing jobs, there are three FDSE elements you can reproduce at your current workplace. You want to shape them into achievements you can talk about in the selection process.

  • Sit in on requirements gathering and write the specifications yourself: Instead of waiting for requirements passed down through sales or PdM, create a channel to touch first-hand information directly.
  • Find and solve one business problem in-house yourself: Turn a department's manual work into a tool and see it through to actual use. Small scale is fine.
  • Go through the data pipeline from upstream to downstream once: Having experience handling ingestion, transformation, and visualization alone, without dividing the work, is a strong point.

What is evaluated in FDSE selection, and how should you prepare?

Beyond coding ability, whether you can articulate how you redefined a customer's problem is the deciding factor in the selection process.

Salary levels vary greatly by company and region. In postings by US companies, salary ranges are sometimes disclosed in accordance with laws and regulations. Always confirm the figures in the latest job posting of the company you are applying to. FDE Soken does not present speculative figures here.

What careers do former FDSEs move on to?

Former FDSEs who have both field knowledge and product implementation skills can easily move into PdM, architecture, or entrepreneurship.

After a few years as an FDSE, you become someone who can speak both "the language of business" and "the language of implementation." This combination is rare and lends itself well to product management, solution architecture, launching new in-house businesses, or starting your own company. In fact, in the US, there is a continuing stream of examples of Palantir alumni launching startups that bring in FDE-style thinking.

On the other hand, if you accumulate experience that depends on a specific platform, your market value risks becoming skewed. FDE Soken recommends consciously maintaining implementation experience with general-purpose technology stacks. Even if you cannot choose the tools used on a project, you should be able to choose what you learn on your own.
