Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer Interview Experience (Rejected, Mar 2025)

Top Tech Transition · 2026-07-08 · 2 min read

Summary

A mid-level candidate recounts being rejected from Palantir's Forward Deployed Software Engineer (Commercial) role in March 2025. The process had four rounds: a headhunter-led recruiter screen, a technical round (design a hobby meetup system, then implement a core feature), a behavioral phone screen, and an onsite with a learning round (solving problems in an obscure language from its documentation) and a decomposition round (design Yelp). The author attributes failure to asking questions in the learning round that suggested needing excessive handholding. Interviews favored practical coding over LeetCode, prioritizing working solutions. The candidate's prospective team built peak-hour detection for an LA power utility to reduce wildfire risk—impactful work, though Palantir's government and defense projects remain controversial.

Key points

  • Four rounds: recruiter screen, technical, phone screen, onsite.
  • Technical round: design a hobby meetup system, implement a feature.
  • Learning round failure: questions implied needing too much handholding.
  • Decomposition round: design Yelp; Palantir values opinionated candidates.
  • Team worked on grid peak detection for an LA utility to cut wildfire risk.

Interview process

Technical interview was enjoyable since it was less so rigorous LeetCode-style problems, and more practical coding to implement particular features. Prioritize a working solution over an unfinished, optimized code.

Interview rounds · 4

  • 1

    Recruiter screen

    Behavioral
    • Q1. Background questions (your past experiences, projects, etc)Follow-up questions
      • This is done by the 3rd party headhunter that eventually referred me to a Palantir recruiter.
  • 2

    Technical round

    CodingDecomposition
    • Q1. Design a system to help internal company teams arrange events/meetups for hobbies and interests.
    • Q2. Write the code to implement one of the core features that you have specified in the design
  • 3

    Phone screen

    Behavioral
    • Q1. Background questions (your past experiences, projects, etc.)
  • 4

    Final / onsite round

    CodingDecomposition
    • Q1. Learning round: given a documentation of an obscure coding language, solve problems.How they answeredYou can't really prepare much for this one. Show up with the right attitude, and ask the right questions. Be careful on asking questions that can make you seem like you need a lot of handholding / guidance to solve the problem -- that caused me to fail this round.
    • Q2. Design Yelp.How they answeredThis was a decomposition question. I went back and forth with the interviewer and created a basic system design to implement Yelp. We discussed technical considerations, and add more layers of complexities as we progress. Be opinionated and be ready to support your design choices.Palantir values opinionated people.

Tips from the candidate

Be careful with the questions you ask during the learning round. High-level, clarifying questions are okay. But make sure your questions dont make you come across like you need too much handholding.

Company culture

I applied for FDSE - Commercial path, and was connected to a team working with the LA power utility company at that time. They were developing a system to detect peak hours for the electric grid so they can adjust the power delivery to minimize the risk of forest fire when the grids are overloaded during a dry season.

The mission felt impactful and beneficial to the community surrounding it.

The downside is that there are other parts of the company that deals with more government / defense work, that is more on the controversial side.
