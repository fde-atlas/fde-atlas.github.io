Summary

This article from FDE总研 explains the role of the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), a position originated at Palantir and now spreading to OpenAI and startups. An FDE works on-site with customers, single-handedly handling problem discovery, requirements definition, implementation, and business advancement. The article contrasts FDEs with software engineers, SES (client-site staffing), consultants, and product managers, noting FDEs spend less than half their time coding and are evaluated on customer outcomes. Drawing on practitioner experience from VACAN Technologies' Tamaki, it outlines four key skills and career paths toward solution architect, PM, or executive roles.

  • FDE originated at Palantir and has spread to OpenAI and startups.
  • FDEs handle problem discovery through business advancement alone, on-site.
  • Unlike SWEs, FDEs code less than half the time and are judged on customer outcomes.
  • FDEs differ from SES by deciding what to build and owning business results.
  • Key skills: problem definition, tech-business crossover, rapid prototyping, trust building.

What is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)?

An FDE is an engineering role originating from Palantir, where the engineer is stationed at the customer's workplace and single-handedly handles everything from problem discovery to implementation and business advancement.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is not an engineer who waits at the office for a spec document to arrive. They embed themselves in the customer's workplace to uncover real problems and solve them with software. At FDE Soken, we call this way of working "cross-boundary," because the FDE doesn't stop at implementation but single-handedly handles problem discovery, requirements definition, and driving the initiative as a business. If you'd like to learn via video, please watch FDE Seminar Video (What is an FDE? Differences from consulting and system development).

Most software development assumes division of labor: customer requests are first compiled into a requirements document, and engineers who receive it implement the software. FDEs eliminate this wall of division of labor, observing problems in the same place as the customer and assembling solutions on the spot. As a result, while being engineers, FDEs work in a manner closer to consultants or business owners.

What is the origin and definition of FDE?

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, meaning "a front-line engineer stationed at the customer's workplace."

"Forward" refers to the customer's actual workplace itself, and "Deployed" refers to the engineer being stationed there and working alongside them. An FDE is not in a position to receive specifications behind the development team. They place themselves in the same workplace as the customer, standing at the front line of the problems.

What FDEs handle is not requirements organized in advance, but unorganized problems buried in the workplace. The essence of FDE—"stepping into the field"—is directly reflected in the name Forward Deployed.

What does an FDE do?

An FDE's job is to continuously cycle through four stages—problem discovery, requirements definition, implementation, and business advancement—single-handedly at the customer's workplace.

Under the traditional division-of-labor structure, these four stages tend to be split among different people. FDEs connect all of them alone, minimizing the gaps in understanding that occur during handoffs between stages.

StageWhat the FDE handles
Problem discoveryEmbed into the customer's workplace and dig up the real problems behind surface-level requests
Requirements definitionDecide "what should be built" on-site with the customer, without waiting for a spec document
ImplementationUse generative AI to quickly build things on the spot and align understanding with the customer
Business advancementDon't stop once the feature is complete; carry through to solving the customer's problems and delivering business results

The value of an FDE lies not in expertise in a particular stage, but in driving all four stages through to the end without interruption.

Palantir, which established FDE, and its spread

FDE is a role systematized by US-based Palantir in the 2010s. In recent years, it has spread to OpenAI and various startups.

Palantir Technologies established the FDE role as a way of working that deeply engages with customers' complex business data and workplace problems. While FDE originated at Palantir, it has now spread to OpenAI and various startups, and is becoming no longer exclusive to Palantir. For more on Palantir itself, see What is Palantir?; for a comparison of the FDE roles at Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic, see What is an OpenAI Forward Deployed Engineer? and What is an Anthropic FDE?.

FDE Soken believes that behind this spread lies a mindset that does not treat software deployment as the goal, but regards solving the customer's problems themselves as the outcome.

How is FDE positioned in Japan?

In Japan, against the backdrop of growing demand for data utilization and business DX, FDE hiring has been expanding since the mid-2020s.

Tamaki, who supervised this article (Representative Director of VACAN Technologies), is one of those who has worked in FDE practice in Japan. At parent company VACAN Inc., Tamaki launched the crowd-visualization platform "vCore" and consistently handled everything from on-site problem discovery to architecture design, implementation, and business advancement.

Organizing Tamaki's practice in the order of "situation → judgment → result → limitations," it looks like this:

StageTamaki's practice (VACAN)
SituationReceiving a consultation from a customer saying, "We want to introduce this product"
JudgmentNot taking those words at face value, grasping the real problem behind them on the spot, and quickly building a prototype using generative AI
ResultQuickly aligned understanding with the customer. This type of consultation, which a typical project structure could not handle, became the background for the founding of VACAN Technologies
LimitationsThis is one practitioner's experience and cannot be directly applied to conditions such as industry, company size, or English proficiency

Tamaki cites "responsiveness" as a characteristic unique to FDEs: the ability to grasp the problem behind surface-level requests on the spot and quickly build and show something tangible.

What is the difference between an FDE and a software engineer?

Comparing along FDE Soken's four axes, the biggest difference is that FDEs start from problem definition and keep implementation to less than half of their working hours.

A typical software engineer focuses on implementing given specifications. In contrast, an FDE starts by deciding "what should be built" themselves at the customer's workplace. Whether or not they handle this stage is the boundary between FDEs and traditional engineers.

How do FDEs and SWEs differ in how they use their working hours?

FDEs keep implementation to less than half of their working hours, using the rest for customer interviews, requirements organization, and relationship building.

Even by the practical standards of Mr. Tamaki at VACAN Technologies, FDEs spend more time on on-site customer interviews, requirements gathering, and relationship building than on pure implementation. In reality, implementation takes up less than half of their working hours.

However, short implementation time does not mean technical skills are unnecessary. For FDEs, implementation ability is the foundational base. FDE is a role that uses that technical skill in combination with problem definition.

How do FDE and SWE differ in customer proximity and success metrics?

FDEs are directly embedded at customer sites and are evaluated not on feature completion, but on solving customer problems and delivering business outcomes.

Typical software engineers interact with customers indirectly through project managers and are evaluated on feature completeness and quality. FDEs go directly into the customer's site and are evaluated on whether problems were solved and whether the business moved forward. How does FDE differ from SES (on-site client staffing)?

Working at the client's site is similar to SES, but FDEs differ in that they decide "what to build" themselves and take responsibility through to problem resolution.

Looking only at the form of "being embedded at the customer's site," FDE resembles domestic SES (on-site client staffing). The difference lies in which phases they handle at the client site and what they are evaluated on. Applying the four axes of the FDE Research Institute, the FDE's starting point is discovering problems at the customer's site, and evaluation is based on solving customer problems and business outcomes. It is not whether they are embedded on-site, but whether they take on everything from problem definition to driving the business forward, that separates FDE from SES.

The differences in contract structure and how they enter the field are organized from a practical perspective in What's the difference between FDE and SES?.

How does FDE differ from consultants, PMs, and SIers?

FDEs take on the roles of consultants and PMs, and go as far as implementing things themselves to move the business forward.

FDEs, with engineering skill as their foundation, take on the roles of consultants and product managers alone. They don't stop at defining problems and setting direction; they build things on the spot and move the business forward. For this reason, FDEs cannot be fully evaluated by technical skill alone or by proposal ability alone.

ConsultantProblem definition and direction-settingImplementing and building things themselves

What skills are required of an FDE?

FDEs need four capabilities: problem definition, cross-domain reach across technology and business, rapid prototyping, and trust building.

According to Mr. Tamaki's observations at VACAN Technologies, what separates FDEs who grow from those who stall is whether they can bridge technology and business, and whether they can conduct interviews themselves to define problems and build trust with customers. Technical skill to implement to spec alone tends to hit a ceiling; the ability to define the problem itself in the field determines growth.

What distinguishes people who thrive as FDEs from those who don't?

The dividing line is whether they can bridge technology and business, define problems themselves, and build trust with customers.

Mr. Tamaki organized the differences he has observed in the field into three perspectives. People who match the right-hand column may not be suited to FDE at this point.

PerspectivePeople who thrive as FDEsPeople who stall as FDEs
Cross-domain reachCan bridge technology and businessStay within either technology or business
Handling of problemsConduct interviews and define problems themselvesRely solely on technical skill to implement to spec
Customer relationshipsCan build trust with customersFail to deepen trust with customers

All three perspectives concern not technical skill itself, but how that skill is used in the field.

Why are problem definition and interviewing skills the top priority?

Because if you cannot define the problem correctly, no matter how strong your implementation skills, you will build something off target.

A customer's stated request, "We want to introduce this product," is in most cases only a small part of the real problem surfacing. FDEs don't take these words at face value; they question on-site why it is needed and uncover the real problem. Problem definition through interviewing is the starting point of all FDE work.

How do cross-domain skills and prototyping work in the field?

By connecting technology and business alone and building things on the spot with generative AI, you can quickly resolve gaps in understanding with the customer.

Mr. Tamaki himself has driven the business forward without being confined to role boundaries, from architecture design to project promotion. Staying only on the technical side tends to produce "things that can be built but won't sell," while staying only on the business side tends to produce "things you want to sell but can't build." Adding the agility of showing prototypes on the spot with generative AI aligns understanding faster than discussing with words alone.

How do the four skills work together?

The four capabilities are not independent of each other; they continuously operate in the field as a single cycle from problem definition to trust building.

Skill areaSpecific content
Problem definition abilityIdentifying the real problem behind the surfaced request through interviews
Technology × business cross-domain reachHandling both architecture design and business promotion
Rapid prototypingUse generative AI to shape ideas on the spot and align understanding
Building trustWork alongside customers on-site and build long-term relationships

The deeper the trust, the more customers will share their real challenges, which also improves the accuracy of the next problem definition. FDEs are professionals who repeat this cycle at customer sites.

How do FDE career paths expand?

FDE career paths expand toward solution architect, PdM, business leader, and executive roles.

The problem-definition and cross-boundary skills gained as an FDE overlap with the skills required in more upstream positions. Therefore, being an FDE is not the endpoint of an engineering career but a stepping stone toward broader roles.

What positions come after FDE?

After FDE, there are examples of moving into solution architect, product manager, business leader, and executive roles.

Next positionHow FDE experience is leveraged
Solution architectLeveraging cross-boundary experience of technology and business in overall system design
Product managerLeveraging on-site problem-definition experience in product direction-setting
Business leader/executiveLeveraging experience launching businesses from problems in steering the entire business

Mr. Tamaki also accumulated FDE-like work at Vacan, Inc. before becoming Representative Director of VACAN Technologies. This is one example showing how FDE experience can lead to executive roles.

Why is FDE a starting point for business talent?

Because the experience FDEs perform daily of "launching businesses from problems" has the same structure as the experience required of business leaders.

What FDEs do routinely is identify on-site problems and connect them to solutions that work as businesses. This skill does not depend on specific products or technology stacks, so it remains useful even when the domain changes.
