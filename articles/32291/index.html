How the European FDE Firms in This Guide Were Chosen

Reveneau · 2026-10-21 · 14 min read

Summary

This methodology page explains how Reveneau selected the seventeen firms in its guide to European forward deployed engineering firms. Each firm had to claim it places engineers inside client teams, name a European office, and sell that work for clients' own systems. Claims were verified against company registers in Poland, France and the UK, plus Anthropic's partner directory of 321 entries. Reveneau and Fluxon appear as stated exceptions with no European office. Nine firms, including ML6, Searce and Xebia, were checked and excluded for specific reasons, and twelve other European firms showed no FDE wording on their homepages. Firm-only outcome figures were omitted, and unconfirmed facts are listed openly.

Key points

  • Three tests: engineers embedded in client teams, a European office, and selling that work to clients.
  • Company registers in Poland, France and the UK confirmed legal names, addresses and ownership.
  • Reveneau and Fluxon are listed first despite having no named European office.
  • Nine firms, including ML6 and Searce, were checked and excluded with reasons given.
  • Self-reported outcome figures were excluded; most named clients lack independent confirmation.

Every firm on the list passed three tests. It says, in its own words or job ads, that it places engineers inside a client's team. It names an office in Europe on its own pages or in a company register, with two stated exceptions. And it sells that work to clients, for the clients' own systems. This page lists the sources, explains how each kind was treated, names the firms that were checked and left out with the reason for each, and lists the facts that could not be confirmed on 21 October 2026.

Published October 21, 2026. Editorial.

Key takeaways

  • A firm had to say it places engineers inside client teams, name an office in Europe, and sell that work to clients. Reveneau and Fluxon name no European office, and their entries say so.
  • Company registers in Poland, France and the UK were used to confirm addresses, legal names and, for Faculty, the owner. A firm's own site and its directory text were treated as the firm's own claims.
  • ML6, Sigma Software, Searce, kiba solutions, Xebia and By The People Technologies were checked and left out, each for a stated reason.
  • Outcome figures that only a firm states, such as percentages for time saved and value delivered, were left out. Staff counts, founding years and named clients are given as each firm's own claims.

This page explains how the seventeen firms in the list of European FDE firms were chosen, what sources were used, and what was left out. Every source was opened on 21 October 2026. The Polish and French registers stamped their replies 22 October in local time, because Europe was already on the next day.

The three tests

1. The firm places its own engineers inside the client's team, in its own words. The strongest evidence is a service page that sells forward deployed engineers, such as Netguru's, 10Clouds' or SFEIR's. Some firms use the words only when hiring. accilium, Polynom and Faculty use the forward deployed title in job ads only, and STX Next uses it as a job title in a blog post. Netlight uses the words only in the text it wrote for Anthropic's directory. Each of those entries says so, so you can judge it.

2. The firm names an office in Europe. It counts when the firm's own pages, or an independent company register, name a European city. Grid Dynamics, Distyl AI and Tribe AI are US firms that pass on their own pages. Reveneau and Fluxon are the two exceptions: Reveneau lists offices in San Francisco and Vancouver, and Fluxon lists open roles located in Poland and Portugal with no European office named. Both entries say this plainly.

3. The firm sells engineers to clients. Software and model companies, such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Salesforce, also employ forward deployed engineers, but those engineers work with users of the company's own products. They appear in this guide as context only.

The registers

The Polish National Court Register, known as KRS, is published by the Ministry of Justice. It gave the legal name, registered seat and date of entry for Netguru S.A., STX Next S.A. and 10Clouds Financial Institutions sp. z o.o. [1]. The Ministry of Finance's VAT register, a list of companies registered to charge value added tax, confirmed 10Clouds' Warsaw address.

The French company register at annuaire-entreprises.data.gouv.fr gave the head office address and creation date for DEVOTEAM, SFEIR and HACKAJOO, whose trade name is POLYNOM [2]. Each French company has a SIREN number, a nine-digit identity number. The register also gives an employee band code for each company. This guide read code 51 as 2,000 to 4,999 employees, code 41 as 500 to 999, and code 12 as 20 to 49, from the register's own list of codes, for the reference year 2024. The band covers only the French company in the register, so Devoteam's band describes its French unit and leaves out the rest of the group. Polynom was matched on its trade name and on the exact street address it publishes.

UK Companies House gave Faculty Science Limited's registered office, incorporation date and its owner, Accenture plc [3]. It also gave the record for Iden Ventures Ltd, the company named in Brdge's footer, including its activity code, a standard number that describes what a company does [4].

No register was opened for accilium in Austria, Enjins in the Netherlands, Twistag in Portugal or Netlight in Sweden. The Dutch register's search page needs scripts to run, and the others were not opened. Their addresses come from their own sites.

Anthropic's directory

Anthropic's Claude Partner Network directory was read on 21 October 2026. It held 321 entries, 312 of them with a tier: 256 Select, 40 Preferred and 16 Global Premier, with 9 entries showing no tier [5]. The page was the same, byte for byte, as a copy saved on 20 October 2026. Each firm was searched for by name in all 321 entries. Anthropic sets the tier. Each partner writes its own one-line description and ticks its own office regions, so those are treated as the firm's own words. SFEIR's own offer page still calls it a Select partner, while the directory shows Preferred, and this guide uses the directory.

Facts this guide could not confirm

  • Accenture's own announcement that its purchase of Faculty was complete. BusinessCloud and Companies House were used instead.
  • Atomico's own figure for AI funding in Europe. It is given as IO+ reports it.
  • Street addresses for Netlight, Distyl AI's London office, Tribe AI's Lisbon office, Brdge's London office and accilium's Berlin office.
  • A headcount for Faculty.
  • Independent confirmation of any client named by Fluxon, Netguru, 10Clouds, STX Next, Devoteam, Polynom, Brdge, Enjins, Twistag or Netlight.
  • Whether OpenAI's purchase of Tomoro has closed.

The order of the list

Reveneau is listed first and Fluxon second. The firms based in Europe come next, grouped by country: Poland (Netguru, 10Clouds, STX Next), France (Devoteam, SFEIR, Polynom), the UK (Faculty, Brdge), Austria (accilium), the Netherlands (Enjins), Portugal (Twistag) and Sweden (Netlight). The US firms with a European office follow: Grid Dynamics, Distyl AI and Tribe AI. No firm gets a score or a rating, and the pages do not compare named firms on speed, price or quality.

Reveneau's entry gives only its present practice, in its own words. Reveneau says AI writes 100 percent of its code and that every change has to pass a large eval suite, a set of automated tests written from the specification, before release. It says Jev grades that suite and makes it run ten times faster than its previous grader did. It says it uses AI in place of hiring more engineers and puts that saving into clients' prices, and that it takes responsibility for the whole project through production and after release. Its offices are at 435 Utah Street, San Francisco, and Floor 33, 909 Mainland Street, Vancouver, and it lists no office in Europe. Anthropic's directory did not list Reveneau when all 321 entries were searched on 20 October 2026 [5]. Its forward deployed engineers page has the rest.

Fluxon's entry uses Fluxon's own pages and its Forbes company page. Forbes gives 200 employees as of March 2026, and Fluxon's own careers page says "300+ builders" [8]. Both are shown because they disagree.

How to repeat a check yourself

  • Open the firm's own service page. Look for words that offer engineers inside your team, and note whether they appear on a page that sells to clients or only in a job ad.
  • Find the company in its country's register. In Poland, search the court register by name. In France, search the company register. In the UK, search Companies House, and open the persons with significant control tab to see the owners.
  • Compare the address and the dates. If the register's date is later than the firm's founding year, ask the firm which company will sign your contract.
  • Search Anthropic's directory by name if the firm claims a partner tier, and compare the tier with what the firm's own page says.
  • Ask for a client you can call. No client named by most of these firms was confirmed by an outside source.

The questions to ask a European FDE firm turn these checks into a printable list. The US guide explains its own method in how the FDE firms in that guide were chosen. To discuss a project, contact Reveneau.

Common questions

Why were company registers used to check the European firms?

A register entry is kept by a government body, so it is independent of the firm. The Polish National Court Register, the French company register and UK Companies House confirmed legal names, addresses, creation dates and, for Faculty, the owner. Where a register date differed from a firm's own founding year, both dates are shown.

Why are Reveneau and Fluxon on a European list without a European office?

They are listed first and second, and both sell engineers who work inside a client's team. Each entry says plainly where the firm is: Reveneau lists offices in San Francisco and Vancouver, and Fluxon lists open roles located in Poland and Portugal with no office named. A buyer can then judge the distance.

Why is ML6 left out of the list?

ML6 is an AI engineering firm active in Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany, and Anthropic's directory lists it at the Select tier. Its Forward Deployed Engineer job ad, read on 21 October 2026, is for work on its own platform, Unum. This guide lists firms that place engineers on the client's own systems, so ML6 was counted as borderline and left out.

Which European technology firms were checked and found to have no FDE wording?

Twelve: Datatonic, Artefact, Fuzzy Labs, Kainos, Satalia, Merantix Momentum, Appsilon, Theodo, Scott Logic, Codurance, Zartis and Godel. Their homepages, opened on 21 October 2026, had no forward deployed wording. Zartis mentions embedded AI specialists for training, and Godel sells its own Godel Factory. This guide did not read their other pages, so ask them directly.

How does this guide treat software companies that hire FDEs in Europe?

As context only. Mistral AI, OpenAI, Anthropic and Salesforce sell their own software or AI models, so any forward deployed engineers they employ work with users of their own products. Computing.es reported on 20 October 2026 that Salesforce's new Madrid office will house a team of Forward Deployed Engineers working with clients. This guide lists firms that sell engineers to clients for the clients' own systems.

What is a SIREN number in the French company register?

A SIREN number is the nine-digit identity number that every French company has. This guide used it to identify each French firm in the register: DEVOTEAM is 402968655, SFEIR is 350142071, and HACKAJOO, whose trade name is POLYNOM, is 830106381. You can search the register yourself at annuaire-entreprises.data.gouv.fr using a company's name or its number.

How did this guide read the employee bands in the French register?

The register gives a code for each company's number of employees, and this guide read each code from the register's own list. Code 51 means 2,000 to 4,999 employees, code 41 means 500 to 999, and code 12 means 20 to 49, for the reference year 2024. A band covers only the French company in the register.

Why does the guide leave out Netguru's outcome figures for its clients?

Netguru's FDE page gives outcome figures for work at ARC Europe and Merck KGaA. Only Netguru states them, and neither client's own statement was opened for this guide, so the figures could not be checked. The same rule left out Brdge's counts of projects and value delivered, 10Clouds' market figures and STX Next's client count.

Which office addresses could the European guide not confirm?

No street address was published, on the pages read, for Netlight's offices, Distyl AI's London office, Tribe AI's Lisbon office, Brdge's London office or accilium's Berlin office. Brdge's company is registered in Uxbridge. For each of these firms, the city comes from the firm's own site, so ask for the address before you plan a visit.

Why are the European firms grouped by country after Reveneau and Fluxon?

Grouping by the country of the headquarters lets a reader find the firms based in their own country: Poland, France, the UK, Austria, the Netherlands, Portugal and Sweden. The US firms with a European office, Grid Dynamics, Distyl AI and Tribe AI, come last. No firm gets a score, and named firms are not compared on speed, price or quality.

How is a Polish firm identified in the National Court Register?

Each company in the register has a KRS number. Netguru S.A. is 0000745671, STX Next S.A. is 0000967074, and 10Clouds Financial Institutions is 0000996025. This guide opened each entry through the Ministry of Justice's open data service, which returns the legal name, the registered seat and the date the company entered the register.

More in Cost and method

What forward deployed engineering costs in Europe

No firm in this guide publishes a rate card, a day rate or a price list for forward deployed engineers. A few describe how they charge: Netguru bills by the hour, Polynom ties its operated service to realised value, and SFEIR shows one figure on its homepage without explaining it. The closest public numbers are official pay figures. The UK's Office for National Statistics gives a median of £56,914 a year for full-time software developers in 2025, and Statistics Sweden a median of SEK 53,500 a month. This page explains those figures, why a firm's rate is higher, and what a quote should list.

Questions to ask a European FDE firm before you sign

This checklist is for the first meeting with any forward deployed engineering firm that will work for a company in Europe. It has ten parts: the firm and its owners, the company that signs, the engineers, hours and travel, where your data is kept and who can see it, testing, platforms and licences, price and invoices, handover, and partner claims. Each question comes with what a clear answer contains and a fact from this guide that shows why it matters. Print it, send it to every firm before the meeting, and compare the written answers side by side.
