Day 37: User research and customer cloud | AI FDE Course

Plank · 2026-10-25 · 10 min read

Summary

Day 37 of Plank's AI FDE Course focuses on user research and handing WorkBot to a customer's cloud environment. Students produce a release checklist, a one-page security review document, and a research plan, then run five usability sessions. Since no customer staff were available, AI agents played the five receiving roles: platform engineer, operations lead, security reviewer, on-call engineer, and finance manager. Observations become findings with evidence, severity, and a proposed change. In the reference run, the deployer hit missing or misordered Google Cloud APIs and service agents; the security reviewer found the questionnaire claimed controls in the present tense that were only written, not applied. The top three findings were fixed same-day; the rest went to the backlog.

Key points

  • A delivery test has someone else deploy WorkBot from the runbook while you observe silently
  • Five usability sessions used agents playing the customer's five receiving roles
  • Findings must name behavior, evidence, and consequence, unlike opinions
  • The reference run surfaced missing or misordered Google Cloud APIs and service agents
  • Top three findings shipped same day; the rest were ticketed

Full text

The release checklist

Before anyone else touches WorkBot, check what must hold for a customer to rely on it. A checklist turns that into items you can mark done, planned or open, each with the place it was checked.

Study first

  • Learn what must hold before WorkBot serves a customer. The evals and the load test pass on the release, a bad release rolls back, and the system can be recovered from code. Secrets, encryption, backups, monitoring, spend alerts and isolation are in place. Runbooks exist, retention is enforced, a security review pack is ready, and an owner is on call.
  • Read the launch checklist in the chapter on reliable product launches from Google's Site Reliability Engineering book. Each area of its checklist, from dependencies and capacity to failure modes and rollout, has questions and the action items they lead to.

Build

  • Write
    docs/release-checklist.md
    , marking each item done, planned or open with the place it was checked. When an item needs a cloud account, say so. In the reference run, the checklist ran against the local cluster from Days 35 and 36, recreated from nothing by the pipeline's scripts.
  • Close the gaps the checklist finds in code. Add anuptime check on 
    /api/health
    and a monthly budget with alerts at 50, 90 and 100%. Put both in 
    infra/gcp
    , and assert them in its tests.

Done when

  • Every item is done or names exactly what it waits for.
