Summary

This introductory article from DeFactory's career media explains four common software development approaches—waterfall, agile, spiral, and prototype development—covering each method's process, pros and cons, and suitable use cases. Waterfall suits large, stable-spec projects; agile fits frequently updated services like apps and web products; spiral development prioritizes quality through repeated prototypes and client feedback; prototype development works for novel concepts or clients new to software development. It also notes the rise of no-code development tools, especially in agile workflows, and closes with promotion of DeFactory's development support services.

Key points

  • Waterfall offers easy schedule management but makes mid-project spec changes costly.
  • Agile iterates quickly per feature, enabling flexibility and lower costs but weaker progress visibility.
  • Spiral development refines quality via repeated prototypes and client feedback, at the risk of longer timelines.
  • Prototype development suits new-concept products and clients with limited development experience.
  • No-code tools are a growing trend, particularly within agile development.

There are various software development methods; here we introduce four representative ones. Let's take a detailed look at each one's characteristics, advantages and disadvantages, and suitable situations.

Waterfall development is a method in which the development process is defined in detail from start to finish, and development proceeds according to that flow. It is called "waterfall" because, like water falling from top to bottom in a waterfall, development follows the defined steps.

In waterfall development, detailed procedures are defined for processes such as the following:

1. Planning 2. Design 3. Implementation 4. Testing 5. Release

Development proceeds according to the predefined procedures, and each process is basically reviewed; once approval is obtained from the entire team, including the client, the project moves on to the next process.

A major advantage of waterfall development is that schedule management in software development is easy.

Since the processes are defined in advance, tasks are clear—such as what stage the project is at and what needs to be done—making it easy to share progress with the client as well.

Furthermore, because development can proceed carefully, it also leads to high-quality deliverables.

A major disadvantage of waterfall development is that, precisely because the development processes are defined in detail, specification changes during development are difficult to make.

For example, even if the schedule shows the project is already about 60% complete, if rework becomes necessary, the team must return to an earlier stage and re-verify each process.

As a result, the workload up to release increases significantly.

The greatest characteristic of waterfall development is the ease of schedule management. Therefore, it is a method suited to large-scale projects requiring strict schedule management, such as when a large number of programmers are involved in development.

It is also suitable for developing products that require high stability and where specification changes are basically not made. Specific examples include software built into communication systems and home appliances.

Agile development is a development method that has become mainstream in recent years, in which development cycles are repeated quickly on a per-feature basis of the product.

"Agile" means "nimble or quick," and in software development it can be said to mean proceeding speedily.

For example, if a single product has various features, the aforementioned processes of "planning, design, implementation, and testing" are carried out for each feature.

In agile development, this PDCA cycle is repeated at high speed for each feature, with repeated trial and error, allowing the product to approach completion speedily.

In agile development, a detailed plan is deliberately not created; work begins with the highest-priority requirements once minimal alignment with the client has been achieved.

Since no plan is fixed, a major advantage is that it is easy to respond flexibly even if specification changes occur midway.

Also, in agile development, you can review each completed feature without waiting for the entire product to be finished.

And if there are improvements to be made at that point, they can be reflected immediately, and since it does not affect the development process of other features, development is ultimately possible with minimal workload.

Being able to reduce workload also directly leads to cost savings.

Agile development's advantages are its speed and flexibility, but on the other hand, its disadvantage is that schedule management is difficult.

Because it is fluid, specification changes tend to occur, and it is difficult to clearly understand what stage the project is at and its progress status.

In contrast to waterfall development, agile development is suited to developing products where services are updated one after another and new features are constantly required.

Specific examples include smartphone apps, games, and web services.

To maximize the advantages of agile development, it is important that an experienced manager capable of judging priorities and managing risks leads and coordinates the project.

Also, engineers who can make decisions and act proactively while valuing collaboration are indispensable. However, there may be cases where securing talent is difficult despite a situation requiring development in a short period.

Spiral development, like agile development, is a method in which processes such as planning, design, implementation, and testing are repeated for each product feature, with development proceeding while receiving feedback as needed.

Spiral development is very similar to agile development in that it advances development by repeating the PDCA cycle for each feature.

However, in spiral development, a prototype is created during development, feedback is received from the client, and development is performed again... repeating this cycle in a spiral fashion.

These processes are carried out many times over a short period, gradually completing the ideal product the client is looking for.

Agile development is characterized by its speed and flexibility, but development periods are generally short, which can make it difficult to bring product quality up to the ideal level in a single pass.

Spiral development, on the other hand, involves repeated alignment with the client based on prototypes, so it is characterized by the ability to prioritize quality throughout development.

Since a prototype is created first and then refined down to the details while listening to the client's opinions, the advantage of spiral development is that it can achieve a high level of completeness.

In addition, because the client can review a prototype close to the release state rather than relying on verbal descriptions alone, misunderstandings with the client are less likely to occur, helping prevent fatal rework.

In spiral development, completeness is improved by creating prototypes repeatedly, so the final form tends to be unclear at the initial stage. As a result, as development progresses, development hours and costs may ultimately exceed expectations.

As mentioned above, the advantage of spiral development is that it maximizes quality. Therefore, it is a method relatively well suited to large-scale projects where quality should be prioritized over development hours.

Also, since development can proceed while receiving client feedback as needed, it can be effective when the client is not familiar with software development.

However, in any case, the development period may become long, so it is important to consider risks such as increased burden on engineers and difficulty seeing the goal.

Prototype development is a method in which a prototype is created first, before working out the details of programming and proceeding with development.

The prototype, in a skeletal state, is shared with the client, and it is revised each time based on concrete reviews and requests, gradually moving closer to the final form.

Prototype development emphasizes the user experience of the client, making it easy to flexibly incorporate requests and leading to early bug detection, so wasteful rework is unlikely to occur.

In addition, since development proceeds with the prototype shared from a very early stage, even if the final form is not envisioned at first, the product can gradually take shape and become the ideal product.

The disadvantage of prototype development is that it is difficult to create a development plan, since the ideal form is unknown at the start. As with spiral development, costs and development hours may also exceed expectations.

In addition, since various improvements are made after actually experiencing the product, the final product may end up quite different from the original concept.

Because development hours are unclear, it is not well suited to projects where deadlines must be strictly met or where development must be completed in a short period.

Prototype development is a method suited to cases where the client has little knowledge or experience in software development, such as when it is unclear whether development is technically feasible or when many aspects are undetermined, as well as to the development of products with new concepts.

It is also well suited to building software where user experience is particularly important, such as payment systems.

Waterfall, agile, spiral, and prototype development explained so far are representative development methods in software development.

In software development, it was a given that development proceeded through programming.

However, as a recent trend, methods of developing without programming are increasing, enabling even staff who cannot program to carry out development.

There is no need to learn programming languages, and with dedicated tools alone, even non-engineers can develop software.

In addition, no-programming development tools come with templates and modules, so one of their features is that they can be operated intuitively even without software development experience.

Since this also helps reduce development hours, no-programming development is attracting particular attention in agile development mentioned above.
