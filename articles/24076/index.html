Summary

This article by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, explains MVP (Minimum Viable Product) development, an approach rooted in Eric Ries's lean startup methodology: release a minimal product quickly, gather user feedback, and iterate. It outlines three benefits (early monetization in new markets, faster development and delivery, reduced development risk), a five-step process (define the problem, set hypotheses, build the MVP, collect quantitative feedback, evaluate and improve), and three cautions (keep a user perspective, secure feedback channels, avoid perfectionism). A case study covers PeecAI, a personalized AI recommendation engine for Shopify stores: the MVP took about four months (roughly three person-months), was offered free to a few committed users, and reached a paid release after about five more months of feature additions.

Key points

  • MVP development follows Eric Ries's lean startup approach: ship minimal, iterate fast.
  • Benefits: early revenue in new markets, faster delivery, lower development risk.
  • Five steps: define problem, set hypotheses, build MVP, gather quantitative feedback, evaluate.
  • Cautions: stay user-focused, build feedback channels, don't aim for perfection.
  • Case PeecAI: MVP in ~4 months, paid release ~5 months later after feedback-driven additions.

MVP development is a development method that uses the "MVP (Minimum Viable Product)" approach of providing the minimum necessary product.

MVP aligns with the "Lean Startup" concept, which aims to quickly launch new businesses under constraints such as limited man-hours and funds, and it spread from the books of American entrepreneur Eric Ries.

Its key feature is that by releasing the product to the market quickly, you can rapidly obtain user feedback and market response.

Also, "waterfall development" is a method that stands in contrast to MVP development.

In waterfall development, development proceeds according to specifications decided in advance, producing deliverables that follow the specifications without the direction wavering midway. However, it has the drawback that it is difficult to make corrections if there are changes to the pre-decided specifications.

Therefore, if you want to release quickly while iterating through trial and error, MVP development is a good choice.

Additionally, a benefit of MVP development is that it allows you to verify the fit and usefulness against market needs. In MVP development, improvements to the minimum necessary product are repeated many times, so you can aim to implement the features users truly want.

There are three main benefits of implementing MVP development. All of them help establish competitive advantages, such as early monetization in new markets and fast-paced development and delivery.

Let's take a look at each of the following items.

By leveraging MVP development, you can expect early monetization in new markets. This is because MVP development has the characteristic of providing only the minimum necessary product.

Especially in new markets, the more man-hours required until release, the higher the likelihood of the market becoming a red ocean. With MVP development, which incorporates user feedback and reactions and makes improvements as needed, you can minimize the man-hours required to bring the product to market.

Another benefit of MVP development is that it enables fast-paced development and delivery. When creating a new product, achieving perfect design and development from the start is extremely difficult.

However, with MVP development, assuming improvements after release, you can bring the product to market with speed.

Another advantage is that you can sensitively capture user needs that shift with the times and incorporate them into the improvement cycle. This makes it possible to refine the service into the form customers truly want, which also helps establish market superiority.

By implementing MVP development, you can minimize risks in product development. If a product developed with enormous man-hours does not match market needs, it may lead to redevelopment or project cancellation.

On the other hand, in MVP development, where the minimum necessary product is provided in advance, costs up to release are kept low, greatly reducing the risk of failure. Even if the product does not match market needs, you can quickly change direction, keeping losses to your company low.

Next, we will introduce the MVP development process in 5 steps.

A free 3-minute diagnosis will tell you whether FDE is a good fit for your company.

First, the key is to clarify the challenges your company needs to solve. By making the awareness of challenges clear, it becomes easier to share the value of new ideas.

Also, by quantifying the scale of the challenges, it becomes easier to grasp the size of the market that can be obtained by providing the product.

In some cases, conducting user interviews to extract the challenges and concerns users face is also an option. There are cases where users can tell you about specific challenges that are not visible from the provider's side.

Next, prepare the hypotheses to be evaluated through MVP development. Specifically, the key is to form hypotheses with perspectives such as "How will the product solve the challenges?" and "What is the value of the product your company provides?"

If the hypotheses are vague, proper evaluation cannot be done, so set them as clearly as possible. It is best if they can be documented in writing so that they can be understood even by people outside the project stakeholders within the company.

In the third step, you begin building the MVP. The key point is to select and implement only the minimum necessary features that the MVP should have.

In some cases, preparing user stories for each product planned for implementation enables more precise MVP development. To maximize the effectiveness of MVP development, it is important to carefully select features before starting development.

Have users actually use the MVP you have built and gather their feedback. To improve the effectiveness of hypothesis validation, it is best to make the interview questions as "quantitative" as possible, so they can be answered with numbers or quantities.

Good examples of questions include: "Please rank the three features in order of usefulness" or "Please rate the search function on a scale of 1 to 10."

Note that if the sample size of data is small, reliability tends to suffer, so it is recommended to set in advance a number of data points that your company finds acceptable.

Based on the feedback obtained in the fourth step, evaluate your initial hypotheses. In some cases, you may have to choose between improving existing features, implementing new features, or even pivoting the product's direction entirely.

In any case, the major key point of MVP development is to repeatedly carry out the cycle of evaluation and improvement. By polishing the product many times, you move closer to a product that matches user needs.

There are several points to keep in mind when undertaking MVP development. In the following sections, we introduce three points of caution.

The first thing to be careful about is approaching development from the user's perspective rather than the developer's. This is because the benefit of MVP development lies in being able to establish positioning in a new market by approaching users from an early stage.

If you release a product that reflects only the developer's perspective, the effectiveness of user validation may be compromised. By focusing on solving problems through the product and appropriately incorporating features heard from users, you can more easily enhance the effectiveness of MVP development.

Creating an environment where user feedback can be obtained is also an important point. This is because in the MVP development process, features are improved and changed based on user evaluations.

Since user feedback is essential, it is important to build good relationships so that users will give opinions on the released product. Also, by asking as specific questions as possible, it becomes easier to share feedback items among stakeholders.

To achieve a rapid product release, it is better to avoid setting the goal of "making it perfect." MVP development is a development method in which you release with a sense of speed and then repeat improvements while gathering feedback.

If you aim for perfection from the start, development will take a lot of man-hours and you will not be able to fully leverage the benefits of MVP development. It is important to approach development with flexibility while keeping sight of the goal of a "minimum viable product."

From here, we introduce "PeecAI," an MVP development case handled by DeFactory. First we explain the overview, then discuss the specific development period and process, so please use it as a reference.

"PeecAI" is a personalized AI recommendation engine specialized for Shopify, an e-commerce site. Its recommendation feature has the characteristic of guiding users to recommended products based on their purchase and browsing history on the e-commerce site.

Among these, "PeecAI" can contribute to product sales through the following four features.

FeatureCharacteristics
Suggestion features・Recommendation feature: Can recommend items bought together, related products, and trending products・Recommendation display settings: Can configure recommendation type, position, kind, and show/hide settings
Customization features・Manual product recommendation: Can manually set products to prioritize・Manual category recommendation: Recommendation logic for prioritized products can be applied per category combination
Cart abandonment prevention・Reminder recommendations: Can display recommendations of items in the cart to returning users
Analysis features・Overall analysis and per-product analysis: Can analyze products purchased through recommendations

In addition, "PeecAI" is characterized by having no initial setup or operational burden, with recommendation display starting in as little as 5 minutes. Using the analysis features, you can visualize the effectiveness of the recommendation feature.

Now, let us introduce the development period and process for "PeecAI."

[Development Period]

・MVP development period: About 4 months (about 3 person-months)

・Evaluation and improvement period toward paid release: About 5 months

[Process]

① Before development, secure several users who will definitely use it

② Define the initial value validation points and hypotheses, and implement only the smallest features

③ During development, continue interviewing prospective users to validate value and features (not yet implemented)

④ Provided to users free of charge

⑤ Based on feedback after provision, add features (additional implementation carried out over about 5 months after the free provision began)

Regarding the development period, free provision to users was reached in about 4 months. After that, features were added based on user feedback, and the paid release was reached about 5 months later.

A key point of the process was securing users who would provide feedback before starting development.

Currently, "PeecAI" contributes to user acquisition as a recommendation engine equipped with only the necessary features, creating success stories at low cost.

By executing MVP development, you can expect benefits such as early monetization in a new market and minimizing product risk. Consider adopting MVP development according to your company's needs.
