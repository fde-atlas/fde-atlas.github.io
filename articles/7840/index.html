Tencent Research: FDE Model Reshapes AI Delivery — From Model-Centric to Organization-Centric Deployment

BestHub · 2026-10-15 · 3 min read

Tencent Research Institute's report reveals seven core insights on FDE (Field Delivery Engineering) as the emerging paradigm for enterprise AI deployment, emphasizing reusable asset creation over one-off projects, the shift from coding to AI orchestration, and platform-partner-customer collaboration for scalable adoption.

Core Conclusions: Seven Shifts in Enterprise AI Deployment

The report identifies a fundamental transition: the primary bottleneck for enterprise AI is no longer model capability — which improves quarterly — but organizational absorption. Over 60% of enterprise AI attempts remain stuck at pilot stage. The challenge lies in integrating models into real systems, embedding them into workflows, meeting compliance, and producing measurable business outcomes.

1. FDE Is a Delivery Paradigm Shift, Not a New Role

FDE recombines pre-sales, implementation, product, and customer success capabilities into a single frontline squad where delivery and learning happen simultaneously. Three cost barriers have dropped simultaneously due to AI: knowledge distillation, custom development, and composite talent supply. This makes heavy-delivery models commercially viable at broader scale.

2. Asset Reuse Distinguishes FDE from Traditional On-Site Delivery

Leaving only a system = outsourcing

Bringing back experience but unable to reuse = project-based

Converting field experience into Skills, templates, test sets, or product capabilities = FDE

Reusable assets that significantly lower cost of next similar delivery = scalable FDE

3. AI Lowers Engineering Cost, Raises Value of Judgment

AI coding and agent tools reduce prototype and system adaptation costs. However, identifying high-value scenarios, understanding industry context, and driving organizational adoption become more valuable. Frontline roles evolve from "people who write code" to "people who command AI to complete business tasks."

4. Sustainable Path: Asset-First, Reuse-First, Gradual Customer Self-Service

Fully autonomous platform building is prohibitively expensive. A realistic path: first distill high-frequency Skills and system connectors, then gradually form industry templates and knowledge bases, ensuring every frontline engagement leaves behind reusable assets.

5. Platform-Partner-Customer Tripartite Collaboration Is Emerging Consensus

Platform: accumulates capabilities and benchmarks

Partners: scale replication and provide localized service

Customers: gradually build self-service capability during deployment

This collaboration lets head customers get deep service while mid-tail customers access mature capabilities at lower cost.

6. Future Value Accrues to Business-Savvy, Org-Savvy, AI-Orchestration Talent

Low-end implementation will be continuously compressed. Scenario definition, customer trust, organizational change management, and industry knowledge accumulation will appreciate. New entrants need not seek "standard answers" — FDE exists for differentiated scenarios. Teams already underway should focus on turning frontline experience into owned capabilities.

Implications for Individual Practitioners

FDE suits those with solid technical skills, willingness to face customers, appetite for learning new industries, and tolerance for ambiguity. It does not suit those seeking stable pure-coding roles without communication.

Career trajectory:

Junior: learn platform, learn communication, shadow projects

Mid-level: run projects independently, build industry cognition

Senior/Echo: define problems, lead teams, create benchmarks

Beyond: entrepreneur, industry product lead, AI business lead, independent industry consultant

Most critical action now: learn industry beyond technology. For every project, leave provable cases and quantified results — how much time saved, how much efficiency gained. Those who accumulate industry cognition will outlast those who only master AI tools.
