Palantir Delta vs Echo: I Read All 98 Live Roles on the Two Teams

Hayat Amin · Operator · 2026-10-11 · 9 min read

Summary

Hayat Amin read all 309 live Palantir job postings on 11 October 2026 and compares the two deployed teams: Delta (66 Forward Deployed Software Engineer roles) and Echo (32 Deployment Strategist roles). Delta builds pipelines and applications inside customer systems; Echo finds the problems and datasets, trains users, and presents to executives. New York pay is $135,000–$200,000 for Delta versus $110,000–$170,000 for Echo, while Echo travels more (25–75%). The author argues companies need both roles, since each fails differently without the other.

Key points

  • 64 of 66 Delta postings require a programming language.
  • 26 of 32 Echo postings list programming only as a plus.
  • Delta's New York pay ceiling is about $30,000 above Echo's.
  • Echo requires 25–75% travel in 14 of 17 US postings.
  • Six Echo roles use the Build to Apply demo route.

Delta is Palantir's team of Forward Deployed Software Engineers, who build inside the customer's systems. Echo is its team of Deployment Strategists, who find the problem, work the data with the users and present to the executives. Delta writes the code. Echo decides what the code is for, and it travels up to three times as much.

I'm Hayat Amin. I spent twenty years as a technology chief financial officer, sold three companies in that seat, and now do forward deployed work myself, so I do both of these jobs at once inside smaller companies. This morning, 11 October 2026, I read every live role on Palantir's own job feed. There were 309. 66 sit on Delta, 32 on Echo and 85 on Dev, the product team. Below is what each of the two deployed teams is asked to do, what it pays and who it suits.

Where the names come from

Palantir explained Delta itself in a 2019 post on its own blog. Software Engineers and Forward Deployed Software Engineers were its two biggest engineering roles by headcount, and internally they're called Devs and Deltas. Delta is a throwback to the early days, when each team in Business Development was named after a letter in the NATO alphabet. Echo is the next letter in that alphabet. I couldn't find a Palantir post that tells the Echo story, so I'll only say it fits the same pattern.

The same post gives the line everyone quotes. A Dev's focus is one capability, many customers. A Delta's is one customer, many capabilities. The Pragmatic Engineer adds that until about 2016 Palantir had more Deltas than Devs, and that Foundry's launch that year moved many of them back into product work. Today the count runs the other way, 85 Dev roles to 66 Delta. Add Echo and the deployed side comes to 98, still more than the product side.

Type palantir delta into Google and the first completion is palantir delta vs echo, ahead of salary and interview. My earlier piece on what a forward deployed engineer is at Palantir covers Delta on its own. This one puts the two teams side by side.

What Delta is asked to do

Every one of the 66 Delta titles is a forward deployed engineer of some kind: software, infrastructure or AI. The New York Forward Deployed Software Engineer posting calls the role the blueprint, and says you'll architect and build solutions on business critical data, develop custom applications and wrangle data at massive scale. It asks for a strong coder in Python, Java, C++, TypeScript or JavaScript, and 1 or more years after college. 64 of the 66 Delta postings name a programming language requirement.

The 2019 post has a Delta in Abu Dhabi describing a normal week. A couple of days at the customer's premises, monitoring, debugging, deploying or configuring the software. Back in the office, minor code changes, pull requests and planning. A Delta in New York says the difference from a consultant is that consultants leave behind a one time analysis, while Palantir builds a long term solution with the customer. I've written on that argument in is a forward deployed engineer just a consultant.

What Echo is asked to do

All 32 Echo titles are Deployment Strategist, with a government, intelligence, country or new grad suffix. The New York posting lists nine things you can expect to do. You go onsite and meet the customer's analysts to find their biggest problems. You identify the relevant datasets. You work with Forward Deployed Engineers to integrate the data into a stable and extensible pipeline. You build custom workflows for new user groups, lead training, present results and proposals to audiences from analysts to C suite executives, feed what you saw back to product, build demos, and scope new engagements.

That third line is the hinge between the two teams. Echo finds the data and the problem, Delta makes the pipeline that carries it, and Echo makes sure people use what comes out. 26 of the 32 Echo postings list Python, R, Matlab or SQL as a plus. The plain New York posting lists it as a requirement, so the bar moves by team.

Six Echo postings use a route Palantir calls Build to Apply. You pick a problem, build a working project on Foundry and AIP, and send a demo video under five minutes within 7 to 10 days of starting. I like that. It tests the job, which is finding a problem worth solving and showing someone why it matters.

The numbers side by side

Every count below comes from Palantir's Lever feed this morning. Locations are each posting's main city, and a band is a range across levels, not an offer.

  • Live roles: Delta 66, Echo 32. Delta has 19 internships, Echo 2.
  • New York pay: Delta $135,000 to $200,000, Echo $110,000 to $170,000. Delta's floor is $25,000 higher and its ceiling $30,000 higher, my subtraction. All 17 Echo postings with a band say the same figures.
  • New grad pay: Delta $135,000 to $145,000. The one Echo new grad posting in the US with a band, in intelligence, uses the full $110,000 to $170,000.
  • Travel: the New York Delta posting says up to 25 percent and flexible on personal preference. London Delta says 25 to 50 percent. Echo says 25 to 75 percent required in 14 of its 17 US postings with a band.
  • Clearance: mentioned in 45 of 66 Delta postings and 22 of 32 Echo.
  • Where: Delta has 15 in New York, 15 in Washington DC and 10 in London. Echo has 6 in Washington DC, 4 in New York and 2 in London.
  • Remote: none on either team. Delta is 35 onsite and 31 hybrid, Echo 21 onsite and 11 hybrid.

The pay gap is smaller than I expected. Palantir pays the strategist about $30,000 less at the top for a job that asks less code and more airport. One Delta role, Tactical Edge in Washington DC, asks for 25 to 75 percent travel, which is the Echo number.

Which team fits you

If you want to write production code and own what you built, it's Delta. If you'd rather be the person who walks into a plant or a ministry, works out which question matters, and stands in front of the executives with the answer, it's Echo. The 2019 post says there's a lot of movement between Palantir's roles, and one Dev it quotes spent about six months in France as a Delta first. Neither door is locked behind you.

Ask yourself one thing. Would you rather be measured on whether it runs, or on whether anyone uses it? That's the line between the two teams, and for the comparison with the product side, read the forward deployed engineering model.

What this means if you run a company

Palantir puts engineers and strategists on the same deployment team, and I think that's because the two jobs fail in different ways. A company that buys an engineer and skips the strategist gets a working pipeline nobody opens. A company that buys the strategist and skips the engineer gets a good slide deck. If you run a company of 20 to 500 people, you probably have one half and are waiting for the other to happen on its own.

Questions people actually ask

Palantir Delta vs Echo: what is the difference?

Delta is the team of Forward Deployed Software Engineers. They build the pipelines and applications inside the customer's systems, and 64 of the 66 live Delta postings on 11 October 2026 ask for a programming language. Echo is the team of Deployment Strategists. They meet the customer's analysts, find the problem and the datasets, train the users and present to executives, and 26 of the 32 Echo postings call programming a plus rather than a requirement.

Palantir Delta vs Dev: what is the difference?

Palantir's own 2019 post puts it as one capability, many customers for a Dev and one customer, many capabilities for a Delta. Devs sit in Product Development and own a part of Foundry or Gotham from end to end. Deltas sat in Business Development and deploy those platforms at one customer. On 11 October 2026 Palantir had 85 live Dev roles and 66 Delta roles.

Palantir Delta vs Dev vs Echo: which team does what?

Dev builds the product. Delta deploys and extends it inside one customer as engineers. Echo works out with that customer what problem to point it at and makes sure people use what Delta builds. The Deployment Strategist posting in New York says Echo works with Forward Deployed Engineers to integrate the data into a stable and extensible pipeline.

What is the Palantir Echo salary?

Every one of the 17 Deployment Strategist postings that published a band on 11 October 2026 said $110,000 to $170,000 a year, in New York, Washington DC, Honolulu and the other US cities, plus restricted stock units and a possible sign on bonus.

What is the Palantir Delta salary?

The Forward Deployed Software Engineer in New York is $135,000 to $200,000 a year on 11 October 2026, and every experienced Delta posting with a US band said the same apart from one Honolulu role starting at $125,000. New grad Delta roles are $135,000 to $145,000.

What is the Palantir Echo interview process?

Six of the 32 Echo postings use a route Palantir calls Build to Apply. You build a project on Foundry and AIP around a problem you care about, record a demo video under five minutes, and submit within 7 to 10 days of starting to build. Palantir says it can give you expedited consideration.
