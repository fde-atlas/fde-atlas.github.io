What is an FDE? Forward Deployed Engineer knowledge base

Summary

This article is a question-and-answer knowledge base about Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), updated as of October 2026. It defines an FDE as someone embedded at a customer's business site who turns AI capabilities into runnable, verifiable, controllable workflows and is accountable for outcomes. It distinguishes engineering-type FDEs, who integrate models into client systems via code, from business-type FDEs, who codify domain rules and validate outputs with samples. It contrasts FDEs with pre-sales, consulting, and implementation roles, cites a August 2026 Ministry of Industry and Information Technology notice encouraging FDE teams, and warns against FDE washing, a Gartner-coined term for rebranding ordinary services at premium prices. Cited Liepin data puts average annual pay at 408,000 RMB for February–August 2026, though most roles require 3–10 years of experience.

Key points

  • FDEs work on-site, turning AI into verifiable workflows and owning business outcomes.
  • Two types exist: engineering FDEs write integration code; business FDEs codify rules and validate results.
  • Unlike implementation engineers, FDEs must first judge whether the requirement itself is correct.
  • China's MIIT encouraged FDE teams in an August 2026 notice to ensure scenario delivery.
  • FDE washing, per Gartner, means charging FDE-level premiums without matching delivery depth.

What is an FDE? Definition and job responsibilities of the Forward Deployed Engineer

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is someone embedded at a company's business site who turns AI capabilities into business processes that can run, be inspected, and be controlled, and who is accountable for the results of implementation.

How many types of FDE are there?

Two types of FDE: engineering-focused and business-focused

FDEs fall roughly into two types: engineering-focused FDEs use code to connect models into client systems, while business-focused FDEs turn business experience into rules, verify results with samples, and design human-machine division of labor. The two differ in division of labor, not in rank, and many projects require both working together.

What's the difference between an FDE and an implementation engineer?

How FDEs differ from implementation, consulting, and pre-sales roles

Pre-sales closes deals, consulting determines direction, and implementation delivers features according to requirement documents; an FDE is accountable for business outcomes: first judging whether the requirement itself is right, then writing rules clearly, verifying with samples, going live, and continuously improving.

What skills does a business-focused FDE need?

Business-focused FDE competency model: one competency chain, two competency lines

A business-focused FDE's competencies form a five-link chain: business observation, structured description, platform capability mapping, simulation and element identification, and delivery and deployment design, supported by a business competency line and a platform competency line.

Frequently asked questions

Can you be an FDE without knowing how to code?

It depends on which type. Engineering-focused FDEs need solid programming skills. The core of a business-focused FDE is business observation, rule extraction, sample verification, and permission design; with platforms like WorkBuddy handling configuration, no programming background is required, but you must be willing to test hands-on and iterate repeatedly. Differences between the two FDE types

What's the difference between an FDE and an implementation engineer?

An implementation engineer delivers features to production according to requirement documents; an FDE is accountable for business outcomes, first judging whether the requirement itself is right, pressing "it depends" into explicit rules, verifying results with samples, and then designing permissions and rollout. Should companies train FDEs in-house or hire external FDEs?

A combination is recommended. For the first scenario, bring in an external FDE to lead the work while internal key staff learn the same methodology alongside; once it runs successfully, the rules and tools are retained within the company, maintained and extended by internal FDEs. Relying solely on external build-out makes later changes difficult—exactly the risk Gartner warns about. How to tell real FDEs from fake ones

Do small and medium-sized businesses need FDEs?

They need the capability, not necessarily a dedicated role. For most SMEs, a more realistic entry point is one department and one concrete task, such as customer service ticket classification and reply drafts, sales weekly report summaries, or expense report pre-review. A business-savvy key employee can take on the FDE role part-time and produce the first template. See implementation scenarios by role

What is FDE pay like? Can you become an FDE after taking a course?

According to media reports citing Liepin data, the average annual salary for related positions from February to August 2026 was 408,000 RMB, but a considerable share of positions require 3–10 years of experience. Courses teach methodology and delivery materials, and do not guarantee positions, projects, or income; whether you can do the job depends on industry experience and practice in real projects.

Are FDE certifications useful?

Vendors in the market have already launched certifications related to agent delivery. A certification shows you have learned a particular methodology, but companies care more about whether you can produce real deliverables: a task specification with clearly written rules, a sample verification record, and an executable permission scheme.
