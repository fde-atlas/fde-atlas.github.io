The Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is rapidly gaining worldwide attention as a professional profile that supports the implementation of AI-era business at the front lines. The role was established by Palantir Technologies, and in recent years leading AI companies such as Anthropic and OpenAI have been actively hiring for it. This article provides a neutral overview of the Forward Deployed Engineer's definition, the background behind the growing attention, how the role differs from traditional engineers and consultants, and the skills it requires.

What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is an engineer who is stationed at the customer's site, writing code while transforming on-the-ground challenges into the company's products or solutions.

The term "Forward Deployed" originally comes from a military term meaning "deployed to the front lines." In the business world, it has come to mean "an engineer who embeds themselves on the front lines of the customer's operations and takes end-to-end responsibility, from discovering challenges to implementation and operation."

What sets the FDE apart is that the role goes beyond mere implementation support or customer success. FDEs dive deep into the customer's data environment, business processes, and on-site decision-making flows, converting generically built AI technology and products into "working solutions" tailored to that customer's specific circumstances.

And they carry out this transformation not on presentation slides, but by writing the code themselves and building it end to end. Not desk-based advice, but hands-on work in the field. This attitude of "building it to completion on-site" is the very core of the Forward Deployed Engineer profile.

Why Is It Attracting So Much Attention Now?

Behind the global attention on Forward Deployed Engineers lies a recent structural shift: it has become difficult to differentiate on AI technology alone.

Over the past few years, high-performance AI models and the tooling for AI development have become widely available to anyone. Access to foundation models, vector search, agent development frameworks. What was once a competitive advantage simply to possess is now approaching commodity status.

Owning technology itself no longer guarantees business advantage. The center of value has shifted to "whether you can turn that AI into working results in the customer's actual environment."

This shift has dramatically raised the importance of the Forward Deployed Engineer. A professional who stands between general-purpose AI and the customer's site, translating between the language of the domain and the language of technology, and building solutions that only work in that specific environment. That is why they have become key to implementing business in the AI era.

Starting from Palantir Technologies systematizing this role, leading AI companies such as Anthropic and OpenAI have established similar positions, and in recent years the number of companies in Japan with Forward Deployed Engineers has been growing as well. Worldwide, the role continues to be counted among the most talked-about positions in the tech industry.

How It Differs from Traditional Engineers, SEs, and IT Consultants

The Forward Deployed Engineer differs from both traditional engineers who build systems in development offices and IT consultants who focus on proposals, in terms of where they stand and the scope they cover. All of these play important roles in supporting business operations, and within that landscape, the FDE has its own distinct profile.

Differences from traditional engineers/data scientists: Traditional engineers and data scientists focus primarily on developing products and models in labs or development offices. FDEs, by contrast, are stationed on the customer's front lines and develop within the context of that environment. Even though both "write code," where they write it and who they face differ.

Differences from SEs/SIers: SEs and SIers build systems based on defined requirements. FDEs enter at the stage before requirements are defined—that is, from discovering "what the problem actually is"—and refine the very shape of the solution while building it in the customer's environment.

Differences from IT consultants: IT consultants focus primarily on strategic advice and proposals, with implementation often handled by the customer or another vendor. FDEs don't stop at the proposal; their focus is on writing code themselves and building things to a "working form."

Each of these roles creates significant value in its own place. The Forward Deployed Engineer is positioned as a professional profile that takes on the distinctive role of "building things to completion on the customer's site."

Skills Required of an FDE

In addition to technical skills, Forward Deployed Engineers need the ability to understand the customer's operations and business, as well as the speed and judgment to drive things through in the field.

The required skills can be broadly summarized into the following four areas.

  • Technical implementation skills: The ability to turn technology, including AI, into something that works in the field. The ability to combine multiple tools and models and complete implementation within limited time.
  • Understanding of customer operations and domains: The ability to interpret the customer's industry, business processes, and on-site constraints. A perspective that frames challenges as business problems, not just technical ones.
  • Communication skills: The ability to engage with a diverse range of stakeholders—not just engineers, but also on-site operations staff, business owners, and executives—and build consensus.
  • Decision-making under uncertainty: Requirements aren't settled, data isn't ready, on-site criteria keep shifting. The judgment to keep prioritizing and taking action in such uncertain conditions.

Standing "between" technology and business, moving back and forth between both languages and building things to completion in the field. This very difficulty is the source of the Forward Deployed Engineer's value.

Is there also a field-based talent profile on the business side?—Forward Deployed Expert

The talent profile that stands in the field under the Forward Deployed model is not limited to Engineers. There is also a framework for the Forward Deployed Expert, a talent profile that stands in the field with practical business development knowledge as their primary asset.

While the Engineer takes on "the field where things are built to completion," the Expert takes on "the field of determining what to build and breaking through internal decision-making." Hypothesis building, consensus-building with stakeholders, and the ability to move organizations. With these as their primary assets, this is a talent profile that stands beside the customer's business owner.

The two are not in opposition. In the field of business implementation in the AI era, two core talent profiles stand side by side: those who build to completion from the technology side, and those who drive things forward from the business side. This is the framework AlphaDrive presents in Japan, positioned as a perspective that views Engineers and Experts in parallel rather than in opposition.

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is an engineer who is stationed at the customer's site, writing code while transforming problems into solutions. This is a talent profile established by Palantir and now actively recruited by leading AI companies worldwide, and its importance is growing even further in an era where the scarcity of AI technology has diminished.

A person who stands "between" technology and business operations and builds things to completion in the field. The Forward Deployed Engineer is a core talent profile supporting business implementation in the AI era from the front lines. And beside them stands another field-based talent profile—the Forward Deployed Expert.

Q2. Why are Forward Deployed Engineers attracting attention? Because high-performance AI technology has become widely available, making it difficult to differentiate through technology ownership alone. As the center of value has shifted to "whether AI can be translated into working results at the customer's site," the importance of FDEs—who are stationed in the field and can translate between technology and business operations—is rising worldwide.

Q3. How do FDEs differ from conventional engineers, SEs, and IT consultants? Conventional engineers focus primarily on development in a lab, SEs build based on requirements definitions, and IT consultants center on advice and proposals. FDEs have a unique role in being stationed at the customer's site and building from problem discovery all the way to a "working form" by writing code themselves. All are important roles; they differ in where they stand and the scope they cover.

Four capabilities are considered particularly important: technical implementation skills, understanding of customer business and domains, the ability to communicate with diverse stakeholders, and decision-making in uncertain situations. Continuously standing "between" technology and business is what makes this role difficult—and at the same time, the source of its value.
