A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who embeds inside a customer's organization to make the product truly solve that customer's problems. The job is half engineering, half people: finding out who makes the decisions, running requirements discovery, capturing the key points from every visit, and delivering on every promise. This page explains the role and the customer work behind it.

FDE: what a Forward Deployed Engineer does

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer. The title comes from Palantir, which has long sent engineers to work on-site at customers. Starting in 2025, AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic have also built FDE teams to bring models into customers' production environments. An FDE works with the customer to define the problem, builds and integrates the solution, then brings what they learned back to the product team. For example: an FDE embedded at a logistics customer runs a workshop with the head of operations on Monday, connects the model to the dispatch system on Wednesday, and writes down what the product team should do next on Friday. Mars records what everyone said each day on their own pages, so Friday's summary quotes the customer's actual words rather than memory.

  • Serves one or a few customers at a time, often on-site
  • Writes production code and integrations, not slides
  • Turns customer problems into product feedback

The difference between FDEs, software engineers, and solutions engineers, and how to become a forward deployed engineer

Software engineers build products for all customers; solutions engineers support sales with demos and technical Q&A; Forward Deployed Engineers own post-signature delivery, working inside one customer's systems until results go live, so they spend far more time with customer stakeholders than the other two. This is also how most people enter the field: FDEs mostly come from software, data, or solutions engineering roles, then fill in the half they're missing. Software engineers add requirements discovery and writing for non-engineers; solutions engineers add production code, APIs, and SQL, and at AI companies also retrieval, agents, and evals. Recruiters then look for evidence on both sides, such as two or three projects launched to real users, and FDE interviews often include case breakdowns and requirements-discovery role plays. For example: before applying, set up short chats with five working FDEs. Mars builds a page for each person, records what they say about the role, helps you prepare a briefing before each call, and reminds you to send the follow-up messages you promised—exactly the customer habits this job demands.

  • Software engineer: one product, many customers
  • Solutions engineer: pre-sales, demos, and proofs of concept
  • FDE: delivering production results for a specific customer after signing
  • Path in: fill in the missing half, then show two or three real launched projects

Stakeholder map: who decides, who uses, who might object

Every FDE project has someone who pays, a technical lead, daily users, and people who can say no. Mars builds a page for everyone you meet on the customer side, grouped by business and team, technology and tools, and mindset and preferences, so the stakeholder map grows naturally from conversations instead of being maintained in a spreadsheet.

  • A page for each stakeholder's role, goals, and concerns
  • Who reports to whom, and who can influence decisions
  • See what each person cares about before the meeting

Requirements discovery to find the real problem

Good requirements discovery asks why the project started, what success looks like, what data and systems exist, and what has failed before. Before every conversation, have Mars generate a pre-meeting briefing from your saved notes, open requirements, and past conversations with that person.

  • A briefing for every meeting, based on your history with the customer
  • List unresolved questions and requirements before you walk in
  • Follow-up messages quote what was actually said in the meeting

Meeting notes and action items from every site visit

Customer workshops always produce promises: "I'll send you the table schema" or "I'll grant API access by Friday." Record the meeting and Mars will list every action item with its owner, task, deadline, and the moment it was said, and every decision is kept together with its conditions.

  • Action items with owners and deadlines, traceable back to the recording
  • Decisions kept together with their "if... then..." conditions
  • Speakers distinguished, so it's clear who promised what

Research the customer before you go on-site

Before your first visit, have Mars do deep research on the customer's company and the people you'll meet. It first confirms the research plan with you, then searches in parallel, and finally delivers a report with citations you can verify one by one.

  • The customer's business, recent changes, and public tech stack
  • Backgrounds of the people you'll meet
  • Sources you can open and check
