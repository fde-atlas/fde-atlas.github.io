Summary

Written by DeFactory CEO Shohei Tokumitsu, this beginner-oriented article explains agile development as repeating plan-build-evaluate-improve cycles of one week to one month, building products feature by feature. Citing a May 2025 Agileware survey of 400 system engineers, it notes the top reason for adopting agile was flexible response to requirement changes (58.5%), and about 80% of adopters said results met or exceeded expectations. The article lists seven benefits, identifies fast PDCA cycling as the biggest, compares agile with waterfall, describes three case studies (a regional bank's app, taxi fare pre-calculation features, and post-merger system integration), and lists prerequisites for outsourcing agile projects: a product owner, client feedback participation, and contracts that allow scope changes.

Key points

  • Agile development runs plan-build-evaluate-improve cycles of one week to one month.
  • In a 2025 survey, 58.5% adopted agile mainly to handle requirement changes flexibly.
  • About 80% of adopters reported results meeting or exceeding expectations.
  • The biggest benefit is fast PDCA cycling, enabling early releases and user feedback.
  • Success requires a product owner, client involvement in reviews, and change-friendly contracts.

Agile development is a methodology in which specifications and plans are not fully fixed at the outset; instead, "planning, development, evaluation, and improvement" are repeated in short cycles of about one week to one month, building the product function by function. The name "agile" means "quick" or "nimble," reflecting the emphasis on speed through repeated testing over short periods.

The benefits can be summarized in three points: flexible response to specification changes, faster time to market, and improved quality by incorporating user feedback. The greatest benefit among these is the ability to develop while running PDCA cycles at high speed.

In this article, for those considering agile development, we explain the 7 benefits of agile development, the PDCA flow, 3 success stories, a comparison with waterfall development, and key points to check when requesting implementation support.

The benefits of agile development are clearly reflected in feedback from practitioners. In a survey of 400 system engineers (200 hired for agile development and 200 hired for waterfall development) published by Agileware in May 2025, the results were as follows.

  • The top reason for adopting agile development was "flexible and rapid response to requirement and specification changes" (58.5%), followed by "accelerated time to market" (34.5%), and "improved quality and faster bug fixes" (30.0%)
  • The most commonly experienced benefit was also "flexible and rapid response to requirement and specification changes" (49.5%)
  • About 80% of respondents said post-adoption results were "better than expected" or "as expected"

Source: Agileware press release (May 2025). Taking these results into account, we organize the benefits into 7 points.

Because not everything is decided upfront, even if requirements change during development, they can be incorporated into the next cycle. This makes agile a great fit for new businesses and new services where direction tends to shift along the way.

You can release working parts first without waiting for all features to be complete. This shortens the time to launch and lets you get user feedback faster than competitors.

Because testing and evaluation are performed at short intervals, defects and misunderstandings can be detected early. Compared to finding them all at once near completion, the impact of fixes is smaller.

Since feedback is received for each completed feature, you can reduce the waste of building features users don't need. Because high-priority features are built first, user satisfaction is also easier to improve.

Because improvement cycles are short, when you notice a deviation in direction, the rework required is small. As a result, it becomes easier to proceed with development while keeping deadlines and costs under control.

Because developers, business stakeholders, and clients check in with each other in short cycles, misunderstandings are resolved quickly. The survey also noted that after adoption, "communication became smoother and misunderstandings decreased."

Because you build and evaluate in small units, you can quickly decide whether to "continue in this direction or stop." Being able to make decisions in stages without committing a large investment all at once is especially useful for businesses with uncertain prospects.

In agile development, short-term goals are set for a fixed period, and execution, analysis, and improvement are performed at high speed, repeating in small function-based cycles.

In typical product development, details are decided at the initial stage and then work proceeds in large cycles, so there is often no expectation of backtracking, making it difficult to respond to sudden specification changes.

However, in agile development, testing and operation are repeated through trial and error, gradually approaching 100% while polishing the product as a whole.

Let's look at the specific PDCA flow. Generally, the PDCA cycle runs on a span of one week to one month, continuously adding and improving features.

In agile development, development content and schedules are deliberately not strictly defined at the initial stage, keeping things flexible.

Once customer requirements are confirmed and minimum requirements are defined, a specification document including an estimate is created, and if there are no issues, development begins immediately.

With Defactory, depending on the client's business planning status, planning (requirements definition) can be completed in as few as 5 business days. We perform current-state analysis and goal setting to create plans that ensure both quality and speed.

Development proceeds with priorities assigned to each feature. The "user story format" is often adopted as the method for assigning priorities.

The user story format is expressed through elements such as "persona," "function," and "added value." For example, if you create the story "On an EC site, a 40-year-old female user can easily find products that suit her,"

- Easy member registration

- Clear and easy-to-follow login process

- Easy search for products that suit her

- Regular guidance emails sent after purchase

Items like the above are identified, and the development order is determined. At this point, the user persona's behavior is shown chronologically, and necessary features are developed through trial and error.

In agile development, as soon as one small feature is completed, you check whether it matches customer needs, and if it doesn't, you repeatedly make specification changes while building something that satisfies user needs.

To quickly deliver results that bring true value to users, "communication" is more important than anything else.

In agile development, rather than giving feedback only after the entire product is complete, you review and provide feedback on each feature as it is finished.

There are several ways to communicate, including face-to-face meetings, phone calls, chats, and emails. The most effective is "face-to-face," where all team members and users gather to share information such as feedback, allowing you to discover improvement points with greater impact.

Also, the client's cooperation is essential to run the PDCA cycle at high speed. When requesting work from a vendor, communicate closely in advance to secure their cooperation.

Finally, improvements are made as needed based on feedback received from users.

Since user needs can be reflected immediately in the product's direction, fine-grained course corrections are possible.

In addition, because the speed of improvement is fast, there is little rework, allowing development to proceed while keeping delivery times and costs down.

With DeFactory, this entire series of steps can be completed in "about 6 to 10 business days," so you can build your product even faster.

So far we have discussed agile development in the context of running the PDCA cycle, and it can be said to have the following characteristics.

- Reduced time required for product development

- Ability to leave room in the requirements definition

- Easier to maximize user satisfaction

- Ability to keep correction costs down

From the above, products suited to agile development are those where specification changes or additions are expected during development.

Here, we introduce three success stories that leverage the benefits of agile development from the perspectives of "cashless payments, taxi services, and system integration."

By the way, typical examples of products suited to agile development are said to be things like smartphone apps, e-commerce, and games, where new services are developed and improvement cycles are fast.

At a certain regional bank, a project to develop a bank-specific app was underway to promote cashless payments in the local area.

The bank had not established a structure for developing smartphone apps in-house, and was looking for a vendor with whom it could communicate frequently.

In addition, since they wanted to launch the app in time for the opening of a commercial facility, development had to proceed under conditions where delays were unacceptable.

The vendor that received the request, centering on a project manager and engineers, worked together with the bank to build a structure capable of running the PDCA cycle at high speed through "design → implementation → testing → release."

During the development process, they held study sessions on app development for the bank's engineers and repeatedly tested the features users needed.

As a result, they were able to launch the app in time for the opening of the commercial facility, and it was used by many users.

In this way, agile development is well suited to app development that involves repeated testing over short periods.

In the taxi industry, with the launch of the upfront fare service in October 2019, responding to ride-hailing apps had become an urgent matter.

The upfront fare service is a system in which, when a user enters their pickup location and destination in a ride-hailing app, the fare is automatically calculated and the taxi can be used at that fare. Adding this service prevents users from being charged unexpectedly high amounts without their knowledge.

The vendor formed a team with the client's development unit, built a user-friendly interface, improved the architecture, and completed the apps and software in a short period.

When adding detailed features to an app, a structure can be built that leaves room in the specifications, as in agile development, making it easier to handle changes made along the way.

At one company, as a result of acquiring multiple companies, information sharing between them did not go smoothly, causing problems in system development.

To achieve system integration, the company divided a project involving more than 100 employees into teams of about 7 members each, split tasks by priority, and had each team complete its work within a delivery period of about one month.

By having multiple teams develop on the same rhythm over the same period, all teams could be managed in an integrated way, and communication through conference calls as needed made the project a success.

If this project had been carried out with waterfall development, it might not have been able to accommodate detailed specification changes, potentially resulting in delayed delivery or uneven quality.

The benefits of agile development become clearer when compared with waterfall development. Rather than one being superior to the other, their suitability depends on the nature of the product.

There are conditions on how you proceed in order to bring out the benefits of agile development. If it is difficult to proceed internally alone, you can seek external implementation support, but in that case, be sure to check the following three points.

You need a person on the business side who can decide feature priorities (a product owner). If decisions are delayed, the benefit of running short cycles diminishes.

To turn PDCA around quickly, the client side also needs to participate in providing feedback. Decide on the frequency of reviews and who will be responsible in advance.

Since changes along the way are assumed, contracts that rigidly fix specifications and scope make it hard to leverage the benefits. Confirm how changes will be handled before making the request.

As a central role in implementation support, there is also the agile coach. The role is explained in What Is an Agile Coach? Differences from Scrum Master, Required Skills, and How to Build a Career. Also, if you are pursuing this alongside an MVP for a new business, The Differences and Relationships Between MVP Development, Agile, and Lean Startup is also a helpful reference.
