Full Text of the 16 Assessment Questions and Scoring Criteria (Fully Public)

Original title: 16道考核题全文与评分标准（全公开）

深圳市真实智元科技有限公司 · 2026-11-03 · 7 min read

Summary

Shenzhen-based 真实智元 (Zhenzhiyuan) has published the full text and grading rubric of 16 assessment questions for its FDE training course. Assessment spans five components: process assignments (30%), mid-term project (20%), technical delivery project (30%), and final client defense (20%), plus a non-scored diagnostic. Questions are grouped into six sets covering role cognition, discovery, prototyping, production delivery, client communication, and a comprehensive capstone. AI coding assistants are allowed, but candidates must explain, verify, and correct AI output. Final scoring uses seven dimensions, with evals/metrics/attribution weighted highest at 20%. Five one-vote-veto failures apply regardless of total score, including data leaks, missing human confirmation for high-risk actions, and non-reproducible results.

Key points

  • Five assessment components; technical delivery project weighs 30%, final defense 20%.
  • 16 questions in six groups map to a six-stage FDE methodology.
  • AI use is permitted but candidates must explain and verify every key decision.
  • Evals, metrics, and attribution form the largest scoring dimension at 20%.
  • Five veto conditions override total score, e.g., data leaks or no rollback.

Assessment Structure (Five Components)

Enrollment diagnostic (ungraded, 30-minute self-test) → Process assignments 30% → Midterm project 20% → Technical delivery project 30% → Final client defense 20%.

ComponentWeightFormat
Enrollment diagnosticUngraded30-minute self-test: five-question quick check + deployment gap examples (used for placement and diagnosis; no elimination threshold)
Process assignments30%Practice deliverables from each chapter, scored on a two-level scale: "completed / verifiable"
Midterm project20%Deliverables from the Discover + Prototype phases
Technical delivery project30%Deliverables from the Validate + Go live phases: eval report + launch plan + production engineering artifacts
Final client defense20%Three presentations + on-the-spot adjustment of ad-hoc variables (i.e., the F16 capstone defense)

AI usage rules: AI coding assistants are allowed for process assignments, the technical delivery project, and the final defense. The assessment focuses on "explain, verify, modify" — learners must be able to explain every key decision in their deliverables, verify results with tests / evals, and identify and fix errors in AI suggestions (the live defense will spot-check with questions like "why is this line written this way"). Exercises marked "no AI" are optional diagnostics, used to reveal genuine understanding gaps when working without AI, and do not count toward the main assessment score.

Full Text of the 16 Assessment Questions (Six Groups)

Each question is mapped to the six stages of the five-step method. Training method: answer under time limits → self-assess against the practice deliverables of the corresponding chapter → return to the corresponding chapter and redo experiments for weak areas.

Group A · Cognitive Judgment (3 questions)

Tests "whether you truly understand this role." (Failing to articulate the specific mechanism of the product feedback loop means not passing.)

A1 · Prerequisite knowledge Explain in 3 minutes the difference between FDE and pre-sales / implementation / on-site outsourcing — in terms of emphasis of responsibilities (the FDE's focus typically falls on: writing production code and being accountable for outcomes, handling on-site issues, and having a product feedback channel), and explain that this is a spectrum rather than an absolute criterion (compare dimension by dimension against the 5.2 five-dimension evaluation). Tests: whether you understand that adjacent roles are a spectrum of responsibility emphasis rather than a true / false binary. Scoring points: compare dimension by dimension against the 5.2 five-dimension evaluation: problem definition authority, engineering responsibility, production outcomes, reuse and feedback, business involvement; able to explain that this is a difference in responsibility emphasis, not a true / false criterion for roles; able to describe the specific mechanism of the product feedback channel (playbooks / components / product feedback tickets).
A2 · Prerequisite knowledge Use the five-dimension evaluation (problem definition authority / engineering responsibility / production outcomes / reuse and feedback / business involvement, with billing method recorded separately and not used as a veto item) to judge whether an FDE position with "hourly billing, no product team, no eval requirements" is compliant, giving a dimension-by-dimension judgment. Tests: mastery of how to use the five-dimension evaluation and the billing criterion (billing recorded separately, not a one-vote veto). Scoring points: evidence and judgment given for each dimension; billing method recorded separately and not used as a veto item; conclusion identifies "which dimensions are missing and which adjacent role it drifts toward."
A3 · Prerequisite knowledge Explain deployment gap and give examples of model capabilities that cannot be directly converted into enterprise value (at least three of: data / processes / permissions / organization / compliance). Tests: whether you understand the first cause of the FDE's existence: the deployment gap. Scoring points: able to draw / clearly explain the composition of the deployment gap; examples cover at least three of data, processes, permissions, organization, and compliance, and the examples are about "model capabilities that cannot be directly converted" rather than "the model being inadequate."

Group B · Discover (2 questions)

Tests "whether you can converge chaotic demands into feasible, testable, authorizable scenarios."

B4 · ① Discover In 15 minutes, converge "roll out an enterprise-grade AI assistant to all employees": three types of interviewees, two candidate scenarios, the person being assessed, the metric being assessed, baseline, worst case, and the first data permission request. Tests: demand convergence: turning a slogan-like demand into verifiable scenarios and an authorization path. Scoring points: three types of interviewees and their purposes are clear; two candidate scenarios are evaluable; metric, baseline, and worst case are all present; the first data permission request is specific about target and scope.
B5 · ① Discover Three departments, three definitions of "ticket / valid customer / key account": design a lightweight Ontology covering entities, attributes, relationships, data sources, permission boundaries, and conflict handling. Tests: ability to model conflicting business definitions (Ontology design). Scoring points: entities / attributes / relationships are complete; each field's data source is labeled; permission boundaries are explicit; rules for handling conflicts among the three departments' definitions are given.

Group C · Prototype Technology (4 questions)

Tests "hands-on ability and permission engineering." (All can be completed with anonymized data or local models.)

C6 · ② Prototype A 48-hour store inspection report MVP: single scenario, real or anonymized data, a 3-minute demo script, with records of Prompt / model version / output samples. Tests: delivering a demonstrable, reproducible minimal prototype under time constraints. Scoring points: single scenario works end to end; data is real or anonymized; demo script ≤3 minutes; Prompt, model version, and output samples are logged and reproducible.
C7 · ② Prototype (prerequisite for ④ — permission engineering) Permission tiers for the inventory assistant's four tools (check inventory / check transfer orders / create transfer request / send follow-up notification): auto-execute / human confirmation / forbidden / audit log. Tests: tool permission tiering and audit design. Scoring points: each of the four tools is given a tier with rationale; high-risk actions retain human confirmation; audit logs cover who, when, and what was done.
C8 · ③ Validate A RAG eval plan for company policy documents: 30-item golden set, 10 hard cases, recall@5, citation accuracy, refusal criteria, baseline, before-and-after optimization comparison. Tests: RAG eval set and metric design. Scoring points: golden set / hard case composition is reasonable; metric definitions (recall@5, citation accuracy, refusal) are recomputable; a baseline and before-and-after optimization comparison design exist.
C9 · ② Prototype For sales daily report anomaly detection, choose workflow or agent: give rationale across five dimensions — autonomous planning, number of tools, failure handling, step limit, human intervention points. Tests: selection judgment of workflow-first, agent-as-needed. Scoring points: rationale given for each of the five dimensions; conclusion is consistent with the dimensions (rather than fixing the conclusion first and then gathering reasons); human intervention points are explained.

Group D · Production Delivery (3 questions)

Tests "what to do when things go wrong after launch." (Focus on whether the interception layer and rollback are specific.)

D10 · ④ Production Delivery A four-level staged rollout plan: shadow mode / small traffic / dual-track comparison / full rollout, with promotion criteria, rollback criteria, and an owner for each level. Tests: specificity of the rollout path and promotion / rollback rules. Scoring points: each of the four levels has quantified promotion and rollback criteria; each level has an owner; when a staged rollout is not mechanically required, it can be tailored to the scenario with reasons given.
D11 · ④ Production Delivery After launch, the model cited a repealed policy; use trace to locate and fix it: document versioning, index updates, retrieval results, citation validation, regression testing, decommissioning of stale data. Tests: using observable evidence to locate and fix retrieval / index issues. Scoring points: the trace-based diagnosis path is complete; document versioning and index update mechanisms are implemented; citation validation and regression tests are rerunnable; there is a process for decommissioning stale data.
D12 · ④ Production Delivery The agent is induced to call "Create Payment Request"; complete a security retrospective: attack path, risk level, in-process interception, human confirmation, audit evidence, regression cases. Assessed: ability to conduct a security retrospective on privilege escalation and prompt injection. Scoring points: clear reconstruction of the attack path; risk rating backed by justification; in-process interception and human confirmation implemented; audit evidence and regression cases provided.

Group E · Client Communication (3 questions)

Tests whether technical commitments can be converted into numbers on the other party's performance scorecard.

E13 · ⑥ Project Closing Report "85% accuracy is still not good enough" — explain this separately to the business lead, the IT lead, and the CTO, and give each of them their own action items. Assessed: layered communication: one set of explanations and action items for the same technical conclusion, tailored to three types of roles. Scoring points: each of the three versions aligns with the other party's performance metrics; action items are specific and verifiable; no false promises, no dodging the problem.
E14 · ⑥ Project Closing Report Scope negotiation when the client adds three features with only one week left: current scope, impact, cost of change, minimal version, written minutes. Assessed: negotiating and documenting scope changes. Scoring points: impact and cost of change clearly explained; a minimal viable version provided; negotiation outcome captured in written minutes.
E15 · ⑥ Project Closing Report (feedback loop portion connects to ⑤ Feedback Consolidation) Attribution and next-week action plan for low usage despite meeting technical targets: user workflow, permissions, organizational push, training, feedback loop, metric design. Assessed: attribution and action planning for adoption problems. Scoring points: attribute along dimensions such as user workflow / permissions / organization / training rather than blaming users; action plan has owners and timelines; feedback loop mechanism reflected in metric design.

Group F · Final Comprehensive (1 question)

This is the full Chapter 30 graduation project pipeline: precisely the part the 48-hour challenge cannot test and that the graduation project must cover.

F16 · Full Coverage of the Six Stages (Discovery → Prototype → Validation → Production Delivery → Feedback Consolidation → Project Closing Report) Complete delivery of the "Store Operations Intelligent Assistant" for a chain retail business (Chapter 30 graduation project): 13 deliverables + two ad-hoc variables during the defense (the client requests going live a week early; the security team refuses to provide production data), with the plan adjusted on the spot — you cannot just demo a prepared demo. Assessed: end-to-end integrated application of the six stages of the five-step method and on-the-spot adjustment. Scoring points: all 13 deliverables complete and verifiable; a feasible solution for both ad-hoc variables within 10 minutes that does not sacrifice key security gates; able to explain every key decision during the defense.

How the Three Assessment Sets Differ in Purpose

  • Public practice questions (these 16 questions): the public practice set of the course assessment question bank, used for timed self-testing, self-assessment against each chapter's practice artifacts, and redoing experiments after identifying weak areas.
  • Course final defense (F16): the full Chapter 30 graduation project pipeline, covering the six stages of the five-step method — production delivery and feedback consolidation, the two stages the 48-hour challenge cannot test, are assessed separately by the graduation project.
  • FCB 48-hour challenge: another compressed assessment format (four stages: requirements clarification → working prototype → eval report → client defense); scenario packs are sealed separately and not mixed with this question bank; see the FCB white paper for the evaluation rules.

Seven Dimensions of Final Scoring

Evals · metrics · attribution (20%) is the largest single item — the watershed that distinguishes an FDE from a demo engineer.

DimensionWeight
Problem definition15%
Data · Ontology · permissions10%
Prototype and engineering15%
Evals · metrics · attribution20%
Security, compliance, monitoring, rollback15%
Client communication15%
Playbook · feedback · retrospective10%

Scoring principles: artifacts must be verifiable, the process auditable, and AI may be used but must leave a trace; each sub-item within every dimension must still pass individually.

Five Veto Gates

If any of the following occurs, the assessment fails regardless of the total score:

  • Serious data breach
  • High-risk actions executed without human confirmation
  • No rollback possible
  • Submitted results cannot be reproduced
  • Evals inconsistent with success metrics

Relationship with FCB

The course assessment aligns with the L1/L2/L3 capability tiers and the 48-hour challenge of the FCB capability benchmark, without setting up any new certification levels (FCB itself is not a certification and does not issue certificates). The key failure gates of the two are isomorphic: if any item in the table above appears in the final assessment, it fails no matter how high the technical score. Completing the course defense earns an FCB-aligned job capability assessment record; see the "Evidence chain: a deliverable portfolio you can submit" section of the FCB white paper for the evidence chain requirements.
