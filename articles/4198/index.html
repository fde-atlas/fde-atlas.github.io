FDE vs Solutions Architect (2026) - Roles, Pay, and Hiring

Webfuse · 2026-10-12 · 12 min read

Summary

Webfuse compares forward deployed engineers (FDEs) with solutions architects (SAs): SAs typically lead pre-sale solution design and technical validation, while FDEs write production code on customer systems and own the outcome after go-live. Analyzing 93 FDE and 53 SA postings plus Levels.fyi data (October 2026), median pay is nearly identical at roughly $216K–$218K. The piece scores both roles across six dimensions, lists failure modes like unpaid implementation work and accidental lock-in, and advises hiring based on what the posting asks for, not the title.

Key points

  • SAs design and validate pre-sale; FDEs build and productionize on customer systems
  • Median pay is nearly identical: $216,250 for FDEs vs $218,000 for SAs (Levels.fyi)
  • "Production" appears in 75% of FDE postings but only 40% of SA postings
  • Known failure modes include free implementation labor, accidental lock-in, and ownerless hand-offs
  • Large AI accounts often need both roles with a written design-to-build hand-off

Role Comparison

FDE vs Solutions Architect

A solutions architect typically leads solution design and technical validation, often before the contract. A forward deployed engineer typically builds and productionizes the solution with the customer, one customer at a time. Median pay is almost identical on Levels.fyi: $216,250 for FDEs and $218,000 for solutions architects (U.S., self-reported, October 2026). One question sorts them: does the job end at the design, or at running code on the customer's systems?

Quick Quiz

Which role does your situation need?

Five questions for buyers, hiring managers, and engineers weighing a move. We point you to an FDE, a solutions architect, or both.

// Your situation

An FDE fits

Your answers point at production: code on customer systems, one customer at a time, and a result to own after go-live. That is the FDE job. Check that the role carries an outcome metric and a product feedback loop, or it turns into consulting.

The TL;DR

Two roles. Design or delivery.

Neither role replaces the other. Pick by what the customer needs next: a design or a running system.

Pick an FDE if...

  • Signed contracts stall before production
  • The product needs customer data, tools, and permissions to work
  • Someone must own code on the customer's systems after go-live
  • Field findings should change the product roadmap
  • You can fund travel and deep time with one customer

Pick an SA if...

  • Customers need a target architecture before they buy
  • Deals need a technical evaluation, a prototype, or a proof of concept
  • One technical lead should own the design across several accounts
  • Account executives need a technical partner on strategy
  • Delivery teams or partners will build what the design specifies

Pay and hiring

Near-identical medians. Different pay mix.

Median total pay is almost identical on Levels.fyi: $216,250 for FDEs and $218,000 for solutions architects (U.S., self-reported, Oct 12, 2026). Posted U.S. ranges, read on the same day with the same rules, put the median midpoint at $216,104 for FDEs (46 postings, 18 companies) and $221,500 for SAs (19 postings, 12 companies). Our separate Oct 7 sample puts the FDE midpoint at $209,250 (28 postings with a USD base range, base only, from our FDE job postings analysis), so the figure moves with the sample. That is a market signal from selected, non-equivalent samples, not proof of equal pay. Every figure names its source, date, and type. Do not average across sources.

Where each one breaks

What goes wrong, and what goes wrong at the seam.

Both roles fail in known ways, and the worst failures happen at the hand-off from design to production. The items below come from job postings and the sources cited on this page.

Free implementation labor

With no outcome metric and no product feedback, the FDE does custom work the vendor never generalizes. That is consulting with a new title.

Webfuse analysis

Lock-in by accident

An FDE who ships fast without infrastructure as code, evals, or a named owner leaves a system only the vendor can operate. Contracts should list the hand-off artifacts as deliverables.

Webfuse analysis

Talent is scarce

Travel, ambiguity, and production-engineering demands narrow the pool. 52 of 102 FDE postings state a travel percentage, with a median stated maximum of 40%.

Webfuse posting dataset, Oct 2026

A relabeled architect

a16z (Jan 27, 2026) notes Palantir recast solutions and integration engineers as FDEs. A posting with no production code and no outcome metric describes an SA, whatever the title.

a16z, Jan 2026

Prototype on clean data, production on real data

OpenAI SAs build MVPs and proofs of concept with "anonymized or offline cuts of data." Production runs on the real thing, and the gaps land on whoever deploys.

The Pragmatic Engineer, Aug 2025

A design with no builder

Scale AI's SA posting names separate commercial, delivery, and engineering owners. If the hand-off from architect to builder is not written down, design decisions lose their owner.

Scale AI posting, Oct 2026

Portfolio stretch

OpenAI's architect covers "a named portfolio of customers." Breadth across accounts limits time on any one deployment. This is our inference from the posting, not a measured result.

OpenAI posting, Oct 2026

Title drift

OpenAI and Anthropic call the role Applied AI Architect, and Databricks splits pre-sales SAs from post-sale Delivery SAs. Read what a posting asks for, not what it calls the role.

Job postings, Oct 2026

Both lists draw on job postings and reporting published between Aug 2025 and Oct 2026. Titles move fast: read what a posting asks for, not what it calls the role.

Use Case Picks

Which role fits the situation?

Six common situations with a defended pick, then the hand-off that connects the two roles on one account.

The customer needs a target architecture before buying

Architecture discussions, a prototype, and a technical win are the core of SA postings at Databricks, Stripe, and AWS.

Signed deals stall before production

The bottleneck is deployment. Someone has to write production code on the customer's systems and own the result.

Design reviews for several accounts

Some SA roles own a named portfolio, such as OpenAI's Applied AI Architect. Design reviews are core SA work.

An AI agent that acts inside customer systems

Agents need tools, scoped permissions, an identity, and an eval set built on real cases. That work happens on the customer's infrastructure.

Planning a cloud migration

AWS's SA posting describes helping organizations "migrate existing workloads to the cloud." Design and best practice come first.

Strategic AI account, end to end

The SA typically leads solution design and validation, the FDE typically builds and productionizes it with the customer. The hand-off decides how much is lost between them.

How the two roles work one account

  • 1AE

    Qualify

    An account executive qualifies an insurer that wants an AI claims-intake agent.
  • 2SA

    Design and win the evaluation

    The SA runs discovery, draws the target architecture across models, data, integration, and security, and builds a prototype on anonymized claims. Output: a technical win and an agreed design.
  • 3SA + FDE

    Hand off the design

    Contract signed. The SA passes on the architecture, known gaps, promised integrations, and the stakeholder map.
  • 4FDE

    Build for production

    The FDE maps the real workflow, builds an eval set from historical claims, connects tools to the claims system, sets agent identity and permissions, and ships.
  • 5FDE

    Hand over and feed back

    The FDE hands runbooks to a named customer owner and files product gaps. The SA carries the pattern to the next account.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Short answers, each with its source and date.

Is a solutions architect the same as a forward deployed engineer?

No. Both are customer-facing technical roles, but the output differs. The Pragmatic Engineer (Aug 12, 2025) reports that OpenAI SAs "rarely write code on customers' infrastructure" and build MVPs or proofs of concept on anonymized or offline data, and that FDEs write code directly on customer infrastructure with customer tooling.

Do solutions architects write code?

Yes, mostly prototypes. Anthropic's Applied AI Architect posting asks for someone who ships prototypes regularly and can work in a real codebase, and Databricks interviews SA candidates with a live coding assessment. Production code on the customer's systems is more often FDE work: the word "production" appears in 70 of 93 FDE postings and 21 of 53 SA postings in our Oct 12, 2026 keyword analysis.

Who earns more, an FDE or a solutions architect?

Neither, on median data. Levels.fyi lists $216,250 median total pay for FDEs and $218,000 for solutions architects (U.S., self-reported, Oct 12, 2026). Posted U.S. ranges read the same day put the median midpoint at $216,104 for FDEs (46 postings) and $221,500 for SAs (19 postings, 3 labelled OTE). These are selected samples, so treat them as a market signal. Commission wording appears in 9% of postings for each role.

Is a solutions architect a sales role?

Often, but not always. In our Oct 12, 2026 keyword analysis, 51 of 53 SA postings use sales-cycle wording (pre-sales, POC, account executives, or sales team), and AWS's SA posting opens with its Global Sales team. Some SA roles are post-sale: Deepgram's SA owns customer engagements "in a post-sales capacity," and Databricks hires Delivery SAs as post-sale technical leads.

Can a solutions architect become an FDE?

Yes. Some FDE postings name the background directly: Ramp's Forward Deployed AI Solutions posting asks for "experience in solutions architecture, technical consulting, forward deployed engineering, or pre-sales engineering." The usual gap is production code depth and owning a system after go-live.

Why do OpenAI and Anthropic call it an Applied AI Architect?

It is their name for the solutions architect role in AI. Anthropic's posting describes the job as "a Pre-Sales architect," and OpenAI's describes the "CTO of your book of business" for a named portfolio of customers. Both companies hire FDEs as a separate role.

When should a company hire an SA vs an FDE?

Hire an SA when customers need a target architecture, a technical evaluation, or design reviews. Hire an FDE when signed deals stall before production and someone must write code on the customer's systems and own the result. Large AI accounts often need both, with a written hand-off between design and build.

What can an FDE do when the customer's workflow runs in a web app with no API?

Some workflows live in web apps the customer uses but does not own, such as a supplier portal or a legacy SaaS tool. An architect can design around it; the FDE has to make the agent work inside it. Webfuse is browser infrastructure for AI agents: it lets an agent work inside a live web session of a site the customer does not own, with no change to that site's code. Browser-automation frameworks and cloud browsers are the other common options.
