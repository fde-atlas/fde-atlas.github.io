How Much Do FDEs in India Earn: Forward Deployed Engineer Salary Data Comparison

Original title: 印度 FDE 薪资多少：前线部署工程师工资数据对比

FDE Times · 2026-11-04 · 6 min read

Summary

The article compares Indian FDE salary figures from seven sources and finds gaps of more than four times: Salary.com and Glassdoor cluster around ₹13–17 lakh, while Levels.fyi's 75th percentile reaches ₹42.2 lakh and Scaler lists ₹30–65 lakh for mid-level roles. The author argues no source is fabricating data; they simply measure different things—total compensation vs. averages, top product companies vs. the whole market, or borrowed ML engineer salaries—with tiny samples (Levels.fyi has only 12 data points). The practical advice: identify which market your employer belongs to, pick the closest benchmark, and negotiate with a range plus reasoning rather than a single number.

Key points

  • Salary.com and Glassdoor figures cluster around ₹13–17 lakh annually.
  • Scaler's ₹30–65 lakh range reflects top product companies and contradicts itself internally.
  • Levels.fyi's high tail rests on only 12 self-reported data points.
  • Second Talent prices FDEs using ML/AI engineer salary bands instead.
  • Ask four questions of any salary table: pay basis, companies, sample size, recency.

The various figures are not necessarily made up; they just cover different populations and time periods. Understanding them relies precisely on the FDE skill of analyzing customer data.

Key takeaways

  • Salary.com and Glassdoor cluster around ₹13–17 lakh; Levels.fyi's 75th percentile is about ₹42.2 lakh, and Scaler's table gives mid-level salaries of ₹30–65 lakh.
  • Each source measures something different: self-reported total compensation, estimates for top product companies, or salaries borrowed from ML engineers.
  • This job title is still new and samples are small, so averages are of limited use when evaluating your own offer.

Salary.com reports a median annual salary of about ₹14.07 lakh for a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) in India (1 lakh is 100,000 rupees). A table in Scaler's 2026 guide gives ₹30–65 lakh for mid-level roles alone. Scaler's lower bound is more than double Salary.com's median, and its upper bound is more than four times as high.

If you are preparing to negotiate a salary, considering a move from software engineering to FDE work, or hiring for an FDE team, this gap directly affects your decisions. There is no reason to believe any of these sources is fabricating numbers. The more likely situation is that each one measures something different, covers a different population, and reflects a different point in time.

Interpreting these numbers correctly is itself an FDE skill. The questions to ask when facing a salary table are the same as when facing customer data: who was counted, what was counted, when it was counted, and how many were counted.

Where do the low numbers come from?

Salary.com gives a narrow range: a 25th percentile of ₹11,29,889 and a 75th percentile of ₹16,53,596. The site does not publish the sample size for this role, only stating broadly that its database covers more than 25,000 job titles across more than 8,000 organizations.

The page was last updated on April 22, 2025, making it the oldest among these sources.

Glassdoor's numbers fall in the same range. The training provider Prepzee, citing Glassdoor, reports a median total compensation of about ₹13 lakh per year across India. Build Fast with AI cites Glassdoor data for Bangalore of ₹16,99,778 per year.

Such a narrow range may indicate that the data averages across many types of companies, and averaging is exactly what erases the long tail that salary negotiators most need to see. Prepzee itself suggests treating these ranges as reference indicators rather than benchmarks.

The high numbers measure something else

Levels.fyi tracks total compensation. On its India FDE page, the median is about ₹17.7 lakh, the 25th percentile is ₹16,15,766, and the 75th percentile is ₹42,23,893. All of these figures come from 12 user-reported salaries.

Levels.fyi's median is nearly identical to Glassdoor's Bangalore figure, while its 75th percentile is more than double the median. This shows the gap lies in the high-end long tail, not among people in the middle. But with only 12 data points, a few high-salary reports are enough to stretch that tail, so do not treat it as a pattern.

Scaler's numbers are higher, and clearly labeled: its India figures are market estimates for top product companies in 2026. Notably, Scaler lists Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, and ownyourcareer as its baseline sources.

In other words, Scaler shares some sources with the low-number camp, yet its conclusions are much higher. The most plausible explanation is that Scaler keeps only the top of the market, which is consistent with its own "top product companies" label.

Scaler is inconsistent even within the same page: the table gives mid-level salaries of ₹30–65 lakh, while the FAQ says ₹30–55 lakh. When a source contradicts itself by ₹10 lakh at the upper bound, it can only be treated as a rough estimate.

Build Fast with AI cites data from OYC Labs, giving a range of ₹18–28 LPA (LPA means lakh per annum) for those with 0–2 years of experience, clearly higher than Salary.com. The same article also acknowledges that aggregated data sources contradict one another.

Second Talent takes a different route. Since few employers in India use the FDE job title, it borrows salary ranges for ML/AI engineers: ₹6–14 LPA for junior roles and ₹28–65 LPA for senior ones. The rationale is that FDEs and ML engineers come from the same pool of experienced AI, platform, and full-stack candidates. This is not a dedicated FDE sample.

The gap comes from three things: some sources measure total compensation while others give only averages; some cover the whole market while others look only at top companies or borrow numbers from other roles; and some rest on just a handful of data points. It is not a case of one source being right and the others wrong.

One job title, several salary markets

Build Fast with AI points to the root cause: the same FDE job title appears both in the AI delivery units of IT services companies and at global AI labs that hire remotely in India. The article uses exactly this difference in employer type to explain why the data contradicts itself.

Scaler also acknowledges that because the job title is still taking shape, India-specific FDE salary data is limited, so its ranges are drawn from adjacent roles. The consequence is easy to infer: someone doing FDE work under a different job title will not appear on FDE salary pages, and vice versa.

Putting a ₹20 lakh offer on the table

Suppose you have three years of experience, live in Bangalore, and receive an FDE offer with total compensation of ₹20 lakh. Against Salary.com, it exceeds the 75th percentile (₹16.5 lakh). Against Glassdoor's Bangalore data, you are about ₹3 lakh above the average.

Prepzee records self-reported pay for FDEs with 1–3 years of experience in Hyderabad and Bangalore ranging from ₹15 lakh to ₹27 lakh, so ₹20 lakh falls within the range, slightly below the middle. On Levels.fyi, ₹20 lakh is just above the ₹17.7 lakh median and less than half the 75th percentile.

According to Build Fast with AI's data, this number falls within the ₹18–28 LPA range for people with 0–2 years of experience. According to Scaler's table, it is below the ₹30 lakh lower bound for mid-level. The same offer can be interpreted anywhere from "very good" to "below market."

So the real question to ask is: which category does the company making the offer belong to, and which source's sample is closest to that category. The problem is that no source clearly states what types of employers its sample includes, so any choice of benchmark here is only an informed guess, not a conclusion.

Scaler's range is a reasonable benchmark only if the employer is indeed a top product company, since that is the label Scaler gives itself. It says nothing directly about AI labs.

For the AI division of an IT services company, market-wide averages like Salary.com or Glassdoor may be closer, but verify them against what people actually working at that company say.

For globally hiring remote AI labs, none of these sources measures this group separately. The high-end long tail of Levels.fyi is the closest clue, but 12 data points are too few to serve as your only argument.

Read salary numbers the way you read customer data

Before using any number, ask four questions: Is this base salary or total compensation? Which companies are in the sample? How many people are in the sample? When was the data last updated?

Tested against the table above, no source can answer all four questions. Salary.com falls short on sample size and recency, Levels.fyi falls short on sample size, and Scaler and Second Talent do not specifically measure FDEs. So don't bring just one number into the negotiation room—bring a range, along with your reasoning for choosing that source for this type of company.

This way of reading applies beyond India. In any market where the FDE job title is still new, you may encounter the same three sources of distortion: different measurement definitions, different company populations, and samples that are too small.

Since Second Talent prices FDEs within the ML engineer range, the strongest basis for negotiating a salary within that range is evidence on your resume of both sides: the systems you built, and the results clients achieved with them.

Averages only answer the question "how much does the market pay." Before you sit down to negotiate, the question you need to answer is "which market does this company belong to," and no salary table can answer that for you.
