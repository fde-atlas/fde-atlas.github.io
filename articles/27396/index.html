Today's Judgment

Putting today's two domestic signals together forms a clear chain of warning: entering "production deployment" does not mean the return on investment has been realized.

A survey by Deloitte China and the University of Hong Kong shows that enterprises entering the production deployment stage rose from 56.0% to 64.4%, but nearly half reported AI returns below expectations, and 60% have yet to establish a systematic value measurement framework. On the same day, Shanghai Electric offered a manufacturing sample that connects real production line results, a national-level pilot testing base, and the FDE model: scheduling, inspection, and capacity all have concrete numbers, and the pilot testing base explicitly adopts the FDE model for outward replication.

The missing step in between is connecting real processes, data permissions, task baselines, and pilot validation. The core task of the FDE is shifting from "helping enterprises install a platform" to "pushing every scenario through value, integration, governance, and production validation".

Key Signals

Deloitte × HKU survey: 64.4% in production deployment, 48.5% report ROI below expectations

  • Sources: Deloitte China official release; Yicai
  • On September 22, Deloitte China and the University of Hong Kong released the report "Enterprise AI Adoption from the Decision-Maker's Perspective", surveying 205 executives. Enterprises in the production deployment stage rose from 56.0% to 64.4%, while the exploration and pilot stages fell from 36.7% to 24.9%. But value realization clearly lags behind: 48.5% reported actual returns below expectations, 44.4% could not clearly calculate ROI, and 60.4% have not yet established a systematic value measurement framework. The measurement criteria are also narrow: 87.3% mainly look at speed and work hours, 70.7% at cost, and only 21.5% involve revenue and customer metrics.
  • Why it matters: these numbers show there is a wide gap between "deployment rate" and "return rate". Enterprises can measure work hours but not revenue; they can count users but cannot calculate where the money went. If an FDE only delivers "going live" and "how many people are using it", what they deliver lands exactly at the starting point of that gap. Note: this is a survey of 205 executives; both "production deployment" and ROI depend on respondents' own definitions, so it cannot be extrapolated as a nationwide census conclusion.

Shanghai Electric: AI flexible production line delivers results, national-level pilot testing base adopts the FDE model for replication

  • Sources: Shanghai SASAC; Development Research Center of the State Council citing Xinhua; Pilot testing base adopts the FDE model
  • On September 22, the Shanghai SASAC disclosed: the AI flexible blade production line at Shanghai Turbine Works can adapt to thousands of models; the scheduling system, with over 1,900 embedded process rules, compressed the annual scheduling of more than 300,000 blades—previously requiring 7-8 planners—down to 2 people; full-size inspection shrank from 2 days of manual work to 6 hours, with the missed-detection rate dropping from 3% to below 0.1%; after the full retrofit, blade capacity increased by 60%. The same day, Premier Li Qiang of the State Council visited Shanghai Electric's National AI Application Pilot Testing Base, proposing to build a manufacturing production service platform. The base's official plan from earlier this year already made clear that it adopts the FDE model, training, tuning, and repeatedly validating in real production environments before outputting replicable agents and standardized models.
  • Why it matters: this is a more complete industrial chain than "releasing 100 scenarios": the real production line first delivers task results, the pilot testing base validates stability and adaptability, and then replication to other enterprises happens through integration services. Worth noting is the "over 1,900 process rules": the foundation of scheduling performance is writing veteran engineers' process experience into rules, with the model being just one layer. The FDE's position in this chain is the connective tissue between industrial experience, model engineering, systems integration, production acceptance, and public services. Note: the figures come from SASAC/enterprise sources and only apply to the specific blade production line; the base's outward replication remains a construction goal, with no second customer acceptance yet.

Additional signal: model prices drop again, platform control planes continue to mature

  • Sources: OpenAI; Anthropic; Alibaba Cloud AgentCore
  • On September 22, OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol/Luna, saying API prices are 50% lower than the corresponding GPT-5.6 prices; Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 the same day, saying it matches Fable 5.1 on most tasks with running costs 40% lower than Opus 5. At the Yunqi Conference, Alibaba Cloud unveiled AgentCore, covering build-and-run, collaboration and governance, and observability and evals, supporting Workspace isolation, unified identity authentication, and token quotas.
  • Why it matters: model invocation costs are falling fast, but enterprises' integration, evals, governance, and change costs will not automatically fall along with them. Much of that 48.5% "returns below expectations" in the survey lies outside the model.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Build a "three-layer value ledger" for every project: the first layer is usage and tasks; the second is quality, time, human takeover, and cost; the third is revenue, conversion, retention, risk, or cash flow. A project with only first-layer data cannot be written up as an ROI success. Work-hour savings only become business value when they are released, cancelled, or reallocated to high-value work.
  • Freeze the business baseline before entering: 44.4% of enterprises cannot calculate ROI, and the common reason is failing to record "what it looked like before" before starting work. Once work has begun, the baseline cannot be recovered.
  • Use the "five pilot-testing certificates" for manufacturing project acceptance: real equipment/data, baseline operating conditions, continuous operation cycles, anomaly and human takeover records, and results replicated to a second line or second enterprise. A working demo doesn't count.
  • Pass six gates before platform procurement: real need → existing systems and data → organizational permissions → scenario baselines → secondary development and exit autonomy → operational responsibility and total cost. A good platform demo does not mean these gates can be skipped.
