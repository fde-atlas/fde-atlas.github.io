What is an FDE? A Complete Beginner's Guide to the Forward Deployed Engineer

Summary

This beginner's guide from FDEChina explains the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role: engineers who work on-site with customers, turning vague requirements into working AI systems while feeding field experience back to product teams. The role became known through Palantir's delivery model, and companies like OpenAI and Anthropic now post similar openings. The guide contrasts FDEs with solutions engineers, consultants, and AI engineers, outlines a typical project lifecycle from discovery to handover, and describes a four-layer skill model combining engineering, AI application skills, business communication, and on-site adaptability. 1. What Is an FDE

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role that brings engineering capability directly to customers and business sites: they both write code and understand the business, rapidly turning vague real-world requirements into working AI systems or software products in the customer's environment, and feeding frontline experience back to product and engineering teams.

Unlike the traditional "deliver and hand off" model, FDEs emphasize long-term presence at the customer's site: discovering requirements, defining solutions, implementing them, and validating value are often done by the same person or a small team. This role is especially common in AI application deployment, because the requirements of such projects are often hard to fully define upfront in an office.

2. Where the FDE Came From

The FDE role became widely known through Palantir's long-standing practice: its "forward deployed" delivery model has frontline engineers working directly with customer organizations to solve concrete problems (see public materials and blogs at palantir.com). Since then, more and more AI and software companies have adopted FDE or similar roles and delivery models, including several leading AI labs and AI application companies—OpenAI, Anthropic, and others have publicly posted job openings for Forward Deployed Engineer or similarly named positions (see the careers pages at openai.com and anthropic.com); investment firms such as a16z have also written about the rise of this role (see a16z.com).

Note: There is no single industry-standard definition of FDE. Different companies vary considerably in the scope of responsibilities, degree of on-site presence, and evaluation methods for this role; reading a specific company's job description matters more than memorizing any definition.

3. What an FDE Does in a Day

A typical workday may involve three types of activities: communicating with customers (clarifying requirements, demoing progress, gathering feedback), engineering implementation (building prototypes, integrating customer systems, debugging models and data), and retrospective consolidation (organizing field experience into reusable components and documentation). The time split among the three varies by project stage: communication dominates early on, while engineering and validation dominate in the middle and later stages.

Compared with engineers developing products in an office, an FDE's schedule is driven more by the customer's rhythm: customer launch deadlines, issues with on-site systems, and feedback from business stakeholders all go directly onto the FDE's to-do list.

4. How the FDE Differs from Other Roles

A few common comparisons:

  • FDE vs Solutions Engineer (SE) / Sales Engineer: The Sales Engineer's focus is on "selling the solution," with delivery usually handed off to an implementation team; the FDE's focus is on "delivering a working system," writing the code themselves and owning the outcome.
  • FDE vs Consultant: Consultants produce recommendations and solution documents; FDEs produce running systems and use those systems to validate whether the recommendations hold.
  • FDE vs AI Engineer: AI Engineers typically iterate on products within a product team; FDEs work in the customer's environment, facing the constraints of a real organization—data, processes, compliance, and people's habits.
  • FDE vs Traditional Outsourced Delivery: Outsourcing delivers according to contract scope; FDEs are tied to the customer's business goals, continuously adjusting solutions as requirements change, and channeling experience back to their own product teams.

More complete comparisons can be found in this site's "Delivery in Practice" and "FDE Observations" channels (see the navigation at the end).

5. Why the AI Era Needs FDEs More and More

AI application projects share a common trait: requirements are hard to fully specify in a contract. Large model capabilities are evolving rapidly, and enterprises' internal processes, data, and compliance constraints all differ, so "what counts as usable" often has to be defined through repeated validation in the real environment. This matches the FDE skill set exactly: understanding engineering implementation while staying close to the business to iterate quickly.

At the same time, AI coding tools have significantly raised the delivery ceiling of a single engineer—one person can now do the work that once required a small team—making "a small elite team delivering on-site" economically viable. This is an important part of the context in which many AI companies are re-emphasizing this role.

6. The Typical Lifecycle of an FDE Project

Although there is no unified template, most FDE projects go through similar stages:

  • Discovery: Interviewing business stakeholders to turn vague goals into verifiable problems;
  • Scoping: Choosing an entry point small enough to prove value;
  • Proof of Concept (PoC): Building a runnable prototype as fast as possible to validate technical feasibility and business value;
  • Engineering: Handling integration, data, security, and evals in the real environment;
  • Launch and Operations: Delivering to production and establishing monitoring and feedback channels;
  • Handoff and Retrospective: Transferring the system to the customer's team or an internal team, and consolidating reusable methods and components.

This site's "Delivery in Practice" channel is organized around these stages, with corresponding methods and practical materials for each.

7. What Skills an FDE Needs

Based on public job descriptions and frontline practice, the FDE competency model typically has four layers:

  • Engineering fundamentals: Solid programming, system design, and debugging skills, able to independently deliver production-grade code;
  • AI application skills: Understanding the capability boundaries of large models, and familiarity with common paradigms such as prompt engineering, RAG, AI agents, and evals;
  • Business communication: Understanding business language, turning vague requests into technical solutions, and translating technical constraints back into business language;
  • On-site adaptability: Driving work autonomously in customer environments with incomplete information and complex constraints, managing expectations and risks.

Among these, the combination of engineering and communication is most critical—roles strong on either side alone have well-established career paths; the scarcity of the FDE lies in being effective on both sides simultaneously.

8. Who Is Suited to Transition into an FDE Role

Common transition sources include: software engineers (adding business communication and domain understanding), Solutions Engineers and Sales Engineers (adding engineering implementation depth), technical consultants (adding delivery execution capability), and product managers (adding system implementation skills, usually requiring stronger programming). To judge whether you're a fit, ask yourself three questions:

  • Are you willing to work long-term in a mode where "requirements change and the answers are in the field"?
  • Can you drive work and deliver autonomously without a complete specification?
  • Do you enjoy working with both customers and code, rather than choosing one or the other?

If all three answers are yes, the FDE role is worth serious consideration. This site's "Career Development" channel offers advanced reading on competency models, transition paths, and interview preparation.

9. Which Companies Are Hiring for These Roles

Publicly visible FDE or similar job openings mainly come from a few types of companies:

  • AI labs and foundation model companies: such as OpenAI and Anthropic (deployment and delivery teams serving enterprise customers);
  • AI application and tool companies: such as Palantir and Cognition, companies centered on on-site delivery or AI coding products;
  • Large tech and B2B enterprises: among customer success, solutions, and AI deployment teams at cloud providers and software companies, many roles have responsibilities similar to FDEs, just with different job titles.

Note: Specific positions are subject to each company's recruitment page (this section only lists company types and representative titles, and does not constitute any job listing aggregation). This site's "Ecosystem Resources" channel continuously tracks public job postings.
