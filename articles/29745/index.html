FDE Deep Dive · In-Depth Breakdown of the China FDE Market

Original title: FDE 深度专题 · 中国 FDE 市场深度拆解

blog.ytso.com · 2021-08-10 · 18 min read

Summary

This in-depth piece dissects China's FDE market, where job postings have grown roughly 42-fold in two years with salaries of 450k-1.1M RMB, though only 20-30% of postings match the overseas definition of the role. Supply comes from cloud vendors (Alibaba, Tencent, Huawei), model companies (ByteDance, Zhipu, Moonshot), and integrators, while government, state-owned enterprises, hospitals, banks, and manufacturers account for ~80% of budgets. Hard constraints include the xinchuang domestic tech stack (Ascend adaptation is the biggest pain point) and four compliance gates including model filing (748 registered services by end-2025). Work is onsite-heavy, relationship-driven, with 6-18 month decision cycles and private deployment as default. The author argues the market is underestimated but talent overestimated, and that xinchuang adaptation skills form a unique moat—though premiums may erode within 3-5 years.

Key points

  • Job postings grew ~42x in two years, but only 20-30% match true FDE definitions.
  • Salaries run 450k-1.1M RMB; ByteDance's Doubao pays up to 1.1M annually.
  • Ascend adaptation PoCs take 2-4 weeks versus 1-2 days on Nvidia stacks.
  • Four compliance gates apply; 748 generative AI services registered by end-2025.
  • Work is onsite-heavy and relationship-driven, with 6-18 month government decision cycles.

Placed in the Chinese context, the three letters "FDE" no longer carry Palantir's romantic narrative of dismantling IEDs in the Iraqi desert, but instead follow a completely different set of rules: the hard constraints of Xinchuang (indigenous IT) full-stack requirements, the lengthy decision chains of state-owned and central enterprises, compliance thresholds around classified protection (MLPS) and algorithm filing, an obsession with private deployment, and a field-work style where "relationships drive" more than "code drives." This special report delivers a merciless teardown of the Chinese FDE market, covering scale, players, customers, compliance, working style, policy, and the bubble.

I. Market Size and Growth Rate: How the 42x Was Calculated ​

The most frequently cited figure for the Chinese FDE market comes from the explosive hiring curve. According to reports from Cailian Press and JM News, over the past two years, job postings for "FDE/Forward Deployed Engineer" roles on mainstream recruitment platforms grew roughly 42x (some platforms report 800% growth; the difference stems from the width of statistical windows and keyword sets). Over the same period, comparable roles on overseas platforms like Lagou and LinkedIn grew about 5-8x, making the Chinese market the absolute "overspeed zone."

In terms of job counts, based on public aggregated data from Liepin, BOSS Zhipin, and Zhaopin for 2024-2025, FDE-related JDs (keyword hits for "Forward Deployed Engineer / FDSE / Forward Deployed Engineer / AI Solutions Engineer / LLM Implementation Engineer") stably number around 1,500-2,500, of which:

  • Cloud vendors (Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud, Huawei Cloud, Baidu Intelligent Cloud, Volcengine) account for roughly 35-40% combined;
  • LLM companies (ByteDance Doubao, Zhipu, Moonshot AI, Baichuan, MiniMax, StepFun) account for roughly 25-30% combined;
  • System integrators and industry ISVs (Digital China, Chinasoft International, iSoftStone, Taiji, Neusoft, Yonyou, Kingsoft Office) account for roughly 20% combined;
  • Overseas vendors' China teams (Palantir largely shut out; Databricks and Snowflake mainly serving foreign enterprises in China; Anthropic/OpenAI with limited penetration via partners) account for less than 5% combined;
  • Industry-vertical FDEs (specialist providers in finance, healthcare, energy, and government) account for roughly 10-15%.

Salary bands are the most direct yardstick for judging a role's "value." The salary distribution for Chinese FDEs is as follows (public JDs and offers, 2024 Q4 – 2025 Q2):

For comparison: OpenAI / Anthropic frontline FDEs earn roughly $170-200K annually (about 1.15-1.36M RMB), with total packages exceeding 2M RMB after equity. Chinese top employers can approach the cash portion, but after liquidity discounts on equity there remains a 30-50% gap. This salary band means FDE is clearly a "high-paying new role" in China, but not everyone starts at 500K—at tier-2/3 integrators, the FDE is in reality just a "senior delivery/pre-sales person with an AI label," and the pay is not impressive.

The flip side of the growth is "water": of the 42x growth, only perhaps 20-30% of roles are genuinely hired to the overseas FDE definition (embedded, accountable for business outcomes, able to independently write code + tune models + run field work); many JDs are simply traditional "AI Solutions Architects / Industry Delivery Managers" with a new title. This is explored further in the bubble discussion in Section 9.

II. Driving Players: Three Types of Players and Their Respective FDE Positioning ​

The supply side of the Chinese FDE market is held up by three types of players, each with different DNA, and their FDE positioning differs accordingly.

2.1 Cloud Vendors: Alibaba Cloud / Tencent Cloud / Huawei Cloud ​

Cloud vendors are the "infrastructure providers + largest employers" of Chinese FDEs, and the three companies' playbooks diverge markedly:

  • Alibaba Cloud: pursues a dual engine of "industry solutions + LLM platform." The FDE role is officially titled "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)," headquartered in Hangzhou, primarily serving the implementation of Qwen for government and enterprise customers. Alibaba Cloud FDEs' typical customers are government cloud, financial cloud, and manufacturing cloud-migration clients; FDEs must understand both in-house platforms like PAI / Bailian / Function Compute and the business flows of the customer's industry. Customer scale determines the FDE's leverage—a single customer can consume tens of millions of RMB in compute annually, and the FDE's value lies in "using the model correctly, saving tokens, and drilling deep into scenarios."
  • Tencent Cloud: follows the "industrial internet + C2B" path; FDEs sit within CSIG (Cloud and Smart Industries Group), serving the AI transformation of Tencent Meeting, Tencent Docs, and WeCom, as well as private LLM deployment in finance, healthcare, and cultural tourism. Tencent FDEs' hallmark is "product + delivery in one"; many FDEs also leverage the Tencent Hunyuan LLM and the Tencent ecosystem (WeChat Mini Programs, Channels) for scenario grafting.
  • Huawei Cloud: follows the "full-stack Xinchuang + Pangu LLM" route and is the first choice for central/state-owned enterprises and government customers. Huawei's FDEs are deeply bound to the "Kunpeng + Ascend + openEuler + Pangu" full stack; customers are mainly State Grid, China Southern Power Grid, the three major telecom operators, the big four banks, and ministry information centers. Huawei FDEs carry the heaviest field workload, because the Ascend compute stack requires extensive adaptation work and customers have the strongest private-deployment demands.

In addition, Baidu Intelligent Cloud (ERNIE + Kunlun chips), Volcengine (Doubao LLM + ByteDance's algorithm engineering capabilities, extremely strong in recommendation/advertising/e-commerce scenarios), and JD Cloud (retail and supply chain scenarios) each have their own focus.

2.2 LLM Companies: ByteDance Doubao / Zhipu / Moonshot AI / Baichuan ​

LLM companies are the fastest-growing category of FDE roles, because they must upgrade from "selling APIs" to "selling solutions" and therefore must build field delivery capability:

  • ByteDance Doubao: FDEs are on Volcengine's headcount but in substance serve the To B implementation of the Doubao LLM. ByteDance's algorithm engineering capabilities (recommendation systems, A/B testing, data flywheels) are its FDEs' moat, and its salaries are the market's top tier (35-70K/month). Customers skew toward internet, e-commerce, and content platforms.
  • Zhipu AI: maker of the GLM series of LLMs; FDEs serve finance, government, and research customers. Zhipu FDEs are characterized by "deep technical expertise"—many have research backgrounds and can do joint modeling with customers' research institutes.
  • Moonshot AI (Kimi): FDEs focus on long-context and knowledge-work scenarios (legal, consulting, research institutes); customers have strong willingness to pay but small deal sizes, so FDEs must run "high-density small-customer" operations.
  • Baichuan / MiniMax / StepFun: each has industry focus (Baichuan in healthcare/government, MiniMax in content/going global, StepFun in multimodal); FDE teams are smaller but growing fast.

2.3 Integrators: The Real Operators of Government and Central/State-Owned Enterprise Projects ​

Doing FDE work in China means you can't avoid system integrators. Government and state-owned enterprise procurement rules dictate that the contracting entity is usually a prime integrator like Digital China, Tongfu, Chinasoft International, Taiji, Neusoft, Yonyou, the CETC group, or the CEC group, and FDEs (whether from cloud vendors, LLM companies, or employed by the integrators themselves) must embed themselves in the prime integrator's delivery framework. This is the biggest structural difference between Chinese and overseas FDEs: overseas FDEs deal directly with the customer's business side, while Chinese FDEs spend over 70% of their time first managing integrator relationships before ever reaching the real business pain points.

III. Typical Customer Profiles: Who Pays for FDEs ​

Chinese FDE customers are highly concentrated, showing a pattern of "five industries eating 80% of the budget":

3.1 Government (Central Ministries + Local Governments) ​

  • Typical customers: provincial big data bureaus, city operations centers, "one-stop government services" operators, 12345 government hotlines, and government clouds at all levels.
  • Typical scenarios: government LLM Q&A, intelligent retrieval of policy documents, intelligent dispatch and quality inspection of 12345 tickets, and decision support for city governance.
  • Decision characteristics: the longest decision chain (business division → IT center → big data bureau → executive in charge → bidding), with cycles of 6-18 months; FDEs must endure "repeated research, repeated PoCs, and only then a signed deal."

3.2 Central and State-Owned Enterprises (Energy, Telecom, Finance, Infrastructure) ​

  • Typical customers: State Grid, China Southern Power Grid, the three major carriers (China Mobile/Telecom/Unicom), the Big Four banks plus joint-stock banks, PetroChina/Sinopec, China Railway/China State Construction.
  • Typical scenarios: intelligent equipment O&M (image recognition for grid inspections, transformer failure prediction), customer service and agent assistance, contract and bidding document review, knowledge bases and employee assistants.
  • Decision characteristics: Xinchuang (IT application innovation) compliance is a hard threshold (see Section 5); budgets are large (millions to tens of millions per project), but internal IT capabilities vary widely, making the FDE's "on-site companionship" extremely valuable.

3.3 Top-Tier (Grade IIIA) Hospitals ​

  • Typical customers: top Grade IIIA hospitals in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou and other provinces, regional medical groups, and national regional medical centers.
  • Typical scenarios: intelligent quality inspection of electronic medical records, imaging-assisted diagnosis, research literature retrieval, pre-consultation triage, and follow-up phone bots.
  • Decision characteristics: extremely strict data compliance (medical data cannot leave the hospital, so FDEs must do on-site private deployment); doctors' time is precious (requirements gathering must happen between ward rounds and case discussions); payment decisions require approval from three parties: the medical affairs office, the IT office, and hospital leadership.

3.4 City Commercial Banks and Joint-Stock Banks ​

  • Typical customers: leading city commercial banks (e.g., Bank of Beijing, Bank of Jiangsu, Bank of Ningbo) and joint-stock banks (China Merchants, Ping An, Industrial Bank).
  • Typical scenarios: corporate due diligence report generation, credit approval assistance, anti-money laundering and anti-fraud, intelligent customer service, and investment research report generation.
  • Decision characteristics: high demands for model controllability (preferring privately deployed open-source models like Qwen / GLM / DeepSeek), strict compliance review (model outputs must be traceable and explainable), and high per-scenario value (a single corporate due diligence scenario can save dozens of FTEs).

3.5 Large Manufacturing Groups ​

  • Typical customers: BYD, Sany Heavy Industry, Haier, Midea, CATL, Baowu Steel, SAIC.
  • Typical scenarios: process knowledge capture, predictive equipment maintenance, visual quality inspection, supply chain coordination, R&D knowledge bases, and sales configurators.
  • Decision characteristics: fragmented scenarios (FDEs must deliver "N small scenarios in the factory" rather than one big platform), poor data quality (50% of FDE work is data governance rather than modeling), and harsh on-site environments (workshops, production lines, warehouses).

IV. Full-Stack Xinchuang: The Hard Constraint Chinese FDEs Cannot Bypass ​

"Xinchuang" (IT application innovation) is a full-stack constraint that Chinese To B delivery cannot bypass, directly determining whether an FDE can get in the door and close out the project. The complete Xinchuang stack's "PKS system" + "domestic middleware" is usually described as:

Ascend adaptation is currently the most painful part of FDE work. Since Nvidia H100/A100 exports to China are restricted, many central and state-owned enterprise customers can only use Ascend 910B/910C, Cambricon MLU, or Hygon DCU as substitutes, and FDEs must get inference running on a hybrid PyTorch + CANN + MindSpore stack, do quantization (INT8/INT4), replace operators, and handle accuracy degradation. A typical "Ascend adaptation PoC" often consumes 2-4 weeks of FDE time, far exceeding the 1-2 days on the Nvidia stack. This is the most tangible gap between Chinese and overseas FDE work: overseas FDEs tune prompts and agents, while Chinese FDEs spend half their time wrestling with heterogeneous compute stacks.

The "PKS system" of full-stack Xinchuang (Phytium CPU + Kylin OS + the CEC ecosystem) and Huawei's "Kunpeng + Ascend + openEuler + Pangu" system are two parallel domestic solutions, and FDEs often have to support both, doubling the workload. This is also why Huawei Cloud and the CEC group (CEC Huada, CEC Les) win so many central and state-owned enterprise orders—they hold the most complete Xinchuang stacks.

V. The Four Compliance Essentials: MLPS / PIPL / DSL / Filing ​

Compliance is the FDE's "birth certificate" in China; every scenario must pass four gates first:

5.1 MLPS 2.0 (Cybersecurity Multi-Level Protection Scheme) ​

  • Scope: almost all government and enterprise information systems; core systems must be classified at Level 3 or above.
  • Impact on FDEs: LLM applications, as newly added subsystems, must be included in MLPS classification; FDEs must assist with security boundaries, access control, log auditing, and vulnerability scanning; Level 3 MLPS requires "active-active/disaster recovery + centralized auditing," directly driving up deployment costs.

5.2 Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL, effective 2021) ​

  • Scope: all activities involving the processing of personal information.
  • Impact on FDEs: personal information in training data must be desensitized/de-identified; customer service, imaging, and medical record scenarios require separate consent; FDEs must deliver "data usable but not visible" solutions (federated learning, privacy-preserving computation, trusted execution environments)—a layer that is practically a hard requirement in finance and healthcare scenarios.

5.3 Data Security Law (DSL, effective 2021) ​

  • Scope: all data processing activities, with key industries having "important data" catalogs.
  • Impact on FDEs: cross-border transfer of government, energy, and telecom customers' "important data" is strictly restricted, making cross-border calls to overseas model APIs nearly infeasible; data classification and grading, plus full-lifecycle data management, must be written into the solution; private deployment becomes the default option.

5.4 Algorithm Filing and Large Model Filing ​

This is the newest compliance increment since 2023, and a regulatory design unique to China:

  • Algorithm filing: Under the *Provisions on the Administration of Algorithmic Recommendations for Internet Information Services* (effective 2022), algorithm services with public opinion attributes or social mobilization capabilities must be filed with the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC).
  • Large model (generative AI service) filing: Under the *Interim Measures for the Administration of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services* (released July 2023, effective August 15, 2023), providing generative AI services to the public requires filing with the CAC and conducting a security assessment.
  • Growth in filing numbers: In April 2024, the first batch of about 117 large models completed filing; by August 2024, over 190 large models had been filed and launched; by the end of 2025, a cumulative 748 generative AI services had completed filing; by early 2026, Beijing alone had 218 and Zhejiang 70.
  • Local incentives: Wuxi and other cities offer a one-time reward of up to 500,000 RMB for enterprises completing national-level large model filing for the first time, plus tiered rewards based on the number of application adopters (up to 300,000 RMB each).

Practical implications for the FDE: To C or public-facing large model applications must use already-filed models (otherwise they cannot go live); To B private deployments are not required to file, but customers' compliance departments usually require "the model itself is filed" as a precondition for procurement. This means that for an FDE, whether a model is on the list of 748 filed models is a hard threshold during model selection. Meanwhile, content safety (value alignment, sensitive word filtering, refusal mechanisms) is a capability that FDEs in China must enable by default—running overseas open-source models out of the box is essentially unusable.

VI. The Localized Working Style of China's FDEs: On-Site Presence, Relationships, Long Cycles, Private Deployment ​

Transplanting the overseas FDE working style (traveling light, two-week sprints, remote collaboration, reporting to engineering leads) to China will basically fail. The Chinese FDE working style has four distinctive characteristics:

6.1 Heavy On-Site Presence: Trust Requires Being There ​

Chinese customers are inherently distrustful of "remote delivery," especially government agencies, central and state-owned enterprises, and top-tier (Grade IIIA) hospitals. FDEs average 10-20 days of on-site work per month, with some key projects requiring continuous on-site presence for 3-6 months. On-site presence is not just a technical necessity but a relationship necessity—customer IT department staff need to see you at your desk, eat with you in the cafeteria, and chat with you after work before they are willing to hand over real requirements and real data. This closely resembles Palantir's early Echo-Delta model of "embedding engineers at customer workstations," but Chinese FDEs' on-site intensity is often higher and the cycles longer.

6.2 Heavy on Relationships: The "Soft Skills" Beyond Technology ​

In Chinese To B decision-making, the technical solution may only carry 40-50% of the weight, with relationships, brand, compliance endorsements, and past case studies making up the other half. An FDE is not just an engineer but also serves as the "second touchpoint for customer relationships" (a trust anchor beyond the primary salesperson): able to explain ROI clearly to customer leadership, coordinate internal resources to fight for priority, and "vouch" for the customer in the customer's internal meetings. The Echo role of Echo–Delta is amplified in China—many FDEs spend 60% of their time on relationship exploration and requirement framing, and only 40% writing code and tuning models.

6.3 Long Decision Chains: A 6-18 Month War of Patience ​

The typical decision chain for Chinese government and enterprise customers is: business department raises requirements → IT center initiates the project → procurement via bidding → prime contractor wins → subcontracting to ISVs → FDE enters the field. A project worth tens of millions of RMB takes an average of 6-12 months from first contact to contract signing, and large central state-owned enterprises may take 12-18 months. FDEs must provide continuous technical support throughout the long "project initiation-bidding-winning-entry" cycle (free PoCs, solution reviews, bid document writing support); this "pre-sales FDE work" accounts for 30-40% of an FDE's total working hours in China, far higher than overseas.

6.4 Private Deployment First: Data Stays In-Domain Is the Bottom Line ​

Chinese customers have low acceptance of calling large model APIs on public cloud; the four major industries—government, finance, healthcare, and energy—almost 100% require private deployment. This directly affects the FDE's tech stack: they must be familiar with offline deployment on K8s + domestic OS + domestic GPUs, model quantization, local vector databases, local knowledge bases, and local content safety modules. An overseas FDE can demo by writing a bit of LangChain calling the OpenAI API; a Chinese FDE must first set up the entire infrastructure in the customer's server room, a step that often eats up the first two weeks.

VII. Policy Drivers: Digital China, New Quality Productive Forces, the AI+ Initiative, and Data as a Factor of Production ​

The explosion of the Chinese FDE market is half driven by technology and half by policy. Four policy mainlines serve as the market's "water level":

  • Overall Layout Plan for Digital China Development (2023): Elevates digitalization to a national-level strategy, establishing the "2522" framework (two foundations, five-in-one, two capabilities, two environments), directly spawning massive projects in government cloud, city brains, and industry clouds, with FDEs as the execution endpoint of these projects.
  • New Quality Productive Forces (a high-frequency policy term since 2024): Lists AI, new energy, biomanufacturing, and others as representatives of new quality productive forces, requiring central and state-owned enterprises to "take the lead in using AI," which directly translates into large model procurement budgets. Large model tenders from customers like State Grid, PetroChina, and China Mobile are essentially the implementation of new quality productive forces policy.
  • "AI+" Initiative (first proposed in the 2024 Government Work Report): Promotes deep integration of AI with all industries, spawning special initiatives such as "AI+healthcare," "AI+education," "AI+manufacturing," and "AI+agriculture," with an FDE team behind each initiative.
  • Marketization of Data as a Factor of Production (the "Data Twenty Measures" + the National Data Administration): Establishes data as a factor of production, promoting data exchanges, data assets on balance sheets, and authorized operation of public data. FDEs play a key role in the intersection of "data as a factor × large models" (e.g., training large models on public data, compliant circulation of medical data).

The other side of policy is local AI computing vouchers and computing subsidies: Beijing, Shanghai, Hangzhou, Chengdu, Shenzhen, and other cities issue computing vouchers (from hundreds of thousands to millions of RMB per enterprise) to enterprises using local computing power or locally filed large models, directly lowering the customer payment threshold for FDE projects. FDEs need to know how to "stack subsidies" when quoting prices—a local skill that overseas FDEs never need to consider.

The bar is higher, but once you make it work, the moat is deeper.

IX. Opportunity and Bubble: Don't Be Fooled by the 42x Growth ​

The opportunity in China's FDE market is real, and so is the bubble. A sober look:

9.1 Real Opportunities ​

  • The installed base of AI transformation at central and state-owned enterprises: Central and state-owned enterprises with assets in the hundreds of trillions are moving from "informatization" to "intelligentization". The next 3-5 years are the window for LLM deployment, and FDEs are a scarce execution resource.
  • The engineering dividend of the domestic compute stack: The ecosystems of Ascend, Cambricon, and Hygon DCU are still immature. Whoever can achieve end-to-end engineering of "domestic GPU + domestic models + industry scenarios" captures a premium. This is an engineer dividend unique to Chinese FDEs.
  • The compounding of vertical industry know-how: Domain knowledge in healthcare, legal, energy, and manufacturing runs deep. The industry model assets FDEs accumulate are irreplaceable, and in 5 years these people will become the "top experts" in industry AI.
  • Policy stability as a floor: Digital China, new quality productive forces, and the AI+ initiative are medium-to-long-term national strategies. Budgets won't suddenly disappear, and the market ceiling is guaranteed.

9.2 Real Bubbles ​

  • Title inflation: Within the 42x growth, many JDs are simply "Solutions Architect / Industry Delivery Manager / Sales Engineer" renamed as FDE, with the actual work unchanged. Job seekers should beware of the trap of "doing pre-sales work under an FDE title".
  • Skill mismatch: Overseas FDEs are expected to "write code + tune models + understand customers", but many FDE-titled roles in China only require "understanding customers + writing proposals", diluting technical depth. There are no more than 1,000 full-stack FDEs on the market who understand both Ascend operators and customer business.
  • Homogeneous involution: LLM companies cluster around government LLM projects, integrators cluster around bids, and scenarios overlap heavily (government Q&A, contract review, customer service assistance). FDEs repeatedly run PoCs on homogeneous projects, making it hard to accumulate lasting value.
  • Price wars: Cloud vendors and LLM companies slash project bids for market share, compressing FDE team productivity. "High salary + high intensity + low leverage" becomes the norm, and some teams' per-capita output is actually below salary cost.
  • Compliance arbitrage risk: Some FDE projects go live quickly by "using unfiled models + skirting the rules". Once regulation tightens (especially content safety and cross-border data transfer), projects may be halted, and FDEs personally bear career risk.

9.3 Judgment Criteria for Practitioners ​

Whether an FDE role is a "real FDE" comes down to three things: first, whether it is accountable for business outcomes (KPI/ROI), not just delivering systems; second, whether it has hard technical requirements to write code and tune models, not just write proposals; third, whether it allows accumulating transferable industry know-how, not just internal coordination at one company. If all three hold, it's a real opportunity; if only the title holds, it's a bubble.

X. Three Judgments on China's FDE Market ​

Breaking the market down to its foundations, three not-so-gentle judgments can be made about China's FDE market:

Judgment one: China's FDE market size is underestimated, but talent quality is overestimated. Real job demand will keep doubling over the next 3 years, but the supply of talent that truly meets overseas FDE standards is severely insufficient. What the market lacks is not "FDEs", but hybrid FDEs who "can simultaneously handle the domestic compute stack + domestic LLMs + industry business + customer relationships". The salary ceiling for such people is now well above 1.1 million RMB, and top employers are willing to pay 1.5 million+.

Judgment two: Integrators will become the largest employers of FDEs in China, but with the lowest capability ceiling. The bidding structure of government and central/state-owned enterprise projects makes integrators the main project entities, and integrators will build large in-house FDE teams. But integrators' business model (per-person-day billing, thin gross margins) means they can't offer the highest pay and struggle to attract top technical talent; FDE growth curves at integrators will lean toward "relationships + management" rather than "technology + innovation".

Judgment three: Xinchuang (IT application innovation) adaptation capability is the only moat of Chinese FDEs that overseas counterparts cannot replicate. Overseas FDEs coming to China can't do Ascend adaptation, can't pass LLM filing, and can't navigate central/state-owned enterprise decision chains; when Chinese FDEs go abroad, this skill set has no export market. This means Chinese FDEs are "a scarce resource in a closed market": in the short term their bargaining power will keep rising, but in the long term (3-5 years), as toolchains mature and the engineering barrier drops, the premium will be flattened. Now is the best window to enter.

Summary of This Special Topic ​

  • China's FDE market hiring demand grew 42x in two years, with a salary band of 450k-1.1 million RMB; top LLM companies (ByteDance Doubao) can reach 35k-70k RMB/month, with annual packages of 550k-1.1 million RMB.
  • Three types of players on the supply side: cloud vendors (Alibaba Cloud/Tencent Cloud/Huawei Cloud, with full-stack Xinchuang advantages), LLM companies (ByteDance/Zhipu/Moonshot/Baichuan, shifting from selling APIs to selling solutions), and integrators (Digital China/Taiji/Neusoft, the real operators of government and central/state-owned enterprise projects).
  • High customer concentration: five industries—government, central and state-owned enterprises, top-tier (Grade 3A) hospitals, city commercial banks, and large manufacturing—consume 80% of budgets.
  • The full Xinchuang stack (CPU Kunpeng/Phytium/Hygon + GPU Ascend/Cambricon/Hygon DCU + OS Kylin/UOS + DB DM/OceanBase + domestic models) is a hard constraint that cannot be bypassed, and Ascend adaptation is currently the most painful part of FDE work.
  • The four compliance essentials: MLPS 2.0, PIPL, Data Security Law, and algorithm/LLM filing (a cumulative 748 generative AI services filed as of the end of 2025) are the birth certificate of FDE projects.
  • Localized working style: heavy on-site presence (10-20 days per month on average), relationship-focused, long decision chains (6-18 months), private deployment first.
  • Policy-driven: four main threads—Digital China, new quality productive forces, the AI+ initiative, and data as a factor of production—support the market's water level.
  • The opportunity is real (central/state-owned enterprise AI installed base, domestic compute engineering, compounding vertical know-how), and so is the bubble (title inflation, skill mismatch, homogeneous involution, compliance arbitrage).
  • China vs. overseas FDE: China's bar is higher (domestic stack + compliance + relationships + on-site presence), and the moat is deeper; but the market is closed, and the premium will be flattened within 3-5 years as toolchains mature. Now is the best window to enter.
