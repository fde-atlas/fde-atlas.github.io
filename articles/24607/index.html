How to Become an FDE? A Roadmap from a Practitioner

Original title: FDE なるには？実践者が教えるロードマップ

日本Fde協会 · 2026-07-28 · 5 min read

Summary

This guide from the Japan FDE Association explains how to become a Forward Deployed Engineer, arguing there is no single path: engineers should build customer-facing skills, business people should learn AI and automation basics, and beginners should start by building something that works. It stresses that technology is only a prerequisite—problem framing wins deals—and highlights overlooked areas like access control, audit trails, and documentation. The association's 5-level FDE certification, starting with level 3, is suggested as a learning milestone, though the credential itself guarantees no job.

Key points

  • FDEs need technical, business-understanding, and communication skills used together on-site.
  • Roadmaps differ by starting point: engineers, business roles, and beginners each fill different gaps.
  • Building the product matters less than correctly framing the customer's problem.
  • Access control, auditing, and documentation are underrated FDE skills employers want.
  • The association's FDE certification level 3 is a suggested milestone, not a job guarantee.

"What should I start with to become an FDE?"—many of you who have found this article are likely interested in the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer—a engineer who works on-site with customers to solve challenges from both technical and business perspectives) way of working, but can't see the first step. We at the Japan FDE Association work to raise awareness of this role from the perspective of practitioners. Reading this article will make clear "what you should do next" from where you currently stand. Rather than directing you to specific job openings like a job site, we plainly present the conditions that anyone will need, no matter who you are or what you're aiming for.

What You Need to Become an FDE

There is no magic skill that instantly transforms you into an FDE. What's needed is the ability to simultaneously exercise three things—technical skill, business understanding, and communication—in a single field setting.

The Three-Piece Set: Technical, Business, and Interpersonal Skills

For technical skill, it's enough to be at a level where you can make judgment calls to "use AI, apps, and data integration where they're useful." Rather than cutting-edge research knowledge, implementation ability to build things that actually work matters. Business understanding is the ability to listen to and organize the flow of a customer's work. Interpersonal skill is the ability to explain the same issue in different words to both executives and frontline staff.

How This Differs from Being a "Jack-of-All-Trades"

Misunderstanding this will make you lose your direction. An FDE is not a handyman who takes on miscellaneous tasks, but a specialist who connects technology and business. For more on this distinction, also see What Is an FDE? A Practitioner Explains the Differences from SEs and SIers.

A Roadmap by Starting Point

Everyone's starting point is different. Rather than forcing yourself onto a single path, the fastest route is to build on your current strengths and fill in what's missing.

Current PositionStrengthWhat to Prioritize
EngineerImplementation skillsBusiness interviews and client relations
Business/Sales rolesBusiness understanding and interpersonal skillsAI utilization and basics of simple automation
No experience/Still learningRoom to growExperience building something that works

If You're Coming from Engineering

The more technical background you have, the more likely you are to talk about technology before understanding the customer's challenges. First, develop the perspective to ask, "Is that really necessary?" For ideas on how to approach interviews, FDE Interview Techniques is a helpful reference.

If You're Coming from a Business Role

If you know the business inside and out, we recommend starting with small experiments to reduce manual work using AI agents or automation tools. The steps are summarized in How to Approach AI-Driven Business Improvement.

What Practitioners Struggled with First

Maekawa, the representative director of the association, moved from being a financial trader to launching an e-commerce business, and initially experienced a period where, despite having technical skills, sales barely materialized. This was due to the assumption that "if you build something good with technology, it will sell." *What follows is based on his own reflections, but afterward, by thoroughly conducting market research and customer interviews, the numbers began to move.

Technology Is a Given; Problem Definition Is What Wins

This experience offers significant insight for those aiming to become FDEs. Being able to build something and solving a customer's problem are two different things. In startup settings, showing prototypes and iterating on feedback before requirements were finalized was effective. On the other hand, at manufacturing clients, care not to disrupt existing work procedures and permission structures built trust. Even within FDE work, the effective approach varies by industry.

Cover the Often-Overlooked Areas

Most market explanations of FDEs stop at "engineers who enter the customer's field." However, in actual implementation settings, you're also judged on permission management—who can access which data—audit considerations for keeping records of work, and documentation that keeps things running even when the person in charge changes. When talking with SaaS company executives, we often hear that what they lack is precisely people who can do this "design that prevents dependence on individuals." Mastering this raises your value as an FDE to another level.

Use Level 3 Certification as a Milestone

When thinking about "how to become an FDE," self-study tends to make you lose sight of where you stand. That's where the FDE Certification can serve as a learning goal.

Why Start with Level 3

The FDE Certification is a private credential operated by the Japan FDE Association, with five levels. It is not a national qualification, and not having it doesn't mean you can't do the work. Still, if you set the entry-level Level 3 (FDE Practitioner) as your first goal, fragmented knowledge will connect into a single map. The background behind creating the system is explained in Why We Created the FDE Certification.

A Concrete Next Step

First, grasp which areas you currently cover. Check where you stand with the Skill Check, and get an overview of the levels in What Is the FDE Certification? An Overview of the Five Levels and Exam Content. What you'll be able to do at Level 3 is summarized in What Is FDE Certification Level 3?.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Can I aim to become an FDE without prior experience?

A. Yes, you can. What matters is starting from one of technical skill, business understanding, or interpersonal skill, and filling in the gaps through field experience. Building experience creating something that works, however small, is the shortcut.

Q. Can't I become an FDE if I can't program?

A. Advanced development skills are not required. The ability to use AI and automation tools where they're useful and make judgment calls to solve customer problems is more important. If business understanding or interpersonal skills are your strengths, you can build on those and supplement the technical side.

Q. If I get the FDE Certification, will I definitely become an FDE?

A. The certification is a learning goal and a way to confirm where you stand. The credential itself does not guarantee a job, but it helps you systematically organize the necessary knowledge. For details, please see the certification page.
