Your Team Can Demo AI. Can They Deploy It Inside a Customer’s Stack?

ADaSci · 2026-05-25 · 4 min read

Summary

ADaSci argues enterprise AI projects usually fail between a working demo and a system running inside the customer's stack, and that closing this gap requires Forward Deployed Engineers. Its Forward Deployed Engineer Program combines a four-hour self-paced foundation, six instructor-led workshops (integrations, agentic AI, production DevOps, security and compliance, customer solutioning), and a capstone project. Completing the capstone earns the Certified Forward Deployed AI Specialist (CFDAS) designation and produces an enterprise-grade solution demo. The program targets engineers, solutions teams, and pre-sales staff, and can be run as corporate training.

Most enterprise AI projects don't fail at the model — they fail in the gap between a demo and a system that runs inside the customer's stack, and the Forward Deployed Engineer Program (leading to ADaSci's CFDAS certification) trains your team to close it.

Enterprise AI projects rarely fail at the model. They fail in the gap between a demo that works on a laptop and a system that runs inside the customer’s environment, under their security policies, against their APIs, on their data, at their scale. The engineers who close that gap are not classic data scientists and not traditional pre-sales architects. They are Forward Deployed Engineers: customer-facing builders who can run a discovery session in the morning, draft an architecture by midday, and stand up a working proof of concept in the client’s stack within days.

If your people sell, build, or deliver AI into other organizations, this skill set is now the limit on how fast you can grow. The Forward Deployed Engineer Program is designed to remove that limit, and to certify it through ADaSci’s Certified Forward Deployed AI Specialist (CFDAS) designation.

How the program is structured

The program runs on three components. A four-hour self-paced foundation brings everyone to a common baseline. Six instructor-led workshops cover the working knowledge an FDE applies on live engagements. A 30-minute assessment and a self-paced capstone project confirm that each participant can integrate the pieces, not just recall them. The full arc moves an engineer through discovery, architecture, integration, agentic systems, and production reliability.

The foundation that sets a shared starting line

Before the workshops, every participant completes Navigating Generative AI and LLMs. It refreshes core GenAI and LLM concepts, introduces Hugging Face and LangChain, and covers Docker, containers, and cloud basics. The point is to eliminate the uneven starting knowledge that slows most technical training, so the live sessions can move at pace rather than spending day one leveling up.

The six workshops

Technical Solution Leadership sets the operating model. Participants learn how the FDE role differs from solutions architecture and platform engineering, how to run enterprise discovery and extract real requirements, how to translate business workflows into technical architectures, and how to deliver a rapid proof of concept inside a customer’s environment.

Enterprise Integrations and Distributed Systems addresses the part of every deployment that breaks first: connecting to systems you do not own. The workshop covers REST, GraphQL, gRPC, webhooks, and API gateways; OAuth2, service identities, and token rotation; circuit breakers, retries, idempotency, and rate limits; and event-driven orchestration and streaming patterns.

Agentic AI Systems for Enterprise Apps moves beyond single prompts into systems that act. Participants work with LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen, and workflow graphs; build RAG and ingestion pipelines with vectors and embeddings; implement tool use, function calling, and structured outputs; and wrap evals, guardrails, and prompt-injection defenses around them.

Production Deployment and DevOps is where demos become services. The workshop weighs containers against serverless for customer environments, covers deployment patterns across AWS ECS, Kubernetes, and Cloud Run, sets up CI/CD for rapid PoCs with logs, traces, and metrics, and teaches blue-green rollout, rollback, and scaling.

Security, Compliance, and Reliability Engineering prepares engineers for regulated buyers. It covers secure APIs, auth enforcement, and secrets handling; data governance for AI systems and LLM-specific risks; an overview of SOC2, ISO 27001, and HIPAA; and secure architecture patterns for regulated domains. This is often what separates a vendor that clears procurement from one that stalls in it.

Technical Solutioning and Customer Caselets ties the program back to the customer. Participants run discovery sessions and map workflows, write architecture documents and diagrams, present PoCs and handle technical objections, and design a complete enterprise scenario from end to end.

The capstone

The program closes with a knowledge check and a self-paced industry project that pulls together solution leadership, distributed systems, agentic AI, DevOps, and secure architecture. The output is an enterprise-grade FDE solution demo, and participants who complete it earn the Certified Forward Deployed AI Specialist (CFDAS) designation. You get a tangible artifact from each participant, not a completion certificate alone, which makes the impact easy to review internally.

Why this matters for your team

Consider where deals and projects actually stall. A capable engineer builds an impressive prototype, then loses three weeks integrating with the customer’s identity provider. A promising pilot clears the technical review but fails the security questionnaire. A PoC that wins the room never reaches production because no one on the team can take it from notebook to deployed service. Each of these is a skills gap, not a talent gap, and each one is expensive in both calendar time and pipeline.

The Forward Deployed Engineer Program targets those specific failure points. Run by ADaSci, it is built for engineers, solutions teams, and pre-sales technical staff who already write code but need the breadth to operate in front of customers and across the full delivery path. By the end, a participant can sit with a client, scope a problem, design a secure and integrated architecture, build and deploy it, and defend the technical decisions when challenged.

The Forward Deployed Engineer Program is a direct answer to that constraint: a structured way to turn capable engineers into the people who get AI from a convincing demo into a system that runs, securely and reliably, inside the customer’s stack.

Bring it to your team

The program can be run for your whole team as a corporate training engagement, with the CFDAS designation as the outcome. To scope cohort size, scheduling, and customization for your stack, reach out to ADaSci at info@adasci.org.
