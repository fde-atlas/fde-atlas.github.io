Should You Continue Leveraging FDEs After the AI Bubble Bursts? Decision Criteria for Continuing or Scaling Down, and a Comparison of Organizational Structures

Original title: AIバブル崩壊でFDE活用は続けるべきか？継続・縮小の判断基準と体制比較

FDE総研 · 2026-11-06 · 11 min read

Summary

This article from FDE Soken addresses whether companies should continue, shrink, or abandon Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) investments amid talk of an AI bubble collapse. It proposes a three-axis test per project: business integration, handover feasibility, and price-increase tolerance. Projects meeting two or more axes should continue; those meeting none should be retired with documentation. It recommends capping exploratory PoCs at two concurrent efforts and reviewing KPIs quarterly. For staffing, external FDEs suit short-term results, internal development suits long-term use, and SIer partnerships suit maintenance; a hybrid of external launch followed by internal handover is advised. For engineers, building a track record of solving and embedding customer problems matters more than job titles.

Key points

  • Judge each FDE project on three axes: business integration, handover feasibility, price tolerance.
  • Two or more passes means continue; zero means retire with documented learnings.
  • Cap exploratory PoCs at two concurrent projects; review KPIs quarterly.
  • External FDEs for speed, internal training for longevity, SIer for maintenance.
  • Engineers should build embedded-results portfolios and industry knowledge over titles.

With the AI bubble collapse making headlines, many companies are being forced to decide whether to continue, reduce, or stop their investment in FDEs. Engineers considering a career change are also wondering whether the FDE role can weather this phase. An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role that embeds deeply at the customer's site and handles everything end to end, from problem discovery through implementation and adoption. It is known as a working style popularized by Palantir.

In this article, the FDE Research Institute organizes the decision points for continuing, scaling down, or withdrawing using three criteria established for this topic (business criticality, handover feasibility, and price-increase resilience). We also show how to compare external sourcing, in-house development, and SIer partnerships, and how the answer varies by preconditions. The overall ROI calculation for AI investment is summarized in How to Choose AI Investments That Pay Off Even in an AI Bubble Collapse, so this article focuses on decisions about the FDE operating model itself.

Amid the AI Bubble Collapse, Should FDE Usage Be Continued, Scaled Down, or Stopped?

As a rule, FDE projects that meet two or more of the FDE Research Institute's three criteria should be continued after verifying the price-increase resilience calculation.

Concerns about an AI bubble collapse have been reported in Japan and abroad, centered on overheated fundraising and overinvestment in computing resources. However, there is currently no basis for determining when or how severely a collapse might occur. Rather than betting on market outlooks, it is more reliable to evaluate each FDE project one by one. For the long-term trajectory of AI investment, it is safest to consult third-party annual surveys such as the Stanford HAI "AI Index".

The FDE Research Institute's Three-Criteria Table for Deciding to Continue, Scale Down, or Withdraw

Mark each of the three criteria—business criticality, handover feasibility, and price-increase resilience—with a pass or fail, and decide to continue, scale down, or withdraw based on the number of passes.

CriterionConditions for a passConditions for a fail
Business criticalityEmbedded in daily frontline operations or decision-making; stopping it disrupts the businessStuck at demo/PoC stage; stopping it causes no disruption
Handover feasibilityDesign documents and operating procedures exist; operations can continue in-house even if the FDE leavesExists only in the individual FDE's head; dependent on one person
Price-increase resilienceBenefits still outweigh costs even if API fees or external FDE rates rise 2–3xBarely profitable at current rates, or benefits are not measured
  • Three passes: Continue. In fact, a candidate for horizontal expansion.
  • Two passes: Continue. Create a plan to improve the failed criterion within three months.
  • One pass: Scale down. Reduce FDE hours and prioritize handover.
  • Zero passes: Withdraw. Document deliverables and lessons learned, then stop.

From the perspective of a frontline practitioner, the criterion most likely to fail is "handover feasibility." We often hear of cases where external FDE contracts were cut abruptly due to budget pressure, leaving no one able to fix a running system. If you decide to scale down, securing a handover period before terminating the contract comes first.

What You Lose by Withdrawing Across the Board Citing the AI Bubble Collapse

Withdrawing from all projects across the board means losing both the means to maintain AI that has become embedded in operations and the role of surfacing frontline issues.

The value of an FDE lies not in the code itself but in the translation that connects frontline business processes with technology (What Is an FDE?). Stopping this role risks halting improvements to systems already in operation and pushing frontline staff back to manual work. What should be cut during an AI bubble collapse is exploratory projects whose benefits cannot be measured. There is no need to cut projects that have become embedded in operations. Typical withdrawal failures are organized in Common FDE Implementation Failure Patterns.

What Work Should Be Entrusted to FDEs During an AI Bubble Collapse, and What Are the Criteria?

The FDE Research Institute recommends that, during an AI bubble collapse, FDEs focus on work whose benefits can be measured monthly, and that exploratory PoCs be limited to at most two at a time (Use Case Selection).

In a period of constrained budgets, FDE working hours become the scarcest resource. Where those hours are spent can dramatically change results even with the same headcount.

Comparison of Work to Entrust to FDEs vs. Work to Assign Elsewhere

Entrust FDEs with work that alternates between frontline interviews and implementation, and route routine maintenance and documentation to other owners.

CategoryWork for FDEsWork to assign elsewhere
Problem discoveryFrontline interviews, visualizing business flowsMarket research, competitive analysis documentation
ImplementationPrototyping with frontline data, designing exception handlingMass production of screens with finalized specs
OperationsInvestigating causes of accuracy degradation, prioritizing improvementsRoutine monitoring, first-line inquiry handling
AdoptionHandover to frontline owners, establishing operating rulesManual formatting, training schedule coordination

If FDEs are bogged down with the work in the right-hand column, you can free up their capacity without scaling down. Interview methods are covered in detail in Business Interviews, and handover in System Handover.

Four Steps to Narrow Down FDE Projects

Decide where FDE hours go through four stages: project inventory, three-criteria evaluation, setting PoC limits, and quarterly reviews.

  • List all projects involving FDEs and write out the working hours and benefit measurement method for each
  • Apply the pass/fail evaluation from the previous chapter's three criteria and make a provisional decision to continue, scale down, or withdraw
  • Set a cap of two exploratory PoCs at a time and route any excess into a waiting queue
  • Review KPIs quarterly ([ROI and KPI design](/practice/measurement/roi-kpi/)) and update your assessments

From VACAN's practical perspective, we find that many organizations get stuck at step 3, "setting a limit." Starting a PoC is easy, but because there are no criteria for stopping, the number of PoCs keeps growing. If you set a limit in advance, when a new request comes in, the discussion shifts to a constructive one: "which one do we replace?"

Should FDEs be externally sourced, developed in-house, or handled through SIer partnerships?

According to FDE Soken's comparison, if you need results within 6 months, external FDEs are the way to go; if you plan to continue for 2 or more years, in-house development; if the focus is maintenance, SIer partnerships fit best (FDE costs).

During an AI bubble collapse, organizations tend to avoid structures that easily become fixed costs. However, if you rely entirely on external resources, the know-how disappears the moment the budget is cut. Using them according to duration and purpose is the realistic approach.

How to combine structures: VACAN's practical perspective

From VACAN's practical perspective, the sequence with the least strain is to launch with an external FDE, transfer to in-house staff, and hand over only the parts with fixed specifications to an SIer.

Trying to complete everything with a single structure creates strain somewhere. Include handover periods and deliverables in the external FDE contract from the start. That way, even if budgets are cut during an AI bubble collapse, the means to operate remain in-house. For in-house development, rather than making everyone an FDE, an easy way to start is to have one or two staff members with on-the-ground business knowledge work alongside an external FDE. However, whether this combination works depends on whether you have in-house personnel who can handle multiple roles. It is not a one-size-fits-all solution.

Do the answers for FDE utilization change by years of experience, current role, English proficiency, and company size?

At FDE Soken, answers differ for individuals based on years of experience and business knowledge in their current role, and for companies based on employee count and the presence of an IT department (English proficiency guidelines).

Even for the same question of "should I continue as an FDE?", the answer changes depending on your position. We organize this separately for individual engineers and for adopting companies.

Answers for individual engineers by prerequisites

With less than 3 years of experience, focus on implementation skills; if you have industry knowledge in your current role, focus on that industry; if you have English proficiency, consider foreign companies as well.

During a contraction phase, we believe that "a track record of solving customer problems and making solutions stick" is valued more than the FDE title. Rather than chasing titles, accumulating achievements you can record in your portfolio leads to a career less dependent on market conditions.

The smaller the employee count, the more suitable short-term use of external FDEs becomes; the larger, the more suitable a combination of in-house development and SIer partnerships.

Differences between FDEs and SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers—and who is not suited to be an FDE?

Under FDE Soken's comparison axes, FDEs differ from SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers in that both their "depth of immersion in the field" and "scope of hands-on implementation" are large (Differences from IT consultants).

During an AI bubble collapse, the strengths and weaknesses of each role become more pronounced. Whether you are an individual considering a career change or a company choosing a structure, understanding these differences helps you avoid misjudgments.

Characteristics of people not suited to FDE

People who want to focus on development with fixed specifications, or who find coordinating with customers burdensome, may not be suited to FDE.

  • People who want to pursue only implementation quality once the specifications are decided
  • People for whom coordinating with customers or reconciling conflicting requests is a major source of stress
  • People who want to avoid changes in their work environment, such as business trips or working at customer sites
  • People who want to be evaluated based on "the amount of code they wrote"

Not being suited to FDE does not mean low ability. Many people can excel as SWEs or PdMs. How to assess your fit is explained in detail in People suited to FDE and Is FDE tough?.

How aspiring FDEs should prepare during the AI bubble collapse phase

For FDE aspirants during a contraction phase, it is safer to first build up a "track record of seeing things through to adoption" and "knowledge of one industry" rather than relying on job titles.

Regardless of how severe the AI bubble collapse turns out to be, it is unlikely that demand will disappear for the ability to understand customers' business operations and solve their problems. On the other hand, AI-related job openings may temporarily decrease. To prepare for that, it is reassuring to keep your options broad, considering not only dedicated FDE positions but also SWE and solution-oriented roles. The overall career picture is summarized in FDE Career Guide, and perspectives on future prospects in The Future of FDE.
