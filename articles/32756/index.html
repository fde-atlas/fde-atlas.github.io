How to become a forward deployed engineer

Kernavique · 2026-11-20 · 16 min read

Summary

Kernavique maps two entry paths into forward deployed engineering. Early-career hiring is narrow: of 298 US postings titled “forward deployed” across 177 job feeds read on November 20, 2026, only 26 were intern or new-grad roles, 21 of them at Palantir, whose new-grad ads list $135,000–$145,000 a year; C3 AI lists $120,000–$150,000. The second path is moving across: in Bloomberry’s sample of 100 LinkedIn profiles, 45% were previously software engineers and 22% came from solutions roles. Ads commonly require a graduation window, a general-purpose language (usually Python), applied AI exposure, daily use of AI coding tools, and travel. The article stresses that no dataset exists on where these engineers go next, and offers résumé positioning advice covering both the coding and customer-facing halves of the job.

Key points

  • Only 26 of 298 US “forward deployed” postings were intern or new-grad roles, 21 at Palantir.
  • Palantir new-grad pay is listed at $135,000–$145,000; C3 AI at $120,000–$150,000.
  • Bloomberry’s 100-profile sample: 45% were software engineers before, 22% from solutions roles.
  • Ads require graduation windows, Python-level coding, applied AI exposure, and travel.
  • No data exists on career exits from the role; only anecdotes from employers and investors.

Two ways in

There are two routes into forward deployed engineering, and the evidence for them is uneven.

The first is early-career hiring: internships and new-grad programs. This route is well documented, because the employers publish ads with dates, locations and pay. We read them on November 20, 2026.

The second is moving across from another job: software engineering, solutions work, data or consulting. This route is thinly documented. One third-party sample of 100 profiles and a handful of employer statements are all we could trace.

A third question, where these engineers go afterward, has no data behind it at all. We say so below.

For what the job is, see our forward deployed engineer role page. For which employers advertise it, see who is hiring forward deployed engineers.

Internships and new-grad programs, by employer

On November 20, 2026, we read the public job feeds of 177 mostly AI and tech employers. Of 298 US postings with “forward deployed” in the title, 14 carried an intern title and 12 a new-grad title. That is 26 of 298.

The route is concentrated in one employer. Palantir accounts for 11 of the 14 intern postings and 10 of the 12 new-grad postings. Coinbase, Domino Data Lab and Saronic have one intern posting each, and C3 AI has the other two new-grad postings. One feed entry is one posting, so a program advertised in three cities counts three times.

Some notes on the table.

Palantir. Its new-grad postings for US Government teams and for infrastructure engineers show the same $135,000 - $145,000 in the feed, in New York, Washington, D.C., Honolulu and Palo Alto. The Year at Palantir program is also posted for US Government teams in New York and Washington, D.C., at the same monthly range.

Coinbase and Saronic. These two rows come from the feed alone. We did not read those ads in full.

Sierra. Its careers page lists early-career titles without “forward deployed” in them: Software Engineer, Agent (New Grad 2027), Software Engineer Intern, Agent (Summer 2027) and APX (New Grad 2027), in San Francisco and New York. The page says people in these roles work directly with customers to build AI agents. We read the titles only, not the ads, and the page shows no pay.

Faros. A copy of its ad on an investor’s job board describes a new-graduate forward deployed engineer role in Redwood City, CA, at “USD 100k-150k / year”. The copy says it was posted more than six months ago, so the role may be closed.

Near-entry roles. Two ads ask for very little experience without being campus roles. AssemblyAI’s Forward Deployed Engineer, Triage ad in San Francisco asks for 2+ years in a technical, customer-facing role, says it will consider 1 year for someone with a strong solutions background, and lists a salary range of $115,000 - $135,000. FurtherAI’s ad in San Francisco asks for 1+ years, lists $120K - $180K with equity of 0.10% - 0.20%, and says it will sponsor visas.

For experienced-hire pay on a like-for-like basis, see forward deployed engineer pay by employer.

What the early-career ads ask for

Five things recur across the early-career ads we read on November 20, 2026.

A graduation window. All four campus employers state one. Palantir’s Warp Speed internship asks for graduation in December 2027 or Spring 2028. Its new-grad commercial ad is for December 2026 or Spring 2027 graduates. C3 AI’s two ads are for December 2026 or Summer/Fall 2027. Domino Data Lab asks for Spring 2028 and a Bachelor’s or Master’s degree in computer science, machine learning, data science or a related field.

Palantir’s Year at Palantir ad is the exception on age. It says it is open to freshmen and sophomores as well as juniors and seniors, and the placement lasts a year.

One general-purpose language. Palantir asks for a strong coder with proficiency in languages such as Python, Java, C++, TypeScript/JavaScript, or similar. Where the ads list languages, Python comes first.

Exposure to AI, not years of it. C3 AI asks for “Familiarity with agentic AI architectures”, prompt engineering or the basics of LLM tool calling. Domino Data Lab asks for exposure to building applied AI systems, and gives RAG pipelines, agentic workflows and model evaluation as examples. Palantir’s Year at Palantir ad asks for hands-on experience applying AI, and counts coursework, personal projects and internships.

AI tools in daily use. Faros asks for a new graduate who already builds with AI coding assistants, LLM APIs or AI-powered tools. AssemblyAI asks for daily use of coding agents, with real projects shipped using them.

Travel, loosely stated. Palantir’s Warp Speed internship says up to 25%, as needed, and flexible by personal preference. Its new-grad commercial ad says to expect 25–50%. C3 AI says only that applicants must be able to travel as needed.

Federal versions add eligibility lines. C3 AI’s federal new-grad ad asks for US citizenship and eligibility to obtain and maintain a US Government security clearance. Palantir’s Warp Speed internship says “U.S. Person Required”. If that is the route you want, read cleared forward deployed engineer roles.

Moving across from another role

The one sample with a stated size. Bloomberry analyzed 100 LinkedIn profiles of people then working as forward deployed engineers (Henley Wing Chiu; page shown as updated January 25, 2026). We first read it on October 11, 2026, and the figures were unchanged when we read it again on November 20, 2026.

Prior role Share of the 100 profiles
Software engineer (backend or full-stack) 45%
Solutions engineer or solutions architect 22%
Data engineer or data scientist 15%
Technical consultant 10%
Founder or early startup employee 8%

Bloomberry’s summary is that most start as software engineers who develop customer-facing skills. Two cautions: the article does not say how the 100 profiles were chosen, and 100 is a small sample.

What employers say about who they take. These are single statements, each from the employer’s own page or ad, read November 20, 2026.

  • Software engineering. Anthropic’s Applied AI Engineer, Commercial ad asks for 4+ years of technical experience as a software engineer, forward deployed engineer, ML engineer, product engineer or technical founder.
  • Consulting. Cresta’s senior ad asks for 3+ years in software development or consulting, AI/ML engineering, or forward deployed engineering.
  • Solutions and delivery. Tennr asks for 3+ years of experience in SaaS delivery or solution design, and 6+ for its enterprise version. Salesforce’s blog gives one example of an employee who moved from a technical architect role at the company to its forward deployed team.
  • Inside the same company. Salesforce says around 40% to 50% of forward deployed engineer movement at the company is internal. It gives no counts behind that range.
  • Founders. Ramp says 7 of its 16 forward deployed engineers are previous founders. Decagon says its Agent Engineers are technical generalists who may have explored product roles or founded companies.
  • Outside engineering. AirOps asks for 4 to 7 years in growth, business operations, product, technical account management or analytics. Eudia’s Forward Deployed Lawyer ad asks for 4+ years of practicing law and a JD or equivalent.

What named leaders told an investor. Insight Partners published two panel write-ups in July 2026. It is an investor in Databricks, Workato and Wonderful, so read these as reports from an interested party. According to the articles, Workato hires from systems integrators and university partnerships. Databricks looks for product management, software engineering and client-facing experience. At Wonderful, between one in six and one in seven new hires are former founders.

On data engineers. LeadDev (July 23, 2026) reports the view of Adam Moore, founder of the hiring firm Morela, that data engineers with strong analytical backgrounds have moved into the role. That is one person’s observation, paraphrased by the publication.

The neighboring titles overlap more than their names suggest. See forward deployed engineer vs solutions engineer.

Where these engineers go next: nobody has measured it

We found no dataset or study with a stated sample on what forward deployed engineers do after the role. Bloomberry’s article has nothing on it. So anything you read about exits is opinion or anecdote, including what follows.

  • Product leadership. Kabir Sial, a former Palantir forward deployed engineer who is now an investor, told CIO.com (August 7, 2026) that much of Palantir’s core product leadership came from the role.
  • Founding companies. Jake Stauch, chief executive of Serval, told First Round Review that people in the role are “often former or future founders.”
  • Growing inside the role. Decagon says growth comes from taking on larger and more complex problems. Insight Partners reports that Workato has a career ladder that climbs to an “architect” level, and that Databricks moves people between its forward deployed team and product in both directions.

None of these is a count. If the next step matters to you, ask the employer where the last few people who left the team went. For how employers label the rungs, see forward deployed engineer levels.

Positioning a résumé for both halves of the job

This section is our reading of the ads and statements above. It is not advice from any employer.

The job has two halves: writing production code, and working directly with a customer. A résumé that shows only one reads as a different job.

The code half.

  • Lead with what you built, in one main language. The ads ask for a strong coder in a general-purpose language, with Python listed first. Name the language beside each project.
  • Show AI you have applied. The early-career ads ask for exposure. Coursework, personal projects and internships count in Palantir’s wording. Say what the thing did: a retrieval pipeline, an agent workflow, a model evaluation.
  • Say which AI coding tools you use. Faros and AssemblyAI ask for it outright. One line is enough.
  • Put the graduation date at the top if you are applying to a campus program. Every campus ad we read filters on it.

The customer half.

  • Show you have been responsible for someone else’s success. Ramp says it favors people with that background, and gives “instructors or teaching assistants” as examples. Tutoring, teaching and support work belong on the page.
  • Count customer-facing technical work. AssemblyAI measures experience in years spent in a technical, customer-facing role. If you have done solutions, support or consulting work, say who the customer was and what changed for them.
  • If you founded something, say so. Ramp and Wonderful both report a high share of former founders.

Which half to put first depends on where you are coming from. If you are a software engineer, the code is assumed and the customer contact is the gap, so lead with the times you worked with users. If you come from solutions work, data or consulting, lead with code that ran in production.

State the practical lines plainly. Say you can travel if you can, since the ads ask. For federal roles, state citizenship and clearance eligibility only where they are true and the ad asks for them.

In practice

If you are a student, the route is narrow and dated. Most of the postings are at Palantir, each program has a graduation window, and the ads ask for a strong general-purpose language plus some applied AI. Apply inside the window.

If you are a working engineer, the most common way in is from software engineering, by adding customer-facing work. From solutions, data or consulting, the gap to close is production code.

Either way, read the ad for travel, eligibility and pay basis before you apply, and prepare for an engineering interview. Our forward deployed engineer interview guide covers what employers publish about that.

What we left out

  • Where these engineers go next. No dataset exists that we could find. One investor’s article names former forward deployed engineers who became founders; we did not check those career histories, so we do not repeat the names.
  • A vendor dataset of more than 3,000 profiles. It gives no collection method, period or sampling rule, and it reports neither prior roles nor next roles.
  • Google’s forward deployed engineering internship. Its careers page returned only a title to our reader, with no location, pay or requirements.
  • Most of Palantir’s intern ads. We read two in full. The rest appear here as feed entries only.
  • Sierra’s early-career ads and pay. We read titles on its careers page. A pay range for its new-grad role appeared only on an aggregator, so we left it out.
  • A new-grad ad from Northslope. We could read it only as an aggregator copy that was removed on July 22, 2026.
  • Early-career roles outside the US. The feeds held 35 intern or new-grad postings in all locations, 26 of them in the US. We did not open the other nine.
  • Employers outside the 177 feeds. Amazon, ServiceNow and Deloitte were not part of this count, and employers on Workday are mostly missing.

Questions

  • Can a new graduate become a forward deployed engineer?
  • Yes, at a small number of employers. On the 177 public job feeds we read on November 20, 2026, 12 of 298 US postings titled “forward deployed” carried a new-grad title: 10 at Palantir and 2 at C3 AI. Palantir’s new-grad ads list an estimated $135,000 - $145,000 a year, and C3 AI’s list a base range of $120,000 to $150,000.
  • Are there forward deployed engineer internships?
  • Yes. The same feeds held 14 US postings with an intern title on November 20, 2026: 11 at Palantir and one each at Coinbase, Domino Data Lab and Saronic. Palantir’s Year at Palantir internship lists $5,900 - $10,500 a month, and Domino Data Lab’s 10-week internship lists $50 to $55 an hour.
  • What did forward deployed engineers do before the role?
  • The only breakdown with a stated sample is Bloomberry’s, from 100 LinkedIn profiles: 45% were software engineers, 22% solutions engineers or architects, 15% data engineers or data scientists, 10% technical consultants and 8% founders or early startup employees. We first read it on October 11, 2026, and it was unchanged on November 20, 2026. Bloomberry does not say how the profiles were chosen.
  • Do I need AI experience to get an entry-level role?
  • The early-career ads ask for exposure, not a track record. C3 AI asks for familiarity with agentic AI architectures and prompt engineering. Domino Data Lab asks for exposure to building applied AI systems. Palantir’s Year at Palantir ad counts coursework, personal projects and internships. All were read on November 20, 2026.
  • Do I need US citizenship or a security clearance?
  • Only for some roles. Among the early-career ads we read on November 20, 2026, C3 AI’s federal new-grad ad asks for US citizenship and eligibility for a clearance, and Palantir’s Warp Speed internship says “U.S. Person Required”. The commercial versions of the same programs carry no such line in our read. Check the ad for the role you want.
  • Where do forward deployed engineers go next?
  • Nobody has measured it. We found no dataset or study with a stated sample on what these engineers do afterward. What exists is opinion from named people, such as a former Palantir engineer who says many of that company’s product leaders came from the role. Treat it as anecdote.
