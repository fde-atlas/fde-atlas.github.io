FDE Expanded by AWS to Partner Companies — Implementation Frameworks That SES Providers and SIers Should Put in Place

Original title: AWSがパートナー企業へ広げるFDE——SES・SIerが整えたい実装体制

株式会社本質 · 2026-09-17 · 3 min read

Summary

On June 30, 2026, AWS announced the Partner-Led Forward Deployed Engineering Motion, a program to build lasting implementation capability inside partner companies rather than a certification or training course. In initial projects, AWS engineers work alongside partners and leave behind knowledge and structures reusable in later engagements; the program starts with a limited set of partners and expands after validation. Honshitsu, a Japanese consultancy, argues SES and SIer firms should design FDE development around both individual skill and organizational readiness: fix staffing and hours for the first project, set business-level acceptance criteria with customers (e.g., response drafting time, correction rates, misdirection counts), and record the reasoning behind decisions so later projects can reuse evaluation methods while separating customer-specific data from reusable practices.

Key points

  • AWS announced the Partner-Led FDE Motion on June 30, 2026.
  • AWS engineers join the first project and leave reusable knowledge for later ones.
  • Start with a limited partner group, expanding after validation.
  • First projects need defined roles, hours, and customer-agreed acceptance criteria.
  • Keep records of decision rationale, separating client data from reusable methods.

Full text

When developing FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers), alongside training content you should also consider a structure that allows trained individuals to carry out implementation at customer sites. AWS's announcement provides material for thinking about building such an organization.

Key Points of the Official Announcement

On June 30, 2026, AWS announced the "Partner-Led Forward Deployed Engineering Motion" for partner companies. This is an initiative to build continuous implementation capability within partners, and is distinct from certification programs and training programs themselves.

In initial projects, AWS engineers will work on implementation together with partners, with the policy of leaving behind knowledge and mechanisms that can be used in subsequent projects. The initial scope is limited to selected partners, and AWS explains that the scope will expand after validation. Details can be found in the original AWS announcement.

Our Perspective on the Essence

From this announcement, we believe that FDE development at SES and SIer companies needs to be designed around both "individual proficiency" and "the company's readiness to run projects." Below are our proposals for Japanese companies based on this announcement.

For the First Project, Decide Who Will Be Involved and How Much Time They Will Have

Even if people learn AI tools, it will not lead to practice unless time is secured for customer interviews, prototyping, and evaluation. The first thing to decide is not the number of trainees, but which customer's which business process, who will work on it, and for how many hours.

For example, to improve inquiry handling, assign someone to organize requirements with the customer, someone to handle implementation, and someone to verify response quality. These roles may be combined in one person. If you also decide who to consult when decisions stall and who on the customer side will review prototypes, it becomes easier to try what was learned in an actual project.

Not everyone needs to cover the same scope. Divide the stages into using AI in daily work, handling implementation, and designing from business challenges, and assign roles that match each person's current level — this becomes the responsibility of the training lead.

Go One Step Beyond "It Works" — Set Acceptance Criteria for the Business

If prototype evaluation ends with "it produced an answer," the customer cannot make an adoption decision. For inquiry handling, compare the time to draft responses, the proportion of corrections made by staff, and the number of incorrect guidance instances before and after implementation. {{14} }Target values should be decided with the customer. Even if time is reduced, if the burden of verification increases, it may not constitute an improvement for the overall business. Also include in the initial evaluation whether ambiguous inquiries can be escalated to a human, and how to notice when reference information is outdated. The experience of discussing these criteria with the customer becomes practice in problem framing, which is essential for FDEs.

What to Carry Over to the Next Project: the Reasons Behind Decisions

What should remain within the company is not just source code. Record briefly "why this business process was chosen," "which responses were rejected," and "who took over operations." Having both the conditions for success and the conditions where it cannot be applied makes it useful when other team members make proposals.

However, customer data and individual business information cannot necessarily be carried over as-is to other projects. Manage customer-specific information separately from evaluation procedures and design approaches that your company can reuse.

As a first step, pick one existing project and summarize on a single sheet: the business owner, the scope to try, the acceptance criteria, and the assigned engineer's time. Once you get that specific, it also becomes clear what needs to be acquired through training.
