Snorkel AI FDE Salary and Job Requirements

Original title: Snorkel AI FDE 薪资与岗位要求

On October 29, 2026, Snorkel AI updated its job posting for a Senior/Staff Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) on its Data-as-a-Service (DaaS) team, with a base salary of $180,000 to $320,000.

The top of the range is nearly 1.8 times the bottom. But what deserves closer reading than the numbers is the job description. The posting still requires experience integrating systems via APIs, yet the first listed responsibility is building evals and measuring data quality.

Snorkel calls itself a "frontier AI data lab" and was incubated from the Stanford AI Lab in 2019. According to its website, the company sells training data and specialized environments to frontier AI labs and AI teams, with products including Data Series, custom data development, and specialized agents.

When customers receive datasets rather than software systems, "deployment" means something quite different. So even if you don't plan to apply to Snorkel, this job posting is worth a read: it showcases a type of FDE role with evals at the core of customer work.

When the product is data, what does an FDE deliver?

Snorkel describes this role as an "engineering delivery" role: turning customers' still-vague requirements into production-grade data. The FDE belongs to a team numbered "310 - DaaS FDE", works closely with the DaaS delivery operations team, and provides technical support to delivery managers.

A company press release dated September 22, 2026 explains why this job requires real engineers. Snorkel says its products are expert-level agent tasks, environments, and scoring rubrics, and that even the best people take hours, sometimes days, to build them.

A product this hard to build cannot be "finished and shipped." Someone has to stand between the customer and the data production line, figure out what the customer wants, and verify that what gets delivered is correct.

How a vague request passes through an FDE's hands

Imagine an AI lab tells Snorkel it needs "data that helps agents do invoice reconciliation better." Nothing can be produced from that sentence alone. The FDE's first job is to write the spec: what steps the task includes, what the inputs look like, and what counts as "done."

The next step is the scoring rubric, which is also where things most easily go wrong. Suppose each solution has three steps: read the correct invoice line, match it to the purchase order, then compute the difference.

If the rubric only checks the final number, a solution that reads the wrong line and matches the wrong order but happens to compute the correct total would still pass, and the customer would use these flawed solutions to train models.

An eval turns the scoring rubric into code that can run over an entire batch of data. A minimal version looks roughly like this:

def grade(sample, gold):
    checks = {
        "read_lines": sample["lines"] == gold["lines"],
        "match_po": sample["po_match"] == gold["po_match"],
        "final_diff": abs(sample["diff"] - gold["diff"]) < 0.01,
    }
    return all(checks.values()), checks

Running this function on 50 samples yields two comparable numbers: the pass rate when only

final_diff

is checked, and the pass rate when all three steps are checked.

The difference between the two is the amount of flawed data that a final-answer-only scoring approach would let through. The FDE then uses synthetic data to fill in the missing case types, and only then delivers the dataset.

Snorkel's job posting lists exactly this chain of work: building and deploying evals, designing quality measurement systems, generating synthetic data, delivering datasets, and writing production specs.

Evals are the backbone, at every level

On the same Data-as-a-Service team, Snorkel is also hiring a Director, Forward Deployed Researcher, with a base salary of $228,800 to $403,200. That role's explicit responsibility is to set and maintain technical standards for specs, eval frameworks, and customer deliverables.

Reading the two job postings together reveals something neither shows on its own: the difference between the engineering track and the research track lies in scope of impact, not in the work itself. The FDE writes specs and evals for each project; the Director sets a unified standard across all specs and eval frameworks.

The two salary ranges also overlap between $228,800 and $320,000, a span of $91,200. So a Staff FDE at the top of the range could have a higher base salary than a Director at the bottom. On this team, if your eval skills are deep enough, you don't need to move into management to earn more.

$180,000-$320,000 is not your number

This posting merges the Senior and Staff levels, with a range spanning two career levels. Treating the $250,000 midpoint as the expectation is a misreading: which level you are placed at determines which half of the range you land in.

And that's just base salary. The posting mentions a variable bonus and says most offers include equity and benefits, so two offers with the same base salary could still differ considerably in total compensation. What you really need to negotiate is your level, not the top of the range.

Who fits this role, and how to prepare

Snorkel requires at least five years of experience in data science, data engineering, machine learning engineering, or solutions engineering, proficiency in Python and SQL, and experience integrating systems via APIs.

This list opens the door to multiple backgrounds: a data engineer who has built data quality pipelines has no less of a chance than a machine learning engineer. What truly sets candidates apart is being able to demonstrate that they know how to measure quality.

The fastest way is to build a small end-to-end project like the invoice example above, with a short write-up showing that the eval catches errors that a final-answer-only check would miss. Such a project maps directly to the responsibilities in the posting and gives you something concrete to discuss in interviews.

On your resume, write measurable results rather than listing tools, for example: "Designed scoring rubrics and evals for N agent data samples, finding that X% of solutions had correct final answers but incorrect intermediate steps," using numbers from your own projects.

On Snorkel's DaaS team, the question "Is this batch of data good enough?" is one that someone is paid to answer at every level, from Senior up to Director. If you want to target roles like this, start with an eval that can run on 50 samples, not with chasing certifications.
