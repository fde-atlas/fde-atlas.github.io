Owkin FDE hiring requirements: how do junior and senior roles differ?

Original title: Owkin FDE 招聘要求：初级与高级有何不同

FDE Times · 2026-10-29 · 5 min read

Summary

The article compares Owkin's junior and senior FDE job postings and finds the technical requirements are identical: Python, coding Copilots, LLM and Agent framework experience. The difference is responsibility, not tooling. Senior FDEs need 5+ years (vs 2+ for junior), work deep inside customer sites, turn validated patterns into reusable components and playbooks, mentor juniors, and join on-call rotations. The piece also covers Owkin's background in federated learning and its K Navigator and K Pro products, and offers advice for engineers aiming at either level.

Key points

  • Junior and senior FDE postings list the same tech stack.
  • Junior requires 2+ years backend experience; senior requires 5+.
  • Senior adds reusable assets, mentoring, and on-call duties.
  • Owkin's FDEs build production AI systems on K Navigator and K Pro.
  • Resumes should show discovery, custom solution, then reusable asset.

Key takeaways at a glance

  • Both Owkin's junior and senior FDE roles require solid Python skills, proficiency with coding Copilot-type tools, and experience with LLMs and agent frameworks.
  • The junior role requires 2+ years of backend experience, supporting client projects alongside senior colleagues; the senior role requires 5+ years, working deep inside client organizations and distilling proven patterns into components, tools, and runbooks.
  • Senior FDEs also mentor junior FDEs and join the team's shared on-call rotation, meaning they remain responsible even after systems go to production.

Owkin is hiring two roles with the same title: Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), one junior and one senior. Placing the two job postings side by side, the technical requirements are nearly identical: both require proficiency in Python, comfort with coding Copilot-type tools, and experience with LLMs and agent frameworks.

The differences lie in the remaining lines. The senior role requires more years of experience, uses heavier language when describing client work, and adds three responsibilities not mentioned in the junior posting: turning solutions into reusable assets, mentoring newcomers, and participating in on-call duty.

For engineers with two to five years of experience who want to move into an FDE role, this is worth noting. Moving up doesn't require learning another framework; it requires proving that you take ownership of what you build: ownership when the system runs at the client's site, and ownership when others need to reuse it.

What does Owkin sell, and what do FDEs build on?

Owkin was founded in Paris in 2016 and first became known for federated learning. This technology lets multiple parties collaborate without moving or sharing data; the data always stays with its owner.

In May 2025, the company launched K Navigator, an agentic research environment that Owkin calls a "playground" for researchers to explore, refine, and validate hypotheses.

On October 16, 2025, the next product, K Pro, was released: a Copilot for biopharma that lets enterprise and academic clients securely upload their proprietary data.

The FDE's work is built on this foundation. According to the junior posting, the job is to design, build, and deploy production-grade AI systems on Owkin K. The senior posting states that a significant portion of the work happens on-site at clients.

The postings don't explain why on-site work is needed. Since K Pro allows clients to upload data, the reason is unlikely to be "data can't leave the building." A more plausible reading: connecting agents to sensitive data scattered across a client's systems is much easier when you sit next to the people who own that data.

Whatever the reason, FDEs here don't just write prompts. They connect agent systems to clients' sensitive data, and when the connections break, they're the ones on site.

Same tech stack, different roles

Listing the two postings line by line makes the overlaps and differences immediately clear.

The most telling row is actually the tools row, because the two columns are identical. Owkin does not differentiate seniority by "juniors use simple tools, seniors use advanced tools." Everyone is expected to work with LLMs, agents, and Copilots from day one.

What truly distinguishes the levels is the next three rows. Juniors are responsible for the code they write; seniors are responsible for outcomes on the client side, for whether the next project moves faster, and for the people working alongside them.

In a project, what's the difference between "supporting" and "going deep"?

Imagine a case. A biotech company wants to connect its internal research data to K Pro so scientists can ask questions of an agent. The data lives in several systems in different formats, and the client's IT team demands to know exactly how the data is handled at every step.

In this scenario, a junior FDE is best suited to work with clear boundaries: writing data pipelines to read and normalize the data, building an API for the agent to query, writing tests, and then deploying. This is why the posting requires backend fundamentals, not just the ability to call LLM APIs.

A senior FDE's work goes one step further. They sit with the scientists to figure out which questions are truly worth answering and decide what to do first; when the agent's results conflict with the scientists' understanding, they are the ones accountable.

A "high-impact problem" is one whose solution changes how the client works, not just one that adds another feature.

When a third client shows up with a similar need, the gap between the two levels widens further. Someone who only provides support will rewrite the connector a third time; someone operating at a senior level will recognize the pattern and package it into something any team can use.

Reusable assets are the real test

The senior posting requires distilling proven patterns into reusable, extensible components, tools, and runbooks. This is a hard requirement, because on-site client work constantly pulls people toward custom solutions.

Custom work is fast, and the client is immediately satisfied. But if everything is custom, every new project takes as much effort as the first, and the platform learns nothing from the field. A senior FDE must be flexible enough to solve the client's problem, yet disciplined enough to extract the common parts.

A good runbook lets the next person redo the work faster without asking the author. In the example above, it might be a one-page document: listing the questions to ask the client's IT team before touching any data, and the most common format errors.

The last two responsibilities in the senior posting, mentoring and on-call duty, point in the same direction. On-call means that if the system you deployed at the client breaks down at midnight, it's still your problem; mentoring means the knowledge accumulated in the field must be passed on.

When clients put proprietary data on the platform and engineers work inside the client's organization, production incidents involve not just technology but trust. The people assigned on-call are exactly those the company trusts to uphold that trust.

With two years of experience, what should you practice?

The junior posting sets the bar at two years of backend experience, which means engineering fundamentals must already be in place: designing APIs, handling messy data, and deploying things that run reliably. The LLM and agent layer is built on top of this foundation; it cannot replace it.

If your goal is the junior role, build a small agent system that actually runs: it should read data from a few sources in different formats, have tests, and be deployable repeatedly. A polished notebook demo won't prove what the posting asks for, namely production-grade AI systems.

If you have three to five years of experience and are aiming for the senior role, the most valuable interview question to prepare for isn't technical: have you ever turned a custom solution into something an entire team uses? If not, start with the smallest example: find a script you've written three times for three different people, and package it.

On your resume, don't just write "built a RAG pipeline for Client X." Tell it in three steps: the problem discovered in the field, the custom solution, and the reusable asset it eventually became, along with its users. The third step is exactly what Owkin's senior posting is looking for.

When reading any FDE job posting, pay attention to the verbs. "Shadow support" indicates a junior role where you learn alongside senior colleagues; "dive deep," "on-call," "mentor newcomers," and "runbooks" indicate the company needs someone who takes ownership of outcomes.

Owkin is bringing agent-style AI to the proprietary data of pharmaceutical and biotech customers, and its two postings already sketch out a career path: first, be able to deploy systems into production, then grow into someone willing to take the on-call phone for them.
