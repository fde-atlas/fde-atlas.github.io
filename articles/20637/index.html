FDE 90-Day Onboarding Plan: A Getting-Started Guide for New Forward Deployed Engineers

Original title: FDE 入职 90 天计划：新人前线部署工程师上手指南

FDE Times · 2026-10-27 · 6 min read

Summary

Drawing on onboarding handbooks from Tandem, Cloudsoft, fde.academy and job postings from Supergood, Vanta and Palantir, this guide lays out a 90-day plan for new forward deployed engineers. Its core idea: the slow first two weeks of shadowing are a ladder to climb, not neglect. It distinguishes context questions (self-serve) from judgment questions (mentor's time), and sets milestones: a day-30 account walkthrough, owning a small low-risk scope in month two, and by day 60 customers coming to you first. It closes with common mistakes and how to frame the period on a resume.

Key points

  • First two weeks are for internal learning and shadowing; climb proactively rather than wait for assignments.
  • Context can be handed over, judgment cannot—classify questions before asking your mentor.
  • Day 30: narrate the full customer account to your mentor without notes.
  • Month two: own one low-risk scope with a single data source and explicit out-of-scope lines.
  • Day 60 milestone: customer questions reach you first, with agreed exceptions for mentor involvement.

The first two weeks are mostly spent shadowing experienced engineers; this slow start is a ladder you can actively climb, not a sign of being sidelined.

Key Takeaways

  • The first two weeks focus on internal learning and shadowing senior FDEs; you won't have work of your own yet
  • In month 2, you shift from observing to doing; per Tandem's onboarding playbook, by day 60 customers come to you first
  • Context can be handed over, judgment cannot: your mentor's time should be spent on judgment

According to Tandem's onboarding playbook, by day 60 the order flips: customer questions reach the new FDE first, and senior engineers are only pulled in for exceptions. Just two months earlier, this new FDE hadn't taken on any work of their own.

The distance between these two milestones is your first 90 days. According to fde.academy, a Forward Deployed Engineer's (FDE) first two weeks are typically spent on internal learning and shadowing a senior FDE on an ongoing customer project. Managers also deliberately assign new hires low-risk, clearly scoped work first.

If you're about to accept an FDE offer, or have just transitioned from a software engineering role, this slow pace may make you feel sidelined. In fact, it's a step-by-step progression, and you can actively climb it rather than waiting for work to be assigned. Note that the specific day milestones come from individual companies' onboarding playbooks, not industry standards.

What do you need to produce, and what should you prepare in advance?

You need to create a

90-day-plan.md

file in your personal notes repository with four sections: call notes, a question log, a day-30 account review, and a statement of the scope you'll own.

You need three things: an ongoing project that lets you shadow, a mentor who agrees to meet with you regularly, and a calendar for marking milestones. If you don't know who your mentor is, ask your manager in the first week.

# 90-day-plan.md  （简化模板）
## 里程碑
- 第 1-2 周：内部学习，跟随资深 FDE 旁听
- 第 30 天：向导师完整讲述客户账户
- 第 2 个月：承担一块低风险的范围
- 第 60 天：客户先来问我
- 第 3 个月：把一项工作推进到演示之后
- 第 90 天：决定账户归属
## 电话会记录
## 问题记录
## 范围

Check: every milestone must have a specific date on the calendar. Any milestone without a date is the first question you take to your manager.

1. Shadowing doesn't mean just sitting and listening

Cloudsoft's 90-day playbook sets the first month's goal as "contributing safely," not producing extra output. The playbook also warns against passively attending calls: for every meeting, assign yourself a task.

That task could be listing all the systems the customer mentioned, or noting the commitments the senior FDE just made. After the meeting, compare your notes with your mentor's; wherever they disagree is where you haven't understood something yet.

## 电话会记录（假设示例）
2026-11-04 | 客户：运营团队
自定任务：列出数据源
- 我记下的：ERP、每周库存 Excel 文件
- 导师补充的：一个我漏掉的内部 API

Check: by the end of week one, every call you attended must have a line for "self-assigned task." An empty line means you were just listening in that meeting.

2. Which questions go to your mentor, and which you look up yourself

Tandem's entire playbook rests on one distinction: context can be handed over, judgment cannot. So a senior engineer's time is best spent on judgment. For you, this comes down to one concrete habit: before taking a question to someone, classify it first.

Imagine a retail customer. "Where is the order data stored?" is a context question; you can find the answer yourself in docs, tickets, or call notes. "The sync job only runs overnight—should we promise the customer hourly alerts?" is a judgment question; that's what's worth taking up your mentor's time.

## 问题记录（示例）
| 日期 | 问题 | 类型 | 谁来回答 |
| 11-05 | 订单数据存在哪里？ | 背景 | 自己查 |
| 11-20 | 该不该承诺每小时告警？ | 判断 | 导师 |

Check: each week, count how many context questions you asked someone instead of looking up yourself. That number should steadily decline.

3. Day 30: walk your mentor through the entire customer account

Tandem schedules a check-in on day 30, where the new FDE walks the mentor through the entire customer account. Since you're the one presenting, every stumble shows where you're still lacking.

## 账户复盘（简化提纲）
1. 客户要解决什么问题，谁拍板
2. 正在使用的系统和数据源
3. 已经向客户承诺了什么，截止时间是什么
4. 当前最大的风险
5. 三件仍不确定的事

Check: you must cover these five points without looking at your notes. If point 3 is still empty, fill it in first before requesting your own scope for month 2, because you can't deliver on a commitment you don't even know about.

4. Month 2: take on a small scope with real risk

Cloudsoft treats the second month as the transition point from shadowing to independently owned work. fde.academy notes that the first independently owned piece of work is often full ownership of a small customer—real but manageable risk.

What does such a scope look like? Supergood's job posting describes the FDE role as customer deployments, ongoing support, and new integrations. The advice here is to pick just one of those, and only one data source: if you take on two at once and everything breaks, you won't be able to tell what went wrong where.

## 范围（假设示例）
客户：小型零售连锁 X
工作类型：新集成
用例：缺货告警
数据源：ERP 中的一张库存表
不在范围内：供应商数据、用户界面

Check: the "out of scope" column must not be empty. When the customer wants to add requirements mid-project, this is the line you'll point to.

5. Day 60: customers come to you first

According to Tandem, by day 60 customer questions reach the new FDE first, with senior engineers pulled in only for exceptions. To make this milestone land smoothly, you and your mentor should agree in advance on what counts as an exception.

A reasonable approach is to use the question log: you handle context questions yourself; any question involving commitments, scope, or customer risk that requires judgment gets your mentor involved. Write this rule as three lines into your plan file so both of you can see it.

6. Month 3 and day 90 are two different milestones

Cloudsoft's goal for the third month is to push a piece of work past the demo stage. One way to draw the line: a demo only needs to work once on carefully chosen data, while something past the demo stage is something the customer can use on real data without you standing by.

fde.academy identifies two signals during days 60-90 that supervision can be reduced: a working deployment, and a customer relationship where trust in you is evident. Tandem's day-90 milestone is a different matter: the mentor decides whether to keep running the account or hand it over entirely to the new FDE.

The two signals mentioned by fde.academy are exactly the evidence to bring to the day-90 conversation. Record them as concrete items in your plan file: which deployment is running, and which questions the customer sent directly to you.

Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is trying to deliver something in week 2 to prove yourself. Your manager deliberately gives you low-risk work first, and jumping the queue at this point makes you look rash, not capable.

Another mistake is taking all context questions to your mentor, leaving them no time for judgment questions—when judgment is the one thing you can't teach yourself. Also, don't skip the account review just because you feel you've got the picture. If your company doesn't have a day-30 checkpoint, propose one yourself.

How to put these 90 days on your resume and bring them into interviews

Palantir describes its Forward Deployed Software Engineer role as working directly with customers, often on-site, and seeing projects through from start to finish. Since employers are looking for people who can finish projects, your resume should tell a complete loop: what scope you took on, and how far you pushed it.

So instead of writing "participated in customer deployment projects," write "independently owned an ERP inventory integration for one customer and took it from demo to production use."

When reading job descriptions, watch for whether senior FDE requirements include mentoring newcomers. For example, Vanta's Senior FDE job posting explicitly mentions curating best-practices documentation and mentoring team members. In interviews, you can ask directly: "By day 60, who do customers call first?"

The goal of these 90 days isn't lines of code—it's the day customers come to you first, with you knowing which questions you can answer yourself and which need your mentor.
