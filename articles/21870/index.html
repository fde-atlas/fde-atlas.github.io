The 1.5 million dollar salary rebrand

The Spinman · 2026-11-03 · 3 min read

Summary

The author reacts to a Wall Street Journal report and a search firm's study claiming elite forward deployed engineers earn $586,000 to $1.45 million, that only about 17,000 FDEs exist in the US with roughly 2,000 considered elite, and that about 80% of the elite are current or former Palantir engineers. He argues the model is not new: resident engineers, consultants, and professional services have long embedded engineers at client sites. What changed, he says, is the AI-era branding and manufactured scarcity narrative. The real value of an embedded engineer has always been trust, judgment, and follow-through, and the high pay reflects that rather than a genuine technological breakthrough.

Full text

# The $1.5 Million Rebrand

Read the Wall Street Journal this morning and had to set my coffee down.

Forward-deployed engineers are pulling down as much as $1.45 million a year. One search firm's study says elite FDEs make between $586,000 and $1.45 million, that there are only about 17,000 of them in the whole country, and only 2,000 or so are considered elite. About 80% of those elite folks are current or former Palantir engineers.

Now let me be fair before I get cranky. Some of these people are the real deal. They have gone inside big organizations, built real things, and made a measurable impact. That work deserves respect, and top talent has always commanded top money.

But here is my question... and I have been sitting with it all morning.

When did embedding an engineer with a customer become a new invention?

Engineers have been parking themselves at client sites for as long as I have been doing my thing, in fact long before actually. Consulting firms built empires on it. Contract engineers have spent entire careers working on the customer's floor, on the customer's biggest and most visible projects. They called them resident engineers, solutions engineers, professional services, staff augmentation, good old fashioned consulting. Different badge on the shirt, same chair in the same building.

So what exactly changed?

The honest answer is the label changed... and the label changed because AI showed up.

Palantir took an old delivery model and gave it a special forces name. Forward-deployed. It sounds like you need a helmet and a security clearance. And once the name sounded elite, the scarcity story wrote itself. Only 17,000 in the country!! Only 2,000 elite!! It is no wonder compensation went vertical. When you convince the market that a thing is rare, the market pays like it is rare.

Let me tell you what I think actually makes an embedded engineer valuable, because it is the same thing that made them valuable in 1995.

It is the human being.

The engineer who shows up, learns how the customer actually works, earns trust in the room, translates between the business people and the technical people, and stays until the thing works... that person has always been worth their weight in gold. Companies did not struggle with consultants and contractors because the model was broken. They struggled when the person in the chair treated it like a transaction instead of a relationship.

AI did not invent that person. AI just gave the job a new costume and a bigger number.

And I say this as somebody who loves what AI can do. I write about it. I use it. But I also know the difference between a genuine breakthrough and a marketing department doing what marketing departments do. Putting agentic AI into production is new... that part is real. Sending a smart engineer to sit with the customer until it works is older than most of the people reading this post.

Here is the part nobody wants to say out loud. If your company suddenly needs to pay $1.45 million for an embedded engineer, the technology is not your bottleneck. You are paying a premium for trust, judgment, and follow through... the very things that were always the job. The AI hype just finally put a price tag on them that got everybody's attention.

So welcome back, forward-deployed engineers. You never left. You were just called something else, and apparently you were underpaid.

Have Fun!!

Less Egos Means More Amigos
