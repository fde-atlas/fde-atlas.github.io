Forward Deployed Engineer - The Complete 2026 Guide to the Role

Scaler Topics · 2026-10-12 · 18 min read

Summary

This Scaler Topics guide explains the Forward Deployed Engineer role: its origins in Palantir's Dev vs Delta model, and its core work of building, integrating, and deploying products inside customer environments. It compares FDEs with software engineers, solutions engineers, AI engineers, and consultants, and uses job-posting data to debunk five myths. For example, an analysis of 160 postings found 53% were mostly building work and only 1% mostly advising, and of 1,181 active postings, 838 were mid-level rather than senior-only. It also covers typical responsibilities, required skills, hiring at OpenAI and Anthropic, and opportunities in India.

Key points

  • FDEs adapt existing products to specific customer systems, popularized by Palantir.
  • Titles like FDSE, Delta, and Deployment Strategist vary in emphasis by company.
  • 53% of 160 analyzed FDE postings were mostly building, not consulting.
  • 838 of 1,181 active postings were mid-level; ~40% listed no experience requirement.
  • Monthly FDE listings grew over 800% between January and September 2025.

Not quite a software engineer. Not quite a consultant. So, what exactly is a Forward Deployed Engineer? Let us help you understand what the role involves, what companies really look for in FDE job listings, which common claims about the role hold up, and how you can prepare for it. This guide also takes a look at FDE opportunities in India and the skills you need to build in 2026.

What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

A forward deployed engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who works with customers to build, integrate, and deploy a company's technology within their existing systems, data, and workflows. The role was popularised by Palantir, where engineers worked directly with organisations that had complex, customer-specific technical environments.

A traditional product engineer generally builds features for a broad customer base. An FDE takes those capabilities and adapts them to a particular business and its technical requirements.

Here’s how you can tell the difference:

Traditional product engineerForward deployed engineer
Primary focusBuilding product capabilitiesSolving a specific business problem
Where the work runsThe company's product or platformThe customer's technical environment
Success is measured byProduct performance and reliabilityWhether the solution works once deployed

For a deeper explanation of the role and its origins, check out this guide to what a forward deployed engineer is.

Where Did the FDE Role Come From?

Palantir developed the Dev vs Delta model around the different needs of product development and customer deployment. Devs build capabilities that can be used across organisations, while Deltas work directly with individual customers to apply those capabilities to their specific systems and requirements. This model particularly worked for organisations such as governments and large institutions, where data and infrastructure could be highly restricted or customised.

The approach has since spread to other technology companies. OpenAI, for example, hires FDEs to work on customer discovery, production systems, deployment, and technical problem-solving.

Are FDE, FDSE, Delta, and Deployment Strategist the Same?

These titles can describe similar types of work, but their responsibilities can differ by company. FDSE usually refers to a forward deployment role that is more focused on software, Delta is Palantir's term, and Deployment Strategist may place more emphasis on implementation and adoption.

You can look into how FDE, FDSE, and Deployment Strategist titles differ for the differences between these roles.

What Does a Forward Deployed Engineer Really Do?

As an FDE, you would be working on a product that already exists, but your job is to make it work for a particular customer. That could mean figuring out where an AI agent fits into an existing process, connecting it to internal systems, writing the code needed for the deployment, and fixing problems that appear once people start using it. Current FDE openings show that this work can stretch from the first customer discussion to running the system in production.

Here’s what you can expect from the role:

Understand what needs to be built

A customer may approach an FDE with a broad requirement such as automating a manual process, improving internal search, or introducing an AI agent. You need to understand how the team currently handles that work, which systems are involved, what data is available, and where the technical constraints are happening.

This will help you have enough information that will be needed to define the solution. You can then decide which parts require new development, which existing product capabilities can be used, and what integrations are needed to make the system work within the customer's setup.

Build and integrate the solution

Once you have the technical scope, a large part of the role involves writing and integrating software. You could work with APIs, databases, authentication systems, internal applications, cloud infrastructure, or data pipelines. For an AI product, the work may also involve connecting models to company data and tools, building retrieval systems, setting up evaluations, and testing the application's output.

The customer's existing infrastructure becomes part of the engineering work, so you may have to work with specific security requirements, access controls, legacy systems, or restricted data environments. If you're wondering whether forward deployed engineers actually write code, many FDE positions involve substantial hands-on development, although the amount of coding depends on the task itself.

Test the solution in the customer's environment

Before a system becomes part of someone's daily work, it needs to be tested with the data, systems, and users involved. An FDE may run pilots, check outputs, investigate failures, and make changes based on what happens during testing.

This can involve several rounds of technical changes. An integration may need to be adjusted, an AI application may need better evaluation criteria, or a workflow may need to be redesigned after users start testing it.

Help teams use the system

After deployment, you may work directly with the people using the system to test it, resolve issues, and document the new process. You may also conduct training sessions or explain how the product fits into existing tasks.

Use customer feedback to improve future deployments

The problems you encounter through the deployments can also become useful input for future work. If several customers need the same integration, repeatedly run into the same product limitation, or require similar workarounds, the pattern can be shared with the product and engineering teams.

What Does a Typical Week Look Like For an FDE?

Your time as an FDE is split between customer-facing work and engineering work. You may spend part of the week in discussions with customers and stakeholders, then use the information from those conversations to build, test, or improve the solution. Documentation, technical discussions, and follow-ups take up part of the schedule as the project progresses.

If you want a more detailed version of how these activities fit into an FDE's day, read our day in the life of a forward deployed engineer.

What Skills Does a Forward Deployed Engineer Need?

You need a solid software engineering foundation, followed by skills that help you work with integrations, AI applications, and customer requirements. Programming, APIs, databases, cloud infrastructure, and system design give you the technical base, while working with AI systems and understanding customer requirements help you apply that knowledge to FDE projects.

These skills are used when connecting a new application to existing systems, working with customer data and infrastructure, or adapting an AI application to a specific use case. The work can also involve discussing requirements with customers, explaining technical decisions, and dealing with constraints specific to their environment.

For the specific skills, tools, and learning areas to focus on, you can learn more with this complete forward deployed engineer skills breakdown.

How is an FDE Different From a Software Engineer, Solutions Engineer or AI Engineer?

When you look at the role broadly, these titles might even end up looking similar, but they do have their own specific aspects which make them quite different from one another. A software engineer generally builds for the product, an AI engineer focuses on AI systems, a solutions engineer works around the sales process, while an FDE takes responsibility for adapting and deploying technology for a specific customer.

Here is how each of these roles is quite different from one another:

Let us give you a detailed one on one comparison:

Software engineer

A software engineer typically builds software for a wider user base, with technical decisions driven by the product's overall requirements. An FDE works with a specific customer and may have to adapt the product, build integrations, or write additional software around the customer's systems.

For the detailed distinction, you can check out this forward deployed engineer vs software engineer guide.

Solutions engineer

Solutions engineering is usually connected to the buying process, which is understanding a prospect's technical requirements, demonstrating the product, and helping move the deal forward. An FDE may also be involved before a deal closes, depending on the company, but the role then typically continues into building and deploying the solution in the customer’s environment.

AI engineer

An AI engineer can work on the underlying models, AI applications, evaluation systems, and infrastructure that support a product. An FDE is more likely to take those capabilities and adapt them to a particular customer's data, systems, and requirements. You can read more about how the FDE and AI engineer roles split the work.

Consultant

Consultants generally advise organisations on a business or technical problem and may recommend a course of action without owning the software implementation. An FDE can work with customers in a similar advisory setting, but the role also involves building, integrating, and deploying the resulting technical solution.

The Biggest Myths About Forward Deployed Engineering

Rumour has it that the FDE is THE next big thing, and people have even been calling it a million-dollar earning opportunity, or that the role is expanding rapidly. But how much of it is true, and what should or should not be expected? Let’s debunk some of the myths circling FDE.

Myth 1: Every FDE Job Is Basically the Same

Based on everything we have mentioned so far, you must have made a judgment about the role. But companies may have their own set of expectations which could end up looking different. An analysis by Adam Boudjemaa of 519 FDE-family postings across 57 employers found that 28% of the postings labelled FDE or FDSE did not meet the study's broad definition of an engineering or product role.

The variation is visible in company hiring as well. OpenAI currently has FDE roles for general deployments, healthcare, legal, financial services, and government, alongside Forward Deployed Software Engineer, Forward Deployed Security Engineer, and Deployment Lead positions. Anthropic's current openings similarly include FDEs, Technical Deployment Leads, and managers within its applied AI organisation.

So, before you apply, it is always better to first go through the responsibilities demanded by the role posted. That will tell you whether the role involves building software, deploying systems, working with a particular industry, or leading deployments. The difference in responsibilities can also affect compensation, so you can check out forward deployed engineer salary ranges in India and the US before comparing offers.

Myth 2: FDE Is Basically Consulting With Some Coding Added to It

A July 2026 analysis by MORT of 160 FDE postings found that 53% of the roles were classified as mostly building, 31% as balanced between building and other work, and only 1% as mostly advising. Post-sales delivery appeared in 84% of the postings, while communication appeared in 76%.

The customer-facing part is there, but the engineering work remains central. The role can involve understanding requirements and discussing the solution, but it also involves building and deploying it.

If you are still trying to place the role between engineering and consulting, read our guide on whether a forward deployed engineer is just a consultant.

Myth 3: Every FDE Deployment Is a One-Off Custom Project

The work starts with a customer's specific systems and requirements, so some level of customisation is expected. A solution built for one organisation may need different integrations, data handling, or evaluation methods when it is deployed somewhere else.

But the engineering work does not necessarily disappear once that deployment is finished. OpenAI's financial services FDE role includes turning deployment work into repeatable solution patterns, operating standards, and evaluation practices.

The customer problem may be different the next time around, but the team can take the technical patterns and deployment practices from one engagement into another.

Myth 4: FDE Roles Are Mostly for Senior Engineers

This role sounds like it is meant for senior-level candidates only, and that is true for a lot of postings. But here's the thing: there are also openings that ask for less experience, and some even welcome new graduates.

FDE Pulse's analysis of 1,181 active FDE postings breaks the roles into junior, mid-level, senior+, and manager/lead positions. It puts 133 roles in the manager/lead category and 220 in senior+, while the largest group, 838 postings, falls under mid-level roles.

Also, in her analysis of more than 1,000 FDE listings, Aish Reganti found that around 40% of the postings did not state an experience requirement, although the median requirement across listings was around four years.

The early-career end is there too. Palantir's current careers page lists Forward Deployed Infrastructure Engineer roles for new graduates, alongside its other new-grad engineering openings. Its early-talent program is also open to people with less than six months of industry experience.

So if you're looking at this career early on, you don't have to wait until you have several years of experience before searching for these roles. The experience requirement changes with the level of the opening, so check the individual posting to look for the role that suits you best. You can also read our guide on how to become a forward deployed engineer as a fresher.

Myth 5: FDE Is Just a Temporary AI Hiring Trend

FDE hiring has definitely picked up with the growth of enterprise AI, but that does not necessarily tell us how the role will look a few years from now. A PYMNTS report reported that monthly FDE job listings increased by more than 800% between January and September 2025. Microsoft has also committed $2.5 billion and around 6,000 engineers, technical consultants and industry specialists to work with customers, while AWS has committed $1 billion to a similar effort.

At the same time, some companies are trying to make customers less dependent on engineers for every change. Jesse Zhang, Co-founder and CEO of Decagon, has written about giving business teams more control over building and improving AI agents instead of relying heavily on professional services.

He gives an example of a Fortune 50 company that had spent nine months trying to get five complex customer-facing experiences working with a competing AI CX product. With Decagon, the company built, tested, and iterated on those same experiences in three weeks.

This could reduce the need for FDEs for simpler deployments. But enterprise systems still involve integrations, security requirements, existing infrastructure, and production issues that may require engineering work.

The role is growing, but how much of that work remains under the FDE title will depend on how these products develop.

How Can You Become a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Build production-ready software first

Start with the engineering skills that let you take responsibility for a working application. That means being able to write solid code in one language, work with APIs and databases, use Git, package applications with Docker, and deploy them on a cloud platform. SQL, authentication, error handling, logging, and basic monitoring are also important because customer environments hardly look like a clean local development setup. You can use this forward deployed engineer roadmap to begin your learning journey.

Learn how AI applications are put together

An FDE working on AI products may need to connect an LLM to company data, build a retrieval pipeline, give an agent access to external tools, or evaluate whether the system is producing reliable results. Learn how RAG works, how tool-using systems are designed, how context is supplied to a model, and how evaluation and monitoring are handled once an AI application is running. You can use this structured AI engineer roadmap to understand all the topics you need to cover.

Put the skills together in integration-heavy projects

A simple chatbot or CRUD application may not show the kind of problems you could face in an FDE role. Instead, build something where different parts have to work together. For example, you could build an AI assistant that pulls information from a database, connects to another application through an API, controls who can access the data, and checks how well its responses work. A project like this gives you more to discuss in an FDE interview because you can explain how you handled the data, connected different systems, tested the application, and dealt with issues when something did not work as expected.

Practice solving a defined business problem

Take a business problem and build the project around the people who will use it. For example, a small support team could have customer information spread across documents and internal systems. You could build a search assistant that brings this information together, connects with the existing data source, and gives answers with references to the original documents.

You can then add the parts that make the project closer to a real deployment: user permissions, API connections, handling incomplete information, and testing the quality of the responses. Deploy the project and document how each part works.

This gives you a project where you can discuss the business requirement, the systems you worked with, the technical choices you made, and the results you achieved when you apply for an FDE role.

Forward Deployed Engineer Roles in India

If you are searching for FDE jobs in India and globally, you may find around 250-270 open FDE roles in India at a given time, based on 2026 market estimates. The main hiring locations include Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Mumbai, and Gurugram/NCR.

You will also see different experience requirements across these openings. Some ask for several years of software engineering experience, while others are open to candidates with less experience. Read the responsibilities closely and look for the amount of coding, system integration, customer interaction, and production deployment involved.

So, when you search, don't limit yourself to looking for the title of Forward Deployed Engineer. You can try titles like Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE), AI Engineer - FDE, AI Deployment Engineer, Customer Engineer, Solutions Engineer, and Solutions Architect as well. The Indian FDE market is still relatively small compared with mainstream software engineering, so your search will be broader if you track related titles and look across multiple locations. The exact title may change, but the responsibilities in the job description will tell you whether you are looking at an FDE-type role.

Is the FDE Role Here to Stay?

You may see the FDE title change as companies figure out how they want to organise AI deployment. Some companies already use titles such as Forward Deployed Software Engineer, Deployment Lead or Customer Engineer for similar work.

The work itself is already being used at scale. Atlassian has 46 FDEs working across the US, EMEA and India and has deployed more than 80 AI agents for 100+ enterprise customers. Other companies have also created dedicated teams around enterprise AI deployment.

At the same time, AI products are becoming easier to configure, and some tasks that currently require an engineer could eventually be handled by the customer or built into the product. More complex deployments can still involve existing systems, company data, security requirements, and production environments.

So, if you are considering FDE as a career, then the future of the role depends on the adaptation of AI in workplaces. Companies will continue to need engineers who can take a product into a customer's environment, connect it to their systems, and get it working in production. And the role may sit under a different name as the market develops.

Is the FDE Role Right For You?

The role may suit you if you want customer interaction to be part of your engineering work, can work with requirements that are still being defined, and can explain technical decisions clearly. Communication is one of the strongest signals in FDE hiring, so you need to be able to understand a customer's problem and translate it into a technical solution.

If you want your work to stay focused on one narrowly defined technical problem with little change in direction, an FDE role may not suit you. To get a better understanding of the role, you can look into the honest pros and cons of an FDE career.

FAQs

What does FDE stand for?

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer. You can think of the role as a software engineer who works closely with a customer to build, integrate, and deploy a product in the customer's environment. The role combines engineering with customer interaction, technical discovery, and deployment work.

What does a forward deployed engineer do?

As a forward deployed engineer, you may work with a customer to understand a problem, decide what needs to be built, connect the solution to existing systems, and take it into production. Coding is a significant part of many FDE roles, alongside integration, testing, and customer interaction.

Is a forward deployed engineer a software engineer?

Yes. If you are applying for an FDE role, you are still applying as an engineer, and most roles expect you to build and ship software. The difference is where the work happens and who you work with: FDEs often build directly around a customer's systems, requirements, and production environment.

Is a forward deployed engineer the same as a solutions engineer?

No. A solutions engineer usually works around the sales process, helping a prospective customer understand how a product could solve their problem. An FDE generally works after the customer problem has been identified and is responsible for building or deploying the solution. Check the job description for sales targets, quota, or OTE if you are unsure which type of role you are looking at.

Do you need a lot of experience to become a forward deployed engineer?

It varies with companies. Some current postings ask for several years of software engineering experience, while others are less specific about years of experience. If you are applying with less experience, the projects you have built, your engineering fundamentals, and your ability to work with customers can carry more weight than the job title in your previous role.

Why are companies hiring so many forward deployed engineers?

AI products can be difficult to deploy inside an existing business. Someone still has to connect the product to company systems and data, adapt it to the customer's processes, test it in production, and deal with problems that appear during deployment. That has created demand for engineers who can handle this work alongside the core product team.

Is forward deployed engineering just hype?

There is real hiring for FDE roles, particularly around AI deployment, but the market is still young, and companies use the title differently. You may see the responsibilities move into other engineering or customer-facing roles as AI products mature. The title is harder to predict than the underlying need to make complex technology work in a customer's environment.

Are there forward deployed engineer jobs in India?

Yes. You can find FDE openings in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and NCR, with additional roles in other Indian cities. Don't search only for “Forward Deployed Engineer”; try FDSE, AI Engineer – FDE, AI Deployment Engineer, and Solutions Engineer as well, because companies do not use one consistent title for this work.
