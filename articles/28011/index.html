What is a forward deployed engineer?

A forward deployed engineer (FDE) is a senior software engineer who works directly inside a customer’s or business’s real environment to build, integrate and ship software, usually AI, that makes a product work there. Instead of building one feature for many users, an FDE owns the outcome for one context: they learn the problem on site, write production code, and stay accountable until it works.

The role combines three things: an engineer’s ability to build, a consultant’s ability to understand a business, and a founder’s comfort with ambiguity. Some teams call it forward deployed software engineer or FDE engineer; the job is the same.

FDEs went from a niche Palantir title to one of the fastest-growing roles in tech. This guide covers what they do, how they differ from software and solutions engineers, what they cost, and how to hire one, including the new type of FDE we see companies asking for in 2026.

Where forward deployed engineering came from, and why it is everywhere now

Palantir coined the term, borrowing from the military idea of units deployed in the field. Its engineers embedded with government and enterprise clients, built prototypes on Palantir’s platform, and fed what they learned back to the core product. Marty Cagan of SVPG describes it as applying the product model to custom solutions.

Generative AI turned that model into a hiring wave:

  • Demand exploded. FDE job postings rose about 800% between January and September 2025, according to an Indeed and Financial Times analysis cited by Salesforce. A review of 1,000 FDE job posts counted 1,165% year-over-year growth in 2025.
  • The biggest AI companies built FDE teams. OpenAI, Anthropic, Salesforce, Ramp and the major cloud providers all hire them. Salesforce says it tripled its FDE team in six months.
  • AI pilots kept stalling. An MIT study from 2025 found that about 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots failed to deliver measurable returns. The gap was rarely the model. It was integration, data and workflow, which is exactly the work FDEs do.

At BEON we saw the first FDE-style openings in May 2025. By early 2026 it had become the fastest-growing new role category among the U.S. companies we work with.

What does a forward deployed engineer do?

An FDE turns a business problem into working software inside a real environment, with incomplete documentation and people waiting on the result. A typical cycle looks like this:

  • Discover. Sit with the people who do the work, map the process and find where software or AI can change it.
  • Scope. Turn that into a concrete plan: what to build first, what data it needs, what “done” means for the business.
  • Build. Write production code: integrations, data pipelines, AI agents and workflows, internal tools.
  • Deploy and debug. Ship into systems they do not control, and fix what breaks: permissions, messy data, legacy APIs.
  • Explain. Walk executives through trade-offs, risks and results in plain language.
  • Feed back. Turn what they learned into reusable components, or tell leadership when it is time to add people.

In AI-heavy roles, most of the build work is now LLM integration, retrieval (RAG), agent workflows and evaluations. In the job posts analyzed by Bloomberry, AI agents appeared in 35% of FDE listings and Python in 66%.

Forward deployed engineer vs. software engineer and other roles

The FDE overlaps with several roles, which is why job descriptions for it are so often confusing. The clearest line is ownership: an FDE writes the code and owns the result in a live context.

Software engineer Forward deployed engineer Solutions engineer Implementation specialist Technical consultant
Main focus One capability for many users Many capabilities for one context Pre-sales demos and technical questions Configuring an existing platform Diagnosing and advising
Writes production code Yes Yes Rarely Little or none Sometimes
Works with customers or business users Occasionally Daily Daily, before the deal Daily, after the deal Daily
Owns the outcome after launch For their feature Yes, end to end No For the setup Rarely
Typical environment Product team, roadmap, tickets Ambiguous, high-visibility, few docs Sales cycle Defined onboarding process Engagement with a scope

So the short answer to forward deployed engineer vs. software engineer: a software engineer builds the product; an FDE makes it work where it is needed, and changes what gets built along the way. The best FDEs are strong software engineers first.

Do you need a forward deployed engineer? The two types of FDE

Most articles describe only one kind of FDE: the engineer a software company sends to its customers. In our conversations with U.S. companies in 2026, a second kind is growing fast: the FDE a business hires to bring AI into its own operations.

Vendor-side FDE Internal FDE
Who hires SaaS and AI product companies Companies that are not software businesses, or whose core team is busy on the product
Works for The vendor, embedded with customers The business itself, across its teams or companies
Main goal Get deployments live and keep strategic accounts growing Automate processes and build AI tools that change how the business runs
Typical counterpart Customer engineering and IT teams Operators, finance, sales and leadership, often with no technical team

Signs you need a vendor-side FDE

  • Proof-of-concept deals close, then stall during implementation.
  • Enterprise customers need custom workflows or integrations beyond standard onboarding.
  • Product engineers keep getting pulled into customer escalations.
  • One flagship customer can materially move your revenue.

Signs you need an internal FDE

These come from real hiring conversations, described by industry:

  • An investment firm with a portfolio of about eight companies hired its first SVP of AI and needed a right-hand engineer who could walk into each business, talk to the team, map the process, build the solution and say when to add people.
  • A private lender with about a dozen employees and no technical staff wanted AI inside its lending operations. The first use case: an agent that reads the insurance declarations page on each loan and checks the coverage against the lender’s rules. As the CFO put it: “If we have to hire a person and have a project manager, we found the wrong person.”
  • A global application security company opened AI forward deployment roles inside its marketing technology team, to build and ship AI workflows for the business rather than for external customers.

If you have AI use cases piling up, tools that work “on the side” but are not connected to your systems, and nobody technical who can own the result, you are describing an internal FDE.

When it is too early

Skip the hire if your product is mostly self-serve and onboarding is repeatable. For an internal FDE, make sure there is a real backlog first: the risk is rarely the cost, but not having enough meaningful work to keep a senior engineer busy for 6 to 12 months.

Forward deployed engineer skills: an AI-first profile

An FDE is only as useful as their judgment in unfamiliar territory. The skills fall into three groups.

Technical depth

  • Full-stack in the production sense: has shipped end-to-end integrations in environments they did not control
  • Systems integration: APIs, authentication and permissions, data pipelines, legacy systems
  • AI implementation: LLM APIs, RAG, agents and memory, guardrails, evaluations, and the ability to choose and justify a model instead of defaulting to one
  • Infrastructure awareness: deployment constraints, security and data handling in the customer’s environment

Agentic ways of working

FDEs are often the only engineer in the room, so they need to move at the speed of a small team. That usually means working with coding agents: planning with them, giving them context, and verifying what they produce. How to assess that is covered in our guide to agentic coding and how to evaluate engineers.

Operating skills

Skill What it looks like in practice
Communication Explains a technical failure to a non-technical executive without losing precision
Ownership Does work beyond the assigned scope because the customer or business needs it
Comfort with ambiguity Creates structure while still learning; turns unknowns into a plan
Business judgment Understands why something is needed, pushes back, and ties technical choices to outcomes

Red flags

  • “Just call the API” engineers who handle the happy path but not the real system
  • Demo-only profiles with no recent production code
  • Brilliant engineers who cannot hold a conversation with an executive
  • Order-takers who wait for precise instructions

How to interview a forward deployed engineer

Algorithm puzzles tell you little about an FDE. Test deployment judgment, communication under pressure and ownership instead. A four-stage loop works well:

Stage Format What you learn
1. Communication and operating style 30 min, no coding Clarity, listening, curiosity about the business, comfort discussing messy projects
2. Technical scenario 60 min, with their usual AI tools How they break down a real integration failure, make assumptions, gather evidence and plan recovery
3. Customer simulation 45 min role play Whether they stay calm with a frustrated executive, avoid overpromising and explain trade-offs
4. Ownership validation References or case review Evidence of going beyond scope, earning trust and handling failure

Two adjustments make the loop fit 2026:

  • Let candidates use AI in stage 2. An FDE will work with coding agents every day. Watch how they plan, prompt and verify, not only the final answer.
  • For an internal FDE, add a discovery exercise. Give them a short description of a real process, such as loan document review, and ask what they would automate first, what they would ask the team, and how they would know it worked.

If nobody on your side is technical, the vetting partner has to carry the technical assessment. Ask to see how they evaluated each candidate, and keep stages 1 and 3 in your own hands: you are the best judge of whether this person can work with your team.

How much does a forward deployed engineer cost?

In the U.S., FDEs are paid at or above senior software engineering levels. The Bloomberry analysis of 1,000 postings found a median salary of about $174,000, and 70% of roles include equity. At frontier AI companies, packages with equity go much higher.

Option Typical annual cost What it includes
U.S.-based FDE, first year $250,000+ Salary, benefits, payroll taxes, recruiting fees
Senior FDE in Latin America through a staffing partner $80,000–$120,000 Compensation, benefits, equipment, payroll handling; no recruiting fee

That is a difference of roughly $130,000–$180,000 per engineer per year. Latin America works well for this role because the overlap with U.S. hours is full, and senior engineers there often have years of experience working directly with U.S. stakeholders. How long it takes to hire

A traditional FDE search often takes 8 to 12 weeks. Most delays come from the hiring side, not the market: a job description that mixes several roles, disagreement on how customer-facing the role is, or interviews that test algorithms instead of deployment judgment. With a pre-vetted talent pool, the timeline drops to about four weeks: profiles in week one, interviews in week two, and a two-week notice period before the start date.

Forward deployed engineer job description template

The clearer the role, the more the right candidates self-select in. Avoid generic lines like “customer-obsessed engineer to help clients implement our platform.” Name the environment, the work and the constraints.

Job title: Forward Deployed Engineer (AI)

About the role. You will be the engineer closest to [our customers / our business teams]. You will work directly with [enterprise financial services clients / our operations and finance teams] to find where software and AI can change how work gets done, then build and ship it.

What you will do

  • Discover and map processes with business stakeholders, and turn them into a scoped plan
  • Build production integrations, data pipelines and AI workflows using LLM APIs, RAG and agents
  • Deploy into systems we do not fully control, and debug what breaks
  • Explain trade-offs, risks and results to executives
  • Turn repeated work into reusable components, and flag when the team needs to grow

What you bring

  • 5+ years of production software engineering, including end-to-end integrations
  • Hands-on experience with LLMs, agents or RAG in production
  • Strong written and spoken English, and comfort presenting to executives
  • A record of owning outcomes beyond your assigned scope
  • An agentic workflow: you use coding agents daily and know how to verify their output

How you will work. [Remote, with 4+ hours of overlap with U.S. Eastern / occasional travel to customer sites]. Limited documentation, high visibility, and a lot of autonomy.

FAQ

What is a forward deployment engineer? It is another name for a forward deployed engineer: a senior engineer who works inside a customer’s or business’s environment to build and ship software that works there, and owns the result.

How much do FDEs get paid? In the U.S., the median salary in 2025 job postings was about $174,000, often with equity. First-year total cost for a U.S. hire commonly exceeds $250,000. Senior FDEs in Latin America cost about $80,000–$120,000 a year through a staffing partner.

Is a forward deployed engineer a good role? For engineers who like variety, customers and ownership, yes: it is one of the fastest-growing roles in tech and pays at or above senior engineering levels. It is a poor fit for engineers who prefer deep focus on one codebase with clear tickets.

What is the difference between an FDE and a solutions engineer? Solutions engineers work before the sale: demos, technical questions, closing. FDEs work after it: they write code, debug integrations and own the outcome in production.

Can a forward deployed engineer work remotely? Yes, in most cases. Discovery and deployment work well remotely with good access and communication. Some roles add occasional on-site time for workshops, security reviews or high-stakes launches.

When is it too early to hire an FDE? For a product company, usually before you have several enterprise customers with complex integrations. For an internal FDE, when you cannot yet name enough real use cases to keep a senior engineer busy for six months.
