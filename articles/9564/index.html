ibl.ai vs OpenAI Forward-Deployed Engineers

ibl.ai · 2026-08-19 · 8 min read

Summary

ibl.ai published a comparison with OpenAI's forward-deployed engineers. It notes OpenAI capitalized a separate deployment company at over $4 billion, backed by TPG with Advent, Bain Capital and Brookfield, and acquired Edinburgh-based Tomoro for roughly 150 deployment engineers, following Palantir's model. ibl.ai says it also deploys OpenAI models and builds production agents, but customers keep full code and data ownership, model-agnostic routing (Claude, Gemini, open weights), and AI sovereignty. Its platform reportedly serves 1.6M+ users across 400+ organizations, including NVIDIA, MIT and Syracuse University, with usage-based pricing, no per-seat fees, and deployment options up to fully air-gapped networks. The article argues frontier models are API-only and cannot run in isolated networks, unlike open weights on owned platforms, and offers migration steps from OpenAI engagements.

Key points

  • OpenAI's deployment company valued over $4B; Tomoro acquired with ~150 engineers
  • ibl.ai claims customers own all code and data, with switchable models
  • Platform runs 1.6M+ users across 400+ organizations, including NVIDIA and MIT
  • Frontier models are API-only and cannot run air-gapped
  • Usage-based pricing with no per-seat fees; on-premise and air-gapped support

Verdict

You do not have to choose between GPT and sovereignty. Hire the partner that gives you the agents AND the ownership, rather than trading one for the other.
