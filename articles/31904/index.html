Capability Map: From Being Able to Do Tasks to Being Able to Own Outcomes

Original title: 能力地图：从会做任务到能承担结果

pafa.github.io · 2021-07-12 · 7 min read

Summary

This chapter offers a capability map for aspiring forward deployed engineers (FDEs) and those developing them, arguing that vague adjectives like "good communicator" should be replaced with observable actions and checkable artifacts. It proposes four condition labels for each skill—concept only, done under guidance, done independently within clear boundaries, able to help others under changed conditions—and urges writing "no evidence" when materials are missing. A diagnostic exercise using a four-item handover shows how to separate capability gaps from authorization gaps and requirement conflicts. References include Vlad Shulman's hiring reflections at Baseten, Dropbox's 2021 engineering career framework, and AWS's October 2026 FDE training pathways, each with explicit limits on what they can prove.

Key points

  • Replace skill adjectives with observable actions and checkable artifacts.
  • Rate abilities with four condition labels; write "no evidence" when lacking.
  • Separate capability gaps, authorization gaps, and requirement conflicts.
  • Cites Vlad Shulman, Dropbox's 2021 framework, and AWS's 2026 FDE pathways.
  • Includes a four-material handover diagnostic for assigning work responsibly.

Vlad Shulman, who leads FDEs at Baseten, has described a shift in his hiring judgment: the team was initially too fixated on finding experts who already knew machine learning, and later put solid software engineering fundamentals back at the center, while valuing customer awareness, communication, and coachability. This is a team reflecting on its own hiring practice; it should not be read as "machine learning doesn't matter," nor does it prove that any engineer can quickly handle all FDE work. 1

It reminds us that a capability map should help people judge whether the next piece of work can be handed over. Merely drawing lines of different lengths for coding, product, communication, and business still leaves you not knowing: what this person can do on their own, where they need a partner, and in which situations they must stop.

This piece is aimed at practitioners preparing to enter FDE roles, and also at technical and business leaders who need to develop them. The question is not how to obtain a new title as fast as possible, but how to gradually earn this kind of trust: give this person a problem that is not yet well understood, and they can investigate it, build it, verify it, and also clearly state which parts they cannot yet own.

First, identify which starting point you are at

The two people who later moved into FDE roles that you will read about had already done technical work beforehand. Just joining a company, just encountering an industry, and just starting to learn programming are different starting points. You cannot read their few weeks of adapting to a new role as the time needed to build all capabilities from scratch. For someone without an engineering foundation, it is more appropriate to first complete small programs, understand data, and troubleshoot failures under guidance, rather than directly imitating someone independently owning a client project.

For someone who has written server-side programs for years, the new exercise may be to first understand how the client works before deciding what to change. For someone used to technical demos, the new exercise may be making the solution keep running after the presenter leaves. For someone very familiar with an industry, the new exercise may be filling in programming, data, and debugging fundamentals, and personally verifying whether the process they propose can be executed accurately by a system.

Therefore, a learning path should begin with an honest inventory: which things you can already do independently, which things require guidance, and which judgments you have only heard about. Distinguishing these three is more useful than adding another line of skill keywords to your resume. Someone familiar with warehousing can absolutely spot the ambiguity of "shipped" earlier than a model expert; but having spotted the ambiguity, someone still needs to implement the fix in the system and prove it did not break other processes.

Different starting points call for different preparation paths: first find the evidence that is still missing, then decide who should guide you and how to check progress.

Replace adjectives with observable actions

You can pick a target role and rewrite each responsibility as an action others can observe. For example, "good at communication" can become: after hearing two conflicting requirements, can you accurately restate the disagreement and propose a checkable next step? "Systems thinking" can become: after changing one step, can you point out whose wait time, error rate, or workload might increase? "Ownership" can become: when you discover that delivery depends on a system you have no authority to change, can you promptly find the person in charge instead of silently making promises?

The table below is a suggestion this book uses for learning and assigning tasks; it is neither an industry certification nor a hiring scorecard. Pick your most recent piece of work or project, and for each row find a piece of material that others can examine.

Understanding the problemRestating who is affected at which step; leaving behind an original hypothesis corrected by actual materialHaving written a requirements document does not mean the business owner agreed to the rules
Making trade-offsComparing two feasible options, noting who bears the cost and what evidence would change the choiceListing pros and cons does not mean you have the authority to take risks on others' behalf
Completing engineering workTracing from an input to the output, reproducing an error and explaining the impact of the fix; keeping the corresponding checksCorrectness on a single-machine sample does not mean concurrency, permissions, and production operation are all correct
Coordinating actionWriting disagreements down as questions to be decided, finding the person with authority to decide, and verifying their responseA meeting ending does not mean all parties have made the same commitments

Leaving behind capabilityHaving another person complete a varied task using the materials, and fixing the real points where they get stuckThe author's smooth demo does not mean others can take over

One piece of material can support more than one row. For example, an incident handled with clear inputs, a documented rationale for the fix, and a review record can show both engineering capability and verification capability. But the same material cannot be counted as multiple independent successes, let alone cover environments it never went through.

When Dropbox publicly introduced its engineering career framework in 2021, it emphasized impact and behaviors, while making clear that the framework is not a promotion checklist and cannot replace ongoing communication between employees and managers. What is borrowed here is that limitation: the table helps discuss concrete performance; it cannot make decisions for the discussants. Dropbox's leveling system is also not a universal standard for FDEs. 2

On October 1, 2026, AWS divided partner FDE capability training into three paths—Ground, Orchestrate, and Prove—focusing respectively on trustworthy business data, reliable execution and recovery, and evals and control; it explicitly stated that these are not three fixed roles. Its FDE Validated and Advanced credentials require corresponding AWS certifications and hands-on verification. 3 This provides comparable evidence of capability, but an individual completing a set of verifications does not mean the whole team can already own a client deliverable.

After "can," you still need to write the conditions

For each action, you can use four descriptions: only understands the concept; has completed it under guidance; has completed it independently within clear boundaries; can help others complete it under changing conditions. When there is no material, write "no evidence yet." These descriptions represent the currently observed scope; they do not attach permanent grades to people.

For example, "I can handle duplicate messages" is too broad. A more reliable description is: "In one serial run of the booking synthesis exercise, I could explain why the same number with the same content no longer changes business state; how to persist records after a restart is not yet implemented and needs guidance." The second half does not weaken the first half; it makes task assignment more accurate.

"Independent" also does not mean avoiding documentation, tools, or peers. It means being able to organize your own judgment: knowing what to ask, being able to check the answers you receive, being able to explain decisions, and escalating when something exceeds your authority or knowledge. Handing over AI-generated code directly while being unable to explain the inputs and failure conditions does not count as independent work just because you never asked a human.

If a reviewer proposes a new condition, first check whether the original evidence still applies. Someone who can fix a single erroneous record is not necessarily able to handle an overall change in data structure; someone who can explain to technical colleagues is not necessarily able to help frontline staff make decisions. Capability expansion should become visible through new behaviors.

A gap diagnosis you can start immediately

The following is a teaching diagnostic, not an employer test. Imagine you receive a handover note for a piece of work, with only four items of material:

  • A demo that can extract event names from requests, but with no results for ambiguous requests.
  • All ten fixed sample cases of the program pass; the process of others running it has not been recorded.
  • The requirements say "automatic notifications," but the organizer only agreed to generating drafts pending confirmation.
  • The author can explain the code; sending notifications requires an account that has not yet been approved for use.

Do not start by judging "is this person a good FDE." Instead, write an assignment recommendation: which piece of work can be handed to him now, which commitment cannot yet be made, what the next most valuable piece of evidence is, and who needs to provide what support.

One defensible judgment is: he can be allowed to continue refining the extraction and draft workflow in a synthetic environment; the ten samples only prove the checked scope; automatic notifications lack both authorization and the organizer's acceptance. The next step may be to verify the "draft" scope and add ambiguous inputs, rather than adding a model first. If you choose differently, you should explain how it handles these four items of material, rather than defaulting the gaps to already solved.

There are three concrete criteria for evaluating this diagnosis. First, does it separate "never done" from "done wrong"; having no record of a human running it does not necessarily mean handover is impossible. Second, does it separate capability gaps, authorization gaps, and conflicting requirements; learning more programming cannot substitute for account approval. Third, can the recommendations be carried out as a piece of work, rather than a vague call to "improve comprehensively".

Then apply the same method to your own work. Pick only the one gap that most affects your next task, and write down the materials you already have, the observations you need to fill in, who is willing to give feedback, and the stopping condition. If you cannot even explain the basic request path, you should return to the mini-program first; if the technology is reliable but a conflicting requirement remains unresolved, shift the exercise toward business judgment. Do not open six learning tasks with no completion criteria just because the capability table has six rows.

Negotiating a suitable piece of responsibility from others

When discussing growth with a mentor or manager, you can bring a request like this: "I can already reproduce this class of failure, but I have not yet independently decided production changes. I would like to take charge of investigation and alternative proposals first, with you reviewing the risks; after completing checks for two different inputs, we can discuss whether to expand the scope." The number of checks should be determined by the actual risk, not set by this book as a promotion threshold.

This makes it easier to form an actionable development arrangement than "give me a bigger project". The manager should likewise state which parts will not be handed over for now, and what actual performance would change the arrangement. If there is never a reviewer, necessary information, or authorization, the problem should be logged as a missing working condition, not turned back on the newcomer as insufficient initiative.

The chapters after this one each train a judgment on this map. You can start reading from wherever most blocks your next step, then connect it through the same project as in Learning and Portfolio Work. How many chapters you finish is only a record of effort; outcomes you can explain, modify, and hand off to others are the evidence of capability.
