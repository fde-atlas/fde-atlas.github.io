Forward deployed AI engineer and agent engineer roles

Kernavique · 2026-11-20 · 16 min read

Summary

Kernavique analyzed 298 US job postings titled “forward deployed” across 177 public feeds on November 20, 2026: 21 titles contained “AI” and 8 contained “agent” wording. Reading 15 ads in detail, the authors found evaluation was the most frequently named skill (7 of 15), followed by retrieval, prompting, and tool use, while fine-tuning appeared only three times and was never a requirement. The ads emphasize building and operating production AI agents for customers, and some employers like Decagon and Sierra use the title Agent Engineer instead. The article also compares published pay ranges, including Scale AI's base range of $180,000–$315,000 depending on level, and contrasts these ads with classic forward deployed engineer postings that list only general programming languages.

Key points

  • 21 of 298 postings had “AI” in the title; 8 had agent wording.
  • Evaluation was named in 7 of 15 ads, the most common skill.
  • Fine-tuning appeared in 3 ads and was never required.
  • Ads stress production AI agents built with customers, not just deployment.
  • Scale AI publishes base ranges from $180,000 to $315,000 by level.

How many postings carry AI or agent wording

We counted titles on 177 public job feeds on November 20, 2026. Of the 298 US postings with “forward deployed” in the title, at 65 employers:

  • 21 of 298 have the word “AI” in the title. They sit at 15 employers: Cresta, Databricks, Handshake, Reddit, Snowflake and Turing with two each, and Anyscale, CoreWeave, Nebius, OpenAI, Palantir, Ramp, Sardine, Stripe and Traversal with one each.
  • 8 of 298 have “agent”, “agents” or “agentic” in the title: Scale AI with three, Cresta with two, and Cohere, CoreWeave and Snorkel AI with one each.

A title can count under both words, so the two figures must not be added. CoreWeave’s “Senior Forward Deployed Engineer, AI Agents” is in both.

Three limits apply. The count is a word match on the title, so it includes “Forward Deployed AI Accelerator, Marketing” at Stripe and a Nebius role titled for “Physical AI Infrastructure”. The feeds are a convenience sample of mostly AI and tech employers, and Amazon, ServiceNow and Deloitte are not in them. And the title undercounts the work: Anthropic’s “Staff Forward Deployed Engineer” has neither word in its title and is an agent-building role in its text.

For the full employer list, see who is hiring forward deployed engineers.

What the ads say the work is

We read 15 ads for these roles on November 20, 2026. Our reading tool shortens sentences, so we paraphrase and keep quotations to a few words.

The common thread is production. Scale AI’s Frontier Agents Engineer ad lists four verbs for production AI systems: “architect, integrate, deploy, and operate”. Turing’s Forward Deployed AI Engineer ad describes building production systems from multi-agent workflows, orchestration and tool use. Giga asks its senior engineers to design and ship “production-ready AI agents”.

The customer is in the room. Anthropic’s Applied AI Engineer, Commercial ad describes partnering with customer engineering teams to take hard agent builds from idea to production. Its Staff Forward Deployed Engineer ad describes building production applications and agent components with Claude. Labelbox calls its engineers the company’s “technical and commercial front line” inside its largest enterprise customers.

Some roles are narrower:

  • Platform configuration. Cresta’s senior ad is about developing, configuring, deploying and tuning agents on Cresta’s own platform.
  • Integration. OpenRouter’s ad is about helping customers fit its product into their applications, workflows and infrastructure.
  • Agents that operate computers. Snorkel AI has a senior ad for customer engagements built around computer-use agents.
  • Reinforcement learning environments. Labelbox has an ad that owns their design, build and operation.
  • Internal agents. AssemblyAI’s Triage ad includes building and running the agents that handle its own first line of customer contact.

Agent engineer as a title. Some employers drop “forward deployed” altogether. Decagon’s blog (Amy Jin, June 5, 2025) says the company calls the role the Agent Engineer, and describes full ownership of designing, building and deploying agents. Sierra’s careers page lists an Agent Engineering department, with “Software Engineer, Agent” roles split by industry and by language, and tells early-career applicants they will work directly with customers to build agents. We read Sierra’s careers page, not its individual ads.

For how the role compares with solutions architects and product engineers, see forward deployed engineer vs solutions engineer. For the day-to-day shape of the job, see our forward deployed engineer role page.

Evaluation leads. It is the only skill named in 7 of the 15 ads, across six employers. Two ads make it a deliverable. Anthropic’s describes evaluation suites that become the customer’s acceptance criteria. Snorkel AI’s names “LLM-as-a-judge” evaluation.

Fine-tuning is rare and never required. It appears in 3 of the 15 ads: as a preferred item at Scale AI and Snorkel AI, and as a duty in Snorkel AI’s Applied AI Engineer ad. No ad we read lists it as a requirement.

Bloomberry’s analysis of 1,000 postings points the same way. It reports “AI Agents (35%)”, “LLM experience (31%)” and retrieval-augmented generation at 12%, with LangChain at 4% and LlamaIndex at 2% (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026, read November 20, 2026). Bloomberry states no method for its classification.

Shipped agents are not always required. Anthropic’s Staff Forward Deployed Engineer ad says hands-on experience with language models and agents is “expected”, and also says the applicant does not need to have shipped agents to production.

Experience asked for runs wide. C3 AI’s federal new-graduate ad asks for none. Labelbox’s RL Environments ad asks for “2+ years”, Cresta for “3+ years”, Scale AI for four or more, Snorkel AI for “5+ years” and Turing for “8+ years”.

If the work you do sits nearer model serving and pipelines than the customer’s site, see our LLM and MLOps engineer role page.

How these ads differ from classic forward deployed ads

We limit this to what the ads show.

  • A skill line about AI replaces the general language line. Palantir’s New York Forward Deployed Software Engineer ad asks for “Python, Java, C++, TypeScript/JavaScript, or similar” and, in our read, names no AI skill. The agent-era ads name MCP, evaluation, retrieval and context engineering.
  • Evaluation is written in as something the engineer delivers. Anthropic’s and Snorkel AI’s ads do this, as the skills section shows.
  • Coding agents become a requirement. AssemblyAI asks for daily use of coding agents such as Claude Code or Codex, with real projects shipped. C3 AI lists the same kind of experience as preferred for new graduates.
  • Named frameworks are uncommon. Only 2 of the 15 employer ads name one.
  • One employer shows the split inside a single team. Scale AI runs two Frontier Agents Engineer ads in the same department, “Enterprise Engineering”, and our read found the same range on both, $180,000 to $225,000. The forward deployed ad leads with integrating and operating systems. The Applied AI ad leads with moving research into production and with measuring systems by quantitative metrics, and lists fine-tuning as preferred. The forward deployed ad says its engineers work closely with the Applied AI engineers.

The interview is likely to follow the skills. Our forward deployed engineer interview guide covers what is public about those loops.

In practice

If you are an engineer working with language models, read the skill lines before the title. The ads reward evidence that you can measure a system, not only build one: an evaluation suite you designed, a retrieval system you ran in production, tools you exposed to an agent. Fine-tuning will not hurt, and no ad we read asks for it. If this is the work you want, send us your details and résumé.

If you are hiring, name the skills. The clearest ads say which of evaluation, retrieval, MCP and context engineering are required and which are preferred, and say whether the engineer delivers the evaluation the customer will accept the system on. State the pay basis in the ad, because applicants will read it beside Scale AI’s base ranges. If you want help defining or filling the role, ask Kernavique for help.

What we left out

  • A market size for these roles. The title counts come from 177 job feeds chosen for convenience. They describe that list, not the US market.
  • Counts for employers outside the feeds. Amazon, ServiceNow, Deloitte and every employer on Workday are not in the 298.
  • Long quotations. Our reading tool shortens sentences, so we paraphrased and quoted only a few words at a time. Open the ad before you rely on any wording.
  • Any claim that an ad is silent on a skill. A skill missing from our counts means our read did not return it.
  • Pay figures matched from the feeds by pattern. Cresta and others show ranges in the feed data, but no person read them on the ad, so they are not in the pay tables.
  • OpenAI’s ads. Its ad pages could not be read on November 20, 2026.
  • Sierra’s individual ads and their pay. We read its careers page only. A copy of one closed Sierra ad on an investor’s job board was not used.
  • Contract postings from agencies. Some name specific frameworks as requirements. They are not employer ads, so they are not in the 15.
  • Bloomberry’s shares as market facts. We quote them as one third party’s unexplained classification.

Questions

  • What is a forward deployed AI engineer?
  • In the ads we read on November 20, 2026, it is an engineer who builds production systems on language models for one customer at a time. Turing’s ad with that exact title describes production AI systems built from multi-agent workflows, orchestration and tool use. Scale AI, Anthropic and Cresta describe the same work under other titles.
  • How many forward deployed roles are AI or agent roles?
  • By title, 21 of 298 US postings on the 177 job feeds we read on November 20, 2026 contain the word “AI”, and 8 of 298 contain “agent”, “agents” or “agentic”. A title can count under both. The title undercounts the work: Anthropic, Giga and OpenRouter describe agent work in ads whose titles carry neither word.
  • What skills do forward deployed AI engineer ads ask for?
  • In the 15 ads we read, evaluation is named in 7, retrieval or retrieval-augmented generation in 5, prompting or prompt engineering in 5, and tool use or function calling in 4. MCP, context engineering and observability are each named in 3. These counts rest on what our read of each ad returned, so an ad may name a skill we did not capture.
  • Do I need fine-tuning experience?
  • Not on the evidence of these ads. Fine-tuning appears in 3 of the 15, and in none of them as a requirement: it is a preferred item in two and a duty in one. Bloomberry’s analysis of 1,000 postings, which has no stated method, reports agents in 35% and language model experience in 31%.
  • What do forward deployed AI and agent engineer roles pay?
  • Two employers label a base range. Scale AI publishes $180,000 to $225,000 for its Frontier Agents Engineer, rising to $252,000 to $315,000 at staff level, and Turing publishes $200,000 to $250,000 for its Los Angeles ad. Anthropic, Palantir and Guidehouse publish salary ranges that do not say base, so we list them separately. All were read on November 20, 2026.
  • Is an agent engineer the same job as a forward deployed engineer?
  • Some employers use the name for the same kind of work. Decagon’s blog says it calls the role the Agent Engineer, and Sierra’s careers page has an Agent Engineering department with a “Software Engineer, Agent” title split by industry. Neither title contains the words forward deployed, so neither appears in our posting counts.
