For those who have experienced requirements definition, client negotiations, and project management at an SIer, FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a strong candidate for their next career move. The FDE way of working—embedding yourself at the customer's site, understanding their business, and taking responsibility through implementation—overlaps considerably with what SIer engineers do every day, and in fact, some FDE job postings in Japan list "practical experience as a systems engineer" or "PM/PL experience in large-scale system implementations" as requirements.

This article explains, based on official job postings from SoftBank, Andpad, JDSC, and Money Forward, official blogs from LayerX and Sansan, official announcements from Deloitte Tohmatsu, Hitachi, and Salesforce, and articles from The Pragmatic Engineer, which parts of SIer experience are valued in FDE roles and which tend to be lacking, the different transition routes at major companies, user companies, and independent firms, how to rewrite your resume, changes in salary, and the trend of major SIers and consulting firms themselves starting to create FDE positions.

Key Takeaways of This Article

  • SIer experience in requirements definition, client negotiations, and large-scale system implementation is explicitly listed as required or preferred qualifications in public FDE job postings, making SIer alumni one of the main recruitment targets for FDE roles (job postings from Andpad, SoftBank, and Money Forward, as of October 2026).
  • What SIer alumni tend to lack in FDE selection processes is threefold: experience building applications using LLMs, experience with evaluation (designing PoC evaluations), and the mindset of solving problems within the constraints of an in-house product.
  • Transition routes from SIer to FDE fall into three categories: FDE roles at AI startups and SaaS companies, in-house FDE roles at large enterprises, and newly established FDE organizations within major SIers and consulting firms—and the experience required differs depending on which you choose.
  • In your resume, the most effective rewrite for FDE selection is to reframe your SIer achievements not in terms of "the systems you delivered" but in terms of "how the customer's business changed."
  • In public Japanese job postings, SoftBank's FDE role (seconded to SB OAI Japan) lists a theoretical annual salary of ¥8.12 million to ¥20.34 million, JDSC lists ¥5 million to ¥25 million, and Money Forward's internal FDE role lists ¥7.9 million to ¥15 million per year (as of October 2026).

Conclusion: Why FDE Is a Candidate as a Next Step for SIer Professionals

An FDE is an engineer who implements and deploys their company's product to fit the customer's site and stays alongside the customer until results are achieved. In its official blog from July 2025, LayerX describes the FDE as "an engineer 'Deployed' to the 'Forward' line—standing on the front line with customers, truly understanding their challenges, and driving product implementation and deployment." The flow of understanding the customer's business, solidifying requirements, and delivering something that works is exactly the work SIer engineers have repeated project after project.

However, SIer and FDE are not the same. In its official blog from January 2026, Sansan describes the FDE's position as "different from consultants, whose role ends at the proposal stage, and also different from SIers, who build systems from scratch based on requirements." FDEs have their own product, solve customer challenges within its constraints, and feed insights gained in the field back into the product. SIer experience forms a foundation for FDE work, but it does not translate directly—you need to understand "what is valued and what is missing" and prepare accordingly.

Three Areas of SIer Experience Valued in FDE Roles

FDE is still a new role, and there are almost no experienced FDEs in the market. For this reason, companies hire people transitioning from adjacent roles, and SIer alumni are one of the main sources. Of the experience accumulated at an SIer, the following three areas are clearly valued in FDE job postings and official blogs.

1. Requirements Definition: The Ability to Structure Unarticulated Business Processes

The starting point of an FDE's work is understanding the customer's business and structuring their challenges. In its official blog, LayerX writes that FDEs "need to deeply understand the business domain, not just superficially trace business flows," and explains that "the ability to untangle unstructured information and incorporate it into business logic in implementation is essential." Sansan also cites "the ability to understand sales processes and business structures and articulate challenges" as a requirement for FDEs.

Experience in requirements definition as an SIer—eliciting business flows from customer representatives and translating them into specifications, including exception handling and handoffs between departments—is precisely this skill. JDSC's job posting lists as a mandatory requirement "experience organizing challenges through dialogue with stakeholders even when requirements are unclear, and carrying them through to implementation" (as of October 2026). Anyone who has handled upstream processes at an SIer should be able to answer this requirement with concrete projects.

2. Customer engagement: the ability to win agreement from executives to frontline staff

FDEs deal with people in many different positions at the customer. Sansan's official blog cites "the ability to engage with diverse stakeholders, from executives to frontline staff, and drive consensus-building" as a stance required of FDEs. In an interview with Levtech LAB, Mr. Sakamoto of Loglass stated that what matters for an FDE is "not just technical and logical correctness, but the ability to move forward while accounting for human complexity" (March 2026).

In SIer projects, experience finding common ground when the customer's IT department and business departments disagree, or negotiating scope changes with the customer, is work of the same nature as FDE customer engagement. SoftBank's FDE job posting lists "project management experience including customer engagement" as a mandatory requirement (as of October 2026), making this experience the entry point for selection.

3. Large-scale operations: familiarity with enterprise environments

Many of the customers FDEs work with are enterprises with core systems, existing data platforms, security requirements, and approval workflows. SoftBank's job posting lists "experience in large-scale system implementation for enterprises" as a preferred requirement, and Andpad lists "PM/PL experience in large-scale system implementation and integration projects, such as ERP overhauls and core system integration" as a preferred requirement (as of October 2026).

For an AI company's FDE, knowing how the customer's core systems and existing data are managed, and what approvals are required for production releases, is a major strength. People who have handled maintenance and operations, release management, and integration with core systems at an SIer possess this knowledge that web engineers typically lack.

Three gaps common among SIer alumni and how to fill them

That said, SIer experience alone leaves some gaps in FDE selection. Based on job postings and official blogs, the three commonly lacking areas are: an in-house product mindset, LLM implementation experience, and hands-on coding ability.

1. In-house product mindset: solving within constraints

SIers build systems to match customer requirements, but FDEs solve customer challenges based on their own company's product features. In an interview with Levtech LAB, Mr. Shirai of LayerX stated that FDEs "work within the constraints of their own platform, but these constraints are precisely their strength." The idea is that constraints allow individual solutions to accumulate as product assets, which then become value for the next customer. The same interview frames the difference from SES as whether the work "ends once you leave the client company," and holds that the essence of the FDE role is running a cycle where "individual optimization directly feeds into product scaling."

If you are used to "building exactly what the customer asks for" as an SIer, you may struggle to draw the line between what can be solved with product features and what requires custom development. You can fill this gap by trying out the target company's product in advance and being able to explain in your own words, "this feature can solve this business challenge in this way."

2. LLM implementation: a mandatory requirement in many postings

Many FDE job postings in Japan require experience building applications using generative AI or LLMs. SoftBank lists "experience building or implementing applications using LLMs/generative AI" as a mandatory requirement and "experience with RAG/agent design/evaluation infrastructure" as a preferred requirement. Money Forward's internal FDE role requires "experience developing solutions using generative AI, LLMs, and AI agents" and "experience in evaluation design for PoCs" (both as of October 2026).

For those who had no opportunity to work with generative AI in SIer projects, this is the biggest gap. To fill it, build your own tool—such as an internal document search tool using RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) or an agent that automates part of a business process—and be ready to explain how you measured its accuracy. Note that the job postings ask for experience not just "building" but also "evaluating."

3. Hands-on coding: can you write production-quality code yourself?

If you have long handled upstream processes or PM roles at an SIer, opportunities to write code yourself decrease. But FDEs implement things themselves. SoftBank's job posting lists "experience designing/implementing production-quality code using Python, JavaScript, etc." as a mandatory requirement, and JDSC lists "development experience as an engineer (2+ years)" as a mandatory requirement (as of October 2026).

However, the depth of technical skill required varies by posting. Andpad's FDE posting requires practical experience as "an IT solutions presales, implementation consulting, or systems engineering" professional, while on the technical side it asks for the ability to read and write code and knowledge of architecture design and API integration, without specifying years of development experience (as of October 2026). Some postings emphasize coding heavily, while others emphasize problem structuring and design, so it is important to choose where to apply based on your current position.

Different career-change routes by major, user-affiliated, and independent SIers

Even under the single label of "SIer," the experience you can accumulate differs among major SIers, user-affiliated SIers belonging to manufacturers or financial groups, and independent SIers. Destinations for moving into FDE roles fall into three categories: FDE roles at AI startups and SaaS companies, internal FDE roles at major enterprises, and newly established FDE organizations at major SIers and consultancies. How you leverage your strengths changes depending on the combination of your background and destination.

FDE at AI startups and SaaS companies

This is the FDE role at companies with their own products, such as LayerX, Sansan, JDSC, andpad, and Loglass. It is the most typical form of FDE: entering the customer's workplace, solving problems with your company's product, and feeding insights back into the product. andpad's job posting lists "DX project experience in 'non-desk worker' domains such as construction, manufacturing, and logistics" as a preferred qualification, so there are openings that favor people who worked deeply on specific industries' operations at user-company-affiliated SIers (as of October 2026).

In-house FDE at major companies

There are also internal FDE roles responsible for AI adoption within a company's own business divisions. Money Forward's "Forward Deployed Engineer (Internal)" is a role responsible for structuring business challenges through interviews and workshops with business divisions, designing and developing prototypes using generative AI, and setting evaluation metrics needed to judge production readiness; it lists "experience in business process improvement and system implementation projects at consulting firms or SIers" as a preferred qualification (as of October 2026). Only the customer changes from external to internal; the work of handling everything from business interviews to implementation closely resembles SIer work.

FDE organizations newly established by major SIers and consulting firms

Starting in 2026, consulting firms and major SIers themselves, such as Deloitte Tohmatsu and Hitachi, have begun creating FDE organizations (details below). While this allows you to move into the FDE way of working in an environment close to your current job, if the company has no product of its own, implementation will be on top of partner companies' products or AI platforms. This is the path where SIer experience is most directly applicable, but how far the FDE essence of "feeding insights back into the product" holds needs to be confirmed for each organization.

What to Change in How You Present Yourself in Your Resume

SIer resumes typically list, for each project, "system name, scale, phases handled, and technologies used." If you apply for FDE roles with this format, you will come across as "someone who built what they were told to build." FDE selection evaluates two things: what happened in the customer's business, and what you decided in ambiguous situations. Here are the specific sections you should rewrite.

Reframe Delivery Track Record as "Customer Outcomes"

A description like "Built an order management system (300 person-months; handled requirements definition through integration testing)" should be rewritten with the customer's business change as the subject, e.g., "To eliminate duplicate data entry in ordering operations, organized exception handling into 12 patterns through interviews with business departments and redesigned the approval flow; after go-live, rework in order processing decreased." Sansan's official blog states that the FDE mission is to "make the most of the existing product's capabilities, adapt it to the customer's specific operations and challenges, and accompany them until value is actually realized." You need writing that conveys experience of seeing things through to the point where value emerges.

Describe Decision-Making in Ambiguous Situations

An August 2025 article in The Pragmatic Engineer notes that OpenAI's FDE job postings seek the ability to "work in a ton of ambiguity." JDSC's job posting also describes the job as deciding "what to build" and "how far to build it" through dialogue with the customer, starting from a state where requirements are not fully defined. If, in an SIer project, you proposed a scope while requirements were undecided, or proposed an alternative approach after hearing the customer's request and had it adopted, write that as a standalone item.

Explicitly Show Code You Wrote Yourself

Simply writing "design through testing" in your responsibilities doesn't convey whether you implemented it yourself or delegated it to subcontractors. Explicitly state the parts you wrote yourself (language, features you handled, whether they run in production). If you have personal projects or internal tools using LLMs, write how you measured accuracy along with them.

Describe Experience with Post-Deployment Operations

LayerX writes on its official blog, "We don't stop at deployment. Rather, post-deployment is when the real work begins." In an SIer's maintenance and operations phase, experiences like finding improvement points from post-launch inquiries and implementing them, or changing configurations based on usage patterns, directly connect to the FDE work of "accompanying customers until adoption takes hold." Don't omit operations as a "downstream phase"; write how you improved the customer's usage situation.

What's Asked in Selection: Questions Where SIer Backgrounds Tend to Struggle

FDE selection commonly includes case interviews or role-plays simulating customer-facing situations, in addition to technical interviews. Where SIer backgrounds tend to struggle is with questions like "Why FDE instead of staying at an SIer?" and "What would you do if a customer makes a request that the product can't solve?"

The former tests whether you can explain, in your own words, the difference between SES/SIer work and FDE. In an interview with Levtech LAB, Sakamoto of Loglass and Shirai of LayerX shared the view that "FDE is closer to a business model than a role." You will sound more persuasive if you can answer by connecting why you want to be part of the cycle in which individualized work becomes a product asset and then value for the next customer, with concrete experience at an SIer (e.g., knowledge being siloed per project).

The latter is a question that examines your orientation toward in-house products. With an SIer mindset, you tend to answer "build it," but for FDE you are expected to answer by considering, in order: whether existing product features could substitute for it, whether it could be built in a form that can be fed back into the product, or whether there is an option to change the business side instead of building anything. An article in The Pragmatic Engineer lays out the difference as consultants making "one-off recommendations," whereas FDEs work with customers over the long term and contribute to the core product.

Major SIers and consulting firms themselves have begun creating FDE roles

In 2026, not only AI companies but also consulting firms and major SIers have officially announced moves to build FDE organizations. This means an option is emerging to move into an FDE way of working while remaining at an SIer. Here we cover only announcements that could be confirmed on each company's official website.

Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu: Establishes an FDE Management Office

In a press release dated April 30, 2026, Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu announced that it would fully launch the use of FDE talent. It will establish an FDE Management Office in June 2026, responsible for recruiting and developing FDE talent, designing systems, sharing knowledge, and coordinating assignments. FDEs are positioned as roles that "handle AI implementation in the field," working alongside DSs (Deployment Strategists), who analyze corporate value structures and identify priority areas for transformation, to "connect strategy and execution and support AI-driven, company-wide transformation." It states that FDEs "require a broad range of skills spanning frontend, backend, and infrastructure to design and implement AI-driven systems and data integration," and that the firm will build internal certification programs and career models to develop FDEs through both external hiring and internal development.

Hitachi: Expanding an FDE model with Google Cloud globally

In a press release dated June 9, 2026, Hitachi announced that it would expand its strategic alliance with Google Cloud and advance the social implementation of physical AI through FDEs. Hitachi defines FDEs as "a group of specialists who go directly into customers' business operations and carry out everything end to end, from identifying management issues to early value validation through PoCs and agile implementation into actual operations." Under a framework in which Hitachi consultants, AI transformation (AX) experts, AI-native software engineers at its US subsidiary GlobalLogic, and Google Cloud engineers collaborate, the knowledge gained will be fed back to Hitachi's systems engineers in Japan.

NTT Data and Fujitsu: Joining Salesforce's FDE Partner Network

On June 8, 2026, Salesforce announced the full-scale launch in Japan of its "Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) Partner Network" to accelerate production deployments of its AI agent "Agentforce." The nine initial partners in Japan are Accenture, NTT Data, Globalway, Terrasky, Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu, Dentsu Soken and Dentsu Digital, PwC Consulting, Fujitsu, and Fractea. NTT Data announced its participation on its own website on June 30, 2026, stating it will handle organizing complex data structures and permission management design, building environments that meet security and regulatory requirements, integrating systems, APIs, and data pipelines, and supporting deployment to production environments. In addition, NTT DATA's German subsidiary has posted a job opening under the title "Forward Deployed Engineer - FDE," seeking, for a role that rapidly builds customer demos, prototypes, PoCs, and MVPs, over 5 years of software/AI engineering experience and over 2 years of experience building generative AI/LLM solutions (as of October 2026).

FDEs at major SIers and consulting firms mainly implement on partner companies' platforms (such as Agentforce and Google Cloud's AI infrastructure) rather than on their own products. Since "where the knowledge gained in the field is fed back" differs by organization, be sure to confirm this when applying. In September 2026, Fujitsu announced it would strengthen its framework as a Palantir Global FDE Partner, with an FDE division within its Cross-Industry Solutions Business Headquarters. In October, it announced a case study of an AI agent deployment at MISUMI involving FDEs.

These moves carry two meanings for people from SIer backgrounds. One is that it has become possible to choose an FDE career path as an extension of their current role. The other is that major SIers and consulting firms are trying to secure FDE talent through both external hiring and internal development, which raises the market value of people with FDE experience across industries.

The order of preparation for transitioning from SIer to FDE

Let's organize what we've covered so far into the actual steps a current SIer employee should take.

  • 01 Take inventory of your current projects along two axes—how the client's operations changed and what decisions you made—and rewrite them into an FDE-oriented resume
  • 02 Build an LLM-based application yourself (RAG, agents, etc.) and go through accuracy evaluation as well. If you have the opportunity at work, propose introducing generative AI in a current project
  • 03 Based on your background (major SIer, user-company-affiliated, or independent) and your current implementation skills, decide whether to target an AI company's FDE role, an in-house FDE role, or an FDE organization at a major SIer or consulting firm
  • 04 Try out the target company's product in advance so you can explain how it could solve the challenges of clients you've worked with
  • 05 Prepare answers to "Why FDE instead of SIer?" and "How would you handle requests the product can't solve?" by connecting them to your own experience

Experience in requirements definition, client negotiations, and large-scale implementations at an SIer is clearly valued in FDE job postings. What's missing is LLM implementation and an orientation toward your own company's product—both of which can be addressed through preparation. If you're considering FDE as your next move from an SIer, start by rephrasing your experience in terms of "customer outcomes."

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q Is SIer requirements definition or PM experience valued in FDE hiring processes?

Yes, it is. SoftBank's FDE job posting lists "project management experience including client negotiations" as a required qualification, and Andpad lists "PM/PL experience in large-scale system implementation and integration projects" as a preferred qualification (as of October 2026). JDSC requires "experience organizing issues through dialogue with stakeholders and carrying them through to implementation even when requirements are unclear," so requirements definition experience serves directly as an entry point to the selection process.

Q I had few opportunities to write code at my SIer. Can I still become an FDE?

It depends on the job posting. SoftBank and JDSC list experience implementing production-quality code or 2+ years of development experience as required qualifications, while Andpad requires practical experience in "pre-sales, implementation consulting, or systems engineering" and, on the technical side, asks for code reading/writing skills and knowledge of architecture design and API integration (as of October 2026). There are two paths: choose a job posting with less emphasis on implementation, or build an implementation track record through personal LLM-based development projects before applying.

Q Will my salary increase if I move from an SIer to an FDE role?

It depends on your current salary and your destination, so there's no single answer. Among publicly available job postings in Japan, SoftBank's FDE (seconded to SB OAI Japan) lists a theoretical annual salary of ¥8.12 million to ¥20.34 million, JDSC lists ¥5 million to ¥25 million, and Money Forward's in-house FDE lists ¥7.9 million to ¥15 million per year (as of October 2026). All have wide ranges, and experience with LLM implementation and a track record in client negotiations will influence the offered amount.

Q What's the difference between an FDE and an SIer SE?

The differences lie in whether there is an in-house product and where the knowledge goes. Sansan explains on its official blog that FDEs "differ from SIers that build systems from scratch based on requirements." An FDE solves client challenges within the constraints of their company's product and feeds the knowledge gained from custom implementations back into the product. An SIer SE builds systems project by project, and knowledge stays siloed within each project.

Q Is there a path to becoming an FDE while staying at an SIer?

Yes, such paths are emerging. Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu announced in June 2026 the establishment of an FDE Management Office to develop FDE talent through both external hiring and internal development. Hitachi announced it will roll out an FDE model with Google Cloud globally, and NTT Data and Fujitsu have become initial partners in Salesforce's FDE Partner Network. However, if the company doesn't have its own product, where the knowledge gets fed back varies by organization, so this needs to be confirmed.

Q When rewriting an SIer resume for FDE applications, what should I fix first?

The project headings. Instead of "system name, scale, and phases handled," make "the client's business challenge and how it changed" the heading. Next, create a separate section for what you decided or proposed when requirements were unclear, and explicitly state the code you wrote yourself. Also describe, without omitting anything, how you improved usage through post-implementation operations.

Q For SIer alumni without hands-on generative AI experience, where should they start?

Build a document search tool using RAG or an agent that automates part of a business process yourself, and be able to explain how you measured its accuracy. Money Forward's in-house FDE role lists "experience with evaluation design in PoCs" as a required qualification (as of October 2026), so experience evaluating—not just building—is required. If you can propose introducing generative AI in a current project, it also counts as professional experience.
