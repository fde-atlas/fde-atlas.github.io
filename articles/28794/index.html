A Breakdown of the Requirements for OpenAI's Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) Role

Original title: OpenAI 前线部署工程师 FDE 岗位要求解读

FDE Times · 2026-11-07 · 3 min read

Summary

This article analyzes OpenAI's San Francisco Forward Deployed Engineer job posting. The role covers technical work across deployment projects from prototype to stable production, requires 5+ years of experience with LLM exposure, and caps travel at 50%. Notably, driving customer adoption is the engineer's own responsibility, not something handed off after delivery. The author also notes OpenAI is expanding its FDE team quickly, partly via acquisitions such as Northslope in July 2026, and advises candidates to treat the posting as a skills checklist: prove your system reached production, who uses it daily, and how much. The core question to prepare for: is anyone actually using what you built?

Key points

  • OpenAI's FDE role caps travel at 50% of time.
  • Scope runs from prototype through stable production deployment.
  • Driving customer adoption is the engineer's own duty.
  • OpenAI is scaling FDE hiring partly by acquiring Northslope.
  • Resumes should show production use, users, and usage volume.

Key Takeaways

  • OpenAI's San Francisco FDE role owns the technical work across multiple deployment projects, from the first prototype all the way to stable operation in production.
  • The role is also responsible for customer adoption, requiring 5+ years of experience, LLM-related experience, and up to 50% travel.
  • OpenAI's deployment business is rapidly expanding its FDE team, including through acquisitions such as Northslope in July 2026.

Up to 50% of your time spent traveling. OpenAI's job posting for a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) in San Francisco states this plainly as a hard requirement. That single line says a lot about the job: half your working hours may be spent in the customer's office, not your own company's.

OpenAI has also just published an article explaining what forward deployed engineering means inside the company.

If you're working as an FDE, or hoping to move into the role, this job description is worth reading closely. It reveals what a leading model company values when evaluating engineers who work directly with customers.

The code works — but the job isn't done

The posting specifies that the FDE owns the technical work across multiple deployment projects, starting from the first prototype and continuing until the project runs stably in production. What's more notable is the next sentence: the FDE must work closely with the customer's team, understand their needs, and then guide them to actually adopt the systems that have been built.

Imagine you're building an agent for a customer's insurance claims department. The demo works in week two, and the system goes to production in month three. If employees are still processing claims manually because they don't trust the agent's output, then under the adoption requirements in the posting, the work cannot be considered complete.

Driving adoption is therefore part of the engineer's own job, not someone else's after handoff. When you join a project, the first thing to clarify is who will use the system every day, and to agree upfront on what signals will show that they're genuinely using it.

Not a support engineer, and not an account manager

An article on DEV Community analyzing OpenAI's deployment model puts it bluntly: an FDE is not a support engineer, and not an account manager.

The author describes the FDE as someone bound to the customer over the long term — building hands-on while also serving as a bridge between the capabilities of the most advanced models and the realities of enterprise operations.

This is a community perspective, not OpenAI's official definition, but it's consistent with the job posting.

AI Weekly holds a similar view: the FDE is someone who works inside the customer's organization. According to the site, OpenAI's deployment business has expanded its FDE team partly through acquisitions — specifically, the July 2026 acquisition of Northslope, a company with Palantir roots.

AI Weekly argues that the speed at which OpenAI is assembling its FDE team is something legacy consulting firms simply cannot match through hiring alone.

For job seekers, this cuts both ways. The demand is real, and it's growing. But the advantage will likely go to those who have taken systems into production, not those who stopped at the demo.

Read the job posting as a skills checklist

The posting requires 5+ years of experience, plus experience working with LLMs or generative models. If you fall short on years, what you can control is the quality of your evidence. A resume line like "built a RAG pipeline in Python" says almost nothing.

Go back to the insurance claims agent above.

A rewritten resume line could be: "Took a claims inquiry agent from prototype to production; the claims department uses it daily in place of manual inquiries; tracked weekly the share of claims handled through the agent." That single sentence answers all three implicit questions in the posting: Did it reach production? Who is using it? How much?

The travel requirement also deserves serious consideration. If spending half your time on-site with customers doesn't fit your situation, raise it directly in the first-round interview — in any country, with any company hiring FDEs.

There are many possible definitions of an FDE at OpenAI. But if you read this posting as an exam question, the one you most need to prepare an answer for is: Is anyone actually using the systems you build?
