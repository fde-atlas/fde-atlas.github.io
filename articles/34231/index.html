How do you move from software engineer to forward deployed engineer?

FDE Club · 2026-11-25 · 6 min read

Summary

This FDE Club note, based on published sources, explains how software engineers move into forward deployed engineering. A Bloomberry study of 100 LinkedIn profiles found 45 percent of FDEs were software engineers first, the most common path. Sources including The Pragmatic Engineer and First Round Review agree the engineering bar stays high: Palantir required FDEs to pass the same interview loops as software engineers. What changes is the customer focus: 55 percent of postings list direct customer work first and 68 percent require travel, with onsite time varying from Palantir's living onsite to Serval's roughly 20 percent. Employers usually ask for three to five years of experience. The author warns that about 30 percent of postings are closer to sales engineering, and suggests checking for quotas, production coding, and reporting lines before accepting.

Key points

  • 45 percent of FDEs were software engineers first, per a 100-profile study.
  • The engineering bar stays high; Palantir used the same interview loops as SWE.
  • 55 percent of postings put customer work first; 68 percent require travel.
  • Most employers ask for three to five years, though Palantir hired many new grads.
  • About 30 percent of postings are closer to sales engineering; check for quotas and code.

Field notes

Most forward deployed engineers were software engineers first. In one study of 100 profiles, 45 percent held that title before. Published sources say the engineering bar stays high, and the move adds work with customers, open problems and travel. They also warn that some jobs with the title hold little engineering, so read the posting closely.

This note covers what changes when a software engineer takes a forward deployed role: what stays, what is added, how much experience employers ask for and what to check first. It is drawn from published sources only. For the role itself, start with the guide: What is a forward deployed engineer?

Is the move from software engineer common?

Yes. It is the most common way into the role, in the one published count the club has found. The number in brackets is the source in the list at the end.

Bloomberry looked at 100 LinkedIn profiles of people who hold the title and noted the job each held before [3].

Role held before Share of 100 profiles Source
Software engineer, backend or full stack 45 percent [3]
Solutions engineer or solutions architect 22 percent [3]
Data engineer or data scientist 15 percent [3]
Technical consultant 10 percent [3]
Founder or early startup employee 8 percent [3]

The same study gives its reason for the order: "It's much easier to teach an engineer how to talk to customers than to teach a salesperson how to write production code." [3] It describes the usual path as three to five years of building a technical base, and then a move into engineering work that faces the customer [3].

What stays the same after the move?

The engineering. Every source in this note treats the role as an engineering job first.

  • The Pragmatic Engineer, which interviewed people in the role at OpenAI and Ramp, writes: "Almost every recruiter seeks a solid software engineering background" [1].
  • First Round's head of talent, a former Palantir recruiter, says the company's forward deployed engineers "had to pass the same interview loops and facets as software engineers" [2].
  • First Round Review writes that the person in the role "is still very much an engineer who writes and debugs production code" [2]. The chief executive of Serval says in the same article: "The way I see an FDE is as an actual member of the software engineering team." [2]
  • A Palantir engineer quoted by The Pragmatic Engineer describes the week: "Some weeks, I spend most of my time developing and reviewing my team's code, like a typical software engineer." [1]

What an interview tests is in the note on interviews.

What changes in the daily work?

Who the work is for, where it runs and how it is judged.

At Palantir a software engineer on the core product is a "Dev" and a forward deployed engineer is a "Delta". The Pragmatic Engineer quotes Palantir's own description of the second: "They measure success in terms of impact on the customer's goal." [1] The head of the team at OpenAI describes where the code runs: "We write the code on the customer's infrastructure and use their tooling to deliver a working solution." [1]

The postings say the same in numbers. In Bloomberry's sample of about 1,000 postings, working directly with customers is the first responsibility, named in 55 percent. 68 percent of the roles require travel [3].

How much time goes to the customer differs by employer:

  • A former Palantir engineer who later led recruiting for the role there says: "Living onsite with the customer is such a core part of being an FDE." [2]
  • At Salesforce, small teams "focus full-time on one client for about three months" [5].
  • At Serval, people in the role "are expected to spend around 20% of their time with customers" [2].

Which skills does a software engineer have to add?

The ones that face the customer: finding the real problem, explaining it plainly and working without a specification.

Salesforce, which started its team in April 2025, lists five skills for the role: "Problem-solving", "Technical skills", "Communication skills", "Business acumen" and "A learning mindset" [5]. A director of the team there says: "It's very important to have those customer-facing skills" [5]. Another says: "Problem-solving is the number one skill an FDE needs to have" [5].

Bloomberry counts 47 percent of postings that explicitly require the ability to work with customers [3]. Its own judgement is blunt: "If you're a brilliant engineer who hates people, stay in core engineering." [3]

First Round Review adds the tolerance for open problems. The person who led the equivalent team at Ironclad says: "We looked for forward deployed engineers who got energy from a very open-ended prompt." [2] The former Palantir recruiting lead says: "An FDE isn't somebody who brings a playbook with them." She also says: "Forward deployed engineering is painful." [2]

At Ramp, in The Pragmatic Engineer's account, new hires "also need to be good at communicating with customers, asking clarifying questions, and scoping things out" [1].

How much experience do employers ask for?

Most often three to five years, with exceptions in both directions.

Of the postings in Bloomberry's sample that name a level, 12 percent are entry level with up to two years, 60 percent ask for three to five years, 20 percent for six to eight and 8 percent for nine or more [3].

The Pragmatic Engineer reports that Ramp "prefers 5+ years of experience for Senior FDE roles, but does hire some exceptional new grads as FDEs" [1]. Two of the signals Ramp looks for are time as a founder and time at a young startup [1]. At Palantir, in First Round Review's account, "recent college grads made up a bulk of the FDE roster" [2].

A known name on a CV is not always an advantage. First Round Review writes: "In some cases, titles and pedigree can even be an anti-signal." [2] It also tells employers that they need not look for someone who has held the title before [2].

Can you make the move inside your own company?

Sometimes. The sources give a few cases and no count.

  • Salesforce describes one of its engineers who "transitioned from a technical architect role at Salesforce" into the team. New hires to the team go through a six week programme with "technical training, field work, and a capstone project" [5].
  • At OpenAI the team was started from the inside. The head of solutions architecture made the case for it at the start of 2025. It began with two engineers and had more than 10 by August 2025 [1].
  • The move also runs the other way. After Palantir launched Foundry, "more FDEs went back to working as software engineers" [1].
  • Ramp, for now, uses "the software engineering career ladder for FDEs" [1].

What should you check before you move?

Whether the job is engineering at all. The title does not settle it.

The Pragmatic Engineer reported in March 2026 on two engineers who took the title and were disappointed. One is quoted: "The company wanted to use me more on the engagement lead side, and nothing on software development." The same developer adds: "I quit 4 weeks later." [4] The same article notes: "Some engineers figure out the nature of the role during the interview process and pass on it." [4] In a 2026 update to its first article, the author writes: "In practice, most FDEs do not seem to contribute back to platforms." [1]

Bloomberry classes about 30 percent of the postings it read as closer to sales engineering [3]. Its tests can be put as questions:

  • Is there a quota or a commission? If so, it counts the job as sales engineering, whatever the title [3].
  • Are you expected to write production code? [3]
  • Does your manager report to sales? [3]
  • "Are you still there 3 months after go-live?" [3]

Two more points come from The Pragmatic Engineer. Pay "is probably the same as or slightly lower than software engineer compensation". At a company with very few people in the role, it sees "a risk of getting “stuck” career-wise" [1].

The line between the two kinds of job is in the note on solutions engineers. The roles open in Switzerland today are on the roles list, each labelled FDE or adjacent.
