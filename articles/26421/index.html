Today's Assessment

This issue reviews two public materials: the October 20 report on industrial applications in Shandong, and the October 21 announcement of the partnership between OpenAI and Google Cloud. The former showcases production tasks and response metrics, while the latter concerns procurement and budgeting pathways. For an FDE, a delivery proposal needs to answer both "how to verify business impact" and "how to procure, meter, and operate."

Key Signals

1. Shandong refining and steel cases disclose response times and temperature hit rates

According to a conference report republished by the Hunan Provincial Department of Industry and Information Technology on October 20, China Mobile's Shandong branch and Hongrun Petrochemical built a refining-specific vertical large model that outputs parameter adjustment plans in response to changing operating conditions. The report states that the average response time for hydrocracking operations was reduced from four hours to two hours, and that the solution has been replicated to more than ten refining enterprises. The report also describes how Shandong Iron and Steel Group integrated flue gas, video, audio, and process data, with a one-click steelmaking AI agent outputting execution instructions and achieving a temperature hit rate above 95%.

This information is closer to on-site tasks than simply releasing a model, but the metric definitions, statistical periods, execution authority, exception handling, and post-replication stability still need to be verified.

Evidence boundary: The above is based on a conference report republished on a government website, not an independent acceptance report; no sample sizes, cost-benefit figures, or proof of the replicated projects were provided. Response time and temperature hit rate cannot be directly converted into overall ROI.

2. OpenAI and Google Cloud announce plans for model and Codex workflow collaboration

On October 21, OpenAI announced plans to bring its frontier models and Codex agent workflows to Google Cloud Model Garden. The announcement states that customers will be able to transact through Google Cloud Marketplace and use their existing GCP consumption commitments to pay for related purchases.

Availability boundary: The announcement says general availability is planned for the coming weeks, so it cannot be written as though all customers can purchase now. Regions, contract terms, specific product support, and actual costs still need to be verified item by item.

The implication for an FDE is that model selection also involves the customer's existing cloud environment, contracting entity, budget source, access control, usage metering, and exit path.

Evidence boundary: This is a partnership plan, not a report of customer production outcomes; no actual purchase volumes, business impact, or availability in China were disclosed.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Structure acceptance around clearly defined tasks and baselines, recording response, quality, human intervention, and cost separately.
  • For production operations, specify which outputs are suggestions only and which will be written or executed, as well as who approves and who takes over.
  • Include the contracting entity, budget, metering, and migration exit clauses in the proposal, rather than only comparing model scores.
  • Distinguish among the three stages of planned launch, already available, and customer-accepted, and avoid treating announcements as results.
