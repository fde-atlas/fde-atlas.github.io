FDE Job Postings Surge More Than Sevenfold in One Year

The article attributes the growth to AI's shift from model development to real-world deployment, since LLMs often underperform in enterprise systems. Hiring is notable for customer-simulation interviews, which eliminate about 60% of candidates who pass technical tests, showing the role demands requirements gathering and operational skills beyond coding.

finance.biggo.com reported that job postings for Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) are rapidly expanding in Silicon Valley. According to data from the job platform Indeed, FDE job postings grew from 643 in April 2025 to 5,330 in April 2026, an increase of more than 7-fold in one year. Annual salaries are said to reach approximately 21.5 million yen at the top end.

News Details

According to the report, FDEs work directly with customers, translating vague business requirements into AI systems that can withstand real-world operation. OpenAI, Anthropic, Tencent, ByteDance, and others are actively hiring, and Anthropic is said to offer annual salaries of approximately 18.8 million to 21.5 million yen (about $280,000 to $320,000).

According to a LinkedIn report, this role has grown 42-fold since 2023, far exceeding the 13-fold growth of AI engineers. The selection process consists of technical assessments plus customer interaction simulations and values-based reviews, and roughly 60% of candidates who pass the technical exam are said to be eliminated at the customer simulation stage. The Wall Street Journal described this role as "the most sought-after job in tech."

Background

It has been pointed out that while large language models are impressive in demos, they often fail to perform as expected when deployed in actual corporate systems. Against this backdrop, the focus of the AI industry is shifting from model research and development to real-world deployment and adoption. The surge in FDE job postings is seen as a movement reflecting this shift. The article also notes that the AI skills framework presented by Andrew Ng is close to the abilities required of FDEs.

What This Means for FDE Practitioners

The fact that customer interaction simulations have become a major hurdle in the selection process shows that FDEs are evaluated not only on technical skills but also on their ability to listen to and organize frontline business needs. In the process of translating vague requirements into operationally viable AI systems, both the design skills for business improvement using AI agents and LLMs, and a mindset that looks ahead to post-deployment operational adoption, are required. Overseas compensation levels and hiring trends also serve as a useful reference for practitioners in Japan to assess their own position.
