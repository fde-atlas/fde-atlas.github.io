FDE Interview Prep | What Technical, Case, and Practical Interviews Involve and How to Pass Them

Summary

Based on official hiring pages from OpenAI, Anthropic, JAPAN AI and others (as of October 2026) plus testimony from former Palantir recruiting lead Shilpa Balaji and Ironclad's James Honsa, this article maps out FDE interview processes and preparation tactics. It stresses that coding is never skipped, but FDE interviews additionally test how candidates structure ambiguous problems, ask clarifying questions, and explain to non-technical stakeholders. It offers a five-step framework for case interviews and advice on take-home and demo tasks.

Key points

  • Hiring flows vary widely: OpenAI's final round spans 4-6 hours; JAPAN AI runs 4-5 interviews plus a coding test.
  • Coding is still assessed: Anthropic uses live coding with Colab or CodeSignal.
  • Palantir's case questions ask candidates to design solutions and identify needed data and customer questions.
  • Case interviews follow five steps: restate the problem, inventory data, propose minimal scope, agree on metrics, surface risks.
  • Best preparation: build one production-quality LLM app and narrate it as problem, approach, and customer outcome.

Written and reviewed by the FDE Talent editorial team / Cites primary sources such as official job postings and official company announcements

The FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) hiring process differs significantly from typical engineer interviews in what it evaluates. In addition to coding ability, the ability to structure ambiguous situations and to face customers is tested through practical exercises. Shilpa Balaji, who led FDE hiring at Palantir, says interviewers asked candidates "how they would solve a problem the customer is working on that no one has solved yet" (First Round Review, February 2026). As of October 2026, SoftBank's job posting states that FDE interviews include practical discussions covering system design and case studies, and Firecrawl (an FDE role in San Francisco) has an interview using real customer scenarios.

Based on OpenAI and Anthropic's official hiring guides, the selection processes described in FDE job postings in Japan and abroad (as of October 2026), testimony from people involved in hiring at Palantir and Ironclad, and hands-on recruiting experience, this article covers the overall picture of FDE interviews, evaluation points and preparation strategies by interview type, and a list of expected questions.

Key Takeaways from This Article

  • The FDE hiring process varies by company. As of October 2026, public information shows OpenAI with a flow of resume screening, intro call, skills assessment, and final interview (4-6 people, 4-6 hours total), and JAPAN AI (Geniee) FDE with resume screening, a coding test, and 4-5 interviews.
  • Coding evaluation is not omitted from FDE hiring either. As of October 2026, Anthropic uses live coding with tools such as Colab and CodeSignal for technical interviews, and JAPAN AI includes a coding test in its FDE selection process.
  • According to Shilpa Balaji, who led FDE hiring at Palantir, the company's FDE interviews asked candidates to explain insider trading and then design a solution, covering what data was needed, what questions to ask the customer, and what to investigate.
  • FDE case interviews are easiest to answer completely in terms of the "data needed" and "questions for the customer" when structured in five steps: articulating the problem → taking inventory of data → proposing a minimal scope → agreeing on success metrics → surfacing risks upfront.
  • The most effective preparation for FDE interviews is to build one LLM application to production quality and be able to describe the achievement in terms of "problem → approach → customer outcome." Some companies, like Lovable, even include a stage in the process where you present something you built.

Technical interview prep: Technical evaluation is never skipped; the bonus comes from "questioning skills"

First, as a premise, technical evaluation is not omitted from FDE hiring processes either. In a First Round Review interview (February 2026), James Honsa, who led the equivalent role (legal engineering) at contract management SaaS company Ironclad, said that many of the best-performing FDEs at his company were people who could have become staff engineers at top companies, and Tiffany Siu, a former Palantir recruiter, said that early Palantir FDEs went through the same interview loop as software engineers. In publicly available hiring formats, Anthropic uses live coding with Colab or CodeSignal, and JAPAN AI conducts a coding test. OpenAI's interview guide states that in engineering interviews they look for well-designed solutions, high-quality code, optimal performance, and sufficient testing (as of October 2026). Do not expect that "technical standards will be relaxed a bit since the role also involves customer-facing work."

On the other hand, what makes an FDE stand out is system design. If a design problem is presented with insufficient information, consider "what questions you ask" itself to be part of the evaluation. The Palantir example question discussed later asks candidates to answer "what questions would you ask the customer," and JAPAN AI also states that interviews check "structuring of ambiguous problems." According to a The Pragmatic Engineer interview (August 2025), the first thing an OpenAI FDE does on a project is scoping (understanding the customer's business processes and identifying high-value areas), so asking clarifying questions in a design interview is a reproduction of this real-world practice.

  • Always confirm before starting the design: Who are the users? / Where is the data and in what form? / What is the definition of success? / What are the constraints (security, legacy integrations, budget)?
  • Explicitly articulate a phased design: "First build a minimal end-to-end, then expand"
  • In designs that use LLMs, always verbalize the separation between business rules that should be enforced by deterministic code and parts delegated to the LLM (a ZenML case study compiling interviews with the head of OpenAI's FDE program also introduces this design principle: verify must-hold constraints deterministically, and use LLMs where probabilistic judgment adds value)
  • Don't forget to mention evaluation (evals): Proactively discussing "how would we measure the accuracy of this design" makes a big difference

Are AI tools allowed in interviews?

The treatment of AI tools in interviews also varies by company. As of October 2026, OpenAI's interview guide states that there are formats that intentionally allow tools such as AI and formats that assess the ability to think without AI, and that what tools are allowed will be explained in the preparation materials. Anthropic asks candidates not to use AI such as Claude for take-home assignments and live interviews unless instructed otherwise, while encouraging the use of Claude for company research and practicing answers. In addition, Glean's FDE job posting states that the hiring process includes a short exercise or conversation to see how you think about, design with, and use AI, so some companies evaluate AI usage itself. To be sure about at which stage AI is allowed, check with the recruiter before the interview.

Case interview / role-play prep: How to approach Palantir-style "real problems"

What differs most from a typical engineering interview is the case interview. Shilpa Balaji, who joined Palantir as an FDE and later led FDE hiring, gives as an example a question where candidates are briefed on insider trading and asked to design a solution, probing what data is needed, what questions to ask the customer, and what to investigate. She said the question was designed to assess both business and technical thinking (First Round Review). These questions assess your thought process itself when facing real problems with no single correct answer. As of October 2026, job postings show that SoftBank's FDE role includes a case-study discussion in the interviews, and Firecrawl (an FDE opening in San Francisco) has an approximately 55-minute interview using real customer scenarios (see the table above).

This format has a "template" you can prepare for. In coaching practice, we recommend training with the following five steps. The "questions for the customer" and "data needed" that the Palantir example question probes correspond to steps 1 and 2. In Palantir's official blog (September 2020), an employee also said that the practice of breaking down problems and articulating an approach step by step was helpful.

  • 01 Articulating the problem: Restate the other party's words by translating them into "whose work, what process, and what loss" (e.g., "My understanding is that the compliance department relies on manual sampling to detect suspicious transactions, and the issues are the risk of missed cases and the workload. Is that correct?")
  • 02 Taking inventory of data: List the data sources relevant to the problem and confirm their existence, quality, and accessibility through questions
  • 03 Proposing a minimal high-value scope: Instead of a grand vision, propose an entry point such as "if we start here, we can deliver value in four weeks"
  • 04 Agreeing on success metrics: Tentatively quantify "what changes by how much counts as success" and confirm with the other party
  • 05 Surfacing risks proactively: Raise likely stumbling points yourself, such as data quality, exception handling, and on-the-ground acceptance

In role-plays (mock interviews), be prepared for scenarios where the counterpart is an uncooperative field worker or an executive skeptical of AI. The deciding factor is whether you can connect your message to the counterpart's business interests (workload, risk, evaluation) rather than trying to persuade with technical correctness.

Practical / demo assignment prep: Build a "working end-to-end" as fast as possible

Some companies include take-home assignments or explanations of things you have built in their selection process. OpenAI's interview guide lists take-home assignments as an example of skill assessment formats, Lovable's London FDE job posting places "show something you've built that you're proud of" at the final stage of the process, and Firecrawl's San Francisco posting includes a paid, roughly 40-hour real-work trial such as building a PoC for a simulated real customer scenario (as of October 2026). Honsa, who led a comparable role at Ironclad, also stated that the format of "pick one problem from your career and explain how you solved it with technology" was the most effective, so preparing to describe your achievements in a "problem → approach → result" structure will directly pay off.

  • What is evaluated is not polish but prioritization: get it working end-to-end first, then polish with the remaining time. Declare this order verbally as well.
  • As preparation, getting hands-on familiarity with your "standard LLM app stack" (auth, APIs, prompt management, simple Evals) makes a huge difference in your initial speed.
  • In the walkthrough, always cover three points: "why you chose this problem," "how you evaluated it," and "what you cut."

List of anticipated questions and questions to ask

  • "Tell me about a time you advanced a project with ambiguous requirements. How did you structure it?"
  • "Have you ever had a technically correct proposal rejected by a customer? How did you handle it?"
  • "How do you evaluate the quality of an LLM application? How do you detect hallucinations?"
  • "When a customer's request conflicts with your company's product direction, how do you decide?" (Hint: Ironclad's Honsa's criterion of cutting off one-off customizations that offer no incremental value as software is a useful reference.)
  • "At your first customer site after joining, what would you do in the first two weeks?" (Hint: OpenAI's scoping phase provides the skeleton of a model answer.)
  • "Tell me about a failure and what you learned from it." (Hint: Palantir's official blog notes that employees "ask about actual failures and what was learned from them, not success stories, in interviews.")

Questions to ask the interviewer are the greatest opportunity to demonstrate your understanding of the FDE role. Questions such as "Is there a mechanism for feeding FDE discoveries back into the product?", "What proportion of the work is on-site?", and "What are the evaluation metrics for FDEs (features shipped or customer outcomes)?" serve as strong evidence that you understand the essence of this role.

Summary

The essence of the FDE interview is "a simulation of the actual work after joining." Technical evaluation is not omitted, and on top of that, questioning skills, scoping ability, and the ability to explain to non-technical people are tested in practical exercises. The most effective preparation consists of three things: ① build one LLM app to production quality, ② reframe your achievements as "problem → approach → customer outcome," and ③ get comfortable verbalizing the five-step case framework. FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do I need to prepare for LeetCode for FDE interviews?

It depends on the company. As of October 2026, OpenAI, Anthropic, JAPAN AI, and others publicly state that their selection process includes coding assessments (pair programming, live coding, coding tests, etc.), so basic algorithm preparation will not go to waste. On the other hand, someone who led FDE hiring at Palantir has said that the company's FDE interviews differed from typical coding interviews, with many questions centered on real customer problems. What is commonly tested is "the ability to write to production quality," and mentioning error handling and edge cases makes a difference.

Q: I have no experience with case interviews and I'm worried. Can I substitute with prep books for consulting?

Partially effective, but FDE cases are not the "analyze a market with frameworks" type; they are the "identify the problem through questioning and cut it down to the minimal scope solvable with technology" type. The biggest difference from consulting cases is the inclusion of technical elements such as taking inventory of data and agreeing on evaluation metrics.

Q: How polished does the practical assignment need to be?

What is being evaluated is prioritization judgment rather than polish. Rather than polishing a single feature within the time limit, you will be rated higher if you first build something that works end-to-end even if rough, and can articulate "what you would do next." This corresponds to how OpenAI's FDEs prioritize "a minimal end-to-end solution" in actual work (The Pragmatic Engineer, August 2025).

Q: When and how should I bring up salary negotiation in the interview?

The timing depends on the company, but around the final interview is a good guideline. Since FDEs are involved in winning deals and expanding deployments, being able to quantify "how much and in what ways the customer will change by having me on board" is your strongest negotiation lever. For market benchmarks, see the related article "FDE Salaries." If you apply through an agent, they can handle negotiations on your behalf.

Q: Are FDE interviews conducted in English?

It varies by job posting. As of October 2026, OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting requires proficiency in both Japanese and English, states that resumes should be submitted in English, and that interviews include conversation in both languages. Meanwhile, Sakana AI's Defense-oriented FDE (Defense) states that interviews are conducted in Japanese. Check the language requirements in the job posting before applying.
