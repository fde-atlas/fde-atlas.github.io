What is an FDE

"FDE" stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, often translated into Chinese as "Forward Deployed Engineer". It means engineers go into the customer's business site and turn software and AI into systems that run in real workflows, rather than handing over a proposal or a demo and leaving.

Lewei Technology calls it, in plain language, an "AI forward deployed engineering team": the same engineering team goes from clarifying the problem and building a small prototype to on-site development and post-launch support, with acceptance at each phase; where an existing product can solve the problem, the product comes first.

What FDE engineers do every day

  • Clarify one task on the business site: who does it, how many times a week, how long each time takes, what the expected result is, and what counts as doing it right
  • For the high-risk parts, build a small prototype first using the customer's real data, and hand over the eval records
  • Integrate AI into existing systems: use APIs where stable interfaces exist, use UI automation only for legacy systems without interfaces, and fall back to manual work where neither is possible
  • Write an acceptance checklist before each phase starts, and only move to the next phase after every item passes
  • Leave deployment and operations documentation at handover; rules, configurations, and eval records can be exported

When FDE is a good fit, and when it is not

A good fit

  • There is a task done repeatedly every week, with a clear definition of what counts as doing it right
  • You need to integrate AI into your existing website, online store, ERP, or back office, not build a separate system
  • You want to replace a legacy system without shutting down operations
  • There is an owner willing to use it long term and participate in acceptance at each phase

Not a good fit

  • There is no clearly defined business problem yet
  • You demand guaranteed results upfront
  • No one will own and use it long term
  • Your needs match Chuangying or Zhigong closely: activating the product directly is cheaper, and FDE is unnecessary

How Lewei does FDE: five steps

  • 01 Clarify the problem: who does it, how many times a week, how long each time takes, what the expected result is, and what counts as doing it right. You get: a judgment on whether this task is worth doing
  • 02 Prototype validation: for the high-risk parts, build a small prototype first using your real data. You get: a prototype, eval records, a scope list, and a "not doing" list
  • 03 Proposal and quote: work is broken into small packages; third-party costs such as cloud services, SMS, and storage are listed separately. You get: a written proposal, a feature list, and a quotation
  • 04 On-site co-building: phased development with acceptance at each phase; a fixed point of contact and a written weekly report. You get: a runnable version, stage demos, and an issue list
  • 05 Launch and support: deployment, training, monitoring, and iteration; ownership and delivery scope are written into a contract appendix. You get: a production version and deployment and operations documentation

Three common objections to FDE, and how we answer them

Is FDE just "throwing people at it", starting from scratch for every customer?

That is a fair question in this industry. Our answer is: customizations we have built are distilled into modules that can be offered on their own, with the capability modules page stating who they are for and how to get them; whatever the next customer can reuse is not rebuilt from scratch.

Is FDE a way to cover up an immature product?

Lewei Chuangying and Lewei Zhigong are products we built ourselves, and their production URLs can be opened on the spot. Where a product can solve the problem, the product comes first, with no custom work.

After the investment, how is the return calculated?

We cannot, and will not, promise that. We are accountable for the concrete results accepted at each phase: what gets built and what counts as doing it right is written into the acceptance checklist before work starts and confirmed item by item.

Verifiable evidence

50 projects, 77 capability modules, and 23 systems that can be offered on their own, each stating what was built and how to get it.

Frequently asked questions

What does FDE mean?

"FDE" stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, often translated into Chinese as "Forward Deployed Engineer". It means engineers go into the customer's business site and turn software and AI into systems that run in real workflows, rather than handing over a proposal or a demo and leaving.

Does Lewei Technology do software development?

Yes. "Business system co-building" is custom software development: websites, online stores, mini-programs, apps, ERP, and back offices, advanced through four stages—requirements and prototyping, development and production, launch and delivery, and maintenance and iteration—with acceptance at each phase. Unlike software outsourcing that only hands over code, our engineers go into the business site to sort out workflows and integrate AI into specific steps of specific roles.

How is FDE different from software outsourcing?

In software outsourcing, the client usually writes the requirement documents, features are delivered per the documents, and it ends with a code handover. With FDE, engineers sort out workflows on the business site and are accountable for the concrete results accepted at each phase; at the end, documentation, rules, and eval records can be exported.

How do I choose between FDE and buying SaaS tools directly?

When your needs closely match an existing product, use the product directly—it carries the lowest risk. API integration: keep your existing systems and only add image generation, video generation, voice, or task capabilities, with controlled changes. Custom development: only go this route when workflows are unique, roles are complex, or you need to build your own platform, with scope confirmed first.

How are FDE projects priced?

No bundled packages. We clarify the scope first, then provide a written quote. Service fees and third-party hard costs such as cloud servers, SMS, storage, and model calls are listed separately; annual operation service fees are charged separately; changes are negotiated separately.

I only have a vague idea. Can I still approach FDE?

Describe the single most time-consuming task you have today in five sentences: who does it, how many times a week, how long each time takes, what the output is, and what counts as doing it right. We first judge whether it is worth doing; for the high-risk parts, we build a small prototype using your real data.
