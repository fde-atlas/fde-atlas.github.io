What is a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), and are FDE jobs for IT contractors ripe?

Contractor UK · 2026-05-18 · 10 min read

Summary

Recruitment specialist Adam Moore explains the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role and its implications for UK IT contractors. Pioneered by Palantir around 2003-04, FDEs write production-quality code inside client environments and own outcomes, addressing why enterprise AI projects fail in implementation rather than technology. FDE job postings grew over 800% between 2024 and 2025, with OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, Salesforce and Cohere building go-to-market strategies around the role. UK demand now outpaces full-time supply, so a contract market has emerged: most engagements run three to six months, are typically Outside IR35, and pay £600–£2,000 per day depending on seniority, with rates still rising as Anthropic and OpenAI push up permanent salaries.

Key points

  • FDEs are not rebranded solutions engineers; they own outcomes in client environments.
  • Palantir pioneered the model in 2003-04; the industry is now adopting it.
  • FDE job postings grew over 800% between 2024 and 2025.
  • UK contract demand outpaces full-time supply, driving contractor briefs up.
  • Rates range £600–£2,000/day, mostly Outside IR35, and keep rising.

Demystifying the role of Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) — and why it matters to contractors in 2026/27 — is my own brief for this article, exclusively for ContractorUK.

But in light of this LinkedIn post trying to define what an FDE is, let’s first clear something up before my deep dive into this environment, which I’ve specialised in as a hiring leader since October 2024.

Forward Deployed Engineer: the meaning

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is not a Solutions Engineer with a fancier job title.

It is also not a Customer Success Manager who can write a bit of code.

An FDE is a completely different breed of software professional that is changing the enterprise AI space, writes Adam Moore, managing director of Morela, an emerging technology recruitment specialist with a focus on forward-deployed engineering.

Why do companies use Forward Deployed Engineers?

The vast majority of enterprise AI projects fail to deliver meaningful value.

Not because the technology does not work; it does.

The problem is what happens between buying a platform and actually getting it working in a business. Messy data, legacy systems, and teams not operating the way anyone imagined when the contract was signed.

The hard part isn’t the technology — it’s making it work in the real world.

What is a Forward Deployed Software Engineer at Palantir?

Software giant Palantir Technologies figured out this hard part a long time ago — specifically in 2003-04 when they pioneered FDEs.

Instead of just shipping software and crossing their fingers, they embedded their engineers directly with clients (calling them the slightly wordier, Forward Deployed Software Engineers or ‘FDSEs’).

Don’t be fooled by the difference in acronym; this is the same model that is now being adopted in 2026/27 across the software industry because the alternative — leaving implementation to sales engineers and customer success teams — is visibly failing.

Are Forward Deployed Engineer jobs on the rise?

I receive phone calls related to this visible, avoidable failure every week.

So, it’s no surprise to me that Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) job postings grew by over 800% between 2024 and 2025.

Is the FDE hiring spree a fad?

OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, Salesforce, and Cohere aren’t just hiring a handful of these deeply technical people.

At the time of writing, they are building entire go-to-market strategies around them.

I’ve been watching this shift happen in real time, and I can assure ContractorUK readers that Forward Deployed Engineering is not a hiring fad.

It is a structural response to a problem that has been quietly killing enterprise AI projects for years.

What is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer, also known as an ‘FDSE’ — Forward Deployed Software Engineer) is a software professional who writes production-quality code inside a client's ‘live’ environment, often working with incomplete information and a hard deadline over their head.

The Forward Deployed Engineer owns the outcome. Not the task, the outcome.

The FDE (or FDSE if you prefer) walks into systems they’ve never seen before, navigates internal politics they were not briefed on, and stays accountable until the whole thing is genuinely working.

In the field, what does a Forward Deployed Engineer actually do?

When an FDE finds something in the field that matters, they ensure that intelligence goes back into the product roadmap.

Therefore, an FDE is very much not just a deployer, despite what their title might imply.

Rather, they are very probably the best feedback loop that a product team can have in 2026/27.

What are common paths leading to an FDE role?

The best Forward Deployed Engineers rarely come from the same background.

But they do tend to share the same way of operating — ownership, adaptability, and a focus on solving real-world problems.

In terms of established IT professionals who tend to align with the capabilities key to be a strong FDE, it’s those engineers who already gravitate toward the messiest, most ambiguous problems.

Those who got pulled into client calls because they communicated clearly under pressure. And those who insisted on understanding why something mattered, not just how to build it.

What backgrounds do Forward Deployed Engineers hail from?

Many FDEs come from ‘founder’ backgrounds, having built something from nothing and learned how to navigate uncertainty while solving practical problems for real users.

The backgrounds of other FDEs I know include the development of deep domain expertise, often by working directly with clients and understanding why something mattered, and, again, not solely what they were building it for.

I also see more people from military backgrounds than some might expect. The ability to operate under pressure, adapt quickly, and lead without authority translates directly to the Forward Deployed Engineer role.

What does it take to excel at Forward Deployment Engineering?

Exceptional FDEs who are thriving in 2026/27 tell me about both the drive they have to act and the desire they feel to solve the client’s specific problem.

Such a combination of deep and determined technical ability, bolted on to commercial instinct and operational ownership, is genuinely rare in the UK technology labour market.

And right now (on the eve of Q2 2026), the demand for the skills, attributes and capabilities that go into being a top Forward Deployed Engineer is enormous.

How easy is it for an IT/Technology worker to move into the FDE space?

Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) isn’t for everyone.

But those technologists who are well-placed to move into the FDE space will have a certain technical background, alongside a blend of ‘soft skills’ and the right mindset.

Out of these three, ‘mindset’ is perhaps the trickiest to bottle.

What’s a good mindset for a Forward Deployment Engineer?

Working as an FDE puts the software professional in situations where change is happening in real-time. Therefore, there is an expectation on the type of person to have the right mindset, typically:

  • an entrepreneurial mentality, blended with
  • a focus on understanding the mission, blended with
  • what the organisation is trying to achieve and why, blended with
  • an awareness of how/where they, as an engineer, will bring value to the organisation.

What skills to start as a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Having Data Engineering and Data Analysis experience is an excellent place to start if a career in Forward Deployment Engineering appeals.

The mix of backend capabilities and an analysis perspective means you’ll likely have been customer-facing, or worked with a complex network of stakeholders.

What’s key to becoming an FDE?

The key to FDE work is problem-solving.

Therefore, with demand for FDE support being high, we’re sometimes seeing companies utilising technical architectsand consultants, giving such individuals excellent opportunities to break into the FDE space.

Why are technical architects/consultants hired for FDE roles?

The mix of troubleshooting, critical thinking skills, and confidence in finding solutions in real-time in front of customers gives technical architects and consultants the foundations for success in Forward Deployed Engineering.

But if you’ve been a technical architect/consultant as a contractor, the FDE market hasn’t always been open to you on your preferred contract basis.

Why a Forward Deployed Software Engineer contract is still a relatively novel thing

Until recently, Forward Deployed Software Engineers were almost exclusively permanent hires.

Companies wanted to build something proprietary, keep the institutional knowledge in-house, and lock in the people who understood how they operated.

Contracting happened only at the edges of the FDSE space.

But that has changed significantly of late.

Why the FDE contract market has taken off

Demand in the UK at almost this halfway point of 2026 is outpacing the supply of full-time FDE job candidates.

So if a business needs FDE capability right now, to rescue a stalled deployment, to accelerate a go-live, or just to prove the model works before they commit to headcount, they very often cannot wait three-to-six months for a permanent hire to clear.

The contract market has stepped in to fill that gap.

And as emerging technology recruitment specialists, we are seeing more non-permanent briefs for FDEs come through every month.

What’s another key benefit of an FDE contractor vs. a full-timer?

There is also a structural argument for engaging contractors as Forward Deployed Engineers.

FDE engagements are often project-shaped by nature — a defined problem, a period, and a clear outcome.

That’s essentially why IT contractors exist in the first place!

But be aware, many of the best FDEs in 2026/27 have now recognised this reality, and so are often choosing to operate independently, rather than commit full-time to a single employer.

How is a Forward Deployed Engineer salary best described in 2026/27?

With Claude creator Anthropic opening their first UK office, and as OpenAI continues to expand its London base, Forward Deployed Engineer salaries are only going in one direction.

Many UK scale-ups, plus so-called Series A and B businesses, simply cannot compete with these exceptionally highannual salaries that FDEs command.

A contractor engagement gives such nascent firms the capability they need without the long-term commitment. And in some cases, when the full cost of a permanent FDE hire and salary is totted up, an FDE contractor hire can actually be the more cost-effective option.

Given the UK’s current financial climate and the state of the IT contractor jobs market, this is an increasingly attractive proposition for both sides.

What’s the final benefit of an FDE consultant over a full-timer?

The final driver towards Forward Deployed Engineers on a consultant or contractor basis is risk management.

For organisations that have not yet committed to building an FDE function, a contract engagement is a low-risk way to test the value before making permanent headcount decisions. We are seeing more and more of these ‘discovery-style’ FDE engagements, especially among enterprise SaaS businesses moving into AI deployment for the first time.

How are FDE contractor pay rates as of Q2 2026?

FDE contracting rates vary depending on experience, the complexity of the engagement, and IR35 status.

What does not change is that this is a premium segment. These are not standard software engineering contract roles, and the rates for Forward Deployment Engineers reflect that.

Therefore, and as previously mentioned, a growing number of experienced FDEs, particularly at the senior end, are actively choosing contract placements over permanent positions.

Why are FDEs going freelance?

Motivations will vary with the individual, but there are three main reasons why full-time FDEs are making the move to freelance.

  • the rate premium is significant,
  • the variety of work is better, and
  • the best people know their worth and know they are in extremely high demand.

How much per day does a Forward Deployed Engineer Contractor make?

Rates for Mid-level FDSE: (3-5 years, some client-facing experience): £600 - £750 per day, Outside IR35.

Rates for Senior FDSE (5-8 years, proven FDE / Palantir background): £750 - £1,200 per day, Outside IR35.

Rates for Principal FDSE (8 years+ experience, built or led a Forward Deployed Software Engineering function): £1,200-£2,000 per day, Outside IR35.

How are Forward Deployed Engineer earnings on the rise in 2026?

As ContractorUK readers will no doubt see, the current market rate for an FDE is very much in an appealing direction— and more than that actually, the pay ceiling is even rising.

Contract candidates for temporary Forward Deployed Engineering assignments who would have accepted £700 a day about 18 months ago are now benchmarking at £850 or more.

With Anthropic and OpenAI indirectly driving permanent salaries upward in the UK market, we don’t anticipate that this steep, upward, FDE pay trajectory will slow down anytime soon.

What IR35 status for Forward Deployed Engineer roles?

IR35 is also interesting around FDE roles.

Inside IR35 engagements exist, of course, but are less common for Forward Deployed Engineer roles.

The autonomous, outcome-driven nature of FDE work typically supports an Outside IR35 determination.

But as ever with IR35, do not assume!

Assess each engagement thoroughly and review the role on its own specifics.

How is the demand for Forward Deployed Engineers faring in 2026/27?

The volume of FDE contract briefs coming through our staffing business has increased sharply since the start of 2025, and that trajectory has continued into 2026.

So, it’s no surprise to us that Forward Deployed Engineers were last week hailed as “one of the most in-demand jobs in tech, and one of the most important functions for AI rollouts.”

This is according to Aaron Levie, CEO of enterprise and cloud company BOX.

Levie posted on May 13th 2026, here on X.

What are four Forward Deployed Software Engineering contractor hiring trends?

But for contractors seeking to exploit the FDE hiring spree, four trends are worth noting:

  • Engagements requiring FDE skills are typically three to six months, but with strong extension prospects where the initial deployment goes well
  • Palantir alumni remain a highly desirable placeable profile, with businesses recognising the brand as a genuine signal of FDE experience, and many being willing to pay for it
  • Ex-founders who have taken a product from nothing to something are also desirable
  • The most active buyers of FDSE skills are enterprise SaaS companies and AI-native businesses, scaling their customer deployment functions faster than their permanent headcount allows.

Forward Deployed Engineers: the outlook for 2026 Q2

In a potential sign of the sheer buoyancy in the Forward Deployed Software Engineering space, since identifying the above four, a fifth pattern has emerged.

In fact, we’re now also seeing growing demand for IT contractors who could combine FDE delivery with their own specific sector specialism or experience, notably healthcare data, financial services infrastructure, and government systems. It really is tricky to see 2026 as anything but the year that Forward Deployed Engineering is truly hotting up.
