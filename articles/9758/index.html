Frontier Deployed Engineer: what Anthropic's new Academy tests, and how to prepare without a nomination

A10X · 2026-10-03 · 5 min read

Summary

On 2 October 2026 Anthropic announced Claude Frontier Academy, a $100 million program to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by end of 2027. Entry is nomination-only, with first cohorts from Accenture, Deloitte, McKinsey and five other organizations. Phase one is an in-person simulated enterprise deployment with a graded practical; phase two is a 12-week residency leading a real Claude use case, earning the FDE badge. The author argues this sets a public bar for FDE interviews and offers self-preparation paths for engineers without nominations.

Key points

  • Anthropic committed $100M to train 10,000 FDEs by end of 2027.
  • Participation is by nomination only; first cohorts come from eight organizations.
  • Training combines a graded simulated deployment with a 12-week residency.
  • The practical tests use case selection, discovery, evals, security review, and handover.
  • Unnominated engineers can prepare via unseen cases and leading a real deployment at work.

On 2 October 2026 Anthropic announced Claude Frontier Academy, a $100 million commitment to "train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers (FDEs) by the end of 2027." It is the clearest sign yet that the forward deployed engineer is a real job with a real bar.

There is one catch. You cannot sign up.

A10X is not affiliated with Anthropic. Everything I say about the Academy below is quoted from their announcement. Everything else is my own read, as someone who does this job.

What the Academy is

  • Who gets in: "Participation is by nomination." An organization asks its Anthropic account team or partner manager whether it is eligible.
  • Who it is for: "hands-on software engineers with strong fundamentals, a record of building with LLMs, experience helping others adopt AI." Nominees arrive "with a named Claude project to lead when they return."
  • First cohorts: Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk.
  • Phase one: a multi-day in-person program where engineers "work through a simulated enterprise deployment, from the right use case through security review to handover, and finish with a graded practical on a new scenario." Passing earns the Claude Resident Engineer badge.
  • Phase two: a 12-week residency to "lead a real Claude use case at their own organization," then a second assessment for the Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer badge. Anthropic expects the first of those in early 2027.

Anthropic compares it to how doctors are trained: learn from practitioners, train on real cases, get assessed before you practice alone.

Frontier Deployed Engineer or Forward Deployed Engineer?

Same job, two names, same abbreviation. Forward Deployed Engineer is the title on job boards today: OpenAI, Palantir, Salesforce, Wonderful and dozens of other companies post it. Frontier Deployed Engineer is Anthropic's name for the engineer its Academy certifies. If you are looking for roles, search both titles.

What the graded practical tests

Anthropic does not publish its rubric. But the stages it names are the stages of every real deployment, and each one is where candidates lose FDE interviews:

  • The right use case. Cutting a first workflow that can reach production, and saying out loud what is out of scope. Most candidates try to automate everything at once.
  • Discovery. You only learn what you ask. The customer will not volunteer the constraint that breaks your design.
  • An architecture you can defend. Narrow tools, failure points you can name, orchestration you own.
  • Evals. Catching the failure that matters, not the one that is easy to measure.
  • Security review. What data the agent can touch, who can see what, and what gets logged.
  • Rollout and handover. A shadow run, a kill switch, who signs off, and a team that can run it after you leave.

"A graded practical on a new scenario" means you cannot memorize answers. You need reps on cases you have not seen.

What it changes

  • More trained FDEs by 2027, mostly inside consulting firms and large companies.
  • A public reference for the bar. Until now, "FDE interview" meant something different at every company. A graded practical on a new scenario is now the standard a major lab says it uses. My guess is more hiring loops will start to look like it.
  • The job board does not change. The roles open today are posted by AI companies hiring now, and none of them can wait for 2027. See the live board.

FAQ

What is a Frontier Deployed Engineer?

It is Anthropic's name for the engineer certified by its Claude Frontier Academy, announced on 2 October 2026. The job is the same as a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE): an engineer who works inside a customer's organization to take an AI use case to production, from choosing the use case through security review to handover.

How do I join Claude Frontier Academy?

You cannot apply as an individual. Anthropic says participation is by nomination: an organization asks its Anthropic account team or partner manager whether it is eligible, and nominates engineers who arrive with a named project to lead. The first cohorts come from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk.

Is a Frontier Deployed Engineer the same as a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Yes, it is the same job under a different name, and both are abbreviated FDE. Forward Deployed Engineer is the title most companies use on job postings today, so search for both.

How can I prepare for an FDE role without a nomination?

Practice on deployment cases you have not seen, get graded on them, and lead one real AI deployment at your current job. A10X has a free rapid round and a free 60-minute case at a10x.dev/arena, and a free offline case kit on GitHub. A10X is not affiliated with Anthropic.
