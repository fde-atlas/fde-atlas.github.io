Switch to Forward Deployed Engineer: Skills Gap Planner

FDE Times · 2026-11-14 · 15 min read

Summary

This piece is a skills-gap planner for engineers switching into Forward Deployed Engineering, based on 838 live FDE postings from 169 companies (updated 14 Nov 2026). Its central finding: 84% of postings ask for customer-facing work, more than any technical skill, followed by Python (59%) and APIs/system integration (57%). For eight starting roles—backend, full-stack, data, ML, DevOps, solutions/sales engineer, consultant, new graduate—it lists transferable skills, ranked gaps, and an eight-week preparation plan with Week 0 fundamentals. It stresses this is preparation, not a hiring timeline: without deployment experience, 12–18 months of such work is the realistic path. Palantir (95) and Databricks (94) account for 23% of postings, and only 54 roles (6%) are early-career.

Key points

  • Based on 838 live FDE postings from 169 companies.
  • 84% of postings require customer-facing work, the top demand.
  • Python (59%) and APIs/integration (57%) lead technical asks.
  • Eight starting roles each get transferable skills and ranked gaps.
  • Only 6% of roles are early-career; 12–18 months deployment experience advised.

Your first 8 weeks of preparation

Eight weeks is preparation, not a hiring timeline. For most engineers in Vietnam without deployment experience, our analysis of new-grad FDE hiring finds 12–18 months of deployment work is the more realistic path into an FDE role.

  • Week 1

    Customer-facing work and communication

    Interview three people about a workflow they dislike and write up the real problem, not the feature they asked for.
  • Week 2

    LLMs, agents and RAG

    Build an agent loop with tool calls over your own data, without a framework, and log every step.
  • Week 3

    TypeScript, JavaScript and front end

    Build a one-page React and TypeScript demo on a public API, with loading and error states.
  • Week 4

    Machine learning

    Train a baseline model on tabular data and write down when you would not use deep learning.
  • Week 5

    Security and compliance

    Fill in a security questionnaire for your own project: data flows, personal data, access control.
  • Week 6

    Ship a portfolio project

    Ship one project end to end with a README, an eval set and a short write-up of the decisions you made.
  • Week 7

    Practise the interview

    Practise the decomposition round out loud: clarify the goal for the first 5–15 minutes before proposing a solution.
  • Week 8

    Apply

    Rewrite your CV around measured results, then apply to the open roles that fit below.
  • Week 0

    Python

    Write a small command-line tool that pulls data from an API, cleans it and loads it into a database, with tests.
  • Week 0

    APIs and system integration

    Connect two APIs end to end with retries, idempotency keys and a webhook receiver.
  • Week 0

    Distributed systems and system design

    Load-test your service, find the bottleneck and write the numbers down.
  • Week 0

    Cloud (AWS, GCP, Azure)

    Deploy one small service to a cloud account with least-privilege access, then list what would change inside a customer's VPC.
  • Week 0

    Java, Go, C++ or another systems language

    Read an unfamiliar Java or Go codebase and trace one request from end to end.
  • Week 0

    Data pipelines and ETL

    Build an incremental pipeline that can rerun safely after a failure without duplicating rows.
  • Week 0

    Evals and AI quality measurement

    Write a 50-case eval set for your LLM project and run it on every change.
  • Week 0

    SQL and relational databases

    Take a real dataset, write the five questions a customer asks on day one as SQL, and check the row counts of every join.
  • Week 0

    Docker, Kubernetes and infrastructure as code

    Put your project in a container, run it on a local Kubernetes cluster and break it on purpose to practise debugging.
  • Week 0

    Make a demo production-ready

    Take a demo and add error handling, tests, monitoring and a runbook.
  • Week 0

    Practise discovery

    Sit with a user and map how the work is done today before proposing anything.
  • Week 0

    Write for the customer

    Write the design doc and the weekly status report a customer would read.

Frequently asked questions

Can a backend engineer become an FDE?

Yes. Backend skills are what most postings ask for first: 59% of the 838 open FDE roles ask for Python, 57% for APIs and integration and 42% for distributed systems or system design. What backend jobs rarely train is the customer side: 84% ask for customer-facing work. Our analysis of the switch finds many software engineers lack direct customer experience, uncovering undocumented workflows and taking LLMs to production.

Do I need customer-facing experience?

For most roles, yes: 84% of open FDE postings ask for customer-facing work, stakeholder management or communication with executives, more than any technical skill. Our analysis notes that Anthropic's posting asks for 4+ years in a technical, customer-facing role, and suggests building that experience in your current job first.

How long does it take to switch into an FDE role?

It depends on the gap. The planner on this page gives eight weeks to work through your five biggest skill gaps, ship one project and practise the interview; that is preparation, not a hiring timeline. For engineers without deployment experience, our analysis of new-grad FDE hiring says the more realistic path for most engineers in Vietnam is 12–18 months of deployment experience before moving into an FDE role. Only 54 of 838 open roles are early-career by title.

Do FDEs travel?

Often. 45% of open FDE postings mention travel or on-site work with customers, rising to 59% of senior roles. Our analysis of travel requirements puts the usual range at 25–50% of time at the customer, and our look at remote FDE jobs found even remote-friendly Cohere asking for 20–40% travel. Ask for the actual figure before accepting an offer.

Is FDE a step down from software engineering?

Not on the evidence we have. US FDE postings that publish a range have a median base salary of $200k (168 posts, see FDE salary data); we do not collect software engineer pay, so we cannot compare the two directly. The role has its own ladder: our salary ladder analysis found an OpenAI FDE manager tier asking for 8+ years of experience, and our guide to career paths lists principal FDE, product manager, engineering manager and founder as routes out. What changes is how you are judged: on one customer's outcome rather than on a component you own.

What do FDE interviews test?

According to the course our guide to the decomposition round draws on, the loop has four rounds: decomposition, customer simulation, technical and behavioural. The decomposition round is scored on structured decomposition, empathy for the end user, reasoning about data and judgement on scope; the most common mistake is jumping straight to a solution. See the FDE interview, round by round.

Do I need AI or LLM experience to become an FDE?

Increasingly: 57% of open FDE postings ask for LLMs, agents or RAG and 21% mention evals or evaluation, while 40% ask for machine learning more broadly. If you have none, the plan above puts LLMs and evals in your first weeks when they are among your biggest gaps, starting from LLM basics.

Which skills should I learn first?

The ones most open roles ask for that you lack. Across all 838 postings the most requested are customer-facing work and communication (84%), python (59%), APIs and system integration (57%), LLMs, agents and RAG (57%), typeScript, JavaScript and front end (45%). Tick what you already have in the planner and it orders the rest by demand at your level.

How this works

Data. 838 open Forward Deployed Engineer roles at 169 companies, read from official careers pages (Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, Workday, and the career sites of Amazon, Microsoft, ByteDance and Tencent) and refreshed several times a day; the page rebuilds when the roles change.

Skills. A fixed list of 15 skills, each matched by one fixed pattern in the requirements part of the posting. Benefits, pay, "about us" and equal-opportunity sections are dropped first, and so is the company's own name, so a company describing itself or its product does not count as a skill it asks for. A posting either asks for a skill or not; how strongly it asks is not measured.

Seniority. Read from the job title: intern, graduate, junior and associate are early career; senior, staff, principal, lead and manager are senior; the rest are mid level. With 5+ years you are matched to senior and mid-level roles.

Readiness. For every open role at your level, we take the skills it asks for and the share of them you ticked, then average across those roles. 100 means you cover every listed skill of every role at your level; it does not measure how good you are at them. Roles in which we can read no skill are left out.

What it cannot see. Domain knowledge, security clearance, languages and degree requirements are not in the list. A posting that says "Python" in a list of examples counts the same as one that requires it.
