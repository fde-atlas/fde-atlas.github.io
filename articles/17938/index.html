SaaS Cancellation and Migration in Practice | Renewal Notice Deadlines, Data Extraction, and Parallel Operation

Original title: SaaS解約と移行の実務｜更新通知の期限、データ持ち出し、並行稼働

TIMEWELL · 2026-09-29 · 22 min read

Summary

Ryuta Hamamoto of TIMEWELL walks through the practical steps of canceling a SaaS contract and rebuilding in-house, arguing the first obstacle is dates, not technology. Annual auto-renewal clauses require notice before the renewal date (30 days in his example contract), and reducing seat counts at renewal can reset discounted pricing. He recommends test-exporting full production data before deciding, narrowing migration scope, running two rehearsals, and completing parallel operation within the already-paid final contract term against pre-agreed quantitative criteria. The article includes a backward-planned schedule from the renewal date and describes how the company's FDE service supports export verification, data conversion, and parallel-run comparison.

Key points

  • Cancellation deadlines fall before the renewal date; missing them costs another year
  • Reducing license counts at renewal can strip discounts and reprice at list rates
  • Test full-data exports before deciding, checking attachments, history, and permissions
  • Narrow migration scope and plan two rehearsals, expecting late-stage issues
  • Finish parallel runs inside the paid final term using pre-agreed criteria

Hello, I am Ryuta Hamamoto of TIMEWELL Corporation.

After deciding to cancel a SaaS subscription and rebuild it in-house, the first obstacle you encounter is not a technical one. It is the calendar. SaaS contracts that run on a yearly basis often include an auto-renewal clause. To stop the service, you must give notice by a specified date before the renewal date. If you miss that deadline by even one day, you will pay another full year for a service you no longer intend to use.

Not many companies are experienced at switching vendors. According to a report published by the Japan Fair Trade Commission in 2022, of the 548 businesses with revenue of 5 billion yen or more that use IaaS or PaaS, only 15.7% had switched cloud providers in the past ten years. And when asked whether they would switch to another service or on-premises if the service they were using raised prices by 5-10%, only 14.1% of respondents (excluding "don't know") said they would 1. While these figures are not limited to SaaS, it is safe to assume that few people within a company have hands-on experience with the process of cancellation and migration.

This article is the fourth in a five-part series on canceling expensive SaaS and bringing it in-house. In the first article, on decision criteria, I covered how to decide whether to cancel at all, and in the third article, on a five-year total cost estimate, I compared the total cost of building versus continuing to use the service. Here, I walk through the steps to take once you have decided to proceed, in this order: notice deadlines, data extraction, data migration, parallel operation, and post-termination deletion verification, and I close with a schedule table calculated backward from the renewal date.

One caveat up front. The discussion of contract clauses and laws in this article is general in nature. Contract terms differ by provider and by contract. For any specific contract, always check your own agreement and consult your legal department.

The cancellation notice deadline is determined by "30 days before the renewal date"

Let us take one example from the master agreement published by a major SaaS provider 2. It states that, unless otherwise specified in the order form, the subscription renews automatically every year, and that it will not renew only if either party gives written notice (email is acceptable) at least 30 days before the end of the contract term. The practical deadline for cancellation is therefore not the renewal date itself, but 30 days before it. If the renewal date is April 1, the deadline falls in early March.

The same contract contains two more clauses that are easy to overlook. The first concerns fees. Fees are based on the number of subscriptions purchased, not on actual usage; fees already paid are non-refundable; and the quantity purchased cannot be reduced mid-term. Even if you have unused accounts, you must keep paying for them until the end of the term.

The second is the latter half of the renewal clause. For renewals where the quantity is reduced or the term is shortened compared with the previous period, prices are reset at renewal, regardless of the unit price of the previous period. Even if you signed up at a promotional or one-time price, renewal will be at the then-current list price.

Putting these two together, you can see why a phased reduction plan—"let's keep just a few licenses for read-only access before canceling everything"—does not turn out as cheap as expected. The moment you reduce the quantity, the discounts you had may fall away. Should you scale down and keep using it, or cancel entirely? You should get a renewal quote first and then compare.

Along with the deadline, you should verify the method and recipient of the notice. Is written notice required, or is email sufficient, or do you process it through the admin console? Where must contractual notices be sent? If you purchased through a reseller, the notice may need to go to the reseller. Whichever method you use, keep a record that allows you to later prove the notice reached the other party.

One more point: the contract document you have on hand may not be the latest version. For SaaS governed by terms of service, the provider may have revised those terms. Article 548-4 of the Civil Code provides that, for standard form contracts (uniform clauses prepared by a business for transactions with an unspecified large number of counterparties), the content of the contract may be changed without individual consent if the change is in the general interest of the counterparty, or if it is reasonable in light of the necessity of the change and the fairness of the changed content, among other factors. In exchange, the business must specify when the change takes effect and publicize the change and its content on the internet or otherwise 3. Whether an enterprise SaaS's terms qualify as a standard form contract is a case-by-case judgment, but either way, what you should read is not the version you signed years ago, but the currently effective version and the revision history.

Try extracting your data once before deciding to cancel

After the deadline, the next thing that matters is getting your data out. IPA's (Information-technology Promotion Agency) "Guide to the Safe Use of Cloud Services for Small and Medium Enterprises" (June 2026) lists "secure your data upon termination of use" as check item number 13. The items it says to verify include return or download of all data to a PC, data compatibility and portability, complete erasure of residual data, and assurance that other users cannot reuse it 4.

In the master agreement example above, it says that if you request within 30 days of the contract end date, you will be able to export or download your customer data. After 30 days, the provider no longer has an obligation to retain your data, and it will be deleted thereafter 2. Thirty days looks like enough. But if you attempt the export for the first time after termination and discover that attachments are missing or change histories were dropped, it is already too late to keep running your operations on the original system.

That is why you should test the export once, on the full production dataset, before deciding to cancel. The minimum things I want to verify are the following five.

What to verifyWhat goes wrong if you overlook it
Does the exported record count match the count shown on screen?Even if only part of the data is exported, you will not notice what is missing
Are attachments included in the export?Only the body text migrates, and receipts and drawings are left behind in the original system
Are change histories, comments, and approval records exported?When an audit or inquiry comes, you will be unable to trace what happened
Are user, organization, and permission data exported?You will have to rebuild who can see what from scratch in the new system
Are character encodings, date formats, and field meanings documented?On import, you will run into garbled text and shifted dates

This table is not copied from an official guide; it is my own list of items I believe should be verified.

It is not uncommon to hear about struggles at the export stage. A report by the Fair Trade Commission includes a comment from users stating, "Groupware makes it difficult to take data out, and once you start using it, switching services is not easy, so we sometimes had no choice but to accept price increases." In a survey of IaaS and PaaS users as well, some cited the cost of extracting data and the fact that data formats differ between old and new services, requiring reprocessing, as factors that make switching difficult 1.

National policy also asks you to verify this point at the entry stage. The Digital Agency's standard guideline DS-310 stipulates avoiding vendor lock-in by choosing cloud services that meet conditions such as guaranteed data portability and a published, reasonable pricing structure 5. This is something you should check before signing a contract. That said, for those considering cancellation now, the only option is to check from here on.

For companies based in the EU that contract for SaaS there, there is one more right available. The EU Data Act has applied since September 12, 2025, and requires PaaS and SaaS providers to provide open interfaces and to allow data to be exported in at least commonly used machine-readable formats. From January 12, 2027, switching charges, including data egress fees, can no longer be billed 6. According to commentary from law firms, contracts are also required to include clauses allowing customers to begin switching with up to two months' notice and to complete the transition within up to 30 days after the notice period ends 7. However, this applies to services provided to customers within the EU, and it does not necessarily apply directly to contracts signed by a Japanese headquarters. Please have your legal team confirm whether it applies.

Narrow the data to be migrated, and plan for rework at the end

How do you get the exported data into the new system? Here, IPA's "User Guide for Successful System Rebuilding, 2nd Edition" is a helpful reference. Although it is not a document that directly addresses migration from SaaS, what it says about data migration planning applies as is 8.

The guide first states that if consideration of the data migration plan is delayed, it can have a major impact on the overall project plan and costs. This is because there are many points to verify in advance, such as migration targets and layouts, and data cleansing (correcting invalid values and duplicates) is also required, so preparation takes time. It then lists six areas where problems tend to occur: organizing migration targets, confirming layouts, data cleansing, mapping character encodings, selecting the migration method, and verifying migration results.

What I believe is most effective is the first one, "organizing migration targets." The guide says it is better to narrow the scope, since migrating everything increases workload, time, and cost, and gives as an example clearly setting a migration scope policy, such as discarding data and transaction data older than three years of operation. When you have used a SaaS for a long time, years of history have accumulated. You do not use all of it daily in the new system. Migrate only the most recent few years and the cases still active, and keep the older data as exported files in a read-only storage location. This alone greatly reduces both conversion and reconciliation effort. However, for records whose retention periods are set by law or internal regulations, you need to confirm that anything placed in storage can be found and retrieved at any time.

The guide also states, "Since problems from data migration surface late in development, a resolution period should be built into the plan." This is because only when real data flows through do unexpected values and usage patterns of fields not visible on screens come to light. Do not assume one migration rehearsal is enough; schedule with the assumption of at least two runs.

The difficulty of migration also shows up in statistics. In IPA's "DX Trends 2025," when 334 Japanese companies pursuing in-house development were asked about its challenges, "securing and training talent is difficult" stood out at 82.3%, and "migrating externally developed systems to in-house development is difficult" was also cited by 19.5% 9. Migration is a process within in-housing that consumes a lot of labor and experience. If it is hard to handle internally alone, I think bringing in outside help for this process alone is a reasonable decision.

Finish parallel operation within the final contract period you have already paid for

Once the new system is running, for a while you run the same operations on both the old SaaS and the new system and compare the results. This is parallel operation. IPA's rebuilding guide lists parallel operation as an example of post-release risk countermeasures, describing it as "performing identical operations on both the current and new systems for a set period to uncover defects." Two points of caution are noted: since the burden on user departments increases, verify how much parallel operation they can tolerate; and verify that it can be completed within the current system's EOL (end of life) or EOS (end of support) deadlines 8.

With SaaS, this "current system deadline" is the contract end date itself. From this comes the approach I recommend: run parallel operation within the final contract period you have already paid for, and reach a conclusion before the cancellation notice deadline. If, as in the contract example above, fees are based on the number of seats purchased rather than usage, with no refunds, continuing to use the old SaaS during that period incurs no additional cost. Conversely, if the notice deadline arrives mid-parallel-operation, you face a choice between two options: notify cancellation without seeing how the new system turned out, or renew for another year just to be safe. Both are choices you want to avoid.

I could not find an official guideline on how long it should be done. In my view, the minimum is to run through one full cycle of the business. If a process has a month-end closing, run one closing; if there is quarterly aggregation, run one aggregation, on both old and new. It is not rare for daily input to work correctly while only the closing process produces different results.

Before the duration, what you should decide in advance is the stopping criteria. The rebuilding guide points out that if test completion criteria remain vague, completion cannot be judged and the process can drag on indefinitely, and that this is especially likely in tests comparing old and new results. It therefore says you should set quantitative criteria demonstrating that the business can continue, and agree on them with stakeholders before starting 8. For example, write down criteria such as "closing amounts match between old and new," "counts for the same period match," and "when discrepancies appear, the cause can be explained" before starting parallel operation. For user departments as well, being able to see when the double entry will end gives them a reason to take on the burden.

Appendix: Cancellation and migration schedule working backward from the renewal date

Here is everything discussed so far, organized into a schedule working backward from the renewal date. This example assumes a notice deadline of "30 days before the renewal date"; the length of each step varies with the volume of work and the contract. If your contract has a different notice deadline, shift the dates in the table accordingly.

Timing (relative to the renewal date)What to doMain owners
6 months beforeCollect the contract, order forms, and the currently effective terms of service. Confirm the renewal date, notice deadline, notice method and recipient, and data return clauses. Inventory the data and sort it into what to migrate and what to keep only as an archiveProcurement, Legal, IT
5 months beforeTest export of all production data and verify counts, attachments, history, and permissions. Begin prototyping the migration destinationIT, Development
3 months beforeFirst migration rehearsal and correction of inconsistent data. Decide the completion criteria for parallel operation and agree on them with the user departmentsIT, user departments
75 to 40 days beforeParallel operation (covering one full cycle of business processes such as month-end closing). Finalize the import procedure in the second rehearsalUser departments, IT
40 days beforeJudgment against the completion criteria and internal decision on whether to cancel. Compare with quotes for renewing at a reduced scaleManagement, IT, Procurement
Until a few days before the notice deadline (30 days before)Send the notice by the specified method and record that it was receivedProcurement, Legal
Until the day before the renewal dateFinal export and import, and switching users overIT
Within 30 days after termination (in the example contract)Final check for anything missed in the export. Work on the assumption that once this period passes, the export cannot be redoneIT
After terminationConfirm deletion of residual data. Stop integrations, delete accounts and authentication settings, and confirm billing has stoppedIT, Accounting

Looking at this table, some of you may find you have less than six months until the next renewal date. In that case, rather than forcing a termination this term, it will often be cheaper in the end to target the next renewal date and start with the export test. If you cut parallel operation to meet this term's deadline, the burden falls on the user departments after the switchover.

The last two rows of the table are tasks that are easily forgotten. IPA's guide includes complete erasure of residual data and assurance that it cannot be reused by another user among the checks at the end of use, because data does not necessarily disappear naturally when you cancel 4. Some providers, as in the example contract, specify erasure after a grace period, but how users can verify when the data was deleted varies by contract. Can they provide proof of deletion? If not, how can you verify it? These are questions worth asking before sending the notice.

Information security standards are also starting to look at this process. JIS Q 27001:2023 (ISO/IEC 27001:2022), the certification standard for ISMS (Information Security Management System), newly adds "8.10 Information deletion" to the controls in Annex A 10. For companies operating an ISMS, canceling a SaaS is also an occasion to keep records for this control. At the same time, stop the single sign-on settings, API keys, and automated integrations from other systems that were connected to the old SaaS. If integrations remain, other systems will keep trying to send data to a service that should have been canceled, producing errors that are hard to diagnose.

What our FDEs can do

We offer an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) service. An FDE is an engineer who works on-site at the client and turns problems into working software. We believe that SaaS cancellation and migration is a process where the FDE way of working applies directly.

Specifically, we work with you through the technical steps in the table above, from the export test to the final import. The decision on whether to cancel and the notice to the provider are made by your company. We build a mechanism to export all production data and verify counts and attachments, run a prototype of the migration destination using data with personal information masked, write the conversion and cleansing processes, and, during parallel operation, set up a mechanism that automatically compares old and new results. Deciding the completion criteria for parallel operation numerically together with the user departments is also part of the FDE's work in this process. We set an initial frame of 45 to 90 days and define the end conditions before starting.

Where and how to place the migration destination system is covered in Designing to put your fifth business app on your own cloud. Which business processes to consider for in-house development is addressed in How to tell in the second article. The overall FDE approach is summarized on our FDE service page.

How this differs from contract development

When outsourcing a migration, there is something to watch out for. One purpose of leaving a SaaS should be to create a state where your business runs without depending on a specific provider. Yet if a system remains that only the company that handled the migration can fix, you have merely swapped who you depend on.

This is why we distinguish FDE work from contract development. In contract development, billable hours are revenue, so the longer parallel operation runs and the more rework there is, the more the vendor earns. We set fees by duration rather than an accumulation of hours, and we do not enter contracts where longer means more profit. We treat hours as an investment in making the next project faster. Mechanisms that can be reused at other companies, such as export verification and old-versus-new comparison, are grown into components of our products and reused in the next project, while procedures specific to your company remain as your company's configuration. And we decide the ending up front. Our work is done when your staff can fix the migrated system themselves and export the data themselves.

Our practice and its limits

To be honest: first, contract negotiation and legal judgment are not our role. We can help up to the point of extracting the renewal date and notice deadline from the contract into a schedule, but how to read the clauses and how to negotiate with the provider are the job of your legal team and, if necessary, a lawyer. The cancellation notice is also sent by your company, as the party to the contract.

Second, data that cannot be extracted cannot be extracted by us either. For information not supported by the SaaS's export function or API, you must choose between copying it manually from the screen or giving it up. One reason to run the export test early is precisely to make this decision early.

If we come into contact with personal data during migration work, we become a subcontracted handler of that personal data. Article 25 of the Act on the Protection of Personal Information requires the entrusting party to provide necessary and appropriate supervision of the subcontractor 11. Although we are ISO/IEC 27001 certified (with a registration scope covering the planning, development, and provision of SaaS products that leverage AI technology), this does not remove your obligation to supervise. The subcontracting agreement and the scope of data we will access are decided before the work begins.

I also do not believe all SaaS should be canceled. For operations such as accounting or payroll, where you must continuously keep up with changes in laws and accounting rules, it is often more reasonable to leave them to SaaS or packaged software. The procedures in this article were assembled from public guidelines and publicly available contract clauses, not from a specific customer case. Finally, a word about capacity. We are a small company, so the number of engagements we can take on simultaneously is limited.

Summary

SaaS cancellation and migration are planned by counting backward from the renewal date. There are four key points.

  • The notice deadline is not the renewal date itself, but before it. In the example contract it is 30 days prior, and a renewal with a reduced quantity triggers a re-set unit price
  • Test the data export with the full production dataset before deciding to cancel. The grace period after termination is only 30 days in the example contract
  • Narrow down the data to be migrated, and schedule two rehearsals on the assumption that problems will surface late in the process
  • End parallel operation within the final contract period you have already paid for, according to criteria set in advance

All of these are unglamorous tasks. However, the money you should save by dropping SaaS will be pushed back a full year by missing the notice deadline just once. Start by opening the contract in front of you and writing the renewal date and notice deadline into your calendar.
