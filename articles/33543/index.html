Forward Deployed Engineer Roles in India: Career Prospects, Outlook, and Compensation

BuildAI Careers · 2026-09-23 · 25 min read

Summary

This career guide examines the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role in India, arguing it is a distinct track rather than a rebranded solutions consultant: FDEs write production code in customer environments, own deployments from prototype to production, and feed field learnings back into core products. TCS plans a pool of roughly 5,900–8,900 FDEs (1–1.5% of its workforce), and verified openings exist at Google Cloud, Apple, Sarvam AI, Atlassian, Wipro, Notion and others. Global demand is surging—Indeed counted FDE postings rising from 643 in April 2025 to 5,330 in April 2026, a 729% increase. The role is not entry-level: employers typically require 4–10+ years of experience. Compensation data is sparse and dispersed, ranging from ₹10–13 lakh to ₹70–90 lakh in advertised postings; the author models indicative total-compensation bands by seniority. Bengaluru is the strongest hub, followed by Mumbai/Pune. The verdict: a high-upside transition for experienced engineers, with trade-offs including travel, ambiguous ownership, and inconsistent role definitions.

Executive summary

Forward Deployed Engineering has moved from a relatively specialized deployment model into one of the clearest new career tracks created by enterprise AI. An FDE is not simply a solutions consultant with a more fashionable title: at companies such as Google Cloud, Sarvam AI, OpenAI, Atlassian, and Accenture, the job explicitly requires engineers to write production code inside or alongside customer environments, solve integration and data problems, take systems from prototype to production, and feed lessons from the field back into the core product.

For India, the outlook as of September 23, 2026 is unusually strong. TCS told Reuters that it is building a pool of roughly 5,900–8,900 forward-deployed engineers, equivalent to 1–1.5% of its workforce. At the same time, current India postings can be verified at Google Cloud, Apple, Sarvam AI, Atlassian, Wipro, Notion, Mactores, and Supervity. This means FDE is no longer merely a Silicon Valley title being imported experimentally: it is appearing across Indian AI startups, hyperscalers, global product companies, and IT-services firms.

The international demand signal is also exceptional. Indeed data reported in 2026 showed FDE postings increasing from 643 in April 2025 to 5,330 in April 2026, a 729% year-over-year increase; separate Lightcast data cited in September 2026 indicated growth of more than 1,000% for January–August 2026 versus the same period in 2025. These figures are not India-specific, but they are useful evidence that companies globally are reorganizing enterprise AI deployment around this model.

The strongest Indian market appears to be Bengaluru, followed by Mumbai/Pune, with specialized opportunities in Delhi/NCR and likely spillover into Hyderabad and Chennai as enterprise AI deployment matures. Bengaluru currently has verified FDE openings from Sarvam, Apple, Atlassian and Wipro; Google's role allows Bengaluru, Pune, and Mumbai; Mactores has a remote Mumbai role.

The catch is that FDE is not predominantly an entry-level occupation. Sarvam says 4+ years is preferred, Wipro asks for 5–8 years, Google asks for eight years in cloud or technical customer-facing work, and Atlassian's principal opening requires 10+ years of backend development plus significant production AI experience. Entry-level openings exist, but the center of gravity is clearly experienced engineers who can independently debug production systems and handle customers.

Compensation is correspondingly attractive but unusually dispersed. There is still no statistically reliable, India-wide FDE salary median from Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, or PayScale. Direct advertisements span from a ₹10–13 lakh India-remote FDE opening at Peakflo to a current employer-provided ₹70–90 lakh range for Cartesia's Bengaluru FDE role. Meanwhile, Levels.fyi reports a 2026 India software-engineering median total compensation of about ₹30.0 lakh, with the 75th percentile at ₹49.1 lakh and 90th percentile at ₹70.3 lakh.

My best estimate, after triangulating these postings with SWE, Solutions Engineer, Customer Engineer, AI-engineering and seniority data, is that a commercially strong FDE in India should think in approximately these annual total-compensation bands rather than one national average:

FDE level Typical experience Base salary estimate Cash bonus Annualized equity* Indicative total compensation

FDE / mid-level ~3–6 yrs ₹20–35L ₹1–5L ₹2–12L ₹23–52L

Career verdict: for an Indian software, cloud, data, ML, or solutions engineer who likes both deep engineering and customer interaction, FDE is one of the highest-upside career transitions available in the current AI cycle. It offers a path toward Staff/Principal Engineering, AI architecture, product engineering, field CTO roles, deployment leadership, technical product management, or even founding a company. The main trade-offs are travel/customer pressure, ambiguous ownership, less opportunity to specialize narrowly, and wide variation between companies in what “FDE” actually means.

What a Forward Deployed Engineer actually does

The defining characteristic is ownership, not location

“Forward deployed” originated as a model associated particularly strongly with Palantir: engineers were put close to users and made responsible for the technical success of deployments rather than handing an implementation over to a separate services organization. The contemporary AI version has retained that principle but broadened it into a combination of software engineering, applied AI, architecture, integration, product discovery, and customer engineering. Palantir's own description of its Forward Deployed Software Engineer role emphasizes responsibility for technical success at deployments and direct work with users.

The most useful distinction is therefore:

A normal product engineer primarily builds the product; an FDE uses and extends the product to make a customer's specific outcome work in production—and then turns what was learned into improvements that can benefit other customers.

That definition closely matches current official postings. Sarvam says its FDE owns the technical arc “from scoping the solution through to the system running in production,” builds across whichever parts of the stack are required, deploys into customer infrastructure, constructs evaluations, and returns field learning to product and research. Google's GenAI FDE similarly codes the “connective tissue” between AI products and customers' APIs, legacy data, and security boundaries, builds evaluation and observability systems, and converts recurring friction into reusable modules or product feature requests.

Accenture's current formulation is even more explicit: it describes a Forward Deployed AI Engineer as a production engineer embedded inside the client's enterprise, accountable for time-to-value, adoption, reliability, and scalability rather than conventional project milestones. Its senior role includes architecture, code-with sessions, executive workshops, security/governance integration and the codification of reusable deployment patterns.

Typical responsibilities

Across the current Indian and global postings reviewed for this report, six responsibilities recur consistently:

Responsibility What an FDE is expected to do in practice Current evidence
Discovery and problem definition Understand workflows, constraints, data, business economics, and what outcome should actually be optimized Sarvam; Google; Supervity

AI/ML deployment Build RAG, agentic systems, evaluation pipelines, inference integrations and model/tool orchestration Google; Sarvam; Databricks
Infrastructure/integration Make the solution work with legacy applications, identity, databases, APIs, security controls and cloud/on-prem infrastructure Accenture; Supervity; Mactores

This makes FDE substantially more technical than conventional implementation consulting at its best employers. Sarvam explicitly says its FDSEs are “hands-on engineers, not technical project managers,” while Google characterizes its engineer as an embedded “innovator-builder” who goes beyond high-level architecture to code, debug and jointly ship production systems.

Where FDE stops and adjacent roles begin

The borders are fuzzy because companies use titles inconsistently. A Solutions Engineer may be largely pre-sales at one company and write substantial code at another. “Customer Engineer” can mean a cloud architect at a hyperscaler, an implementation engineer at a SaaS company, or even a field-service engineer in salary databases. FDE usually differs by requiring both substantial production coding and direct accountability for a particular customer's deployed outcome.

That ambiguity matters both for salary comparisons and job searches: candidates should search not just Forward Deployed Engineer, but also terms such as Forward Deployed AI Engineer, Deployment Engineer, Strategic Deployment Engineer, Applied AI Engineer, Resident Engineer, Customer AI Engineer, AI Solutions Engineer, Field Engineer, and Forward Deployment Engineer. Current employers themselves use several of these variants.

Industries using the model

The strongest early adopter is enterprise generative AI. Google Cloud's FDE role explicitly covers RAG, vector databases, multi-agent systems, MCP, evaluation and observability; Sarvam's covers voice/conversational AI, translation, developer APIs and agentic workflows; Supervity focuses on enterprise AI agents and integrations with legacy systems.

The second large category is IT services and consulting. TCS's plan alone could create several thousand FDE-designated positions, while Accenture says it is building a large FDE capability around enterprise platforms from OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, Google, Salesforce, SAP and Palantir. Wipro already has an FDE L1 opening.

A third category is enterprise SaaS and productivity software, illustrated by Atlassian and Notion. A fourth is industrial/operational transformation, exemplified by Apple's Global Sourcing and Supply Management posting.

Regulated sectors should be particularly fertile ground. Sarvam says it works with enterprises and public institutions and names customers including financial-services organizations; Supervity explicitly targets banking, insurance, healthcare and government; Atlassian calls regulated-industry experience in finance or healthcare useful. Such environments create exactly the integration, security, governance and on-premise complexity for which embedded technical teams are valuable.

Demand and market size

There is no credible published market-size estimate specifically for FDE employment in India. Any report giving a precise national FDE workforce or CAGR should therefore be treated skeptically.

There are, however, several strong proxies.

First, India sits inside a roughly $315 billion IT-services industry, according to Reuters' July 2026 report, and TCS—the country's largest software-services company—is targeting 1–1.5% of its workforce for FDE-type work, or about 5,900–8,900 people. TCS's CEO explicitly argued that enterprise AI still requires deep knowledge of the customer's environment to connect models with existing systems and data flows.

Second, India's Global Capability Centre economy gives FDEs a very large enterprise customer and talent base. A 2026 NASSCOM–Zinnov assessment cited by Reuters put India's GCC workforce above 2.36 million and annual GCC revenue at nearly $100 billion. These are not FDE-market figures, but they indicate the scale of sophisticated engineering, data and enterprise-transformation work already located in India.

Third, global title growth is extreme. Indeed counted 5,330 FDE listings in April 2026 versus 643 one year earlier, while Lightcast data reported later in 2026 indicated more than 1,000% year-on-year growth for the first eight months of the year. Because these datasets are global/US-heavy, they should be interpreted as a directional demand indicator, not an India forecast.

Taken together with the current India vacancies, the evidence supports a strong conclusion: 2026–2029 demand should grow materially even if the exact title “FDE” does not. The economic function—getting AI from demonstration to integrated production system—is broader than the title. TCS's scale plan, Google's India expansion of the title, and Accenture's formal deployment-engineering organization all point in the same direction. This is an inference from the cited hiring and organizational evidence rather than a published forecast.

Regional hubs and working model

Bengaluru is currently the clearest hub. Sarvam's FDE is based there; Apple, Atlassian and Wipro have Bengaluru postings; multiple other employers appear in contemporaneous job-board results.

Mumbai and Pune form the next visible cluster. Google's opening covers both cities as well as Bengaluru, Mactores has a Mumbai-based remote FDE role, and Mumbai's concentration of financial-services headquarters makes it structurally suited to regulated enterprise deployments. The last point is an inference rather than a measured FDE vacancy count.

Delhi/NCR is relevant particularly for sovereign AI, government, defense and other constrained deployments. Sarvam, for example, has separately advertised strategic deployment work in Delhi involving client-site, on-premises, air-gapped and classified environments.

The market is not uniformly on-site. Sarvam's current Bengaluru role is explicitly on-site; Mactores' Mumbai position is remote; Atlassian describes itself as distributed-first and allows office/home combinations. Thus the right conclusion is neither “FDEs are remote” nor “FDEs must sit at the customer.” Rather, roles involving secure infrastructure, stakeholder discovery and difficult enterprise integration have a stronger reason for physical presence than ordinary product SWE jobs, while cloud-native deployments can support a distributed model.

Skills, education, and experience

The technical profile

The ideal FDE is a generalist with at least one deep technical strength, not someone who knows a small amount about every technology.

Current Indian postings converge on the following technical foundation:

Software engineering. Python is especially common; JavaScript/TypeScript is useful for full-stack and integration work. Candidates need to be comfortable with APIs, backend services, microservices, databases, queues, authentication and production debugging—not merely notebooks or prompt engineering. Sarvam calls for strong Python and comfort across backend, data pipelines and cloud infrastructure; Atlassian expects Python, JavaScript or comparable languages plus APIs and microservices.

Applied generative AI. RAG, embeddings/vector databases, agent/tool use, structured outputs, context and memory, evaluation, model selection and failure analysis are becoming baseline FDE capabilities at AI-oriented employers. Google's posting calls out vector databases, RAG, multi-agent frameworks and LLM-native performance metrics; Sarvam names context engineering, memory, RAG, tool use and agent frameworks.

Production AI engineering. It is not enough to build a demo. Google requires experience moving AI solutions from conception to production and building evaluation/observability frameworks; Sarvam emphasizes reliability, latency, accuracy, cost and production-load architecture.

Cloud and infrastructure. Containers, CI/CD, cloud architecture, networking, observability, IAM/security and—in certain roles—Kubernetes or on-prem deployment matter because the hardest problems occur at system boundaries. Mactores couples FDE work with AWS modernization; Supervity asks for GCP and Docker proficiency; Accenture's requirements include cloud-native APIs, microservices, containerization and serverless systems.

Data and integration. SQL, relational databases, data pipelines, vector search, ETL/ELT concepts and the ability to connect legacy enterprise systems are highly valuable. Supervity explicitly mentions SAP/Coupa integration, custom APIs, WebSockets and data pipelines; Google's FDE builds connections to APIs, legacy data silos and security boundaries.

The non-technical profile matters unusually much

A brilliant programmer who dislikes customers can be a mediocre FDE.

The recurring non-technical requirements are high agency, ambiguity tolerance, written and verbal communication, stakeholder management, commercial judgment, rapid problem decomposition and the ability to explain technical trade-offs to both engineers and executives. Google's posting explicitly asks for outcome ownership, ambiguous problem-solving and stakeholder influence; Sarvam emphasizes self-unblocking and customer instinct; Supervity describes communication and executive-facing sales engineering as core competencies.

This makes the role particularly attractive for engineers with a “founder” temperament. Google's description literally refers to a founder's mindset, while Supervity seeks founder-like ownership and Atlassian values cross-functional influence without formal authority.

Education and experience expectations

There is no universal education requirement. Google's advanced India role requires a bachelor's degree in engineering/computer science or equivalent practical experience; a master's or PhD is preferred but not required. Sarvam's current posting places more emphasis on demonstrated ability to ship and explicitly says capability matters more than the precise number of years.

The experience distribution is more revealing:

Stage Rough experience profile What employers usually expect Evidence
Early / Associate ~1–3 yrs Strong SWE foundation, smaller customer deployments, ability to learn rapidly; relatively uncommon under exact FDE title Peakflo listed 1+ yrs for an India-remote FDE
Core FDE ~3–6 yrs Independent production engineering, cloud/data/AI work and customer ownership Sarvam prefers 4+ yrs
Senior FDE ~5–9 yrs Architecture, production AI, ambiguous engagements, stakeholder leadership Wipro asks 5–8 yrs; Google asks 8 yrs
Staff / Principal ~8–12+ yrs Cross-account architecture, reusable platform thinking, mentoring and major customer ownership Atlassian asks 10+ yrs backend plus 5+ yrs production AI/ML

Therefore a new graduate should generally not optimize narrowly for the FDE title. Building two to four years of excellent backend/full-stack, ML, data, cloud or solutions-engineering experience may be a faster route to a strong FDE role than accepting a weak “FDE” position that involves little real engineering.

Compensation and comparison with adjacent roles

What the available salary data actually tells us

The data problem is important. Searchable Indian salary databases have much richer samples for SWE, Solutions Engineer and Customer Engineer than for FDE. There is no mature, standardized Indian FDE compensation dataset comparable with Levels.fyi's software-engineer dataset. Salary estimates should therefore be treated as triangulation rather than false precision.

A useful anchor is Levels.fyi. As of September 23, 2026, its India software-engineering dataset reported 23,834 submissions, with median total compensation of approximately ₹30.0 lakh, a 25th percentile of ₹16.8 lakh, a 75th percentile of ₹49.1 lakh and a 90th percentile of ₹70.3 lakh. Bengaluru's reported median/typical level on the same page was higher than the national figure.

At the much broader Solutions Engineer level, Glassdoor currently reports approximately ₹8 lakh average base pay plus ₹1 lakh average additional pay, with a ₹5–11.9 lakh base-pay range. The data encompasses thousands of employers and therefore includes many jobs considerably less technical than an AI FDE.

For Customer Engineer, Glassdoor reports approximately ₹12 lakh average base plus ₹1 lakh additional pay, with a broad ₹4–17 lakh base range. Individual employer data varies dramatically; the page, for example, showed Microsoft customer-engineering compensation materially above many traditional service-engineering employers.

Direct FDE advertisements show how wide the new market is. Peakflo advertised an India-remote FDE job at ₹10–13 lakh for candidates with 1+ year of experience, while Cartesia's Bengaluru FDE listing was advertised at ₹70–90 lakh. The latter was employer-provided according to both Indeed and Glassdoor's current listing.

That spread is not a contradiction. “FDE” can describe an early-stage implementation engineer at one company and a scarce senior AI/product engineer trusted with multimillion-dollar enterprise deployments at another.

The salary comparison should not be interpreted as proof that an FDE automatically earns more than an SWE. At top product companies, Staff/Principal SWE compensation can exceed most FDE offers. Rather, FDE expands the set of highly paid opportunities available to engineers whose comparative advantage is the combination of technical depth and customer-facing execution. Levels.fyi's India SWE 90th percentile of approximately ₹70.3 lakh demonstrates that ordinary product-engineering ladders can already be extremely lucrative.

The more interesting comparison is with Solutions Engineering. The average Indian Solutions Engineer in Glassdoor's broad dataset earns much less than a top FDE because traditional SE roles do not necessarily require the same degree of production coding. An experienced Solutions Engineer who can genuinely build production systems may therefore find FDE a particularly attractive lateral transition.

Career progression, lateral moves, and hiring

Typical progression

Unlike conventional SWE, where progression can stay almost entirely inside one codebase or product organization, an FDE accumulates technical breadth, customer-domain knowledge and organizational influence. That combination creates several exits.

The exact sequence is not universal. Current postings demonstrate that an FDE ladder can stretch from junior/early positions through principal level: Sarvam is hiring across levels; Google's current opening is advanced; Atlassian has a Principal FDE role with mentoring responsibilities.

The founder path is not hypothetical as a skill fit. FDE work forces an engineer to discover actual customer pain, make scope and architecture trade-offs, ship under uncertainty, and understand willingness to adopt/pay—capabilities unusually close to early-stage startup work. Google's and Supervity's explicit preference for a founder-like mindset reflects this overlap.

Common lateral moves include product/backend engineering, applied AI/ML engineering, solutions architecture, enterprise architecture, technical product management, AI consulting, customer engineering, developer relations for technical products, engineering management and technical sales leadership. Former Palantir forward-deployment talent has also moved into leadership roles across newer AI organizations, illustrating the portability of the model.

One career risk deserves attention: spending years doing bespoke customer customization without building reusable technical depth can make an FDE look more like an implementation consultant than a senior engineer. Candidates should favor teams where field work feeds product architecture, where engineers still write substantial production code, and where promotions can lead to Staff/Principal engineering rather than only account management. Google's, Sarvam's and Atlassian's descriptions all exhibit the stronger version of the role.

Typical hiring process

No single standardized FDE interview process exists. The flow below is a synthesis of the capabilities that current employers explicitly test or require. Google notes that its India position generally involves in-person interviews, while the role descriptions across Google, Sarvam, Atlassian and Supervity imply assessment of coding, architecture, AI, debugging and stakeholder communication.

  • Application or referral
  • Recruiter or hiring-manager screen
  • Coding or practical engineering exercise
  • System design and production debugging
  • AI, data or cloud architecture round
  • Customer case or technical presentation
  • Cross-functional and ownership interview
  • References, compensation and offer

For strong FDE organizations, expect interviews to probe questions such as: How would you integrate an LLM into a customer's existing data architecture? How would you detect whether a RAG system is improving? How do you debug latency across an API/model/database chain? How do you handle a customer asking for a one-off feature that should not enter the core product? How do you turn a vague business request into measurable acceptance criteria? Those themes follow directly from the responsibilities in current Google, Sarvam, Accenture and Supervity openings.

Candidate strategy and future outlook

What to learn

For somebody targeting an Indian FDE role over the next six to eighteen months, the highest-return curriculum is software engineering first, applied AI second, deployment/customer skills third.

A useful technical target is being able to take an ambiguous enterprise problem and independently build the following chain:

business workflow → API/data ingestion → backend service → LLM/agent/RAG layer → database/vector store → evaluation → observability → authentication/security → container/cloud deployment → user feedback

That architecture closely mirrors what current FDEs are expected to own at Google, Sarvam, Mactores and Supervity.

For coding, prioritize Python and add TypeScript/JavaScript if possible. For systems work, understand HTTP/APIs, SQL, caching, queues, distributed-system fundamentals, Docker, cloud networking, identity and CI/CD. For AI, become comfortable with RAG, agent/tool orchestration, structured outputs, prompt/context management, model evaluation, tracing and cost/latency optimization.

Do not build a portfolio made only of chatbots. A much stronger FDE portfolio project looks like a small enterprise engagement: define a messy business problem, document discovery assumptions, integrate two or three external systems, build an AI workflow, establish accuracy/latency/cost metrics, deploy it, deliberately induce failures, document the debugging process and identify which customer-specific components could become reusable product primitives. That directly demonstrates the abilities current employers request.

Certifications

Certifications are secondary to evidence that you can ship, but they can help candidates coming from consulting, support or traditional enterprise IT establish cloud credibility.

An AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate remains a reasonable foundation for AWS-oriented roles; AWS describes it as validating knowledge across a broad range of AWS services.

For GCP-oriented FDE positions, Google Professional Cloud Architect is more directly aligned with enterprise system architecture, while Professional Machine Learning Engineer is relevant to candidates specializing in production ML/AI. Google's official certification material covers designing and managing cloud solutions for the former and deploying/operationalizing ML applications for the latter.

For reliability-heavy candidates, Google's Professional Cloud DevOps Engineer track is also relevant because the role centers on delivery speed and service reliability.

The right decision rule is simple: one relevant certification plus one excellent production project beats five certifications with no deployment evidence. Sarvam's posting is a useful reality check—it explicitly values shipped work, production artifacts, open source or personal projects as proof of builder capability.

Interview preparation

Coding preparation should resemble SWE preparation but emphasize practical engineering over purely algorithmic tricks: API design, data transformation, concurrency, error handling, testing and debugging. System-design preparation should cover service boundaries, databases, queues, caching, authentication, observability, failure modes and cost/reliability trade-offs. AI rounds should include RAG design, retrieval evaluation, agent failure modes, tool execution, model selection, hallucination mitigation, tracing and LLM cost/latency. These themes map directly onto current FDE requirements.

The differentiating preparation is the customer case interview. Practice turning a sentence such as “we want an AI agent for our support team” into discovery questions: Who are the users? Which systems contain the source of truth? What actions may the model take? What error rate is tolerable? What should be human-approved? How will ROI be measured? What data cannot leave the customer's environment? What is the latency target? How will the system be evaluated before and after rollout? Google and Accenture explicitly frame FDE work around measurable ROI, production constraints and enterprise integration rather than demonstration quality alone.

Candidates should also rehearse explaining one architecture at three levels: to a backend engineer, to a CISO/CTO, and to a business owner. That communication range is explicitly sought by Supervity and Atlassian.

Networking and job-search strategy

The highest-yield networking is likely to be unusually technical. Instead of messaging recruiters with “I am interested in FDE roles,” contact current FDEs, deployment leads and applied-AI engineers with a concrete artifact: a deployed system, an architecture write-up, an evaluation framework, an open-source integration or a short postmortem showing how you debugged a difficult production problem.

Search employer career sites directly as well as LinkedIn/Indeed because the terminology is still unstable. The 2026 market already includes Forward Deployed Engineer, Forward Deployed AI Engineer, Forward Deployment Engineer, Strategic Deployment Engineer, and adjacent Deployment Engineer titles.

For engineers already at TCS, Wipro, Accenture or another large services firm, internal mobility may become one of the largest channels into FDE. TCS explicitly has not said how much of its targeted 5,900–8,900-person pool will be external hiring versus internal retraining, while its management says the company spends around $1 billion annually on talent development.

The outlook through the next several years

The base-case outlook is favorable because generative AI has changed the economics of customization. AI can generate code and accelerate prototyping, but enterprises still have proprietary data, legacy applications, security constraints, organizational workflows, governance requirements and adoption problems. Current FDE job descriptions are increasingly centered on precisely those “last-mile” constraints rather than raw model development.

This also explains why FDE could remain valuable even as AI makes routine programming faster. The bottleneck shifts toward context acquisition, architecture, integration, evaluation, judgment and organizational adoption. A recent account from a KPMG forward-deployed engineer describes the role moving beyond writing code toward gathering customer context, connecting disparate systems and coaching clients on AI use; the account also notes that on-site interaction can improve collaboration.

The bull case is that the TCS pattern spreads throughout India's services sector: instead of selling large generic implementation teams, providers deploy smaller groups of engineers who understand both AI systems and the customer's domain. Accenture is already formalizing a comparable model and Wipro is recruiting under the title.

The main downside is title inflation. Companies may relabel implementation or consulting work as FDE without granting engineers real product access or meaningful coding responsibility. The title itself could also fade as its practices get absorbed into Applied AI Engineer, Customer Engineer, Solutions Architect or Resident Engineer roles. That would not necessarily reduce demand for the underlying skills; it would mainly make title-based market statistics less useful.

A second risk is career over-specialization around one vendor or one customer's bespoke stack. The best protection is to keep building transferable skills—backend engineering, distributed systems, cloud architecture, AI evaluation, data engineering and strong product judgment—and to prefer organizations where field discoveries are turned into reusable product capabilities. Google's, Sarvam's and Atlassian's current role designs are good examples of that feedback-loop model.

Methodology, assumptions, and data gaps

This report reflects information available on September 23, 2026. I prioritized live company career pages and official role descriptions, followed by Reuters and recent industry reporting, and then salary/job databases for market triangulation.

For employer identification, I treated a company as a particularly strong data point when an exact India FDE or clearly equivalent deployment-engineering role could be found on its own career site. This produced strong current evidence for Google, Sarvam, Apple, Atlassian, Wipro, Notion, Mactores, Supervity and Accenture. Job-board results were used as secondary evidence because aggregators frequently retain expired jobs, duplicate positions and match unrelated use of the words “forward” or “deployment.”

For demand, there is no known authoritative India-only time series for FDE openings. Consequently, this report does not present job-board search counts as an Indian market size. Instead it combines the unusually concrete TCS workforce target with current hiring evidence and the global Indeed/Lightcast growth figures.

For compensation, direct India FDE observations remain too sparse for a defensible median. The level-by-level pay ranges are therefore estimates built from: direct FDE advertisements such as Peakflo and Cartesia; Levels.fyi's 23,834-submission India SWE dataset; Glassdoor's Solutions Engineer and Customer Engineer data; the experience requirements of current FDE openings; and the expectation that highly technical FDE jobs compete substantially with experienced software/AI engineering roles rather than with generic implementation consulting.

I did not find a sufficiently robust PayScale India dataset specifically for Forward Deployed Engineers to justify incorporating a PayScale number. That omission is preferable to mapping a differently defined engineering title onto FDE compensation.

Equity is the weakest part of any compensation estimate. Public-company RSUs have a relatively observable market value; private-company options can ultimately be worth considerably more or less than their headline grant value, including zero. The annualized-equity columns should therefore be used for offer-comparison scenarios, not personal cash-flow planning.

Finally, FDE is still an evolving occupational label. Google, Sarvam and Atlassian use it for deeply technical production engineering; other firms may use it for implementation, consulting, professional services or sales engineering. The underlying job description is more important than the title. A strong FDE offer should give the engineer meaningful coding ownership, direct access to customer problems, responsibility for production outcomes, and a mechanism for feeding deployment learning back into product development. Those are the characteristics most consistently visible in the strongest current roles.
