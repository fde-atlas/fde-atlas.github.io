How to Read an FDE Job Posting: Comparing Abu Dhabi vs. India Travel Requirements

Original title: FDE 招聘启事怎么看：阿布扎比与印度出差要求对比

FDE Times · 2026-10-16 · 5 min read

Summary

This article compares FDE job postings in Abu Dhabi and India. OpenAI's Abu Dhabi role specifies three office days per week and up to 50% travel, while Palantir requires working with minimal supervision, like a startup CTO. Indian postings from HackerRank and Vendasta mention on-site customer work but give no travel percentages. The author argues candidates should treat posting details as lifestyle descriptions, ask pointed interview questions about travel frequency and partners, and prove themselves with concrete prototype-to-production delivery stories, since experienced FDEs remain scarce in the Gulf.

Key Takeaways

  • OpenAI and Palantir roles in Abu Dhabi specify travel percentages and require ownership of deployments; only Palantir requires working with minimal supervision.
  • HackerRank and Vendasta roles in India require working at customer sites, but give no travel percentage.
  • A UAE recruiting firm says that engineers with real FDE experience remain scarce, whether in Dubai or Abu Dhabi.

OpenAI is hiring Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) in Abu Dhabi, and the job description lists two requirements side by side: three days in the office per week, and travel of up to 50% of the time. At first glance, the two conditions seem contradictory. Read closely, they paint a fairly candid picture of how an FDE team operates in a new market.

The Gulf and India are worth comparing carefully, because the job postings are written very differently in the two regions. In Abu Dhabi, OpenAI and Palantir give concrete numbers. In India, HackerRank and Vendasta make clear that you will work at customer sites, but never say how much.

If you are considering applying, one thing to understand: the same job title can come with wildly different clarity about what the work actually involves. Learning to read what a job posting says—and what it leaves out—is the first skill for anyone who wants to be an FDE.

In Abu Dhabi, the office is just a transit point

According to OpenAI's job description, the FDE in the UAE is responsible for the technical delivery of multiple deployment projects, from the first prototype all the way to a system running stably in production. Each engineer owns the full lifecycle of several projects, not just one phase.

Palantir is hiring a Forward Deployed Software Engineer in Abu Dhabi and goes a step further. It compares the role's responsibilities to those of a startup CTO, requiring candidates to learn, execute, and make decisions autonomously with minimal supervision. Notably, OpenAI's job description contains no such "minimal supervision" requirement.

Consider a hypothetical calculation. A four-person FDE team, each member traveling half the time, leaves an average of only about two people in the office each week. If each person owns three deployment projects, the team is simultaneously looking after twelve systems and twelve different environments, with few colleagues on hand to consult.

So the days in the office are best used to sync progress, review architecture, and pass lessons between deployment projects. The work that actually creates value happens at the customer site.

Who are the customers, and who works alongside you?

A UAE recruiting firm, in an article introducing the local market, argues that FDEs there must deploy, customize, and integrate solutions directly within complex enterprise and government systems.

This is a middleman's view, not a requirement written by an employer, so treat it only as a directional signal.

If the signal is accurate, knowing how to write code is not enough. You also need to know who approves data access, which environments allow models to run, and which processes must never be touched.

In Saudi Arabia, Microsoft has taken a different approach. Microsoft and HUMAIN announced a long-term partnership agreement (this is a partnership announcement, not a job posting). The announcement states that HUMAIN's AI experts will work side by side with Microsoft's FDE team.

One agreement does not represent the norms of the entire Gulf region. But it points to an option worth considering: the FDE does not go to the customer alone, but works alongside personnel from a local partner, which happens to fill the process and access gaps mentioned above.

When interviewing with a Gulf FDE team, you can ask directly: Will I be on-site alone, or working with people from a partner? Which one?

In India, the postings only say "on-site," without saying how much

HackerRank's FDE role in Bangalore requires working from the office, but also states that engineers will work at customer sites. New hires need to lead various activities face-to-face with customers: requirements discovery, pilots, and demos.

Vendasta's role in Chennai, posted on Built In Bengaluru, is more direct: the position requires traveling to customer locations and embedding with the customer's engineering team.

Neither posting gives a travel percentage, which differs from the two Abu Dhabi postings. You have no way to tell whether "on-site" means one demo a month or three weeks a month in another city. This gap should be clarified in the interview, not after signing the contract.

A blog post discussing FDE salaries in India offers another reason for caution: FDE is a title, not a level, so salaries vary widely. Two roles with the same name may be jobs with entirely different levels of responsibility.

The first row of the table shows that the main difference between the two markets is whether numbers are given, not how high they are. Even within Abu Dhabi there are two formulations: OpenAI says required, Palantir says preferred and team-dependent.

Which rhythm can you sustain for two or three years?

Treat the details in a job description as a description of a lifestyle, not administrative footnotes. Travel of up to half your time could mean nearly half the year away from home; "multiple deployment projects" means switching contexts every day.

If your family and health cannot sustain that rhythm, you may well leave exactly when a deployment needs you most. For roles that give no numbers, ask something concrete: Last month, how many days did the FDEs on the team spend at customer sites?

What your resume and interview need to prove

In the same article, the UAE recruiting firm said that engineers with real forward deployed experience remain scarce, whether in Dubai or Abu Dhabi. If true, what sets you apart is evidence that you have actually done the work, not a list of technologies.

Your resume should address two requirements: owning technical delivery from prototype to production, as OpenAI's posting describes; and making decisions with minimal supervision, as Palantir requires.

For example, a line like: "Took a document classification model from prototype to production for a banking client, integrated it with legacy storage systems, and independently decided the deployment architecture" is more persuasive than ten lines of tool lists. If you have served the public sector, spell out how you handled data access and deployment environments.

For interviews, prepare three stories with quantifiable results: a system you took from prototype to production; a decision you had to make alone when there was no one to ask; and a requirements discovery or pilot you led face-to-face with a customer. The third story is especially relevant for roles like HackerRank.

Whether in Abu Dhabi or Bangalore, the title is the same, but the actual work is determined by the local customer. Someone who knows which pace they can sustain and has a story of personally pushing a deployment to production doesn't need a title to convince anyone.
