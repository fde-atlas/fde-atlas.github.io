Today's Judgment

Two adoption-side signals today, one from Chinese manufacturing and one from an Australian law firm, together answer the most underrated question in enterprise AI: why do accounts get distributed but AI never actually gets used?

JinkoSolar's 200 pilot accounts were quickly claimed, but two-plus months later only 95 people were identified as deep users—half naturally dropped off along the way; law firm Gilbert + Tobin pushed the active rate of provisioned seats to 87% (more than double the typical level for other tools), not through KPI mandates but through CEO modeling, role-based workflow demonstrations, and human sign-off boundaries.

The numbers enterprise AI rollouts most easily use to fool themselves are how many accounts were issued and how many training sessions were held. What really needs tracking is the adoption funnel:

账号发放 → 7 日激活 → 30 日深度用户 → 稳定工作流 → 业务负责人签收 → 第二岗位复用

. The FDE's job is not to hand out accounts, but to turn every drop-off reason into an asset for the next delivery round.

Key Signals

JinkoSolar: 200 pilot accounts produced 95 deep users, financial data organization compressed to about 15 minutes

  • Source: Shanghai Securities News | Original link
  • Shanghai Securities News reported on September 3: JinkoSolar launched the WorkBuddy "Lobster Exploration Camp" in April, and 200 pilot accounts were quickly claimed; two-plus months later, 95 people were identified as deep users, with applications spanning finance, IT, intellectual property, and production. A named task case: a finance BP distilled monthly budget data organization rules into a Skill—the first export and organization across multiple systems took about 2 days, subsequent monthly updates took 2-3 hours, and after integration the same task takes about 15 minutes. The report also noted that Mengniu frontline employees have developed 45 AI digital workers around their job needs.
  • Why it matters: These numbers reveal the real decay curve of enterprise AI adoption—200 accounts claimed proves interest, 95 deep users proves initial adoption, and the 15-minute named task is where business evidence begins. These three levels must not be conflated. Another important detail: the efficiency gain comes from "distilling organization rules into a Skill"—the employee turned their own experience into a reusable asset, which is precisely the marker of enterprise AI moving from tool usage to capability accumulation. Note the baselines: 2 days was the first-time build, 2-3 hours was monthly updates, and 15 minutes was post-integration—three different baselines that must not be conflated into "from 2 days down to 15 minutes."

Related developments: The WorkBuddy open platform also launched on September 2, with officially disclosed figures of 100+ co-creation partners, 100,000+ ecosystem assets, and 30 million+ cumulative installs, opening up five capability categories—Buddy apps, experts, Skills, connectors, and hardware—with third-party apps using OAuth 2.1 and audit boundaries (official documentation). Platform inventory doesn't equal production adoption, but ecosystem openness means enterprise-side Skill assets now have a circulation channel.

Gilbert + Tobin: Role-based training and human sign-off pushed AI adoption to 87%

  • Source: OpenAI official customer case | Original link
  • OpenAI disclosed on September 1: as of June 2026, the Australian law firm Gilbert + Tobin had 87% of provisioned seats active, more than double the typical adoption rate of other tools at the firm, with active users spanning partners and non-technical roles. Task outcomes: recruiting research and data extraction dropped from about 4 hours to 20 minutes; some conflict, KYC, and AML checks dropped from 3-8 hours to about 5 minutes; Codex prepared audit reports for 300 entities, avoiding about 1 day of manual work.
  • The organizational approach is key: the firm set no usage mandates, instead having the CEO model usage while the business transformation team entered departmental meetings to demonstrate role-based workflows; for sensitive tasks, they clearly defined allowed input scopes, access controls, data residency, output review, and human sign-off.
  • Why it matters: The 87% wasn't forced by KPIs—adoption at scale is an operating system: leadership modeling + role-based workflows + permission boundaries + on-site coaching + human accountability, all five indispensable. Particularly noteworthy is "entering departmental meetings to demonstrate role-based workflows"—this is exactly the embedded FDE playbook: no big lectures, going on-site by role, co-creating around real documents. The other side of high-risk industries is equally clear: the 5 minutes applies only to selected check items, and final reports are still reviewed and signed by humans—efficiency and accountability boundaries must be designed together before you dare to unleash AI in a high-sensitivity environment like a law firm.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Build an adoption funnel for every rollout project: account issuance → 7-day activation → 30-day deep user → stable workflow → business sign-off → cross-role reuse → renewal/business outcomes. At each layer, record headcount, drop-off reasons, FDE intervention actions, and conversion rates—never pass off registration counts and training attendance as production adoption.
  • Replace training with "department-embedded adoption": managers model usage, FDEs join meetings by role, and co-create around real documents and systems. Training completion doesn't equal delivery completion—within 30 days after a course, track at least the first runnable task, the first independent rerun, and business sign-off.
  • Fix six elements for high-sensitivity tasks: allowed inputs, data location, role permissions, human sign-off, exception escalation, audit logs—none can be skipped in finance, legal, or public service scenarios.
  • Encourage employees to distill their experience into Skills: the JinkoSolar finance BP case shows that the best Skill authors are the business experts themselves—the FDE's role is to teach them the packaging method, not to write it for them.
