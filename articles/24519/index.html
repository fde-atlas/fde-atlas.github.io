03 Overseas Roles, DeployCo, and Enterprise AI-as-a-Service

Original title: 03 海外岗位、DeployCo 与企业 AI 服务化

FDE中国社区 · 2026-07-11 · 4 min read

Summary

This article argues that the profit pool in enterprise AI is shifting from model API usage to deployment, integration, evaluation, and adoption. Using OpenAI's Deployment Company (DeployCo) and its announced acquisition of UK firm Tomoro (roughly 150 deployment and consulting staff, pending closing) as examples, it shows model companies, cloud vendors, and consultancies converging on the deployment layer. It maps how FDE-type roles are converging on discovery, scoping, integration, rollout, adoption, and eval-driven feedback, explains how to read job descriptions, and advises candidates to demonstrate verifiable delivery skills—such as building a PoC in 2–4 weeks and proving impact with golden datasets—rather than listing tools.

Key points

  • Enterprise AI value is moving from API calls to deployed, measurable systems.
  • OpenAI's DeployCo and the pending Tomoro acquisition signal model firms entering delivery.
  • FDE roles converge on discovery, integration, rollout, adoption, and evals.
  • JDs should be judged by production, eval, and adoption requirements, not titles.
  • Candidates should show verifiable delivery evidence, not tool familiarity.

Chapter Objectives

Understand the latest role profiles for FDE / Applied AI Engineer, and why model companies, system integrators, and consulting firms have begun competing for the enterprise AI deployment layer.

Opening question: Why are model companies getting into "delivery"

In the past, the growth logic for model companies was simple: make the API strong and get more developers to call it. But enterprise customers don't just buy model capability. A banking customer won't go live with a credit assistant just because the model can summarize documents, and a manufacturing customer won't let an agent change production schedules just because it can call tools.

What enterprises are truly willing to pay for is a deployment capability that can enter workflows, connect systems, pass security reviews, be adopted by frontline users, and continuously produce results. As a result, model companies, cloud providers, SaaS platforms, consulting firms, and system integrators have started competing at the same layer: who can turn AI from an API into a production system that runs in the field.

The rise of DeployCo, Applied AI teams, Forward Deployed AI Engineers, and Professional Services all points to the same shift: the profit pool for enterprise AI is moving from model calls to deployment, integration, evals, and adoption.

Model Companies and DeployCo

OpenAI Deployment Company (publicly called Deployment Company / DeployCo) represents model companies shifting from "selling APIs" to "embedding delivery at customer sites." Public information shows that its engineers go into customer organizations, identify high-value workflows, redesign the way work is done, and connect models to data, tools, controls, and core processes.

In early 2026, OpenAI announced the acquisition of the UK deployment company Tomoro (roughly 150 deployment and consulting staff). As of 2026-07-11, the deal is announced and pending regulatory approval and closing; it should not be described as "already merged into OpenAI." The pending staff mainly handle enterprise deployment, change management, and workflow redesign, closely overlapping with the FDE role profile.

How to Read a Job Description

Don't just look at the job title. FDE-type roles usually spread six categories of responsibility throughout the JD:

JD keywordWhat you should read it as
customer-facing / onsiteRequires directly facing customer sites and non-technical users
prototype / build quicklyMust be able to build a verifiable pipeline under incomplete requirements
production deploymentNot just a demo; must pass permissions, audits, and operations
eval / measurementMust prove effectiveness with samples and metrics
cross-functionalMust coordinate sales, product, engineering, compliance, and the customer
adoption / change managementMust drive users to genuinely change how they work

If a role only requires writing prompts and building demos, without production, evals, or customer adoption, it is more likely an ordinary application engineering role; if a role only requires communication and solutions, without code or system design, it is more likely a strategy/pre-sales role.

On-site customer capabilities

  • Explaining risk boundaries.
  • User adoption and training.

The significance of DeployCo

DeployCo represents a shift in the enterprise AI business model from "selling model usage volume" to "selling deployable systems and business outcomes."

Its implications for FDEs:

  • The value of the deployment layer is rising.
  • Customers are willing to pay for measurable outcomes.
  • Model providers will move closer to customer workflows.
  • Traditional consulting firms, system integrators, and AI startups will all enter the same battlefield.
  • FDE is both a job opportunity and an opportunity for small teams to start a services business.

Implications for individuals

If you want to transition to an FDE role, don't write your goal as "familiar with LLM tools." A more competitive statement would be:

  • I can turn an ambiguous business scenario into a PoC within 2–4 weeks.
  • I can design a minimal verifiable pipeline for RAG / Workflow / Agent.
  • I can prove effectiveness with a Golden Dataset, not just screenshots.
  • I can handle permissions, tool calls, auditing, and launch gates.
  • I can distill project retrospectives into templates, components, or a portfolio.

This kind of evidence aligns better with the screening logic of overseas FDE / Applied AI Engineer roles than "how many models you've used."

Common pitfalls

  • Looking only at salary, not skill requirements.
  • Understanding FDE as merely on-site development.
  • Ignoring product feedback loops and reusable assets.
  • Ignoring vendor lock-in, procurement governance, and exit costs.

Checklist

  • Can you explain why DeployCo emerged?
  • Do you know which capabilities FDE roles value most?
  • Can you distinguish between the model API business and the deployment services business?
  • Can you plan a path to fill your FDE skill gaps?
