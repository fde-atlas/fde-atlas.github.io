Forward Deployed Engineer | Customer Operations and Scope of Responsibility at Time of Hire

Original title: Forward Deployed Engineer｜採用時の顧客業務と担当範囲

HRdev · 2026-10-30 · 5 min read

Summary

This Japanese HRdev article profiles the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role: engineers embedded at customer sites who customize their own product until it delivers measurable business results. The role traces back to Palantir Technologies, which formalized its internal Delta structure into the FDE title in the early 2010s; in Japan, job postings appeared around 2025 as generative AI adoption showed that generic products alone rarely produce outcomes. The article details the mission (customer results plus feeding insights back into the product), typical duties, required skills (implementation, data integration, LLM evals), career paths, and differences from solutions architects, SIer engineers, product engineers, and customer success. It also gives hiring advice, including legal checks under Japan's Worker Dispatch Law, and warns that the domestic talent pool is thin and published salary figures are unreliable secondhand data.

Key points

  • FDEs embed with customers and own outcomes, not just delivery.
  • The title originated at Palantir in the early 2010s.
  • Japan saw FDE postings around 2025, driven by generative AI adoption.
  • Unlike SIer work, FDEs return field learnings to the product.
  • Hiring should widen the funnel and verify dispatch-law compliance.

In a nutshell

An engineer who embeds themselves in the customer's workplace, builds out the company's own product until it fits that company's operations, and takes responsibility through to delivering results.

In job postings for engineers who develop on-site at customers, you describe which business operations the company's product will be adapted to and the results expected from the person in the role.

Origin and emergence of this job title

This job title became known alongside the structure of Palantir Technologies, founded in 2003. According to Japanese commentary articles, the company formalized what it had internally called Delta into the job title Forward Deployed Engineer in the early 2010s. The company also uses the notation FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer).

According to the company's explanation, while the developers of its own products build a single feature for many customers, an FDE implements many features for a single customer.

In Japan, the title began appearing in job postings around 2025. The background is that as business adoption of generative AI spread, situations increased where simply handing over a general-purpose product did not lead to results. Hiring for this position began in Japan as well, centered on startups in the AI and SaaS space.

Discussion continues in Japan about how this role differs from Japanese SIers and contract development. Contract development implements the customer's requirements and delivers the finished product. An FDE extends the company's own product to fit the customer's operations and feeds the insights gained there back into the product as shared features.

Mission

The core of the responsibility is for the assigned customer to achieve results with the company's product. The scope does not end with implementation; it extends until the product is established in operations and the numbers move. Customer results are produced jointly with sales, customer success, and the customer's own organization, and the FDE is responsible for the technical implementation and adoption within that.

At the same time, the role also includes feeding insights gained in the field back to the product side. Not letting engagement end as one-off customer work, but proposing generalization into features usable by other customers, determines the value of this role. Decide before assignment who sets the priorities for where to stop customer-specific development and shift it toward productization.

Main scope of work

  • Understanding the customer's business. Enter the field and grasp the actual state of workflows and data
  • Applying and extending the product. Perform configuration, custom implementation, and data integration to make it usable in operations
  • Post-implementation adoption support. Monitor usage and improve on both operational and implementation fronts
  • Feedback to the product team. Propose generalizable items among customer-specific requests as product requirements
  • Building consensus with the customer. Explain what will and will not be built from the perspective of results

Required abilities

On the technical side, beyond the implementation skills needed to extend the target product (Python / TypeScript / SQL, etc.), you will be assessed on whether you can independently handle work connecting the customer's existing systems and data (data integration). In the generative AI space, an increasing number of postings require experience embedding LLMs into business operations and measuring output quality. Because you handle customer data, adherence to access control and confidentiality practices is also a given.

In selection, as much as technical skill, you will be checked on whether you can explain the customer's business in your own words. Rather than implementing requests as-is, you need the ability to trace back to the business purpose and judge which features are necessary. Suitability for a working style involving travel and on-site presence is also a selection criterion.

Career paths of people in this role

Many come from backend engineering or data engineering. Others enter as developers with customer-facing experience or as technologists with consulting experience.

From here, paths open toward product manager or product engineer on the product side, head of the customer-facing organization, or solution architect.

Screening points for recruiters

  • In job postings, clearly state the ratio of on-site, travel, and online work, and the scope of discretion. A working style where the customer gives direct work instructions at the customer's site may, depending on the actual conditions, constitute worker dispatch under Article 2, Paragraph 1 of the Worker Dispatch Act, even if the contract is a business outsourcing agreement. Confirm the contract type and chain of command with legal counsel
  • In resumes, check for experience interacting directly with customers. Even highly technical candidates may be unsuited to the work of drawing out the background behind requests. Example questions: "How did you rephrase the customer's initial request in light of the business purpose?" and "What requests did you decline, and how did you explain the reasons?"
  • Decide the relationship with the product team in advance. Without a path to feed customer-specific requests back into the product, FDE results will not accumulate

Hiring and market trends

The domestic talent pool is extremely thin, with almost no one experienced under this exact title. A realistic approach is to broaden the target to developers with client-facing experience and technologically strong consultants, then screen.

Salaries vary widely by company, with different assumptions at foreign enterprise product vendors versus domestic startups. Figures for individual companies circulate in commentary articles, but these are all secondhand and vary in level, so do not treat them as market rates. Set your company's expected range by comparing with domestic postings recruiting for the same scope of work.
