How to get an FDE involved

PostHog · 3 min read

Summary

This PostHog handbook guides customer success managers and TAMs on when and how to bring in a forward deployed engineer, and how often to avoid it. Most setup requests can be self-served: the read-only wizard audit runs the same checks FDEs would do manually and produces a fix-priority report, while a skills library and PostHog AI handle migrations and smaller questions. TAMs can self-serve pricing for two standard packages, PostHog Foundations 0-1 and Managed Migrations. If hands-on help is still needed, gather the customer's pain point, technical evidence like audit reports, rough price expectations, and access details before posting in the #team-fde Slack channel, where an FDE will scope the work.

Key points

  • Most setup requests don't need a paid FDE engagement.
  • The wizard audit auto-checks configs and outputs a prioritized report.
  • TAMs can self-price Foundations 0-1 and Managed Migrations packages.
  • Route non-standard cases to #team-fde with gathered context.
  • Include pain point, audit evidence, price shape, and timelines.

Contents

This page is for customer success managers, technical account managers (TAM), and anyone sitting on a customer who needs hands-on help setting up PostHog. It covers how to pull in a forward deployed engineer (FDE), and, just as often, how to sort the problem out without us.

New to FDE? Start with the overview. For our engagement lifecycle, see how we work.

First, try to self-serve

Most "we need help setting up PostHog" requests don't actually need an engagement. FDE work is often paid, so solving it yourself saves the customer both time and money.

Before anything else, point the customer at the PostHog wizard audit:

It runs the same checks our FDE team would do by hand. It's read-only, takes a few minutes, and produces a report (a Markdown file plus a shareable PostHog notebook) that names exactly what's wrong and what to fix first. Plenty of teams find their own engineers can take it from there.

We also have a growing library of PostHog skills you can point an agent at to do a lot of this yourself. They cover far more than audits: migrations, config cleanup, spotting what's misconfigured, and plenty else. Most work directly against a customer's instance through the MCP tools, so you don't always need their codebase to dig in. To run them against the customer's own data, do it on their behalf by impersonating their account.

For the smaller questions that come up along the way, PostHog AI and the PostHog Slack app handle most of them without needing to pull in a person.

Self-serve and price our standard FDE packages

For certain types of FDE engagements, TAEs / TAMs can self-serve and price standard packages without needing to wait for FDE to scope it out manually. Just let us know there's an opportunity on the horizon so we can factor it into our capacity planning.

  • PostHog Foundations 0-1, for customers in a PoC or trial who would benefit from FDE assistance to ensure they are production-ready from day 1. Use the PostHog Foundations 0-1 proposal builder.
  • Managed Migrations, for customers moving to PostHog from another product analytics tool. Use the Managed Migrations proposal builder.

If the customer doesn't fit a standard package, bring it to #team-fde instead.

If the customer still needs hands-on help

Before you route anything to us, gather the context we need to get started – it saves you a round-trip. If you find yourself blocked on answering any of the below, just bring it to us and we'll figure it out:

  • Start with the gap or pain point. What problem does the customer have today, and what's the business and operational context around it? It also helps to know the dynamic between the business side and their engineers.
    • If it's to implement a new use case, share your discovery. Tell us which use cases are in play now, how they could lead into others later, and one layer deeper on each: if they say they want Growth & Marketing, what do they actually mean, and why? The discovery questions in the library cover where the customer is today, what happens if they don't solve it, and what outcomes they want. Agreeing on a set number of use cases up front also helps us keep the scope of the technical work contained.
  • Add the technical evidence. Share the wizard audit reports, so we start from real evidence instead of a blank page, and a read on what's actually blocking them: engineering bandwidth, trust in the implementation, internal coordination, or "we want someone to do it for us."
  • Set rough expectations on price. Use the shape, not an exact number; see what it costs. If the customer wants an exact figure, tell them you'll get a quote within a day and route it through the account owner.
  • Confirm access and expected timelines. Let us know what codebase or data access is available, and give us a rough expected timeline (a week, a month, longer).

Once you have that, bring it straight to the Forward Deployed Engineering TeamForward Deployed Engineering Team team in #team-fde on Slack. We'll review it and come back to you with any questions. From there, an FDE picks it up and scopes it properly from the technical side.

Not sure if it's an FDE fit, or want help gathering this context from the customer? Just ask, even if you don't have everything yet, and we'll let you know.
