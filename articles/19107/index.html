Summary

This Rocketlane guide covers TSIA Vegas 2026, the Technology & Services World conference held October 19–21, 2026 at ARIA Resort & Casino in Las Vegas. TSIA Vegas 2026 is Technology & Services World, the conference run by the Technology and Services Industry Association. It takes place October 19–21, 2026, at ARIA Resort & Casino in Las Vegas.

The event brings together technology and services leaders across professional services, customer success, support, field services, operations, finance and go-to-market teams, with this year's agenda centered on the AI customer lifecycle.

If you're sending a team, October 12 is the date worth putting on the calendar. Groups of five or more qualify for discounted registration, with the discount increasing as the group gets larger.

TSIA stands for the Technology and Services Industry Association, a research and advisory organization focused on technology and services businesses.

It publishes research, benchmarks and frameworks across areas including professional services, customer success, support services, managed services, field services and revenue growth.

It's worth being precise because "TSIA" can also refer to completely unrelated things:

  • TSIA, the association: The Technology and Services Industry Association. This is the organization behind Technology & Services World.
  • TSIA, the Texas Success Initiative Assessment: A college-readiness assessment used in Texas.
  • TSIA / TSI agar: A microbiology test known as triple sugar iron agar.

If you're looking for the technology services conference in Las Vegas, the first one is the TSIA you want.

Technology & Services World is the current name for TSIA's flagship fall conference. Older pages, posts and event materials may still refer to it as TSIA World ENVISION, which is why you'll see both names in search.

For 2026, the confirmed event is Technology & Services World, taking place October 19–21 at ARIA Resort & Casino in Las Vegas.

So if you were searching for "TSIA World ENVISION 2026" or "TSIA Vegas 2026," you're looking for the same fall TSIA conference.

The 2026 agenda is organized around one central theme: the AI customer lifecycle.

Rather than treating AI as a standalone technology topic, the program looks at how AI changes the way technology companies design services, price and monetize them, and operate customer-facing functions.

That makes the agenda particularly relevant for teams working through questions such as:

  • Where should AI sit inside a services operating model?
  • How should services be priced as less work is tied directly to human hours?
  • What does outcome-based pricing require?
  • How should professional services, customer success and support roles evolve?
  • Where should humans remain involved as agents take on more execution?

Those questions run across all three tracks.

This track focuses on operating models, organizational design, Forward Deployed Engineers and how services portfolios change as AI becomes part of delivery.

It's particularly relevant for professional services, implementation and operations leaders deciding how work should get done as AI handles a larger share of execution.

This track focuses on the commercial side of the AI shift: packaging, pricing, margins, value metrics and moving beyond purely labor-based services models.

For leaders responsible for a services P&L, pricing strategy or commercial models, this is where many of the most immediate business questions sit.

The third track looks at running AI across support, professional services, customer success, education and field services.

The focus is not simply automating customer interactions. It's also about agent orchestration, human-AI collaboration and deciding where human judgment continues to matter.

Together, the three tracks cover how organizations design the model, monetize it and operate it across the customer lifecycle.

If you're attending with more than one person, splitting across tracks may be more useful than having the entire team follow the same agenda.

The 2026 keynote lineup also includes TSIA leaders J.B. Wood, Thomas Lah and George Humphrey, alongside speakers including Sanjay Kulkarni of SAP and Bernard Slowey of Salesforce.

The standard full-access pass for Technology & Services World 2026 is $3,150, while the limited-access pass is $2,100.

TSIA currently offers tiered group pricing:

  • 5–14 attendees: 20% off
  • 15–24 attendees: 25% off
  • 25+ attendees: 30% off

The entire group needs to be registered by October 12, 2026 to qualify.

If your company is already a TSIA member, check your membership benefits before purchasing as member pricing and conference vouchers may also apply.

The biggest difference between the two passes is Monday.

Full-access attendees get:

  • Monday meetups
  • Expert-led workshops
  • Curated Expo Tour
  • Welcome reception
  • Monday lunch
  • Tuesday and Wednesday keynotes, breakouts and expert-led sessions
  • Networking opportunities
  • Expo and reception access

Limited-access attendees get:

  • Monday's curated Expo Tour
  • Monday's welcome reception
  • Tuesday and Wednesday keynotes, breakouts and expert-led sessions
  • Networking opportunities
  • Expo and reception access
  • Tuesday and Wednesday lunch

So the limited pass is not restricted to one track. The decision largely comes down to whether Monday's full-day programming and workshops are worth the additional cost for your team.

Technology & Services World is being held at ARIA Resort & Casino.

For reservations made after September 16, TSIA says attendees can receive 10–20% off ARIA's prevailing room rate, subject to availability, along with a discounted $39-per-night resort fee.

When putting together an internal budget, the simplest calculation is:

Registration + hotel + flights + ground transportation = estimated cost per attendee

Then apply the group registration discount if five or more people from your company are attending.

Technology & Services World is particularly relevant if you're responsible for questions that sit at the intersection of services, AI and business outcomes.

You'll likely find the agenda useful if:

  • You own or influence a professional services or delivery P&L (profit and loss)
  • You're redesigning your operating model around AI
  • You're reconsidering services pricing, packaging or margins
  • You're exploring outcome-based commercial models
  • You're deciding how AI agents and human teams should work together
  • You need benchmark data or research to support an internal strategy
  • You lead customer success, support, professional services or another post-sale function

The event is more strategy and operating-model oriented than hands-on product training.

Ultimately, the best way to evaluate the agenda is to look at the problems your team is actively trying to solve and see how closely they map to the three tracks.
