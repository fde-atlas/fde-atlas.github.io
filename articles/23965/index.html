What skills are needed for an FDE? Prioritizing the abilities that truly work in the field

Original title: FDEに必要なスキルは？現場で本当に効く力の優先順位

FDE総研 · 2026-07-23 · 6 min read

Summary

This Japanese article ranks the skills a Forward Deployed Engineer needs, presenting them as a layered model: programming ability (Python) is the foundation, with LLM production skills (prompting, agent development) built on top, followed by customer-facing skills like discovery and communication, and finally high agency for pushing through ambiguous, unprecedented projects. It cites Palantir's blog to define the FDE role and maps Anthropic's FDE job requirements almost one-to-one onto the four layers. English requirements vary by employer—OpenAI's Tokyo FDE role requires Japanese-English bilingualism—while domain knowledge can be acquired on the job. The fastest way to grow, the author argues, is running small cycles of building something and delivering it to real customers.

Key points

  • Implementation skill is the prerequisite foundation for FDEs.
  • Four layers: coding, AI usage, customer handling, high agency.
  • Anthropic's FDE job posting mirrors the four-layer model.
  • English is employer-dependent; OpenAI Tokyo requires bilingualism.
  • Best growth comes from building and delivering to customers simultaneously.

What skills does an FDE need?

At its core, implementation skill is the foundation, on top of which customer engagement, domain understanding, and high agency (self-driven initiative) are layered. No single one is enough on its own; the essence of an FDE lies in the combination of "an engineer who can build things and who independently defines the customer's problems and carries them through to results."

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role that embeds itself in the customer's field and personally handles everything from workflow design to implementation and testing (Palantir official blog). Its distinguishing feature is that the engineer themselves takes on everything from problem definition to building the solution, rather than handing requirements off to someone else to build, as a salesperson or consultant would.

What kind of role is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)?

An FDE is an engineer who personally and consistently handles workflow design, implementation, and testing in the customer's field. This closeness determines the breadth of skills required of an FDE.

As Palantir's official blog describes, an FDE's work seamlessly connects customer meetings with the task of translating the issues gathered there into code. Unless you are an engineer who can understand the customer's words while also getting hands-on and validating things on the spot, you cannot keep up with the pace of the field. That is why the required skills cannot be narrowed down to just one; the premise is that multiple abilities overlap.

Why implementation skill is the foundation

Because you can build and run things yourself, you can quickly turn the customer's problems into concrete solutions. For an FDE, implementation skill is not "nice to have" but a prerequisite: "without it, you can't even start."

An FDE's job doesn't end with hearing the customer's problems; it extends to converting those problems into working software. Without implementation skill, no matter how accurately you grasp the problem, you cannot shape a solution and cannot even step through the doorway of delivering value. In particular, for FDEs in the generative AI space, strong Python implementation skills are repeatedly listed as a requirement. It is precisely because this foundation exists that customer engagement and domain understanding can be built on top of it.

How should you prioritize?

The practical priority is to build on the foundation of implementation skill, then layer on AI utilization, customer engagement, and self-driven initiative in that order. If you view it as a structure where lower layers support the upper ones, it becomes clear where to start training.

LayerSkillWhy it's needed
1. FoundationProgramming implementation skill (Python, etc.)To build and run things yourself
2. AI utilizationLLM production operations, prompt/agent developmentCore for FDEs in the generative AI space
3. Customer engagementdiscovery (drawing out problems), negotiation, explanationTo define the true problem
4. Self-driven initiativehigh agency (moving forward amid ambiguity)To move unprecedented projects forward

How to stack the four layers of skills

When you stack the four layers in order from the bottom, from implementation skill as the foundation up to self-driven initiative, your abilities develop as an integrated whole. The layers are not independent; the lower ones support the upper ones.

Without the foundation of implementation skill, AI utilization flounders; and even if you can implement and use AI, without customer engagement everything ends as "built but never used." And to carry an unprecedented project through to the end, high agency—the ability to move forward amid ambiguity—is indispensable. That is why, rather than fully completing one layer before moving to the next, the realistic approach is to build them up in layers with the lower layers as the axis.

What Anthropic's job posting requires

Anthropic's FDE job posting lists Python implementation skill, LLM production operations, high agency, and more as requirements. Reading the job requirements reveals the priority of needed skills as-is.

Anthropic's FDE job posting lists as requirements: strong Python implementation skills, LLM production operations experience (advanced prompt design and agent development), "high agency to move forward amid ambiguity," and strong communication skills for conducting discovery with customers (Anthropic official job posting). These correspond almost one-to-one with the four layers described above, and read in reverse, the job requirements serve as a table of skill priorities.

Where does AI utilization skill fit in?

For FDEs in the generative AI space, LLM production operations and prompt/agent development are central. AI utilization is a key skill positioned right above implementation skill.

What is required is not merely the ability to call a model, but the ability to run LLMs stably in production business workflows through prompt design and agent development. Only when you can handle LLMs on top of the foundation of implementation skill do proposals to solve customer problems with AI become realistic. AI utilization is not an independent skill; practically speaking, it should be understood as a skill that works in tandem with implementation skill.

How much English and domain knowledge do you need?

It depends on the company and the domain. Some postings require English, while others are domestic recruitments centered on Japanese. Rather than assuming one rule fits all, the right approach is to assess how necessary it is for each employer.

Which companies require English?

OpenAI's Tokyo-based FDE posting requires Japanese-English bilingualism, and requirements vary by company. At Japanese offices of global companies, English tends to be a given.

OpenAI's Tokyo-based FDE job posting states that being bilingual in Japanese and English is required (OpenAI official). This is because in organizations where the internal common language and documentation are in English, English proficiency tends to be a given. On the other hand, FDE-like roles at domestic companies sometimes recruit primarily in Japanese. It is more accurate to understand that English is not a "universal requirement for all FDEs," but that whether it is needed varies by the company and organization you apply to.

Can domain understanding be developed after the fact?

Domain understanding can be developed after the fact if you have the attitude of quickly getting up to speed on each project. You don't need to be an expert in the target industry at the time of joining.

FDEs often change target industries from project to project, and each time they are expected to understand and structure the industry's problems in a short period. In other words, what is needed is not knowledge of a specific industry itself, but "the ability to quickly understand an unfamiliar industry." This speed of learning, like implementation skill and high agency, can be treated as a skill honed through experience.

How do you develop these skills?

The fastest way is to simultaneously experience "building × delivering to customers" and keep running small cycles. Not leaning too far toward either implementation or client negotiation is the key to developing the skills an FDE needs as one integrated whole.

Running "building × delivering" simultaneously

Rather than focusing only on implementation or only on negotiation, even if small, building something yourself and delivering it to the field develops your skills as one integrated whole. The cycle of observing reactions and rebuilding is itself training.

Even if it's small, deliver something you built yourself to an actual field, observe the reactions, and rebuild—running this "building × delivering to customers" cycle simultaneously is the fastest way to grow. By running this cycle, implementation skills, AI utilization, customer handling, and high agency develop not separately but as one integrated whole. The experience of moving unprecedented projects forward is itself an opportunity to train high agency.
