Zhidx, May 18 — According to a Business Insider report, as the global tech industry layoff wave continues to spread and most general technology roles face downsizing and optimization, a batch of new roles centered on generative AI is rapidly emerging.

Among them, the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) has become one of the fastest-growing hot professions. Data shows that last April, the Indeed platform had only 643 forward deployed engineering job listings. Just one year later, that number has grown to 5,330, a surge of more than 7x year over year.

The role can pay an annual salary of up to $200,000 (about RMB 1.36 million). Leading overseas AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic are all continuously recruiting forward deployed engineering talent.

Beyond the FDE, the AI wave is also giving rise to a series of new professions such as AI evangelist, AI philosopher, Vibe Coder, and Chief AI Officer, with average annual salaries exceeding one million RMB.

01. Forward Deployed Engineer: Serving external enterprise clients

Responsible for integrating and customizing AI tools

According to Business Insider, citing public data from Indeed, job postings for Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) positions have exploded over the past year. In April 2025, there were only 643 related job listings on the Indeed platform; as of April this year, the number of postings had climbed to 5,330, a year-over-year increase of 729%, ranking among the fastest-growing tech roles.

▲ Cumulative growth in Forward Deployed Engineer job postings since January 2025.

The core work of a "Forward Deployed Engineer" is to truly integrate AI tools into a company's existing systems, workflows, and organizational processes, and to customize AI tools as needed.

These roles typically require both software engineering skills, an understanding of AI applications, and a certain degree of business communication and project management ability. Responsibilities include model deployment, system integration, workflow transformation, customizing to client needs, and optimizing the implementation of AI tools in real business scenarios.

This role was first widely popularized by Palantir Technologies. For a long time, Palantir has used an "embedded engineer" model, dispatching engineers directly to client sites to rapidly develop and deploy software systems based on actual needs. This model is now being adopted by more and more AI companies.

In terms of salary, the role also sits at the high end in overseas markets. Indeed data shows that Forward Deployed Engineers generally earn between $170,000 (about RMB 1.15 million) and $200,000 (about RMB 1.36 million) per year, with compensation rising further for senior practitioners.

Currently, AI commercialization is clearly accelerating, and many tech companies have begun expanding related teams. Overseas tech companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir Technologies, and Stripe are all continuously hiring forward deployed engineering talent.

Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian also said on LinkedIn that the company is further expanding hiring for this role, because enterprise customers and partners' demand for AI product deployment keeps growing, and the market urgently needs technical talent capable of implementing and customizing AI systems.

02. AI Accelerator: Focused on the enterprise's internal side

Helping employees become proficient with AI

While Forward Deployed Engineers mainly serve external enterprise clients, the AI Accelerator focuses on internal enablement, helping company employees become proficient at using AI in their daily work.

Currently, tech companies such as Stripe and Box are hiring for related roles. Stripe has embedded AI Accelerators in its marketing team to push employees toward routine use of AI at work; Box is hiring an AI Business Automation Engineer to oversee the integration and deployment of AI agents across the group.

The role requires solid AI expertise and teamwork skills, using AI technology to help teams achieve their work goals; applicants need at least 2 to 3 years of hands-on experience in IT or software engineering. In terms of salary, the role starts at $132,000 (about RMB 900,000) to $146,500 (about RMB 1 million).

03. AI Evangelist: Promoting AI technology externally

Starting salary higher than a communications director's

In the process of AI commercialization and promotion, brand communications and ecosystem building are crucial, and the AI Evangelist has emerged accordingly. These professionals are mainly responsible for promoting AI technology externally, serving as the company's brand ambassadors.

According to the report, Anthropic is hiring a "Claude Evangelist," hoping they will become the company's "image ambassador" in the startup ecosystem; Adobe is also hiring a "Business Architect and AI Evangelist." Currently, both OpenAI and Anthropic are expanding their communications teams, with Anthropic's communications team having grown threefold.

For communications management roles, companies typically require candidates to have nearly ten years of experience and deep connections with the media. Some roles also give priority to candidates with experience in government, developer ecosystems, or the startup world. For example, Anthropic's Claude Evangelist role requires candidates to have at least 7 years of experience as a startup founder or builder, as well as relevant experience working with developers.

In terms of salary, Indeed data shows that the average annual salary of a communications director in the US is about $106,000 (about RMB 720,000); while starting salaries for related communications roles at Anthropic and OpenAI have both exceeded $200,000 (about RMB 1.36 million), with the Claude Evangelist role's starting salary reaching $240,000 (about RMB 1.63 million).

04. AI Philosopher: In charge of AI ethics and values

Annual salary over 1.5 million

The development of AI has even activated the job market for philosophy majors.

Both Anthropic and Google DeepMind have established AI Philosopher roles, mainly responsible for issues related to AI ethics, safety, and values, ensuring that model behavior stays aligned with human value systems. Similar roles include AI ethicist and AI safety researcher. These roles typically require candidates to have experience in AI ethics, safety governance, and related areas.

Anthropic has even published a dedicated "Claude Constitution," detailing the value system it wants its AI models to follow.

While not every tech company will hire philosophers at scale, the philosophy world has indeed opened up a new path into the tech industry.

In terms of salary, a recent AI ethics and safety-related role at Google DeepMind has a base annual salary of $212,000 (about RMB 1.44 million) to $231,000 (about RMB 1.57 million).

05. AI Gig Work: Concentrated in model training and data labeling

Widely varying skill requirements

Some of the foundational work in the AI industry chain is increasingly showing a "gig-ification" trend, especially in model training and data labeling.

Companies such as Scale AI and Mercor have become major players in this field. Such work includes giving feedback on AI models' creative writing output, helping train translation capabilities, and even improving models' ability to express humor.

Meanwhile, platform companies including Uber, DoorDash, and Instawork have also begun offering gig tasks related to AI training. Users can provide training data for AI models by uploading videos and photos of household chores or everyday tasks.

Skill requirements vary significantly across roles. Scale AI and Mercor typically require candidates to hold a bachelor's degree and have some professional experience, while the threshold for related tasks on platforms like Uber and DoorDash is relatively lower, closer to the traditional gig model.

Depending on experience and task complexity, hourly pay for this type of work typically ranges from $15 (about RMB 102) to $200 (about RMB 1,360).

06. Vibe Coder: AI Gives Rise to a New Creative Programming Role

Assisted Development and Low-Code Tools

The proliferation of generative AI programming tools is giving rise to an entirely new career direction: the "Vibe Coder."

AI programming platforms like Lovable have already begun hiring Vibe Coders. Meanwhile, a growing number of tech companies have started explicitly requiring candidates to have "Vibe Coding" experience in their job postings.

For example, TikTok is hiring product designers who can use code and AI tools to rapidly create prototypes; YouTube is hiring an "AI Solutions Architect," requiring candidates to be able to use AI-assisted development and low-code tools to "bypass traditionally slow development cycles."

These roles typically still require a strong technical background. For example, YouTube's related role gives priority to applicants familiar with Python, SQL, and API integration, and also requires at least 6 years of product or project management experience.

Salaries vary considerably across roles. TikTok's related role starts at about $108,000 (about RMB 730,000), while YouTube's related role starts at $149,000 (about RMB 1.01 million).

07. AI Executives: AI Enters Corporate Management

"Chief AI Officer" Roles Are Rapidly Increasing

AI is also transforming the executive suite.

In July 2024, PwC announced the appointment of a "Chief AI Officer." Similarly, Accenture announced its first Chief AI Responsibility Officer in 2024. In 2025, Raymond James established the position of "Chief AI Architect."

It's not just corporate America—local governments are also hiring Chief AI Officers.

According to Glassdoor data, salaries for these roles typically range from $265,000 (about RMB 1.8 million) to $494,000 (about RMB 3.36 million). Government positions pay relatively less; for example, the "Chief AI Officer" position being recruited in Arkansas has a starting salary of just over $117,000 (about RMB 800,000).

08. Conclusion: AI Commercialization Advances

A Diverse Range of Roles Reaches a Development Inflection Point

From Forward Deployed Engineers, to AI evangelists and AI philosophers, to vibe coders and Chief AI Officers, AI is not only changing companies' products and business logic, but is also rapidly reshaping the talent structure and organizational systems of the tech industry.

On one side, traditional general technology roles continue to shrink; on the other, a large number of new professions centered on AI implementation, AI collaboration, and AI governance keep emerging. It is foreseeable that as more and more companies begin their intelligent transformation, talent who can understand business scenarios, drive AI commercialization, and solve real problems may become the most stable and most premium-earning category of profession in the future tech industry.
