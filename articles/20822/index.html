LangChain Forward Deployed Engineer Interviews Explained

Tech Team Strategy · 2026-11-17 · 4 min read

Summary

Stephen Chu, a deployed engineer at LangChain, explains in an Aced interview how the company hires forward deployed engineers. At LangChain the role spans pre- and post-sales as the technical owner of an account. Hiring involves a case project—building a fictional agent and presenting it to a customer with business impact in mind—and a behavioral interview testing hands-on generative AI experience, curiosity, and initiative. Chu notes the team includes engineers, MBAs, and former consultants, and that hiring loops are speeding up, giving candidates more leverage.

  • Interview 2 tests hands-on AI experience, enthusiasm, and agency.

Stephen Chu, a deployed engineer at LangChain, previously a software engineer at Google with a recent Stanford MBA, tells Aced (formerly Exponent) how LangChain hires forward deployed engineers (FDEs): what the role means there, the two main interviews, how to keep up with AI, and why people from non-traditional backgrounds should apply.

This article is our own summary, with short quotes, from the video's automatic transcript. Watch the full video above for everything that was said.

The short version

  • The role varies by company. Deployed engineer, forward deployed engineer, sales engineer and solutions architect mean different things in different places. At LangChain, deployed engineers cover pre- and post-sales as the technical owner of an account, from prospect to customer.
  • LangChain looks for fast learners and storytellers. You need to learn the commercial product (LangSmith), the open-source tools (LangChain, LangGraph), agent building and infrastructure debugging, and explain what is possible with AI.
  • Interview 1: a case project. Build a fictional agent and present it as if to a real customer, tying every choice to business outcomes.
  • Interview 2: behavioral, with a peer. Show you have built with generative AI, that AI excites you, and that you take initiative.
  • Time management counts. Don't spend 30 minutes on slides and five on the demo.
  • Backgrounds are diverse. His team includes software engineers, MBAs and former strategy and implementation consultants. "Don't count yourself out based on your background."

What LangChain does

Chu describes LangChain as building an end-to-end agent platform. It is best known for the open-source packages LangChain and LangGraph, sells LangSmith for observing, evaluating, monitoring and deploying agents, and recently released a no-code agent builder for less technical users.

Interview 1: the case project

It is "less about" solving an unseen algorithm on the spot, he says, and more project-based: learn the platform and tell a story of how a company would benefit from building on it. Candidates usually build a fictional agent and show why LangChain is the right platform and how the agent improves over time across its development life cycle.

The prompt usually describes a company's AI initiative and suggests features to explore. The test is judgment: which features would actually move metrics such as retention, spend or customer satisfaction, not which are merely cool. You won't have every detail, so state your assumptions, just as with a real customer you have spoken to only once. He stresses time management: balance slides and demo, and don't rush half the presentation in the last five minutes.

Interview 2: behavioral

Usually with a teammate, plus a hiring manager screen. Interviewers look for:

  • Hands-on generative AI experience, at work or in projects.
  • Genuine interest: AI moves so fast that keeping up is part of the job.
  • Agency: projects you took on that weren't assigned, problems you found and fixed. As the people closest to customers, deployed engineers are the biggest power users of the platform; Chu often pushes fixes to the production code base himself.
  • Personality and energy, and the ability to connect your experience to business impact.

Keeping up with AI

His highest-signal sources are The Information, venture capital podcasts from firms such as Sequoia, FirstMark and Redpoint, sometimes 20VC, and LangChain's own podcast with people building agents in production. He suggests picking a few sources and following them regularly rather than trying to read everything, and he asks ChatGPT and Claude questions whenever one comes to mind: "using AI to learn about AI."

Advice for candidates

  • Find out what the role really is. At LangChain it is broad pre- and post-sales work; in the traditional Palantir model you might badge into a customer's on-premise deployment and debug their clusters. Decide whether you want breadth across many companies or deep, hands-on production work.
  • Expect projects and some rejections. Every company tests different things.
  • Loops are moving faster. LangChain is cutting unnecessary steps to hire more deployed engineers, which gives candidates more leverage.
  • Your background can be an asset. With AI tools to help you ramp up, agency and enthusiasm matter more than deep specialist skills.

What it means for your business

  • Define the role before you hire. "Forward deployed engineer" covers very different jobs.
  • Test with a realistic case: a demo for a fictional customer, judged on business impact and time management.
  • Hire for agency. People who fix problems unasked are what make customer-facing engineering work.
  • Look beyond software engineers. Consultants and MBAs with technical curiosity can do this job well.
  • Keep your hiring loop short. Strong candidates have options.
