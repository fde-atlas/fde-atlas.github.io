Today's Judgment

What deserves the most attention today is not a new model release, but three pieces of evidence from different dimensions pointing to the same conclusion: enterprise AI adoption is entering the organizational stage—the tools are good enough, but rule codification, role stratification, and scaling replication are the real bottlenecks.

  • Palantir's Q2 revenue grew 93% year over year, with US commercial revenue up 149%, proving that enterprises are paying for implementation services that "put AI into the business"
  • The MIIT's published list of typical AI application cases provides a trackable project radar for industrial agents
  • Practice from multiple Chinese enterprise AI training programs shows that business owners and execution backbone staff must be designed as distinct roles with separate learning paths

Key Signals

Palantir Q2 2026 earnings: enterprise AI adoption is forming a real procurement market

  • Source: Palantir Investor Relations | Original link
  • On August 3 (after US market close), Palantir reported Q2 revenue of approximately $1.935 billion, up 93% year over year; US commercial revenue grew 149% year over year. The company, famous for its FDE model, is rapidly entering enterprises with its core product AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform) through Bootcamp-style on-site co-creation.
  • Why it matters: Palantir's growth proves one thing—enterprises are not just "trying out AI"; they are paying for implementation systems that integrate data, decisions, and operations. For FDE practitioners in China, the worthwhile comparison is not the valuation, but how Palantir turns diagnosis, on-site co-creation, system integration, and continuous iteration into a procurable long-term service. The earnings reflect the company's overall business, and not all growth can be attributed to the FDE role alone.

MIIT's typical AI application cases: a project radar for industrial agents

  • Source: MIIT | Original link
  • The MIIT recently published its 2025 list of typical AI application cases, covering industrial agent scenarios such as "AI furnace operators," drilling optimization, hot rolling scheduling, refining and chemical planning, fault diagnosis, and financial intelligence, spanning steel, petrochemical, manufacturing, and finance industries.
  • Why it matters: This list provides a trackable project map of industrial AI adoption in China. However, the list only proves supply direction and application scenarios, not individual ROI. FDE practitioners should track each case's applying organization, deployment timeline, production metrics, and implementation teams, turning "being on the list" into "verifiable evidence of results."

Role stratification in enterprise AI training: owners and execution backbone staff are not the same kind of user

  • Source: Practice from multiple Chinese enterprise AI training programs
  • Feedback from multiple AI training programs targeting small and medium business owners shows: many business owners born in the 1970s and early 1980s are not proficient with computers and typically need two or three passes when first encountering AI tools; some owners prefer to only understand the logic and delegate tool learning to subordinates. Course content received positive reviews, but there is a clear gap between "the owner has learned it" and "the enterprise is using it."
  • Why it matters: Enterprise AI education cannot treat decision-makers and executors as the same kind of user. A more implementable model is a three-role closed loop: the owner sets strategy, scenarios, and acceptance criteria; the execution backbone handles tool operation and workflow building; the FDE enters the enterprise to do diagnosis, deployment, and continuous review. Training is only the entry point; the real value lies in on-site implementation after the training.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Benchmark Palantir not for its valuation, but for its service structure: turn diagnosis, co-creation, system integration, and continuous iteration into a procurable long-term service, rather than one-off tool training.
  • Use the MIIT list as a source of customer leads: track the applying organizations in each case and proactively reach out to learn about deployment progress and follow-up needs. These enterprises have already demonstrated willingness to adopt AI and may need FDEs to help push them to the production stage.
  • Enterprise training must be followed by "implementation services": if the engagement ends when the training ends, the enterprise most likely will not implement on its own. Design a continuous service chain of "training → scenario diagnosis → on-site deployment → review and iteration" to convert training insights into business results.
