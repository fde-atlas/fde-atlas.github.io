Where do you stand as a Forward Deployed Engineer?

FDE Council · 2026-10-24 · 2 min read

Summary

The FDE Council has released draft v0.3 of an open Professional Capability Framework for forward deployed engineers, along with a browser-based self-assessment. It uses 22 questions, two per core domain, across 11 domains scored at Associate, Practitioner, and Lead levels; the overall level is the highest reached in at least 8 of 11 domains. AI and agent engineering is context-required and unscored, while tool stacks, hiring practices, and industry clearances sit outside the framework. Answers stay local in the browser. The Council stresses this is a self-reflection tool, not a credential or hiring test, and invites feedback to shape the next draft.

Key points

  • Self-assessment has 22 questions covering 11 core domains, two each.
  • Levels are Associate, Practitioner, Lead, based on typical work, not best days.
  • Overall level requires reaching it in at least 8 of 11 domains.
  • AI and agent engineering is unscored; tools, hiring, clearances are excluded.
  • Results stay in the browser; it is a reflection tool, not a credential.

About this self-assessment

It’s built on the FDE Council Professional Capability Framework v0.3, a draft open for comment. The check scores the 11 core domains. AI and agent engineering is context-required and is not a twelfth score. Levels describe scope and behaviour, not years of experience.

Your overall level is the highest level you reach in at least 8 of the 11 domains. Each domain’s level is the average of its two answers.

This is a self-reflection tool, not a credential, test or hiring instrument. It hasn’t yet been validated through a job task analysis, and self-ratings are only a starting point. You’ll get the most from it by discussing your result with a manager, mentor or peer. Employers can use the domains for structured hiring: see How should employers assess FDE skills?
