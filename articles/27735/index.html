Today's Judgment

What's most worth seizing today isn't how many more models have appeared, but that three production prerequisites for enterprise AI are being spelled out more clearly:

Data side—The National Data Administration revealed that China now has over 126,000 high-quality datasets, with supply shifting from construction volume toward registration and productized circulation; Method side—Manufacturing frontline teams are starting to clarify AI division of labor: machine learning handles computation, digital twins handle verification, and large models handle lookup/explanation/recommendations; Business side—Uber disclosed that over 70% of PRs are attributed to agents, and has begun managing its agent factory using "cost per outcome."

The core work of the Forward Deployed Engineer is shifting from "connecting AI" to three things: making results trustworthy, making actions controllable, and making every qualified task economically viable.

Key Signals

National Data Administration: Over 126,000 high-quality datasets, but "having data" doesn't mean "usable for enterprise tasks"

  • Source: National Data Administration | Original link
  • On August 29, the National Data Administration disclosed: as of August 2026, China has over 126,000 high-quality datasets with a total volume exceeding 1,815 PB, up more than 89% from the end of Q1; the National High-Quality Dataset Management Service System launched in April has published over 1,700 datasets, and the data marketplace has aggregated 95 data enterprises and 253 data products.
  • Why it matters: High-quality datasets are moving from "construction volume" toward "registration, publication, and productized circulation"—this is substantive progress in AI data infrastructure. But what enterprises actually lack on the ground is usually not "whether datasets exist," but whether tasks can find the correct version, obtain authorization, match business objects, pass quality regression, and write back to production systems. Turning macro-level data supply into task-level "data contracts" is precisely the translation engineering that is the FDE's work. Note: the 126,000 figure is a national supply-side statistic and does not represent enterprise production adoption.

Manufacturing AI begins clarifying division of labor: machine learning handles computation, digital twins handle verification, large models handle lookup, explanation, and recommendations

  • Source: Manufacturing ToB frontline practice sharing
  • An industry expert with 28 years of manufacturing experience recently shared a production structure for manufacturing AI: machine learning handles computation, digital twins handle simulation verification, and large models primarily handle lookup, explanation, and recommendations. A POC at a large equipment manufacturing group was completed within one month, used for cross-factory routing, capacity, and delivery-time verification; practice at a commercial vehicle company showed that enterprise RAG, collaborative office bots, task dispatch, reminders, and MCP system actions can form a closed loop—high-risk plans and system modifications still require human confirmation.
  • His definition of the FDE is also precise—a dual role: delivering on-site externally, while internally distilling common capabilities from the first 30 customers so the 31st customer can reuse them directly. POCs should be paid for, and accepted against timeliness, human involvement, business outcomes, and No-Go conditions.
  • Why it matters: The easiest mistake in industrial AI is having the large model both do the computation and prove its own computation is correct. A structure that can actually enter production should separate: deterministic computation → simulation/rule verification → large model explanation → human confirmation or controlled execution → results written back. Large model explanation cannot replace deterministic computation, and a polished demo cannot replace verification under real operating conditions. This aligns with this site's earlier judgment that "deterministic tasks use scripts, uncertain tasks use agents"—industrial scenarios draw this line even more finely.

Uber: Over 70% of PRs attributed to agents, begins managing the agent factory with "cost per outcome"

  • Source: Uber Engineering | Original link
  • On August 27, Uber disclosed its internal agent production data: PRs participated in/attributed to local and cloud agents have exceeded 70%; internally there are over 3,600 Agent Skills, executed more than 30,000 times daily. From February to August 2026, weekly active users across agents grew 7x and weekly requests grew 9.4x, while total AI spend has remained relatively stable since April—on a same-model basis, cost per thousand requests dropped nearly 34%, and cost per session fell 52% from its peak. Context engineering details: its gateway manages over 1,000 MCP servers; directly injecting tool schemas would consume 50,000-70,000 tokens, so they switched to CLI and tool retrieval.
  • Why it matters: Once agents scale, the object of governance is no longer model unit price, but "how many sessions, turns, requests, tokens, human reviews, and failure rollbacks a qualified task goes through." Uber's six-factor cost accounting can be directly transferred to any enterprise project: active users × sessions per user × turns per session × requests per turn × tokens per request × token unit price = total cost, with merged PRs/resolved tickets/business sign-offs as the outcome denominator. Only when cost and outcome are on the same ledger does scale growth become manageable—usage grew 9x while spend stayed flat, thanks to this accounting, not luck. Note: all figures are self-reported by Uber; "PR attributed to agents" does not mean the code was fully completed independently by agents.

Additional signal: Shanghai Hongkou disclosed progress on the Yangtze River Delta FDE Action Program—20 above-scale enterprises were selected from over 100 applicant companies and matched one-on-one with 20 FDE experts, using a "business problem diagnosis → FDE matching → on-site co-creation" model (Hongkou District Government). 100 applicants show that enterprise-side demand is real, and the one-on-one matching model is worth tracking through the acceptance results of the first batch of projects.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Add "triple trustworthiness acceptance" to manufacturing projects: computational accuracy, simulation/rule explainability, and version regression after business changes—if any layer fails, do not proceed to controlled execution.
  • Practice the FDE dual role: after every on-site delivery, ask yourself—how many of this engagement's exceptions, rules, and interfaces have been consolidated into assets the next customer can use directly? The quality of distillation from the first 30 customers determines the delivery speed for the 31st.
  • Build a six-factor cost ledger for every agent project: active users, sessions, turns, requests, tokens, unit price, then divide by the number of qualified tasks. When a customer asks "how much did AI actually cost, and was it worth it," this table is the answer.
  • POCs should be paid for: accept against timeliness, human involvement, business outcomes, and No-Go conditions—free POCs cannot earn genuine commitment from the business side, and POCs without genuine commitment cannot validate anything.
