FDE Salary in Vietnam: English Drives a 60% Gap

FDE Times · 2026-10-09 · 7 min read

Summary

A 2026 salary guide from recruiter Second Talent claims Vietnamese FDEs can earn 60% more if they can handle client-facing English, though the figure is unverified. Domestic FDE bands run from VND 20–35 million monthly at junior level—below many ordinary developers—to VND 95–120 million for leads. FDEs working remotely for US, European and Australian clients earn VND 65–138 million+, with the ceiling above the domestic lead level. The article argues technical skill is not the bottleneck; running client meetings independently is. It also lists US benchmarks (Palantir $125k–200k, OpenAI $185k–300k plus equity, Anthropic $280k–320k) and warns readers to compare base against base, keeping equity separate.

Key points

  • Second Talent claims client-facing English drives a 60% pay gap among Vietnamese FDEs, but the figure is unverified.
  • Domestic FDE pay spans VND 20–120 million monthly; junior levels sit below average developer pay.
  • Remote FDEs serving US/EU/Australia clients earn VND 65–138 million+, exceeding domestic lead ceilings.
  • The differentiator is running client meetings alone, not general English or extra frameworks.
  • US FDE bands range from Palantir's $125k–200k to Anthropic's $280k–320k; compare base salary only.

In brief

  • According to Second Talent, domestic FDEs in Vietnam earn VND 20–35 million a month at junior level and VND 65–95 million at senior level; FDEs working remotely for US, European and Australian companies earn VND 65–138 million+ a month.
  • The remote band starts exactly at the domestic senior floor but goes beyond the VND 120 million ceiling for leads. The recruiter sees client-facing English as the main reason for the gap.
  • In the US, posted FDE bands run from $125,000–200,000 (Palantir) to $280,000–320,000 (Anthropic). When comparing, put base against base and keep equity separate.

Two Vietnamese engineers with the same technical profile can still be paid 60% apart. That is the view of Second Talent, a recruitment firm, in its 2026 FDE salary guide for Vietnam. In its reading, technical skill is not the bottleneck; the main reason is the ability to work with clients in English at senior level.

The 60% figure comes from a firm that sells recruitment services and has not been independently verified, so it is best treated as a signal. Second Talent itself notes that Vietnamese companies are not yet hiring for the FDE title in large numbers. People doing the role are usually paid on the bands for senior AI and platform engineers.

If you are aiming for an FDE role from Vietnam, or from any non-English-speaking market, this bears directly on how you spend the next six months. On the recruiter’s reading, the thing that creates the largest earnings gap is not learning another framework.

The FDE title carries less of a premium than it sounds

Second Talent sets out 2026 FDE salary bands in Vietnam across four levels: junior VND 20–35 million a month, mid-level VND 35–65 million, senior VND 65–95 million and lead/principal VND 95–120 million. These are fixed monthly salaries.

For a benchmark, a TopDev report cited by VnEconomy puts average pay for Vietnamese developers in 2024 at roughly $1,100–3,000 a month, or about VND 27 million to more than VND 73 million, depending on skills and experience.

Set side by side, junior FDEs sit at the lower end of the general range, in part below the VND 27 million mark. Mid-level FDEs fall squarely within it. Only at senior level does the VND 65 million floor come close to the ceiling for developers in general. The gap is real, but smaller than the “FDE” title suggests.

The two data sets are also two years apart. According to an ITviec report cited by VIR in January 2025, average pay for IT staff in Vietnam rose 27.9% in a year, largely because more experienced people entered the market.

Part of the gap between the two data sets may therefore simply reflect when the surveys were taken.

The ceiling is in remote contracts

The clearest difference appears when the client is abroad. Second Talent gives a band of VND 65–138 million+ a month for FDEs in Vietnam working remotely for companies in the US, Europe and Australia. The band is not split by level. Its floor is exactly the domestic senior floor, while its ceiling exceeds the VND 120 million ceiling for lead/principal.

For this group, Second Talent states that clients require strong written English and a few hours of overlapping working time each day.

Arc offers another reference point, drawn from the salary expectations self-reported by remote developers in Vietnam in general, not FDEs specifically. The average is about $45,848 a year, and about $75,369 a year for seniors.

Arc also observes that in non-English-speaking countries, people with strong English can usually command pay above the local norm.

Why does English matter more for FDEs?

A backend developer on a distributed team can work mostly through pull requests, tickets and asynchronous messages. Written English that is clear enough gets the job done.

FDEs cannot easily work that way, because the role is built around working closely with clients. It is no surprise, then, that Second Talent singles out client-facing English rather than English in general. In its view, the technical interview round does not separate candidates; a conversation with a client does.

Picture two engineers building the same RAG pipeline for a logistics company in Australia. The first writes good code but needs a PM alongside in every meeting.

The second runs the discovery session alone and tells the client directly that their current data is not good enough for the feature they want, so a smaller piece should come first. With the second engineer, the client needs nobody in the middle, and has a reason to pay more for that.

Market demand leans the same way. According to ITviec via VIR, English was the soft skill employers looked for most in the first half of 2025, emphasised by 40.3% of companies.

An older ITviec survey, published in 2018, found that 85% of companies rated Vietnamese engineers below their foreign peers in English. That figure is dated and cannot explain today’s gap on its own; it only shows that English has long been flagged as a weakness.

Do not compare your pay with Anthropic’s on instinct

US figures are easy to be dazzled by. According to job postings read directly by Valletta Software on 8 September 2026, Anthropic lists a band of $280,000–320,000 for FDEs. OpenAI lists $185,000–300,000 in base salary plus equity for FDEs on its commercial side.

Palantir, where the FDE role originated, lists $125,000–200,000 in base salary.

Even these bands are not calculated the same way. According to Aced, Anthropic’s band already includes target bonus, while for Palantir, Valletta notes that equity and bonus sit outside the band. So when comparing, put base salary against base salary and list equity on a separate line.

The same rule applies to any offer you receive: ask which currency you will be paid in, how much is fixed and how much depends on performance.

A remote contract signed from Vietnam will not be paid on a San Francisco pay scale. These figures show only where the FDE role is posted in the US market, not what you should expect.

How to reach the upper tier

If the gap sits at the ceiling, the task is to prove you can stand in front of a client without an interpreter or a PM at your side. Practise the situations the job will actually throw at you: asking questions to clarify requirements, turning down an out-of-scope request, explaining why a demo broke. General conversational English is not enough.

When reading a job description, look for phrases such as “customer-facing” and “client-facing”, time-zone overlap requirements or travel to client sites. Those lines tell you the company is paying for the communication part, and that is where you have room to negotiate.

On your CV, do not write “good English communication skills”. Write a line with context: what kind of client you worked with, in which country, which meeting you ran yourself and what decision came out of it. Recruiters need evidence that you have stood in front of a client on your own.

In this market, the same code can command two different salaries. Which one is yours gets decided in the meeting room with the client, not in a pull request.
