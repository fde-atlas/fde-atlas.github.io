Today's Take

Two public signals today—one from a Chinese ministry, one from an overseas vendor—each answer a fundamental question about enterprise AI:

"How should this scenario be positioned?"—The Ministry of Transport has organized the first batch of 41 "AI + Transportation" flagship scenarios into three categories: application rollout, innovation demonstration, and breakthrough R&D, with different types matched to different lead parties, data requirements, and acceptance criteria.

"How do we leave evidence along the way?"—Anthropic released a handbook for the AI-native software development lifecycle, turning the deliverables of each stage into inputs and an audit chain for the next stage.

One governs "choosing the right scenario," the other governs "leaving evidence." Put together, they capture the essential difference between an FDE and "someone who deployed an AI tool."

Key Signals

Ministry of Transport launches creation of the first batch of 41 "AI + Transportation" flagship scenarios

  • Source: Digital China Summit | Original article; Action plan and construction guidelines
  • At a special press briefing on August 20, the Ministry of Transport disclosed that it has released construction guidelines for the first batch of 41 key flagship scenarios across 10 directions, including intelligent driving, smart highways, intelligent railways, and smart postal services, officially launching the creation of the first batch of projects. Tasks are organized into three categories: application rollout, innovation demonstration, and breakthrough R&D. Public figures: over 16,000 unmanned delivery vehicles and more than 400 drones already deployed in smart postal services; 56,000 cameras connected along high-speed rail lines, with nearly 310,000 valid alerts handled to date.
  • Why it matters: The "three-category organization" is the mechanism in this policy most worth borrowing for enterprises—a scenario is not just an "AI idea." First determine whether it belongs to mature rollout (mature technology + clear business model + widespread demand), integrated demonstration (multi-system/multi-party integration validation), or critical breakthrough (unresolved data, model, or process bottlenecks), then match it with the corresponding lead party, data, acceptance criteria, and accountability. Internal AI projects should likewise build a "three-tier scenario card": rollout-type projects move fast, demonstration-type projects first document integration requirements, and breakthrough-type projects first document risks and human takeover. Mixing all three types into a single schedule is a common starting point for project failure.

Anthropic releases AI-native SDLC handbook: deliverables at every stage become an audit chain

  • Source: Anthropic official | Original article
  • On August 21, Anthropic released a handbook for the AI-native software development lifecycle, restructuring the six stages—plan, design, build, test, deploy, maintain—into a cyclical process: intent.md, spec.md, plan.md, code and tests, PR review findings, and incident records are committed to version control in sequence, forming a traceable chain.
  • Key points of the method: after AI-accelerated coding, the bottlenecks shift to planning, review/testing, and deployment; governance should be enforced while agents execute—through Skills, Hooks, continuous evals, and human approval gates—rather than only through a final human review.
  • Why it matters: This approach can be fully transferred to non-code enterprise AI projects—chaining "original requirements → rules/scenario cards → deliverables → test records → client acceptance → incident retrospectives" into a versioned chain. When a client asks "why was this rule defined this way in the first place," being able to pull up the original record is trust; not being able to is a dispute.

Takeaways for FDE Practitioners

  • Case numbers must pass a "double-ledger" review: The case ledger—original evidence → calculation methodology → time window → client authorization → external version; the asset ledger—first-development investment → ownership → client-specific portions → maintenance responsibility → reuse revenue sharing. Content that fails the double-ledger review is not promoted, not quoted, and not counted toward performance. Inflating the savings in a client case is the fastest way to destroy an FDE's brand.
  • Write module asset ownership into the contract before the project starts: Who bears the first-development cost of reusable modules, who owns the intellectual property, and how revenue is shared when those modules are later reused for other clients—the more standardized the business, the more important it is to spell these out upfront; otherwise, the bigger the scale, the bigger the risk.
  • Build a three-tier scenario card for every project: Manage rollout, demonstration, and breakthrough scenarios separately. For the latter two, document data requirements, risks, acceptance criteria, and human takeover before committing to delivery timelines.
