05 Business Interviews and Customer Discovery

Original title: 05 业务访谈与客户发现

FDE中国社区 · 2026 · 3 min read

Summary

This chapter from the FDE China community explains how forward deployed engineers run business interviews and customer discovery, turning vague requests like "we want AI" into concrete processes, pain points, data sources, permissions, risks, and acceptance criteria. A customer-service chatbot case shows how probing questions narrow scope into a deliverable task with measurable evals. It covers interview targets, JTBD framing, shadowing frontline users, three-layer questioning, and a documentation template.

Key points

  • Discovery is an engineering activity, not sales small talk.
  • A vague chatbot request can narrow into ticket classification and reply drafts.
  • Interview six roles: management, frontline users, process, data, admins, compliance.
  • Shadowing captures waits, switching, re-entry, judgment, and exception handling.
  • Drill down to acceptance criteria and responsibility to prevent scope creep.

Chapter Objectives

Master the FDE business interview methodology, translating a customer's "we want AI" into real processes, pain points, data, permissions, risks, and acceptance criteria.

Why It Matters

Enterprise AI projects often fail not because the models aren't strong enough, but because the real business was never understood at the start. An FDE's discovery is an engineering activity, not sales small talk.

Opening Case: The Customer Says "I Want an Intelligent Customer Service Bot"

A customer asks for "an intelligent customer service bot," which sounds like a RAG or Agent project. But an FDE shouldn't immediately start building a knowledge base; instead, they should probe: How many tickets does customer service handle per day? What are the most common questions? Which questions must be escalated to humans? Does a wrong reply lead to compensation or complaints? Where can order status be looked up? Who maintains the FAQ? How is customer satisfaction currently measured?

After probing, you may discover that what's truly worth building is not a "general-purpose chatbot," but "classification, knowledge recommendation, and reply drafting for after-sales policy tickets." At this point the scope shrinks, the data becomes clearer, and the evals can be written: classification accuracy, first-response time, agent adoption rate, and accuracy of high-risk escalation to humans.

That's the value of customer discovery: breaking a vague wish into a business task that can be delivered, evaluated, and piloted.

Interview Subjects

  • Management: goals, budget, priorities, and acceptance methods.
  • Frontline users: real operations, workarounds, pain points, and sources of distrust.
  • Process owners: process definitions, approval nodes, and responsibility boundaries.
  • Data owners: data sources, quality, permissions, and update frequency.
  • System administrators: interfaces, accounts, deployment, and security restrictions.
  • Compliance owners: sensitive data, audit trails, audits, and regulatory requirements.

Core Questions

  • Who uses it? Who reviews it? Who bears final responsibility?
  • How is it done now? Which systems, spreadsheets, or documents are used at each step?
  • Where is it slow? Where do errors occur? Where is rework needed? Where does human judgment come in?
  • Where is the data? Who can access it? How often is it updated?
  • What happens when something goes wrong? Who has the authority to change it? Who has the authority to release it?
  • What counts as success? How much time saved, how much improvement, what risks reduced?

FDE Working Method

After interviews, consolidate the information into five types of materials:

  • Process diagrams.
  • Pain point lists.
  • Data inventories.
  • Risk lists.
  • Sample libraries.

Don't take the customer's "automation" at face value as a requirement; break it down into retrieval, extraction, summarization, recommendation, reminders, approvals, or execution.

JTBD: Identifying Real Jobs

Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) helps shift from "feature imagination" back to the progress users are trying to make:

When I am in [a situation], I want to accomplish [a job], so that I get [an outcome].

Example: A customer service agent doesn't want to "own a chatbot"; rather, when a customer presses on complex policies, they want to quickly find trustworthy answers, reduce anxiety about making mistakes, and maintain a professional image. The more specific the situation, job, and outcome, the clearer the technology choices become.

Shadowing

The process users describe is the "standard process"; the process users actually follow is the "real process." Shadowing requires observing a frontline worker completing a real task:

  • Which windows they open, where they copy and paste.
  • When they switch to IM to ask colleagues, which fields they judge from experience.
  • How they bypass the system when exceptions occur.

Focus on recording five types of signals: waiting, switching, duplicate data entry, manual judgment, exception handling. Observation is not about catching violations, but about uncovering tacit knowledge that formal processes don't capture.

Three Levels of Interview Probing

A good FDE interview isn't about finishing the question list, but drilling down layer by layer:

LevelProbing DirectionExample
Surface-level needWhat do you want AI to do"We'd like automated report generation"
Current processWho does what in which system today"An operations colleague copies data from 5 spreadsheets every day"
Acceptance and responsibilityHow do you judge whether the generated output is usable"Metrics must match the manual daily report, and anomalies must be traceable to the source data"

If you stop at the first level, scope creep is inevitable later; only by probing to the third level can the FDE design scenario cards, eval samples, and launch gates.

Hands-on Task

Design an interview guide for a target scenario, preparing at least:

  • 5 management questions.
  • 8 frontline user questions.
  • 5 data and system questions.
  • 5 compliance and risk questions.

Deliverables

  • Interview guide.
  • Interview Notes Template
  • Post-interview materials checklist.

Common Pitfalls

  • Only interviewing management, not frontline users.
  • Only listening to formal processes, not probing real operations.
  • Only asking what they want, not how things are done now.
  • Promising too early that AI can fully automate the work.

Checklist

  • Have you identified the real users and reviewers?
  • Have you found the current alternative solution?
  • Do you know where the data lives?
  • Are success criteria and failure responsibilities clearly defined?
