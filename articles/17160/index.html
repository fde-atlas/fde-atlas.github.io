Why Did the FDE Market Expand So Rapidly? Reading 2026 Through Numbers on Job Openings, Investment, Acquisitions, and Performance

Original title: FDE市場はなぜ急拡大したのか｜求人・投資・買収・業績の数字で読む2026年

TIMEWELL · 2026-09-15 · 11 min read

Summary

This TIMEWELL article uses four sets of numbers—job postings, investment, acquisitions, and financial results—to explain why the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) market expanded rapidly in 2025–2026. Indeed data shows FDE postings up 5,230% over 16 months. OpenAI launched a deployment company with over $4 billion in initial funding and acquired Tomoro for roughly 150 FDEs; AWS committed $1 billion to embed FDEs with customers; Anthropic formed a joint venture with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs, plus a partnership with FIS. Palantir's Q2 2026 results (revenue up 93%, 47% GAAP operating margin) show the FDE model can reach software-level margins when learning feeds back into product. The author attributes the timing to three causes: reports showing most enterprise AI investments lack measurable returns, agents requiring on-site domain knowledge, and coding agents lowering first-day delivery costs. A final section warns that hype masks imitations and offers three questions to distinguish genuine FDE work from rebranded contracting.

Key points

  • FDE job postings grew 5,230% in 16 months per Indeed data.
  • OpenAI put over $4 billion into a deployment company and bought Tomoro.
  • AWS committed $1 billion to embed FDEs at customer companies.
  • Palantir's revenue rose 93% with a 47% GAAP operating margin.
  • Three tests separate real FDE work from rebranded contracting.

Hello, this is Hamamoto from TIMEWELL, Inc.

This is the eighth article in the FDE practice series. The previous seven articles covered on-the-ground techniques and how to run a week. This time, I shift the perspective and read, with numbers, why this role suddenly spread between 2025 and 2026. In the article What is an FDE, I lined up the milestone events; in this article, I focus on four sets of numbers—job postings, investment, acquisitions, and business results—and cite them from primary sources.

To state upfront: the numbers show the heat of the market, but heat does not distinguish the real thing from the name alone. So in the final chapter, I write about what the numbers do not say—that is, how to tell apart the real thing from something that merely put the "FDE" label on contract development. If you want to check first whether your own team can operate in the FDE mold, please use the AI Literacy Diagnostic.

The job posting numbers. 5,230% in 16 months

First, job postings. According to Indeed data reported by Business Insider in May 2026, job postings for Forward Deployed Engineer were up 543% from January 2025 as of April 2025, and up 5,230% from January 2025 as of April 2026. On a year-over-year basis, that is an increase of roughly 729% 1. The base figure was small, so the rate is inflated, but a role that grows more than 50-fold in 16 months is almost unheard of elsewhere.

This movement had already begun in the first half of 2025. In a June 2025 essay, a16z's Joe Schmidt called the FDE "the hottest job in startups," writing that at the time 22 of OpenAI's 311 public job openings were FDE or solutions-related roles 2. It was the first sign that the side building the models had started hiring people to put the models into the field.

In Japan as well, things have been moving since the start of 2026. In August, a chaos map organizing domestic companies offering FDE-style support was published, categorizing 38 companies into seven segments. It was compiled based solely on the descriptions on official websites and press releases, noting each company's start date, presence of case studies, and pricing and contract terms 3. The figure of 38 companies shows that a market that did not exist a year ago has entered the stage where clients compare vendors.

The investment numbers. $4 billion, $1 billion, and a joint venture with investment firms

Next, investment. From spring to summer 2026, the side building the models and the side providing the infrastructure placed capital on implementation. Here is the timeline.

Accenture's announcement is suggestive in its wording. It states that the barrier to AI adoption lies not in a lack of technology, but in the fact that engineering expertise is not being exercised in the field 4. It is a declaration that a consulting firm is shifting its focus from reports to on-the-ground engineering.

Anthropic's two announcements came out on the same day. One is the joint venture with investment firms, which states that it targets companies such as regional banks, mid-market manufacturers, and regional medical institutions—companies that would benefit from AI but lack the manpower to build and run cutting-edge deployments themselves 5. The other is the partnership with FIS, which explicitly states that the aim of embedding FDEs is not to remain permanently stationed, but to transfer knowledge so that FIS can run on its own 6.

OpenAI's announcement is the largest in scale. With an initial investment of over $4 billion, 19 investment firms and consulting firms led by TPG contributed capital, and the group of more than 2,000 companies held by the investment firms will serve as deployment targets. The contributors include consulting firms such as Bain, Capgemini, and McKinsey 7. The structure is that the side building the models created an implementation company together with consulting firms.

What is important about AWS's $1 billion is that the announcement's wording includes "customers can run on their own by the end." It states that they will leave behind something that works, documentation, and trained internal staff 8.

The acquisition numbers. Buying people

The acquisition numbers are smaller than the investment numbers, but their meaning is weighty. The fact that OpenAI acquired Tomoro and had approximately 150 FDEs in place from day one shows that FDE has become a role that cannot be filled by "hiring and training" alone 7. In the same announcement, OpenAI states that it will continue to acquire companies that can accelerate implementation.

The reason FDE supply cannot keep up is that the skills this role requires are not just the ability to write code. The skills covered in articles one through six of this series—listening, observing, being present, structuring, capturing, and handing dictionaries to machines—are not part of engineering curricula. Palantir distributed a practical handbook on user interviews to new FDEs precisely because these skills had to be cultivated internally 9. Things that take time to cultivate get bought.

The business results numbers. The FDE mold shows up in margins

Finally, business results. Let's look at the numbers of Palantir, which has run the FDE mold for 20 years. Its second-quarter results announced in August 2026 showed revenue of $1.935 billion, up 93% year over year; U.S. commercial segment revenue of $764 million, up 149%; and GAAP operating income of $912 million, for an operating margin of 47%. Full-year revenue guidance was raised to $8.15–8.16 billion 10.

Read this figure in the context of the FDE model. According to the recollections of a former FDE, around 2016 the company was seen as "a Silicon Valley services firm," which was not far off the mark at the time, but by 2023 it had reached an 80% gross margin—software-level margins, entirely different from the 32% of the major consulting firms, as written in 9. The person who led the company's commercial division cited as public information that the top 20 customers generate over $1.1 billion in annual revenue, attributing this to deep alignment with customers 11.

Here is one answer to the question of whether the FDE model is genuine. If learning flows back into the product, effort per customer decreases and margins approach software levels. If learning does not flow back, effort scales with revenue and margins stay at consulting levels. The numbers reveal the truth of the model, with a lag of a few years.

Why 2025 to 2026

Lining up the four figures, the timing aligns. Why this period? I believe there are three reasons.

First, the problem of not reaching production was quantified. A report released in July 2025 stated that despite an estimated $30–40 billion in corporate investment in generative AI, 95% of organizations were not seeing measurable returns. The cause, it said, was not model performance but the absence of mechanisms to adapt to frontline context. I wrote about this report in the article The difference between FDEs and AI copilots. For those selling models, this is a serious figure. If the models they sell go unused, there is no next contract. Hence the decision to send people all the way to the point of use.

Second, agents began to act. An AI that summarizes text can work even when frontline language is vague. But an agent that "matches pending orders and issues shipping instructions" cannot function without knowing that company's nouns and verbs. As I wrote in the ontology article, this dictionary can only be built on the front lines. The age of agents is the age that requires people sitting on the front lines.

Third, coding agents lowered the cost of "code on day one." Previously, only skilled engineers could ship something working on day one, making FDEs expensive talent. Now, given the nouns and verbs, a provisional screen appears within hours. The center of gravity of FDE work has shifted from writing to listening, observing, and structuring, and the number of engagements a single FDE can handle has grown. I believe this slight easing of the supply constraint drew in investment.

In June 2025, a16z's Schmidt wrote about trading short-term gross margin for depth 2. With the three reasons converging, I believe 2025 to 2026 was the year when giving up gross margin became a rational decision.

What the numbers don't say: FDEs in name only vs. the real thing

Here is what I most want to say in this article. When job postings grow 5,230% and 38 companies line up on a chaos map, most of them are in name only. Job postings that simply relabel outsourced development SEs as "FDE," proposals that rebrand resident consultants as "Forward Deployed." The numbers do not distinguish these from the real thing.

The person who led Palantir's commercial division foresaw this phenomenon as early as 2024, writing that imitators copy the form but not the function: they redraw role boundaries, internalize the costs of outsourced SI work, and change how they collect product feedback, but never go as far as aligning interests with customers 11. In January 2026, a16z's Andrusko offered three pressure tests for spotting the real thing: Is engineering effort decreasing at mature customers? Can they say "No" to customization? Can the model still work past 50 customers? 12.

We will write out three verification methods we recommend to those commissioning such work. First, ask: "Where in your product today is what you learned from the engagements you entered before us?" If the answer is concrete, it's real. If the reply is "We optimize for each customer," it's outsourcing. Second, ask: "When does it end, and what will we be left with when it does?" Can they answer: something that works, the dictionary, and a state in which we can fix things ourselves? Third, deliberately raise a peripheral request: "Would you decline this request of ours?" Whether they can explain the grounds for declining reveals the model.

And this verification applies to us as well. Since we say "FDE style," we are obligated to answer these three. Learning flows back into the standard features of ZEROCK and TRAFEED, the engagement ends in 45 to 90 days, the dictionary's owner stays on the front lines, and peripheral requests are declined with stated grounds. This series, including this article, is written to publish those answers in advance.

Summary

The FDE market can be told through numbers: job postings grew 5,230% in 16 months, OpenAI committed over $4 billion and AWS $1 billion, Anthropic formed a joint venture with an investment firm, and Palantir recorded 93% revenue growth and a 47% operating margin. The timing converged on 2025 to 2026 because the problem of not reaching production was quantified, agents began to act, and coding agents lowered the cost of shipping code on day one.

What the numbers don't say is the distinction between the real thing and name-only versions. To tell them apart, ask three things: where the learning has entered the product, what remains at the end, and whether peripheral requests can be declined. We, too, will answer those three.
