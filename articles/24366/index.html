A New Career Path: Forward Deployed Engineer | Skills Required for FDEs and Future Prospects

A new career option is emerging for engineers in the AI era: the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)—a hybrid role between engineer and consultant who embeds with customers on-site and handles everything from AI implementation to adoption, end to end.

According to a report by the UK's Financial Times based on Indeed data, FDE job postings grew by more than 800% between January and September 2025. OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, and others are actively hiring, and in Japan as well, companies such as LayerX, Loglass, and AI Shift have successively created new FDE positions.

This article provides a comprehensive overview of the FDE career—the required skills, salary levels, future prospects, and a concrete roadmap for becoming an FDE.

A Day in the Life of an FDE, and How It Differs from Traditional Roles

The meaning of the FDE role and how it came about are summarized in "What is an FDE?". Here, we look at the flow of an FDE's day and how it differs from other roles.

Flow of a Typical Day (Example)

An FDE's day differs significantly from that of a traditional engineer or consultant.

Morning: Meeting with business stakeholders at the client site. Interviewing them about usage of the AI workflow deployed last week. Receiving feedback such as "the AI output in this part isn't quite right."

Morning to afternoon: Improving prompts and adjusting the workflow based on the feedback. Writing code and deploying it immediately. Preparing before-and-after comparison data.

Afternoon: Progress report to management. Presenting the impact of the AI implementation using a dashboard visualizing KPI trends. Discussing which business areas to tackle next.

Evening: Planning the next day's work. Validating a new AI model, preparing to roll out to other departments.

Skills Required for an FDE

The BTC Framework

The skills required for an FDE can be organized into three areas: Business × Technology × Creativity.

Technology (Technical Skills)

Essential:

  • Programming (Python, TypeScript, etc.—at least one language at a professional level)
  • Fundamental understanding of AI/LLMs (prompt engineering, RAG, fine-tuning)
  • Data engineering (SQL, data pipelines, ETL)
  • Cloud infrastructure (AWS, GCP, or Azure)

Strongly preferred:

  • Full-stack development (frontend + backend)
  • MLOps (model deployment, monitoring, and updating)
  • Security and compliance (especially in enterprise environments)

Business (Business Skills)

Essential:

  • Ability to design KPIs and measure impact
  • Stakeholder management (from executives to frontline staff)
  • Presentation and communication skills
  • Project management

Strongly preferred:

  • Domain knowledge in a specific industry (finance, healthcare, manufacturing, etc.)
  • Consulting experience
  • Experience in business development or new business ventures

Creativity (Creative Problem-Solving Skills)

Essential:

  • Structuring ambiguous problems
  • Distinguishing between "what should be automated" and "what humans should decide"
  • Workflow design and prototyping

Strongly preferred:

  • UX design thinking
  • Systems thinking
  • Facilitation

FDE Salary Levels

Global (2025–2026)

  • Entry level (2–5 years of experience): $150,000–$250,000 (approx. ¥23–38 million)
  • Senior level (5–10 years of experience): $250,000–$400,000 (approx. ¥38–60 million)
  • Lead level (10+ years of experience): $400,000+ (over ¥60 million)

Japanese Market (2025–2026)

  • Entry level: ¥7–10 million
  • Senior level: ¥10–15 million
  • Lead level: ¥15–25 million

FDE compensation in the Japanese market still lags behind overseas, but it is rising rapidly due to the talent shortage.

FDE Career Paths

Entry Paths: What Backgrounds Can Lead to Becoming an FDE

Path 1: From Software Engineer

You already have the technical skills. By honing your business understanding and client-facing skills, you can transition to FDE most smoothly.

Required steps:

  • Raise your hand for projects with customer contact
  • Practice articulating KPIs and business impact
  • Sharpen your presentation skills

Path 2: From IT Consultant

Your business understanding and client-facing skills are already strong. The challenge is acquiring hands-on implementation ability.

Required steps:

  • Acquire programming skills (up to a professional level)
  • Deepen your technical understanding of AI/LLMs
  • Gain experience going beyond "proposals" to actually "implementing" with your own hands

Path 3: From Data Scientist

You already have AI/ML technical skills. By adding business understanding and full-stack development skills, you can thrive as an FDE.

Required steps:

  • Acquire application development skills
  • Understand business processes (go deep in a specific industry)
  • Develop a product mindset oriented toward end users

Path 4: From Product Manager

You already have business understanding and a customer-centric perspective. By developing technical implementation skills, you can open up a career as an FDE.

What Lies Ahead in Your Career

After gaining experience as an FDE, your career options are diverse.

  • FDE Lead / Manager — Lead the FDE team and oversee support for multiple clients
  • CTO / VP of Engineering — The technology × business perspective cultivated as an FDE proves valuable in technology management
  • Entrepreneurship — FDEs who deeply understand customer challenges are well-suited to problem-solving startups
  • Product Leader — The ability to create products from real-world challenges

The Future of FDEs

Why demand for FDEs will continue to grow

1. AI adoption has only just begun

As of 2025, only about 10% of Japanese companies are making full use of AI. As the remaining 90% advance their AI adoption, demand for FDEs who can work hands-on in the field will be enormous.

2. AI doesn't end with "implementation"

Because AI operates probabilistically, it requires continuous tuning and improvement. Unlike SaaS, where deployment is the end of the process, the role of FDEs who continuously maximize AI's value will be needed over the long term.

3. Talent with both technology and business skills is structurally scarce

Professionals with both engineering and consulting skills are rare in any market. This structural supply shortage will sustain the market value of FDEs over the long term.

4. FDEs become core members of organizations

FDE experience directly leads to core positions such as CTO, VP of Engineering, and business unit leader. In the AI era, FDE experience is the most versatile career asset.

Summary

FDE is a new career path born in the AI era. Combining technical expertise with business understanding and delivering results at the customer's site — this role will be in ever greater demand going forward.
