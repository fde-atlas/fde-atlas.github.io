FDE Median Salary: Levels.fyi vs. Recruiting Data

Original title: FDE 薪资中位数：Levels.fyi 与招聘数据对比

FDE Times · 2026-11-05 · 5 min read

Summary

The article compares three US FDE salary medians: Levels.fyi reports $223,000 from 155 self-reported total compensation entries (base plus stock plus bonus), while Recruiting From Scratch ($195,000, 924 postings) and Fast AI Jobs ($182,000, 259 postings) use published salary ranges. The $41,000 gap reflects methodology, not errors. India data is weak: Levels.fyi has only 12 entries and Salary.com omits sample size. The author advises matching compensation types when comparing offers and citing sources with their methods.

Key points

  • Three US FDE medians span $182,000 to $223,000.
  • Levels.fyi includes stock and bonus; job-posting sources do not.
  • Levels.fyi's P25–P75 spread is $120,000, twice the postings-based range.
  • India medians rest on 12 entries or undisclosed samples.
  • Compare base salary to posting ranges, total comp to self-reported data.

Levels.fyi's $223,000 includes self-reported stock and bonuses, while the other two only look at posted salary ranges. Understand the methodology before negotiating.

Key Takeaways

  • In the US, the self-reported median total compensation for FDEs on Levels.fyi is $223,000, while two sources based on job postings report $182,000 and $195,000 respectively.
  • The gap mainly comes from statistical methodology: Levels.fyi includes stock and bonuses, while the other two use companies' publicly posted salary ranges.
  • In India, Levels.fyi's median is based on only 12 salaries; Salary.com's data is as of April 2025 and does not state a sample size, so neither is solid enough.

Ask how much a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) earns in the US, and you will get three answers. As of November 4, 2026, Levels.fyi shows a median of $223,000, based on 155 user-reported salaries.

Fast AI Jobs, based on data from November 2026, gives a median of $182,000. Recruiting From Scratch's June 2026 report puts it at $195,000. Same role, same market, a $41,000 spread between the two ends.

If you are preparing to negotiate an offer, or considering moving from Software Engineer (SWE) to FDE, this gap matters to you directly. None of the three numbers is wrong; they simply measure different things. Therefore, interpreting FDE salaries is much like the day-to-day work of an FDE: before trusting a number, ask where it came from.

What exactly does $223,000 include?

Levels.fyi defines total compensation as base salary plus annualized stock plus bonuses. So the $223,000 median cannot be compared side by side with figures that count base salary only. It is also self-reported data: users fill in their own offers or income.

The distribution on the page reveals more. P25 is $170,000, P75 is $290,000, and P90 is $351,400. The gap between P25 and P75 is $120,000. A plausible explanation is that stock varies widely across companies, stretching both ends of the distribution.

The median is not fixed either. Every new batch of self-reported data can shift it. When citing Levels.fyi, note the date you viewed it.

Job postings tell a different story

The other two sources do not ask practitioners; they look at job postings. Fast AI Jobs collects salary ranges from AI startup job listings, takes the midpoint of each range, and aggregates across listings, arriving at a median of $182,000 from 259 public salaries.

Recruiting From Scratch collected 924 FDE job postings from company career pages. It explicitly states that the data comes not from surveys but from job postings and actual hiring cases. The median is $195,000, with P25 to P75 ranging from $160,000 to $215,000.

This P25–P75 range is only $55,000 wide, less than half of Levels.fyi's $120,000. A narrower range is expected: one side measures the ranges companies themselves publish, while the other adds each person's self-reported stock and bonuses. There is an easily overlooked detail in the UK row: the page is filed under the FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer) job title, not FDE. Data is archived by job title, so searching only one name may miss part of the data.

India: a median derived from 12 people

In the US, two methods produce two numbers, but at least each has a decent sample. India is different. Levels.fyi shows a median of ₹1,770,112, slightly above 17.7 lakh, but it comes from only 12 reported salaries.

The distribution among these 12 people is very dispersed. P75 is ₹4,223,893 and P90 is ₹5,592,066, meaning P75 is already more than twice the median.

With only 12 data points, a few employees at US companies with offices in India are enough to push up the entire upper end. The page title still says United States, so it is impossible to confirm whether the region label is correct.

Salary.com's numbers are lower: a median of about ₹14.07 lakh, with P25 at ₹11.29 lakh and P75 at ₹16.54 lakh. The data is dated April 2025, and no sample size is given. Levels.fyi's P75 is more than two and a half times Salary.com's P75.

Neither source is reliable enough to settle on a single number. The former has too few data points; the latter does not say how many it has. If you work as an FDE in a market with such thin data, the safer approach is to treat both as a broad reference range and look for more local job postings in that market that list salaries.

One offer, two conclusions

Suppose you receive an FDE offer in the US: $200,000 base salary, no stock, no bonus. Against Levels.fyi's median of $223,000, it is $23,000 short; against Recruiting From Scratch's median of $195,000, it is $5,000 ahead. The same offer: one source says you should negotiate, the other says it is already good.

The mistake lies in comparing against the wrong type of number. This offer is all base salary, so putting it next to Levels.fyi's total compensation, which includes stock, is not a fair comparison. Conversely, if the range in the job posting is base salary only (worth asking the recruiter directly), then $200,000 falls within the $160,000–$215,000 P25–P75 range, in the upper half.

Now consider another offer: $175,000 base salary plus $50,000 per year in stock. That totals $225,000, close to Levels.fyi's median. The base salary is below the job-posting median of $195,000 but still above the P25 of $160,000, placing it in the lower half of the range.

On paper this offer is more balanced, but the $50,000 in stock is just an annualized figure. If the stock vests over several years, you have to stay long enough to receive it all; if the company is not yet public, its value depends on a valuation that could be revised downward.

Before treating that portion as cash, ask about the vesting schedule and how the stock price is calculated.

The practical rule is simple: compare total compensation against self-reported figures that include stock and bonuses; compare base salary against published salary ranges, and first ask what those ranges actually include. Never compare a number from one methodology against a number from another.

Reading salary data is also an FDE skill

Who entered the data, when it was entered, which fields it covers, how many rows there are: these are exactly the questions an FDE asks when receiving customer data. A dashboard showing revenue growth is meaningless if you don't know how revenue is defined. The same goes for salary medians.

So when negotiating, cite the source along with the methodology, not just a number. Saying "the median self-reported total compensation on Levels.fyi, 155 data points, accessed November 4, 2026" is far more persuasive than "I saw $223,000 online."

Citing it this way also shows the person across the negotiating table that you read data carefully, which is exactly the habit an FDE needs when working with customers.

When reading job descriptions, pay attention to which postings state a salary range and which don't. When preparing for interviews, practice breaking an offer down into base salary, stock, and bonus the moment the recruiter gives you a number.

The FDE salary median will continue to shift with each new batch of data. The numbers will change, but the habit of asking "what does it measure" will stay useful, whether you're negotiating salary or dealing with customers.
