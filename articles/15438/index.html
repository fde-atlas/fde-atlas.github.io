OpenAI’s enterprise AI problem: The hard part starts after the model

Ground Level AI · 2026-10-05 · 2 min read

Summary

A Ground Level AI podcast interview with Colin Jarvis, OpenAI's global head of forward deployed engineering, explores why enterprise AI deployments stall even as models improve. Jarvis estimates model capability explains only about 20% or less of the deployment gap; the rest involves changing how companies manage data, permissions, evals, and engineering organization. He describes FDE work at John Deere, oil and gas, and a semiconductor customer, insists OpenAI aims to avoid customer dependency, and argues better models will not eliminate deployment work.

Key points

  • Model capability accounts for roughly 20% or less of the deployment gap.
  • OpenAI wants customers to build their own capabilities, not depend on FDEs.
  • Cases include John Deere farm data guidance and a semiconductor firm's shared agent approach.
  • Agent evals require domain expertise, governance, and permissions.
  • Better models won't remove the need for deployment work.

Full text

A couple of weeks ago I was in Amsterdam moderating panels at HumanX, a conference devoted to enterprise AI, meaning the unglamorous work of getting AI out of pilots and into production inside real companies.

I also sat down with Colin Jarvis, OpenAI’s global head of forward deployed engineering (FDE). I wrote about the FDE phenomenon back in July, when it became clear that OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Microsoft had all converged on the same big billion-dollar bet: enterprise AI adoption won’t succeed unless specialized engineers are embedded inside customer organizations to help companies as they rush to deploy agentic AI.

The idea may sound counterintuitive. At the same time AI companies are promising to automate white-collar work, they’re racing to hire thousands of highly paid humans to make sure the work actually, well, works. But Jarvis told me the goal isn’t to make customers dependent on OpenAI’s engineers.

“We want to help our customers build the capabilities so that they can build the next use cases themselves,” he said. “We don’t want to be there long term. We don’t want to build dependency on the FDEs.”

In our conversation, Jarvis described what that work looks like, from helping John Deere turn machine data into guidance for farmers to rethinking how a semiconductor company builds and scales AI agents. He also offered a revealing estimate: model capability accounts for “about 20% or less” of the deployment gap. Much of the remaining work involves changing companies themselves—how they manage data, grant permissions, evaluate results and organize their engineers.
