Forward Deployed Engineer at EY: Role, Salary, and Interview (2026)

fde.directory · 2026-11-11 · 6 min read

Summary

This article examines EY's Forward Deployed Engineer roles, launched in the UK and Ireland in April 2026 and now posted across New York, San Francisco, Warsaw, Dublin and other cities. The job changes with grade: Seniors build RAG and agentic systems for banks and insurers, Managers run delivery workstreams, and Senior Managers set reference architectures. Posted base pay for a New York Senior runs $134,700–$202,200, with the top near the $203,000 median midpoint across 397 FDE postings in the Plank census. As a partnership, EY offers no equity, and roles require 40–60% in-person presence. The author recommends EY for consultants and regulated-industry specialists, not those seeking equity or product influence.

Key points

  • EY launched dedicated FDE roles in April 2026; seats now span the US, Ireland and Poland.
  • Responsibilities scale with grade: hands-on GenAI delivery at Senior, workstream management at Manager, architecture at Senior Manager.

  • Expect 40–60% in-person or client-site work; interviews emphasize RAG trade-offs over live coding.

EY’s posted base salary band for a Financial Services Forward Deployed Engineer, Senior in New York runs from $134,700 to $202,200. The top of that band sits just below $203,000, the median posted base midpoint across the 397 FDE postings that disclose pay in the Plank FDE census (CC BY 4.0, collected October 30, 2026). In other words, the best-paid person in EY’s New York Senior FDE band earns roughly what the typical FDE posting offers at its midpoint.

That is not a reason to skip EY. It is a reason to understand what the title means there, because “Forward Deployed Engineer” at a Big Four firm is a different job from the same title at OpenAI or Palantir.

How EY got into the FDE business

EY announced dedicated FDE roles for the UK and Ireland on April 28, 2026. The press release describes them as “senior engineers who work directly inside client delivery teams,” designing, building, integrating and running AI systems in live environments rather than stopping at pilots. Its examples are telling: a case-triage model for a UK public-sector department, performance bottlenecks in retail forecasting engines, and AI work in insurance underwriting, claims, risk mapping and bank lending.

Preetham Peddanagari, EY UK & Ireland’s CTO, framed the hiring this way: “The limiting factor is no longer the ambition or initial funding, it’s the specialist AI engineering capabilities.” The release gave no headcount and no pay. It did put the program in context: EY says it has invested more than $1 billion in its EY.ai platform and that its global AI-related revenue grew 30% in FY25. Both are company-reported figures.

Since April the title has spread across EY’s careers site. As of this week we can see FDE-titled roles in New York, San Francisco, San Antonio, Atlanta, Dublin, Warsaw and Katowice, plus an “Advanced Forward Engineering” group in Atlanta. EY is not alone. Dutch financial daily Het FD reported in August that Deloitte, Accenture, Capgemini and McKinsey are all chasing the same profile, and Accenture has its own FDE postings. The consultancies have turned the lab title into a service line.

What the job actually is

Read EY’s postings side by side and a pattern appears: the title is constant, the job changes with the grade.

At the Senior grade (3+ years, per the New York and Warsaw postings), the work is hands-on delivery. Build RAG and agentic components, wire integrations, tune performance and cost, and get a GenAI prototype into production for a bank or insurer. The Warsaw seat names Python, SQL, LLM frameworks and Azure as the preferred cloud.

At Manager (6+ years, San Francisco posting dated November 2, 2026), the description shifts toward running workstreams: scope, schedule, budget, risks, executive reporting, proposals and estimates. That is a consulting manager with an engineering background, not an IC.

At Senior Manager (10+ years for the New York Applied AI seat), the job is setting reference architectures, governing design patterns for RAG and agentic systems, and stepping in on escalated technical problems, including hands-on coding.

The Katowice posting is the exception that proves the rule: it explicitly describes an individual contributor “who prefers building production systems over managing teams,” but asks for 8+ years. Note what is missing almost everywhere: no model training, no product roadmap to influence. EY’s FDEs deploy other companies’ models and platforms (the Katowice posting lists OpenAI, Claude, Cursor and Palantir exposure as a plus) into EY’s clients. Your output is a successful engagement, not a better product.

US postings carry pay-transparency ranges. Non-US postings say only “competitive.”

Midpoints are our arithmetic. Ranges are base salary only.

Two things matter more than the headline numbers. First, EY is a partnership, so there is no stock grant. At a lab or a late-stage startup, equity can be half of total compensation; at EY, base plus bonus is most of what you get. Compare EY offers on total cash, and compare them against total comp elsewhere, not base. Second, the bands are wide, roughly $68,000 at Senior and $200,000 at Senior Manager, so where you land depends heavily on how EY maps your experience to its grades. EY’s Senior grade asks for 3+ years and Manager for 6+, so an engineer with six years and a Big Tech “senior” title should check which grade they are being slotted into before negotiating. Logistics

EY’s US Senior Manager posting says most client-serving staff are expected in person 40–60% of the time over an engagement, project or year. Katowice is two office days a week. The Dublin Senior Manager posting warns that client-facing staff may be “based at client sites.” This is a travel-and-presence job, and the client, not EY, often sets the schedule. It is not a route into remote FDE work.

The interview loop

EY has not published an FDE-specific process, and we found no verified first-hand FDE reports. Interview Query’s guide for EY AI Engineer roles, built on a small number of candidate reports from 2026, describes this shape:

Stage Reported format
Recruiter screen ~30 min, background and fit, sometimes salary expectations
Manager / senior manager 45–60 min, CV and past client-facing projects
Technical 45–60 min, AI and cloud with a heavy RAG focus (simple vs. advanced RAG trade-offs)
Offer discussion 15–30 min with HR

That is lighter on live coding than a lab loop and heavier on how you explain trade-offs to a non-engineer. One reported candidate found the offer well below expectations, so align on grade and band early. For scenario practice, use our FDE interview guide.

Who should apply

EY is a sensible move for three kinds of engineer: a consultant or systems integrator who wants an AI-delivery title on their CV; a regulated-industry specialist (banking, insurance, public sector) whose domain knowledge is worth more at EY than at a startup; and an engineer in Poland or Ireland, where EY has several FDE seats open at once (two in Warsaw, one in Katowice, one in Dublin as of this week). Warsaw’s 3+ year bar makes it one of the more accessible seats in Europe; see our Europe guide.

It is a weaker fit if you want equity upside, product influence or model work. For those, compare Palantir, AWS or Anthropic, and read FDE vs Solutions Engineer to check which job the title is really describing.
