FDE glossary: forward-deployed engineering terms defined

AIXL Academy · 2026-10-08 · 8 min read

Summary

AIXL Academy has published a glossary defining, in one or two sentences each, the terms its author uses in engagements and training cohorts. It covers the FDE role and deployed teams, the author's four-gate assessment structure (context, architecture, integration, defend and hand over) and their written outputs such as discovery briefs and handover packs, plus agent and harness engineering, evaluation practice, token economics, retrieval patterns, and governance terms spanning the EU AI Act, India's DPDP Act, Singapore's MAS guidelines and UAE PDPL. Useful as a quick reference rather than a narrative article.

Key points

  • Defines FDE as engineers working inside the customer environment, owning outcomes.
  • Describes four gates: problem framing, architecture, integration, defend and hand over.
  • Covers agent terms including guardrails, prompt injection and tool contracts.
  • Includes regulation entries: EU AI Act, DPDP Act, MAS guidelines, UAE PDPL.
  • Favours 'cost per completed task' over cost per call as a metric.

Full text

The terms I use in engagements and cohorts, defined in one or two sentences each. Where a term has a common meaning elsewhere, this is the meaning I use.

Terms

Forward-deployed engineering (FDE)

A way of delivering technology in which engineers work inside the customer's environment, close to the problem, and stay until the result runs. The engineer owns an outcome, not a backlog.

Forward-deployed engineer

An engineer who does forward-deployed engineering. Needs two things: technical depth and customer judgement. Also abbreviated FDE.

Deployed team

A small group of forward-deployed engineers placed with one client, with a named lead who is accountable for the result.

Discovery brief

A one- or two-page record of the task, its owner, the evidence that matters, the data and systems that can be reached, and what is out of scope. The output of the first gate.

Architecture decision record (ADR)

A short document that records one technical decision, the options considered and the reasoning. A set of them is the output of the second gate.

Evaluation harness

The code and test cases that measure whether an AI system does the task to the agreed standard, run repeatedly as the system changes.

Responsible AI controls

The evaluation, human review steps, data boundaries and audit records that are written down before a build starts, so the system can be trusted and checked after launch.

Handover

The point at which a named owner inside the client takes over running the system, with a runbook, the evaluation results and the recorded decisions. The output of the fourth gate.

The four gates

My assessment structure for forward-deployed work: G1 context and problem framing, G2 architecture, G3 integration, G4 defend and hand over. Each has a written output and a rubric. See the framework.

AI Delivery Readiness Review

My two-week advisory engagement on one workflow or pilot, ending in a written recommendation to proceed, reframe or pause. See Engagements.

Architecture Sprint

My two-to-four-week engagement that turns an agreed use case into a solution architecture, recorded decisions, an evaluation plan and a delivery backlog.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

A pattern in which a language model is given relevant documents or records at the time of the question, so answers rest on the organisation's own data rather than on the model's memory.

Agent

A system in which a language model decides which tools or steps to use to complete a task, within limits set by the designer. In enterprise work the limits and the review steps matter more than the model.

Capability centre

A unit inside a services firm or large enterprise, often in India, that builds and staffs a technical capability for the wider organisation. Many of my cohorts are for capability centres.

All terms

Practice

  • AI Delivery Readiness Review: My two-week advisory engagement on one workflow or pilot, ending in a written recommendation to proceed, reframe or pause.
  • Architecture decision record (ADR): A short document that records one technical decision, the options considered and the reasoning.
  • Architecture Sprint: My two-to-four-week engagement that turns an agreed use case into a solution architecture, recorded decisions, an evaluation plan and a delivery backlog.
  • Capability centre: A unit inside a services firm or large enterprise, often in India, that builds and staffs a technical capability for the wider organisation.
  • Deployed team: A small group of forward-deployed engineers placed with one client, with a named lead who is accountable for the result.
  • Discovery brief: A one- or two-page record of the task, its owner, the evidence that matters, the data and systems that can be reached, and what is out of scope.
  • FDE operating model: How an enterprise or GCC organises forward-deployed engineers: a central pool, embedded deployed teams, or a hub and spoke design.
  • First working slice: The smallest version of a system that does the whole job on real data, outside production, and is scored against agreed evaluations.
  • Forward-deployed engineer: An engineer who does forward-deployed engineering.
  • Forward-deployed engineering (FDE): A way of delivering technology in which engineers work inside the customer's environment, close to the problem, and stay until the result runs.
  • Handover: The point at which a named owner inside the client takes over running the system, with a runbook, the evaluation results and the recorded decisions.
  • Handover pack: The package a client owner receives at handover: a runbook, the evaluation set, the named owner and an escalation path.
  • Readiness gate: A readiness gate is a two-week decision on whether an AI workflow should proceed, be reframed or paused, based on four written preconditions.
  • The four gates: My assessment structure for forward-deployed work: G1 context and problem framing, G2 architecture, G3 integration, G4 defend and hand over.
  • Time box: A fixed duration for a piece of work. When the work does not fit, the scope is cut or re-planned rather than the time extended.

Agents and harnesses

  • Agent: A system in which a language model decides which tools or steps to use to complete a task, within limits set by the designer.
  • Guardrails: Checks placed around a model's inputs, outputs and actions that block or flag what the system should not do.
  • Harness engineering: Designing the scaffolding around a model (tools, permissions, pause points, logs and context) so that an agent behaves reliably.
  • Loop engineering: Loop engineering sets an agent's done conditions, turn limits, error policy and the state it keeps between turns.
  • Prompt injection: Untrusted text (a web page, an email or a document) that tries to override an agent's instructions or make it act against its owner.
  • Tool contract: The typed inputs, outputs and error cases for each tool an agent can call, so the harness can validate every call before it runs.

Tokens and cost

  • Context window: The maximum number of tokens a model can take in one call, covering instructions, retrieved material, conversation history and output.
  • Cost per completed task: Total model and tooling cost divided by the number of tasks completed without human rework. A better measure than cost per call.
  • Model routing: Sending each step of a workflow to a model chosen for its difficulty, cost and latency, rather than sending every step to one model.
  • Prompt caching: A provider feature that reuses a stable prefix of a prompt across calls, lowering cost and latency when the prefix is kept the same.
  • Token: The unit of text a language model reads and writes. Model prices are usually quoted per million tokens, with input and output priced separately.

Evaluation

  • Eval set: A collection of real cases, each with an agreed expected outcome, used to measure an AI system before and after every change.
  • Evaluation harness: The code and test cases that measure whether an AI system does the task to the agreed standard, run repeatedly as the system changes.
  • IMDA agentic testing starter kit: IMDA's version 1.0 starter kit for testing agentic AI systems, with four steps: identify the risks, create tests, run them and analyse logs, then monitor and re-test.
  • LLM as judge: A language model scores another system's outputs against a rubric. The judge must be calibrated against human reviewers before it is trusted.
  • Regression eval: A re-run of the full evaluation set after every change to a model, prompt, tool or data source, to catch anything that got worse.
  • Rubric: A written set of criteria, each with levels and examples, that a reviewer or a judge uses to score an output the same way every time.

Data and retrieval

  • Chunking: Splitting source documents into pieces that a retrieval system can search and return, cut along headings and sections rather than fixed character counts.
  • Embedding: A list of numbers that captures the meaning of a piece of text, so that a retrieval system can find passages with similar meaning.
  • Hybrid search: A retrieval method that combines keyword matching with embedding search, so exact names, codes and numbers are found alongside passages of similar meaning.
  • Knowledge graph: A store of entities and the typed relationships between them, such as customer owns contract, so that a system can follow links rather than match text alone.
  • Permission-aware retrieval: Search results filtered by the access rights of the person asking, so that an agent never shows content the user could not open directly.
  • Re-ranking: A second step that orders the candidate passages from a first search more carefully, so that the best evidence reaches the model first.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): A pattern in which a language model is given relevant documents or records at the time of the question, so answers rest on the organisation's own data rather than on the model's memory.

Governance and regulation

  • AI literacy (EU AI Act): The EU AI Act duty, in Article 4, to support a sufficient level of AI literacy among staff and others who work with AI systems on the organisation's behalf.
  • Data-access negotiation: Agreeing with four kinds of owner (data, security, legal and platform) what data an AI system may use, and under which controls.
  • Deployer (EU AI Act): Under the EU AI Act, a deployer is any person or body using an AI system under its authority, except for personal non-professional use.
  • DPDP Act (India) and Rules 2025: India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its 2025 Rules, which govern personal data processed in India and, in some cases, outside it.
  • EU AI Act: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the EU law that classifies AI systems by risk and sets duties for providers and deployers, as amended in 2026.
  • High-risk AI system (EU): Under the EU AI Act, a high-risk system is one covered by Annex I product rules or listed in an Annex III area, such as employment or essential services.
  • Human in the loop: A design in which a named person approves, corrects or can stop an agent's actions at defined points, not after the fact.
  • MAS AI risk management guidelines: The Monetary Authority of Singapore's final guidelines on AI risk management for financial institutions, dated 7 October 2026, taking effect on 7 October 2027.
  • Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI: IMDA's Singapore framework for governing AI agents, built on four dimensions: assess and bound risks, make humans accountable, implement controls, and enable end-user responsibility.
  • Responsible AI controls: The evaluation, human review steps, data boundaries and audit records that are written down before a build starts, so the system can be trusted and checked after launch.
  • UAE PDPL (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021): The UAE federal personal data protection law, Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, in force from 2 January 2022. Its executive regulations were not listed on the official portal in November 2026.
