Is FDE Career Change Right for SaaS Engineers? Transferable Experience and Skill Gaps

Original title: FDE転職はSaaSエンジニアに向く？活かせる経験と不足スキル

FDE総研 · 2026-11-26 · 10 min read

Summary

This article from FDE総研 (run by VACAN) assesses how well SaaS engineers can transition into Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roles, a job type popularized by Palantir that covers problem discovery through implementation and production operations. It contrasts FDE and SaaS development across four axes: unit of outcome, source of requirements, scope of work, and success metrics. Design and production-operations experience transfers well, while direct customer-interaction experience is the biggest differentiator; product-specific feature knowledge does not carry over. The piece offers preparation priorities by years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size, lists three common skill gaps (articulating ambiguous problems, customer negotiation, understanding client data infrastructure), and advises evaluating job postings by customer contact, implementation scope, and operational involvement rather than job titles.

Key points

  • FDE, popularized by Palantir, handles problem discovery through implementation and production operations at customer sites.
  • SaaS design and operations experience transfers well; direct customer-dialogue experience differentiates candidates most.
  • Product-specific feature knowledge does not carry over; resumes should emphasize judgments made, not feature names.
  • Recommended skill-building order: articulating problems, customer interaction, then data-integration understanding.
  • Judge FDE postings by customer contact, implementation scope, and operational involvement, not job titles.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who works on-site with customers, handling everything from identifying issues to implementation and production operation. Below, we organize the experience that transfers well and the skills that tend to be lacking when a SaaS engineer moves to an FDE role, using FDE Soken's evaluation framework. FDE Soken is a media outlet covering FDE careers and practices, operated by VACAN.

What is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? How does it differ from SaaS development?

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role responsible for everything from problem framing to implementation at customer sites.

FDE stands for "forward deployed engineer," and the role is to deliver results by embedding yourself in the customer's business. SaaS development polishes a product for an unspecified number of customers. An FDE solves a specific customer's problems through the product and surrounding code. Understanding this difference clarifies which SaaS engineer experiences can be transferred.

What is the role of the FDE that Palantir created?

The FDE role popularized by Palantir is an engineer who consistently handles everything from identifying issues to production operation at customer sites.

The term FDE became known through the US data analytics company Palantir's practice of sending engineers to customer sites. Since then, it has been reported that more companies are adopting the same approach. An FDE's work doesn't end with "building." It includes searching for problems together with the customer, forming hypotheses, and delivering something that works in a short period of time.

How do the responsibilities of SaaS development and FDE differ?

SaaS development polishes a product shared by many customers, while FDE solves one company's on-site problems; the unit of responsibility differs.

FDE Soken organizes the differences between the two along the following four axes.

Which SaaS engineer experiences are valuable for an FDE career change?

FDE Soken considers that, among SaaS engineers' experiences, hypothesis-building around customer problems and production operation experience are the most valuable for FDE roles.

The design skills and operational instincts developed in SaaS development apply directly to FDE work. However, whether they apply depends on whether you have worked close to the customer's problems.

How far do design and operations experience carry over?

SaaS experience in design, incident response, and operations serves as a foundation for FDEs delivering things that work in production environments.

In SaaS, you can gain experience in multi-tenant design, API design, monitoring, incident response, and release management. Since FDEs also handle production operations in customer environments, these become strengths. From a practitioner's perspective, it is often thought that "being able to diagnose the cause yourself when something breaks" builds customer trust more than "being able to build things that work."

How is customer interaction experience evaluated?

Experience directly talking with customers to draw out requirements is the experience that makes the biggest difference in FDE hiring.

Experience accompanying customer success or sales teams, explaining incidents to customers, and proposing alternatives instead of implementing requests as-is are all close to FDE work. Conversely, if you have only implemented specifications handed down from the product department, it becomes difficult to talk about "how you understood the customer's problems" in interviews.

Which experiences are hard to leverage?

Experience focused solely on knowledge of a specific SaaS product's features is hard to leverage in FDE roles.

Deep knowledge of your own product's internal specifications cannot be brought to other companies' sites in FDE work. What transfers is the design judgment you developed through that product and your attitude toward understanding the customer's business. In your resume, it is easier to convey your value if you focus on "what decisions you made for what problems" rather than feature names.

FDE Soken recommends that SaaS engineers think through their preparation by breaking it down in the order of years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size.

By years of experience: What differs between less than 3 years, 3-7 years, and 8+ years?

The fewer years of experience you have, the more you are tested on independent implementation ability; the more experience you have, the more you are tested on coordination with customer decision-makers.

At an early stage of your career, you are evaluated on whether you can complete design, implementation, and operations on your own. From mid-level onward, what matters is whether you can explain the reasons for your technology choices in terms of the customer's business.

By current job type: What are in-house SaaS developers and SIer engineers missing?

In-house SaaS developers tend to lack customer-facing experience, while SIer engineers tend to lack product-oriented design and operations experience.

In-house SaaS developers can close this gap by deliberately creating opportunities to directly observe customers' business operations. SIer and outsourced-development engineers often already have customer-facing experience, but they may be accustomed to implementing from finalized requirements. Organizing "what decisions you made when requirements were still ambiguous" tends to lead to favorable evaluations.

How do English proficiency and company size affect your options?

English proficiency tends to affect the breadth of jobs you can apply for, while company size tends to affect the breadth of discretion you are given.

Large companies have well-established systems, but your scope of responsibility may be divided. Startups offer broad discretion, but their training systems may be thin. Which is better depends realistically on matching the choice to the experience you currently lack.

When conditions conflict (e.g., high English proficiency but few years of experience), what should take priority?

If your English is strong but you have few years of experience, prioritize building a track record in production operations and customer handling before English.

FDE is a role that requires working independently at customer sites, and English proficiency remains merely a factor that broadens your options. If you apply widely without a solid track record, you will likely struggle in interviews because you cannot articulate the basis for your decisions. The recommended order of priority is: 1) operational track record, 2) experience in dialogue with customers, 3) English proficiency.

What skills are FDE candidates commonly missing? In what order should they be filled?

FDE Soken cites three skills that SaaS engineers tend to lack: articulating problems, customer negotiation, and understanding of data platforms.

The gaps vary by individual, but those from SaaS backgrounds share common tendencies.

What skills tend to be lacking?

What SaaS engineers tend to lack is the ability to articulate ambiguous problems, negotiation with customers, and understanding of customers' data platforms.

Skill often lackingTypical situationExample of how to fill the gap
Articulating problemsOrganizing the real issue behind a customer's requestBuild the habit of writing requests broken down into "purpose, constraints, and success criteria"
Customer negotiationCommunicating what cannot be done, along with the reasonsJoin meetings with customers present and write the minutes yourself
Understanding data platformsGrasping customers' existing systems and data integrationsRead the specifications of integration targets and diagram them

In what order should these gaps be filled?

The recommended order is: articulating problems, experience in customer dialogue, then understanding of data integration.

  • At your current job, build the habit of writing in three lines "why the request came about," not just the requested specification.
  • Create opportunities to talk directly with customers or internal users, even just once a month.
  • Diagram the outside of the systems you work with (integration targets and where the data comes from).
  • Summarize these experiences in your resume in the order of "situation → decision → result → limitations."

From a practitioner's perspective, starting with item 1, which you can do on your own, makes it easier to keep going.

How does FDE differ from SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer? Who is not a good fit?

FDE Soken positions FDE relative to SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer along two axes: depth of implementation and proximity to customer sites.

FDE differs from SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer in that it involves doing the implementation yourself while staying close to customer sites.

What kind of person is not a good fit for FDE?

Those not suited to FDE are people who prioritize deeply polishing a single product over the long term, and people who want to avoid customer interaction altogether.

Those who want to pursue a product's internal quality will likely find more satisfaction as SWEs. Those for whom being driven by customer requests is a heavy burden may also be a poor fit. On the other hand, those who find it rewarding to see customer sites and build things that actually work are likely a good match for FDE. Fit is not fixed; it changes with how you accumulate experience.

How do you identify FDE job openings? What criteria should you use when browsing listings?

FDE Soken recommends identifying FDE openings not by the label "FDE" in the job title, but by descriptions of customer contact and scope of implementation.

Even if a job title says FDE, the actual role may be closer to SE, implementation support, or sales. Conversely, there are openings with different titles whose work is close to FDE.

Where in a job posting can you see the actual nature of the role?

In job postings, focusing on three points makes the actual role clear: the proportion of work at customer sites, the scope of implementation, and involvement in operations.

CheckpointDescription close to FDEDescription with distance from FDE
Customer contactDirectly hearing about problems at customer sitesSales gathers requirements and specifications are handed over
Scope of implementationResponsible from design through implementation and deploymentMainly executing configuration and operational procedures
Involvement in operationsResponsible through production operations and improvementWork ends upon delivery

How should job listings be used?

Use job listings to broaden your pool of candidate employers, and confirm the above three points directly in an interview before applying—that is the most reliable approach.

Even when using FDE Soken's job listings, the posted content is based on companies' public information, so details may change. For openings that interest you, ask specifically in the interview about the frequency of customer contact and the scope of responsibility. Checking your own readiness against the condition-based tables mentioned earlier makes it easier to decide your application priorities.
