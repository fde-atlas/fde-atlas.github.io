What Is the Cognition Forward Deployed Engineer Interview Like? (Round by Round)

Design Gurus · 2026-11-14 · 3 min read

Summary

This guide walks through the six reported stages of Cognition's forward deployed engineer interview: recruiter screen, a take-home completed inside Devin, a 45-minute project presentation, leadership one-on-ones, an executive pitch, and a simulated customer call. Most stages test customer-facing skills rather than algorithms. The author notes Cognition has not published its loop, so the structure comes mainly from one interviewer's account and should be confirmed with a recruiter. Preparation tips include using Devin on a real task, leading the project talk with impact, and practicing unscripted customer calls.

Key points

  • Six stages: recruiter screen, Devin take-home, project presentation, leadership one-on-ones, executive pitch, customer call.
  • The take-home requires little code; candidates run a real task in Devin and connect it to customer value.
  • Presentations should state project impact in the first five minutes, then cover architecture and decisions.
  • The executive pitch fails most often when candidates list features without business outcomes.
  • No confirmed timeline exists; similar startups typically take two to five weeks.

Prep guides describe six stages for the Cognition forward deployed engineer interview, and most of them simulate customer work rather than test algorithms. The stages are a recruiter screen, a take-home done inside Devin, a project presentation of about 45 minutes, leadership one-on-ones, an executive pitch, and a simulated customer call. Devin is Cognition's AI software engineer, a product that writes, tests, and fixes code on its own. Many Cognition job postings use the title "Deployed Engineer" for this role, so search for both titles. Cognition does not publish its loop, and the main guide is based on one interviewer's account, so confirm the stages with your recruiter.

Quick Overview

Stage Format What is evaluated
1. Recruiter screen Call with a non-technical recruiter Motivation, background, clear communication
2. Technical take-home Hands-on exercise in Devin Product understanding and customer value
3. Project presentation About 45 minutes, panel Impact, design reasons, composure
4. Leadership one-on-ones Conversations with VPs and leaders Presence with senior people, judgment
5. Executive pitch Panel playing company executives Explaining business value
6. Customer call Timed prep, then a live simulated call Fast structuring and real-time answers

Stage 1: Recruiter Screen

A non-technical recruiter asks about your background, what you want next, and why you want to move into forward deployed work. The recruiter is also checking whether you can explain your work to someone outside engineering. Practice a two-minute version of your background with no jargon.

Stage 2: Technical Take-Home in Devin

The take-home is hands-on but requires little code. You set up Devin, run a realistic engineering task with it, and connect what it does to value for a customer. The difficulty is running a real workflow in a developer tool and explaining the result, not writing a hard algorithm.

Stage 3: Project Presentation

You present one past project to a panel of engineers and leaders for about 45 minutes. Some panelists may play a less technical or difficult customer. Guides advise stating the project's impact within the first five minutes, before any build details, and then covering the architecture and your reasons for each choice.

Stage 4: Leadership One-on-Ones

These are unscripted behavioral conversations with VPs and engineering leaders. They simulate the range of customers an FDE meets, from very engaged executives to ones who barely take part. Expect questions such as "Tell me about a time you owned a project end to end" and "How do you handle a stakeholder who knows the subject as well as you do?"

Stage 5: Executive Pitch

You pitch Devin to a panel playing company executives. Frame the product around outcomes that executives measure: faster delivery, lower engineering cost, and time freed for harder work. A feature list without a business outcome is the most common weak answer.

Stage 6: Customer Call

You receive a fictional company's problems and a timed prep window. Then you join a simulated call with the customer, whose questions may not follow the structure you prepared. Interviewers grade how fast you structure the problem and how calmly you answer questions you did not expect.

Timeline and Decision

The guides do not report a reliable timeline for this role. Startups of Cognition's size usually move in two to five weeks, but that range is typical, not confirmed. The loop for Cognition's software engineer roles includes more coding and is described in What is the Cognition interview process like?

How to Prepare

  • Use the product before you apply. Run a real task with Devin and write down what worked, what failed, and what a customer would pay for.
  • Prepare a 45 minute project talk. Open on impact, then architecture, then decisions. Practice it once for an engineer and once for a non-engineer.
  • Keep a short reference. Cognition Forward Deployed Engineer Interview is a free guide on GitHub that covers the reported stages, the executive pitch, and the simulated customer call, and includes an 18-hour plan.
  • Practice a customer call. The method in What is the Palantir decomposition interview? works for the prep window: ask, scope, break down, then propose.
  • Prepare for customer simulations. Grokking the Forward Deployed Engineer Interview covers discovery calls and project presentations, and has a lesson on Cognition and other AI startup loops.
  • Build behavioral stories. Grokking Modern Behavioral Interview shows how to structure ownership and conflict stories.
  • Compare loops. What is the forward deployed engineer interview process like? shows how Cognition differs from OpenAI and Anthropic.
