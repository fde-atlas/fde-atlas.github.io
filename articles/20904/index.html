Written and reviewed by: FDE Talent Editorial Team/Primary sources such as official job postings and official announcements from each company are cited

Transitioning to an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) from customer-facing technical roles such as pre-sales, sales engineer, customer engineer, or customer success is one of the most common career backgrounds among FDE job changes in 2026. Many people have repeatedly demonstrated value through demos and PoCs, but then handed things off to an implementation team before production, and are motivated by a desire to "see it through to the end"—and FDE is precisely the role that owns that "to the end." However, there are clear differences between FDEs and pre-sales in terms of where involvement ends, who owns the code, and how performance is evaluated. Changing roles without understanding these differences leads to "this isn't the job I expected."

In this article, using as primary sources the remarks of OpenAI's head of FDE (The Pragmatic Engineer), an explainer from The New Stack, and job postings published in Tokyo by Andpad, Channel Talk, RevComm, Databricks, Notion, and Anthropic (all as of October 2026), we organize the boundary between pre-sales-type roles and FDEs, the experience that gets valued in a career move, the implementation skills to build up, and how to reframe your resume.

Key Takeaways

  • The biggest difference between pre-sales (sales engineers and solution architects) and FDEs is where involvement ends: pre-sales' job is to "demonstrate that it works" before the deal closes, while FDEs take responsibility through writing code in the customer's production environment after the deal and "keeping it running."
  • Colin Jarvis, who leads the FDE organization at OpenAI, explained in an interview with The Pragmatic Engineer that while SAs (solution architects) serve in an advisory role and rarely write code on the customer's infrastructure, FDEs write code directly on the customer's infrastructure and handle more ambiguous situations.
  • In Japan, as of October 2026, Andpad lists "practical experience in IT solution pre-sales, implementation consulting, or as a systems engineer" and Channel Talk lists experience serving Enterprise customers as "Presales / Solution Engineer / Technical Consultant / CSM, etc." as required qualifications in their FDE job postings—pre-sales experience is clearly valued as an entry point.
  • On the other hand, Databricks' Tokyo FDE posting requires 8+ years of experience and coding in Python, Scala, and similar languages, and Notion's Tokyo FDE posting requires the ability to write production-quality code—people who have stopped at the PoC stage need to deliberately accumulate experience "running things in production and operating them."
  • In your resume, reframing your story around "how the PoC I designed led to production" rather than "deal size," "how I influenced the customer's decision-making," and "what feedback I fed back into the product" will align with FDE selection criteria.

Conclusion: The line between pre-sales and FDE is "before or after the deal" and "who owns the code"

Pre-sales (sales engineers, solution architects, solution engineers) work alongside sales to resolve technical concerns before a deal closes. FDEs go on-site with the customer after the deal and take responsibility through getting their company's product running in the customer's actual production operations. Both start from the same place—"solving customer problems with technology"—but they differ in where the work ends, who owns the code, and what they are evaluated on.

The Pragmatic Engineer explains that roles such as "Agent engineer," "Solutions Architect," "Sales Engineer," and "Technical Delivery Engineer" are quite close to FDEs, and that the difference lies in the fact that FDEs contribute to their own company's product while working with customers. In other words, when someone with pre-sales experience moves to an FDE role, the two new things they take on are "production implementation" and "giving back to the product."

The line between pre-deal (pre-sales) and post-deal (FDE)

The clearest dividing line is the timeline. Pre-sales work concludes with the deal, after which it is handed off to the implementation team or customer success. FDE work begins in earnest after the deal and continues even after production launch.

The New Stack explains that FDEs work within the customer's environment with the explicit goal of "getting systems running in production and keeping them running." According to the article, FDEs join the customer's communication channels, work closely with the customer's product and engineering teams, and build directly on the customer's data and systems. They are involved not only in deciding what to build but also bear responsibility through building, integrating, and post-launch stability, and their success is measured not by hours spent but by how much the system is used and how it performs.

The same article also describes how this differs from traditional consulting. Consultants work for a fixed period and are evaluated on deliverables, whereas FDEs are evaluated on whether the system keeps running and delivering value after go-live—their work doesn't end at handover. Presales work, too, is closer to consulting than to FDE in structure, since it is bounded by a deliverable called "the deal."

There are cases where "FDEs join during the sales process"

However, the boundary isn't always just a matter of timing. According to The Pragmatic Engineer, when a customer is unsure whether they can make good use of the product, sales proposes bringing in an FDE to ensure a successful integration. In other words, FDEs enter in the later stages of the sales process and also play the role of demonstrating "it works in production" as the deciding factor in winning the deal. For those with presales experience, this is where their background is most directly applicable. The difference lies in whether you take what you built there all the way to production yourself.

Anthropic's "Applied AI Engineer" role in Tokyo (as of October 2026) is an example of this boundary being reflected in the job title itself. The responsibilities listed in the posting include "acting as the primary technical advisor to prospective customers evaluating Claude," "working closely with account executives to understand customer requirements," and "developing custom pilots, prototypes, and evaluation suites to support customer adoption," along with "serving as an ongoing technical advisor both during implementation and after production deployment." The role is designed so that a single person owns everything from pitching prospective customers to advising after go-live. Required qualifications include "experience as a technical product manager, Forward Deployed Engineer, or platform engineer" and "high proficiency in Python."

From "demonstrating it works" to "making it work in production": differences in code ownership and scope of responsibility

The person who most concisely articulates the difference between presales and FDE is Colin Jarvis, who leads the FDE organization at OpenAI. In an interview with The Pragmatic Engineer, he stated that FDEs are more hands-on than SAs (solution architects) and work in situations that are more ambiguous than what SAs have traditionally handled.

"SAs play a role closer to advisory, rarely writing code on customer infrastructure, and typically building MVPs or PoCs from anonymized or offline data extracts. FDEs, by contrast, are far more hands-on, writing code directly on customer infrastructure and using the customer's tools to do so."

This statement contains three changes that people moving from presales to FDE roles face.

  • The data becomes real: You work with the customer's actual data (including missing values, permissions issues, and legacy formats) rather than the anonymized or sample data used in PoCs. "It worked in the demo" no longer cuts it.
  • The environment belongs to the customer: You write code within the constraints of the customer's infrastructure and toolchain (CI/CD, authentication, monitoring, security policies), not in your own sandbox.
  • You keep owning the code: With a PoC, you hand it off once the deal is won, but as an FDE you own the production code and are responsible for post-launch incidents, improvements, and handover.

Japanese job postings also make this "production" requirement explicit. Notion's Forward Deployed Engineer posting in Tokyo (as of October 2026) defines the role as "leading hands-on technical delivery for the most complex customer engagements," and lists among its duties "creating and maintaining custom code," "designing and deploying production-quality custom agents and AI workflows," "building data pipelines," and "solving complex problems around scale, permissions, and governance." Among the required qualifications is "proficiency in at least one programming language (such as Java, JavaScript, Node.js, SQL, or Python) and comfort writing production-quality code for customer-facing or internal engineering."

Databricks' FDE posting in Tokyo (as of October 2026) is even more explicit, listing its first responsibility as "Production Solution Delivery" and stating that it "delivers production-quality systems, reference architectures, custom applications, data ingestion, and ML/AI model integrations." It also includes an "Own the Architecture" item, indicating that the FDE is expected to be the owner of design decisions.

The added responsibility of feeding insights back into the product

Another way FDEs differ from presales is their responsibility to feed insights gained at customer sites back into their own company's product. The Pragmatic Engineer describes how OpenAI's FDE team collected performance data on a voice automation engagement and worked with the research division to improve the Realtime API for all customers. Japanese job postings reflect this as well: Andpad states that you will "feed the 'firsthand information' gathered on the front lines back to the development team to evolve the next generation of products," and Channel Talk explicitly lists "sharing customer feedback with the Product/Engineering teams" among its duties. In presales, requests do reach the product team via sales, but as an FDE you are in a position to influence product specifications based on the results of what you implemented yourself.

Differences in the presence or absence of quotas (sales targets) and evaluation criteria

Presales professionals generally belong to sales organizations and are evaluated in connection with the deals and sales targets of the salespeople they support. Even at companies where individuals don't carry quotas directly, the basis of evaluation remains "how much you contributed to winning deals." FDEs are evaluated differently. As The New Stack puts it, FDEs are measured by "how much the system is used and how it is performing"—not by hours spent, and not by deals won.

That said, FDEs are not detached from revenue. The Ramp FDE posting featured by The Pragmatic Engineer lists among its responsibilities "working closely with sales and GTM teams to close compelling deals, activate customers, and expand the value Ramp delivers over time." The structure is that FDE involvement helps win deals and also drives post-implementation expansion. The difference is that an FDE's contribution is evaluated not by "deal size" but by "sustained usage and growth resulting from the system working in production."

What matters when considering a career move is that this difference in evaluation criteria separates who is a good fit. People who feel fulfillment at the moment of winning a deal are better suited to pre-sales, while people who feel "it pains me to walk away after the deal is won" or "I want to be thanked by the customer once it's running in production" are suited to FDE.

Situations where pre-sales/CE experience becomes a strength

Pre-sales and customer engineers who transition to FDE have strengths that pure software engineers do not. Of the capabilities demanded in FDE job postings, the following four are cultivated in pre-sales.

1. Designing PoCs and the ability to "win agreement with something that works"

Andpad's FDE job posting (as of October 2026) lists as a required qualification "communication skills based on prototyping thinking, winning agreement with 'something that works.'" Channel Talk's FDE posting also explicitly lists "designing and driving PoCs (Proof of Concept)" as a job duty. The ability to design a PoC by working backward from "what needs to be validated for the customer to make a decision" is a core pre-sales skill, and it applies directly to FDE work.

2. Understanding customer decision-making structures

Who holds the budget, who holds technical veto power, and who will actually use it on the ground. The ability to read this structure is essential for an FDE to identify "what should be built." Notion's Forward Deployed Architect posting in Tokyo (as of October 2026) lists as required qualifications "the ability to translate ambiguous customer goals into clear workflows, implementation plans, solution architectures, and measurable success criteria" and "consulting-style discovery skills: mapping current workflows, identifying customer pain points, understanding operating models, surfacing technical constraints, and defining a practical future vision." This is exactly pre-sales discovery.

3. Enterprise readiness (security, legacy systems, many stakeholders)

Channel Talk's FDE posting lists "proposing and accompanying customers on technical and security aspects, covering everything from legacy systems to the latest SaaS, in Enterprise Deals" as a job duty, and Databricks' posting asks for "experience working with enterprise customers and managing conflicts among a wide range of stakeholders." RevComm's FDE posting (as of October 2026) also requires "the ability to build consensus among multiple stakeholders." Experience coordinating between IT, security, and business departments in enterprise pre-sales is clearly valued in FDE selection processes.

4. Translating between business and technology

Andpad includes in its job duties "translating between business and engineering: bridging the gap between 'what is possible (technology)' and 'what should be done (business)' and drawing up a feasible roadmap," and lists as a required qualification "the translation ability to engage with engineering teams as an equal and convert technical constraints into business value." Translating technology into the language of business challenges, and business challenges into technical requirements, is what pre-sales does every day.

On people from customer success (CSM): Channel Talk includes "CSM" among examples of required backgrounds. Experience watching post-implementation adoption and expansion pays off in situations where the FDE continues supporting customers after production launch. However, if technical implementation experience is limited, the weight placed on the "implementation skills to fill in" discussed in the next section becomes greater.

A benchmark for the implementation skills to fill in

The biggest reason pre-sales professionals fail FDE selection processes is insufficient proof of implementation ability. Japanese FDE job postings vary widely in the level of implementation skill they require. How much you need to fill in depends on which level of posting you target.

From this table, a realistic entry path for those with presales experience becomes clear. Domestic SaaS FDE roles (Andpad, Channel Talk, RevComm) require presales, implementation consulting, or CS experience, and you can enter with implementation skills at the level of "being able to read and write code" and "hands-on API integration and SQL." On the other hand, FDE roles at foreign platform companies (Databricks, Notion FDE) demand production-quality code or senior-level experience, so moving directly from presales requires building up additional implementation experience.

The three minimum things to have

  • 01 Hands-on API integration and data processing: Get to the point where you can say you "wrote" Webhook and REST API integrations, SQL data extraction and joins, and Python preprocessing, rather than just "configured" them. This satisfies the requirements at RevComm, Channel Talk, and Andpad
  • 02 At least one production experience: Take even one project that ended at the PoC stage and create an experience of running it in a production environment, including being involved in incident response and improvements. Options include asking to sit in with the internal implementation team, or volunteering to handle the PoC's migration to production yourself
  • 03 Something with an LLM built in at production-level quality: Build one complete application including authentication, error handling, and evaluation (a mechanism to measure how correctly it works). FDE and Applied AI roles at AI companies like Anthropic and OpenAI place strong emphasis on this

FDE job postings requiring presales experience (as of October 2026)

Here we quote from official job postings of Japanese FDE openings that explicitly list presales or customer-facing technical experience as a mandatory requirement. All descriptions are as of October 2026, so be sure to check the original text before applying.

Andpad Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)

This posting accompanies the launch of "ANDPAD Stellarc," an AI project specialized for the construction industry. The first mandatory requirement is "practical experience as a presales professional, implementation consultant, or systems engineer for IT solutions," followed by "technical literacy to 'read/write code'" and "communication skills with a prototyping mindset, building consensus through 'working things.'" Preferred qualifications include "DX project experience in 'non-desk worker' domains such as construction, manufacturing, and logistics" and "PM/PL experience in large-scale system implementation and integration projects such as ERP overhauls and core system integration," meaning enterprise presales and implementation experience is directly valued. Salary is undisclosed (determined by experience and ability, including fixed overtime pay for 45 hours per month), and the location is Minato-ku, Tokyo.

Channel Talk Development - Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)

A newly created position to "lead implementation, deployment, and utilization from a technical standpoint" as enterprise customers increase. The first mandatory requirement is "experience supporting enterprise customers in the SaaS/IT industry (Presales / Solution Engineer / Technical Consultant / CSM, etc.)," followed by "experience structuring and organizing customer issues and proposing solutions from a technical perspective" and "basic understanding of APIs and system integration." Responsibilities include "supporting API / Webhook / external system integrations," "designing and driving PoCs (Proof of Concept)," and "relaying customer feedback to Product / Engineering teams." The posting describes the FDE as a "technical anchor" connecting customers and the product. Salary and location are not stated in the posting.

RevComm Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)

Mandatory requirements are "experience in customer negotiations in the BtoB SaaS / IT solutions domain," "experience independently driving projects from requirements gathering through design and implementation," "experience with either API integration or data integration," "experience using data with SQL and similar tools," and "the ability to build consensus among multiple stakeholders." Preferred qualifications include "experience working on-site and driving projects at SIers / IT consultancies / BPOs, etc." and "knowledge of the CRM / contact center domain." Responsibilities include "organizing customers' business requirements and structuring them into technical requirements," "designing using API integration / data integration / Webhooks, etc.," and "aligning technical assumptions with PdMs and developers." The wording seeks people who have independently handled "everything from requirements gathering to implementation" rather than presales per se. Salary is determined by experience and ability, and the posting gives an example annual salary of 12 million yen (1 million yen per month: 720,000 yen base salary plus 280,000 yen overtime allowance). The location is Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo, with full remote work available.

Notion Forward Deployed Architect (Tokyo)

Although the title is "Architect," this is the role closest to those with presales or customer engineering experience. Mandatory requirements include "5+ years in technical services/consulting, solution architecture, implementation, customer engineering, or a similar customer-facing role," and while explicitly stating that "writing production code is not required," it asks for "the ability to reason about technical constraints and troubleshoot effectively." The posting describes a division of labor where, in situations requiring deeper engineering (migrations, API constraints, security architecture), the FDA works with Forward Deployed Engineering — a design that pairs the FDE and FDA. Fluency in reading, writing, and speaking Japanese is mandatory, and the posting includes the notation "#LI-Onsite."

As shown here, even among jobs with the same "Forward Deployed" title, the required implementation skills vary greatly by company and role. When transitioning from presales, a realistic path is to first choose a posting where you can enter with your current implementation skills, accumulate production implementation experience after joining, and then move on to more implementation-focused roles or companies.

Reframing your resume

Presales resumes are typically written in terms of "number of projects handled and deal value." In FDE selection processes, this style is unlikely to be evaluated favorably. What gets evaluated is "what you built and how it performed in production," "how you influenced customer decision-making," and "what you fed back to the product." Even with the same experience, you need to change how you tell the story.

Delivered a demo for a major manufacturer, contributing to a deal winIdentify the concerns of the customer's IT, security, and business departments respectively, and write about which technical proof (on-premises connectivity verification, audit log presentation, etc.) moved the decision-making

Built relationships with customersWrite about the process of how you translated vague requests ("we want to do something with AI") into verifiable problems ("target ○% accuracy in inquiry classification")

Especially important is the description of projects that reached production. The New Stack quotes a field FDE as saying, "The model is usually the cleanest part. The hard part is the workflows nobody has documented, the data sources people actually trust, and finding the person who knows why the process is the way it is." If you have experience with this "finding" work beyond the PoC stage, you can speak of it as FDE experience.

The interview question "Why are you leaving presales?"

In FDE interviews, the reason for transitioning from presales is almost always asked. You will be more persuasive if you can speak to one of the following—"It feels unsatisfying that the work ends at the deal win," "I want to take things all the way to running in production myself," or "I want to bring customer-side insights back into the product"—with one project you actually regretted. Conversely, citing "I want to get away from sales targets" or "I got tired of working with sales" as your main reason will lower your evaluation, since FDEs also work closely with sales.

Steps to Transition from Presales to FDE

  • 01 Create one experience of "reaching production" at your current job: Join the production migration of a PoC you designed as part of the implementation team. This will be the core of your resume
  • 02 Prepare proof of implementation skills: Prepare deliverables where you wrote API integrations, SQL, and Python data processing yourself, and build one application incorporating an LLM
  • 03 Decide the level of jobs to target: Depending on your implementation skills, choose between domestic SaaS companies requiring presales experience (Andpad, Channel Talk, RevComm, etc.), foreign companies requiring production-quality code (Notion FDE, Databricks), or AI companies where you own everything from proposal to post-production (Anthropic Applied AI Engineer)
  • 04 Reframe your resume: Write in terms of PoC design, reaching production, how you moved decision-making, and contributions back to the product—not deal size
  • 05 Be able to tell your transition story through one project: Prepare a specific project you "wanted to see through to the end"

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q What is the difference between presales and FDE?

The biggest differences are the endpoint of involvement and code ownership. Presales is a role that resolves technical concerns before the deal closes, building PoCs and demos with anonymized data or in your own company's environment. FDEs write code directly in the customer's production environment after the deal closes and are responsible for stability and improvement after go-live. OpenAI's head of the FDE organization explains that SAs are advisors who rarely write code on the customer's infrastructure, whereas FDEs write code directly on the customer's infrastructure in more ambiguous situations.

Q Can you transition from Sales Engineer to FDE?

Yes. As of October 2026, Andpad lists "practical experience as IT solutions presales, implementation consulting, or systems engineer" and Channel Talk lists experience serving Enterprise customers as "Presales / Solution Engineer / Technical Consultant / CSM, etc." as required qualifications for FDE roles. However, how smoothly you pass the selection process depends greatly on whether you have implementation experience with API integrations, SQL, Python, etc., and experience taking a PoC all the way to production.

Q Are Customer Engineers (CEs) the same as FDEs?

There is a lot of overlap, but they are not the same. Customer Engineers focus on post-implementation technical support and helping customers use existing features, and they usually do not write or own production code in the customer's environment. FDEs handle production implementation and are responsible for feeding the insights gained back into their own product. Notion's Forward Deployed Architect job posting includes "Customer Engineering" experience as a required qualification, so CE experience is valued as an entry point into FDE-type roles.

Q Do FDEs have quotas (sales targets)?

FDEs are evaluated on whether the system is used in production and continues to deliver results, not on deal size. That said, it is not unrelated to sales: the Ramp FDE job posting featured by The Pragmatic Engineer lists working with sales and GTM teams to close deals and expand customer value as part of the role. Understand that contribution is measured not by the deal itself, but by continued use and expansion as a result of things working in production.

Q How much programming skill do you need to transition from presales to FDE?

It varies greatly depending on the jobs you target. Domestic SaaS FDE roles (Andpad, Channel Talk, RevComm) have an entry bar at the level of reading and writing code, API integrations, and using data with SQL. Notion's Tokyo FDE posting requires the ability to write production-quality code, and Databricks' Tokyo FDE posting requires 8+ years of experience, coding in Python, Scala, etc., and deep experience with Apache Spark. At minimum, I recommend preparing one deliverable where you wrote API integrations and data processing yourself, and one experience of something going into production.

Q Can you become an FDE from Customer Success (CS)?

Channel Talk's FDE job posting includes "CSM" among examples of required qualifications, so experience watching post-implementation adoption and expansion is valued. However, since people from CS backgrounds often have limited implementation experience, supplementing it with practical experience in API integrations, SQL, and Python is a prerequisite. Start by expanding the scope of technical configuration and integration work you do with your own hands in your current role.

Q: Is it wrong to include deal size in my resume?

You can include it, but on its own it won't be evaluated. What FDE hiring looks at is: how many of the PoCs you designed made it to production, what challenges you were involved in during the production transition, what technical evidence you used to move customer decision-making, and what requirements you fed back into the product. Keep deal size as supplementary information and reframe your story around these points.
