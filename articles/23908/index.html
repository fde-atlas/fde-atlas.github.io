Key points

  • FDEs take end-to-end responsibility for customer outcomes, not just code quality.

  • Key skills: full-stack breadth plus structuring ambiguous problems and managing expectations.
  • Transition paths differ by background: SWEs need customer exposure; consultants need hands-on building.
  • The main trade-off is less time to deepen any single technical specialty.

What does an FDE engineer do?

The FDE engineer is a role that was systematized by Palantir. At the customer's site, they handle five phases, from problem discovery to adoption and feeding insights back into the product (What is FDE).

FDE engineers (Forward Deployed Engineers) don't treat their company's product as "sold and done." Their role is to get it into a state where it is actually used, within the customer's organization, data, and business workflows. Since the work combines the roles of developer, consultant, and project manager, the scope of duties is broad.

FDE originated at Palantir

The FDE role is widely known as a way of working systematized by Palantir, which provides data analytics platforms.

Palantir has introduced on its official blog and elsewhere that it internally calls Forward Deployed Software Engineers "Delta." FDEs go into the field paired with Deployment Strategists, who handle problem analysis on the customer side (internal title "Echo") (What is a Deployment Strategist). Integrating the data of complex organizations and making it usable for decision-making required more than simply handing over the product. From this, the idea of "forward deploying" engineers to the customer's site was born. An overview of the company is covered in What is Palantir.

FDE engineers' daily work

An FDE's work is a continuous cycle of short iterations: "problem discovery → data connection → implementation → validation in the field."

PhaseMain tasksExample outputs
Problem discoveryField interviews, business observation, requirements alignment with stakeholdersProblem hypotheses, prioritized list
Data connectionIntegration with existing systems and databases, data cleansingData pipelines
ImplementationBuilding applications on the company's product, developing custom featuresBusiness apps, dashboards
Validation and adoptionRollout to field users, incorporating feedbackOperating procedures, improvement backlog
Feedback loopSharing field insights with the product teamFeature requests, proposals for generalization

FDE Soken believes the final "feedback loop" should not be taken lightly. Whether accumulated case-by-case work can be fed back into product improvement determines whether an FDE organization drifts toward contract development or becomes a strength of a product company.

Why FDEs are attracting attention in recent years

In generative AI adoption, the problem of "having the technology but not using it in the field" is prominent, driving demand for FDE-type talent.

Since 2025, AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic have begun posting Forward Deployed Engineer job openings (OpenAI's FDE, Anthropic's FDE). Because LLMs are highly general-purpose, results vary greatly depending on how they are designed into the customer's business workflows. As a role that bridges product and customer with technology, the job title FDE is spreading beyond Palantir.

How do FDE engineers differ from SWEs?

The difference between FDE engineers and SWEs can be organized along FDE Soken's six comparison axes. The biggest difference is that FDEs take responsibility for customer outcomes (Detailed comparison).

Both write code, but the axes on which they are evaluated, where they work, and the types of uncertainty they handle differ.

Differences in scope of responsibility and evaluation criteria

An FDE's results are measured by "whether the customer's business changed." Evaluation is not determined by code quality alone.

Comparison axisFDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)SWE (Software Engineer)
Main responsibilitySolving customer problems and creating resultsProduct feature development and quality
Where they workAt the customer's site, or in an environment close to the customerWithin the company's own development team
Source of requirementsDiscovered and defined by themselves in the fieldOften handed down from PMs or roadmaps
Evaluation criteriaImplementation results, business impact, customer satisfactionDesign quality, development speed, maintainability
Uncertainty handledOrganizational, data, and operational uncertaintyTechnical uncertainty
Lifespan of codeOften short-lived code for validation purposesBuilt for long-term operation

Differences in working style and career

FDEs have many customer conversations and business trips. Experience handling both technology and business becomes a career strength.

While SWEs tend to be evaluated more highly the deeper they go into technology, FDEs are evaluated on the combination of technical breadth and business understanding. It is often said that former FDEs later become product managers, business leaders, or entrepreneurs. Conversely, for people who want to go deep in a single technical area for a long time, the FDE way of working may feel like a detour.

What skills are required of FDE engineers?

FDE engineers need both technical skills across five areas and four business skills. If either is lacking, it becomes difficult to deliver results in the field (How to develop FDEs).

Technical skills: the ability to build broadly and quickly

The technical skills an FDE needs are full-stack-oriented implementation ability: the capacity to quickly turn everything from data processing to the UI into a working product single-handedly.

Skill areaExamplesIndicative importance
Data engineeringSQL, ETL, data modelingHigh
Backend developmentPython, TypeScript, API integrationHigh
FrontendBuilding business application UIsModerate
Cloud and infrastructureAWS, GCP, Azure, permission designModerate
AI and LLM utilizationPrompt design, RAG, evaluation designGrowing in recent years

Existing systems and technology stacks differ from customer to customer. For this reason, my impression is that being able to quickly get used to unfamiliar environments is often more useful than deep knowledge of a specific language.

Business skills: The ability to define problems and move people

The business skill essential to an FDE is the ability to translate frontline language into requirements and move things forward while building consensus.

  • Structuring problems: Extracting the questions to be solved from a vague "something feels off" state
  • Business understanding: Learning the workflows and constraints of the customer's industry, such as manufacturing, logistics, or finance
  • Stakeholder alignment: Coordinating the interests of management, frontline staff, and IT departments
  • Managing expectations: Communicating honestly and early about what can and cannot be done

What makes the biggest difference in practice is managing expectations. If the initial promises are too ambitious, you can lose the customer's trust even if things go well technically.

How does the path to becoming an FDE engineer vary by years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size?

According to FDE Soken's view, whether you are close to being an FDE engineer is determined not by years of experience but by whether you have delivered results through customer-facing work (Can you switch to FDE with no experience?).

Since FDE is a new role, there are few cases of being hired directly with no prior experience. Rather than setting a uniform standard for how many years of experience are needed, it is more realistic to identify what experience is missing from your current work.

Shortcuts by years of experience and current role

The shortcut to becoming an FDE engineer differs by current role. SWEs should add customer-facing experience, while those from SIers or consulting should build up implementation skills.

This table is a guideline assembled by FDE Soken based on the skills organized in this article. It does not represent the hiring requirements of individual companies.

Points to check regarding English ability and company size

English ability and company size are best treated not as conditions that determine fit, but as points to verify in the job postings of prospective employers.

  • English ability: For FDE openings at overseas companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic, check the job description for work location, customer location, and the language used in daily work before applying
  • Company size: More than size itself, check in interviews whether there is a mechanism for "feeding back" frontline insights into the product
  • Salary and number of openings: These figures change frequently, so this article makes no definitive claims. Please check the latest conditions via How to find FDE job openings and each company's official job postings

How do FDE engineers differ from IT consultants, PdMs, and SIers?

Viewed through FDE Soken's three comparison axes—accountability for outcomes, where requirements come from, and whether you implement things yourself—FDE engineers stand apart from other roles in that they handle implementation as well (Differences from consultants).

Comparing roles across the three axes

The boundary between FDE engineers and adjacent roles lies in defining requirements yourself on the frontline, implementing them yourself, and taking responsibility through to results.

The trade-offs of choosing FDE

Choosing FDE gives you experience spanning technology and business. On the other hand, you tend to have less time to deepen a single technology over the long term.

  • What you gain: Experience defining problems on the frontline, a track record of seeing customer businesses actually change, and industry knowledge
  • What you tend to lose: Time to focus on a single technical domain, and an environment where you can develop calmly against fixed requirements
  • Associated burdens: Schedule changes due to customer circumstances, frequent task switching, and business trips or on-site work

What kind of person is suited to being an FDE engineer?

Among the five traits, the people best suited to being FDE engineers are those who can "form hypotheses and act in situations with no clear right answer" (FDE careers).

Beyond skills, whether your attitude toward work and values align with the role is also important.

People suited to FDE

People who are comfortable with ambiguity and can pose their own questions and act tend to thrive as FDEs.

  • Able to form hypotheses and act even when requirements are not yet defined
  • Feel fulfillment in the moment their code is actually used in the field
  • Can choose a solution that helps right now over a technically elegant one
  • Have curiosity about learning industries and business processes
  • Can take harsh feedback from customers positively

People for whom FDE may not be a good fit

For people whose primary motivation is deep technical exploration, or who want to develop in a focused way against fixed requirements, the role can be a heavy burden.

Some of the load can be overcome with experience, but it is also true that forcing yourself to keep adapting tends to lead to burnout. Before taking on FDE, we recommend reflecting on which moments of your work give you a sense of satisfaction.

How can you become an FDE engineer?

The fastest path to becoming an FDE engineer is to build on your implementation skills as an SWE and gradually increase customer touchpoints through five steps (How to Find FDE Job Openings).

Five Steps to Move from SWE to FDE

When moving from SWE to FDE, a realistic approach is to broaden your implementation skills while gradually increasing opportunities to engage with customers.

  • Gain full-stack implementation experience: Build everything from data processing to the UI on your own
  • Raise your hand for internal customer-facing work: Participate in onboarding support, customer success, PoCs, and more
  • Acquire knowledge of one industry: Understand a specific industry's business workflows well enough to explain them
  • Learn to tell your achievements as a story: Organize your track record in the form of "problem → action → business change"
  • Apply for FDE positions: Look for FDE or solutions engineer roles at Palantir, AI companies, and SaaS companies

Key Evaluation Points in the Selection Process and How to Prepare

In FDE hiring processes, in addition to technical interviews, you may face case interviews that assess how you break down ambiguous problems.

Evaluation CriteriaWhat Is AssessedHow to Prepare
Implementation skillsCan you build something that works within limited time?Coding interview prep, personal development projects
Problem decompositionCan you structure ambiguous questions?Practice case problems, articulate your business improvement experience
Customer orientationCan you prioritize outcomes over technology?Prepare concrete episodes of working with customers
CommunicationCan you explain things to non-engineers?Practice explaining technical concepts in plain language

In interviews, talking about "what changed for the customer and how" rather than "something technically impressive" is more likely to convey your suitability as an FDE. Even if your job title wasn't FDE, experience delivering results through onboarding support or PoCs is more than enough to showcase.
