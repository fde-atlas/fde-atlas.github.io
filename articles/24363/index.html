What Is an FDE? | The Meaning and Role of a Forward Deployed Engineer

Original title: FDEとは｜Forward Deployed Engineerの意味と仕事内容

FDE Consulting · 2026-02-26 · 16 min read

Summary

This Japanese article explains the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role: a job born at Palantir in the early 2010s where engineers embed at customer sites, build production systems themselves, and keep fixing them until the business actually uses them. It details a five-stage workflow, contrasts FDEs with SES, SIer, and IT consulting roles, and surveys the 2025–2026 wave of FDE organizations at OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, AWS, and Japanese firms. The author argues FDEs bridge the gap between successful AI pilots and real adoption, while warning buyers about FDE-washing.

Key points

  • FDEs own customer outcomes end-to-end: define KPIs, decompose workflows, build, iterate, and hand over.
  • FT reported FDE job postings grew over 800% in nine months of 2025; Microsoft committed $2.5B and AWS $1B.
  • In Japan, LayerX, Hitachi (1,000-person plan), Fujitsu, and Dentsu Group launched FDE initiatives.
  • Gartner predicts 70% of vendor-built AI agents will be abandoned by 2028 and warns of FDE-washing.
  • Japanese FDE salary listings most often cap at ¥10–20M; US roles at OpenAI reach $300K plus equity.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who goes into the customer's company, talks with the people in charge of operations, builds the system that will be used in production themselves, and keeps fixing it until it is actually used. In Japanese, it is called "Forward Deployed Engineer" (phonetically). The role originated at Palantir Technologies in the US in the early 2010s; from 2025, OpenAI and Anthropic placed FDEs, and in 2026 Microsoft, AWS, Fujitsu, Dentsu Group, and others announced FDE organizations and training programs.

We at FDE Consulting support AI adoption at Japanese companies using this FDE approach. I myself have worked in strategy consulting and have seen situations where sound strategies stalled without ever taking root in operations. I believe FDE is a role born to fill that final gap.

Below, based on information verified as of November 2, 2026, I will cover in order: the meaning and naming of FDE, how it came about, what the work involves, how it differs from similar roles, why it is growing, companies hiring for it, how to become one and salaries, and what to check when commissioning the work.

The Meaning and Naming of FDE

Nikkei Cross Tech defines FDE as "an engineer who deeply understands the customer's operations, organization, and internal systems, clarifies the issues, and implements systems to solve them". In my own words, an FDE is an engineer who takes responsibility for the outcome of a single customer and builds whatever is needed to achieve it.

"Forward Deployed" comes from the military term "forward deployment." It refers to a way of working where people are stationed away from headquarters, at the customer's site, making decisions and building things there.

The naming varies slightly by company and context.

NameUsage

FDSEForward Deployed Software Engineer. The job title at Palantir, where the role originated
Frontier Deployed EngineerThe name used in the training program Anthropic launched in October 2026. It abbreviates to the same FDE
FDE EngineerA name that adds "Engineer" to FDE. Same meaning as FDE

Forward Deployment Engineer (Japanese translation)The Japanese translation. Also called "forward-deployment-type engineer"

Names of roles and organizations used around FDE, such as Delta, Echo, and Deployment Lead, are summarized in the "FDE Glossary".

FDE Was Born at Palantir

The company that created FDE is the US data analytics firm Palantir Technologies. According to Palantir's official blog, the company's engineers fall broadly into two roles: "Dev" and "Delta." Dev is an engineer who delivers "one feature to many customers," while Delta is an engineer who delivers "many features to one customer" (Palantir Blog). This Delta corresponds to the Forward Deployed Software Engineer, i.e., the FDE.

The name Delta is said to be a remnant of the early business development team being referred to using NATO phonetic code (Alpha, Bravo...). Palantir also has another role called "Echo" (Deployment Strategist), which stands on the side of the customer's operations and issues; Echo handles the more PM-oriented role and Delta the more technical one (Palantir Blog).

According to research by The Pragmatic Engineer, the FDE role was created in the early 2010s, and until around 2016 the number of FDEs within Palantir was said to be larger than that of regular software engineers (The Pragmatic Engineer). This means the company allocated people to producing results at customer sites before selling products. I wrote in detail about this business model of feeding what is built in the field back into the product in "What Is the FDE Model?".

What FDEs Do

We have described FDEs on stage as "not people who build AI, but people who make AI used." Building AI is just the means; the FDE's job is to create a state where AI is used every day within operations and the numbers are changing. The actual work proceeds through the following five stages.

1. Identify issues and set KPIs

Take stock of on-site operations and decide which issues to solve. At this point, agree on KPIs: which numbers changing will count as success. In our case, we spend about two weeks on this stage. It is rare for "what to build with AI" to be decided at the time of the request. Starting from what is undecided is what FDE is about.

2. Break down the operations and decide what to delegate to AI

With a large unit like "introducing AI into tax filing," you cannot decide what to delegate to AI and what humans should judge. So we break the work down into individual steps. Here is an example from an accounting firm.

StepContentDegree suitable for AI
ReceiptReceiving documents from the clientLow (involves human contact)
SortingAssigning to ledger accountsHigh
ReviewJudging exceptions and correctionsMedium (AI proposes candidates, humans make the final call)
EntryTranscribing into the accounting systemHigh
AuditChecking by senior staffLow (judgments involving responsibility)

Only after breaking things down this far can you decide which steps to automate, in what order, and to what extent. Introducing AI while skipping the breakdown of operations tends to leave you stuck at the PoC stage. I wrote about the approach in "Breaking Out of AI PoC Stalls" and "Requirements Definition for AI Development".

3. Build something that works and have the field use it

Once the design is set, the FDE themselves writes the code and builds the system. Because the person who proposes and the person who builds are the same, the intent of the design is not lost in handoffs. We use AI coding tools such as Claude Code and Codex, building in short cycles and testing in the field.

4. Keep fixing until the KPIs move

LLMs and AI agents return slightly different answers each time, even to the same input. It is not a matter of building to spec and being done. You create evaluation criteria for what counts as a passing answer, pick up exceptions from the field, and steadily improve accuracy and usability. I summarized how to build evaluation criteria in "How to Build LLM Evaluations (Evals)".

5. Make it stick and hand it over

Finally, we document the usage rules on-site and hand over operations to the customer's team. Looking back at the projects we have supported, companies where AI takes hold share three common traits: "rules first, tools second," "usage is explicitly documented on-site," and "management clearly states that it must be used." My job is complete only when these three are in place and things run even without an FDE.

How FDE differs from similar roles

FDE is often confused with SES (on-site dispatch), because it works at the customer's site, and with IT consulting, because it makes recommendations. Here is the difference in one line each.

Role/ModelDifference from FDE
SES (on-site dispatch)Takes on tasks decided by the customer, billed per person-month. An FDE decides what to build from scratch and is accountable for business metrics
SIer (contract development)Builds after specifications are fixed, and the contract ends at delivery. An FDE discovers specifications on-site and stays until results appear
IT consultantFocuses on recommendations and hands implementation to another company. An FDE implements what they themselves recommended
Solution architectFocuses on designing and proposing configurations using their own products. An FDE implements and remains on-site even after deployment
Sales engineerFocuses on technical explanations and validation before contracts. An FDE enters the site after the contract and builds
AI engineerA technical specialist who builds AI systems. An FDE embeds them into business operations and drives them to actual use
Data scientistImproves analysis and model accuracy. An FDE incorporates those results into business procedures

Why FDEs are increasing now

AI can now be built, but it is not being used in business

Generative AI has greatly reduced the effort of building AI itself. On the other hand, many companies see PoCs succeed but never move to production, or deploy AI that is never used on-site. Most causes lie outside the technology: no defined use case, work runs fine without it, no one owns operations, KPIs stop at usage counts. All are problems of organizational and business design.

This gap cannot be closed by simply adding a high-performance AI model. Only someone who understands both technology and business can enter the site and close it. That is why FDEs are in demand. The reasons AI fails to take hold and the countermeasures are covered in "Why AI implementations fail".

Job postings grew over 800% in less than a year

According to a report by the UK's Financial Times based on Indeed data, FDE job postings grew over 800% between January and September 2025. Subsequent Indeed data reportedly showed postings in April 2026 up about 729% year over year (Business Insider).

Companies that build AI are creating separate organizations to put AI into customers' operations. I see this as evidence that the builders themselves have concluded that model performance alone does not translate into business results.

In June 2025, US venture capital firm a16z published a piece calling the FDE "the hottest job in startups" (a16z). Its argument: while margins drop due to labor intensity, deep involvement in customers' operations becomes a competitive advantage.

Gartner warned about FDEs in name only

According to a report by Publickey (October 7, 2026), US research firm Gartner predicted that 70% of AI agents built by vendors' FDEs will no longer be used by enterprises by 2028 (Publickey). It also pointed to growing risk of "FDE-washing," where implementation work and AI consulting are sold under the FDE name. Gartner recommends narrowing the problems assigned to FDEs, transferring FDE knowledge to internal staff and end users, and deciding the exit plan upfront.

As the term FDE spreads, the same name gets attached to services with different substance. What clients should verify is covered later in "What to check when hiring an FDE".

In Japan, FDE is starting to be used in two senses. One is a role, like at Palantir and OpenAI, that embeds a company's own products with customers. The other is a new form of support from SIers and consulting firms, where value is measured by outcomes instead of person-months. We at FDE Consulting fall into the latter category: we have no products of our own and start from the customer's challenges.

Required Skills and Career Paths

Required Skills

We think of the capabilities an FDE needs in three categories: Business, Technology, and Creativity.

AreaDescription
Business (understanding the business)Understands the customer's revenue structure, KPIs, and decision-making flows, and can explain technical results in terms of return on investment. Can move people from executives to frontline staff
Technology (technical implementation)Can get hands-on with everything from designing and evaluating LLMs and AI agents to full-stack development, integration with existing systems, and security
Creativity (structuring problems)Can break vague pain points down into solvable problems and determine what to automate and what humans should decide

Of the three, I believe the hardest to develop is Creativity, that is, the ability to frame questions from ambiguous situations. Technology can be learned, and industry knowledge comes on the job. The ability to identify the problems truly worth solving can only be forged through experience.

The people suited to this role are those who find messy real-world environments more interesting than polished specifications. Data is scattered. Permissions are complicated. Exception handling exists only in certain people's heads. This role suits people who feel a sense of accomplishment in turning such environments into working systems and smooth operations. I wrote about how to think about this career in "Forward Deployed Engineer: A New Career."

No Certifications Required

There are no national or public certifications for FDEs. In the private sector, the Japan FDE Association has announced the "FDE Certification" (levels 5 through 1), and the IoT Certification Committee has announced an "FDE Certification Program" (three tiers). As of November 2, 2026, the Japan FDE Association is designing the exam format and preparing the exam fee (Japan FDE Association). The IoT Certification Committee's Level 1 is planned to have an exam fee of 19,800 yen and 100 questions over 60 minutes, with the main exam scheduled to start in October 2026, though the application opening date and schedule are to be announced later (IoT Certification Committee).

What hiring managers look at is not certifications but experience finding problems in the field, implementing solutions, and delivering results. I have summarized the content of the two certifications in "What Is the FDE Certification?" and how to build skills and prepare in "How to Become an FDE."

What Backgrounds Lead to FDE

There are still very few people in Japan with FDE experience. So transitioning from an adjacent role is the realistic path. AI engineers, IT consultants, product managers, and solution engineers at SaaS companies each already possess one of Business, Technology, or Creativity. They just need to fill in the missing areas on the job.

I have covered companies hiring and how to read job postings in "FDE Jobs and Career Change Guide," what gets asked in interviews in "FDE Interview Questions," and working without belonging to a company in "FDE Freelancing." We are also hiring teammates to work with us as FDEs.

What to Verify When Hiring an FDE

An FDE is not a fit for everything. When the deliverables and specifications are already fixed and only manpower is lacking, it is more rational to use an SES or SIer. FDEs perform best at companies in situations like the following.

  • The AI PoC succeeded, but the project has not moved on to production deployment
  • No one in-house can implement AI. Beyond advice, someone who writes code hands-on is needed
  • The business has many exceptions, and standard tools alone cannot handle the workflow
  • The company wants to build AI in-house but has no one who can create the initial blueprint

Before hiring, it is a good idea to verify the following three points. They also overlap with the Gartner recommendations mentioned above.

  • Does the person who made the proposal write the code themselves and build it all the way to production?
  • Is there a plan to transfer knowledge so that in-house staff can maintain what was built?
  • Are the timing and conditions for the external FDE's departure defined at the start of the contract?
