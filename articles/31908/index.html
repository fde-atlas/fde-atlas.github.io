C25: How a one-week schedule limits new commitments

Original title: C25：一周排期怎样限制新的承诺

pafa.github.io · 2026-11-04 · 1 min read

Summary

Using a synthetic maintenance project as an example, this article shows how a one-week schedule constrains new commitments. Filled schedule hours sum by role: FDE 40, customer systems engineer 12, maintenance supervisor 6, plus 4 for a product contact. The CSV's available totals repeat per role and cannot be summed row by row as incremental capacity. Region two lacks 16, 6, and 2 hours for a simultaneous two-site launch, so R2 is deferred. The alternative requires the supervisor to approve delaying R1 development and cuts FDE development from 18 to 14 hours, freeing 4 hours for observation, keeping the total at 40 with no launch commitment and no silent reduction of incident-response reserve. If the FDE takes 8 hours off, existing commitments already exceed capacity; defer work or add reliable backup rather than filling gaps with assumed efficiency gains. The product contact judges shared capability but does not decide business scope for the customer.

Key points

  • Schedule hours sum by role: FDE 40, systems engineer 12, supervisor 6.
  • CSV available totals repeat per role; row-by-row sums are not new capacity.
  • Two-site launch lacks 16, 6, 2 hours, so R2 is deferred.
  • Alternative cuts FDE development 18 to 14, freeing 4 observation hours at total 40.
  • An 8-hour FDE absence exceeds capacity; defer or add backup, don't assume efficiency.

Full text

This is a synthetic maintenance project; the hours are not industry-standard. The filled schedule sums by role: FDE 40, customer systems engineer 12, maintenance supervisor 6, plus 4 for the product contact. The CSV shows available totals repeated per role, so you cannot sum rows as new capacity.

The second-region decision states that launching both sites simultaneously is still short 16, 6, and 2 hours; the choice is to defer R2. The alternative only applies if the supervisor agrees to postpone R1 development and local observation conditions are in place: reduce FDE development from 18 to 14, freeing 4 hours for observation. The total remains 40, with no launch commitment and no quietly taking away the incident-response reserve.

Use the blank table to change one item: if the FDE takes 8 hours of leave this week, the existing 40-hour commitment already exceeds capacity. Explicitly defer work or add reliable backup coverage before discussing R2; do not use "improved efficiency" to automatically fill the gap. The product contact is responsible for judging shared capabilities, not for deciding business scope on behalf of the customer supervisor.
