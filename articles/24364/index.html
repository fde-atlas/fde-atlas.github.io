What's the Difference Between FDE and SES? A Comparison with SIers, Consultants, and Data Scientists

Original title: FDEとSESの違いとは？SIer・コンサル・データサイエンティストと比較

FDE Consulting · 2026-09-29 · 8 min read

Summary

This article answers the common question of how FDEs differ from on-site SES engineers, comparing six roles—FDE, SES, SIer, IT consultant, data scientist, and solutions architect—in a single table. The author argues the real distinction is not physical presence at the client but who decides what to build and how the work ends: FDEs start before requirements exist, co-define solutions with clients, and finish only when business KPIs move. They handle probabilistic systems like LLMs and AI agents, building evals first and shipping working prototypes in 2–6 weeks. The piece closes with five questions to help buyers identify SES work masquerading as FDE.

Key points

  • FDE differs from SES in decision authority and completion criteria, not on-site presence.
  • FDE work ends when KPIs improve; SES is billed per person-month until contract expiry.
  • FDEs handle probabilistic LLM/AI agent systems, building evals before deployment.
  • Unlike SIer's fixed specs, FDEs ship prototypes in 2–6 weeks and iterate on-site.
  • Five pre-contract questions help spot client-side dispatch work labeled as FDE.

"How is that different from on-site client staffing?"

Whenever I explain FDE Consulting, I get asked this. I completely understand the sentiment. Both FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) and SES engineers work on-site at clients. From the outside, they look the same.

The difference is not "whether you're on-site." It's who decides what to build, and what marks the end of the work. These two factors clearly separate SES, SIer, IT consulting, data scientists, solution architects, and FDEs.

The definition of FDE itself is summarized in "What is an FDE? Meaning, job description, and differences from SES". Here, I'll focus on the comparison.

The most important rows in the table are the first and third. For SES and SIer, work begins after "what to build" has been decided. For FDEs, work begins before "what to build" has been decided. And only FDEs judge the end of their work not by delivery or contract expiration, but by "whether the business numbers moved."

The difference between FDEs and SES (on-site client staffing)

Both are on-site. The difference is decision-making authority

SES is a model where engineers execute tasks decided by the client. The client's project manager decides the specifications, and SES engineers do the best work within the scope of their instructions. This is not a bad model. When the work is already defined and you're short on hands, there's no more rational option than SES.

FDEs don't wait for instructions. They start from on-site problems that haven't even taken shape as issues yet, such as "too many expense reimbursement rejections" or "inquiry handling can't keep up." They break down the business processes, decide which parts to delegate to AI and which to human judgment, build it, get people to use it, and fix it. Deciding what to build is itself part of the job.

Paying by person-month or paying for results

SES compensation is based on person-month rates. The basis for billing is how many hours the engineer worked.

FDEs link compensation to results as much as possible. In our case, the basic structure combines a fixed monthly fee with performance-based compensation tied to pre-agreed KPI improvements. Whether to include performance-based pay depends on the project, but at minimum, "which KPIs must move and how for the project to count as a success" is decided before the contract.

To be honest here, if you look only at the contract form, our FDE support is also basically the same quasi-delegation type as SES. The two cannot be distinguished by contract format alone. The difference lies in whether performance metrics are agreed upon before the contract, and whether there is a system in place to keep tracking those metrics.

Deterministic systems and probabilistic systems

Business systems and infrastructure, the main workplaces of SES, return the same output for the same input. Working exactly as written in the specification is the correct answer.

The LLMs and AI agents that FDEs handle return slightly different answers to the same question every time. At the time the specification is written, you don't know whether it will actually be usable. That's why FDEs first build an evaluation framework (what counts as an acceptable answer) and improve accuracy by testing in the field. This "technology for bringing uncertain things into business operations" is the technical difference that separates FDEs from SES.

Nikkei Cross Tech positions FDEs as "similar to but distinct from on-site client staffing," describing SES resident engineers as labor-intensive, handling downstream processes like programming and testing, while FDEs are knowledge-intensive elite small teams strong in upstream processes like problem clarification and requirements definition. This matches what I've seen in the field.

The difference between FDEs and SIer (contract development)

The SIer's job is to finalize specifications at the requirements definition stage, build the system to those specifications, and deliver it. For things that should be built after solidifying the specifications, like large-scale core systems, this approach is the most reliable.

With AI implementation, this "solidify the spec first" approach struggles to work. Because until you actually try using AI in the field, you don't know which parts of which processes can truly be replaced. I've heard the story many times: three months on requirements definition, six months on development, and by the time it was delivered, the on-site operations had already changed.

FDEs build something that works in 2 to 6 weeks, get it used in the field, look at the numbers, and fix it. Specifications are not something to fix at the start, but something to discover in the field. Delivery is merely a milestone; the goal is for the business numbers to move.

The difference between FDEs and IT consultants

The IT consultant's main battlefield is analyzing business problems and designing the ideal state. Their ability to map out "what should be done" through extensive research and analysis is often superior to FDEs. If you're uncertain about the management decision itself, hiring a consultant is the right call.

The difference lies in what comes after the proposal. When a consultant's proposal says "AI can reduce inquiry handling workload by 30%," who builds that system, and who makes it stick in the field? In most cases, implementation is handed off to an SIer or SES. In this handoff, the intent of the proposal and the realities of the field leak away.

FDEs write the code themselves. Because the same person handles both the consultant's "thinking" and the engineer's "building," nothing is lost in the handoff. Our deliverables are not proposals, but business systems running in production and the KPIs that moved.

For types of AI consultants and how to choose, see "How to choose an AI consultant" for a detailed comparison.

The difference between FDEs and data scientists

A data scientist is a specialist who analyzes data and improves the accuracy of predictive models. Predicting with high accuracy "which customers are likely to churn" is the job of a data scientist.

What an FDE cares about is whether that prediction is actually used in business operations. Even if a list of customers likely to churn arrives every week, nothing changes if the sales team never looks at it. The FDE makes sure the list appears on the sales team's usual screen, attaches the order and reasons for who to contact, and follows up on whether sales actually took action.

A model with 80% accuracy that is used every day delivers greater results than a model with 90% accuracy that goes unused. Whether you operate on this premise is what separates a data scientist from an FDE.

The difference between an FDE and a solution architect

A solution architect (also called a sales engineer or pre-sales engineer) is responsible for the design and proposals needed to introduce a company's products or cloud services to customers. Their work centers on the pre-implementation phase, and implementation is often carried out by the customers themselves or partner companies.

An FDE not only designs but also implements the solution themselves and remains on-site even after deployment. While a solution architect's work is focused on "until the implementation is decided," an FDE's work continues "until it is actually used every day in the field."

5 questions to distinguish genuine FDEs from on-site contractors

As the term FDE spreads, we often hear the question, "Isn't this just the same as on-site SES contractors?" The Levatech LAB interviews with LayerX and Loglass also address such doubts raised on social media. Since the name alone doesn't tell you anything, ask the following five questions before placing an order.

  • What will you decide in the first two weeks? — If the answer is "just give us the requirements," that's the conventional model. An FDE starts with an inventory of business operations and KPI design.
  • Will you agree on the definition of success in numbers before signing the contract? — If not, the engagement will end with "expiration of the contract period."
  • Who will write the code? — If the person who proposes and the person who builds are different, intent gets lost in the handover.
  • Will you speak directly with the people who do the work on-site? — If you only talk to the IT department or a point of contact, you won't capture the exceptions that occur in actual operations.
  • How will it end? — Is the internalization or handover of operations designed in from the start? For an FDE, the job isn't done until they've built a state where things run smoothly even without them.

Which one should you choose?

Current situationBest-fit option
What to build and the specifications are already decided. Only manpower is lackingSES
Want to build a large-scale core system with fixed specificationsSIer
Want to decide as management which areas to invest inIT consulting
Want to improve the accuracy of predictive models or analysisData scientist
Want to implement a specific product or cloud serviceSolution architect at the product vendor
Want to introduce AI but haven't decided what to build. Stuck at PoC. Many operational exceptions that a standard implementation can't handleFDE

None of these is inherently superior. The most important thing is to choose the model that fits the nature of your problem. If you're unsure, start by considering whether your current challenge is "already decided in terms of what to build." If it isn't, that's where an FDE shines most.
