What is AX? | The Meaning of AI Transformation and How It Differs from DX

Original title: AXとは｜AIトランスフォーメーションの意味とDXとの違い

AX (AI Transformation) is the fundamental rethinking of decision-making and how work is done, with AI as the premise. The Artificial Intelligence Basic Plan (Phase II), approved by the Cabinet on July 14, 2026, carries the subtitle "Japan's AX: Stronger and Richer," and calls for advancing AX in both government administration and industry.

On the surface, it looks like what came after DX (Digital Transformation). But what actually differs? Is it AX just because you add AI? Here, I verify the meaning from government documents and corporate announcements, and organize the differences from DX, where Japanese companies currently stand, and how to proceed. I write from the standpoint of an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) who goes on-site and takes responsibility through to production for rebuilding operations with AI. The role of FDE is explained in What is FDE? The meaning and work of a Forward Deployed Engineer.

The meaning of AX — how the government and companies use it

As a definition of AX, the most readily citable source right now is the government's document. The Artificial Intelligence Basic Plan (Phase II) states the following in the basic vision in Chapter 1.

In every organization, we will advance "AI Transformation (AX)," which fundamentally rethinks decision-making and how work is done with AI as the premise. (Cabinet Office, "Artificial Intelligence Basic Plan," Phase II, approved by the Cabinet on July 14, 2026. Full-width Latin letters in the original converted to half-width)

The same plan, premised on society as a whole working on AX, calls for redesigning how humans and AI collaborate as well as regulations and institutions, and positions Japan's path to winning in the implementation of domain-specific "Vertical AI" usable in industry and government operations, and "Physical AI" that creates value in the real world. It also states that, starting from the social implementation of agentic AI, large problem-solving effects can be expected from promoting AX. At the Artificial Intelligence Strategy Headquarters meeting on July 10, 2026, prior to the plan's approval, the Prime Minister said, "We will start with government administration first."

The Phase I plan, approved by the Cabinet on December 23, 2025, does not contain the abbreviation "AX." Instead, "AI Transformation" appears once, with a footnote defining it as using AI to transform products, services, and business models based on the needs of customers and society, and to transform operations themselves, organizations, processes, and corporate culture and climate in order to establish competitive advantage. This definition takes the DX definition (shown later) and replaces "data and digital technologies" with "AI."

On the corporate side, the term has been in use for a bit longer.

AnnouncementTimingHow AX is used
SoftBank (Gen-AX Inc.)July 1, 2024Announced as a company supporting corporate AI Transformation (AX). Notes that AX refers to efforts aiming to improve efficiency of operations and the like by embedding AI into business processes
Money ForwardApril 2025 (reported by ITmedia AI+)Launched "AX Consulting" for back-office business improvement. States it is promoting AX rather than DX
Hitachi SolutionsOfficial page (no date given)Explains that it advances AI Transformation (AX) under the slogan "DX by AX toward SX"

The government's definition is "fundamentally rethinking decision-making and how work is done," while the corporate note says "improving efficiency of operations" — there is a range in strength. In my view, things that stop at efficiency improvement do not need to be called AX. It is worth calling AX when, with AI as a given, you rebuild who makes which decisions and in what order work flows.

The difference from DX

DX has a definition in METI's Digital Governance Code 3.0 (revised September 19, 2024): companies responding to drastic changes in the business environment, using data and digital technologies to transform products, services, and business models based on the needs of customers and society, while transforming operations themselves, organizations, processes, and corporate culture and climate, to establish competitive advantage.

Comparing only the definitional sentences, the difference between DX and AX appears to be merely "data and digital technologies" versus "AI." At a study session reported by ITmedia Executive in April 2026, Kota Saito of Algomatic defined AX as "transformation that automates an entire specific workflow end-to-end," whereas conventional DX had "replacing part of the work with systems" as its scope. I hold a similar view. The differences can be tabulated as follows.

DX AX
Unit of changeTasks (input, aggregation, routing for approval)The entire flow of the operation and the decisions within it
Role of the systemRuns fixed procedures accurately and quicklyReads documents, creates drafts, and advances parts of the procedure on its own
Role of peopleInput into the system and judge the resultsVerify AI output and handle exceptions and final decisions
How to buildDecide requirements first, then buildDecide accuracy and scope through trial and error
Key management pointsSystem uptime and accuracyWrong answers, handling of information, authorities granted to AI

This table is my own way of organizing things. AX does not replace DX. For operations where data is not in order, or where paper and hanko seals remain, DX-stage cleanup is needed before rebuilding with AI as the premise.

Where Japanese companies stand

Tool adoption has progressed. According to the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications' 2026 (Reiwa 8) Information and Communications White Paper (corporate survey conducted January–February 2026), 86.4% of Japanese companies said they use generative AI in some of their operations. Companies answering that they have a policy to "actively use" generative AI or "use it in limited areas" were 68.9%, up from 49.7% in the previous year's survey, but still lower than the US, Germany, and China.

According to IPA's "DX Trends 2026" (1,799 domestic companies, survey conducted April–June 2026), companies that have "implemented" generative AI grew from 22.6% in the 2024 survey to 44.0% in the 2025 survey. On the other hand, results are still small.

Results from IPA's "DX Trends 2026"Figures
Combined "more than expected" and "as expected" for AI implementation effects31.8%
As a concrete effect, "operations became more efficient or faster"91.6%
As a concrete effect, "sales or profits improved"3.9%

The same report states that the use of AI agents remains at low levels in every department. In my view, this is the gap between the stage of "having introduced AI" and the stage of "having rebuilt operations with AI as the premise." Efficiency effects are appearing, but they have not reached sales or profits. This is because the flow of the operations themselves has not been changed. I wrote about where AI implementation tends to stall in Why AI implementation fails.

How to proceed with AX — five stages

In my view, AX moves faster when you start by rebuilding a single business process rather than starting from a company-wide vision. I've divided the approach into five stages.

StageWhat to doDefinition of done
1. Choose a processPick one process that has high volume, criteria that can be put into words, and effects that can be measured numericallyThe process owner and manager are decided
2. Set the numbersMeasure the numbers you want to move and their current values, such as processing time, volume, and error rateOne target number is decided
3. Redesign the processRewrite the procedures and roles assuming AI is in place. Separate the decisions delegated to AI from those kept by humansThere is a new procedure document and a list of authorities granted to AI
4. Run it in productionDeploy small in production, record and fix incorrect answers and exceptions. Set rules for handling information and permissionsStaff use it daily and the numbers are being measured
5. Scale itTransfer the proven model to adjacent processes. Build an operational structureThe same model is working in a second process

The third stage is the dividing line from DX. If you just replace part of the work with AI while keeping the current procedures, you'll stop at mere efficiency gains. How to redesign the process flow is covered in AI Business Flow Design, and how to set the numbers to move is covered in KPI-Driven AI Adoption.

The fourth stage requires rules and structure. If you delegate work to AI agents, you need to decide what they may read, what they may do, and what they must not do. Building an internal structure is covered in Building an AI Governance Structure, and the steps for introducing AI agents are summarized in Introducing AI Agents.

People Who Drive AX

Looking at the stages of AX makes clear what kind of people you need: people who understand the business, know what AI can do, build and run it in production, and measure the numbers. Neither someone who only does planning nor someone who only builds what they're told is enough.

In my view, this is exactly the job of an FDE. They go into the field, observe the process flow, decide where to introduce AI, build and run it in production, and keep fixing it until the numbers move. If you have someone in-house who can fill this role, entrusting them with the first process is the fastest path. If not, you can run the first one with outside help while transferring the model to in-house staff in the meantime. How much we take on in the field is described in Service Details.
