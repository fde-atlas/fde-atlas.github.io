The American-style "FDE" is similar to but distinct from Japan's on-site client work; companies need to rethink their understanding.

Original title: 米国流「FDE」は日本の客先常駐とは似て非なるもの、企業は認識を改めよ

日経クロステック（Xtech） · 2025-12-05 · 3 min read

Summary

This Nikkei Cross Tech column argues that the US-style Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) differs fundamentally from Japan's resident engineer (client-site dispatch) model, and the author admits having conflated the two. The article uses Treasure Data as a case study: the CDP SaaS vendor set up an FDE team in the first half of 2025 to help customers build custom AI agents using AI Agent Foundry, released in 2024. Co-founder and CEO Kazuki Ota explains the key distinction: SaaS is deterministic (input A always yields output B), while LLM-based AI agents are probabilistic, producing varying outputs from the same input. This shift, he says, is why FDEs are needed on-site at customer marketing departments.

  • FDEs support customers' marketing departments in developing bespoke AI agents.

Full text

AI (artificial intelligence) tool and SaaS (Software as a Service) vendors are putting effort into training and deploying Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs). Japan's IT industry has a very similar arrangement called "on-site client assignment" (kyakusaki jōzoku), but the role required of an FDE is quite different. It seems that many of us, including this author, need to update our understanding.

FDEs are engineers from IT vendors who embed themselves in user companies' business departments, gather on-the-ground needs, and develop custom applications. Just recently, this very author described them in a column as "a system development model similar to Japan's 'on-site client assignment' model."

However, when we spoke with IT vendors that are actually training FDEs and deploying them to user companies' sites, it became clear that the reality differs greatly from the traditional on-site client assignment model.

One company that established an FDE team internally in the first half of 2025 and began deploying it to user companies is Treasure Data, based in Silicon Valley in the US. The company offers a SaaS CDP (Customer Data Platform) that centrally manages customer data collected from various sources and leverages it for marketing analysis.

In 2024, Treasure Data began offering "AI Agent Foundry," a development tool for AI agents that utilize customer data accumulated in its CDP. This tool works by linking Treasure Data's pre-developed AI agent, the "Treasure Data AI Agent," to develop AI agents unique to each user company.

Examples of AI agents that Treasure Data has prepared in advance include the "Audience Agent," which classifies (segments) customers based on attributes and behavioral history, and the "Deep Research Agent," which analyzes internal and external data and then plans campaigns for each target customer. Without writing SQL queries or the like, user companies can run various types of data analysis simply by sending instructions (prompts) to the AI agents.

By combining these Treasure Data AI agents, users of AI Agent Foundry can create their own "marketing effectiveness analysis agents," "campaign email creation agents," "campaign email effectiveness analysis agents," and more.

Traditional SaaS and AI agents differ greatly in their characteristics

Treasure Data's FDEs work by going to the front lines, such as user companies' marketing departments, and supporting the development of AI agents tailored to each company. Kazuki Ota, CEO and co-founder of Treasure Data, said, "Because traditional SaaS and AI agents differ greatly in their characteristics, an FDE team became necessary."

What exactly differs? According to CEO Ota, the biggest difference is that SaaS is a deterministic system where "if you input A, B is always output," whereas AI agents based on LLMs (large language models) are probabilistic systems where "even with the same input A, the output may be B, or D, or G."
