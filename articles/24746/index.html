  • Managers need adaptive planning; engineers need autonomy and TDD skills.
  • Organizational reform: pilot teams, executive backing, low-code tools.

Agile development has two drawbacks.

- The project direction tends to change easily

- Schedule management is difficult

Agile development is designed to respond flexibly and adaptively with speed, which is why it is said to make it difficult to keep a grip on direction and manage schedules.

However, these two drawbacks can be sufficiently addressed depending on the development approach.

In this article, we will introduce solutions for how to eliminate these drawbacks while leveraging the strengths of agile development.

Here we introduce two drawbacks of agile development.

In agile development, work begins without deciding detailed specifications in the early stages, so as improvements are repeated to meet user needs, the project can end up deviating significantly from the original development plan.

When DeFactory developed its own product, we also started from a state of "not knowing what we didn't know."

When deciding the direction of the first project, we started from questions like "What purpose and goals should we have, what should we do, and what perspective should we take?", which consumed a great deal of development effort.

The method DeFactory used to determine project direction is as follows.

1. Requirements definition

2. Market research (market viability hypothesis)

3. Business research (business viability hypothesis)

4. Competitive advantage research (advantage hypothesis)

5. Feasibility research (feasibility hypothesis)

6. Verification

In the initial requirements definition, we carefully define how to design the features users want, the approximate cost and development period, and what the bottlenecks will be.

- Purpose

- Users

- Hardware and software design

- Target performance

- Constraints

By summarizing these into a requirements definition using tables and diagrams, the content becomes easy for team members to understand.

Based on this definition, we align the project direction with the client, but as work progresses, the requirements definition may sometimes change.

Also, to aim for launch in short cycles of about two weeks to one month, accurate input of requirement definitions from the development side and a client-side acceptance structure that enables close, rapid communication are required.

If work begins without such an acceptance structure in place, opportunities for communication become scarce, the implementation may differ from user requests, and critical issues may arise.

Keep in mind that in agile development, repeated improvement loops can change the project direction and lead to costs exceeding expectations.

In agile development, since "requirements → development → testing" is carried out in short cycles, it can become difficult to grasp schedules and progress, and in some cases the original development deadline cannot be met.

For example, development effort may be spent on unexpected areas such as user feedback or unimplemented parts discovered during the cycles, leaving no time for the planned feature implementation, resulting in a launch that missed the schedule.

Causes of delays in schedule management include insufficient fundamental understanding of agile development on both the development and user sides.

To organize the requirements to be implemented in a short period and accurately communicate them to the development side, communication must continue not only at the start of project development but throughout development.

In addition, users need to understand the product's mechanisms and features, and must secure time to actually use the software and build that understanding.

Since users have to secure development effort while handling their daily work, if it is done without proper advance explanation, the benefits of agile development are diminished.

In this way, agile development involves frequent interaction between the development side and the user side, making schedule management on both sides important.

In the previous section, we presented two drawbacks of agile development: "the project direction tends to change easily" and "schedule management is difficult."

Here are methods to resolve these two drawbacks.

For the first drawback, we mentioned that "the project direction tends to change easily."

To set clear objectives and goals and quickly realize rapidly changing user requirements, it is important to assign a "manager" and "engineers" capable of direction.

A "manager" needs the skills to prioritize member initiative, delegate decision-making authority—such as rules for agile development—to engineers, and enable the engineering team to think for themselves and deliver results.

<Skills Required of Managers>

- Adaptive planning

- Agile estimation

- Value-based prioritization

- Evaluation design

- Risk management

Listen to the concerns of engineers and users, draw out ideas, and put in place a structure that allows members to perform at their best.

On the other hand, "engineers" are expected to actually carry out product development and continuously increase the product's value.

In agile development, roles are not divided in detail among members, so each individual is expected to act autonomously while leveraging their own strengths.

Also, since agile development involves thinking and developing in an end-to-end manner, everyone needs to share a common understanding of what they are building together, making communication and teamwork especially important.

<Skills Required of Engineers>

- Pair programming

- Test-driven development

- Value stream mapping

- Collaborative communication

We have introduced the skills required of managers and engineers in agile development. You may feel that a great many skills are required.

At DeFactory, we have managers who can develop products from a business perspective, as well as infrastructure, front-end, and back-end engineers with more than five years of practical experience, and we are committed to supporting our clients side by side throughout their projects.

To keep a firm grip on the direction of a project, member assignment is crucial. If it is difficult to handle in-house, turning to an external vendor is one option worth considering.

As the second disadvantage, we mentioned that "agile development makes schedule management difficult." One solution is to change the organizational structure.

There are four key points when changing the organizational structure.

- Create a pilot team

- Have top management actively involved

- Accept that building an organization takes time

- Adopt low-code development

A pilot team means establishing a separate management structure for app development, apart from the existing organization. This allows the team to operate on a track independent of existing schedule management.

It is also a good idea to have this team report directly to top management, making close communication easier.

When shifting away from the existing schedule-based approach, there is a possibility of pushback from staff on the ground. However, having executives with strong authority on board should make it easier to gain cooperation from others.

In addition, adopting low-code development is one way to make schedules easier to control.

Low-code development is an approach that enables rapid application development while writing as little source code as possible.

Since it reduces development workload, it makes it easier to handle situations typical of agile development, such as short deadlines and resource shortages.

Here we introduce a success story that brilliantly overcame the disadvantages of agile development.

A company providing video lesson services for junior and senior high school students wanted to create lessons that would allow students to freely take classes on their smartphones or tablets.

At a time when online learning was not yet widespread, the company planned a video lesson service, but since it lacked the know-how, it decided to outsource to an external vendor.

First, based on the know-how accumulated through its lessons and behavioral survey data on which students were studying at what times, the company designed the service for efficient studying.

During development, through repeated trial and error, ideas such as "shake your smartphone to ask the instructor a question" were also put to use.

By having the vendor and the client work as one and repeatedly practice and improve, this project can be said to have brilliantly overcome the "project delays" often seen in agile development.
