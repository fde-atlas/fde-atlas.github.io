Why Are FDEs on the Rise? Explaining the Background Behind the Global Boom

Original title: FDEはなぜ増えている？世界的ブームの背景を解説

株式会社Sai · 2026-07-25 · 17 min read

Summary

Drawing only on annual reports, official announcements and job postings, the article explains why forward deployed engineers (FDEs) are proliferating. OpenAI launched a dedicated deployment company in May 2026, acquiring Tomoro with roughly 150 FDEs, while Anthropic partners with Accenture and DXC to train thousands of Claude-certified FDEs. The role originated at Palantir, whose latest 10-K still centers on working inside customer organizations. Generative AI's generality creates a paradox: the better models get, the more they must connect to enterprise data and systems, raising the value of on-site builders. Job postings have spread from the US to Tokyo, Asia and Europe. Japan's structure—73.6% of IT talent at vendors and AI-specific shortages—makes FDE-style support a structural fit.

Key points

  • OpenAI's May 2026 Deployment Company added ~150 FDEs via the Tomoro acquisition.
  • Anthropic is training FDEs at scale through Accenture and DXC partnerships.
  • Palantir created the role; its 2025 10-K still centers on embedded customer work.
  • Better models widen the scope of data and workflows needing on-site deployment.
  • Japan's vendor-heavy IT workforce and AI talent shortage favor FDE-style support.

Key Takeaways

  • The direct cause of the rise in FDEs is that model providers such as OpenAI and Anthropic acknowledged that simply handing over models does not change customers' businesses, and began investing in a business of placing engineers inside customers' organizations.
  • The FDE is a role created by Palantir's strategy of embedding itself inside customers to build out products, and the company's annual reports still place its policy of working as one with customers at the center of its business.
  • Generative AI requires connection to each company's business data and existing systems the more general-purpose it becomes, and a paradox whereby the better models perform, the more valuable the people who deploy them in the field becomes is driving hiring.
  • FDE job postings by overseas AI companies have spread from the US to Asia, including Tokyo, and Europe, so if Japanese companies seek the same profile, they will end up competing for the same talent pool.
  • In Japan, most IT talent belongs to vendors, and shortage estimates also concentrate on AI talent, so FDE-style support that works on-site, builds, and transfers knowledge is structurally a good fit.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role in which AI companies place engineers inside customer organizations and have them build solutions connected to business data and existing systems until results are achieved.

This term has started appearing in Japanese IT and business media, and OpenAI is also recruiting FDEs in Tokyo. No article that merely cites growth figures has answered "why do companies that build models all send people into the field?" I will explain the reasons for the increase using only annual reports, official announcements, and job postings written in each company's own words.

The figures in this article are limited to those verifiable in Palantir's annual report (Form 10-K), OpenAI and Anthropic's official announcements and recruiting pages, and public materials from Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry and IPA. These are figures published by each company, not third-party audited comparisons. Since job postings and recruiting pages change with hiring conditions, the dates checked are noted in the same paragraphs. Only OpenAI's early team size and Ramp's headcount are sourced from industry newsletter articles. Growth rates in job postings and hiring are not used because no sources tracing back to the underlying surveys were available.

The direct cause of the rise in FDEs is that companies selling models themselves admitted that "models alone do not produce results"

On May 11, 2026, OpenAI announced the establishment of a new company, "OpenAI Deployment Company," to send FDEs into enterprises. The announcement defines FDEs as "engineers specializing in the deployment of frontier AI" and explains their role as finding where AI is most effective together with executives, field operations staff, and front-line teams, and rebuilding business processes around those places. The reason the company gives is its recognition that as model performance improves, enterprises can apply AI to larger and more important business processes, and what is needed now is help reorganizing how organizations structure their important work around AI. The announcement also states that through the acquisition of applied AI consulting firm Tomoro, approximately 150 experienced FDEs and deployment specialists will join from day one, and that this is a partnership with 19 companies in total—investment firms, consulting firms, and SIers—led by TPG (OpenAI official announcement "OpenAI launches the OpenAI Deployment Company", May 11, 2026).

In the same announcement, OpenAI writes that it has held since its founding the belief that building powerful AI models is only part of the job, and that actual results come from helping people and organizations use them safely and effectively. The company's Chief Revenue Officer also states that now that AI can perform meaningful work within organizations, the challenge is helping embed those mechanisms into companies' infrastructure and business processes (OpenAI official announcement cited above, May 11, 2026). The fact that a company that builds models admitted in a public document that its own product alone is insufficient is the strongest basis for the increase in FDEs.

Anthropic is moving in the same direction. In its December 9, 2025 partnership announcement with Accenture, it states that approximately 30,000 Accenture employees will receive Claude training, including FDEs who embed Claude inside customer environments (Accenture's term is reinvention deployed engineer) (Anthropic official announcement, December 9, 2025). In its June 11, 2026 partnership announcement with DXC, it states that DXC will train tens of thousands of Claude-certified FDEs, describing FDEs as "engineers placed directly inside customer organizations" (Anthropic official announcement, June 11, 2026). From these two announcements, it can be read that model providers have entered a stage of training not only their own employees but also staff of major IT services companies as FDEs.

The FDE was born from Palantir's strategy of building out products inside customers, and that philosophy remains in its latest annual report

The company that created the FDE role is US-based Palantir. In a November 2020 blog post, the company defines the Forward Deployed Software Engineer (internally called Delta) as "a software engineer who embeds directly inside customers and configures Palantir's existing platform to fit the customer's hardest problems." The same post contrasts this with conventional software engineers, who build one feature usable by many customers, whereas FDSEs make many features work for a single customer (Palantir official blog "A Day in the Life of a Palantir Forward Deployed Software Engineer", November 3, 2020). Venture capital firm a16z wrote in a January 2026 article that Palantir gave solutions engineers and integration engineers the new title of forward-deployed engineer in 2011 (a16z "Forward-deployed Job Titles", January 27, 2026).

This strategy was not just an early-stage experiment; it is also described as central to the business in the annual report (Form 10-K) for the fiscal year ended December 2025, filed in February 2026. The report states that "to tackle the most complex and valuable problems customers face, we must experience and understand those problems firsthand. To that end, we work side by side with users," explaining that R&D opportunities are found by working alongside users. It also notes that software engineers assigned to existing customers are responsible for managing platform deployment and operations while discovering new use cases, and that Palantir runs AIP bootcamps—building actual business workflows on customers' real data within days—at its own expense in principle. According to the report, the company had 4,429 full-time employees as of December 31, 2025, and 2025 revenue grew 56% year over year (Palantir Form 10-K, fiscal year ended December 2025, filed February 17, 2026).

Current job postings use the same language. Palantir's job posting positions the role as "the role we pioneered," and includes directly engaging with customer stakeholders ranging from technical teams to executives (Palantir official job posting, Forward Deployed Software Engineer, New York-based position, accessed September 16, 2026).

The more general-purpose generative AI is, the more on-the-ground customization it requires—and the smarter models become, the more valuable the people who deploy them

The turning point that spread the model once seen as unique to Palantir was generative AI. The reason lies in generative AI being a general-purpose technology. Even if you are handed a model that can be used for anything, on-the-ground work will not change unless it is connected to your company's business data and existing systems. In a June 2025 article, a16z compared companies buying AI to grandparents with smartphones—"they want to use it, but want someone else to set it up"—and wrote that advanced agents require context gained from read/write access to internal systems and ongoing management (a16z, "Trading Margin for Moat", June 4, 2025).

The substance of this customization is shown concretely in each company's job postings. OpenAI's FDE job posting for its Tokyo office describes the scope of responsibility as spanning problem discovery, assessing technical requirements, system design, building, and production deployment, and defines success by three measures: adoption in production, measurement of business impact, and feedback into products and models based on evals. Required experience is five or more years of hands-on engineering or technical implementation work including customer-facing responsibilities, and fluency in both Japanese and English is mandatory (OpenAI official job posting, Forward Deployed Engineer - Tokyo, accessed September 16, 2026). Anthropic's job posting lists MCP servers, subagents, and agent skills as FDE deliverables—components used in customers' production operations—and describes the role as building production applications using Claude inside customers' systems. Required experience is four or more years in a technical role involving customer interaction, with travel of roughly 25% expected for on-site work at customer locations (Anthropic official job posting, "Forward Deployed Engineer," accessed September 16, 2026. Applications had closed as of October 6; current openings can be found on Anthropic's careers page).

Within OpenAI, the FDE role is distinct from traditional technical roles. According to an article in The Pragmatic Engineer, a newsletter for engineers, the company's Solutions Architects often build MVPs and PoCs using anonymized or offline-extracted data, whereas FDEs write code directly on the customer's infrastructure using the customer's own tools (The Pragmatic Engineer, "What are Forward Deployed Engineers, and why are they so in demand?", August 12, 2025). In other words, work built on validation data and work built in production environments have become separate roles.

The view that "as model performance improves, people will no longer be needed" runs opposite to OpenAI's own announcements. The better performance gets, the larger and more important the tasks it can be applied to, and the wider the range of data and workflows that need to be connected. That is why deployers are needed. It is a paradox: the smarter models become, the more valuable the people who deliver them to the field.

AI companies choose field deployment because controlling implementation is a competitive strategy for securing the entry point of data

Another reason is competitive strategy. The aforementioned a16z article analyzes that, as foundation models have become interchangeable with one another and the importance of owning the application layer has grown, even model providers like OpenAI and Anthropic are hiring FDEs and Solutions Engineers to win in the enterprise. The article describes the moat AI application companies should aim for as "controlling, within your own company, where data comes from and how it flows in," and counts that 22 of the 311 openings on OpenAI's careers page at the time of writing were this kind of role (aforementioned a16z, "Trading Margin for Moat," June 4, 2025).

The mechanism by which field deployment strengthens products is also described on Palantir's official blog. The article states that FDSEs are responsible for feeding technical insights gained in the field back to the business development and product development teams, and that some of the most valuable product additions originated in the field (aforementioned Palantir official blog, November 3, 2020). For software companies, field deployment is a choice that raises costs—but as long as it secures the entry point of customer data and surfaces product improvements from the field, that cost can be recouped as an investment in the moat. The conventional wisdom that "assigning engineers to each customer doesn't pay off" collapsed here.

Hiring has expanded from the US to Asia, including Tokyo, and Europe, and overseas AI companies are beginning to place FDEs directly in the field in Japan as well

You can verify the growth by looking at the job listings. Searching for "forward deployed" on OpenAI's careers page returned 25 results as of September 16, 2026. The locations span New York, San Francisco, and Seattle, plus London, Madrid, Seoul, Singapore, Sydney, and Tokyo, and there are also industry-specific FDE openings in areas like healthcare and legal. In Tokyo, in addition to FDEs themselves, there are openings for an FDE manager and a Technical Deployment Lead (search results on OpenAI's careers page, checked September 16, 2026). Anthropic's careers page had 7 openings titled Forward Deployed as of the same day, located in New York, San Francisco, and Seattle, as well as London, Paris, and Munich (Anthropic careers page, checked September 16, 2026).

Team sizes have also changed over a short period. According to The Pragmatic Engineer article, OpenAI's FDE team started with 2 people at the beginning of 2025 and had grown to more than 10 by the time of the August article. The new company announced in May 2026 was said to add roughly 150 people through acquisition; while a separate organization, this is an order of magnitude larger in just over a year. The trend is spreading beyond companies that build models: Ramp, a US provider of corporate cards and expense management software, launched its FDE function about 9 months before that article and was operating roughly 15 people organized into pods (team units) (The Pragmatic Engineer cited above, August 12, 2025).

Institutionalization as a job role is also progressing. In the summer of 2026, a16z launched an 8-week fellowship for FDE and applied AI leaders, targeting people who deploy frontier systems inside real companies (a16z Build "FDE Fellowship", checked September 16, 2026). What matters for Japanese companies is that if you recruit the same profile in Tokyo, you will be competing for the same talent pool as these companies.

The FDE model works in Japan because IT talent is concentrated on the vendor side, creating a structure where the shortage of AI talent keeps widening

The spillover into Japan is not a case of following foreign companies; Japan's own structure provides the receptacle. Based on the 2020 census, IPA's "DX White Paper 2023" shows that of Japan's 1,253,930 people working in information processing and communications, 73.6% belong to IT companies and 26.4% to non-IT companies. In the US, the 2021 occupational employment and wage statistics show 35.1% at IT companies and 64.9% elsewhere (IPA "DX White Paper 2023," Part 4, published March 16, 2023, page 192). In a country where more than 70% of the people who can build things are on the vendor side, user companies cannot build an in-house capability through hiring alone. A model of embedding on-site, building while transferring knowledge into the company, is the direct prescription for this imbalance. The order for developing in-house builders is covered in the in-house development roadmap.

The nature of the shortage is also characteristic of Japan. In the "Survey on IT Talent Supply and Demand" published by METI in April 2019, under a median scenario assuming IT demand growth of about 2.7% per year and labor productivity gains of 0.7% per year, the IT talent supply-demand gap in 2030 was estimated at 450,000 people. The same document divides the market into "traditional IT" (contract development and maintenance/operations) and "advanced IT" (leveraging IoT and AI), projecting a shortage of 270,000 to 550,000 advanced IT talent in 2030, while noting that traditional IT talent could see an oversupply of 100,000 depending on the pace of the transition (METI "Survey on IT Talent Supply and Demand (Summary)", April 2019, Tables 1 and 4). The figures are 2019 estimates and need to be discounted accordingly, but the projection that the shortage concentrates not in contract work or maintenance but in embedding AI into business operations overlaps with the substance of the work FDEs do.

On-the-ground sentiment points the same way. In IPA's "DX Trends 2025" survey of companies in Japan, the US, and Germany, more than 80% of Japanese companies said they lack talent to drive DX, while in the US and Germany the combined share of "no shortage or surplus" and "slight surplus" was about 70% and 50%, respectively. Fewer than 30% of Japanese companies have set metrics to measure DX outcomes, compared with more than 80% in the US and Germany (IPA "DX Trends 2025" press release, published June 26, 2025; survey conducted February 10 to March 28, 2025, 1,535 Japanese companies). If you have no builders and rely on external parties only for validation, PoCs that never go beyond the pilot stage pile up, and without metrics you cannot tell whether anything has taken root. A model that builds while transferring knowledge and installs metrics along the way is the prescription for this imbalance in staffing and the absence of metrics.

The FDE is an engineering role, and at the same time, for commissioning companies, it is an investment model for tackling challenges whose requirements are not yet fixed

Rereading the reasons above from the company's perspective, the FDE has two faces. For individual engineers, it is a career option whose value keeps rising as model performance improves. For commissioning companies, it is an investment model that produces answers on-site by building while learning, for challenges whose requirements cannot be fully pinned down; it is not suited to large-scale development with finalized specifications or challenges that off-the-shelf SaaS can handle. The sorting of fit is covered in where FDE support fits, and the differences in scope of responsibility versus SES and contract development are covered in how FDEs differ from traditional engineers.

Frequently asked questions about the growth of FDEs

Q. Does the claim that FDEs are growing apply beyond Palantir?

Yes, it does. OpenAI has established a new company to send FDEs into enterprises, and Anthropic has entered a phase of developing FDEs in partnership with a major IT services company. The details of each announcement are summarized in the first section, and the spread of job openings in the job listings section.

Q. If AI model performance improves, will FDEs become unnecessary?

The answer of this article is that performance improvement works to increase demand for FDEs. OpenAI itself stated in its announcement that as model performance improves, the business processes to which AI can be applied become larger and more important, and the wider the range of data and operations to be connected, the more people are needed to build on-site. The reasoning is written in the section on generative AI.

Q. Should Japanese companies hire FDEs in-house, or rely on external providers?

The criterion for the decision is whether full-time development demand continues without interruption, not a comparison of capability. The point that you would be competing for the same talent pool that overseas AI companies seek is covered in the job listings section, and the details of the comparison are written in should you hire FDEs or use external resources.

Q. Isn't the FDE boom a passing fad?

The grounds for not treating this as a passing trend are that companies selling models are investing in forward deployed engineering for their own revenue, and that Palantir continues to place its policy of working hand-in-hand with customers at the center of its business in its annual reports. The spread of the term as a buzzword and the reasons it persists as an investment need to be read separately. For details, see the section on competitive strategy.

SAI's perspective: The structure in which model providers invest in forward deployed engineering for their own revenue is the same reason mid-sized companies bring external FDEs on-site

The structure in which OpenAI and Anthropic invest in deployment talent to sell their own models applies directly, at a different scale, to Japanese mid-sized companies adopting AI into their operations. The reason is the same: simply contracting a model does not change operations, and people are needed on-site to connect business data and existing systems and build things out. Our company, SAI Inc., offers FDE-accompanied development as a monthly-fee external service, providing the kind of FDE that overseas AI companies maintain as an internal function. From identifying issues to minimal implementation, we work primarily through on-site visits, and once operations are established, we switch to a primarily remote approach. Those going on-site are engineers with 3-5 years of experience, following the process outlined in FDE job details and the 6 phases.
