Summary

This article compiles publicly available data (as of August 2026) on Forward Deployed Engineer pay in the US and Japan. In the US, levels.fyi lists Palantir FDE total compensation at $185k–$631k (median $278k), while OpenAI's San Francisco FDE posting shows a base salary of $162k–$280k plus equity. Japanese job postings from domestic companies range roughly from ¥6 million to over ¥20 million. The author attributes the US–Japan gap mainly to equity compensation, Japanese salary structures, and smaller funding bases, and identifies four factors driving individual pay differences: English ability, industry domain knowledge, implementation depth, and client scale. Readers are warned that posted range ceilings reflect top grades, not starting offers.

Key points

  • Palantir FDE median total compensation is $278k per levels.fyi.
  • OpenAI's SF FDE base salary is $162k–$280k plus undisclosed equity.
  • Japanese domestic FDE postings span roughly ¥6M to ¥20M+ annually.
  • The US–Japan gap stems mostly from equity, salary tables, and funding scale.
  • English, domain knowledge, implementation depth, and client scale drive pay.

FDE salaries are said to be "high," but just how high, and why, is hard to know without lining up actual job postings. In this article, I compare publicly available US compensation data as of the time of writing (August 2026) with the ranges listed in Japanese job postings, and break down where the Japan-US gap comes from and what creates differences even among FDEs. All figures are ones that could be verified from public sources; anything that could not be verified is omitted rather than guessed.

The Bottom Line

  • In the US, the total compensation for Palantir's Forward Deployed Engineer listed on levels.fyi is $185,000–$631,000 (median $278,000, as listed on 2026-08-31), and the base salary range for OpenAI's San Francisco FDE job posting is $162,000–$280,000 plus equity.
  • The ranges publicly disclosed in Japanese job postings are spread broadly across domestic companies, from roughly ¥6 million to the ¥20 million range in annual salary. Foreign companies' Japan offices generally do not disclose salaries, but given their requirements (Japanese-English bilingual, 5+ years of experience, etc.), it is natural to assume they are positioned above the top ranges of domestic companies.
  • Even among FDEs, differences arise from English ability, industry domain, depth of implementation skills, and the size of customers handled. Note that the upper end of a range is "the figure for the company's highest grade," not a number you can expect to reach when joining.

There are two caveats. First, levels.fyi figures are total compensation (base salary + equity + bonus), while company job postings often disclose only base salary. Ranges Listed in Japanese Job Postings

On the Japan side, the situation is that foreign companies' Japan offices generally do not disclose salaries, while domestic companies publish them on job boards. The ranges that domestic AI and SaaS companies publish for FDE or equivalent roles are spread quite widely, from around ¥6 million at the low end to the ¥20 million range at the high end. Looking at FDE features on job boards for engineers, multiple companies offer ranges of ¥10–20 million, and some set their upper limit at ¥25 million or more.

This wide spread exists because under the same job title of "FDE," the actual work varies in nature: some roles are close to supporting the rollout of the company's own product, some involve building from scratch for each customer, and some involve leading a team. Comparing based on job posting ranges alone leads to misjudgment, so you need to read the job description and required qualifications together.

FDE board allows you to search and sort the salary ranges of listed jobs. The number of listings is on the order of a dozen to several dozen as of the time of writing; you can check which companies are hiring FDEs in Japan and the distribution of salaries on the FDE board job listings and salary pages. Since individual posting ranges change daily, this article does not map company names to specific figures.

Why the Gap Between Japan and the US Is So Large

Looking at US FDE figures, it is natural to feel that "Japan pays less," but when you break down the gap, it turns out not to be simply a difference in how talent is valued.

The biggest factor is equity compensation. At US tech companies, especially highly valued private AI companies, equity makes up a large share of total compensation, and the gap narrows if you compare base salary alone. Even in the levels.fyi data for Palantir, the breakdown shows a base salary of $164,000 against equity worth about $57,300 per year, and the total compensation figure includes equity. Since the annual salary figures in Japanese job postings are almost always cash compensation, the comparison targets are fundamentally different.

Next, there are the constraints of Japanese salary tables. Even at foreign companies' Japan offices, salaries rarely match the home country's as long as they must fit the Japanese entity's grading system. Furthermore, Japanese AI startups raise far less capital than their US counterparts, so even for the same role, the funding base differs. This is not something individual effort can change, so if you want to raise your salary in Japan, it is more realistic to aim for the factors discussed in the next section.

Four Factors That Create Differences Among FDEs

Reading across Japanese job postings, there are common differences between high-range and low-range postings. This serves as a guide for deciding what to work on if you want to increase your salary.

Of these four, English is the one that pays off in the short term. As far as I could confirm as of the time of writing (August 2026), both OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting and Palantir's Tokyo posting list both Japanese and English as required qualifications. In other words, without English, you cannot even apply for the highest-paying tier of jobs in Japan. Conversely, industry domain knowledge takes time to accumulate, but experience in regulated industries such as finance, manufacturing, and healthcare directly becomes a reason to be sought after.

  • English — Foreign companies' Japan offices almost always require Japanese-English bilingual ability. This is the entry condition for the highest-paying tier
  • Industry domain — Whether you have prior knowledge of the customer's business, such as finance, manufacturing, healthcare, or the public sector. Industry names appear in the preferred qualifications of job postings
  • Depth of implementation — Whether the work ends with configuration and integration, or whether you can write production code and make architectural decisions. Postings for full-stack engineers have higher ranges
  • Size and difficulty of customers handled — Whether you can be entrusted with enterprise and government projects. Even within the same company, some firms vary compensation by the grade of customers assigned

Caveats When Reading Job Posting Ranges

If you take salary ranges at face value, your expectations will be off. In practice, please check the following points.

The last two points are especially easy to overlook. FDE is a role that assumes business travel and on-site work at customer locations; Palantir's Tokyo job posting states 25-75% travel, and OpenAI's FDE posting states up to 50% travel (Tokyo postings are mostly domestic travel). If you don't factor this burden into the salary, you may regret joining after looking only at the headline figure. Compensation rates for contract work and freelancing are covered in a separate article.

  • The upper limit is the figure for the highest grade of that role, not the offer at hiring. Postings with figures closer to the median better reflect the actual market rate
  • Check whether the figure is total compensation or base salary. Figures from US companies often include equity, while salary figures in Japanese job postings are often cash compensation only
  • Whether the salary includes deemed overtime pay (fixed overtime allowance). If included, your effective hourly rate is lower than it appears
  • Travel ratio and frequency of on-site work at customer locations. FDE is a travel-heavy role, and this directly affects your cost of living and time commitments
  • Terms for equity and stock options. For unlisted companies, the paper valuation and the amount you actually receive are different things

Realistic ways to increase your salary

First, for people currently working as FDEs at domestic companies, the next target should be Japanese offices of foreign companies. The gap in compensation ranges is large, while the gap in requirements is concentrated in English and depth of implementation, so what you need to fill is clear. English at the level of "being able to handle technical discussions and meetings with customers" is required, so building experience using it in actual work is more effective than certification exam scores.

Next, how you present your track record. In FDE selection processes, what gets evaluated is not "what you built" but "what kind of ambiguous state you started from, how you defined it, and how it is used in production." The number of customers you deployed for, the time to go live, and usage after adoption. If you have one project where you can state these three things with numbers, the persuasiveness of your resume changes completely.

Finally, monitor market rates at regular intervals. FDE is a role where the number of openings is still growing, and market rates move. FDE board, operated by Fdestria Inc., aggregates FDE job postings in Japan and allows sorting by salary range, and its salary page summarizes the distribution of published ranges. If you would like to be contacted when a posting matching your criteria appears, please use candidate registration on FDE board.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What is the market salary range for FDEs in Japan?
  • As of writing (2026-08), the ranges published in Japanese job postings span roughly from the 6 million yen level to the 20 million yen level at domestic companies, so no single market rate can be stated. While multiple companies offer ranges of 10-20 million yen, some set their upper limit at 25 million yen or higher. For the actual distribution, please check the published ranges of current postings on the FDE board salary page.
  • How large is the gap between foreign companies and domestic companies?
  • Japanese offices of foreign companies often do not publish compensation in their job postings, so a comparison by amount is not possible as of writing (2026-08). However, given that the same role at US headquarters pays total compensation in the hundreds of thousands of dollars (median of $278,000 for Palantir on levels.fyi), and that Japanese postings impose high requirements such as Japanese-English bilingualism and 5+ years of experience, it is natural to assume they are positioned above the top ranges of domestic companies.
  • If I switch to an FDE role without prior experience, will my salary go down?
  • It depends on your previous role. Moving from an SIer or consulting firm often means an increase, while moving from pre-sales at a foreign company or a high-salary software engineering role may mean staying flat or a temporary decrease. Since market rates for FDE as a role are still forming, even people with the same years of experience see large differences in offers between companies. Compare multiple companies side by side.
  • Why are FDE salaries so high?
  • Because the same person must have both the ability to write production code and the ability to talk with customer executives to define problems. People with this combination are scarce in the market, and enterprise LLM adoption is currently bottlenecked on this role. In addition, there is the burden of travel and on-site work at customer locations, and compensation reflects that as well.
