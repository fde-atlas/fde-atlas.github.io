What Is a GTM Engineer? | Job Description and How It Differs from an FDE

The job title "GTM Engineer" has started appearing in overseas job postings and on Japanese job-change services. From the name alone, it is hard to tell whether it is a sales job or an engineering job.

In this article, I will organize where the GTM Engineer role came from, what the work involves, and what skills it requires. At the end, I will compare it with the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), which likewise sits between technology and business. A detailed definition of the FDE role is summarized in What is an FDE? The meaning and work of a Forward Deployed Engineer.

What is a GTM Engineer?

A GTM Engineer is an engineer who builds sales, marketing, and customer-facing systems using AI, data, and business process automation. GTM stands for Go-To-Market, referring to the activities involved in getting a product to market and turning it into revenue.

The role was named by Clay, a company that builds tools for collecting and enriching sales data and drafting messages with AI. Clay writes on its blog that it named this role in 2023. The same blog defines GTM Engineering as "building self-running revenue systems using AI, data enrichment, and workflow automation."

The name has also arrived in Japan. overflow, which operates the job-change service Offers for engineers, announced on March 10, 2026 that it had added "GTM Engineer" to its list of job categories. In the announcement, it described the GTM Engineer as "an engineer who integrates CRM, MA, and AI tools to design and build 'reproducible growth systems' in order to maximize the results of marketing, sales, and customer success."

There is also disagreement about whether it is a new role. In an article dated August 18, 2025, diginomica argued that the GTM Engineer is a rethinking of marketing operations and sales operations work. The view is that it is not merely a renaming, but not an entirely new job either. The article also touches on the earlier titles of "growth hacker" and "growth engineer."

In my view, the substance of the work existed before. What changed is that, thanks to AI and no-code tools, people close to the sales front line can now build systems themselves. I believe the name GTM Engineer is a label attached to that change.

What GTM Engineers do

Clay's blog divides the work of a GTM Engineer into three areas: RevOps (operations of revenue-related processes), growth (acquiring prospects), and customer success (retaining and expanding existing customers). It explains that the day-to-day work is to find bottlenecks in revenue, define the scope of a fix, build a prototype, and scale up what works.

The specific duties are easier to understand by looking at Japanese job postings. The GTM Engineer posting from SalesNow lists duties such as the following.

  • Designing and improving the sales flow from lead acquisition to booked meetings, using AI and data
  • Building systems that use company data to tag prospects with firmographic attributes and signals such as hiring or fundraising
  • Using Claude Code and MCP servers to build systems such as meeting-preparation reports and prospect notifications
  • Maintaining CRMs such as Salesforce and building dashboards to track meeting conversion rates and win rates
  • Being accountable for metrics such as the number of meetings, meeting conversion rate, and win rate

I think the fifth item captures the character of this role well. You are evaluated not on how well the systems you built were made, but on revenue metrics.

Skills required of GTM Engineers

Clay's blog divides the requirements into two categories: technical literacy and business sense. On the technical side, it says familiarity with Clay, n8n, Zapier, TypeScript, Python, SQL, and the like is desirable, while the biggest indicator is a willingness to learn by hands-on experimentation. Business sense means being able to ask whether the system actually moves deals forward.

The same two categories appear in the required qualifications of the SalesNow posting: three or more years of practical experience in B2B sales or sales operations/RevOps, experience automating workflows with Python, SQL, or no-code tools, and experience designing and operating a CRM.

GTM Engineer vs. FDE

Both GTM Engineers and FDEs sit between technology and business and are accountable for results using AI. The difference lies in whose business processes they build for.

The FDE originated at Palantir in the United States. Palantir's official blog explains its engineering roles by dividing them into "Dev," which delivers one feature to many customers, and "Delta," which delivers many features to a single customer. This Delta is the prototype of the FDE.

Point of comparisonGTM EngineerFDE
Who they serveYour own sales, marketing, and CS teamsCustomer companies' front lines
What they buildSystems for your own company's prospecting and deal flowAI implementations tailored to the customer's business
How results are measuredRevenue metrics such as your own meeting count and win rateWhether the customer's business KPIs moved
Main toolsCRM, data enrichment tools, workflow automationCustomer systems and data, LLMs, evaluation frameworks
Related rolesRevOps, sales operationsAI engineer, solution architect

In my view, the difference between the two roles is whether the same technology of rebuilding business processes with AI is directed at internal revenue or at the customer's business. Experience as a GTM Engineer, building systems on the sales front line and being evaluated on metrics, transfers directly to the work of an FDE embedded in a customer's business.
