Global FDE in Practice

Original title: 全球FDE实战

pafa.github.io · 2026-11-04 · 4 min read

Summary

This is a Chinese-language open-source book (November 2026 revision candidate) on Forward Deployed Engineers, spanning 54 chapters plus a preface, conclusion, and reading index. It traces FDE's pre-history and Palantir's practices, covers organizational economics, the full delivery process from discovery to handover, industry and country-specific differences, career growth, and comparisons of implementation firms. It emphasizes turning one-off deployments into lasting customer capability, includes teaching materials and synthetic data, and is licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0 with community-driven revisions.

Key points

  • Distinguishes FDE from sales engineering, consulting, and implementation roles.
  • Covers delivery decisions from first week to production handover.
  • Eight country chapters including US, China, Japan, and India.
  • Compares Palantir, Accenture, BCG, and cloud platforms' delivery models.
  • Includes exercises, teaching diagrams, and synthetic datasets per chapter.

From the earliest records of the concept to industry fieldwork around the world. Bring the technology in, leave the capability behind.

Start reading: Prologue · Online reading home · Browse by topic · Raise questions and suggestions

Once a demo runs through, an enterprise still has many questions to answer: Can the ingested data be trusted? Who has the authority to change the process? Who handles things when they go wrong? And after the engineer leaves, can the customer keep using it?

The FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) steps into exactly the place where technical capability meets these real-world conditions. Understanding this role requires seeing at once how engineers work, how customers make decisions, and why a company is willing to invest people in this delivery model.

*Global FDE in Practice* is a Chinese-language book covering both business and technology. It starts from customer engineering before the name existed, traces the early records of FDE and Palantir's practice, and then moves on to projects, industries, and regions in the era of models and agents. The whole book revolves around one question: How do you turn a single field delivery into a capability that customers can actually use, teams can hand over, and products can accumulate?

What you will find in this book

  • A well-grounded historical thread. Distinguishing the prehistory of the working style, the emergence of the name, and the evolution of organizational models, and understanding how FDE relates to and differs from sales engineering, consulting, and implementation.
  • Judgments about organization and business. Which projects merit sending people in, how teams divide the work, how costs and revenue are calculated, and how customer needs flow back into the product.
  • A delivery process from the first week to departure. Requirements discovery, data interpretation, production operation, evaluation and acceptance, adoption and handover—each with specific decisions to make.
  • Real differences brought by industries and countries. From the public sector, healthcare, and finance to manufacturing, energy, logistics, legal, retail, agriculture, and education and research, observing how procurement, accountability, business processes, and local capabilities change delivery.
  • A continuous growth path across seven chapters. From capability diagnosis, business and technical judgment, and field collaboration to portfolio work, role choices, and long-term growth, paired with existing teaching diagrams, exercise guides, and synthetic data to form verifiable outcomes.
  • Practice windows into eight key countries. The United States, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, China, Japan, India, and Singapore each get a chapter discussing companies, institutions, and concrete work—without writing a few cases up as a nation's uniform character.
  • Concrete practices of implementing companies. Taking Palantir's evolution from field engineering to AI FDE as the first key company case, then comparing Accenture, BCG, McKinsey, Capgemini, Deloitte, Infosys, TCS, Fujitsu, and cloud platforms on delivery division of labor, representative cases, and AI automation, examining what customers actually get and what they still have to bear.

Who this is for and how to start reading

The book starts from questions and cases, then explains the necessary technical concepts. Non-technical readers can start with the business and organizational judgments; hands-on readers can continue into in-chapter exercises and appendices.

About the sources and cases

This is the November 2026 revision candidate, with sources current as of 2026-11-04, containing the Prologue, 54 topical chapters, an epilogue, and a reading index. Each chapter keeps source links and key dates, distinguishing contemporaneous records, participants' recollections, company statements, research judgments, and teaching examples; for costs, effects, and details not publicly disclosed in cases, the book keeps to what can be known.

The book separately identifies practices that explicitly call themselves FDE, demonstrably similar delivery approaches, and related AI applications, and discusses announcements, pilots, launches, and business results separately. Contributions of new material, corrections of errors, and alternative interpretations are all welcome, so this book can keep being revised.

Table of contents

Chapters are numbered consecutively from Chapter 1 to Chapter 54 in reading order. The seven parts answer in turn: what FDE is, whether to build a team, how to deliver, how industries change the work, what conditions different countries present, how to become an excellent FDE, and how to choose an implementing company and use AI in delivery.
