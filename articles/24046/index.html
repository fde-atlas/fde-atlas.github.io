Is FDE Freelancing Possible? A Guide to Finding Projects, Balancing with Your Main Job, and Key Considerations

Original title: FDE副業は可能？案件の探し方と本業との両立・注意点を解説

FDE総研 · 2026-10-20 · 11 min read

Summary

This article from FDE総研 argues that Forward Deployed Engineers can do side work in Japan, but not by replicating the Palantir-style full-time on-site model. Instead, they should scope limited engagements such as technical advisory, PoC development, business improvement support, or training. Since few listings use the FDE title, the author recommends searching by task keywords (e.g., generative AI adoption, data infrastructure) across matching platforms, freelance agents, and personal referrals. It covers required skills—especially documenting agreements asynchronously—pricing by contract type (quasi-mandate, fixed-scope, advisory), and legal checks including employment rules, NDAs, tax filing, the invoice system, and Japan's freelance law. The suggested path: start with advisory or short PoCs to build trust before taking larger engagements.

Key points

  • FDE side work is feasible only as scoped, limited engagements.
  • Search by task keywords since few jobs are titled FDE.
  • Asynchronous documentation skills matter more than in full-time FDE roles.
  • Price by contract type and scope of responsibility, not hours alone.
  • Verify employment rules, NDAs, taxes, and the invoice system first.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role pioneered by Palantir, in which engineers are sent to the customer's site and handle everything from problem definition to implementation and adoption. In recent years, alongside support for introducing generative AI, this job title has become increasingly common in Japan as well. At the same time, we often hear questions like "Can I leverage my FDE experience in a side job?" or "Where can I find such projects?" This article walks through, in order, how engineers interested in FDE work can structure a side job, how to find projects, how to think about skills and rates, and what to check before getting started.

Is FDE Side Work Possible? How Can You Balance It with Your Main Job?

FDE side work is possible. However, it is difficult to do as-is in an embedded format; the realistic approach is to break it out into limited-hours, hands-on support.

The Palantir-style FDE works by embedding deeply at the customer's site, interacting with business stakeholders daily while building systems. You cannot replicate this style of engagement as-is in a side job. That is why deciding upfront "which part of the FDE role to carve out" is what determines whether you can balance it with your main job.

Why Does FDE Side Work Tend to Work Well?

The FDE's ability to "solve on-the-ground problems with technology" is a skill that readily creates value even in side work.

Among companies working on generative AI adoption and data utilization, quite a few want to start small with external talent before hiring full-time FDE professionals. We also often hear that even when they have in-house engineers, no one can play the role of bridging business departments and technology. Demand for this kind of "bridge" role can sometimes be met without full-time commitment.

What Engagement Styles Are Easy to Balance with Your Main Job?

The following four formats are easy to balance because working hours and scope of responsibility are easy to define upfront.

Engagement styleMain tasksEase of adjusting hoursBest suited for
Technical advisor / advisoryAdvice at regular meetings, review of technology choicesHighPeople with extensive experience in design and decision-making
PoC / prototype developmentImplementation for validating generative AI and data utilizationModeratePeople who can build working things in a short time
Hands-on business improvement supportBusiness interviews, automation, dashboard buildingModerate to lowPeople who are good at talking with business departments
Training / workshop instructorIn-house AI training, hands-on sessionsHighPeople who are good at explaining and teaching

From what we see in the field, people who start with a fixed-scope format like "technical advisor" or "PoC development" tend to clash less with their main job. Hands-on business improvement support is the most distinctly FDE-like format, but client expectations tend to balloon and working hours often exceed assumptions.

Which Aspects of FDE Work Are Hard to Replicate in a Side Job?

Being embedded on-site to observe daily operations, and having frequent contact with decision-makers, are aspects that are hard to replicate in a side job.

One of the FDE's strengths is seeing the business frontline with your own eyes and picking up on problems that are never articulated. In a side job, contact is limited to a few hours a week, so you need to deliberately design your interviews and clarify role division with the client's point of contact. Deciding before signing the contract "who will gather frontline information and pass it to you" helps prevent misaligned expectations.

Where Can You Find FDE Side Jobs?

Since side-job listings titled "FDE" are still rare, the shortcut is to search by job content and combine multiple channels.

FDE is still a new job title in Japan, so job postings and project listings almost never use the word "FDE." Many projects are posted under different job titles even though the substance is exactly FDE work.

What Keywords Should You Search With?

Searching with keywords based on the tasks FDEs actually perform in the field, rather than the job title, makes it easier to find relevant projects.

A practical way to identify FDE-style projects is to search with combinations of multiple keywords and check whether the project description includes both "requirements alignment with the client" and "implementation."

How Should Practitioners Land Their "First Project"?

My impression is that the first deal often comes not from public listings, but through referrals from acquaintances or your own outreach.

FDE-type work presupposes trust—"this person can safely be let into the field"—in addition to technical skill. For that reason, without a track record, being chosen by a completely unknown company is not easy. If you publish past business improvement work or PoC experiences as articles or presentation materials, within the bounds of confidentiality, it adds persuasiveness when you receive referrals.

What skills are required for FDE side work, and how should rates be considered?

What's required is not just technical skill but the ability to handle everything alone from problem definition through implementation and adoption, and rates are set according to that scope.

What skills are required for a side-gig FDE?

The deciding factor is whether you can demonstrate three things—technical skill, business understanding, and communication—within limited working hours.

Skill areaExamplesImportance for side work
Problem definitionBusiness interviews, organizing key issues, prioritizationVery high
Data and AI implementationPython, SQL, LLM APIs, workflow automationHigh
Asynchronous communicationThe ability to record agreed content in meeting notes and design docsVery high
Business domain knowledgeUnderstanding of specific industries such as manufacturing, retail, or local governmentHigh depending on the project
Adoption supportCreating operation manuals, handover to staff in chargeMedium to high

In side work, meeting time is limited. That's why I feel that "the ability to accurately record agreed content asynchronously" becomes even more important than in a full-time FDE role. Even without experience with large-scale platforms like Palantir, if you have experience transforming business operations by combining spreadsheets with generative AI, that's plenty of material to showcase.

What should rates be based on?

The basic principle is not to set rates by working hours alone, but to consider the scope of responsibility you take on together with the contract type.

Contract typeHow compensation is determinedSuitable projectsPoints to watch
Quasi-mandate (hourly billing)Working hours × hourly rateOngoing support, requirements definitionSet an upper limit on hours
Contract for work (deliverable-based)Fixed amount per deliverablePoCs, tool developmentClearly document how specification changes are handled
Advisory contract (fixed monthly)Fixed monthly compensationTechnical advisory, reviewsDefine the scope of support and response deadlines

Market rates vary greatly depending on the project's domain and timing. Before applying, it's reassuring to compare the conditions of similar projects published across multiple matching services and agents. FDE-type projects derive value from being able to do both requirements definition and implementation, so be mindful not to set your rate by the same standard as projects where you only do the work.

What materials should you present in rate negotiations?

If you can explain past results in terms of "how the business changed," you convey FDE-style value and negotiations go more smoothly.

  • Business problems you solved (e.g., automated manual aggregation work)
  • The scope you covered (how far you went among interviewing, design, implementation, and adoption)
  • The technologies you used and why you chose them
  • How the client has operated things after your handover

For results you can show numerically, obtain the client's consent and stay within confidentiality obligations.

What should you check before starting FDE side work?

The top priority is to avoid conflicts of interest with your main job by checking four things in advance: internal work rules, non-compete and confidentiality obligations, taxes, and transaction rules.

How do you check work rules and national guidelines?

First, check your main job's work rules to see whether side work is permission-based or notification-based. It's also reassuring to read the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's guidelines alongside that.

In January 2018, the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare established the "Guidelines on Promoting Side Jobs and Moonlighting," and the July 2022 revision encourages companies to publish how they handle side jobs and moonlighting. However, whether side work is permitted is determined by each company's work rules. Be sure to confirm internal procedures in advance. When taking side work as a contracted service (business commission), the aggregation of working hours under the Labor Standards Act is in principle not applicable. Even so, you need to watch out for health effects from long working hours yourself.

How about non-compete, confidentiality, and intellectual property handling?

Since FDEs often handle client business data, it's essential to first establish a system that keeps main-job and side-job information separate.

  • Check whether the project comes from a company that is a client or competitor of your main job
  • Don't use your main job's PC, accounts, or code and prompts created internally for side work
  • In the contract with the side-work client, clearly state who owns the copyright and intellectual property of deliverables
  • Agree in advance whether you may input side-work client data into generative AI services

What to keep in mind regarding taxes, invoicing, and the Freelance Act?

If income other than salary exceeds 200,000 yen per year, a final tax return is required. Also check compliance with the invoice system and the Freelance Act.

What's the pre-start checklist?

If you confirm all five of the following items before signing, you can greatly reduce trouble after starting side work.

  • Completed side-work application or notification in line with your main job's work rules
  • Confirmed that the side-work client is not a competitor or client of your main job
  • Agreed in writing on the contract type (quasi-mandate, contract for work, or advisory) and the upper limit on hours
  • Clearly stated confidentiality, intellectual property ownership, and data handling in the contract
  • Decided how to handle your final tax return and invoice registration

As the FDE Soken editorial team, rather than taking on large ongoing support from the start, we recommend building a track record and trust through technical advisory or short-term PoCs and gradually expanding your scope of involvement. Since the value of an FDE lies in "producing results in the field," reliably accumulating small successes in side work too will lead to the next project.
