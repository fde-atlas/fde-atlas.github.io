Lenovo reveals it has established an FDE organization to drive a transformation in its AI delivery model

Original title: 联想透露已成立FDE组织 推动AI交付模式变革

Full text

By reporter Jia Li — FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) has become the "new darling" of the AI world. On this new track, some companies have already achieved real-world results ahead of the pack.

Lenovo Group Limited ("Lenovo") recently published a case in which, through the FDE model, it built a complete AI-native application for a large domestic home appliance manufacturer, delivering two AI agents in three months and cutting finished-goods inventory by 70%. At the same time, Lenovo has established a dedicated FDE organization whose core purpose is to engage early in customers' business planning, driving a shift from "responding to customer needs" to "defining customer value."

FDE is being pushed comprehensively to the industry front line as the "critical link" in enterprise AI delivery.

FDE fills the gap in AI delivery

Three mainstream models emerge

Industry insiders generally agree that FDE is the key role for bringing AI to every industry. China Galaxy Securities' research report puts the FDE role more concretely — its core is solving the "last mile" of AI application deployment, essentially a system for deep delivery of AI applications and AI agents.

Within this system, FDEs must embed themselves in the customer's business site, untangle complex approval workflows, clean up dirty data scattered everywhere, and tune AI agents in real business systems. What they deliver is no longer a server with software installed, but a "digital employee" that can actually run in the customer's business and do work on people's behalf.

A large number of companies are accelerating their shift toward this model. Gartner analyst Alex Coqueiro predicts that by the end of 2026, more than 85% of technology vendors will adopt the FDE model as their core method for delivering AI.

Currently, the global FDE track is roughly divided into three approaches. Model vendors take the "heavy customization" route: OpenAI, ByteDance and others have chosen to get directly involved, deploying engineers in teams to customer sites, trading high investment for high customer lock-in. For customers, the cost of this model is that the deeper the system is embedded, the higher the switching cost, which may ultimately evolve into long-term dependence on a single vendor.

Consulting firms choose the "replicable universal framework" route: consulting and IT service providers such as Accenture, Deloitte and IBM incorporate FDE into their existing consulting and implementation business frameworks, attempting to standardize and productize it. But universal frameworks struggle to fit customers' highly non-standard business foundations; once beyond standard scenarios, FDE easily degenerates into traditional per-person-day billing consulting.

Cloud vendors choose the "ecosystem certification" route: rather than maintaining massive FDE teams themselves, they publish capability certification systems and recruit third-party service providers. For example, Alibaba Cloud, at the Yunqi Conference, jointly launched a public cloud ecosystem partner FDE system with Qianwen Office, and Tencent Cloud launched an FDE engineer certification.

FDE success is not "project delivery"

but "whether assets are accumulated for the customer"

"If FDE success is defined merely as 'project delivery,' then what the customer buys is just 'professional services.'" So judged Gartner analyst Mukul Saha on the current state of the industry.

In Lenovo's published case of a large domestic home appliance manufacturer, the entire delivery process was divided into three steps: the FDE team embedded at the customer site worked with the business side to identify two high-value scenarios — product lifecycle and inventory challenges, and inaccurate sales forecasting and demand planning; leveraging the AI agent workshop, POC validation was completed within 3 weeks, locking in viable AI agent directions; within 3 months, two AI agents for inventory optimization and planning were rapidly delivered.

The business logic validated through this model was consolidated into the customer's own "AI agent workshop," giving the customer the ability to iterate independently going forward. Insights from on-site scenario surveys become templates for AI agent orchestration; outcomes of process restructuring become rules for skill governance. Through continuous operation, in addition to reducing finished-goods inventory by 70%, the company also achieved a 45% reduction in raw material inventory, a 38% reduction in order lead times, and a 50% reduction in stagnant product inventory.

This case confirms the value of Lenovo's dual-engine "擎天AI + FDE" approach. It is understood that Lenovo is further strengthening front-to-back coordination of FDE. Its newly established FDE organization, building on the integration of solutions expert teams, has also set up a resource pool and established a committee on the R&D side, focused on interpreting customers' intelligent transformation strategies, defining value scenarios, designing solutions, and accumulating AI agent assets, driving the transformation from customer needs to business outcomes.

Industry insiders say Lenovo's move is, in essence, exploring a transformation of the delivery model in the AI era — using organizational mechanisms to convert non-standard experience gained through hard-won front-line work into reusable, standardized assets on the back end, thereby pushing the FDE model deeper into industry territory.
