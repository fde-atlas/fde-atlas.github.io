This appendix lists the people, teams, and sources of knowledge that are essential to understanding the FDE model. For the complete list of sources consulted in writing this book, see the research notes released alongside the manuscript.

I. Key Figures ​

Shyam Sankar — President and CTO of Palantir, early employee. Widely regarded as the inventor of the FDE strategy: the person who redefined "on-site customization" from a cost into "product discovery." His public statements are first-hand material for understanding Palantir's philosophy.

Bob McGrew — Early PayPal engineer, early Palantir executive, former Chief Research Officer of OpenAI (led the development of ChatGPT, GPT-4, and o1). The best expositor of the FDE model. "Gravel roads versus highways" and "doing the unscalable at scale" both come from him. Must-read: the YC Lightcone podcast "The FDE Playbook for AI Startups" (September 2025).

Stephen Cohen — Palantir co-founder. Inventor of the demo loop: the protagonist of "This thing sucks" / "Then how do you want it to be different?"

Alex Karp — CEO of Palantir. Originator of the "French waiter" behavioral model: opposed to a subservient, order-taking engineering culture.

Barry — Former Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer, author of the article "Understanding Forward Deployed Engineering." The most important "insider's warning" about the FDE model: a truthful ledger of costs, chaos, and burnout.

Ted Mabrey — Longtime Palantir employee and writer, eyewitness to the "Sankar bomb" email (Sankar's all-hands email announcing the strategic pivot). He breaks "everything is judged by results" into two questions: "Does it actually work? Does it actually matter?"

Nabeel Qureshi — Spent nearly eight years as a Forward Deployed Engineer at Palantir, author of "Reflections on Palantir" (2024). Spent a year embedded on-site at Airbus in Toulouse; "screw generalizability," recruiting's "bat signal" reverse filtering, "ninety-five percent of data problems are ingestion, cleaning, and joining," and his three-way moral taxonomy of "staying in the room" when facing gray-area projects all come from his public writing.

Vinoo Ganesh — Designer of Palantir's rotation development program (Project Frontline), author of "The Definitive Guide to FDE" (2026). "You can't hire your way to a real FDE organization—you have to grow it"—over 250 engineers entered real deployments through rotations, with alumni scattered across OpenAI, xAI, and Anduril. The six-month self-development checklist in Chapter 7 and the postmortem of the "2.3 million data entries blowing up memory" incident in Chapter 4 both come from his first-hand writing.

Colin Jarvis — Head of OpenAI's forward deployed engineering team, the person who built OpenAI's FDE organization from scratch.

Brad Lightcap — COO of OpenAI, the driving force behind the "deployment company."

Natalie Meurer — Head of agent engineering at Sierra (previously five years at Palantir). The person who coined "agent engineer." Must-read: the Latent Space podcast interview (July 2026).

Bret Taylor / Clay Bavor — Sierra co-founders (the former was previously co-CEO of Salesforce, the latter a former Google VP). The people who turned the FDE model into a business model in itself (paying for outcomes).

Jesse Zhang / Ashwin Sreenivas — Decagon co-founders (the latter came from Palantir). Designers of agent operating procedures, the exemplar of "productizing delivery experience."

David Wakeling — Former head of market innovation at Linklaters, the internal champion of Harvey's first lighthouse deployment. The best sample of the client-side champion's perspective.

II. Landmark Teams ​

The Palantir FDE organization — The inventor and finished form of the model. The "Echo-Delta-Platform" triangle, the bootcamp machine.

OpenAI's forward deployed engineering team — Formed in 2024, globally distributed, the operators behind John Deere and Banco Santander; upgraded to a "deployment company" in 2026.

Anthropic's applied AI team — An FDE organization formed under the banner of "Applied AI Engineer," co-builder of the FIS financial crime agent; in 2026 formed a joint venture for enterprise AI services with Blackstone and others.

Sierra's agent engineering team — The extreme form of pay-for-outcomes plus managed delivery (founded 2023, valued at $4.5 billion in October 2024, reportedly climbing since).

Harvey's deployment team — The textbook case of vertical-industry (legal) FDE (founded 2022, valued at $11 billion in March 2026, with 100,000+ lawyer users).

Cognition's forward deployed team — The exemplar of "embedding inside a partner's body" ecosystem bundling: starting in 2026, an embedded team of Forward Deployed Engineers is stationed at the global IT services giant Cognizant, responsible for project screening, engineer mentoring, and outcome measurement, leveraging decades of the partner's customer relationships to push the product into large healthcare, finance, and insurance enterprises.

Scale AI / Databricks / Salesforce / Google Cloud / Mistral / Cohere — Each has FDE or isomorphic roles under different names (Solutions Architect, Customer Engineer, etc.), but the same skeleton.

Volcano Engine Doubao LLM FDE team — The clearest FDE organization among Chinese internet giants (established in 2026, covering automotive, healthcare, education, finance, and semiconductors; building a thousand-person team jointly with EY).

Chinese domestic FDE pioneers: Qimeng Technology, Qiantuo Technology — Local service providers with real estate facility management and finance as their strongholds, who have publicly produced defining thinking on "FDE is not on-site outsourcing."

III. Sources of Knowledge (Ranked by Importance) ​

  • YC Lightcone podcast: "The FDE Playbook for AI Startups — A Conversation with Bob McGrew" (September 2025) — a 51-minute retrospective from the model's first narrator.
  • a16z: "Trading Margin for Moat" — the benchmark for business model analysis.
  • Barry: "Understanding Forward Deployed Engineering" — an insider's sober warning.
  • MIT NANDA Lab: "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" — the source of the "95% failure rate," the data foundation for why FDEs exist.
  • The New Stack: "Why OpenAI and Anthropic Are Both Racing to Build FDE Teams" (May 2026).
  • CIO.com: "Anthropic's Finance AI Agent Exposes the Forward Deployed Engineer as the New Bottleneck" (May 2026) — the buyer's perspective and the Gartner warning.
  • Latent Space Podcast: "Forward Deployed Engineers and the Future of Software Engineering" (July 2026) — Sierra's perspective.
  • Palantir Official Blog: "A Day in the Life of a Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer" (2020) — an official account, with a Chinese translation circulating.
  • OpenFDE Community (open-fde.com) — an open-source community of practitioners, with a map of roles and companies.
  • 36Kr's "China's To-B Software: Escaping the Trap of Loss-Making Growth" and the FDE series on Chen Guo George's public account — essential reading for the Chinese context.

IV. Compensation and Job-Search References ​

  • GetPerspective: "2026 Forward Deployed Engineer Compensation Report" (1,200 samples) — median total annual compensation for mid-level FDEs at top labs is roughly $385K, senior roughly $610K, and principal over $1.2M.
  • fdenest.com / fde.academy / sundeepteki.org — three specialized sites for interview prep and career paths.
  • Levels.fyi — real-time compensation data for positions at Palantir and others.
  • The job postings themselves are the best textbooks: the FDE job postings from OpenAI, Anthropic, Decagon, Harvey, and Scale AI are worth studying word by word.
