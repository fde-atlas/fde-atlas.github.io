What is the market rate for FDEs? Comparing full-time salaries, contract work, and side-job hourly rates on the same scale

Original title: FDEの単価相場は？正社員年収・業務委託・副業時給を同じ物差しで比較

FDE総研 · 2026-11-10 · 11 min read

Summary

This FDE Soken article converts Japanese FDE compensation across employment types—full-time salary, contracting monthly rates, and side-job hourly pay—into a common hourly metric. Assuming 1,920 working hours per year, a 10-million-yen salary equals roughly 5,200 yen/hour, while contractors need about 1.3 times that (around 6,800 yen/hour) to cover self-paid insurance and idle months. It tabulates posted ranges from LayerX, Loglass, JDSC, Sansan and others, and estimates Palantir FDSE median total compensation of $211,000 at roughly 16,500 yen/hour. Experience is the biggest pay driver, with US senior FDEs earning over twice junior rates. The author proposes evaluating offers via hourly conversion, scope of responsibility, and guaranteed utilization, while cautioning that all figures are job-posting examples, not actual payment statistics.

Key points

  • Converts salary, contract and side-job pay into hourly rates for comparison.
  • Contractors need roughly 1.3x full-time hourly rates as a floor.
  • Palantir FDSE median pay of $211k equals about 16,500 yen/hour.
  • Experience matters most: US senior FDEs earn over 2x junior rates.
  • Offers should be judged on hourly rate, responsibility scope, and utilization guarantees.

What are FDE market rates by employment type?

According to FDE Soken's conversion, the 10 million yen annual salary in job postings aggregated by JAPAN AI Lab corresponds to an hourly rate of about 5,200 yen, and taking on the same salary level as contract work would require a minimum hourly rate of about 6,800 yen.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role that brings a product into the client's environment and delivers results while implementing it. A full-time employee's "annual salary," a contractor's "monthly rate," and a side job's "hourly rate" use different units, so they cannot be compared as-is. FDE Soken uses "hourly conversion"—converting everything to an amount per hour—as a common yardstick. All figures in this article are based on data available as of November 2026 and represent amounts listed in job postings or calculation examples, not statistics of amounts actually paid.

What is the expected annual salary of a full-time FDE in hourly terms?

Dividing full-time FDE job posting salaries by 1,920 hours per year, the range spans from the low 2,600 yen per hour at the bottom to the low 13,000 yen per hour at the top.

The 1,920 hours per year figure treats "8 hours per day × 240 days per year" as the standard working hours and excludes overtime. Converting the expected annual salaries from job postings introduced by JAPAN AI Lab and SB Bit under this assumption yields the following (as of November 2026).

How do contract and side-job FDE hourly rates compare?

Contractors must cover social insurance and non-working months themselves, so about 1.3 times the full-time hourly conversion is the lower bound for an equivalent level.

For full-time employees, the company covers part of the premiums for employees' pension, health insurance, and employment insurance. The employees' pension premium rate is 18.3%, split equally between employer and employee (Japan Pension Service). FDE Soken sets the company's total share at roughly 15% and, accounting for periods when contractors cannot work due to sales or administrative tasks, assumes 10.5 working months per year (1,680 hours) for contract work.

Side jobs do not require this markup because the person is already covered by social insurance through their main job. The benchmark for comparison is the "hourly conversion of your main job." However, side-job income exceeding 200,000 yen per year requires a tax filing (National Tax Agency). Considering the effort and expenses, it is generally safe to assume that a side job paying less than your main job's hourly rate is not worthwhile. The calculation formulas are explained in detail in Is a Freelance FDE Viable? and FDE Side Jobs.

How does Palantir's FDE compensation in the US compare on the same scale?

The median total compensation for a Palantir FDSE is $211,000, which converts to an hourly rate of about 16,500 yen assuming 150 yen per dollar.

According to figures introduced by SB Bit as levels.fyi data, total compensation for a Palantir FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer) ranges from $171,000 to $295,000. At an assumed rate of 150 yen per dollar, that is roughly 25.65–44.25 million yen per year, or about 13,400–23,000 yen per hour over 1,920 hours per year.

However, this total compensation includes stock-based compensation and bonuses. Stock payouts vary depending on the vesting period and share price, and US working hours and tax and social insurance systems differ from Japan's. Therefore, treat this as a reference figure for grasping the order of magnitude, not a number for rigorously comparing "how many times" it exceeds domestic job postings.

How do FDE rates vary by years of experience, current role, English proficiency, and company size?

Of the four factors, years of experience shows the largest difference. According to Movin's data, while junior FDEs in the US earn $180,000–250,000, seniors earn $400,000–630,000—more than double.

There is no single source that simultaneously compares rate differences across these conditions. FDE Soken therefore organized the analysis by separating figures with sources from general observations that can be drawn from the structure of the role.

How do years of experience and current job affect rates?

Years of experience determine the upper limit of your rate, while your current job's experience determines where in the range you are offered at the time of joining.

As with the JDSC position of 5 to 25 million yen introduced by JAPAN AI Lab, there are cases where the same job posting shows offers nearly five times apart. This suggests a design where grade level and depth of experience are directly reflected in the offer amount.

Differences based on current job arise less from years of experience than from "which processes you have personally carried through." According to FDE Soken's assessment, those from SWE backgrounds can easily meet entry requirements through implementation skills, but if they cannot demonstrate experience defining problems with clients, they tend to stall at the middle of the range. Those from IT consulting backgrounds are the opposite. In interviews, it is advantageous to be able to concretely describe at least one experience where you compensated for a missing process.

How do English proficiency and company size affect rates?

English proficiency affects the breadth of the market you can apply to rather than the rate itself, and differences in grade design move offer amounts more than company size does.

FDE job postings at Palantir and Anthropic are published in English, and client interactions are assumed to be in English. Whether you can access US-level compensation tables depends on your English ability, but if you limit yourself to domestic Japanese companies, ranges in the 10-million-yen class exist even without English. The required level is outlined in Is English necessary for FDEs?

Regarding company size, there are postings with narrow ranges like Sansan's 10.29 to 14.56 million yen alongside wide ones like JDSC's. No statistics show that company size alone determines rates, so FDE Soken looks not at "size" but at "range width = grade design." The wider a company's range, the more grade negotiation at offer time affects the final amount.

Are FDE rates higher than SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer?

Since no statistics compare FDE rates with those of the four other roles using the same source, FDE Soken compares them by what is being paid for, using Palantir's official blog definition of "one customer, many capabilities" as the benchmark.

Palantir contrasts regular engineers as "one capability, many customers" and FDEs as "one customer, many capabilities." If you consider that the high rate corresponds to "the breadth of responsibility taken on for a single customer," the differences between roles become easier to understand.

How does the way rates are determined differ by role?

FDE compensation is paid for outcomes from problem definition through adoption, which differs in basis from SIer man-month rates or SWE feature implementation.

RoleMain object of compensationHow rates are determined (general tendency)Detailed comparison
FDEDefining, implementing, and embedding solutions for one customer's problemsJob postings often show ranges exceeding 10 million yenThis article
SWEImplementing features delivered to many customersDetermined by technical grade and scope of responsibilityDifferences between FDEs and engineers
IT consultingProblem analysis and proposalsDetermined by per-project fees and positionDifferences between FDEs and consultants
PdMProduct directionCentered on full-time employee grade systemsDifferences between FDEs and PdMs
SIerWork hours for development with fixed specificationsDetermined by man-month rate × hoursDifferences between FDEs and SIer

SIer man-month rates are tied to "how many hours were worked." FDEs, on the other hand, are evaluated on "how much the customer's problem advanced," so even at the same hourly equivalent, the basis supporting that amount differs. FDE rates are offered high because few people can handle both implementation and client negotiation alone; compensation does not rise simply because of the job title.

For whom does the FDE rate not pay off?

For those who want to focus on development with fixed specifications, or who do not want travel and client-handling burdens included in their hourly rate, FDE rates are unlikely to pay off.

The hourly equivalent of FDE compensation includes compensation for on-site coordination, rework when requirements change, and uncertainty until results appear. People who fit the following tend to be more satisfied with SWE or SIer roles even at the same hourly equivalent.

  • You find fulfillment in building fixed specifications with high quality
  • You find coordination with clients and eliciting vague requirements burdensome
  • You prefer work styles where working hours and compensation are clearly linked

How to assess suitability is covered in Who is suited to being an FDE?

How can you judge whether an FDE rate is fair?

FDE Soken assesses this along three axes: hourly equivalent, scope of responsibility, and guaranteed workload. Start by dividing full-time annual salary by 1,920 hours and contract fees by contracted hours, then line them up in the same table.

Whether an offer is high or low cannot be judged by the amount alone. Even with the same 12-million-yen annual salary, the effective value changes depending on the scope of outcome responsibility and whether there is a cap on working hours.

What is the procedure for putting offer amounts on the same yardstick?

The procedure has four steps: convert annual salary, monthly fee, and hourly rate all into hourly terms, then check differences in scope of responsibility and guaranteed workload.

  • Full-time employee: Divide expected annual salary (confirm whether it includes fixed overtime pay) by 1,920 hours
  • Independent contractor: Divide the monthly fee by contracted monthly working hours and subtract the agent's commission
  • Side job: Compare the hourly rate with your main job's hourly equivalent, factoring in the hassle of tax filing and expenses
  • Line up all three in one table, and add scope of responsibility (outcome-based or hours-based) and guaranteed workload (minimum contract period, cap on hours)

What should you verify when negotiating rates?

The three points to verify are contract type, cap on working hours, and settlement method for scope changes; if these are ambiguous, your effective hourly rate drops.

Knowing how companies estimate FDE costs makes it easier to have a sense of market rates during negotiations. For the client's perspective, see What is the market rate for FDE costs?. Note that VACAN Technologies defines itself as "a technology partner firm that solves on-the-ground challenges with working outputs" (official website). In the FDE work style, compensation is paid for "working outputs" rather than time; keeping this as a premise in negotiations makes discussions go more smoothly.

How did FDE Soken collect and calculate its rate data?

FDE Soken used November 11, 2026 as the evaluation date and converted the job posting aggregates from JAPAN AI Lab and SB Bit into hourly rates based on assumptions of 1,920 hours per year and 15% employer-borne costs.

The sources of the figures and calculation assumptions are published so that readers can recalculate with their own conditions. Since results change when assumptions change, substitute your own working hours and active months when using them.

What are the data limitations and update policy?

All figures are either amounts presented in job postings or calculated examples with assumptions, and do not represent actual payments or contract rates.

Since actual payments for job posting salary estimates are determined by grade, evaluation, and negotiation, the upper end of a range cannot be taken at face value. Also, the figures covered here are limited to some domestic companies and US-based Palantir, and do not represent the distribution of FDE roles overall. Do not apply them as-is to companies or roles outside this scope.

Because salary and job posting figures change frequently, the tables will be reviewed when source updates or new aggregates are published, and updated together with the "as of" notation in the text and the article's update date. Palantir's own role-based ranges are tracked separately in What is Palantir's salary?.
