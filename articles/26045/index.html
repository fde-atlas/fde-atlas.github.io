What does "on-site" mean? — Enterprise AI Implementation Glossary | FDE Encyclopedia

Original title: 驻场是什么意思？— 企业AI落地术语

FDE百科 · 1 min read

Summary

This glossary entry explains on-site embedding (驻场) in enterprise AI deployment: service-provider staff work long-term at the customer's location, which the article frames as the basic working form of the FDE model. It stresses that embedding is not occasional visits but participating in daily operations to gather first-hand information on real pain points, data conditions, and organizational resistance. The OpenAI and Morgan Stanley collaboration illustrates this: the technical pipeline took 6-8 weeks to build, but earning wealth advisors' trust required four months of on-site iteration. The article also notes embedding carries real costs, so profitability depends on choosing the right scenarios, limiting scope, and converting lessons into reusable assets.

Key points

  • On-site embedding means long-term work at the customer's location, the basic FDE working form.
  • It differs from occasional visits: engineers join daily business operations.
  • Its core value is information bandwidth—first-hand access to pain points, data, and resistance.
  • In the OpenAI–Morgan Stanley case, on-site iteration took 4 months versus a 6-8 week pipeline build.
  • Profitability requires choosing scenarios carefully, controlling scope, and building reusable assets.

Full text

On-site means that the service provider's personnel work long-term at the client's office and on the business front line, directly engaging with real processes, data, and people; it is the basic working mode of the FDE model.

On-site is not "showing up for meetings": FDE-style on-site work means treating the client's site as the primary workplace, participating in daily business operations, and discovering problems, building systems, and driving adoption in the real environment.

The value of being on-site lies in information bandwidth—the real pain points of the business, the true state of the data, and the actual organizational resistance can only be captured firsthand on-site. In the collaboration between OpenAI and Morgan Stanley, the technical pipeline was built in 6-8 weeks, but getting wealth advisors to truly trust and use the system took 4 months of on-site iteration—work that could only be done in an on-site capacity.

The costs of on-site work are also real, so choosing the right scenarios, controlling scope, and promptly feeding experience back into reusable assets are key to whether the on-site model can be profitable.
