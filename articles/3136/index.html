FDE vs Platform engineer

Plank · 2026-09-11 · 2 min read

Summary

This article contrasts forward-deployed engineers with platform engineers: FDEs build inside an external customer's environment with almost no control over it, while platform engineers build internal infrastructure for their own colleagues with near-total control. The technical overlap is substantial — 41% of FDE job postings ask for distributed systems experience, and 11% mention production or on-call duty. Drawing on Plank's census of 1,262 verified open FDE roles across 555 companies, the author argues platform engineers are among the strongest candidates for transitioning into forward deployment, with customer-facing judgment (explicitly required in 54% of postings) as the main gap to close. The piece ends with guidance on which role fits which situation.

Key points

  • FDEs serve external customers; platform engineers serve internal developers.
  • FDEs have almost no control over the customer's environment.
  • 41% of FDE postings require distributed systems experience.
  • 54% of postings explicitly require customer-facing skills.
  • Based on a census of 1,262 open FDE roles at 555 companies.

A platform engineer builds internal infrastructure so their own company's developers can ship faster — their customer is a colleague. A forward-deployed engineer builds inside an external customer's environment so that customer gets a working system — their customer is a customer. Both are infrastructure-heavy and integration-heavy; they point in opposite directions.

Side by side

Forward-deployed engineer Platform engineer
Who the customer is An external company paying for an outcome Internal engineers at their own company
Direction of the work Outward, into someone else's stack Inward, under their own product
Environment control Almost none Near-total
Measured on One customer's deployment succeeding Developer velocity and reliability
Customer contact Continuous, external, often on-site Internal, via tickets and RFCs
Shared skills Distributed systems, integration, deployment, on-call Distributed systems, integration, deployment, on-call

What a platform engineer actually does

A platform engineer builds and runs the internal systems other engineers depend on: CI/CD, deployment tooling, observability, service scaffolding, developer environments. Success is measured in the productivity and reliability of the engineering organization around them.

Where they overlap

The technical overlap is substantial and underrated: 41% of FDE job descriptions ask for distributed systems experience, and the day-to-day work — plumbing systems together, debugging across boundaries, owning something in production — is recognisably the same craft. Platform engineers are among the more reliable converts into forward deployment for exactly this reason. What has to be learned is the customer-facing half, which is not a small half.

What the hiring data shows

39 postings — 3.1% of the census — pair forward-deployed with data, platform, or infrastructure engineering in the title, at companies including Palantir, Cohere, Datafold, Spear AI. Alongside the 41% of job descriptions asking for distributed systems experience, it suggests employers see infrastructure depth as a genuine route into the role.

From Plank’s first-party census of 1,276 verified open FDE roles across 547 companies, observed 2026-10-30. The census covers FDE-titled postings, so it describes the forward-deployed side of this comparison; it is not a survey of platform engineer roles.

Which one do you need?

Forward-deployed engineer

  • The work is in a customer's environment and the customer is watching.
  • The bottleneck is external deployment, not internal velocity.
  • Someone needs to be accountable to a party outside the company.

Platform engineer

  • Your own engineers are slowed by tooling and infrastructure.
  • The work is internal and there is no external customer in the loop.
  • You need leverage across your whole engineering organization.

Questions people ask

  • Can a platform engineer become a forward-deployed engineer?
  • Frequently, and it is one of the better-fitting transitions. The infrastructure and integration skills transfer almost completely — 41% of FDE job descriptions ask for distributed systems experience. What has to be built is customer-facing judgment, which 54% of postings require explicitly.
  • Do forward-deployed engineers do DevOps work?
  • Regularly. Deploying into a customer's environment means their CI, their cloud account, their security review, and their on-call rotation — 11% of FDE postings mention production or on-call duty directly.
