Common Pitfalls in FDE Authentication and Authorization Integration

Original title: FDE 認証と権限連携でつまずく勘所

日本Fde協会 · 2026-11-17 · 5 min read

Summary

This article from the Japan FDE Association examines where forward deployed engineers most often get stuck when connecting an AI environment to a company's existing systems: authentication and authorization integration. It argues that a successful SSO login only verifies identity, not permissions, and that mismatches between IdP attributes and what the AI side expects frequently stall design. The author recommends decomposing existing business-application roles and rebuilding them under least privilege rather than reusing them, since AI executing actions on a user's behalf can unintentionally cross segregation-of-duties boundaries. Audit logging also needs early design so service-account actions can be traced back to the logged-in individual, with retention, tamper prevention, and identity attribution agreed before launch.

Key points

  • SSO success is authentication only; authorization is a separate design task.
  • IdP attribute mismatches commonly stall role mapping during integration.
  • Reuse of existing roles risks unintended data access through the AI layer.
  • AI-mediated execution can cross segregation-of-duties boundaries.
  • Audit trace requirements (retention, tamper-proofing, identity) must be fixed pre-launch.

In this article, FDE refers not to Full Disk Encryption, but to Forward Deployed Engineer, a role that embeds into customers' sites and solves challenges from both technical and business perspectives. When connecting an AI-powered environment to existing business systems, many teams get stuck on bridging FDE authentication and permissions. Rather than focusing on permission design on the AI agent side, this article narrows down to the one step downstream: how to connect with the existing system's role structure, and organizes the key points to nail down at the design stage. After reading this article, you will be able to identify the pitfalls of SSO and role integration in advance, and draw up your own permission mapping policy that takes into account segregation of duties and audit trails.

Where FDE Authentication Integration First Gets Stuck

When introducing a new AI-powered environment, there is almost always a request to route logins through the existing SSO platform. The first pitfall here is the mismatch between the attributes returned by the IdP (the platform that centrally manages authentication) and the attributes the AI side expects.

SSO Success Does Not Mean Permission Resolution

Even if login succeeds via protocols like SAML or OIDC, that only confirms "who" the user is. "What they are allowed to do" is a separate issue. Teams tend to relax once SSO is connected and postpone role integration design. This is a classic stumbling block. The obvious principle of separating authentication and authorization often gets lost in the rush of implementation.

Getting Stuck on Attribute Mapping

It is not uncommon for department codes or group names on the IdP side to change with every personnel transfer or organizational restructuring. At a manufacturing client's site, I have seen hundreds of groups with different naming conventions per factory, which could not be straightforwardly mapped to roles in the AI environment, bringing the design to a halt. If you do not inventory attributes first, the breakdown only becomes visible at the integration testing stage.

Permission Mapping Based on Segregation of Duties

What is truly difficult in FDE authentication integration is not the technical connection itself, but how to translate existing segregation of duties into roles. If this is done carelessly, it will need to be rebuilt later from an internal controls perspective.

Do Not Reuse Existing Roles As-Is

Roles in existing business applications were created for those specific business processes. If reused as-is in the AI-powered environment, loopholes emerge where AI can access data that should not be visible. From my experience working on the operations side at a financial institution, the segregation-of-duties principle of separating approvers and executors tends to break down when AI is in the middle. This is because the action of "AI executing on someone's behalf" unintentionally crosses the boundaries of segregation of duties.

Translate with Least Privilege

What we value at design time is the approach of first decomposing existing roles and then reconstructing only the operations needed in the AI environment with least privilege. The table below is a starting point.

PerspectiveExisting System SideWhat to Verify on the AI Environment Side
AuthenticationCentralized via SSO/IdPWhether attributes are returned consistently
AuthorizationBusiness rolesRedefine with least privilege instead of reusing
Segregation of dutiesSeparation of approval/executionWhether AI execution crosses boundaries

Audit Trail and Log Integration Design

After permissions are connected, the next thing that tends to be overlooked is logging. If you cannot trace "who" touched "which data" "when" "via AI," you cannot fulfill accountability under internal controls.

Can IDs Be Correlated in Logs?

AI environment logs only contain service account names, making it impossible to trace back to the individual who logged in via SSO. This frequently occurs in organizations spanning multiple departments. In environments with strict audits, such as healthcare, if you do not decide upfront on a design that can correlate individual IDs with AI execution logs, you will face major rework after operations begin.

Solidify Audit Trail Requirements at the Design Stage

Audit trails are the area where retrofitting is most difficult. Before implementation, we recommend agreeing with the relevant departments on three points: retention period, tamper prevention, and individual identification. For an overview of internal controls, Introduction to Internal Controls for FDE Implementation is also a useful reference. Permission design for AI agents themselves is covered in Permission Management Design for AI Agents. This article focuses on the downstream: bridging with existing systems.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. If SSO works, is permission integration also complete? No. SSO is authentication that confirms "who" the user is, while authorization, which determines "what they are allowed to do," is separate. Designing the two separately is the prerequisite for avoiding pitfalls in FDE authentication integration.

Q. Can existing business application roles be used as-is in the AI environment? Reusing them as-is tends to create loopholes that unintentionally cross segregation-of-duties boundaries. We recommend redefining only the necessary operations with least privilege.

Q. Are authentication and permissions covered in the FDE certification exam? The FDE certification is a private certification independently operated by the association, and its exam areas include information security and internal controls. It is not a national qualification, and whether or not one holds it does not legally restrict engagement in this work.
