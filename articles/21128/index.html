A GTM Engineer is a role that replaces the GTM process—from lead acquisition to contract renewal—with AI-driven systems, taking charge of both efficiency gains and improved results. This article explains how the role differs from similar positions such as sales planning, along with real examples of required qualifications and salaries from 4 job postings found in Japan as of September 2026, and how companies can secure talent for this role.

Published date Last updated

Many companies have introduced AI into their marketing and sales activities. Yet according to a survey by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, only 9.5% of companies in sales and 7.4% in marketing/PR said their business processes themselves had become AI-centered*1. Even though the tools are in place, the work of researching prospects, entering data into the CRM, routing leads, and drafting emails still remains part of staff members' daily routines.

A GTM Engineer is a role that streamlines the entire flow from lead acquisition to contract renewal using data and AI, while building systems that achieve results that were difficult to accomplish manually. This means greatly expanding the scope of research and scoring that could previously only be applied to a subset of prospects. Responding in real time to signals such as surges in sign-ups or usage. Deciding meeting assignments and call priorities based on data. Testing new approaches at a frequency impossible to sustain manually. Building these four things as systems and keeping them running is the job of this role.

The name emerged in the US in 2023*2, and as of September 2026, job postings for this role title could be confirmed at 4 companies in Japan (this article's own tally). In the postings at the time of the tally (September 10), the maximum offered salary was 20 million yen*3.

GTM stands for Go-to-Market and refers to the entire set of activities that bring a product to market and generate revenue. Marketing acquires leads, inside sales converts them into meetings, sales closes deals, and customer success handles onboarding through to contract renewal. This article refers to this series of flows as the GTM process. US explanations likewise describe the GTM Engineer as a role that designs and implements this GTM process as an AI- and data-driven system*4*5.

There are already many articles introducing the definition of the GTM Engineer and typical US salaries. What this article adds is a tally of 4 domestic job postings examined one by one, and the conditions under which companies should place this role in their organization.

What is a GTM Engineer?

A GTM Engineer is a role that replaces the GTM process—from lead acquisition to contract renewal—with AI-driven systems, taking charge of both efficiency gains and improved results.

Clay defines the deliverable of this role as "a running system," stating that cleaned records or sent emails are not deliverables*4. This article treats the role as including the state in which the system is embedded in field staff's daily procedures and keeps running. The job is not finished once it is built—it extends to the point where the field continues to use it.

What is GTM Engineering? The practice of building revenue systems with AI, data, and automation

GTM Engineering is the practice of building revenue systems using AI, data, and workflow automation, instead of running GTM through human labor*4. The relationship is that GTM Engineer is the job title, and GTM Engineering is the name for the work that person performs.

A name coined in 2023 at US company Clay for a person who handled both sales and systems building alone

Clay is a US company that provides enterprise data acquisition and workflow automation*6. Around August 2023, an employee at the company who single-handedly handled sales, data preparation, systems building, and defining sales procedures was making 30–35 sales calls per week. Seeing this, a co-founder wrote in the company Slack, "Let's call them a GTM Engineer." The person themselves recounted this on a podcast*7. An official Clay article also records the naming as occurring in 2023*2.

To give just one number on its subsequent spread: according to a tally by Bloomberry analyzing 1,000 job postings, postings for GTM Engineers combined with RevOps, a role with similar duties, increased by 205% when comparing January–September 2024 with the same period in 2025*8. However, the absolute numbers are still small, and whether this is a market searchable by job title is another matter.

The GTM Engineer is an AI talent in the marketing and sales domain: not building models, but reconfiguring the GTM process

On this site, "AI talent" refers to practitioners who redesign, implement, and embed AI into business operations. Not people who research machine learning models. The GTM Engineer is the marketing and sales domain version of this: someone who reconfigures the GTM process by combining existing AI, CRM, and MA (marketing automation). A map of related roles is organized in "What is AI Talent? Types, Skills, Training Points, and New Options for Implementation and Adoption in the Field".

Differences from other roles

There are already many roles involved in the GTM process: sales planning, Marketing Ops, Sales Engineer, SDR, and more. Because all of them work within the same flow, names alone do not reveal the boundary with the GTM Engineer. The dividing line is the degree to which they build and validate systems themselves. Sales planning sets strategy and goals and manages through reports; its weight of building and validating systems itself is low. The GTM Engineer takes the steps of executing plans that have not yet become systems and turns them into systems, then validates them. Clay, which defined this role, explains it in contrast to RevOps (a role that organizes sales and marketing operations and data across functions): RevOps keeps existing operations running, while the GTM Engineer changes the operations themselves*4. The following summary is based on the definitions of Clay and ZoomInfo, and this site's definition of the FDE*4*9.

RoleFocus (what they are responsible for)DeliverablesWeight of building and validating systems themselves
GTM EngineerReplacing GTM process tasks with systemsSystems that keep runningHigh
Sales Planning (SalesOps)Strategy, goals, reportsPlans and management metricsLow
RevOpsOrganizing and keeping existing operations runningWell-organized processes and dataMedium
Marketing OpsCampaign operations and measurementDeliveries and measurement resultsMedium
Sales Engineer (Pre-sales)Technical explanation and proposals for own productsProposals, demosLow (product side)

In practice, the boundaries are usually blurrier. Sales planning starts as a concurrent role. The relationship with SDRs is not a replacement either; the point is to turn research and preparation into a system, so that each person in charge can cover a wider range *4.

There is one difference from FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer). FDE is a role at companies that have their own products, working on-site at customer companies to implement. GTM engineers build their own company's GTM process from the inside. What they share is going into the field, restructuring operations, and getting AI to run; I wrote about the general form of this in "What is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Salary, Skills, Career, and How Companies Secure Them".

What a GTM Engineer Does

Let's divide the GTM process into five stages: lead acquisition, meeting conversion, meeting preparation, closing/onboarding, and renewal/churn prevention. Every stage involves work like researching, inputting, sorting, and drafting. The GTM engineer's job is to replace that work with systems, stage by stage.

The table below, organized in this article, shows what becomes a system in each stage.

StageWork replaced by a system
Lead acquisitionBuilding lists of target companies, enriching company information
Meeting conversionScoring ideal customer profiles from 1 to 100 with reasons, automatic sorting triggered by signals such as new signups or surging usage
Meeting preparationAutomatic generation of pre-meeting preparation reports, prioritizing calls
Closing/onboardingPost-sale initial setup and guidance on getting started
Renewal/churn preventionDetecting churn warning signs through support ticket analysis

The meeting conversion and churn prediction examples come from Clay's guides, and the meeting preparation examples from domestic job postings and practitioners' published articles *3*4*10.

Daily work: scoring, research, CRM maintenance, and signal-triggered automation

Clay lists as daily operations: lead scoring agents, AI-driven large-scale account research, CRM enrichment that queries multiple data providers in sequence, and automation triggered by new signups, surging usage, and renewal dates *4. These consist of three layers: a data layer that organizes CRM and company data, an automation layer that receives signals and runs processes, and an LLM (large language model) layer that handles text generation and scoring; the tool names listed in job postings correspond to one of these layers.

"What AI replaced was not thinking, but clicking work." That is what Mike Heilmann, Senior Advisor, says in a Norwest article *5. Judgment stays with the person in charge. What becomes a system is the surrounding work: researching, inputting, and sorting.

Measure results by the meeting conversion rate of the chosen stage and time saved

Clay uses meetings booked and time saved as its metrics *2. In this article, tailored to readers' pain points, we define measurement by two metrics: the meeting conversion rate (or number of meetings booked) of the chosen stage and time saved. A single stage's impact is invisible in company-wide conversion rates, so we look at the stage level. Measure the two weeks before starting to establish a baseline, and evaluate at quarterly boundaries.

Here is one benchmark for time saved. Among sellers at companies with 50 to 5,000 employees who used generative AI within the past year, 93% experienced reduced work time, averaging 2.2 hours per week *11. For meeting preparation, the same survey's figure that salespeople spend an average of 68.4 minutes preparing per meeting serves as the baseline *11.

Why GTM Engineers Are Needed Now

The tools have arrived. What hasn't changed is the business processes; companies answering that things have changed that much remain at 9.5% for sales and 7.4% for marketing/PR *1.

The figure below compares Japan and the US on the actual figures for the three categories published by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, for sales. The three categories don't sum to 100% because companies answering "not introduced," "don't know," or "no applicable operations" are excluded. The survey's valid responses were 515 companies in Japan and 309 in the US *1.

In Japan, the most common form is "employees use it for personal work efficiency" at 27.2%, "used for specific tasks within a department" at 22.9%, and "business processes have been changed to be AI-centered" at 9.5%. In the US, "used for specific tasks within a department" is most common at 38.4%, with process change at 19.2% *1. Combining the three categories in this article gives Japan 59.6% and the US 86.4%. The difference lies not in the number of users, but in whether the work itself has changed.

Looking at what it's used for, the skew is clear. Among 3,560 companies using generative AI, the main uses were "writing, summarizing, and proofreading text" at 45.1% and "information gathering" at 21.8%, while "data aggregation and analysis" was 7.4% *12. This is not a breakdown limited to marketing or sales departments across all operations, but the pattern is readable: it penetrates individual document work but struggles to enter operations spanning multiple people and tools.

Before being embedded in business processes, weekly users don't reach 60%

There is a survey showing the relationship between the level of organizational support and adoption. HubSpot Japan's "Survey on Awareness and Reality of Sales in Japan 2026" (published February 2026), a survey by a CRM vendor. Subjects were 1,545 sellers (as stated in the data collection) at companies with 50 to 5,000 or fewer employees *11. The stages follow the survey's Step categories, and the sample size for each stage is not published *13.

Level of organizational supportPercentage using generative AI at least once a week
No support35.6%
Step 1 "Permission" (only an announcement that generative AI may be used)47.2%
Step 2 "Training" (conducting training and study sessions)56.9%

The content of "embedding into business processes" is defined by the survey as CRM integration and shared prompts *13. Whether you stop at training or go all the way to embedding makes a big difference in the percentage using it weekly. What determines adoption is this stage, and whether someone in the organization is responsible for it.

Putting all of this together, the center of adoption is document creation and information gathering, and companies that answered that their business processes had changed account for less than 10% in both sales/marketing and marketing/PR, with adoption highest among the group that went as far as embedding it into business processes. In other words, having the tool in place and having business processes change are two different things. However, none of the three surveys measured by process stage, so whether AI is present in the process that determines the meeting conversion rate cannot be determined from these figures. You won't know how far into your own processes it has penetrated until you measure time by process. I covered how the same structure is occurring at the company-wide level in "What is AI talent? Types, skills, key points for development, and a new option that handles implementation and adoption in the field".

How to become a GTM engineer

When we apply the capabilities of AI talent, as organized in the previous article "What Is AI Talent? Types, Skills, Development Points, and a New Option That Handles Implementation and Adoption in the Field", to the domains of marketing and sales, the required qualifications in domestic job postings can be read at face value. Three of the four companies list three or more years of practical experience as a requirement, and the remaining one seeks experience in business improvement and project leadership*3*14*15*16. The entry point lies in the work you are doing now.

Strengths and gaps to fill, by background

  • Sales/Sales planning background: They have an understanding of GTM processes, CRM operations, and how to read metrics. They need to add prototyping with LLM APIs and automation tools, and data design.
  • Engineer background: They have implementation, prototyping, and system integration skills. They need to add an understanding of the processes that determine the meeting conversion rate, and collaboration with marketing and sales teams in the field.
  • Business design/development lead background: They have skills in breaking down business processes and driving projects. They need to add GTM process metrics and AI implementation.

Regardless of background, what everyone needs in common is judgment about which data may be used. If you cannot decide, within the scope of personal information protection laws, how far customer information and call records may be used, you will stall at the prototyping stage. A survey of practitioners found that about 88% use major CRMs, and nearly 70% use coding agents*22.

Read the required qualifications in job postings directly as learning items

If you are coming from sales, the order is: write out the meaning of CRM fields in sales language, calculate your own department's meeting conversion rate yourself with SQL, and turn one weekly task into a system using no-code automation. If you are coming from engineering, sit in on sales meetings to observe before and after, build a CRM data dictionary, prototype signal-based routing, and get it into the field. The CRM design, SQL, no-code automation, and everyday use of coding agents listed among the required qualifications in job postings are, as they are, your learning items.

The exit paths from this experience are also becoming visible. In Japan, there are examples of combining the GTM engineer and product manager roles*18, and, as in the job postings cited in the next chapter, examples where the role is positioned with accountability for KPIs*3. According to Kyle Poyar's tally, 45% of people with this title work at support or consulting firms, so engaging from outside the company is one exit path as well*23.

From the applicant's perspective, there are two job-posting points specific to this role to check: whether the role has a defined scope of responsibility for KPIs, and whether it belongs to a development organization or a GTM organization. The general checkpoints are the same three points described in "What Is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Salary, Skills, Career, and How Companies Can Secure One".

How companies can secure GTM engineers

The entry point for securing talent is not deciding on the job title to recruit for. It lies in deciding which organization the person will be placed in, and in what form of responsibility.

The placement should be within an organization accountable for revenue metrics

The condition for deciding placement is not the organization's name, but whether the person can be positioned in a form where they are accountable for the metrics of the chosen process. Two of the four companies state this explicitly in their job postings: SalesNow writes "take ownership of KPIs such as pipeline generation volume, meeting conversion rate, and win rate, and pursue business goals in tandem with the CEO/COO," and LayerX writes "evaluation criteria are business impact, not code quality"*3*15. For the remaining two companies, we could not confirm any statement of performance accountability. Norwest also states that GTM engineers "only function when tied to revenue problems and business workflows; they must not be treated like general administrative staff or IT/BI teams"*5.

For each process stage: how far current support goes, and what remains

When we group the process stages where current support providers cover the same scope, the five stages consolidate into three. Here is the summary of this article.

ProcessScope covered by current supportRemaining work
Lead acquisition, meeting conversionSFA/MA vendor customer success, up to tool setup and usageBuilding signal-based routing tuned to your own meeting conversion rate
Meeting preparation, closing and onboardingSales consulting and training, up to program design and proposalsEmbedding preparation reports into sales reps' daily routines
Renewal and churn preventionSales outsourcing and SDR outsourcing, up to executing instructed calls and outreachHanding over churn-risk signals to customer success procedures

The remaining work takes the same shape at every stage: redesigning the process with accountability for revenue metrics, implementing systems that span SFA, MA, and generative AI, embedding them into field staff's daily routines, and handing over the operating procedures to the department that owns that stage's metrics (such as sales planning). This remaining work is the execution gap in the GTM process. The general form of this—the limits of what a product-selling function can cover—is described in "What Is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Salary, Skills, Career, and How Companies Can Secure One".

The market for hiring by job title is still small

Domestic job postings identifiable by job title number four companies. According to Sumble's data, in the three months up to September 10, 2025, there were 128 GTM engineer job postings, 45 in the most recent month, compared with fewer than 10 per month in 2024. By the same tally, the number of openings is one-ninety-second that of SDRs (inside sales responsible for calls and outreach)*23. If you assume you can wait while searching by job title, you will not secure the talent.

The usual stumbling blocks: not narrowing the process, or not getting into daily routines

The failure patterns are fairly predictable. Assigning someone multiple processes at once as a side role, so everything stalls at prototyping. Stopping at the "allowed to use" stage, so nothing enters the staff's daily routines. Measuring against the company-wide meeting conversion rate, so changes in the chosen process go unseen. All of these can be avoided by narrowing down to a single process and carrying it through to embedding in daily routines.

Convert internally, or bring in from outside

There are two conditions for developing talent internally: the CRM holds at least one year of meeting data and metrics can be captured; and there is someone in sales planning, marketing, or inside sales who has automated a weekly task themselves with spreadsheets or no-code tools. You can check for qualifying candidates using the items in "Complete Edition: AI Talent Recruitment, Development, and Utilization Checklist 50 | Explaining Every Step from Design to Results".

When hiring externally, there are three conditions specific to this role: deal data must exist in the CRM; the person who will take over operational procedures must be in a department that owns metrics for that process, such as sales planning; and the metrics must be measurable as deal conversion rates. I wrote about how to hire and develop the first person in "A Thorough Explanation of FDE Development: Hiring, How to Use External Resources, and How to Structure Development".

Sometimes it is still too early. If deal records are scattered across individual emails and spreadsheets with nowhere to collect them, the first task will be deciding where to put the data. In the HubSpot Japan survey mentioned earlier, CRM adoption among all sellers was 38.1%, flat since 2022 *11. If the time spent on tasks is too small to measure savings, or if there is no one to track metrics, putting the groundwork in place first will get you there faster.

Conclusion

Companies that answered that their business processes have shifted to AI-centered ones remain at 9.5% for sales and 7.4% for marketing and PR *1. What moves this number does not seem to be adding tools. In organizations that merely granted permission to "use it," 47.2% of people use generative AI at least once a week; in organizations that also provided training, 56.9%; and in organizations that embedded it into business processes, 74.8% *13. Usage grows with each stage, and whether you go all the way to embedding makes a big difference. Embedding happens only when the work of someone researching, entering, and routing data is replaced by a system and incorporated into the person in charge's daily routine. What the name GTM engineer signals is that this work can be carved out as a dedicated role.

So what a company should decide first is not whether to hire a GTM engineer. It is which of the five processes to assign, and to whom. The market for hiring by job title is still small, and if you assume you will search and wait, no one will be secured. The title can come later; what must be decided first is the person. If someone suitable exists internally, draw them from sales planning, marketing, or inside sales; if not, bring in an external practitioner as a member of the organization accountable for revenue. Either way, once one person is secured, you can start tracking that process's deal conversion rate and time savings.

After the first process is running, the question is not how many people to add, but which process to systematize next. Rather than outsourcing AI initiatives in marketing and sales and waiting, place them inside the organization accountable for revenue and move forward together. What is missing to join the side whose business processes have actually changed is not tools or job postings, but that one person.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. If we already have a sales planning manager, do we not need a GTM engineer?

Sales planning handles planning and management metrics. It depends on who owns the process of replacing GTM work with systems. If the existing manager can take it on, a GTM engineer is unnecessary; if not, someone needs to own it regardless of title.

Q. When starting as a dual role, how do you divide responsibilities between sales planning and GTM engineering?

The division is that sales planning decides the processes and metrics, while the GTM engineer builds the systems for those processes and puts them into the field. In a dual role, split decision-making time and building time across the week. If both are handled in the same meetings, the building work stalls.

Q. How do you divide responsibilities between a dual-role GTM engineer and the CRM administrator or IT department?

The IT department and CRM administrator manage data sources and access permissions; the GTM engineer handles restructuring processes and deploying them to the field. What to decide first is the scope of which data can be touched and to what extent. If this remains ambiguous, every prototype will stall on permission checks.
