The Limits of Active Cyber Defense and 8 Proactive Measures for Companies | How FDEs Turn Insights into Implementation

Summary

This article analyzes the October 2026 breach of GMO's お名前.com Desktop Cloud (FX auto-trading VPS), where 26,739 servers were potentially affected and data exfiltration was confirmed on October 24. It explains that Japan's active cyber defense framework, effective October 1, 2026, only lets the state trace and neutralize attack sources—it does not cover routine corporate defense, user asset protection, or compensation. GMO was not among the 15 critical-infrastructure sectors covered. President Masatoshi Kumagai admitted in-house white hackers had flagged issues beforehand, but availability concerns and prioritization slowed fixes. The article proposes eight pre-incident measures and describes how FDEs can carry security findings through to implementation and operational adoption.

Key points

  • GMO requested the government's first application of active cyber defense, but no mechanism exists for individual company requests.
  • The timeline ran from a user inquiry on October 19 to confirmed data exfiltration on October 24.
  • Kumagai's blog acknowledged pre-existing internal white-hacker findings that were not fully implemented in time.
  • Eight prioritized checks start with credentials, admin permissions, and API integrations.
  • FDEs define completion criteria, prototype fixes without downtime, and embed controls into operations.

In October 2026, an unauthorized access incident occurred at GMO Internet Group's "onamae.com Desktop Cloud." The most affected service was the "FX Automated Trading VPS." GMO Internet Group urgently requested the government to apply "active cyber defense." Meanwhile, representative Masatoshi Kumagai revealed on his blog that an in-house white hacker had pointed out the issues in advance.

There are two things FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) and corporate staff can learn from this incident. One is to correctly understand the extent to which they can rely on national mechanisms. The other is to build, during normal times, a structure that carries findings through to implementation.

What Is Active Cyber Defense? What Can and Cannot Be Done Under GMO's Request?

Active cyber defense is a framework in which the government identifies and neutralizes attack sources. It does not take over companies' routine defenses or damage recovery.

How Does the System Work, and Who Is Covered?

It is a system in which the police or Self-Defense Forces neutralize attackers' servers in response to serious attacks. In principle, prior approval from the commission is required for implementation.

The related law was enacted in May 2025, and neutralization measures became executable from October 1, 2026. Neutralization includes deleting malware, stopping attack programs, and disabling attack command functions. Prior approval is issued by the "Cyber Communications Information Supervision Commission."

The public-private partnership covers critical infrastructure operators in 15 fields (designated critical infrastructure operators). These fields include electricity, gas, water, finance, telecommunications, broadcasting, transportation, and logistics. GMO Internet Group is not included in this scope. However, the government has explained that it does not limit by industry when considering neutralization measures. The criteria for judgment are the impact on national life and security (the severity and urgency of the incident).

What Can and Cannot Be Done Under GMO's Request?

What can be expected from the government extends only to tracking and blocking attack sources. Changing authentication credentials and compensating for damage fall to companies and users.

GMO positioned this case as the system's "first invocation case" and requested that the government lead the tracking, blocking, and neutralization of the attack infrastructure. According to an article by Masumi on Yahoo! News, a senior government official acknowledged receiving the request and stated that defensive support would be provided. On the other hand, an executive of the National Cyber Office (NCO) explained that there is no mechanism to accept requests from individual companies.

PerspectiveWhat Can Be Expected from the System/GovernmentWhat the System Cannot Cover/What Is Uncertain
Dealing with attack sourcesAnalyze traces and communication logs to determine whether the attacking servers can be identifiedWhether neutralization proceeds depends on the progress of the analysis
Decision to invokeJudged based on the severity and urgency of the incident (the government says it does not limit by industry)Whether the request is accepted is up to the government. Results may well not be made public
TimingAimed at preventing damage or stopping it from spreadingSome point out that there are legal interpretation issues in applying it after damage has occurred
Users' assets―Changing passwords, terminating sessions, and reviewing transaction histories are done by users and companies

Some also point out that publicizing the request could put attackers on alert and reduce the effectiveness of neutralization measures. Masumi views active cyber defense as something close to a "last line of defense" for cutting off attack sources. The view is that reducing daily damage depends on the measures that users and companies advance on a routine basis.

How Did the "Gap Between Finding and Implementation" Arise in GMO's Case?

According to the representative's blog, the findings reached the field in advance, and countermeasures were underway. Even so, the depth and speed of the response are said to have been insufficient.

What Are the Published Circumstances and Facts?

According to GMO's official announcements, the situation changed dramatically in about five days, from the inquiry on October 19 to the confirmation of data exfiltration on October 24.

DateContent of GMO's Official Announcements
10/19Started an investigation after receiving an inquiry from a user
10/20Confirmed a malicious program. Suspended new sign-ups and the control panel
10/21 4:00Progressively shut down servers on all plans and issued the first report (26,739 servers potentially affected; stated that no leakage of personal information had been confirmed at that point)
10/22Consulted the police. In the second report, indicated the possibility that login information for trading accounts had been misused
10/23In the third report, clarified that the 26,739 figure was the "number of servers that could be affected," not the number of cases where unauthorized access or data acquisition was confirmed
10/24In the fourth report, announced that customer information had been confirmed to have been taken outside the company (the types and number of items of information were not disclosed)

According to the fourth report, the attacker made threats, including a statement that they would continue unauthorized manipulation of user accounts at more than 70 securities firms, FX companies, and similar firms in Japan and abroad. This "more than 70" is the number stated in the threat, not the number of confirmed victims. GMO has announced that it will not comply with the demands. It also states that the attacker's publicly posted claims may contain falsehoods.

How does the representative's blog explain that "gap"?

Representative Kumagai explains that, as a result of proceeding carefully while weighing availability and priorities against other issues, the attackers found and exploited an opening.

Representative Kumagai's blog (October 25) contains the following statements.

  • "In-house white hackers had previously pointed out issues that needed improvement to the relevant teams."
  • "As a result of proceeding carefully with the response while considering the impact on service availability and priorities relative to other matters requiring action,"
  • "the depth and speed of our response were insufficient, and the attackers exploited that gap."
  • "Although we had professionals on staff, we were unable to fully translate their expertise into flawless organizational defense."

The content and timing of the reported issues have not been disclosed, so the cause cannot be determined from the outside.

What structural factors could make this happen to any organization?

Even technically correct findings tend to be delayed in completion when the service cannot be stopped and other priorities compete for attention.

From here on, this is not about the cause of the GMO incident but a general structural analysis. If there is no agreement between the person who reports an issue and the person who implements the fix on "what counts as done," delays are especially likely. FDE Soken classifies the typical states in which findings stall into the following three.

State of the findingWhy it stallsHow to close the gap
Finding exists but no work has startedThe impact is not translated into business terms and loses out on prioritizationRestate the impact of an attack in business metrics
Work started but remains "in progress"No completion criteria (scope, deadline, verification method)Put completion criteria in a table and agree on them
Fixed but not sustainedNot incorporated into procedures, monitoring, or inspectionsBuild it into operations assuming personnel changes

What preventive measures should companies put in place during normal times?

First, take inventory of credentials and permissions. Next, the basics are to document logs, shutdown decisions, and communication structures during normal times.

What are the eight prioritized inspection items?

First secure credentials, permissions, and APIs, where damage tends to be largest, then move on to public-facing assets, logs, contractors, shutdown decisions, and communication structures.

PriorityItemWhat to do in normal timesRelation to the GMO incident (published facts)
1CredentialsEliminate account and tool credentials stored in plain text on servers; prohibit reusePossibility that login information for trading accounts on servers was misused
2PermissionsList who holds administrator rights and their expiration, and remove unnecessary permissions—
3APIs and external integrationsList integration keys with external services and have procedures to cut them off immediatelyAs an emergency measure, cut off integration access with major exchanges, etc.
4Public-facing assetsRegularly check servers, admin screens, and ports exposed externally—
5LogsDecide retention periods, storage locations, and tamper protection, and keep them in a submittable formSubmitted evidence, including communication records, to the police
6ContractorsWrite contractors' access rights and incident contact routes into contracts—
7Shutdown decisionsDecide in advance who stops what, under what conditions, and over what scopeGradually stopped all plans and disconnected affected systems from the network
8Communication structurePrepare contact points for users, police, and specialized agencies, and templates for public statementsSince the first report, published information in stages through successive updates

Does organizational structure change where you start?

The order of the eight items is the same. However, where to start first changes depending on whether you have dedicated staff, someone who reports issues, or contractors.

Your situationItems to start withReason
No dedicated security staff1 Credentials, 2 Permissions, 8 Communication structureEven a small team can start inventory and contact organization right away
Someone who reports issues (auditors, white hackers) internally or externallyCreating completion criteria for each finding + 7 Shutdown decisionsClose the gap where findings stall as "in progress" first
Infrastructure operations outsourced6 Contractors, 5 LogsSecure access rights and evidence locations in contracts for incidents

How to prepare initial incident response?

The seven steps GMO requested of users in its fifth report can be used directly as a template for a corporate initial response checklist.

The fifth report asks users to do the following (a similar request appears in the third report).

  • Run a full scan of PCs and smartphones with antivirus software and back up data
  • Change passwords for trading accounts and trading tools from devices not used for FX trading
  • Terminate all active login sessions (existing sessions may remain even after changing passwords)
  • Check trading history and balances, and contact the provider about any transactions you do not recognize
  • Enable two-factor authentication
  • Change passwords on other services using the same password, and credentials saved in browsers
  • If contacted by someone claiming to be the attacker, do not open URLs or files and do not reply

For companies, we recommend rewriting this list to fit your own services and actually running through it once in training during normal times. In particular, the third point, "sessions may remain even after changing passwords," is easily overlooked. If money is demanded, write into your procedures to consult the police or specialized agencies before paying (a recommendation from Masumi's article).

How does FDE carry security findings through implementation to sustained adoption?

FDE rephrases findings into requirements with completion criteria. It prototypes ways to fix issues without stopping services and takes responsibility until they are established in operations.

What are the five steps from finding to adoption?

In FDE's approach, known from Palantir, a single person or team carries the work continuously from issue discovery to adoption.

  • Issue discovery: Talk directly with the person who reported the issue (in-house security staff or external auditors) and restate the impact of an attack in business terms
  • Requirements definition: Decide "what counts as done." Put the number of target servers, deadlines, and verification methods in a table
  • Prototyping: Test ways to fix without stopping production (phased switchover, parallel operation, etc.) in a small scope
  • Implementation: Based on pilot results, roll out to the whole system in order. Make progress visible on a weekly basis
  • Establishment: Incorporate into procedures, monitoring, and periodic inspections so it continues even when the person in charge changes

For the FDE role itself, see What is FDE? for an overview.

How do you handle the trade-off between availability and countermeasures?

FDE does not reduce this to a binary choice of "shut it down or leave it running." It lays out options and deadlines for each scope of impact and prepares the materials needed for management decisions.

The "impact on availability" and "prioritization" raised in the representative's blog are factors that tend to delay countermeasures in any environment. What FDE does here is not to make the decision on behalf of others, but to create the comparison table needed for decision-making.

OptionImpact on businessRemaining riskMaterials provided to decision-makers
Stop part of the system this week and fix itServices in the target scope are temporarily suspendedHandling users during the outageScope and duration of the shutdown, announcement text
Fix it by next month without stoppingOperations continueAttacks during the period until remediation is completePhased migration plan and weekly progress
Strengthen monitoring for now and observeSmallest impactRoot cause remainsMonitoring items and a deadline for reviewing the policy

When laid out this way, decision-makers can choose with a deadline attached. Note that we cannot definitively claim that having an FDE would have prevented this incident. The role of FDE is to shorten the time that issues remain stuck in "being addressed."

How do the roles of diagnostic firms, SIers, and FDE differ?

Unlike assessments that end with findings or outsourcing that builds to spec, FDE is responsible for everything from defining completion criteria to establishing operations.

ProviderMain deliverablesScope from findings to establishment
External assessments / security consultantsAssessment results, improvement proposalsFocused on identifying issues. Implementation is handed back to the client
SIers / operations outsourcingImplementation and operations based on specificationsHandles implementation and operations after requirements are set
FDERequirements with completion criteria, prototypes, established operationsContinuously handles everything from issue identification to establishment

This table shows a typical division of roles and is not a comparison of superiority or inferiority. For details of the comparison, see Differences between FDE and consultants and Differences between FDE and SIers.

How do you manage the FDE's own access permissions when entering customer environments?

FDEs themselves who enter customer environments can become a new entry point for attacks. The principles are minimal permissions, time-limited, and logged.

How do you decide what permissions to grant?

Permissions given to FDEs are granted with a scope and deadline defined for each task. The basic rule is not to grant always-available administrator privileges.

ItemRecommended practice
Scope of permissionsLimit to only the targets needed for the task, starting with read-only
DeadlineSet to expire automatically in line with the work period
AuthenticationRequire two-factor authentication and do not store customer environment credentials locally
DevicesUse separate devices for customer work and do not share passwords with personal services
LoggingStore operation logs on the customer side so that FDEs cannot delete them

What happens on departure and in case of an incident?

On departure, permissions, API keys, and shared accounts are all disabled together. In case of an incident, FDEs operate within the customer's communication framework.

FDEs may handle multiple customers. For this reason, devices and accounts are separated so that one customer's credentials do not get mixed into work for another customer. When a project ends, the list of granted permissions is reviewed together with the customer, and a record is kept that everything was disabled. The fact that FDEs enforce this kind of management themselves also adds credibility when recommending countermeasures to customers. Building the structure at implementation is explained in How to introduce FDE.

Active cyber defense is a means for the government to deal with attack sources. It does not take over a company's day-to-day defense. FDE's job is to see through to the end, on-site with the customer, tasks such as organizing permissions, preserving logs, and agreeing on shutdown decisions.
