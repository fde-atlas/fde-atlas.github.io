1. FDE-Related Terms (8 terms)

1-1. FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)

An engineer role belonging to an AI/SaaS vendor who gets deeply involved in client companies' operations to solve their problems. Unlike traditional consultants or SES, the key feature is commitment to business outcomes.

Differences from similar roles: FDEs go deep into a single company's operations, while GTM Engineers work across multiple departments, and Development Strategists focus on technology investment decision-making.

Learn more → What is an FDE? Introduction Strategies for Forward Deployed Engineers, the New AI-Era Role, for Enterprises

1-2. FDE Consulting-Style Support

A support model where a single team provides end-to-end accompaniment from problem discovery through implementation to on-the-ground adoption. Typical cost is 1.5 to 3 million yen per month.

Learn more → What is FDE Consulting-Style Support? Pricing Structure, Differences from SES/Traditional Consulting, and DeFactory's Support Services

1-3. FDE Lab Development

A support model designed to solve the problem of "we can build a PoC but can't take it to production." A multi-person team focuses on "creating the solution."

Learn more → What is FDE Lab Development? The Full Picture of a "Solution-Creating" Support Model Unlike SES or Consulting

1-4. GTM Engineer

A role that uses technical skills to connect business front-line departments—sales, marketing, and customer success—across the board, driving business process automation and data utilization.

Differences from similar roles: The distinction between FDE, GTM Engineer, and Development Strategist can be summarized as: "single-company focus = FDE," "cross-department = GTM Engineer," and "technology investment decision-making itself = Development Strategist."

Learn more → What is a GTM Engineer? Differences from FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), Salary Benchmarks, and the Right Choices for Working Engineers

1-5. Development Strategist

A role that synchronizes business strategy with system development to maximize business value. Its focus is on linking technology investment decisions to business strategy.

Differences from similar roles: Unlike FDEs, who handle implementation and adoption on the ground, and GTM Engineers, who work across multiple departments, Development Strategists are characterized by their focus on technology investment "decision-making" itself.

Learn more → What is a Development Strategist? Roles and Essential Skills

1-6. SES (On-Site Client Dispatch)

A contract arrangement where engineers are stationed at the client's site and are compensated for working hours within the scope of instructed tasks. Problem definition and support for on-the-ground adoption are outside the scope.

Learn more → What is SES? A Thorough Explanation of Its Structure and Characteristics from Both Corporate and Engineer Perspectives

1-7. PoC (Proof of Concept)

A process of verifying on a small scale, before full-scale development, whether a new technology or idea is feasible.

Learn more → What is a PoC? Pros and Cons and 3 Keys to Success

1-8. Completion-Based Quasi-Delegation Contract

A contract type based on a quasi-delegation contract, with the achievement of pre-agreed KPIs and outcome metrics built in as a condition. Unlike hourly billing, business outcomes serve as the evaluation criterion.

Learn more → What is a Completion-Based Quasi-Delegation Contract? Differences from Fixed-Price Contracts and How to Leverage External Partners

2. Roles and Job Types (15 terms)

2-1. Product Manager (PdM)

A role responsible for maximizing product value. It differs from the PjM (Project Manager), whose focus is on progress management.

Learn more → What is a Product Manager? The Role, Differences from PjM, and 3 Common Traits of PdMs in Name Only

2-2. AI Architect

A role responsible for the overall technical design of AI implementation projects, covering everything from data investigation to AI model selection and integration design with existing systems.

Differences from similar roles: Compared to IT Architects, who are responsible for overall system quality; Solution Architects, who design solutions spanning multiple technologies and vendors; and Technical Architects, who set implementation-level technical policies, AI Architects differ in that they specialize in the AI domain.

Learn more → What is an AI Architect? Essential Skills and How to Go Independent as a High-Rate Freelancer

2-3. Solution Architect (SA)

A role that designs technical solutions for entire systems by combining multiple technologies and vendors to address customer problems.

Differences from similar roles: Compared to AI Architects, who specialize in the AI domain; IT Architects, who are responsible for system quality; and Technical Architects, who set implementation-level technical policies, Solution Architects focus on "designing solutions that span multiple technologies and vendors."

Learn more → Complete Guide: What is a Solution Architect? Role, Essential Skills, Salary, and the Path to High-Rate Independence

2-4. IT Architect

A role responsible for the quality of the system itself (availability, scalability, security, etc.). They handle "defining non-functional requirements."

Differences from similar roles: Compared to AI Architects, who specialize in the AI domain; Solution Architects, who span multiple technologies and vendors; and Technical Architects, who set implementation-level technical policies, IT Architects are responsible for "quality assurance of the entire system."

Learn more → What is an IT Architect? Required Roles, Essential Skills, and How to Join as a High-Rate Freelancer

2-5. Technical Architect (TA)

A role that assesses the technical feasibility of a system and defines technical policies at the implementation level.

Differences from similar roles: Compared to the IT Architect, who is responsible for overall system quality, the Solution Architect, who works across multiple technologies and vendors, and the AI Architect, who specializes in the AI domain, the Technical Architect focuses on "defining technical policies at the implementation level."

For more details, see → What Is a Technical Architect? Roles, Essential Skills, and How to Win Projects

2-6. CPO (Chief Product Officer)

A member of the executive team responsible for the overall product strategy, connecting business-side decision-making with product development.

Differences from similar roles: While the CPO is responsible for product strategy, the CTO is responsible for the company's overall technology strategy.

For more details, see → The Complete Guide to Freelance CPOs | Essential Skills and Generative AI Applications

2-7. VPoE (VP of Engineering)

A role responsible for managing the engineering organization, with a focus on practical organizational building such as hiring, evaluation systems, and improving development processes.

Differences from similar roles: While the CTO is responsible for the overall technology strategy as part of the executive team, the VPoE differs in focusing on the practical management of the engineering organization.

For more details, see → What's the Difference Between VPoE and CTO? Role Definitions and How Freelancers Can Win High-Rate Projects

2-8. CTO (Chief Technology Officer)

A member of the executive team responsible for the company's overall technology strategy, involved broadly from technology selection to organizational building.

Differences from similar roles: While the CTO is responsible for the company's technology strategy, the CPO is responsible for the overall product strategy, and the VPoE focuses on the practical management of the engineering organization.

For more details, see → The Complete Guide to CTOs | An In-Depth Look at Why Companies Seek External Freelance CTOs and Essential Skills

2-9. Pre-Sales

A role that supports the technical aspects of sales meetings, positioned between sales and engineering, ensuring the feasibility of proposals.

For more details, see → What Is Pre-Sales? Roles, Salary, and How to Go Freelance

2-10. DX Consultant

A role that supports the use of digital technology from a strategic perspective, with the goal of transforming a company's business model itself.

Differences from similar roles: Compared to the AI Consultant, who handles AI utilization planning and proposals, and the Generative AI Consultant, who specializes in the generative AI domain, the DX Consultant places greater emphasis on "transforming the business model itself."

For more details, see → What Is a DX Consultant? Roles, Essential Skills, and How to Go Independent at High Rates

2-11. Scrum Master

A role that supports the Scrum development team in functioning smoothly; a facilitator who removes obstacles for the team.

For more details, see → What Is a Scrum Master? Role Differences and the Path to High-Rate Freelance Work

2-12. AI Consultant

A role responsible for everything from planning and designing AI utilization to client proposals and requirements definition; a "translator" between technology and business.

Differences from similar roles: While the role overlaps with the Generative AI Consultant, who specializes in the generative AI domain, and the DX Consultant, who focuses on business model transformation, the AI Consultant has the broadest scope, covering planning and proposals for AI utilization in general.

For more details, see → Essential Skills and Project Acquisition Guide for AI Consultants × Freelancers

2-13. Generative AI Consultant

A role/domain specializing in generative AI consulting, requiring deep understanding of technology trends specific to LLMs and generative AI.

Differences from similar roles: Compared to the AI Consultant, who handles AI utilization in general, and the DX Consultant, who focuses on business model transformation, the biggest difference of the Generative AI Consultant is "specialization in the LLM and generative AI domain."

For more details, see → The Complete Guide to Generative AI Consulting × Freelance Work: Job Content and Essential Skills

2-14. Freelance Engineer

An engineer who works on development projects as an independent contractor rather than as a company employee. FDE roles are also often filled under this contract model.

For more details, see → 【2026 Edition】The Complete Survival Strategy and Roadmap for Freelance Engineers | A Comprehensive Summary of Rates, Deal Structures, and Work Styles

2-15. IT Consultant

A role that supports corporate IT strategy and system implementation from an analysis and strategic advisory standpoint; implementation is generally handed off to a separate vendor.

For more details, see → IT Consultant Independence Strategy! Freelance Rate Benchmarks and How to Win Projects

5. Basic Terms (12 Terms)

5-1. DX (Digital Transformation)

A concept referring to initiatives that transform business models and business processes through digital technology.

5-2. SIer (Systems Integrator)

Refers to companies and business models that take end-to-end responsibility for a client company's system development and implementation, from requirements definition through design, implementation, and operation/maintenance.

For more details, see → What's the Difference Between SES and SIer? Characteristics, Pros, and Cons

5-3. In-House Development

Building an organizational structure that enables a company to drive system development, AI adoption, and more internally, without relying on external vendors.

5-4. Domain Knowledge

Specialized knowledge specific to a particular industry or business area.

5-5. Quasi-Delegated Contracts, Fixed-Price Contracts, and Dispatch Contracts

Terms describing engineer contract types. Under a quasi-delegated contract, payment is for the performance of work; under a fixed-price contract, for the completion of deliverables; and under a dispatch contract, for labor based on the Worker Dispatch Law.

For more details, see → What Is an SES Contract? An In-Depth Look at the Differences Between Quasi-Delegated, Fixed-Price, and Dispatch Contracts

5-6. Vulnerability Assessment

The process of checking websites and networks for weaknesses (vulnerabilities) that could serve as a foothold for attacks. The results are compiled into a report, but the assessment itself does not fix the weaknesses; remediation is a separate task.

For more details, see → [How to Choose a Website Vulnerability Assessment Service and Typical Costs | Decide Who Will Fix Issues After the Assessment [November 2026 Edition]](1)

5-7. Penetration Testing (Pen Test)

A test in which the assessor actually attempts intrusion using an attacker's methods to see whether they can reach sensitive information. Compared to vulnerability assessments, which identify weaknesses, it places stronger emphasis on verifying the protection of the entire system.

For more details, see → [How to Choose a Website Vulnerability Assessment Service and Typical Costs | Decide Who Will Fix Issues After the Assessment [November 2026 Edition]](1)

5-8. WAF (Web Application Firewall)

A mechanism that detects and blocks malicious traffic to websites. It does not eliminate vulnerabilities, but is used as a means of reducing damage until fixes are complete.

For more details, see → [How to Choose a Website Vulnerability Assessment Service and Typical Costs | Decide Who Will Fix Issues After the Assessment [November 2026 Edition]](1)

5-9. Prompt Injection

An attack that embeds commands to hijack an AI's behavior within the instructions given to it. If commands are hidden in internal documents or web pages, the AI may follow them and expose information that should not be disclosed.

For more details, see → [5 Measures to Prevent Data Leaks with Generative AI | 4 Leak Pathways and Why Prohibition Rules Alone Can't Stop Them [November 2026 Edition]](1)

5-10. Shadow AI

The use of AI services by employees for work purposes through personal accounts or similar, without the company's knowledge. Because the company cannot verify what information was entered, it can lead to data leaks.

For more details, see → [5 Measures to Prevent Data Leaks with Generative AI | 4 Leak Pathways and Why Prohibition Rules Alone Can't Stop Them [November 2026 Edition]](1)

5-11. DLP (Data Loss Prevention)

Short for Data Loss Prevention. A mechanism that detects and blocks the movement of sensitive information outside the organization. For example, it can be used to stop text containing card numbers from being sent to AI.

For more details, see → [5 Measures to Prevent Data Leaks with Generative AI | 4 Leak Pathways and Why Prohibition Rules Alone Can't Stop Them [November 2026 Edition]](1)

5-12. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

A mechanism that searches internal documents, feeds them to AI, and uses them in responses. Since the range the AI can see directly determines the scope of its answers, organizing access permissions is a prerequisite.

For more details, see → [5 Measures to Prevent Data Leaks with Generative AI | 4 Leak Pathways and Why Prohibition Rules Alone Can't Stop Them [November 2026 Edition]](1)
