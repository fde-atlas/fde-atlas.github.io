Interview questions to ask a forward deployed engineer

Kernavique · 2026-11-21 · 11 min read

Summary

Kernavique proposes an interview question bank for forward deployed engineers covering six areas: decomposing vague customer problems, production coding, customer and stakeholder work, ownership under ambiguity, travel and on-site work, and AI evaluation work where relevant. The article notes almost no employer publishes a role-specific interview process; OpenAI and Databricks guides are general, and Ramp's blog lists four assessment axes. It recommends structured interviews per US Office of Personnel Management guidance: identical questions, a common rating scale, and independent scoring before discussion. It also cites EEOC guidance on prohibited questions about protected characteristics, disability, and photographs. The authors admit the question bank is untested and the sample of ten job ads skews toward government-facing roles.

Key points

  • Six assessment areas drawn from published sources and job ads.
  • No employer publishes a role-specific FDE interview guide, per the authors' check.
  • Structured interviews: same questions, one rating scale, independent scoring.
  • Six of ten ads state travel figures, up to 50% at OpenAI.
  • EEOC guidance bars questions on protected traits, disability, and photos.

Ask a forward deployed engineer questions that test six things the published sources and job ads name: decomposing a vague customer problem, production coding, work with customers and senior stakeholders, ownership under ambiguity, travel and on-site work, and AI evaluation work. Ask every person the same questions and score them against written criteria, as US Office of Personnel Management guidance read on November 21, 2026 describes. The questions here are Kernavique’s suggestions.

What do employers that publish their process say they assess?

Very few publish anything for this role. On November 21, 2026 we could open no employer interview guide written for forward deployed engineers. None of 14 employer ads read on November 20, 2026 describes interview stages.

What exists is below. Three of the four pages are general.

Source Stages or exercises What it says is tested Read
OpenAI interview guide, general, no page date Résumé review, introductory calls, a skills-based assessment such as pair coding or a take-home project, then “4–6 hours of final interviews with 4–6 people over 1–2 days” Well-designed solutions, code quality, communication and collaboration November 21, 2026
Databricks careers page, general A first screening call, a pre-onsite screen, an onsite loop that “typically consists of four to six interviews with various team members”, and a presentation for some roles Role-specific competencies and culture principles November 21, 2026

Whose questions are these?

Ours. The questions below are Kernavique’s suggestions, drawn from the requirement lines and published criteria cited in each group. They are not questions any employer is known to ask.

How do you test decomposing a vague customer problem?

What the sources say. First Round Review, published February 24, 2026 and read November 20, 2026, reports former Palantir staff describing open-ended problem interviews in place of repeated coding tests. Its example is designing a response to an insider-trading problem, including what data you would need and what you would ask the customer. One founder it quotes asked people to present a career problem and “teach us how they used technology to solve it.” This is a third-party report, not Palantir’s own.

  • A customer says its operations data is unreliable. What do you ask in the first meeting?
  • Take a problem from your career and teach us how you used technology to solve it.
  • What data would you need before proposing anything, and how would you find out if it exists?
  • Tell us about a time your first reading of a customer’s problem was wrong. What changed your mind?

How do you test production coding?

What the sources say. Ramp’s blog, read November 20, 2026, treats “technical performance as a bar to pass but avoid optimizing it further.” Of 10 forward deployed ads read the same day, 5 name Python. That sample leans toward government-facing roles, which make up 7 of the 10.

  • Walk us through code you shipped to production for a customer. What broke first?
  • How do you choose between a quick fix for one customer and a change to the core product?
  • Here is unfamiliar code with a fault in it. Talk us through how you would find it.
  • What tests did you put around the last system you deployed?

How do you test work with customers and senior stakeholders?

What the sources say. All 10 ads read November 20, 2026 describe working directly with customers. Scale AI’s public sector ad asks for experience “ideally in customer-facing, forward-deployed, or government contexts.” Databricks’ senior ad, read the same day, wants engineers who “complete projects according to specification with exceptional customer empathy”.

  • Tell us about a customer who asked for something you believed was the wrong solution. What did you do?
  • How would you explain a missed date to a customer’s executive?
  • Describe getting a decision from a senior person who was not technical.
  • How do you carry what you learn at a customer back to your product team?

How do you test ownership under ambiguity?

What the sources say. Palantir’s new graduate ad, read November 21, 2026, values people who “make decisions with incomplete information, and don’t wait to be told what to do next.” OpenAI’s Colin Jarvis told The Pragmatic Engineer on August 12, 2025, read November 20, 2026: “FDEs work in a ton of ambiguity.” A Databricks director wrote on January 22, 2020, read November 21, 2026, that “the most important quality I’ve seen in successful engineers is ownership.” That post is about engineers in general.

  • Tell us about a project where nobody told you what to do next. What did you do first?
  • Describe a decision you made with incomplete information. How did you limit the damage if you were wrong?
  • When did you take on a problem that was not yours to fix?
  • What have you stopped doing because it did not serve the outcome?

How do you ask about travel and on-site work?

What the sources say. Of 10 forward deployed ads read November 20, 2026, 6 state a travel figure and 3 say travel as needed. Databricks’ is 20%. OpenAI’s New York ad, read November 21, 2026, requires up to 50%, and Palantir’s new graduate ad expects 25–50%.

  • Tell us about your longest stretch working at a customer’s site. What did being there change?
  • What do you do in your first week on site with a new customer?
  • How do you keep work moving with your own team while you are away?
  • Our ad states the travel we expect. What questions do you have about it?

How do you test AI and evaluation work?

Use this group only where your ad asks for it.

What the sources say. OpenAI’s ad, read November 21, 2026, measures success by “production adoption, measurable workflow impact, and eval-driven feedback”. Scale AI’s Frontier Agents ad, read November 20, 2026, lists “Running production experiments and measuring real-world business impact” among its duties.

  • How did you decide an AI system was ready for a customer’s users?
  • Describe an evaluation you built. What did it measure, and what did it miss?
  • Results drop after launch. What do you check first?
  • How do you show a customer that a workflow improved?

How should you score the answers?

Use a structured interview. The US Office of Personnel Management’s page on the method, read November 21, 2026, describes asking everyone the same predetermined questions in the same order. It adds: “All responses are evaluated using the same rating scale and standards for acceptable answers.”

A second OPM page, read the same day, names two question types. Situational questions ask what a person would do. Behavioral questions ask what they did, on the principle that “past behavior is the best predictor of future behavior.” It describes panel members rating alone, then discussing large differences.

This guidance is written for federal agencies’ own hiring. It is not a rule for private employers.

The scorecard below is our suggestion.

Criterion What good evidence looks like
Problem decomposition Asks about users, data and constraints before proposing a build
Production coding Describes code they shipped, how it failed and how they fixed it
Customer and stakeholder work Gives a specific case of disagreeing with a customer and keeping the relationship
Ownership Names a decision they made alone and what it cost
Travel and on-site work Describes real time on a customer’s site and what it achieved
AI and evaluation Explains what an evaluation measured and where it fell short

What questions should you avoid?

We report only what official US pages say. This is not legal advice.

  • Protected characteristics. The small business page of the EEOC, the US equal employment agency, read November 21, 2026, recommends avoiding questions about personal characteristics protected by law. Its examples cover race, church attendance, language spoken at home, pregnancy and plans to have children.
  • Disability. Its page on prohibited practices, read the same day, states: “Employers are explicitly prohibited from making pre-offer inquiries about disability.”
  • Job functions. Its enforcement guidance, issued October 10, 1995 and read November 21, 2026, states: “Employers may ask about an applicant’s ability to perform specific job functions.”
  • Photographs. The prohibited practices page also says employers should not ask for one.

In practice

Pick the groups your own ad supports. Fix the questions and their order, and give each interviewer the scorecard in advance. Have them score alone before comparing notes.

What are the limits of this guide?

  • The question bank is untested. It is our suggestion. We have no data on how answers predict performance.
  • The published guides are general. The OpenAI and Databricks pages cover all roles, and neither has a section for this one.
  • Pages change. Each was read on the date shown.
  • The ad sample is small and skewed. Ten ads, 7 of them government-facing, read on one day.
  • The official guidance is US only.

Questions

  • What should I ask a forward deployed engineer in an interview?
  • Ask about the six areas the published sources and ads name: breaking down a vague customer problem, production code, customer and stakeholder work, ownership, travel, and AI evaluation where your ad asks for it. The question bank on this page is Kernavique’s own suggestion, drawn from those requirement lines. It is not a list of questions any employer is known to ask.
  • Do employers publish how they interview forward deployed engineers?
  • Rarely. On November 21, 2026 we could open no employer guide written for this role. OpenAI and Databricks publish general stage-by-stage guides, and Ramp’s engineering blog, read November 20, 2026, names four things it assesses without giving stages.
  • How many interviews should the process have?
  • The sources give no rule. OpenAI’s general guide, read November 21, 2026, describes “4–6 hours of final interviews with 4–6 people over 1–2 days”. Databricks’ general careers page, read the same day, describes an onsite loop of four to six interviews. Neither page is specific to this role.
  • How should I score the answers?
  • Use a structured interview. US Office of Personnel Management guidance, read November 21, 2026, describes asking everyone the same predetermined questions in the same order and rating answers on one scale. That guidance is written for federal agencies’ own hiring, so it is a model, not a rule for private employers.
  • What questions should I avoid?
  • The small business page of the EEOC, the US equal employment agency, read November 21, 2026, recommends avoiding questions about personal characteristics protected by law. Its examples cover race, church attendance, language spoken at home, pregnancy and plans to have children. We report what the pages say and do not give legal advice.
  • Can I ask about travel?
  • Our suggested questions ask about past on-site work and the travel figure in your own ad. Of 10 forward deployed ads read November 20, 2026, 6 state a travel figure and 3 say travel as needed. For anything touching health or personal circumstances, read the official pages cited here and ask your counsel.
