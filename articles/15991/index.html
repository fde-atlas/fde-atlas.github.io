What's the difference between outsourced on-site staffing and FDE? Both work at the customer's site, but they are two different business models

Original title: 外包驻场和 FDE 有什么区别？同在客户现场，两种商业模式

FDE中国 · 2026-08-14 · 2 min read

Summary

This article distinguishes outsourced on-site staffing from the FDE model, arguing the difference lies not in location but in accountability and problem definition. On-site contractors execute scopes defined by clients and bill by person-day; FDEs help define the problem and own whether the scenario actually works. The author notes many jobs marketed as FDE in China are effectively AI-era outsourcing, offers three self-check questions (scope definition, accountability, reusability), and warns that managing FDE teams with outsourcing-style metrics causes the highest attrition.

Key points

  • Core divide: clients buy time from contractors, results from FDEs.
  • FDEs co-define scope and can reject unreasonable requirements.
  • Contractor growth plateaus; FDE growth stays steep across projects.
  • Three-question self-check: definition, accountability, reusability.
  • Person-day metrics on FDE teams drive the highest attrition.

Full text

This piece addresses the most common confusion in the domestic context—many "on-site" roles are sold as FDE positions. The most valuable takeaway is a dividing line: outsourced on-site work executes a client-defined scope by person-day, while an FDE participates in defining the scope and is accountable for the outcome; from this, all the differences in workflow, voice, capability growth, and pricing follow. The most common misuse is managing an FDE team under an outsourcing model—evaluating by person-days and letting the client define all requirements—which systematically destroys the value of the role. Compared with the on-site comparison of implementation consultants, this piece's added value is a three-question self-check for on-site engineers, which can be used directly to assess the true quality of your role. One more market observation: domestic AI delivery services are evolving from person-day contracts to outcome-based pricing, and this trend will accelerate the divergence between the two models; the earlier you position yourself on the side that is accountable for outcomes, the sooner you gain real pricing power. After reading, do one thing: use the three questions to check your current role, and if none of the three are satisfied, factor that into your next career decision.— FDEChina Editorial Board · Sharp Reviewer

What's the difference between outsourced on-site work and FDE?

The most direct answer: the difference is not where the person sits—both are at the client's site—but in the accountability relationship and the right to define problems. Outsourced on-site work executes a scope already defined by the client, by person-day: what to do, to what standard, and how to accept it is all decided by the client; an FDE participates in defining the problem itself, locks the scope together with the client, and is then responsible for whether the scenario ultimately works. One buys your time, the other buys your outcome—these are two different business models.

This dividing line gives rise to a series of day-to-day differences. In terms of work content, outsourced on-site roles mostly involve developing and maintaining pre-defined requirements, while an FDE's work starts from requirement discovery and covers discovery, scoping, delivery, and evals end to end. In terms of voice, outsourced staff can only execute unreasonable requirements or complain about them, while an FDE has the authority to cut requirements and change plans, because he is the one accountable for the outcome. In terms of capability growth, repeating the same type of requirement for three to five years makes the growth ceiling clearly visible, whereas an FDE faces new scenarios in every project—a steep but sustained growth curve. In terms of pricing logic, outsourcing bills by person-day, so more person-days mean more revenue; an FDE's value is anchored to scenario outcomes—see FDE Business Model Analysis. For a dimension-by-dimension comparison with implementation consultant roles, also see The Difference Between FDE and Implementation Consultant.

It must be pointed out candidly that a common phenomenon in the domestic market: many roles branded as "FDE" are in essence outsourced on-site work for the AI era—requirements defined by the client, evaluated by person-days, no participation in discovery, no accountability for outcomes. This is not to say such roles have no value, but rather that if you join one expecting FDE terms (premium, growth, voice), you will most likely be disappointed. On-site engineers can self-check with three questions: Did I participate in defining the requirements, or am I just executing an order? When something goes wrong, am I the one accountable, or does the client absorb it themselves? Is my output reusable in the next project? If none of the three are satisfied, you should reassess the quality of this role; for a transition path, see How to Move into FDE Without On-Site Experience.

A reminder for the enterprise side as well: managing an FDE team under an outsourcing model—filling out person-day reports, accepting all requirements, changing scope at will—is the management style with the highest attrition for this role. For the right approach, see FDE Team Management Guide.
