FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, an engineering role that embeds at the customer's site and implements the company's own product in production, tailored to the customer's actual challenges. Created by Palantir and adopted on a growing scale by generative AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic, FDE job openings surged from 2025 through 2026.

2026 was also the year FDE changed from "a role within certain teams" to "a company-level business." OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS, and Microsoft launched companies and organizations centered on FDEs, and AWS and Anthropic began training and certifying FDEs. Meanwhile, the research firm Gartner criticized "FDE-washing," the practice of selling consulting services under the FDE label. Based on primary sources such as official announcements, job postings, and interview articles, this article explains the definition of FDE, the nature of the work, how it differs from similar roles, salaries, developments in 2026, and companies hiring in Japan, using information current as of October 2026.

Key Takeaways

  • An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who embeds at the customer's site, implements the company's own product in production adapted to the customer's operations, data, and systems, and feeds the insights gained back into the company's product.
  • FDE originated at Palantir in the early 2010s, with the division of labor between Delta (Forward Deployed Software Engineer), who handles implementation, and Echo (Deployment Strategist), who identifies problems, serving as the prototype; in 2026, the model of pairing FDEs with people who define problems continues to spread.
  • In 2026, OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS, and Microsoft successively launched companies and organizations that send engineers to customer sites, and according to Lightcast, US job postings for FDEs exceeded 5,200 in January through July 2026 alone.
  • On September 29, 2026, Gartner predicted that 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendors' FDEs by 2028, sounding the alarm about "FDE-washing," the practice of selling consulting as FDE work.
  • Whether a role is truly an FDE can be judged not by the job title, but by whether the company has its own product, whether performance is evaluated on outcomes and production deployment, and whether the goal is a state in which the customer can continue using the solution on their own.

What is an FDE? The Meaning in Simple Terms

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, an engineer who works not at the company's own office but at the customer's site, building systems using the company's product on top of the customer's infrastructure, data, and business processes, all the way to production. "Forward Deployed" comes from the military term for front-line deployment. It is a hybrid role that requires both software engineering implementation skills and the ability to identify customer problems and build consensus.

"An FDE's scope of responsibility is close to that of a startup CTO. In a small team, they carry end-to-end responsibility for executing projects where high performance is expected."

While a typical product development engineer "optimizes a single product for many customers," the essential difference is that an FDE "optimizes across every technical means for a single customer." Success is measured not by the number of features shipped, but by the impact created for the customer's business. However, FDEs are not solely devoted to serving customers. Their job also includes feeding common problems discovered at customer sites back into the company's product and turning them into features usable by all customers.

Is an FDE an Engineer or a Consultant?

FDEs are engineers. They resemble consultants in that they organize the customer's problems, but they don't stop at proposals; they write code themselves and get it running in production. The differences are compared in detail in Differences Between FDEs and SES, SIers, and Consultants. Some companies, like Fujitsu, pair staff who assess customer problems with FDEs who implement solutions (What is Fujitsu's FDE?).

Is This Different from FDE in IT and Security?

The abbreviation FDE has other meanings as well. In a security context, it can refer to Full Disk Encryption, which encrypts an entire disk, and in a gear context, it refers to a color called Flat Dark Earth. In the context of AI adoption, job changes, and hiring, FDE almost always refers to Forward Deployed Engineer.

How to Pronounce FDE and What It's Called in Japanese

FDE is pronounced "EFU-DII-IE." In Japanese, it is commonly written in katakana as "Forward Deployed Engineer," and is sometimes called an "FDE engineer." Sansan's engineering blog introduces the translation "field-deployed engineer." The official job title at Palantir, where the role originated, is Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE), and FDE and FDSE refer to the same role.

In October 2026, engineers trained and certified by Anthropic were named "Frontier Deployed Engineer," also abbreviated as "FDE." These are not Anthropic employees; the title is a certification earned by employees of customer or partner companies through training and hands-on projects at their own companies. Some companies, like Accenture, use their own job title independent of specific products (RDE: Reinvention Deployed Engineer), while others, like Dentsu Group, promote a marketing version called "FDM (Forward Deployed Marketer)." "Forward Deployed" is becoming a name for a way of working that goes beyond job titles. Conversely, some companies give different job titles to FDE-type work. AI agent company Sierra calls engineers who design, build, and ship agents together with customers "Agent Engineers" (the job title on its job board is "Software Engineer, Agent"), while peer company Decagon recruits for a similar role as "Agent Deployment Engineer" (as of October 16, 2026). When searching for FDE jobs, you need to also look at postings that do not include FDE in the job title.

Where did FDE come from? Palantir's "Delta" and "Echo"

The FDE role was born at Palantir in the early 2010s. It began with the creation of an engineering organization that went on-site to customers and handled implementation, in order to succeed at complex data integration projects for government agencies and traditional large enterprises. According to reporting by The Pragmatic Engineer, until around 2016 Palantir operated with "more FDEs than regular software engineers."

Palantir's field organization consists of two main roles. This division of labor is still referenced today as a model for designing FDE organizations.

Palantir also had a system for training internal software engineers to take on FDE work. Vinoo Ganesh (now CEO of Kepler), who led the rotation program "Project Frontline" at the company, wrote in an essay contributed to Latent Space in September 2026 that about 250 people went through this program, and that many of them now lead forward-deployed teams at OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Anduril, and others. In other words, Palantir alumni are becoming the builders of FDE organizations at generative AI companies.

Notably, Palantir has also given the name "AI FDE" to an AI agent product that builds data pipelines from natural language on its own platform, Foundry. This is an example showing how central a concept FDE is to the company.

What does an FDE do? The nature of the work

The core of FDE work is "bringing customer projects all the way to production," with "feedback and feature development for the company's own product" added on top. OpenAI launched its FDE division in early 2025 and explains that customer projects proceed through the following three phases.

  • 01 Scoping (a few days embedded at the customer): Understand the customer's business processes and identify high-value application areas. Build prototypes with synthetic data
  • 02 Validation: Define evaluation metrics (Evals) together with the customer and validate the solution against success criteria. Adjust scope if technical constraints are found
  • 03 Delivery (several days per week on-site): Nail down the details of the problem and receive data from the customer, build the solution mainly at OpenAI's offices, and demo it to the customer. Prioritize getting a "minimal end-to-end solution" running first

What is distinctive is that discoveries made at the customer site feed back into improving the company's own product. OpenAI highlights a case where evaluation metrics an FDE created for a call center voice automation project exposed performance issues, and collaboration with the research team led to improvements in the Realtime API itself. This back-and-forth movement, where "an implementation for one company" leads to "product evolution for all customers," is the essence of the FDE role.

The FDE workflow: 5 stages

Organizing based on OpenAI's three phases, Palantir's division of labor, and the FDE playbook Atlassian published in October 2026, FDE projects can be divided into the following 5 stages. At Palantir, the problem-discovery-oriented stages are mainly handled by Echo (Deployment Strategist), and the implementation-oriented stages by Delta (Forward Deployed Software Engineer). However, as Palantir itself stated in its official blog, "in theory Echo is more product-manager-oriented and Delta more technical, but in practice the boundary between the two often blurs considerably," so the division of labor is not fixed.

Steps 1-2: Gather "nouns and verbs" in the field and set evaluation criteria

The first step is understanding the customer's business as a structure. In OpenAI's FDE division, engineers spend several days on-site at the customer, sitting next to users, mapping out business processes, and identifying and prioritizing high-value areas. Mr. Ganesh, who led Project Frontline, described the FDE's job as gathering the "nouns and verbs" of the customer's company. Nouns are the things the business treats as real entities, and verbs are how they move; most of these are not written in documents but live in the field, he says.

In one of his examples, a proposal to move a customer's data from CSV to Parquet had been blocked for nearly a year by a single data quality officer. When the FDE observed the officer working alongside them, they saw that the officer's quality check method was opening CSVs on a Windows laptop and visually inspecting rows. The FDE built a Parquet viewer that night, the migration was approved two days later, and the pipeline's runtime shrank from about 17 hours to 2 hours. Picking up on circumstances that never surface in conference-room requirements definition is the reason for going into the field. In Japan too, a representative from Yazaki Corporation stated in an ITmedia Enterprise interview that "much of the FDE's initial work was 'making the data usable.'"

What differs most from traditional system deployments is Step 2, where evaluation criteria are set first. OpenAI places a "validation" phase after scoping, revisiting in the field whether what was decided during scoping is truly the most valuable before agreeing on evaluation criteria. Colin Jarvis, OpenAI's head of FDE, explains that this is because what customers say during scoping often diverges from the reality of field data and systems, so they want to move fast first to find impassable walls, then re-scope toward the most useful work given the constraints.

Steps 3-4: Shape it and show it the next day, then put it into production

Once evaluation criteria are set, build a minimal end-to-end solution with the customer's real data, demo it, and observe reactions. In a First Round Review interview, Shilpa Balaji, who led FDE recruiting at Palantir, described the FDE's job as "not scheduling user interviews, but embedding yourself in the customer and turning what you heard one day into a prototype to show the next day." Once value is confirmed, move on to production-quality implementation on the customer's infrastructure. OpenAI's Deployment Company announcement describes this step as "connecting OpenAI's models to the customer's data, tools, permission management, and business processes, and designing, building, testing, and deploying a production system that can be used with confidence in daily operations."

Step 5: Make the exit condition a state where the customer can keep using it on their own

The FDE's work does not end with the production release. According to Atlassian's FDE playbook published on October 4, 2026, the company's FDE engagements typically run about 12 weeks, going through four stages—Discover, Build, Adopt, and Value—with the goal of leaving behind both AI running in production and a repeatable approach the customer's team can continue to use. CTERA explains that an engagement is complete when the customer can add new use cases without the company's help. Feeding discoveries from customer sites back into the product is also part of this step. OpenAI's FDE team shares knowledge with the research team biweekly and writes field observations in an internal Slack channel called "FDE Field notes."

In terms of working style, on-site work at customer locations accounts for a certain percentage of time. As of job postings on October 16, 2026, Palantir (New York) lists travel to customer sites at up to 25%, Anthropic's London FDE at 25-50%, and healthcare AI company Commure at up to 20%. The Pragmatic Engineer also introduces examples of FDEs working in environments isolated from factory final assembly lines and external networks.

How Do FDEs Differ from SAs, Consultants, and SES?

FDE is often confused with solution architects (SAs), IT consultants, sales engineers, and SES (on-site dispatched engineers), but it can be distinguished by three points: whether the person works for a company that owns its own product, whether they write production-quality code on the customer's infrastructure, and whether they feed discoveries from the field back into their own product. According to a The Pragmatic Engineer article based on an interview with Colin Jarvis, head of OpenAI's FDE division, OpenAI's FDEs are more hands-on than SAs and deal with more ambiguous situations. While SAs rarely write code on customer infrastructure, FDEs write code directly using the customer's infrastructure and tools.

When a company without its own product calls itself "FDE," the reality is closer to SES or consulting.

Why do companies place FDEs even at the cost of gross margin?

Because embedding into customers' business flows and data creates barriers to entry, and because there is value in a mechanism that feeds field discoveries back into the product. Assigning engineers to each customer lowers the high gross margins that are the strength of software companies. We organize the reasons companies still maintain FDE organizations based on writings from US VCs a16z and First Round Review.

In its June 2025 article "Trading Margin for Moat," a16z argued that what AI companies should optimize for now is not high gross margin but growing total gross profit as fast as possible. The article cites the example of ServiceNow starting at a 63.2% gross margin at IPO and Workday at 54.1%, improving to 79% and 75% respectively by 2024, suggesting that businesses requiring manual effort for deployment can sacrifice gross margin in the launch phase and recover once the mechanisms are in place.

The other value is that field discoveries become features for all customers. In a First Round Review interview, Jake Stauch, co-founder and CEO of AI agent company Serval, explained that while it normally takes several months from a customer saying "I want it to work this way" to a resolution, having engineers hear that feedback directly and build it themselves can dramatically shorten that time. At the company, FDEs built more than 60 external app integrations from their field experience and incorporated them into the product.

However, the FDE model does not suit every company. First Round Review lists three conditions for it to work: high customer deal size (FDE is by definition an approach for large enterprises), flexibility in product direction, and diverse customer use cases. From a job seeker's perspective, whether a prospective employer meets these conditions serves as a way to judge whether that company's FDE is the real thing.

What happened around FDEs in 2026?

FDE was elevated from a role within some teams to a company-level business. From April to October 2026, OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS, and Microsoft launched companies or organizations that send engineers to customer sites, while Google Cloud and Salesforce built mechanisms to place FDEs in partnership with SIers and consulting firms. In the fall, AWS and Anthropic began FDE training and certification, and together with Gartner's criticism, "who will serve as FDEs" has become a point of debate.

Job postings are also surging. When our editorial team tallied postings from 133 public job boards (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, etc.; 131 responses) on October 13, 2026, we found 513 postings (about 340 distinct roles) from 80 companies with Forward Deployed, FDE, or FDSE in the job title, led by Databricks (123), Palantir (79), Wonderful (39), and OpenAI (30).

In Japan as well, FDE roles are expanding from AI vendor employees to SIers and consulting firms. Is the FDE just "consulting with a new name"?

The debate as of fall 2026 has reached this conclusion: job titles alone don't tell you anything; what matters is the substance of the evaluation and the contract. On September 29, 2026, Gartner predicted that by 2028, 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendors' FDEs. The reasons are ballooning costs and enterprises' inability to evolve that AI on their own. The firm calls marketing consulting services as FDEs "FDE washing," and says a genuine FDE model must combine the following three principles.

"Many providers are slapping the 'forward deployed' label on deployment support, professional services, solution engineering, and AI consulting. Some companies do this thoughtfully; others do it because it sounds strategic."
Gartnerが示す「本物のFDEモデル」の3つの原則(2026年9月29日のプレスリリースの図を要約)
PrincipleGartner's explanation (summary)Questions for job seekers and client companies to verify (editorial team)
Accountability for outcomesEvaluated not just on whether the project was delivered, but on actual business impact and stable operation in production. If success is defined as "shipping the project," what you're buying is professional servicesAre evaluation and contract metrics based on deliverables, or on business numbers and production stability?
Incentives tied to valueSuccess is measured by business outcomes and realized value, not by billable hours, milestones, or platform usage itselfIs pricing or compensation proportional to effort, or tied to outcomes?
Deep embeddingIn most cases, broad access to both the customer's environment and the vendor's own code, with the ability to build and improve on the spot rather than waiting in a ticket queueIs there authority to modify both the customer's environment and the vendor's own products?

Gartner also recommends that companies using vendors' FDEs define responsibilities for deliverables, knowledge transfer, intellectual property, and withdrawal before signing contracts, pair FDEs with internal experts and users during the engagement, and execute the withdrawal plan agreed at the outset at the exit. In interviews, the firm's analysts have also noted that the right FDE model produces good results, so the target of criticism is not the FDE way of working itself, but the structure of projects where vendor employees build and then leave. The fact that Anthropic and AWS have begun training and certification programs to instill FDE capabilities in the employees of customers and partners can be read as one answer to this criticism.

When evaluating job postings, check four things: ① whether the company has its own product, ② whether there is a mechanism to reflect FDE discoveries back into the product, ③ whether evaluation is based on customer outcomes and production operation rather than hours worked, and ④ whether the engagement's exit leaves the customer able to operate independently. Some companies, like Cursor's October 2026 FDE job posting, explicitly state that success is measured by revenue, cost, time saved, escaped defects, and so on, not by seat adoption.

How much do FDEs earn?

FDE salaries are generally set at or above the level of software engineers with the same years of experience. According to Lightcast data, the median advertised salary for FDE job postings in the US exceeds $188,000, higher than that of traditional software engineers (about $145,000) (Fortune, September 3, 2026). Of the 513 FDE job postings compiled by the editorial team on October 13, 2026, 266 (from 61 companies) listed compensation, and the median of the midpoints of annual USD ranges, aggregated by company, was approximately $215,000. In Japanese public job postings, SoftBank's FDE (seconded to SB OAI Japan) lists a theoretical annual salary of ¥8.123 million to ¥20.346 million, and LayerX's FDE lists an expected annual salary of ¥12 million and up.

For contract work, according to a freelance board aggregation (as of October 7, 2026), the average monthly rate for projects the company classified as FDE was ¥1.068 million. Since job postings are subject to revision, always check the original text before applying.

Who is suited to FDE work, and who is not?

Based on interviews with practitioners who built FDE organizations at companies such as Ironclad, Serval, and Looker, First Round Review lists five qualities common to FDEs: thinking outside the box based on the situation of the customer in front of you; the persistence to see seemingly unsolvable problems through to a solution; technical skill equivalent to a staff engineer at a top company; a disposition to build tools and solutions yourself, one after another; and curiosity about the customer's business. Asa Kalavade, Vice President in charge of FDEs at AWS, also told Business Insider that people who combine all three of a product and customer perspective, deep software expertise, and experience with agentic AI are "unicorns."

  • People who may not be suited: Those who want to work fully remotely (on-site work at customer locations accounts for a certain percentage of the job)
  • People who may not be suited: Those who want to design and improve a single product over the long term (product development engineers are a better fit for that inclination)
  • People who may not be suited: Those who want to focus on implementation once requirements are fixed (FDEs get involved while requirements are still ambiguous and define the scope themselves)
  • People who may not be suited: Those who find interacting with customers burdensome (dialogue itself, from executives to frontline staff, is at the center of the job)

How do you become an FDE?

Because FDE is a new role with few experienced practitioners, companies hire people transitioning from adjacent roles such as software engineers, SIer employees, IT consultants, and pre-sales. The core of preparation is building one LLM-integrated application to production quality and being able to describe your track record in terms of customer outcomes. The strengths you already have and the gaps you need to fill differ depending on your previous role.

In the age of AI agents, will FDEs disappear?

Even as routine implementation is replaced by AI, the work of deciding which business problems to solve and by what evaluation criteria, and the work of embedding solutions into operations, is expected to remain. a16z acknowledges that traditional integration work, such as mapping data fields, can now be done far more efficiently by AI, and in some cases fully automatically, and argues this as a strength of the FDE model, which can reach more customers with fewer people. In a First Round Review interview, Serval's co-founder and CEO said that precisely because AI can in theory do anything, "someone has to steer the product in that direction."

Asa Kalavade, who leads FDE at AWS, also told Business Insider, "Anyone can build an agent today. But how do you make sure that agent has the right guardrails?" suggesting that value has shifted from building itself to making things safe to use in production. In a May 2026 article, The New Stack quoted an FDE as saying, "The model is usually the cleanest part. The hard part is finding the business workflows nobody has documented, the data sources people on the ground actually trust, and the person who knows why the process is the way it is." Atlassian's playbook also cites the company's data showing that agents triggered automatically by business events are 12 times more likely to become an established part of operations than agents users invoke manually, emphasizing placing agents within business workflows over simply building them.

On the other hand, there are skeptical views as well. A Gartner document cited by The Register points out that the FDE model tends to rely on hand-designed structures and business knowledge embedded in code, which becomes obsolete as LLMs advance, leaving behind the cost of rebuilding. According to The Pragmatic Engineer, at Palantir, many FDEs returned to software engineering roles after the launch of its platform Foundry. In the editorial team's view, the more an FDE can deliver value through problem framing and adoption rather than implementation effort, the more their work will grow with the spread of AI agents.

What comes next in an FDE's career?

Since FDEs handle "finding problems in the field, building solutions, and driving adoption" on their own, their subsequent career paths are broad. In an interview with First Round Review, Serval CEO Jake Stauch said that FDEs have strong product sense and an interest in sales, and "are often former founders or future founders." In the editorial team's view, beyond founding a company, career paths include product management that leverages customer understanding, CTO or engineering leadership roles that leverage experience building across diverse domains, and sales or business-side roles that leverage knowledge of customers' inner workings. At Palantir, after Foundry launched in 2016, many FDEs returned to software engineering on the product development side, bringing their field experience into core products (The Pragmatic Engineer). As seen in Project Frontline alumni now leading FDE teams at generative AI companies, management of FDE organizations is also one career path.

Summary

An FDE is an engineering role that performs production-quality implementations at customer sites and is responsible for both customer outcomes and the evolution of the company's own product. Established by Palantir and backed by the enterprise adoption of generative AI, by 2026 OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS, and Microsoft all had FDEs at the company or organizational level. At the same time, criticism has emerged over "FDE-washing," where consulting work is labeled as FDE; whether a role is truly an FDE should be judged not by the job title but by whether the company has its own product, whether outcomes are evaluated in production, and whether customers can become self-sufficient. For people with both engineering skills and the ability to engage directly with customers, this is a role whose options are expanding in Japan as well.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

QWhat is an FDE in simple terms?

An FDE is an engineer who works on-site with customers and implements the company's own product to fit the customer's business, all the way to production. It stands for Forward Deployed Engineer. What sets them apart is that they don't stop at proposals—they write the code themselves and feed the insights gained back into the company's product.

QHow is an FDE different from a consultant?

While consultants' main deliverables are often proposals and plans, FDEs implement systems using their own company's product themselves and are responsible up to running it in production. They share the aspect of organizing business challenges, but FDEs are engineers, and their results are measured by what actually runs in the customer's business and its impact.

QWhat does FDE stand for? How is it pronounced?

It stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, pronounced "F-D-E" in Japan. It derives from the military term "deployed to the front lines," meaning an engineer stationed at the customer's site.

QAre FDE and Frontier Deployed Engineer the same thing?

They are different. An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role in which employees of a company with its own product go into customer sites and implement solutions. Frontier Deployed Engineer is the name of a certification earned through "Claude Frontier Academy," a training program Anthropic announced in October 2026, in which employees of customer or partner companies go through training and a 12-week real project at their own company. Both are abbreviated as "FDE."

QIs FDE a role unique to Palantir?

No. Although it originated at Palantir, many AI and SaaS companies, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Databricks, Atlassian, and Cursor, now offer roles under the same name. When the editorial team tallied 133 public job boards (129 responded) on November 7, 2026, there were 523 job postings from 74 companies with titles including Forward Deployed or similar. In Japan as well, SoftBank, LayerX, JDSC, JAPAN AI, Sakana AI, and others are hiring FDEs (as of October 2026).

QWhat is the difference between an FDE and SES (on-site dispatched work)?

Whether the company has its own product. An FDE is an employee of a company with its own product who works at customer sites, adapts the product to the customer's business, and feeds the insights gained back into improving the product. SES is technical support based on a quasi-delegation contract, with compensation paid for hours worked.

QDo FDEs write code?

Writing. According to a Pragmatic Engineer article interviewing Colin Jarvis, head of OpenAI's FDE division, at OpenAI it is rare for solution architects to write code on customer infrastructure, whereas FDEs write code directly on customer infrastructure. Anthropic's London FDE job posting (as of October 2026) also describes the FDE role as building production applications using Claude within customers' systems.

Q How can you tell whether a job calling itself "FDE" is genuine?

It is effective to check four things: whether the company has its own product, whether there is a mechanism for feeding field discoveries back into the product, whether evaluation is based on customer outcomes and production deployment rather than hours worked, and whether the goal is to leave customers able to operate on their own. In September 2026, Gartner criticized "FDE-washing," where consulting is marketed as FDE, and cited three principles of a genuine FDE model: accountability for outcomes, incentives tied to value, and deep embedding.

Q How much do FDEs earn?

Based on job postings, OpenAI (San Francisco) lists a base salary of $185,000-$300,000 plus equity, Palantir (New York) $135,000-$200,000, and in Japan SoftBank (seconded to SB OAI Japan) lists a theoretical annual salary of ¥8.123 million-$20.346 million, and LayerX from ¥12 million (as of October 2026). According to Lightcast data, the median advertised salary for FDE job postings in the US exceeds $188,000, above the roughly $145,000 for traditional software engineers.

Q Who is not a good fit for FDE?

People who want to work fully remotely, who want to design and improve a single product over the long term, or who want to focus on implementation once requirements are fixed tend to be a poor fit for this role. FDEs spend a certain percentage of their time working on-site at customers and enter at the stage where requirements are still ambiguous, defining the scope themselves.

Q Can you become an FDE without prior experience?

Practical experience in software development or similar is a prerequisite in most job postings. Requirements in public job postings vary widely: Palantir requires at least 1 year after graduating from university, JDSC at least 2 years of development experience, JAPAN AI at least 3 years of practical experience, Anthropic's London FDE at least 4 years in technical customer-facing roles, OpenAI (Tokyo/San Francisco), SoftBank, and Cursor (Japan) at least 5 years, and Databricks (Tokyo) at least 8 years (as of October 2026). There are also tracks for less experienced candidates, such as LayerX's Associate role, and internships for students.

Q Is English required for FDEs?

It varies by job posting. At foreign companies, OpenAI (Tokyo), Cursor (Japan), and Palantir (Tokyo) require both Japanese and English, while Databricks (Tokyo) requires fluent Japanese and business-level English. Among Japanese companies, SoftBank and JAPAN AI list English as a preferred qualification, Sakana AI requires native-level Japanese and lists business-level English as a preferred requirement (all as of October 2026). Check the language requirements in the job posting before applying.

Q Will FDEs become unnecessary as AI agents spread?

Routine integration work will be made more efficient by AI, but the work of deciding which problems to solve and by what evaluation criteria, and the work of embedding solutions in the field, are expected to remain. a16z argues that AI-driven efficiency is a strength of the FDE model, allowing fewer people to reach more customers. On the other hand, Gartner points out the risk that business knowledge embedded in code will become obsolete as LLMs advance, requiring FDEs to leave behind something customers can operate on their own, rather than build and leave.
