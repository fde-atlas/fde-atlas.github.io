Forward deployed engineer vs sales engineer

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 27 min read

Summary

This guide separates sales engineers from forward deployed engineers using real job postings. The core test: after the contract is signed, does the role own the deal or the running system? Sales engineers win the technical decision and hand off delivery; FDEs write production code, sometimes inside customer environments. The line blurs since some FDEs do pre-sales work. Bloomberry's November 2025 analysis of 1,000 FDE postings found none quota-carrying. Titles mislead in both directions—Modal's Stockholm FDE role is really sales, while Ramp's AI Solutions Engineer is explicitly quota-carrying. OpenAI's Colin Jarvis describes FDE work as depth-focused, limited to about 10 engagements at a time.

  • Bloomberry's analysis of 1,000 FDE postings found none with a sales quota.

You have an posting open in one tab and a posting in the other, and they use the same words: discovery, demos, proofs of concept, “work closely with customers”. Is this your next job, or sales in disguise?

The definitions

What a sales engineer is

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics describes sales engineers as selling products that need technical expertise, with duties that include presentations to prospective customers, arranging installation and helping customers troubleshoot. Source 3Sales Engineers - Occupational Outlook HandbookPublisherU.S. Bureau of Labor StatisticsSource typeUS government data The government definition reaches past the signature. The postings below put most of the weight before it.

Twilio’s Principal Presales Engineer posting calls the role “a critical part of the sales team” and has it partner with account executives on qualification, demonstrations, proofs of concept, RFPs and technical presentations; it also says the role is eligible to earn commissions. In the postings on this page, sales engineering also goes by presales engineer, customer engineer and solutions engineer. The sales engineer glossary entry has the short version.

The same request, two jobs

Here is how the difference plays out on one request. A prospect, a regional insurer, asks: “Can your platform read our claim notes and flag the ones a human should review?” Follow each person through the week, and listen to what each one says.

The sales engineer’s week. You qualify the need with the account executive, run a demo on sample notes, answer the security questionnaire, and build a proof of concept on an anonymized extract. What you produce is a one-page note of success criteria for the proof of concept: what the insurer will test, on which notes, and what counts as a pass. The demo goes well on typed notes and wobbles on the scanned ones, so you tell the account executive:

“The demo works on typed notes. I’d put typed notes only in the success criteria, so we win on something we can prove.”

That is good sales engineering. After signature, you hand the delivery plan to the implementation team, or stay on as the account’s technical lead, and the build is someone else’s.

The FDE’s week. You start with questions: where do the notes live, who can grant access, and what does “a human should review” mean to the claims lead? Then you work in this order.

  • Define correct first. You sit with the claims lead and label a small sample of real notes together, flag or no flag, with the reason. That sample is the definition of right, and it is the claims lead’s, not yours.
  • Build on real data. You get into their document system, build the export, and run the flagging on real notes, not the anonymized extract.
  • Measure before anyone promises. You score the output against the labeled sample before anyone quotes an accuracy to the customer. In an interview, this is the step that shows you know the job: you do not promise a model’s quality, you measure it.
  • Find the break. Scanned handwritten notes fail.

The same day, you go to the account team:

“Typed notes flag well on the sample the claims lead labeled with me; scanned handwritten notes don’t. I suggest version one covers typed notes, and we say so in writing before anyone promises the rest.”

What you produce is a scope note, and it can be this short:

Claim-notes flagging: version 1 scope
In:        typed adjuster notes, one claims team
Out:       scanned handwritten notes (failed on the labeled sample)
Correct:   matches the claims lead's labels on the sample set
Measured:  weekly re-check against newly labeled notes
Owner:     the FDE, on call through launch
Open:      who labels new notes after launch?

Then you ship that first version for one claims team, and you stay on call for it.

Both did discovery. Both built something before the contract. Both even spotted the same weakness. The sales engineer steered around it to win the deal; the FDE measured it, wrote it down and shipped around it. Only one of them answers for what runs afterward. That is the test to carry into every posting: what does this role own after the signature, the deal or the running system?

The handoff model

A picture you will meet on recruiting sites is a relay: the sales engineer runs the race to the signature, then passes the baton to the FDE.

The recruiting platform Paraform describes it that way, citing no source: the solutions engineer enters alongside the sales rep and builds proof-of-concept deployments, and the FDE picks up where the SE left off to write production-grade code in the customer’s environment. Source 21Forward-Deployed Engineer vs. Solutions Engineer vs. Customer EngineerPublisherParaformSource typerecruiting platform

Where the relay model holds

Some employers do separate the two stages. Google’s customer engineer, above, hands the delivery plan to implementation teams. Colin Jarvis, who leads forward deployed engineering at OpenAI, Source 22The Future of Forward Deployed Engineering | OpenAI, Ramp, Nominal, DatalandPublisherSouth Park Commons (YouTube)Source typerecorded talk or interview said on a South Park Commons panel in May 2026 that the FDE unit is a business unit separate from two others: solutions engineering, which he described as pre-sales for the scale motion, meaning the wider market, and solutions architecture, the post-sales side of that same motion. He said the FDE unit works only about 10 engagements at a time and has to say no more often than yes. Source 22The Future of Forward Deployed Engineering | OpenAI, Ramp, Nominal, DatalandPublisherSouth Park Commons (YouTube)Source typerecorded talk or interview

That is the sharpest line we know of between the two jobs. In our reading of what Jarvis described, sales engineering is built for breadth, and FDE work for depth.

Where it breaks

The postings in the previous section break the relay: an FDE who scopes before the sale and owns on-call after it is running both legs. Even at OpenAI, where Jarvis described solutions engineering as the pre-sales team for the wider market, the FDE role is not post-sale only: the financial-services FDE posting above starts discovery in pre-sales. LangChain’s Deployed Engineering team describes working with customers “from pre-sales evaluations to post-deployment advisory work”. Source 23Deployed Engineer (UK) @ LangChainPublisherLangChain (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job posting Deloitte’s ConvergeHEALTH FDE posting describes a role that works with account and product teams pre-sale and with client teams post-sale. Source 24ConvergeHEALTH - Forward Deployed Engineer, Data Studio - Innovation_Delivery_TransformationPublisherDeloitte USSource typecompany job posting

So there are three shapes you will meet. The names are ours:

  • The relay. A sales engineer wins the deal, and an FDE or an implementation team builds it. Your interview stories should show how you pick up someone else’s promises.
  • End to end. The FDE scopes before the sale and owns the system after it. Your stories should show scoping a first version under sales pressure without overpromising, and then shipping it.
  • Sales engineering under an FDE title. The Modal Stockholm posting above is one. Prepare as a sales engineer would, and ask about the coding bar anyway.

Handoff stories in the interview

Handoffs are where promises break, and our question bank has a behavioral question about exactly that: when sales promised what you cannot deliver. A sales engineer can answer it from the other side of the table, and that is a strength if you tell it well.

A weak answer blames sales. A strong one names the promise, what you checked, the trade you offered, how it came out and what you changed so it would not happen again. Here are words that work:

“The account executive had told the customer we’d support their on-prem document store in the first release. I checked with the customer’s IT lead and found we’d need firewall changes that took them weeks to approve. I went to the AE the same day, and we went back to the customer together with two options: launch on their cloud copy first, or wait. They chose the cloud copy, and it went live on the date we’d promised; on-prem followed the next quarter once IT approved the firewall change. After that, I asked to join the last technical call before any contract with an integration in it, and we didn’t have that kind of surprise again on my accounts.”

Compare shares, not base ranges

At Google, the customer engineer’s pay splits 70/30 between base and bonus target; the FDE’s splits about 83/17. Here is the arithmetic. The Cloud customer engineer posting states US pay as a base range plus a 42.86% bonus target plus equity, and does not use the word “quota”. Source 15Customer Engineer, Platform, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting The FDE IV posting states a base range plus a 20% bonus target plus equity. Source 7Forward Deployed Engineer IV, GenAI, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting

A 42.86% target on top of base is a 70/30 split of base to variable, the same mix Elastic states. Source 15Customer Engineer, Platform, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job postingSource 25Partner Solutions ArchitectPublisherElastic (Greenhouse)Source typecompany website A 20% target is about 83/17. Source 7Forward Deployed Engineer IV, GenAI, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting The FDE posting is a level IV role that asks for 8 years of software development, so do not compare its base range with the customer engineer’s; compare the shares. The rule works on any posting that states a bonus target: divide the target by one plus the target, and you have the variable share of on-target pay.

A bonus for company results and a variable tied to your bookings are different kinds of pay, and a bonus target alone does not tell you which one you have. Our post on Google Cloud customer engineer vs FDE sets the two postings side by side.

Three questions to ask about any variable pay

When an engineering title comes with OTE, or a pay line that could mean either, ask these on the recruiter call, in these words:

  • “Is the range base or OTE, and what is the split?”
  • “What is the variable part measured on: new bookings, renewals, delivery milestones or company results?”
  • “Who sets the target, and how much of it did the team earn last year?”

An answer tied to bookings means you will be measured like a salesperson, whatever the title says. An answer tied to delivery or adoption means the OTE is closer to a bonus. Our post on why some FDE jobs pay OTE shows how to compare an OTE offer with a base-plus-bonus one. Practice saying your own expectation out loud with the compensation expectations question. Quota: do forward deployed engineers carry one?

Short answer: not in the one published count of FDE postings we know of. How to find out before the onsite

Read the posting in this order, then ask.

Is there a quota behind this FDE title?

  • Pay line: base, OTE, “salary plus bonus target”, or boilerplate that covers both?
  • Department field: engineering, an FDE unit, or Sales and go-to-market?
  • Verbs: “ship”, “own production”, “on-call”, or “technical win”, “close”, “deal strategy”?
  • Who you work with: engineering and product, or account executives?
  • Recruiter question: “Is any part of this role’s pay tied to bookings or a revenue target?”

If the pay line says OTE, the department says Sales and the verbs say “win”, you are looking at sales engineering with an FDE title. That can be a good job. Just prepare for it as the job it is.

Which job fits you

Choose by what you want to be measured on. Neither answer is better; they are different jobs.

You will probably prefer sales engineering if you like the moment a room decides, you want your pay to rise with the deals you help win, and you would rather move to the next customer than own one system for months.

You will probably prefer FDE work if your favorite part of a proof of concept is when the customer’s real data breaks it, you want to be the one paged when it fails, and you care whether people still use the thing next quarter.

A useful test: think of your last proof of concept. Do you know whether it is running today? If you do not know and do not mind, that is a sales engineer’s instinct. If it bothers you, read on.

One request, two endings

Take the insurer again, in the room.

In a sales engineering presentation, you end with a recommendation and a pitch:

“From what you’ve told me, I’d start with a proof of concept on your typed claim notes, scored against success criteria we agree today. It shows your claims team a flagged queue on the platform you’d grow into, before you commit to anything.”

In an FDE customer round, you end with a cut first version and the question you still need answered:

“Before I promise anything, can we pull a sample of real notes, including the scanned ones, and agree with your claims lead what a correct flag looks like? If the typed notes hold up on that sample, version one covers them for one claims team, and we decide on the handwritten ones once we’ve measured them.”

Both answers are good. They are answers to different jobs. If you are coming from sales engineering, the habit to break is ending on the pitch. Practice the other ending in the free practice case.

Moving from sales engineer to FDE

Some FDE postings ask for exactly your background. Modal’s Stockholm FDE posting, above, asks for experience in solutions engineering, sales engineering or customer-facing technical roles. LangChain’s Deployed Engineer posting lists 3+ years in a relevant technical role and names customer engineering and solutions engineering among them. Source 18Deployed Engineer (Bay Area) @ LangChainPublisherLangChain (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job posting

You will be asked some version of “why FDE, not sales engineering?” Here is a shape that works, in words you can adapt:

“In my sales engineering work, the part I was best at came after the demo: getting into the customer’s data and finding what would break. At signature I handed that to someone else, and I often never learned whether it worked. I want to own that part: build it in their environment, ship it, and be the one who gets paged. I’ll bring the discovery and demo skills with me. What I’m adding is production ownership: last quarter I built [the thing], which [who] still runs [how often].”

Fill the brackets with something real. If you cannot, build it before you apply: a small tool a customer or a colleague still uses is worth more in this answer than any demo. What is the difference between a forward deployed engineer and a sales engineer?

Twilio’s Principal Presales Engineer posting puts the role in the sales team, running demonstrations and proofs of concept with account executives, and makes it eligible for commissions. Because the employer ties part of the pay to a target.
