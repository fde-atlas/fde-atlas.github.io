What Is the FDE Model? How Palantir's "Go into the Field" Business Model Works

Original title: FDEモデルとは｜パランティアが始めた「現場に入る」事業モデルの仕組み

FDE Consulting · 2026-09-29 · 7 min read

Summary

This article explains the FDE model as a business model, not just a job title. Originating at Palantir, it sends engineers into customer sites to solve problems and feeds those solutions back into the product as reusable assets—distinct from both SaaS and traditional contracting. Since 2025, AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic, plus Japanese firms such as Fujitsu and Accenture, have adopted it, driven by AI agents that need ongoing tuning. a16z calls this services-led growth, citing ServiceNow and Workday's margin improvements, but warns of low margins, scaling limits, and dependence on individual talent.

Key points

  • The FDE model, pioneered by Palantir, turns on-site work into reusable product assets.
  • Unlike contracting, customizations must be generalized into the product or it becomes a body-shop business.
  • AI agents require continuous adjustment, spreading services-led growth per a16z.
  • ServiceNow and Workday raised gross margins from 54–63% at IPO to 75–79%.
  • Weaknesses include quality depending on individuals, accumulating custom work, and scaling limits.

The FDE model is a business model in which a software company sends its own engineers to customer sites, solves problems specific to each customer, and feeds the insights and features gained there back into its own products. It originated at Palantir Technologies, and since 2025 it has spread to AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic, as well as to major Japanese IT vendors and consulting firms.

The FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) role itself was covered in "What is an FDE? The meaning and job of a Forward Deployed Engineer". Here, we treat FDE not as a job title but as "a way of building a business".

The FDE model is different from both SaaS and contracted development

Ways of selling software can be broadly divided into three.

SaaS（セルフサーブ型） 受託開発・コンサル FDEモデル
What is soldRight to use the productPeople's time and deliverablesThe product plus the work of making it usable on-site
Who enters the customer's siteBasically no oneDevelopers and consultantsEngineers from the company that builds the product
Where customer-specific refinements remainNowhere (customers configure it themselves)In that customer's deliverablesIn the product (usable by the next customer too)
Core of revenueMonthly or annual usage feesPerson-months and project ratesProduct usage fees (implementation labor is an upfront investment)

The bottom row and the row above it are the essence. Contracted development also involves going into customer sites. The difference lies in where what is built on-site accumulates. In contracted development, the deliverables stay with that customer, and the next customer starts from scratch. In the FDE model, what is built on-site is generalized and incorporated into the product, so the next customer can use it from day one. Human work is converted into an asset: the product.

Why Palantir created the FDE

Palantir's engineers fall broadly into two roles: "Dev" and "Delta". According to the company's official blog, Dev delivers "one feature to many customers", while Delta delivers "many features to one customer" (Palantir Blog). Delta's official job title is Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE), in other words, the FDE.

According to reporting by The Pragmatic Engineer, this role emerged in the early 2010s, and until around 2016 the number of FDEs within the company reportedly exceeded that of regular software engineers (The Pragmatic Engineer). The same article also presents the view that the difficulty of getting things done at large enterprises lies not in technology but in organizational barriers. Inside giant organizations, you have to find the data owners, obtain permissions, and move stakeholders to connect systems. The FDE began when the company's own engineers took on that work instead of leaving it to customers.

Palantir has another role called the Deployment Strategist, known as "Echo" (Palantir Blog). Echo handles a product-management-oriented role, while Delta handles a technology-oriented one. The prototype of the FDE model is this pairing of business-side and technology-side people entering the field together.

How the FDE model works: turning field work back into the product

The value of the FDE model lies in the following cycle.

  • FDEs enter customer sites and solve concrete business problems with the product
  • If the product lacks a needed feature, the FDEs build it themselves and also modify the product core
  • Of what was built on-site, the parts useful to other customers are generalized and incorporated into the product
  • For the next customer, it is available from the start. Implementation gets faster, and FDEs can spend time on harder problems

The Pragmatic Engineer reports that when Palantir's FDEs hit the limits of the product, they configure new data models, improve product stability, or commit fixes to the product core. The same article explains the difference from consultants as "working with customers over the long term and contributing to the company's own products, rather than delivering one-off recommendations".

Whether this cycle is actually turning is what separates the FDE model from contracted development that merely calls itself FDE. If on-site refinements never flow back into the product and simply pile up as customer-specific customization, it is no different from a person-month business.

Why the FDE model is spreading now

AI agents never become "set it and forget it"

Generative AI and AI agents produce no results unless they are tuned to each customer's business processes, data, and exception handling. Moreover, they return slightly different answers to the same input each time, so evaluation and improvement continue even after deployment. The territory where "customers configure it themselves after signing up", as with self-serve SaaS, does not work has expanded dramatically.

In June 2025, the US venture capital firm a16z called this situation "services-led growth" and argued that AI startups should place FDEs to go deep into customers' operations (a16z). It uses the analogy that companies buying AI are like people holding a smartphone for the first time: they want to use it, but need help with setup.

Accepting lower margins to build a moat

Naturally, gross margins fall when you invest manpower. Still, as to why companies choose the FDE model, a16z cites examples of established major SaaS companies. ServiceNow's gross margin was 63.2% at IPO but rose to 79% by 2024, and Workday's rose from 54.1% at IPO to 75%. The claim is that if you secure the entry point to customers' operations and data, the initially low margins can be recovered later.

The AI builders themselves have started building FDE companies

OpenAI launched a two-person FDE team in early 2025, and by August of that year reportedly had a team of more than 10 people across 8 cities including Tokyo (The Pragmatic Engineer). In May 2026 it established OpenAI Deployment Company, and with the acquisition of Tomoro it gained roughly 150 FDEs and Deployment Specialists (OpenAI). Anthropic also announced in the same May the establishment of an enterprise AI services company, which launched in July as "Ode with Anthropic" (Anthropic).

In Japan as well, Fujitsu became a Palantir Global FDE Partner in September 2026 (PR TIMES), and in March 2026 Accenture announced it would launch an FDE specialist organization (practice) in partnership with Microsoft, on a scale of several thousand people (Accenture). Nikkei Cross Tech covered Fujitsu's FDE with the headline "Business divisions over IT departments, usage fees over man-months". SIers who have earned revenue through man-month billing are trying to change the very shape of their revenue with the FDE model.

Weaknesses of the FDE model

a16z also clearly describes the pitfalls of this approach: it is harder to scale because it relies on people; low gross margins can be a sign that "the product is not differentiated"; you must hire people who can act on their own initiative; and how you select customers, how you design incentives, and whether you automate deployment work will determine success or failure.

Adding a perspective from someone who supports teams in the field, I believe the following three points are particularly impactful.

  • Quality depends on the person. Even with the same product, outcomes vary greatly depending on which FDE is assigned
  • The deeper you go into a customer, the more customer-specific handling increases, and things that cannot be fed back into the product pile up
  • If you don't define an exit, you can never leave the field. You need to design for customer self-sufficiency from the very beginning

The FDE model is not a magic solution that works just by putting people in the field. It only works when there is the discipline to continuously turn field work into reusable patterns and feed them back.

What Japanese companies can learn from the FDE model

In Japan, "FDE" is starting to be used both as a job role at companies with AI products and as a new form of support offered by SIers and consulting firms. What should be adopted from the FDE model differs depending on your position.

PositionWhat to adopt
Startups with AI productsHave FDEs handle deployment and create a cycle that feeds field insights back into the product. LayerX and Loglass establishing FDE roles follows this model
SIers and consulting firmsMeasure value by outcomes or usage fees rather than man-months. Accumulate field knowledge internally as reusable patterns
Enterprises adopting AIDon't leave everything to external FDEs; also develop people internally who can take on the same role. Aim for a state where the company can eventually run it on its own

We at FDE Consulting are not in the business of selling our own products; we are a support firm that starts from the customer's challenges. Even so, we have incorporated the core discipline of the FDE model—"solve in the field, turn it into a pattern, and let go"—into how we conduct our support.
