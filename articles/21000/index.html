How to Find an FDE Role: A Job-Seeking Guide from Spotting Real vs. Fake JDs to Delivering Results

Original title: FDE 岗位怎么找：从辨别真假 JD 到拿结果的求职指南

FDECareer.work · 2026-09-21 · 17 min read

Summary

This FDECareer guide argues that job titles like FDE or AI delivery engineer mask huge differences in actual responsibilities, so candidates should first identify which segment they want: on-site customer delivery, Agent platform engineering, pre-sales solutions, or product/research work. It teaches readers to spot fake FDE roles by examining core verbs in job descriptions, warning against low-depth "API glue" jobs and pre-sales positions dressed up as engineering. The guide also covers verifying outsourcing contract entities and on-site restrictions, dissecting inflated salary packages, rewriting resumes to highlight high-availability delivery skills, preparing verifiable demos with real troubleshooting cases, and a 90-day plan for surviving probation by securing measurable client results and payment sign-off.

Key points

  • Four main AI delivery role types differ sharply in skills, metrics, and travel burden.
  • Judge real FDE roles by JD verbs, not titles; avoid API-glue and pure pre-sales jobs.
  • Verify contract entity, classified-site constraints, and salary structure before accepting offers.
  • Use a three-stage enterprise AI adoption model to answer interviews with buyer perspective.
  • Bring sanitized demos and fault-recovery cases; spend the first 90 days securing signed results.

Amid the wave of large language models, job postings titled FDE, AI Delivery, or Agent Engineer are proliferating, yet the day-to-day work varies enormously. As we found in the structural divergence documented in the FDE hiring structure map of leading overseas AI companies and the 2026 Panorama Report on AI Delivery Supply and Demand in Chinese Enterprises, the most important step before applying is not memorizing Transformer papers, but figuring out exactly which part of the work you want to own: going on-site to help clients clarify their business and hand-build prototypes, integrating Agent systems into enterprise intranet production environments and ensuring high availability, or taking long-term responsibility for private deployment operations and incident response?

Match yourself against these options, then read the specific JD (if you lack a reproducible project entry point, start by picking a scenario from the FDE Delivery Hands-On Collection to practice). Many roles carry impressive Agent titles but grant neither code access nor production accountability; others have plain-sounding titles but involve end-to-end core delivery. This site vets positions rigorously: only roles whose official responsibilities explicitly include client delivery and system building are classified as precise FDE roles; team or department names alone are not sufficient for classification.

Choosing the Right Direction: Real Responsibilities and Entry Requirements of Four Mainstream AI Delivery Roles (Don't Be Misled by the Same Title)

Even when both say "AI Delivery / FDE," different companies' actual requirements are worlds apart. Blind applications often lead to "strong technical skills but failing interviews, or discovering after onboarding that the job is all business trips and grunt work." Based on hundreds of verified job samples across the web, mainstream AI delivery roles fall into four camps:

  • Client On-Site Delivery (e.g., ZD Consulting, Kognic, traditional system integrators): centered on being stationed on-site to solve client problems. The work includes real business clarification, intranet API integration, private deployment in classified environments, data isolation, and end-user training. The core evaluation metric is not how many lines of code you write, but whether the project passes the client's legal and security acceptance and gets paid on schedule;
  • Agent Platform Engineering (e.g., Meituan, Didi, major internet companies, and leading AI platforms): centered on platform infrastructure and runtime high availability. Mainly responsible for building Agent Harness frameworks, automatic recovery from task interruptions, role-level permission auditing, and cost control. Suitable for engineers with solid high-concurrency, distributed backend backgrounds;
  • Pre-Sales Solutions and Technical Support (e.g., Feishu ecosystem partners, EJ Tech, cloud vendor ecosystems): centered on closing deals and proofs of concept (POCs). Mainly responsible for writing technical bid documents, researching client pain points, delivering standard Demo presentations, and writing out-of-the-box automated deployment scripts;
  • Product and Frontier Research (e.g., SenseTime, frontier LLM labs): centered on model fine-tuning optimization, frontier exploration of Agent Harness architecture, and scientific evaluation of business metrics. Requires solid command of the Transformer architecture, eval set construction, and fine-tuning training.

The Business Cognition Foundation: Three Stages of Enterprise AI Adoption and the Art of Answering Interviews from a Higher Vantage Point

When many engineers transition to FDE or AI delivery roles, their biggest mistake is talking entirely about technical details (how to call LangChain, how to write prompts, how large RAG vector chunks should be), which in the eyes of interviewers or business leads is only worth an "entry-level outsourcing" price tag.

The core competitive advantage of a truly high-value FDE lies in having a "buyer's perspective"—deeply understanding which stage of digital and intelligent transformation the client is in, and helping the enterprise avoid fatal detours:

The three fatal misconceptions enterprises have when embracing AI today:

  • Misconception 1: Treating LLMs as "chat toys" without serious business scenario expectations. Many non-technical executives believe that installing a chat box will transform the business, ignoring the fact that industrial-grade business workflows require 99.9% determinism and transactional consistency;
  • Misconception 2: Blindly building private LLMs and heavy-asset compute infrastructure, falling into a return-on-investment disaster. Many traditional government and enterprise organizations blindly purchase tens of millions of RMB worth of GPU compute clusters without a clear business ROI, only for the models to sit idle and become vanity projects showcasing political achievements;
  • Misconception 3: Training everyone to write prompts while actual business processes remain unchanged with no accumulated knowledge. Scattered, ad-hoc prompt techniques cannot become organizational assets; once employees leave, all experience resets to zero.

The "Three-Stage Evolution Model" of enterprise AI adoption:

  • Phase 1: Personal productivity tools (single-point efficiency gains, scattered individual efforts). Employees each use ChatGPT/Kimi to polish copy and write emails, with no impact on core business workflows;
  • Phase 2: Pilots in local core business workflows (process restructuring, immediate results). Select high-frequency business areas with clear ROI (such as intelligent after-sales ticket routing, contract review, credit pre-screening), and introduce an agent platform with permission gateways and state machine recovery, doubling human efficiency;
  • Phase 3: Organization-level agent networks and business closed loops (capability accumulation, commercial closed loop). Cross-departmental multi-agent collaboration networks break down the enterprise's legacy ERP and CRM data silos, enabling autonomous decision-making and end-to-end delivery.

Interview "dimension-reduction strike" talking points: When the interviewer asks, "If you were asked to launch agents for a traditional client, what would you do in your first week?", never answer directly with "first pick an open-source model and fine-tune it." The high-scoring answer is: "First figure out which stage of the three-stage evolution the client is at. If it's a Phase 2 pilot, never blindly push the client to build their own heavy-compute infrastructure; instead, prioritize using mature general-purpose models plus deterministic business rules to build an MVP, locking in quantifiable payback metrics within 30 days." This single sentence demonstrates the business acumen of a technical decision-maker.

Distinguishing real from fake FDE / AI delivery roles: Beware of "API stitchers" and "pre-sales packaging"

Today's job market is a mixed bag. Many positions labeled "AI Architect / FDE" turn out, upon closer inspection, to be "interface stitchers" who just write prompts and call external cloud APIs—with no enterprise intranet permission design, no hallucination or timeout fallback mechanisms—making it extremely hard to gain technical recognition when job-hopping later; others are pure pre-sales demos or solution sales dressed up in technical clothing.

How to tell the difference? Don't look at the title; go straight for the core verbs in the JD: if the responsibilities center on "making PPTs, supporting sales demos, driving customer renewals," that's pre-sales or a solutions consultant; if the responsibilities are "writing Python/Go, integrating internal vector databases, building harness runtime environments, automatic task retry and state recovery on exceptions, Docker/K8s deployment in private environments," that's a hardcore engineering-delivery FDE.

A pitfall-avoidance guide for outsourcing, headhunter packaging, and third-party intermediaries

When China's leading tech giants (such as Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, Meituan, Ant, etc.) push AI adoption, many non-core-line or on-site embedded positions are posted through outsourcing or third-party staffing companies.

When applying and communicating, you must verify the actual contracting entity first: outsourced roles usually have no stock options, minimal year-end bonuses, no access to core business code, and at some client sites even badges and meal allowances are restricted; also, you must clarify the costs of travel and on-site embedding: many delivery roles require being stationed long-term at banks or government/enterprise client sites. Many confidential intranet server rooms prohibit phones and external internet access for looking up errors, requiring extremely strong troubleshooting under pressure. If you cannot accept long-term closed-site embedding or frequent travel, avoid such requirements early.

See through the real padding in salary structures: Don't be fooled by total annual packages

The "30-50K · 16 months' salary" or million-yuan annual packages commonly seen on Chinese job apps often hide significant padding.

Break the salary down:

  • Hard base salary: the unconditional monthly base pay deposited into your account (usually calculated over 12 months); this is the foundation for job-hopping and daily expenses;
  • Variable year-end bonus: the extra 2-4 months or more is often variable performance pay tied to the company's overall revenue and your department's KPIs, and can drop to zero in a bad market;
  • Options and stock: put a question mark on startup options—before an IPO, exercise costs are high and liquidity is poor; for public-company stock, look at the vesting schedule and buyback terms.

For delivery engineers, focus on the monthly base and subsidies explicitly written in black and white; don't pay for verbally promised pie-in-the-sky total packages.

Don't just write "proficient in large models" — prepare verifiable, presentable Demos

Technical interviewers review hundreds of resumes daily and are most put off by empty claims like "skilled in LangChain, proficient in RAG, familiar with Transformer."

What truly impresses delivery team leads is the real engineering dirty work you have solved:

  • Interface exceptions and long-task recovery: When external model responses time out, the network jitters, or crashes entirely, does your Agent simply error out and freeze, or can it resume from checkpoints, save task state, and degrade gracefully?
  • Data and permission isolation: When different employees or customers query the same bot, can your system authenticate by role and prevent leaking confidential financial reports to ordinary employees?
  • Cost and eval constraints: How many Tokens does a long conversation consume? How do you reduce call costs through context compression and caching?

Prepare a real Demo with anonymized code or anonymized run logs (or an offline rehearsal package) — this is far more effective at landing an Offer than ten lines of "proficient in."

  • Prepare a one-page project brief: clearly explain the customer's real pain points, your system architecture design, and the most critical constraints.
  • Explain 2 key engineering trade-offs: for example, why you chose a local small model for initial screening instead of calling expensive commercial closed-source large models for everything.
  • Show a real troubleshooting case: include reproduction steps, error logs, and how you got it to production through a degradation strategy.

Key points and pitfall-avoidance guide for passing all three interview rounds at major companies and startups

Due to its special hybrid nature, the FDE role's assessment process differs completely from ordinary pure R&D positions. Technical interviewers assess both the depth of your low-level code and system design, and your adaptability and troubleshooting abilities in real customer environments:

Round 1: Low-level engineering and technical deep dive (code-level implementation)

  • Must-know point 1: State checkpoint persistence. Interviewers often ask: "When an Agent executing a long task times out while calling the 4th tool, how does your system ensure already-deducted inventory isn't lost and retries don't double-deduct?"

Winning answer: Don't vaguely say "automatic retry." Instead, explicitly state that you use "Idempotent Keys + local transaction receipts + Redis state machine checkpoints," querying the receipt before retrying and only triggering degradation after confirming non-submission.

  • Must-know point 2: Token budget and concurrency rate limiting. Combined with gateway token bucket algorithms and single-step execution timeout circuit breakers, explain how to prevent Prompt recursive infinite loops from blowing through the budget.

Round 2: System architecture and on-site private deployment (system-level design)

  • Must-know point 1: Offline deployment in confidential server rooms without internet access. Interviewers examine how you prepare pre-built image packages, offline driver dependencies, and smoke test scripts in a physically air-gapped environment where pip, npm, and Docker Hub are inaccessible;
  • Must-know point 2: Multi-model degradation fallback strategy. How to design primary-backup dual routing and seamlessly fall back to lightweight quantized small models when encountering cloud provider 429 rate limits.

Round 3 and live whiteboard: On-site fault diagnosis and customer communication (practical adaptability)

  • Live whiteboard troubleshooting: Given a log of an Agent stuck in an infinite loop, identify the root cause on the spot (e.g., a tool returning unstructured error strings causing the model to retry repeatedly), and write defensive patch code;
  • Business director/HR round: Assess your flexibility for on-site business travel, communication skills for handling customer legal and security deflection, and confirm you have the closed-loop sense of responsibility to push projects through to final acceptance and payment collection.

Survival playbook for the first 90 days on-site during probation

Getting an Offer is only the first step of a long journey. In companies focused on delivery results and business returns, an FDE's probation survival line is entirely determined by "whether you can achieve definitive results at the customer site." Remember the survival rules for the first 90 days:

  • Days 0–30: Listen more, talk less, and map out the topology and permission boundaries. Never rush to tear down and rebuild right after arriving at the customer site. The first step is to understand the customer's existing network topology, approval flows, and confidentiality red lines, compile a clear "Data Dependency and Interface Permission Checklist," and confirm each item with the customer's key IT contacts;
  • Days 30–60: Single-point breakthrough, tackle the first business flow with quantifiable ROI. Don't try to solve everything at once. Pick one pain point that the business owner finds most troublesome but has relatively low resistance to change (such as automated extraction and validation of a common type of ticket), run real data through an MVP with checkpoint recovery, arrange an on-site demo with the business stakeholder's top leader, and obtain signed approval;
  • Days 60–90: Engineering consolidation, deliver the "On-site Operations Handover Manual" and observability dashboards. Write foolproof offline operations documentation, configure full-chain Trace attribution panels, conduct two rounds of live-machine drills with the customer's internal maintenance staff, ensure the system runs stably without you, and smoothly drive final acceptance, payment collection, and your conversion to full-time.

Application and Communication Tips: Find Business Managers Directly on BOSS and Liepin

Large model technical delivery is an emerging role, and many companies' HR departments have no idea what kind of person to hire for it. They often mechanically filter by educational background or big-name company prestige, causing huge numbers of transitioning engineers to be filtered out by machines at the initial resume screening stage.

Efficient application tips:

  • Prioritize direct chats with technical and business leads: On BOSS Zhipin, filter for "direct hiring teams" and prioritize reaching out to accounts with titles like Technical Director, Architect, or Delivery Manager. Open with three points: your engineering foundation skills, similar delivery projects you've completed (with live URLs or GitHub/Demo links), and what business problems you can help them solve;
  • Focus deeply on 3~5 companies per week: Don't blindly mass-send your resume like flyers. For each company, tailor your portfolio presentation to their industry (e.g., healthcare, finance, government/enterprise, e-commerce) and target them precisely.

Frequently Asked Questions

I've never held an FDE title—can I transition from pure backend or full-stack?

Absolutely. Most FDEs in China transitioned from backend (Python/Go/Java), full-stack, or Solutions Engineer roles. What matters is whether you have engineering delivery capabilities in system integration, API integration testing, and getting business workflows running on-site.

The job posting doesn't list a salary range—should I still apply?

It depends on your job search bandwidth. If the responsibilities align closely with your engineering direction, you can reach out first to learn more; during the conversation, ask directly about compensation structure in the first round to avoid wasting interview time.

Will FDECareer submit applications on my behalf or upload my resume for me?

No. This site is a clean open-source and verified information platform. All applications go directly to the recruiter's original page or the company's official entry point. We never store or sell your personal resume data.
