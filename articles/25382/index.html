Learning FDE Implementation Skills from the GMO Unauthorized Access Incident and White Hat Hackers' Findings

Original title: GMO不正アクセスとホワイトハッカーの指摘から学ぶFDEの実装力

FDE総研 · 2026-10-26 · 20 min read

Summary

This article analyzes the GMO incident in which unauthorized programs were planted on servers of its FX auto-trading VPS service, with about 26,739 servers potentially affected and unauthorized trades reported in users' accounts. GMO refused the attacker's ransom demand, offered a flat 5,000 yen condolence payment plus separate loss compensation, and asked the government to apply Japan's active cyber defense framework. Using CEO Masatoshi Kumagai's admission that in-house white hackers had flagged issues but fixes lacked depth and speed, the author examines the gap between detection and implementation, compares division-of-labor versus FDE-style organizations across five stages, and draws lessons for engineers and companies on fixing systems without downtime.

Key points

  • GMO's FX auto-trading VPS was compromised; about 26,739 servers were potentially affected.
  • GMO refused ransom, paid 5,000 yen per user, and set up separate loss compensation.
  • Kumagai said internal white hackers had flagged issues, but fixes lacked depth and speed.
  • The article maps the detection-to-implementation gap across five stages, contrasting FDE-style work.
  • Key lesson: design no-downtime migration paths and carry findings through to implementation.

What happened in the GMO unauthorized access incident?

This is an incident in which a third party installed unauthorized programs on GMO's FX automated-trading dedicated VPS, and unauthorized trades were reported on users' FX accounts.

This article is based on reporting and public disclosures as of October 26, 2026. Because the incident is ongoing, figures and circumstances may change going forward. Speculation and assessments are written separately from facts. This article is an analysis based on reporting, public materials, and general practical knowledge. It is not based on any experience of FDE Soken or its operating company being involved in the response to this incident.

The target was the "FX Automated Trading Dedicated VPS" of "Onamae.com Desktop Cloud." It is a service specialized for FX (foreign exchange margin trading) automated trading, where users remotely operate a Windows environment on the cloud to run automated trading tools. Trading account login information and similar data stored on the servers may have been obtained and used maliciously.

GMO Internet Group's representative Masatoshi Kumagai explained the background in a blog post on October 25, 2026. According to his explanation, in-house white hackers had pointed out improvement issues in advance, and the field teams were working on countermeasures, but the depth and speed were insufficient. Using this explanation as a starting point, this article considers the gap between "detection/reporting" and "implementation/adoption." It then organizes the general insights drawn from the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) approach from FDE Soken's perspective.

What happened between October 19 and 25, 2026?

Unauthorized programs were confirmed on October 20, servers were shut down in the early hours of October 21, and the fourth report on October 24 acknowledged the external leakage of customer information.

How did GMO respond to the monetary demand and compensation?

GMO stated its policy of not complying with the monetary demand from the apparent attacker, and also announced a framework for loss compensation separate from the condolence payment.

According to GMO's 4th report (official), the threats included the following three items.

  • Continuing unauthorized manipulation of users' trading accounts at over 70 securities firms, FX companies, etc. in Japan and abroad (damaging account balances through unauthorized orders)
  • Unauthorized publication of information about the service and connected users' PCs, and execution of ransomware attacks
  • Unauthorized publication of information about GMO Internet's systems and operational structure

GMO's reason for not complying with the demand is that even if payment were made, there is no guarantee of deletion of the obtained information or prevention of its unauthorized use. GMO submitted evidence to police and other relevant authorities and requested an investigation. Users are being asked to change passwords for trading accounts and trading tools, and to terminate all active login sessions.

Why didn't the in-house white hacker's warnings lead to countermeasures in time?

According to Representative Kumagai's explanation, although the team proceeded with countermeasures after receiving the warnings, they lacked sufficient depth and speed as a result of prioritizing service continuity.

This is the core of this article. Note that this is not a case of "warnings being ignored." According to Representative Kumagai, the team was working on the issues after receiving the warnings, but they did not reach a level where they could act ahead of the attack.

What did Representative Kumagai explain in his blog?

He explained that the in-house white hacker had pointed out improvement issues to the team in advance, and the team had proceeded with countermeasures carefully in response.

Representative Kumagai's blog post of October 25, 2026 was introduced in GMO's official announcement, and was also reported by ASCII and ITmedia NEWS. The original text contains the following statements.

  • "Issues that needed improvement had been pointed out to the team in advance by an in-house white hacker"
  • "As a result of proceeding carefully while considering the impact on service availability and priorities relative to other matters to address"
  • "The depth and speed of the response were insufficient, and attackers exploited that gap"

Summarizing the key points, the sequence is as follows.

OrderContent of the explanation
1The in-house white hacker had pointed out security improvement issues in advance
2The team accepted the warnings and proceeded with countermeasures
3They proceeded carefully, considering service continuity (not stopping the service) and other priorities
4The depth and speed of the response were insufficient, and attackers exploited that opening

The specific content of the warnings has not been disclosed in the reports. Therefore, this article does not delve into internal details such as which countermeasures were at which stage.

Why do "not stopping" and "fixing" conflict?

Fixing a running service involves risks of downtime and changes, so the more you try to preserve continuity, the more cautious and slower countermeasures tend to become.

For services that are expected to run continuously, such as automated FX trading, stopping the service or changing its configuration itself affects users. There is a distance between "knowing it should be fixed" and "being able to stop and fix it now." This tension can arise anywhere in continuously running services.

Representative Kumagai's explanation puts this tension into words directly. Setting priorities is something every organization does routinely. What is questioned is whether such decisions are recorded in a form that allows later verification of whether they matched the speed of the attack.

What gap exists between "warnings" and "implementation and consolidation"?

According to FDE Soken's analysis, across the five stages from discovery to consolidation, the people in charge and the criteria for judgment change, and the gap arises when the context for decision-making is broken.

The stages from when an issue is found to when it becomes established can be organized as follows when applied to a typical organization. This is not about the internal affairs of a specific company, but a general comparison framework by FDE Soken.

StageMain people in charge (typical example)Reasons for stalling (general theory)
1. Warning/detectionSecurity staff, white hackersEven when issues are visible, it is hard to show their business impact in numbers
2. PrioritizationBusiness owners, product ownersTend to be postponed compared to matters affecting continuity or revenue
3. Design/implementationDevelopment and infrastructure teamsTend to be gradual and cautious to avoid impact on production
4. Migration/switchoverOperations teams, customer-facing staffUser notification and coordinating downtime take time
5. Consolidation/monitoringOperations teamsWhen responsibilities are divided, verification of effectiveness tends to be interrupted

When the people in charge change at each stage, even if each individual decision is reasonable, the whole process tends to take time. This five-stage table will be used as a framework later when comparing the division-of-labor model with the FDE model, and when considering the skills to develop for each current role.

What is the active cyber defense that GMO requested from the government?

Active cyber defense is a national framework under which the police and Self-Defense Forces identify and neutralize the sources of serious cyberattacks.

On October 24, 2026, GMO Internet Group urgently requested the government to apply this system. GMO positions this incident as the system's "first activation case," and is asking the government to take the lead in tracking, blocking, and neutralizing the attack infrastructure. The system's mechanism and companies' preventive measures are explained in What Is Active Cyber Defense? Companies' Preventive Measures and How to Use FDE.

What kind of system is the Active Cyber Defense Law?

A system under which related laws were enacted in May 2025, and from October 1, 2026, the police and Self-Defense Forces became able to carry out neutralization measures.

GMO's request (October 24) was made shortly after the start of neutralization measures (October 1, 2026).

What did GMO assert, and how did the government respond?

GMO asserted that the private sector alone cannot fully protect the nation's assets, and a senior government official acknowledged receiving the request and stated that the government would support the defense.

GMO asserts that the method used in this attack is unprecedentedly malicious. This is because it went beyond system failures of private services or encryption and data theft, targeting ordinary people's trading accounts (financial assets). On that basis, GMO states that private-sector defense and after-the-fact response alone cannot eradicate situations where the nation's assets are stolen.

What issues have been raised about this request?

It has been pointed out that the purpose of the system is to prevent damage and stop it from spreading, and that applying it after damage has occurred poses legal interpretation issues.

Reports such as Business+IT have pointed out the following points.

  • Applying it after damage has occurred involves legal interpretation issues
  • Publicizing the request could make attackers more cautious, reducing the effectiveness of harm-neutralizing measures
  • Whether a request is accepted depends on the government's judgment, and the outcome may not be disclosed

Masumi positions active cyber defense as something close to a "last line of defense" for cutting off attack sources. The view is that daily damage reduction is the responsibility of the basic measures taken by users and companies. Since whether the system applies and its outcome had not been decided as of October 26, 2026, this article does not make definitive claims.

How does the FDE approach bridge the gap between identifying issues and implementing them?

The FDE model is an approach in which the same person carries an issue through from discovery to implementation and adoption, minimizing waiting time between stages.

To state upfront, we cannot say that "having an FDE would have prevented this incident." Neither the details of the findings nor GMO's internal circumstances have been disclosed, so there is no basis for such a judgment. Here, we frame it as a general discussion of organizational building using the FDE Research Institute's five stages.

What kind of role is an FDE?

The FDE is a role known as the Palantir model: working at the customer's site, identifying issues, implementing them in working form, and accompanying the process through to adoption.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) does not receive specifications at the head office development department and build from them; instead, they are stationed at the customer's site. Their distinguishing feature is that they understand the site's operations and constraints, get technical solutions running in production, and remain responsible until the solution is continuously used. In terms of the FDE Research Institute's five stages, it is a way of working in which the same person or team spans stages 1 through 5. For a full overview of the role, see What is an FDE?.

How does the division-of-labor model differ from the FDE model?

In the division-of-labor model, the person in charge changes at each stage and decision-making tends to break off; in the FDE model, the same person carries the decision-making context through to the end.

PerspectiveDivision-of-labor model (typical model)FDE approach
Issue discoveryA specialized department identifies issues and compiles them into reportsEnter the field and grasp issues together with the business context
PrioritizationA separate decision-maker judges by comparing with other mattersSomeone who feels the on-site risk firsthand builds the materials for judgment and advocates
ImplementationHand off to another teamWork with your own hands and make it functional
Balancing continuityTends to become a binary choice of "shut it down or not"Design a path to fix without shutting down, such as phased migration or parallel operation
AdoptionEffects after handoff are hard to seeAccompany the process until it is in operation and verify

The strength of the FDE model is that it can reduce the information loss and waiting time that occur with each handoff. The difference from consultants, who handle up to proposals and advice, is compared in FDE vs. consultants; the difference from SIers, who handle contracted development, in FDE vs. SIer; and the difference from SWEs, who develop in-house, in FDE vs. ordinary engineers.

What are the limitations of the FDE model, and what kinds of organizations is it unsuitable for?

The FDE model tends toward over-reliance on individuals, and each person's scope widens, so it is not a universal solution that can simply replace the division-of-labor model.

The more the same person spans stages 1 through 5, the more likely it is that the decision-making context is lost along with them when they leave. Also, if one person handles security findings, production implementation, and operational verification alone, there is a limit to how much they can process.

ConditionCompatibility with the FDE modelReason (general)
Organizations without a habit of recording decision-making processesHard to fitWhen the person in charge leaves, no context remains
Organizations that cannot grant implementation/change authority to those entering the fieldHard to fitIt ends up reverting to handoff to another team
People who want to keep digging deep into a specialtyMay not be a good fitSpanning stages means a broader scope
Organizations where the person in charge changes many times from finding to implementationWorth consideringEasier to reduce waiting time caused by handoffs

Adopting the FDE model presupposes recording decision-making processes and keeping them in a state where other members can follow them.

How do you design "fixing without shutting down"?

The key is not to delay countermeasures for the sake of continuity, but to design a migration method that preserves continuity together with the countermeasures.

We summarize a commonly known approach into steps.

  • Translate findings into terms of "magnitude of risk" and "what would happen if left unaddressed"
  • Before a full overhaul, carve out immediately actionable interim measures (narrowing permissions, reviewing stored information, etc.)
  • Design the main countermeasure along a path that allows migration without shutting down, such as parallel operation with a new platform
  • Set a single deadline and person in charge, and make progress visible even to those who raised the findings
  • After migration, remain responsible through verifying effectiveness with logs and monitoring

GMO has announced that this time it will build the replacement service on a new, independent platform. This is close to step 3's "migration to a different platform."

What should engineers aiming to become FDEs and adopting companies learn?

From this incident, engineers can learn the ability to carry findings through to implementation, and companies can learn to build mechanisms so that findings do not stall midway.

This incident is not a lesson for security experts alone. There are also points for FDEs—who enter the field and solve issues in production—to review.

What should engineers aiming to become FDEs acquire?

In addition to the ability to identify issues, they need the design skill to fix things while preserving continuity, and the communication skill to move decision-makers.

Skill to acquireSpecific content
Translating riskExplain technical findings in terms of impact on the business and users
Design skill for phased migrationCreate options beyond the binary of "shut it down or not"
Commitment through to the endNot stopping at delivering a report, but seeing adoption through
Security fundamentalsTreat permission management, log retention, and handling of credentials as design prerequisites

A "correct finding" and a "proposal that gets accepted" are different things. FDEs are expected to bridge the gap between the two themselves.

Does the skill you should develop first differ depending on your current role and experience?

Your strongest stage among the five stages differs depending on your current role. Filling in the stages you have not yet handled is the shortest path to becoming an FDE.

What should companies considering adopting FDE verify?

The starting point is to visualize the path from when a finding is raised until it becomes established practice, and check whether there are stages where it stalls along the way.

Items to verifyExample questions
Receiving findingsIs it decided who will judge findings from inside and outside the company, and by when?
Criteria for prioritizationWhen continuity and security conflict, what criteria are used to decide?
Who implementsHow many times does the person in charge change between finding and implementation?
Migration optionsAre there people who can design a path that "fixes without stopping"?
Post-hoc verificationAre records kept that allow the reasoning behind decisions to be verified later?

Adopting FDE does not eliminate waiting time along the way. However, for organizations that cannot immediately answer the questions in this table, it is worth considering FDE-style talent and ways of working. What should users verify right now?

Users are advised to start by checking official announcements, reviewing their transaction history, changing passwords, and enabling two-factor authentication.

The recommendations in Masumi's article are organized by role below.

RoleRecommended measures
Users of FX automated trading and VPSCheck GMO's official announcements and review transaction history for any unfamiliar transactions
Holders of other companies' FX and securities accountsChange passwords, enable two-factor authentication, and check transaction history regularly
Cloud and server administratorsReview account permissions, log retention, backups, and emergency contacts. If asked for money, consult the police or specialized agencies before paying
All usersBeware of emails and SMS impersonating "condolence payments" or "compensation" that exploit the incident

Masumi states that this incident should not end as merely "a misfortune that befell a major company." In his view, it could happen to anyone who uses cloud or VPS services for work or side projects. Carrying findings through to implementation is an issue that concerns not only the companies providing services, but also their users.
