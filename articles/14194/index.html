Is FDE a step down from SWE? What the hiring bar shows

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 14 min read

Summary

The article asks whether FDE is a step down from software engineering and concludes the title alone cannot tell you. Employers on the record, including Ramp, Baseten and Palantir in its early years, require FDE candidates to pass the same engineering interview loop as SWEs, with customer skills as an additional bar. But some FDE roles are genuinely implementation or pre-sales work, and one developer quit after four weeks when no coding was expected. The author offers a checklist: read the verbs in the posting, ask about the interview loop, what FDEs shipped recently, code review practices, the path back to SWE, and whether levels and pay align with the engineering ladder.

Key points

  • Ramp requires FDE candidates to pass the SWE interview loop.
  • Baseten describes a 'double-bar' process: engineering plus customer skills.
  • Early Palantir FDEs faced the same interview loops as SWEs.
  • Some FDE roles are mislabeled implementation or pre-sales jobs.
  • Judge offers by shipped code, loop design, and path back to SWE.

You mentioned the role to a friend who writes backend code for a living, and they said it with a half-smile: “So, SWE-lite?” Now you are wondering whether taking it quietly ends your engineering career. The short answer: not by the hiring bar at the employers who have spoken on the record, but some roles titled FDE really are a step down, and the title will not tell you which. For the full comparison of the two jobs, read forward deployed engineer vs software engineer; this post answers the status question and gives you a way to judge a specific offer.

Where the ‘SWE-lite’ idea comes from

The idea has three roots, and each one is half right.

Where the role sits. Palantir says its forward deployed software engineers (“Deltas”) deploy its platforms to customers and sit in Business Development, with a mandate to achieve technical outcomes for customers. Source 3Dev versus Delta: Demystifying engineering roles at PalantirPublisherPalantir BlogSource typecompany blog To an engineer, “Business Development” sounds like sales. Read the rest of the sentence: the mandate is technical outcomes.

What the day looks like. Sarah Khalid, an FDE Director at Salesforce, recalled an insurance engagement where, she said, the work was more judgment, trust and communication than coding. Source 4How Forward Deployed Engineers Are Proving AI Makes Tech Jobs More HumanPublisherSalesforce NewsroomSource typecompany blog Hear only that line and you conclude the engineering is thin.

Mislabeled roles. Industry writer Thomas Otter said, in December 2025, that he suspects many roles labeled FDE today are implementation or pre-sales roles with a cooler label. Source 5on the forward deployed engineerPublisherThomas Otter (Substack)Source typeindustry writer’s personal blog That is one writer’s suspicion, not a count.

What the idea misses is how Palantir itself describes the two roles: side by side, by focus. Its blog, in April 2019, called Software Engineers and Forward Deployed Software Engineers its biggest engineering roles by headcount, “Devs” and “Deltas”. Source 3Dev versus Delta: Demystifying engineering roles at PalantirPublisherPalantir BlogSource typecompany blog It describes a Dev’s focus as “one capability, many customers” and a Delta’s as “one customer, many capabilities”. Source 3Dev versus Delta: Demystifying engineering roles at PalantirPublisherPalantir BlogSource typecompany blogSource 6A Day in the Life of a Palantir Forward Deployed Software EngineerPublisherPalantir BlogSource typecompany blog Breadth for one customer is a different shape of engineering, not a smaller amount of it.

The evidence that it differs

The other side of the ledger is real too.

Palantir had a second kind of FDE. In a May 2025 episode of Lenny’s Podcast, YouTube edition, Nabeel Qureshi, who spent nearly eight years at Palantir as an FDE, said Palantir had two types of FDE: one had to pass a software engineering interview, and the other got a technical interview that was more about whether the candidate could reason about data, with hiring criteria closer to whether they were “savvy as a human”. Source 11How Palantir built the ultimate founder factory | Nabeel S. Qureshi (YouTube uploaded en-US captions)PublisherLenny's Podcast, YouTubeSource typerecorded talk or interview So even at one company, “FDE” covered two different profiles.

One Blind commenter thought the loop less deep. In a Blind thread about Palantir’s FDE interviews, one commenter wrote in April 2026 that the FDE loop is less technically deep than the SWE loop “since you’re mostly building on top of Foundry”. Source 12Palantir FDE interview process (Blind)PublisherBlindSource typecandidate report on Blind The commenter does not say they went through the loop. Treat it as one opinion, not a finding.

Coding as the floor, not the job. Salesforce’s Sarah Khalid called coding “table stakes” for the role. Source 4How Forward Deployed Engineers Are Proving AI Makes Tech Jobs More HumanPublisherSalesforce NewsroomSource typecompany blog That cuts both ways: you must have it, and having it is not what sets you apart.

The title spans a wide range. TechCrunch quoted Chris Taylor, CEO of Ode with Anthropic, in July 2026, saying many FDEs can help roll Claude Code out to a workforce, but very few can build a flagship AI product feature. Source 13Forward-deployed engineers are the AI industry's latest talent obsession (Rebecca Bellan)PublisherTechCrunchSource typenews report Note what that compares. It is not FDE against SWE; it is FDE against FDE. The same title covers rollout work and deep product engineering.

Our reading: where an employer has said, the technical bar is the software engineering bar, used as a floor you must clear; above the floor, the customer bar decides. The differences are about which profile a company hires, not a lower floor.

When an FDE role really is a step down

Some are. The Pragmatic Engineer reported, in March 2026, that Gergely Orosz had talked with two engineers who took FDE roles and were disappointed. Source 2Is the FDE role becoming less desirable?PublisherThe Pragmatic EngineerSource typecandidate’s personal write-up The Pragmatic Engineer also quoted a developer who accepted an FDE role and quit four weeks later, saying the company wanted to use them on the engagement-lead side and not at all on software development. Source 2Is the FDE role becoming less desirable?PublisherThe Pragmatic EngineerSource typecandidate’s personal write-up That is one developer’s account, relayed by a newsletter, so it is low-confidence evidence. It is still the clearest picture of what a step down looks like.

In our view, an FDE role is a step down for a software engineer when most of these are true:

  • No code ships under your name. You configure, demo and coordinate, and someone else builds.
  • Nothing you write reaches production. Your output is slides, proofs of concept that are thrown away, or tickets for another team.
  • The loop never asks you to build or read code. If no round tests engineering, the job probably does not use it.
  • There is no path back. No one on the team has moved into a software engineering role, and no one can tell you how they would.

None of that is decided by the word “Forward Deployed”. It is decided by the posting, the loop and the team.

How to judge a specific role before you accept

Run these checks in order. They are ours, built from the evidence above, and each takes one conversation or one careful read.

Is this FDE role a step down?

  • Read the verbs in the posting. OpenAI’s San Francisco FDE posting, as read in September 2026, asks for candidates who “write and review production-grade code” and says FDEs own discovery, scoping, system design, build and production rollout. Source 14Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) - SFPublisherOpenAI (Ashby)Source typecompany job posting That is what engineering verbs look like. “Build”, “ship”, “write and review production-grade code” and “own production rollout” point to engineering. “Drive adoption”, “manage the engagement” and “run demos” with no build verbs point elsewhere. Our free lesson on how to read an FDE job posting line by line walks through real postings.
  • Ask the recruiter about the loop. Say: “Do FDE and software engineering candidates share any of the technical rounds, and what does the coding round look like?”
  • Ask the hiring manager what shipped. Say: “What did an FDE on this team build in the last quarter that is still running, and where does that code live?”
  • Ask who reviews FDE code. Say: “Does FDE code go through the same review as product code, and does any of it end up in the core product?”
  • Ask about the path back. Say: “Has anyone moved from this team into a software engineering role? How did that work?”
  • Check the level. Ask whether FDE levels map one to one onto the engineering ladder, and compare level before pay.

In our view, a vague answer to the shipping question is the strongest warning sign on the list.

A quick scenario. You hold two FDE offers. Posting A says you will “partner with customers to design, build and deploy” integrations and agents, and the loop has a practical coding round and a system design round. Posting B says you will “own customer success for strategic accounts” and “coordinate technical resources”, and the loop is a case discussion and a presentation. Same title. Posting A is a software engineering job facing customers. Posting B may be a fine job, but it is not the one your friend was worried about, and you should know that before you sign. For the exact lines that give a disguised role away, see the red flags in an FDE job posting.

Is FDE a step down in pay or level?

For many people asking this question, “step down” means the ladder as much as the work. The evidence on FDE pay against SWE pay is thin. Two Blind posters in June 2026 relayed different lines from Google recruiters: one was told FDEs and SWEs share pay bands and doubted it; the other, who had not yet interviewed, was told FDE pay can be higher at the same level. Source 15Google L5 Offer Ridiciously Low? - BlindPublisherBlindSource typecandidate report on BlindSource 16Google Cloud FDE role - BlindPublisherBlindSource typecandidate report on Blind That is low-confidence, secondhand evidence pointing both ways: a reason to ask, not an answer.

These are the ladder questions we would put to any recruiter before comparing numbers:

  • “Is the FDE ladder the same ladder as software engineering, with the same level names?” If FDEs sit on a separate ladder, ask how a level there maps to yours.
  • “Is the pay base salary, or with a variable part tied to sales?” On-target earnings with a sales-linked variable is a different kind of job from a salaried engineering one.
  • “What level is this offer, and what would it take to be at my current level?” Get the answer in writing before you negotiate.

If the offer comes in a level below where you are now, what to do about a down-leveled FDE offer walks through the response. If a recruiter asks what level you think you are, our question on what level you think you are shows how to answer without underselling a software engineering background.

How to keep your engineering sharp as an FDE

Say you take a role that passes the checks. The risk now is drift: months of meetings and glue code while your depth quietly erodes. These are our habits for stopping that.

  • Own one production system per engagement. Not a demo: something with tests, monitoring and an on-call owner, and make that owner you.
  • Push one fix upstream. When you patch around a product gap for a customer, write the product change too and get it reviewed by the product team. It turns “one customer” work into “many customers” work.
  • Ask for product-grade code review. If your team reviews FDE code lightly, ask a product engineer to review yours.
  • Keep a build log. After each engagement, write down the system, the hardest bug and the one design decision you would defend. It becomes your answer to the hardest technical problem you have solved in your next loop.
  • Keep fundamentals warm. A short timed coding drill each week keeps you ready for any loop that still tests algorithms. Our free lesson on what FDE coding rounds test sorts the evidence by kind of coding round and gives a drill for each, and do FDE interviews have LeetCode? goes company by company.

There is precedent for moving back. The Pragmatic Engineer reported in August 2025 that after Palantir launched Foundry around 2016, more of its FDEs moved back into software engineering. Source 17What are Forward Deployed Engineers, and why are they so in demand? (Gergely Orosz)PublisherThe Pragmatic EngineerSource typenews report That is one company at one moment, not a promise, which is why the path-back question on the checklist is worth asking.

What to say when they ask “why FDE and not SWE?”

If you have been wrestling with the status question, it will leak into this answer. The weak version sounds like an escape: “I want to code a bit less and talk to people more.” That tells an interviewer you see FDE as SWE-lite, which is the exact worry you were trying to settle.

The strong version names the engineering you want to do and why customers make it better: “I want to own the whole path from a customer’s problem to a system running in their environment. In my current job I get a ticket three steps removed from the person who needs it, and the best work I have done was the one time I sat with the user and rebuilt the integration in a week.”

Our question Why FDE and not software engineering? has the framework and the follow-ups an interviewer uses to test that reason. If you were already told you were “not technical enough” after an FDE loop, what that feedback means and how to fix it is the next read.

The verdict

FDE is not a step down from software engineering by design. Where employers have stated the bar, FDE candidates clear the software engineering bar and then a customer bar on top. Some roles carry the title without the engineering, and those are a step down. Judge the posting, the loop and the team, not the title, and once you are in, keep shipping code on purpose.

If the checklist says the role is real engineering, the loop will test it. Set a 45-minute timer and try the free token bucket or messy CSV question, where you build something small that works and say what you would ask the customer. Then try coding with vague requirements, where you ask what you need before you implement.
