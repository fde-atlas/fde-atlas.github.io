Can You Move from SIer SE to FDE? Job-Change Ease by Role and How to Prepare

Original title: SIerのSEからFDEへ転職できる？職種別の移りやすさと準備

FDE総研 · 2026-11-25 · 11 min read

Summary

This FDE総研 article, with practitioner input from VACAN, examines whether SIer systems engineers in Japan can move into forward deployed engineer (FDE) roles. Its verdict: yes, but not with an unchanged résumé—the experience overlaps, yet the evidence employers want differs. The biggest hurdle is proving hands-on implementation, since SIer work often delegates coding to partner firms. Requirements-definition and PM roles sit closest to the FDE entry point; infrastructure and maintenance engineers must first build automation or improvement track records. The piece contrasts FDE and SE on accountability, coding involvement, customer proximity, and tech-choice discretion, offers strategies by years of experience and English ability, advises a situation–decision–result résumé format, and recommends checking actual job postings rather than generalized salary figures.

Key points

  • SIer SEs can become FDEs, but unchanged résumés rarely pass.
  • Proving hands-on coding is the biggest barrier.
  • Requirements-definition and PM backgrounds are closest to FDE entry.
  • Build at least one verifiable, self-written deliverable first.
  • Write résumés as situation, decision, and result sequences.

Can an SIer SE transition to FDE?

FDE Soken and VACAN believe that SIer SEs can transition to FDE if they can demonstrate both client-facing work and self-driven implementation in their resume.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer, a client-site-based engineer) is a role popularized by Palantir. FDEs embed themselves at client sites and solve problems by writing code themselves. FDE Soken positions SIer SEs as "a group that can make the transition, but whose careers as-is make it difficult to get through." The reason is that while much of the experience overlaps, the types of evidence that are valued differ.

What is the basis for SIer SEs being able to transition?

SIer SEs have accumulated experience translating vague client requests into business problems. This experience overlaps with the core work of an FDE.

FDEs listen to problems in the same room as client stakeholders and build prototypes on the spot. SEs who have experience with requirements definition, business interviews, and stakeholder coordination can leverage their strengths in this first half. VACAN's practical experience also shows that people accustomed to client dialogue get up to speed at client sites quickly.

What is the biggest barrier?

According to FDE Soken, the biggest barrier is proving implementation ability. Achievements limited to design documents and reviews are unlikely to be evaluated as evidence of hands-on capability.

At SIers, implementation is often delegated to partner companies. In such cases, the individual cannot show code they wrote themselves, leaving recruiters without material for judgment. You need to make your deliverables verifiable by third parties, such as personal development projects, internal tools, or automation scripts.

How do FDEs differ from SIer SEs?

FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) write code themselves at client sites to solve problems, and differ from SIer SEs in how accountability for results is placed.

The starting point for understanding FDE duties can be found in sources such as Palantir's job postings. However, even under the same FDE title, the actual duties vary by company. The following comparison is the editorial team's view, organized using comparison axes established independently by FDE Soken.

How do roles and scope of responsibility differ?

SIer SEs are accountable for deliverables within the contract scope. FDEs are accountable for the client's business outcomes and are also entrusted with choosing the means.

How do working styles and evaluation criteria differ?

FDEs are expected to work in a way that shows working things quickly to get client feedback. This differs in emphasis from the SE evaluation criteria of quality management and schedule adherence.

The robust progress management valued at SIers is also a necessary foundation for FDEs. However, in FDE work, a working prototype delivered early is sometimes valued more than a perfect design document. It is said in the field that many SEs struggle with this shift in mindset.

Which SEs transition most easily: requirements definition, PM, infrastructure, or maintenance/operations?

Using FDE Soken's original axis of "density of client dialogue × degree of contact with implementation," requirements definition and PM are closest to the entry point, while for maintenance/operations, reinforcing implementation experience is the top priority.

This assessment is the editorial team's qualitative judgment, not a figure based on statistics. Results vary greatly depending on individual careers.

How should requirements definition and PM SEs act?

Requirements definition and PM SEs can use their strength in client dialogue as is. On top of that, the first priority is creating at least one track record of their own code actually running.

PMs tend to be consumed by progress management and drift away from implementation. From the perspective of VACAN practitioners, what is expected of former PMs is experience building a small but actually working business tool. Rather than scale, what matters is being able to explain that they wrote it themselves.

How should infrastructure and maintenance/operations SEs act?

For infrastructure and maintenance/operations SEs, achievements in automation and improvement serve as evidence of implementation ability. The next challenge is articulating case studies where they solved client problems.

Infrastructure SEs have opportunities to work with code, such as IaC (Infrastructure as Code) and monitoring automation. Maintenance/operations SEs can present as achievements the analysis of inquiry trends and the scripting of routine tasks. For both, writing about how it benefited the client's business brings the story closer to the FDE context.

What requirements and salary ranges do FDE job postings have?

FDE Soken does not post fixed salary figures and, as of November 2026, recommends checking the actual job postings currently listed as the primary source of information.

FDE salaries vary widely depending on the company and business phase, and even under the same title the duties differ. Generalizing figures would cause misunderstanding, so this article does not assert specific amounts. The latest openings can be found in FDE Soken's job listings. Our approach to salaries is summarized in our article on FDE salaries.

What requirements are common across job postings?

What FDE job postings have in common are three things: implementation skills, the ability to converse with customers, and the ability to define problems. Requirements for English proficiency vary by company.

How should you check the salary range?

For salary, always note the posting date and the offered range when comparing. When looking at the difference from your current salary, consider allowances and how discretionary labor is treated as well.

The steps to check are as follows.

  • Record the posting date and update date of the job listing
  • Note the lower and upper bounds of the offered range separately
  • Check how bonuses and stock options are handled
  • Compare the expected duties and scope of responsibility
  • At the interview, ask what results will actually be evaluated

What if you have little experience but high English proficiency?

If you have under 3 years of experience but high English proficiency, create implementation deliverables before relying on English. English only serves as a bonus.

High English proficiency makes it easier to apply to foreign-affiliated job openings. However, if you have little experience delivering results at customer sites, you won't stand out on paper. We recommend first creating one small case study of solving a customer's problem.

What if you have long experience but low English proficiency?

If you have over 10 years of experience but low English proficiency, focus on domestic companies whose jobs don't require English, and apply with case studies of solving customer problems as your centerpiece.

Even if your English is lacking, the ability to engage deeply with Japanese customers is valued by domestic companies. In terms of company size, startups with broader roles likely offer more opportunities to leverage your experience. At large companies, duties are divided finely by division, so confirm your role after assignment in the interview.

Who is suited to FDE, and who is not?

FDE Soken summarizes that people who can define ambiguous problems themselves and carry them through to implementation are suited to FDE, while those who prefer following established procedures are not.

It also helps to understand the differences between FDE and similar roles when making your choice.

What kind of person is suited to FDE?

People suited to FDE are those who can enter the customer's site, build things themselves, observe reactions, and make improvements. People who enjoy both technology and dialogue tend to thrive.

From the perspective of VACAN practitioners, the more you can start working before specifications are finalized and share failures early, the better you fit the field. Not shying away from ambiguity and being able to uncover the problems behind customers' words are also important qualities.

What kind of person is not suited to FDE?

People not suited to FDE are those who prefer environments with clearly defined roles, or who find dealing with customers a burden. Those who struggle with situations where responsibility for results is ambiguous are also a poor fit.

In FDE, role boundaries are fluid. Schedules often change due to customer circumstances, and business trips or on-site assignments may occur. People who want to deepen their expertise within stable role divisions may be able to perform better in other roles such as SWE.

Where should you start when preparing to move from an SIer to FDE?

Preparation starts with creating a working deliverable with your own code. FDE Soken recommends then articulating case studies of solving customer problems and reflecting them in your resume.

What should you do in the first three months?

In the first three months, focus on creating deliverables, taking inventory of your case studies, and analyzing job openings. Deciding the order keeps your preparation from becoming scattered.

  • Pick one problem at your current job and build a small tool by writing code yourself
  • Summarize the tool's purpose, the technologies used, and the results on one page
  • From your past projects, write out three case studies where you solved customer problems
  • Pick five jobs of interest from FDE Soken's job listings and organize their requirements in a table
  • Prioritize the requirements you lack and make a plan to strengthen them

How can you gain FDE-like experience within your company?

To gain FDE-like experience within your company, take on small projects where you enter the field of customers or internal user departments, listen to their problems, and solve them yourself.

Even in your current SIer role, you can raise your hand for projects close to customers. Experience the flow of prototyping on the spot when hearing requirements, receiving feedback, and making revisions. This experience becomes a concrete example to discuss in interviews. Confidential information can be anonymized.

What are common pitfalls in job changes, and how should you write your resume?

Most stumbling blocks are resumes where evidence of implementation is invisible and insufficient explanation of customer results. FDE Soken recommends writing your results and role concretely.

What are common failure examples?

The three most common failures are applying without evidence of implementation, exaggerating your role, and overlooking differences in job duties between companies.

Failure exampleWhy it happensHow to avoid it
Writing only about design document workYou cannot demonstrate implementation experienceAttach deliverables of code you wrote yourself
Describing your role too broadlyYou get contradicted when interviewers dig deeperAccurately describe the scope you were responsible for
Overlooking differences in job dutiesEven roles with the same FDE title differ in contentConfirm day-to-day work details in the interview
Selling yourself only on English abilityIt is not valued without supporting achievementsWrite your implementation and customer-facing achievements first

How should you write your resume?

Write your resume in the order of situation, your own judgment, and result. Present it in a way that shows the reader how you solved customer problems.

Here is a rewriting example. Replace the numbers with your own actual achievements.

Writing styleExample
BeforeWas responsible for requirements definition of a core system and created design documents
AfterHeard that data entry work was a burden for the customer's department, wrote an automation script myself, showed a prototype, and reflected it in the requirements. As a result, the customer adopted it in their operations

In the "after" version, the problem, your actions, and the result read as one continuous story. Omit details you cannot disclose, but keep the role and the reasoning behind your decisions.
