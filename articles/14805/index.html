Forward Deployed Engineer at Applied Intuition: Role, Salary, and Interview (2026)

fde.directory · 2026-09-16 · 5 min read

Summary

This profile of Applied Intuition's Forward Deployed Engineer role describes work resembling a startup CTO: small teams owning end-to-end deployment of autonomy software onto DOD platforms. Posted base salaries ($145K–$200K) are below what Applied pays its own core engineers in total compensation, so the real value is equity — the company hit a $15B valuation (up from $6B) with an estimated ~$830M ARR in 2025. Defense roles require U.S. citizenship, clearance eligibility, and five days in-office. The interview loop averages about 20 days with coding, design, and behavioral rounds, filtering for independence in ambiguous environments.

Key points

  • Role is framed as startup-CTO-like: end-to-end ownership of defense deployments.
  • Base salary of $145K–$200K is low; equity is the main compensation story.
  • Valuation doubled to $15B in a year; estimated ARR around $830M in 2025.

Most forward-deployed roles sell you flexibility: remote-first, travel when you want, a laptop and a customer Slack. Applied Intuition’s Forward Deployed Engineer sells you the opposite. To take the defense seat you need to be a U.S. citizen, hold or be able to obtain a security clearance, and — per the company’s own policy — work from an Applied office five days a week. It’s the most gated FDE job currently on the board. The interesting question is why a role this locked-down is worth it, and the answer is almost entirely in the equity, not the base.

What the role actually is

The live posting frames the job in a way I haven’t seen elsewhere: “your responsibilities will look similar to those of a startup CTO.” You work in small teams and own end-to-end execution of high-stakes projects, acting as the technical bridge between Applied’s vehicle-intelligence platforms and a customer’s real-world operational environment. In the defense variant, that environment runs “from defense contractors to forward operating bases,” integrating Applied’s tools, Vehicle OS, and autonomy stacks with DOD platforms.

The work is deliberately broad. The posting lists software engineering, operating-system configuration, system deployment, and hardware integration as things you’ll do — sometimes in the same week. This is the Palantir deployment model, not the Glean product-builder model: you take a mature commercial platform and bend it to a customer’s operational reality, except here the customer is the U.S. military and the reality is a machine that moves. Applied’s pitch to candidates is that its commercial autonomy stack lets you “deliver capabilities that would take traditional defense contractors years to develop.” If you’ve read the Anduril FDE writeup, the defense-tech flavor will feel familiar — small teams, high ambiguity, mission stakes — but Applied’s center of gravity is autonomy software across every moving machine, not hardware.

Compensation

Here’s where Applied looks underwhelming on the surface and more interesting underneath. The posted base bands are modest for a role this selective:

Role (posted) Base salary range
Forward Deployed Engineer $145,000–$200,000
Senior FDE — Defense (D.C.) $150,000–$200,000

Compare that to what Applied pays its regular software engineers. Levels.fyi puts Applied Intuition SWE total compensation at roughly $209,000–$326,000+, and frontier-lab FDEs routinely clear those numbers on cash alone. So the posted FDE base isn’t just below the market for forward-deployed talent — it’s below what Applied’s own core engineers take home once equity is counted. Base salary is doing very little of the work here.

The equity is doing all of it, and the underlying company is the reason to look twice. In June 2025 Applied closed a $600M Series F at a $15B valuation, co-led by BlackRock-managed funds and Kleiner Perkins — up from a $6B Series E just over a year earlier. Sacra estimates Applied hit roughly $830M in ARR in 2025, about double its ~$415M in 2024. Treat the ARR as a third-party estimate, not an audited figure, but the shape matters: a $15B mark against ~$830M ARR is around an 18x revenue multiple. That’s cheaper than the ~24x Glean is carrying, at a company growing just as fast and with a defense moat that’s hard to replicate. If you believe physical AI is a real category and Applied stays a leader in it, the options are the whole trade — which is exactly why the low base should give you pause if you don’t.

The interview loop

Applied doesn’t publish an FDE-specific process, so read the following as the company’s general engineering loop plus what the posting tells you — not a guaranteed FDE script. Across 249 candidate-reported interviews, Glassdoor pegs the average process at about 20 days with a difficulty of 3.17 out of 5.

Stage Format What’s reportedly tested
Recruiter screen 30 min Background, clearance eligibility, logistics, motivation
Coding round Timed, remote LeetCode-style algorithms; a reported debugging exercise
Onsite (≈4 rounds) In-person Coding, system/architecture design, customer-facing problem-solving
Leads chat Behavioral Culture, ownership, motivation

Candidates consistently describe timeboxed coding rounds where correctness, edge cases, and efficiency all count, followed by design and behavioral conversations that probe trade-off reasoning. For the FDE seat specifically, the posting’s own language is the better tell: it wants a “strong coder” in C++ and Python with “broad technical experience across software engineering, operating systems, and some hardware,” who can “thrive and innovate independently within highly ambiguous and rapidly evolving operational environments.” The filter that isn’t on the schedule is the startup-CTO bar — can you walk into an under-defined deployment, own the outcome, and adapt as requirements change without a spec? Strong engineers who need a well-scoped ticket will struggle here regardless of how clean their coding rounds are.

One hard gate sits before all of it: U.S. citizenship and clearance eligibility are non-negotiable for the defense roles. If you can’t clear, this seat is closed no matter how well you interview.

Should you take it?

The case for: it’s equity in one of the few defense-and-autonomy companies that doubled its valuation in a year, at a revenue multiple that’s saner than several frontier-lab peers, doing work — deploying autonomy onto DOD platforms — that very few engineers on earth get to touch. The startup-CTO framing is real; the ownership is broad; the mission is unambiguous.

The case for caution: the posted base is genuinely low for forward-deployed work, so your upside is concentrated in illiquid private options, and you’re accepting the hardest constraints on the board to get it — citizenship, clearance, and five days in-office, the opposite of the remote FDE market that’s grown up around this title. If you need cash comp now, or you want geographic freedom, this isn’t your seat. But if you can clear, you want to build in defense tech, and you’re betting on physical AI as a category, Applied is one of the more compelling equity stories in the whole FDE field — provided you go in knowing that’s the bet you’re actually making.
