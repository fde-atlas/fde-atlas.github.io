FDE Jobs in Abu Dhabi vs Bengaluru: Postings Compared

FDE Times · 2026-10-16 · 6 min read

Summary

This article compares FDE job postings in Abu Dhabi and India. OpenAI's Abu Dhabi posting requires three office days a week plus up to 50% travel, while Palantir prefers 25-50% travel and demands startup-CTO-like autonomy with minimal supervision. Indian postings from HackerRank (Bengaluru) and Vendasta (Chennai) require onsite customer work but state no travel percentage. A UAE recruitment firm says engineers with genuine FDE experience remain scarce in Dubai and Abu Dhabi. The author advises treating posting details as lifestyle descriptions, asking in interviews how many days FDEs actually spent at customer sites, and proving capability with prototype-to-production delivery stories.

In brief

  • In Abu Dhabi, OpenAI and Palantir postings state travel percentages and require ownership of deployments; Palantir alone asks for work with minimal supervision.
  • HackerRank and Vendasta postings in India require on-site work with customers but give no travel percentage.
  • A UAE recruitment firm says engineers with genuine FDE experience remain scarce in both Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

OpenAI’s posting for a Forward Deployed Engineer in Abu Dhabi lists two requirements side by side: three days a week in the office, and travel of up to 50% of the time. At first glance the two conditions seem to contradict each other. Read more closely, they give a fairly honest picture of how an FDE team operates in a new market.

The Gulf and India are worth examining closely, because postings in the two regions are written in very different ways. In Abu Dhabi, OpenAI and Palantir give concrete figures. In India, postings from HackerRank and Vendasta make clear that you will work at the customer’s site, but not how much.

If you are thinking of applying, the point to grasp is that the same job title comes with very different levels of clarity about the work. Knowing how to read what a posting states and what it leaves out is the first skill for anyone who wants to be an FDE.

In Abu Dhabi, the office is a transit stop

OpenAI’s posting makes FDEs in the UAE responsible for the technical delivery of multiple deployments, from the first prototype until the system runs stably in production. Each engineer owns the full lifecycle of several projects, not just one stage.

Palantir goes further in its posting for a Forward Deployed Software Engineer in Abu Dhabi. It compares the role’s responsibilities to those of a startup CTO, and asks candidates to learn, execute and make decisions on their own with minimal supervision. Notably, the text of OpenAI’s posting does not include this “minimal supervision” requirement.

Try a hypothetical calculation. On a team of four FDEs, each travelling half the time, only about two people are in the office in an average week. If each owns three deployments, the team carries twelve systems in twelve different environments, rarely with a colleague sitting nearby to ask.

That is why office days are best spent on syncing, architecture reviews and transferring knowledge between deployments. The work that creates value happens at the customer’s site.

Who is the customer, and who goes with you?

A UAE recruitment firm, in an article about its own market, argues that FDEs there must deploy, customise and integrate solutions directly into the complex systems of enterprises and government.

That is the view of an intermediary, not a requirement written by an employer, so treat it only as a directional signal.

If the signal is right, being a strong coder is not enough. You need to know who approves data access, which environments are allowed to run models, and which processes must not be touched.

In Saudi Arabia, Microsoft has taken a different approach. Microsoft and HUMAIN announced a long-term partnership agreement (a partnership announcement, not a job posting). In it, the two sides wrote that HUMAIN’s AI specialists would work alongside Microsoft’s FDE team.

One agreement does not make a rule for the whole Gulf. But it suggests an option worth considering: the FDE does not go to the customer alone but is accompanied by people from a local partner, filling exactly the gap in processes and access described above.

When interviewing for an FDE team in the Gulf, ask directly: will I work alone at the customer’s site, or with people from a partner, and which one?

In India, postings say “at the customer’s site” but not how much

HackerRank’s FDE posting in Bengaluru is an in-office role, yet it states that the engineer will work onsite with customers. The hire must lead in-person sessions with customers: customer discovery, pilots and demos.

Vendasta’s posting for a role in Chennai, listed on Built In Bengaluru, is even more direct. The role requires travel to customer locations and embedding within the customer’s engineering team.

Neither gives a travel percentage, unlike the two Abu Dhabi postings. You cannot tell whether “onsite” means one demo a month or three weeks a month in another city. That gap has to be filled in the interview, not after you have signed the contract.

A blog on FDE salaries in India adds another reason for caution: FDE is a title, not a level, so pay varies widely. Two postings with the same name can be two jobs at two different levels of responsibility.

The first row of the table shows that the two markets differ in whether they state a figure at all, more than in whether it is high or low. Even within Abu Dhabi there are two voices: OpenAI says required, Palantir says preferred and dependent on the team.

Which rhythm can you sustain for two or three years?

Treat the details in a posting as a description of a lifestyle, not as administrative small print. Travelling up to half the time can mean close to half a year away from home; “multiple deployments” means switching context every day.

If your family and health cannot sustain that rhythm, you are likely to leave just when a deployment needs you most. For postings that give no figure, ask something specific: last month, how many days did an FDE on the team spend at the customer’s site?

What your CV and interview need to prove

In the same article, the UAE recruitment firm says engineers with genuine forward deployed experience remain scarce in both Dubai and Abu Dhabi. If that is true, what makes you stand out is proof that you have actually done the work, not a list of technologies.

Your CV should answer two requirements: owning technical delivery from prototype to production, as OpenAI’s posting puts it, and making decisions with minimal supervision, as Palantir’s demands.

A line such as “took a document classification model from prototype to production for a banking customer, integrated it with a legacy storage system, decided the deployment architecture independently” says more than ten lines of tools. If you have worked with the public sector, spell out how you handled data access and the deployment environment.

For the interview, prepare three stories with measurable results: a system you took from prototype to production, a decision you had to make alone with no one to ask, and a discovery session or pilot you led in person with a customer. The third story fits postings like HackerRank’s particularly well.

In both Abu Dhabi and Bengaluru, the title is the same, but the real work is decided by the customers in each place. Someone who knows which rhythm they can sustain, and who has a ready story about a deployment they took to production themselves, will not need a title to convince anyone.
