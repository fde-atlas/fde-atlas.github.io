Oliva · Product and after-sales support assistant

AIelsen Agent Field Notes · 12 min read

Summary

This field note describes how Oliva built a support knowledge workstation for cross-border e-commerce after-sales teams. An AI agent retrieves evidence from manuals and policy tables, drafts replies, and hands them to support staff, who review, approve, or revise drafts in their existing workspace before sending. Confirmed corrections feed back into the knowledge base through deduplication and rate-limited imports. The article documents key decisions, such as fixing table retrieval gaps, calibrating reply strictness to avoid invented service commitments, and separating internal validation notes from customer-facing text. It closes with pilot assessment metrics and advice to start with frequent questions for one product.

Key points

  • Oliva built a support workstation where the agent drafts replies and staff review before sending
  • Retrieval was extended to embedded tables after specification answers proved inaccurate
  • Drafts distinguish confirmed facts, conditional explanations, and items needing confirmation
  • FAQ updates gained deduplication, rate limits, and preview checks to control knowledge growth
  • Pilots should start with frequent questions for one product and track draft approval and error rates

WHAT THE TEAM BRINGS

An anonymized set of product and after-sales inquiries, current manuals and policy tables, previous standard replies, commitments that are prone to error, and the team’s existing workspace and review process.

From fieldwork to lasting business capability.FORWARD-DEPLOYED ENGINEERING

Oliva—

Bring answers into the support team’s workspace.

Previously used in real customer support workflows

When customers ask about product specifications, shipping or after-sales service, cross-border e-commerce support teams often have to search manuals, check tables and ask colleagues before deciding how to reply. Oliva helped build a support knowledge workstation that brings information the customer has already provided, verifiable sources and suggested replies into the existing workspace. The Agent finds evidence and drafts; support staff review and send. Their corrections become knowledge for the next inquiry.

01 / THE ASSIGNMENT

gets stuck.

Product knowledge is scattered across manuals, documents and tables. Support staff answering recurring questions about specifications, shipping and after-sales service must find the information and reconcile different answers. Customers may also be asked again for details they already provided when a case changes hands.

The same specification may appear in a manual table, a product document and an earlier reply. Staff who find different versions may give different answers. More training still does not remove the need to find and check information each time.

Quality reviews found incorrect specifications, invented service commitments and requests for order details that customers had already provided. Staff need a draft that helps move the inquiry forward, with a clear indication of facts that remain unconfirmed.

An incident review from a related support knowledge project found that wording from an ended campaign was still appearing in purchase inquiries. Without separating the campaign period from fulfillment, old material can create purchase or service expectations that no longer apply.

Useful corrections deserve to be retained, but importing every piece of feedback directly creates duplicates and conflicts. Knowledge growth had slowed the service. Updates therefore need review, deduplication and capacity controls.

A question-answering backend that uses team documents to handle basic inquiries.

  • Input
  • Common product, shipping and after-sales questions, together with existing manuals and service policies.
  • Decision
  • Staff need replies they can check and act on. The first step is to connect each customer question with the right source material.
  • Action
  • Built a basic flow for uploading, indexing, retrieving and drafting from documents, and retained real questions for later checks.
  • Output
  • A question-answering backend that uses team documents to handle basic inquiries.
  • Handoff
  • When staff reviewed the drafts, incorrect specifications and missing information became concrete tasks for the next iteration.

A knowledge base that can retrieve key tables, plus specification-check results.

  • Input
  • Support staff reported inaccurate answers about power consumption, temperature and other product specifications.
  • Decision
  • Key answers were inside embedded tables and linked material that the original index did not cover. Duplicate and conflicting versions also disrupted source selection.
  • Action
  • Added retrieval coverage for tables and related material, resolved conflicting content and adjusted retrieval components, then checked answers and sources with similar specification questions.
  • Output
  • A knowledge base that can retrieve key tables, plus specification-check results.
  • Handoff
  • With verifiable facts available, staff could then judge how to phrase the answer and which conditions still needed confirmation.

Reply drafts and items to confirm, ready for staff review.

  • Input
  • Retrieved material, the original customer question and quality feedback from batches of replies.
  • Decision
  • Supported information can be explained directly. Exact numbers, service policies and handling times cannot be guessed, and information already supplied by the customer should not be requested again.
  • Action
  • Separated confirmed facts, conditions to explain and missing information; added scenario and role rules; and checked the use of specifications, service commitments and existing order details.
  • Output
  • Reply drafts and items to confirm, ready for staff review.
  • Handoff
  • Used real customer phrasing to check whether drafts answered the question, while avoiding excessive caution that prevented routine inquiries from moving forward.

A collaborative flow from question to draft to human confirmation.

  • Input
  • The team’s table-based workflow and its actual habits for reading, editing and sending replies.
  • Decision
  • Staff need drafts and supporting information where they handle inquiries, with a record of their review decisions.
  • Action
  • Integrated the existing workspace, presented suggested replies and checking information, and recorded approval, revision or rejection. After confirming the content, staff decided whether to send it through their usual process.
  • Output
  • A collaborative flow from question to draft to human confirmation.
  • Handoff
  • Approved or revised content became a candidate for knowledge updates. Rejected content remained an issue record rather than becoming a standard answer directly.

A controlled FAQ update process and cleanup tools.

  • Input
  • Staff-confirmed corrections, along with duplicate content and operating pressure caused by feedback imports.
  • Decision
  • Review experience is worth preserving, but repeated imports make knowledge harder to search and can disrupt continued use.
  • Action
  • Cleaned up excess content, added deduplication, import rate limits and preview checks, and retained reviewable update records.
  • Output
  • A controlled FAQ update process and cleanup tools.
  • Handoff
  • Similar inquiries can reuse useful corrections. New knowledge still needs checks for version, conflicts and scope of use.

A distributable workstation, integration instructions and operating materials.

  • Input
  • Connection, startup, workspace integration and multiple-knowledge-base requirements encountered during continued use.
  • Decision
  • Receiving drafts during a working day sets a different delivery standard from answering one question in a demonstration.
  • Action
  • Fixed connection and startup issues, added warm-up and cache-refresh procedures, and documented installation, bridging, multiple-knowledge-base configuration and maintenance.
  • Output
  • A distributable workstation, integration instructions and operating materials.
  • Handoff
  • Continued checking facts, reply behavior and runtime status with real inquiries, feeding newly discovered errors back into documents and rules.

03 / THE DECISIONS

as much as the outputs.

When a draft, a source or the actual behavior differed from expectations, how did the Agent locate the problem and choose the next step?

  • The signal
  • Similar product specification questions repeatedly received inaccurate answers.
  • The choice
  • Traced the answer to its table, completed the index and resolved conflicting material, then checked again with the same questions.
  • What it changed
  • The fix addressed verifiable source and retrieval problems, allowing staff to return to the correct reference.
  • The signal
  • A strict ban on inference caused many routine inquiries to be deferred. Relaxing it exposed invented services and repeated requests for information.
  • The choice
  • Used the completeness of the facts to distinguish direct explanations, conditional explanations and items to confirm, and extended quality checks to services, actions, timing and policies.
  • What it changed
  • Staff received drafts they could work with and could focus on conditions that actually required judgment.
  • The signal
  • Internal validation notes had appeared in customer replies.
  • The choice
  • Disabled the problematic validation configuration and checked both the outward-facing wording and its fidelity to the facts.
  • What it changed
  • Kept the necessary information in the customer reply and the internal checking process separate, reducing the need for extra explanations and the risk of lost trust.
  • The signal
  • Automatic imports multiplied duplicate knowledge and affected the workstation’s ability to keep serving support staff.
  • The choice
  • Kept the feedback process while adding deduplication, rate limits and checks before import.
  • What it changed
  • A staff correction could inform later inquiries, with explicit controls on knowledge maintenance and operating capacity.

BEFORE

AFTER / THE CHANGE

What needs fixing: intent, validity and source granularity

Check the actual wording against the retrieved sources. Separate campaign-stage knowledge from fulfillment-stage knowledge, and keep expired entries and missing conditions out of current promises.

  • 01

    User wording

    Conversational wording missed the relevant intent, so the intended retrieval strategy did not run.
  • 02

    Candidate material

    One FAQ mixed entries from different business stages; file-level tags were too coarse.
  • 03

    Repair and retest

    Expand coverage of real question phrasing, check retrieval and ranking of old entries, then retest the same questions.
  • 04

    Ongoing governance

    Attach validity periods, applicable stages and source types to individual knowledge entries rather than hard-coding project facts in shared code.
