How to Get Shortlisted for FDE Roles: Resume & LinkedIn Tips

Varsity Blog · 2026-11-23 · 21 min read

Summary

This Varsity Blog guide explains how candidates in India can get shortlisted for Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roles. It argues that employers look beyond coding ability for evidence of integration work, AI implementation, cloud deployment, and customer-facing problem solving. It advises searching beyond the exact FDE title (e.g., Customer Engineer, Implementation Engineer), writing outcome-focused resumes with verifiable results, building a small portfolio of RAG, integration, and end-to-end deployment case studies, and personalising referral and recruiter messages. It also cites salary data: roughly ₹10–30 LPA, with the market midpoint around ₹15–18 LPA, drawn from Glassdoor and Levels.fyi, while cautioning that samples are still small for this new title.

Key points

  • Employers seek both engineering skill and customer-facing problem solving.
  • Relevant openings appear under titles like Customer Engineer or Implementation Engineer.
  • Resumes should show concrete builds and substantiated outcomes.
  • Portfolios should include RAG, integration, and deployment case studies.
  • India FDE pay spans roughly ₹10–30 LPA, midpoint ₹15–18 LPA.

Key Insights

Getting shortlisted for a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role requires candidates to demonstrate both engineering ability and practical problem-solving.

  • Show more than technical knowledge: Employers look for experience with application development, APIs, AI implementation, cloud deployment and customer-facing problem-solving.
  • Search beyond the FDE title: Relevant openings may appear under titles such as Customer Engineer, AI Solutions Engineer, Implementation Engineer and Deployment Engineer. Check the actual responsibilities before applying.
  • Make your resume outcome-focused: Highlight what you built, the technical decisions you made, the systems you integrated and the results you can substantiate.
  • Build a portfolio with practical evidence: Projects involving RAG applications, API integrations and end-to-end deployment can demonstrate your ability to take an idea towards a working solution.
  • Personalise referrals and recruiter messages: Explain why a particular role interests you, mention relevant experience and make one clear request instead of sending generic messages.
  • Prepare for technical and customer-facing interviews: Practise coding, system integration, problem-solving and explaining technical trade-offs to non-technical stakeholders.
  • Use structured learning to strengthen your profile: The IIT Delhi Advanced Certificate in AI Forward Deployed Engineering covers relevant AI development and deployment capabilities, alongside practical portfolio projects.

Applying for a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role can be slightly different from applying for a conventional software engineering position. A recruiter may want to know whether you can write production-ready code, but that is only part of the picture. Can you connect unfamiliar systems? Have you deployed an application outside your local environment? Can you understand a customer’s problem when the requirements are incomplete?

Your resume needs to make these capabilities visible. A collection of programming languages and AI tools may not tell an employer what you can actually build. This guide explains how to find relevant FDE openings, present your experience, develop a convincing portfolio and approach recruiters or employees with a clear reason for getting in touch.

In this guide, we’ll cover how to find relevant FDE openings, tailor your resume, build a strong technical portfolio, approach recruiters and employees, and prepare for the interview process.

What do employers look for when shortlisting FDE candidates?

An FDE needs to move between engineering work and customer requirements. Employers may therefore look beyond coding ability when reviewing applications. They want to understand whether a candidate can take responsibility for a technical problem, work through practical constraints and deliver something that people can use.

Hiring signalWhat you should be able to demonstrate
Software engineeringWriting, testing, debugging and maintaining applications
Applied AI experienceBuilding LLM applications, RAG pipelines or agent workflows where relevant
APIs and integrationsConnecting applications, databases and external systems
Cloud and deploymentDeploying applications and troubleshooting them in real environments
Customer problem-solvingUnderstanding workflows and translating requirements into technical solutions
OwnershipTaking a project from initial problem definition through implementation and iteration
CommunicationExplaining technical decisions and working with different stakeholders
Comfort with ambiguityMaking progress when requirements, data or documentation are incomplete

The combination matters. A candidate might have strong Python skills but little experience working with existing systems. Another might understand enterprise software and customer requirements but have limited experience writing application code. An employer hiring an FDE may look for evidence of both.

Why technical skills alone may not be enough

An FDE does not always receive a neatly defined engineering ticket. A customer might say that an AI assistant is giving unreliable answers. Before changing the model or rewriting the application, the engineer may need to investigate the data, retrieval process, permissions and intended use.

Jove Zhong, Head of Forward Deployed Engineering at Cresta, has discussed the importance of hands-on engineering, production work and customer-facing ability in building an FDE team. His perspective is useful because it comes from someone responsible for the function, although individual employers will have their own hiring criteria.

For candidates, the practical takeaway is to show how you approach a problem, not just which tools you have used.

How to find relevant Forward Deployed Engineer jobs in India

Searching only for “Forward Deployed Engineer” can leave out relevant opportunities. Companies use different titles for roles that involve customer-facing software development, AI implementation and deployment.

Search beyond the exact FDE job title

Try searching for:

  • Forward Deployed Engineer
  • AI Forward Deployed Engineer
  • Customer Engineer
  • Implementation Engineer
  • Solutions Engineer
  • Field Engineer
  • AI Solutions Engineer
  • Deployment Engineer

These titles are not interchangeable. A Solutions Engineer position might focus mainly on pre-sales demonstrations, while an Implementation Engineer may concentrate on configuring an existing product. An FDE position generally involves a stronger software-building and technical delivery component.

Read the responsibilities before deciding whether a listing is relevant. Look for evidence that the engineer will write code, build integrations, work with customer data or deploy applications.

Where should you look?

Start with company career pages, LinkedIn Jobs and startup hiring platforms such as the Y Combinator jobs board. You can also follow engineering teams and AI companies on LinkedIn. Product announcements, engineering blogs and team updates sometimes reveal new projects or hiring needs before you find a listing under the exact FDE title.

Professional connections can help you discover opportunities too. Former colleagues, alumni and engineers working on enterprise AI products may know which teams are expanding.

Build a target-company list

Rather than applying to every role that mentions AI, create a shortlist of companies whose products and engineering work match your experience. Enterprise AI, AI infrastructure, data platforms, automation and industry-specific software companies are useful places to investigate.

Track the details in a simple spreadsheet:

CompanyRole titleRelevant requirementsContactApplication status
Company AAI Forward Deployed EngineerPython, APIs, RAGRecruiter identifiedTo apply
Company BCustomer EngineerCloud, integrations, SQLEmployee contactApplied
Company CImplementation EngineerPython, deployment, AI workflowsTalent teamFollow-up due

These are illustrative entries, not actual vacancies.

Include the job link, experience requirement, location, travel expectations and application date. This makes it easier to compare roles and follow up without losing track of conversations.

How to tailor your resume for an FDE role

An FDE resume should make two things easy to identify: what you can build and how you have used your engineering skills to solve a problem.

A conventional software engineering resume might focus on programming languages, frameworks and development responsibilities. Those details still matter, but an FDE application should also show integration work, deployment experience, project ownership and collaboration with users or stakeholders.

Keep the resume focused

A one- or two-page resume is usually sufficient, depending on your experience and the relevance of your work. Put your strongest engineering experience and projects near the top. Remove generic career objectives, long lists of unrelated tools and descriptions that do not help an employer understand your contribution.

Use the job description to decide which experience deserves more space. A role that emphasises RAG and enterprise integrations should bring those projects into focus. A position that involves cloud deployment may require more detail about infrastructure and production troubleshooting.

Include links to GitHub repositories, a portfolio or project demonstrations where they add useful evidence.

Show what you actually built

Resume bullets often become weak when they describe responsibilities without explaining the work. Compare these examples:

Generic resume bulletStronger FDE-oriented version
Built a Python application using APIs.Developed a Python service that connected three external APIs and brought the resulting data into a single workflow
Worked on an AI chatbot.Built a RAG-based support assistant using internal documentation and tested its responses against a defined evaluation set
Worked with cloud infrastructure.Deployed a containerised application to a cloud environment and configured logging, monitoring and access controls
Communicated with clients.Gathered workflow requirements from customer stakeholders and converted them into technical specifications for an application integration

The stronger versions tell the reader more about the work. Add measurable outcomes when you have evidence to support them. For example, you might mention the number of systems integrated, the size of a test set or the time saved by an automated workflow.

Do not invent percentages, efficiency gains or customer impact to make a bullet sound impressive. An interviewer may ask how you measured the result.

Organise your technical skills

Group your skills so a recruiter can scan them quickly:

  • Programming: Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, Java or C++, where relevant.
  • Backend and integration: APIs, SQL, databases and authentication.
  • AI engineering: LLM APIs, RAG, embeddings, agent frameworks and evaluation.
  • Deployment: Cloud platforms, Docker, CI/CD and monitoring.
  • Collaboration: Requirements gathering, technical documentation and stakeholder communication.

Only include tools you can explain and use. Listing a framework after completing one tutorial may invite questions you are not ready to answer.

Build a portfolio that proves you can do FDE work

A GitHub profile filled with tutorial exercises may demonstrate that you have been learning, but it does not always show how you approach an engineering problem. A useful FDE portfolio should make your decisions visible: what you set out to solve, how you built the application, what went wrong and how you tested the result.

You do not need a large collection of projects. Start with a few that demonstrate different parts of the work.

Three project ideas to include

1. An AI application that works with real data

Build a RAG application that retrieves information from a defined knowledge base. Test its responses against a set of questions and record where retrieval or answer quality falls short.

The point is to show that you understand more than how to call an LLM API. You should be able to explain how the data is prepared, how retrieval works and how you evaluated the output.

2. A system integration project

Choose an application that needs information from an external API or database. Connect the systems, handle authentication and account for failed requests or incomplete data.

Document how information moves between the components. Include the errors you encountered and what happens when an integration becomes unavailable.

3. An end-to-end deployment

Take an application and deploy it to a cloud environment. Add authentication, basic testing, logging and monitoring. Explain the architecture and the steps someone else would need to run the project.

These are suggested portfolio directions, not mandatory requirements for every employer.

Turn each project into a case study

A short case study can make a project much easier to assess. Include:

PartWhat to explain
ProblemWhat user or business issue were you addressing?
ConstraintsWhat limitations existed in the data, systems or environment?
ApproachWhich architecture, tools and technologies did you choose?
Your contributionWhat did you personally design and build?
ValidationHow did you test the solution?
OutcomeWhat changed, and how did you measure it?
DemonstrationWhere can someone view the code, screenshots or working demo?

Be precise about the project’s status. A prototype built for learning should be described as a prototype. Do not imply that it was deployed for a paying customer or used in production when it was not.

How to get referrals for FDE roles

A referral can help your application reach someone who understands the team and its requirements. It does not replace technical qualifications, and it is not a promise of an interview.

Start by identifying people who have a genuine connection to the role. Current FDEs, engineers working on the relevant product, team leads and alumni at the company are often more useful contacts than someone whose only connection is working at the same organisation.

Before contacting anyone, look at their work. Have they written about an AI deployment? Do they work with the product mentioned in the job listing? Is there a shared university, previous employer or professional interest you can refer to?

A good referral request usually follows a simple sequence:

  • Introduce yourself and explain how you found the person.
  • Mention the particular role and why it interests you.
  • Share one relevant project or experience.
  • Ask for advice or context before asking for a referral.
  • Share your resume and job link when appropriate.
  • Ask whether they would be comfortable referring you if they believe your background is suitable.

Avoid opening with “Please refer me.” The other person has no reason to assess your fit yet. Give them enough context to make that decision.

LinkedIn referral request template

Template: Employee referral request

Hi [Name],

I came across the [FDE Job Title] opening at [Company] and noticed that you work with the [team/product area].

My background is in [relevant technical area]. I recently worked on [brief project involving a relevant skill], which is closely related to the role.

Would you be comfortable sharing any advice about the position? And, if you think my experience could be a fit, would you consider referring me?

Job link: [URL]

Thank you for your time.

Recruiter outreach: How to message recruiters on LinkedIn

A recruiter message should explain why you are contacting that particular person. A long summary of your education, work history and technical stack is unlikely to help someone decide whether to respond.

Use this simple structure:

Personal context + role or company + relevant evidence + one clear question.

Find a recruiter who handles technical, software or AI hiring. Check their current role before messaging them. Then choose the approach that matches your situation.

Template 1: LinkedIn connection request

LinkedIn currently allows Basic members to add a personalised note to up to three connection requests per month. Each note can be up to 200 characters. Premium members can add personalised notes to unlimited connection requests. Keep your message focused on why you want to connect.

Hi [Name], I came across your work at [Company] while exploring FDE roles. I have experience in [relevant area] and would be glad to connect and learn about your team’s work.

Template 2: Recruiter message after applying

Use this when you have already submitted an application. Include the job title and, where available, its reference number.

Subject: Application for [FDE Job Title]

Hi [Recruiter Name],

I recently applied for the [FDE Job Title] position at [Company] ([Job ID, if available]).

My experience includes [relevant technical skills], and I recently [describe one relevant project or achievement]. I am particularly interested in the role because of its focus on [specific product, customer problem or deployment area].

I wanted to introduce myself and express my interest. Would you be able to share whether my background aligns with the position?

Thank you,

[Your Name]
[LinkedIn / Portfolio URL]

Template 3: Cold recruiter outreach before applying

Use this when you have identified a company of interest but have not found a suitable vacancy, or want to ask about upcoming opportunities.

Hi [Recruiter Name],

I’ve been following [Company]’s work on [specific product or technical area]. I’m a [current role/background] with experience in [two relevant skills], including [brief project or achievement].

I’m interested in FDE opportunities that combine software development, AI integration and customer-focused problem-solving.

Are there any current or upcoming roles that might align with my background?

Best,
[Your Name]
[LinkedIn / Portfolio URL]

What makes an FDE outreach message effective?

A useful message gives the recipient a reason to take it seriously without asking them to read your entire resume.

Mention one or two relevant skills. A specific project is more convincing than a list of ten technologies. Explain why the company or role caught your attention, then finish with a question that is easy to answer.

Do not send the same message to every recruiter. A note about enterprise integrations will make more sense to someone hiring for a deployment team than to a recruiter working exclusively on unrelated roles.

How to follow up after applying for an FDE role

First, check the application instructions. Some companies provide a response timeline or specifically ask candidates not to contact the hiring team. Follow those directions.

When no timeline is given, wait a reasonable period before following up. Around a week after applying can be a sensible starting point, although hiring schedules vary. Use the original message thread where possible, identify the role and application date, and keep the request brief.

A follow-up is more useful when it includes something new, such as a relevant portfolio update or project you have completed since applying.

Post-application follow-up template

Hi [Name],

I wanted to follow up on my application for the [FDE Job Title] position, submitted on [Date].

I’m still interested in the opportunity, particularly because of [specific product, team or technical challenge]. Since applying, I’ve also [completed a relevant project / added a portfolio demonstration, if applicable].

Please let me know if any additional information would be helpful.

Thank you for your time,
[Your Name]

One polite follow-up is generally enough. If there is no response, continue exploring other openings rather than repeatedly messaging the same person or several members of the same hiring team.

What does the FDE hiring and interview process look like?

There is no standard FDE interview sequence. Some employers use a conventional software engineering process with additional customer-facing questions. Others include a practical integration task or ask candidates to work through an unfamiliar business problem.

A possible process might look like this:

StageWhat may be assessedHow to prepare
Resume screeningRelevant engineering work, technical skills and project evidenceMake your contribution and results clear
Recruiter conversationMotivation, experience, communication and role expectationsExplain why you are interested in FDE work
Coding assessmentProgramming fundamentals and debuggingPractise writing and explaining code
Technical interviewAPIs, databases, cloud, system design and AI applicationsReview the technologies in the job description
Practical integration taskConnecting systems and working with imperfect dataBuild and explain end-to-end projects
Customer or case interviewProblem discovery, ambiguity and technical trade-offsPractise turning an unclear request into a technical plan
Final discussionOwnership, collaboration and working conditionsPrepare examples of handling challenges and stakeholder expectations

Paraform’s 2026 guide to hiring Forward Deployed Engineers identifies practical integration tasks, constrained system design, problem decomposition and behavioural assessment as possible parts of the interview process. These are examples of what candidates may encounter, not a fixed sequence followed by every employer.

Prepare for customer-facing questions

Technical interviews are not always limited to code. You may be asked how you would approach a customer problem when the available information is incomplete.

Practise questions such as:

  • How would you approach a customer who cannot clearly explain the problem?
  • What would you do if the customer’s existing system does not support your proposed integration?
  • How would you explain a technical limitation to a non-technical stakeholder?
  • How would you handle a change in requirements during deployment?
  • How would you decide whether an AI-generated output is reliable enough to use?

You do not need to memorise scripted answers. Work through the situation aloud. Explain what you would ask first, what information you would need, which options you would consider and how you would communicate the trade-offs.

Be ready to explain your projects

A project on your resume can lead to questions about almost every decision you made. Why did you choose that database? What happened when the API failed? How did you evaluate the model? What would you change if the application had to support more users?

Revisit your own code before an interview. Be prepared to explain its architecture, limitations, testing approach and your individual contribution. Knowing a framework’s name is not the same as understanding why you used it.

How to become a Forward Deployed Engineer: A practical preparation roadmap

The route into FDE work depends on where you are starting. A software developer may already be comfortable building applications but need experience with customers and enterprise integrations. A DevOps engineer may understand deployment but need to write more application code. An early-career candidate may need to develop both foundations.

A useful progression is:

  • Strengthen programming fundamentals: Learn Python and build applications you can explain and maintain.
  • Work with APIs and data: Practise connecting systems, using SQL and handling incomplete information.
  • Explore applied AI: Build an LLM application and learn RAG, agent workflows and evaluation.
  • Learn deployment: Use cloud platforms, containers, testing and monitoring.
  • Complete end-to-end projects: Demonstrate how you take a problem from requirements to a working application.
  • Practise customer communication: Explain decisions, gather requirements and discuss limitations.
  • Apply selectively: Match your resume and portfolio to the role rather than applying with the same documents everywhere.

You do not need to master every technology before applying. Focus on the requirements that appear repeatedly in the roles you are targeting, and be honest about the areas where you are still learning.

Conclusion

Getting shortlisted for an FDE role is not about using the right job title or sending the largest number of LinkedIn messages. It is about making your engineering experience easy to assess. Show what you have built, explain how it works, demonstrate that you can handle integrations and deployment, and connect your experience to the employer’s actual requirements. A focused resume, credible portfolio and thoughtful outreach give recruiters a clearer reason to consider your application.

Frequently asked questions

1. How do I get my first FDE interview?

Start with openings that match your current technical experience. Tailor your resume to show relevant engineering work, integrations, AI projects and deployment experience. A portfolio with clear project explanations can help recruiters assess your capabilities, while targeted outreach may help you connect with people familiar with the team.

2. Can freshers apply for Forward Deployed Engineer roles?

Some employers may consider early-career candidates, while others expect previous professional engineering experience. Freshers should check each vacancy’s eligibility requirements and demonstrate practical ability through substantial projects, internships and deployed applications where possible.

3. What should I include in an FDE resume?

Include relevant programming languages, APIs, cloud platforms, AI tools, projects and deployment experience. Explain what you personally built, which technical decisions you made and what outcomes you can verify. Add GitHub or portfolio links when they provide useful evidence.

4. Do FDE roles require customer-facing experience?

Not every employer requires previous customer-facing experience, but communication and stakeholder collaboration are important in many FDE positions. Candidates can show related experience through internships, consulting projects, cross-functional work, user research and presentations of technical projects.

5. Are Forward Deployed Engineer jobs available in India?

FDE-related openings can appear in India, but availability changes with employers and hiring cycles. Search for the exact FDE title as well as adjacent roles such as AI Solutions Engineer, Customer Engineer and Implementation Engineer. Check that the responsibilities include technical implementation and deployment rather than only pre-sales or support.

6. Should I message a recruiter before or after applying?

Either approach can work. When a relevant vacancy is already open, applying first gives the recruiter a specific application to identify. A short message afterwards can explain why your experience matches the position. When you are exploring future opportunities, an introductory message about your background and interest in the company may be more appropriate.

7. How many projects should an FDE portfolio contain?

There is no fixed requirement. A few well-documented projects that demonstrate application development, integrations, AI implementation and deployment are more useful than a large collection of unfinished or repetitive projects.

8. What should I do if a recruiter does not respond?

Respect the employer’s stated timeline. When none is provided, send one short follow-up after a reasonable interval. If there is still no response, continue applying elsewhere instead of repeatedly contacting the same person.

9. Are FDE interviews different from software engineering interviews?

They can be. Alongside coding and technical assessments, some employers evaluate system integration, problem decomposition, customer communication and the ability to work with incomplete requirements. The exact process varies by company.

10. Can a certification help me get shortlisted for an FDE role?

A relevant programme can provide structured learning and opportunities to build technical projects. However, employers may also assess coding ability, integration experience, deployment work and communication. A certificate should complement demonstrable engineering capability, not replace it.
