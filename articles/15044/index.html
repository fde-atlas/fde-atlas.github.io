Running discovery that finds the real bottleneck · Discovery & Customer Craft

Clique Media Group Pty Ltd · 1 min read

Summary

This short lesson argues that a problem statement handed to a Forward Deployed Engineer by a customer engineer is almost never the real problem—it has usually been filtered through managers, planning docs, and personal theories. The author gives an example: a request for Cursor to auto-review PRs may actually stem from review queues taking four days because two staff engineers are the only approvers. Automating the wrong thing adds noise to an already constrained queue. The core skill is locating and proving the real bottleneck before proposing any work, and in decomposition interviews, scoping rather than leaping into design is the key filter.

Key points

  • A customer's stated problem is a symptom, not the real bottleneck.
  • Auto-review requests may mask approval bottlenecks like overloaded approvers.
  • Automating the wrong step adds noise to an already constrained queue.
  • FDEs must locate and prove the bottleneck before building anything.
  • Decomposition interviews test whether candidates scope or leap into design.

Step 1 of 2

A customer engineer will hand you a problem statement. It is almost never the real problem. Your first job as a Forward Deployed EngineerAn engineer who embeds inside a customer's own engineering org to scope ambiguous problems and ship production-grade Cursor workflows a senior engineer keeps using after they leave. Press Enter for the full definition. is to find the bottleneck they can't see, then prove you found it before you propose a single line of work.

The stated ask is a symptom that already passed through a manager, a planning doc and someone's pet theory. "We want Cursor to auto-review PRs" might really mean review takes four days because two staff engineers are the only approvers and they're underwater. Auto-review the wrong way and you've added noise to a queue that was already the constraint.

The interviewer for the decomposition round is watching for one thing above all: do you scope or do you leap? An FDE who restates the brief and starts designing has already failed the most important filter. The one who says "before I build anything, I need to understand where time actually goes" has passed it.
