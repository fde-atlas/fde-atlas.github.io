AWS SAA-C03 Course Study Order: FDE Should Learn IAM First

Original title: AWS SAA-C03 课程学习顺序：FDE 先学 IAM

FDE Times · 2026-11-13 · 4 min read

Summary

This piece reviews Stéphane Maarek's Udemy course for the AWS SAA-C03 exam (about 27.5 hours, 399 lectures) and proposes a study order tailored to forward deployed engineers. Since Security Architecture carries the highest exam weight at 30% and is what clients scrutinize first, the author recommends starting with IAM, KMS, and SSM before data services, EC2, and Serverless. A sample least-privilege IAM policy for an agent reading S3 and writing to RDS illustrates real client work. The article closes by urging readers to pair the certification with a hands-on Free Tier project on GitHub.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • Stéphane Maarek's course runs about 27 hours 25 minutes, requires no AWS background, and its hands-on exercises use the Free Tier.
  • Security accounts for 30% of the total SAA-C03 score, the highest of the four domains, so an FDE should study IAM, KMS, and SSM first.
  • AWS expects candidates to have at least one year of hands-on experience designing solutions; watching videos alone is far from enough.

The course runs 27 hours 25 minutes, with 399 lectures across 33 sections. If you are getting access to a customer's AWS account next week, the real question is not whether you can finish it, but which part to study first.

Stéphane Maarek's "Ultimate AWS Certified Solutions Architect Associate 2026" on Udemy aims to help learners pass the SAA-C03 exam. But for a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), its value lies elsewhere: it covers exactly the services you touch when deploying on someone else's infrastructure.

This matters. At a customer, you cannot set up the environment to your own liking. You first have to understand the systems already running, request the right permissions, and then propose changes the customer's infrastructure team is willing to approve.

Who Teaches It, and What Does It Cover?

On his Udemy instructor profile, Maarek describes himself as a solutions architect, consultant, and software developer. He is an AWS Hero, holds the Solutions Architect Professional and DevOps Professional certifications, and is a co-founder of Conduktor. According to the course page as of November 13, 2026, the course has a 4.7 rating, roughly 301,000 reviews, and about 1.36 million students.

The course requires no AWS background, its exercises use the Free Tier, and it suits software engineers new to the cloud. The first sections cover IAM & AWS CLI, EC2, ELB and Auto Scaling, RDS, and Aurora and ElastiCache, followed by Route 53.

Later sections expand to S3, Serverless (Lambda, DynamoDB, Cognito, API Gateway), Well-Architected, disaster recovery, and security content covering KMS, IAM, and SSM. Nearly everything you encounter when opening a customer console is on this list.

The Exam Itself Already Tells You What to Study First

AWS's official exam guide states that the SAA-C03 tests the ability to design solutions based on the Well-Architected Framework. In other words, it tests design ability, not just operations. The exam has four domains: Design Secure Architectures at 30%, Design Resilient Architectures at 26%, Design High-Performing Architectures at 24%, and Design Cost-Optimized Architectures at 20%.

Do the math: the exam has 50 scored questions, of which about 15 relate to security. The total is 65 questions (including 15 unscored ones), with a 130-minute time limit, averaging about 2 minutes per question. Scores range from 100 to 1,000, with 720 to pass, using compensatory scoring with no minimum score per domain.

The exam costs 150 USD; candidates holding a valid AWS certification get 50% off their next exam.

Compensatory scoring means you can pass even with a weak area. But an FDE should not study just to scrape together enough points, because the domain weighted most heavily on the exam is precisely the part customers scrutinize most.

Imagine Your First Day at a Customer

Suppose the customer is an insurance company that wants an agent to read documents in S3 and write results to RDS. Before a single line of code runs, you need three things: an IAM role that can read the specified bucket, permission to use the KMS key that encrypts that bucket, and a way to obtain the database password without hardcoding it.

All three tasks fall in the security domain: IAM, KMS, and SSM. The policy attached to the agent's role can be trimmed down to this (bucket, key, and parameter names are examples):

{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [
    {
      "Effect": "Allow",
      "Action": "s3:GetObject",
      "Resource": "arn:aws:s3:::claims-docs/*"
    },
    {
      "Effect": "Allow",
      "Action": "kms:Decrypt",
      "Resource": "arn:aws:kms:ap-southeast-1:111122223333:key/claims-key-id"
    },
    {
      "Effect": "Allow",
      "Action": "ssm:GetParameter",
      "Resource": "arn:aws:ssm:ap-southeast-1:111122223333:parameter/claims-agent/db-password"
    }
  ]
}

Note what is not here: no

s3:*

, no

"Resource": "*"

, and no permission to write to the bucket.

So the sensible study order for an FDE differs from the course's own order. Start with IAM & AWS CLI, then jump straight to the sections on KMS, SSM, and VPC security.

Next comes data (S3, RDS, Aurora, ElastiCache), then EC2 plus ELB and Auto Scaling. After that, Serverless, with Well-Architected and disaster recovery last, by which time you know enough to understand why an architecture is designed the way it is.

Three Things Worth Remembering from the Course

First, access is architecture too. For an FDE, the 30% security weighting is a reminder: overly broad policies make it easy for your changes to get blocked at the customer.

The second point is written right in the exam guide: the exam also tests reviewing existing solutions and proposing improvements. This is very close to an FDE's work in customer environments—the system was running before you arrived, and your job is to point out what should change.

The third is the course's own limitation. AWS says the ideal candidate has at least one year of hands-on experience designing solutions on AWS. Watching all 399 lectures cannot replace building a system yourself, breaking it, and fixing it.

Turn the Certificate into Proof

AWS positions this certification as a way to boost holders' confidence. The advice here is not to stop at the certificate: pair it with a lab project you can explain line by line.

Rebuild the insurance company scenario above on the Free Tier, put it on GitHub, and include an architecture diagram, a policy like the one above, and a short explanation of why each permission is necessary.

On your resume, list the SAA-C03 certification together with a link to that repository. In interviews, describe a time you understood an existing architecture and proposed three changes.

Maarek's course can get you into the exam room. But earning a customer's trust in their AWS account starts with requesting only the necessary permissions the first time—and not one more.
