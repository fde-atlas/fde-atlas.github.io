Author: Rita

Goldman Sachs maintains a neutral rating on Palantir with a 12-month price target of $204, implying 7.6% upside. In a report released on September 28, 2026, Goldman Sachs noted that it held an investor meeting in San Francisco on September 11 for Palantir Chief Financial Officer Dave Glazer. The market holds two major debates regarding the Frontline Deployment Engineer (FDE) moat and Total Addressable Market (TAM) depth; Goldman Sachs believes Palantir’s FDE flywheel and productization engine may offer greater stickiness than competitors’ add-on strategies.

Goldman Sachs analyst Gabriela Borges pointed out that Palantir has spent years converting client-specific workflows into reusable software, a flywheel that is now further extended through AI FDE and AIP Evolve. After exploring this growth strategy, a large potential competitor has dropped out of direct competition with Palantir. Palantir is still in the early stages of its verticalization strategy, with three areas warranting attention: financial services, semiconductors, and next-gen cloud.

The FDE Moat Is Deeper Than Expected

Palantir’s FDE moat is more sticky and complex than simply deploying FDEs. Palantir describes a virtuous cycle where FDEs build client-specific workflows, product engineers standardize successful patterns, and these capabilities are then widely rolled out across the platform. Palantir believes this does not erode client advantages because the value resides in the client’s proprietary data, operational logic, and ontology.

This mechanism is being amplified by AIP Evolve (Palantir’s AI agent coordination platform), used by approximately 97% of clients. AIP Evolve coordinates clusters of AI FDE agents to automatically analyze workflows, test changes, evaluate models, optimize prompts, reduce compute costs, and improve performance. Acting as an AI-driven FDE, the AI FDE can build and maintain ontologies, write transformations, manage repositories, and execute Foundry operations via natural language commands. Palantir is increasingly embedding traditional FDE functions into software, enabling deployment knowledge from hundreds of client projects to scale beyond the capacity of existing FDE headcount.

Competition Comes from Internal IT; Model Vendors Are Not Direct Competitors

In terms of the competitive landscape, foundational model vendors focus on selling intelligence and token consumption, whereas Palantir focuses on operating AI within client workflows. Palantir positions Snowflake and Databricks as complementary data infrastructure partners responsible for storing, processing, and governing data, while Palantir’s ontology sits atop the data layer, transforming data into operational objects, workflows, decisions, and actions. Management frames these relationships as complementary rather than competitive, and clients frequently deploy Palantir alongside their existing data platforms.

A more common alternative involves companies piecing together models, data platforms, governance tools, and custom applications internally, facilitated by their own IT organizations or consulting firms and system integrators. Near-term growth opportunities prioritize depth over breadth, expanding wallet share among existing clients by identifying new workflows. Contracts typically last around three years, and clients often expand after approximately 18 months of deployment because Palantir already possesses the underlying datasets, business logic, and ontology, making the unit economics highly attractive.

Three Key Directions for Verticalization Warrant Attention

Regarding verticalization, Palantir is implementing industry-specific strategies, with three new areas drawing attention. In financial services, former AIG CEO Peter Zaffino will join in January 2027, providing another senior enterprise-level voice at Palantir to articulate the value proposition to prospective clients. Management emphasizes his background leading large, highly regulated institutions and his hands-on experience in deploying platforms at scale.

In the semiconductor sector, the partnership with NVIDIA can open doors to this vertical. For next-gen cloud, the collaboration with Nebius similarly unlocks opportunities in that vertical. Regarding the US government vertical, Palantir acknowledges investor concerns about the November administration change but remains optimistic about US government opportunities, viewing them as independent of short-term political agendas. Conversations by Goldman Sachs with multiple procurement experts in Washington indicate that Palantir maintains close ties with both parties, and technological infrastructure modernization has been a priority across the past two administrations.

Platform Stickiness Derives from Capabilities, Not Technical Lock-in

Approximately 97% of clients use AIP Evolve, contracts typically run for about three years, and clients often expand after roughly 18 months of deployment. Palantir emphasizes the platform's portability, noting that clients can switch if they wish. However, over time, the ontology becomes a digital representation of how the business operates, embedded into workflows, decision logic, institutional knowledge, and operational processes. Clients stay because leaving would mean abandoning features already embedded in daily operations, along with operational leverage and business outcomes.

Goldman Sachs maintains a neutral rating on Palantir with a $204 price target based on estimated free cash flow for quarters 5 through 8 valued at a 60x multiple. Risks include commercial momentum slowing due to macroeconomic weakness, intensifying competitive dynamics, and an increase in off-the-shelf AI solutions; upside risks include tailwinds in the government sector driving revenue acceleration and strong hiring trends for commercial sales representatives yielding above-consensus growth.
