How Ramp Hires FDEs: Coding Bar, Drive, Empathy

FDE Times · 2026-11-18 · 6 min read

Summary

Ramp's engineering blog explains its FDE hiring philosophy: technical skill is only a bar to clear, and the company does not reward scores beyond it. The real differentiators are drive, customer empathy, and communication, with drive described as the best predictor of real-world performance. Comparing the Senior FDE and the 2026 AI Solutions postings, the article shows two variants of the role—customer integrations versus LLM systems with agents, RAG, evals, and monitoring—and offers concrete preparation advice: build a small AI project with evals and prepare discovery stories showing initiative, empathy, and clear communication.

Key points

  • Ramp treats coding ability as a threshold, not a ranking metric.
  • Drive is cited as the best predictor of on-the-job performance.
  • Customer empathy and communication are separate, equally weighted criteria.
  • The 2026 AI Solutions role requires agents, RAG, evals, and monitoring experience.
  • Candidates should prepare discovery stories and an AI project with evals rather than grind algorithms.

Candidates who scrape through Ramp's coding round still become outstanding FDEs, and the company has spelled out where it actually does its scoring.

In brief

  • Ramp treats technical ability as a bar to clear and does not optimize beyond it.
  • Drive and hard work are the best predictor of real-world performance; customer empathy and communication are two separate criteria of equal weight.
  • The 2026 AI Solutions posting asks for experience building agents, RAG, evals and monitoring, in a role that works very closely with customers.

On its engineering blog, Ramp admits something few companies will. Some candidates scrape through the coding interview with a lukewarm result, the “yes, but with hesitation” kind, and go on to become outstanding FDEs.

If you are planning to grind more hard problems before an interview, that detail deserves a close read. Ramp says it treats technical ability as a bar to clear, and avoids optimizing beyond that bar.

So where should the rest of your preparation time go? What Ramp has written about its hiring criteria, its two FDE job postings and an independent analysis of 14 FDE postings all point the same way. What separates candidates is drive, customer empathy and communication.

The technical bar still has to be cleared cleanly

A “bar” does not mean easy. Ramp writes that it tests the fundamentals: writing code, debugging, data structures and system design. The company does not describe each interview round, so do not guess at the format; make sure you are solid in all four areas.

What Ramp adds is that once a candidate clears the bar, it does not optimize for a higher score. For candidates, clearing it by a wider margin will most likely earn nothing extra.

This is where many strong engineers make life hard for themselves. They pour the final week into more hard problems, while the deciding factor turns out to be the stories they never prepared. If you are confident you can pass the fundamentals, spend your practice time on everything else.

The best predictor is drive

Ramp splits its FDE hiring criteria into four separate parts: drive and hard work, technical fundamentals, customer empathy, and communication. On drive specifically, Ramp writes that it is the best predictor it has found of real-world performance.

The reason is plain from the job description. The 2026 AI Solutions posting calls the role highly customer-facing and hands-on, and asks for comfort with ambiguity. In a position like that, nobody is likely to hand you work ticket by ticket.

You do not need to have founded a company to show drive. A side project with real users, an internal tool you proposed and pushed until the whole team used it, a time you went to meet users yourself to understand why they abandoned a feature: each demonstrates the same quality.

On your resume, describe them with the verbs of someone who initiates, such as “proposed,” “persuaded,” “shipped to users.” Go easy on passive words like “participated” or “supported.”

Empathy and communication stand side by side

Ramp defines customer empathy as the ability to understand what customers are feeling, paired with emotional intelligence. Communication is a separate criterion of equal weight: the company wants people who listen well and present their thinking clearly and persuasively.

A Paraform guide to FDE hiring also lists the two side by side in Ramp’s criteria, neither ranked above the other. Ramp is not alone, either. Interview Kickstart’s analysis of 14 FDE postings found customer discovery explicitly named in 11, while stakeholder communication appeared more often than many technical skills.

In other words, Ramp has written down what most FDE postings in that sample ask for. To see how the two criteria work in practice, follow a concrete case.

Take a hypothetical situation. A customer’s finance team asks for “an agent that automatically reconciles every invoice against the ERP.” Accept that at face value and you have taken on a months-long project with dozens of edge cases.

A good FDE asks questions before writing code. How many hours a week does reconciliation take now, which invoice types eat most of that time, who handles the mismatches, and what does a single error cost?

Quite possibly the answers shrink the problem to one supplier, one document type and one approval flow, small enough to run for real before expanding. Tell a story like that in the interview room and the listener hears all three things Ramp hires for at once: the drive to pull the work forward, the empathy in the questions, and the communication in the retelling.

Two postings, two kinds of work

Ramp looks for the same kind of person, but its FDE work comes in more than one form. Set the Senior FDE posting beside the FDE AI Solutions posting from July 29, 2026, and you see one role with two different centers of gravity.

Aspect Senior FDE posting FDE AI Solutions posting (2026)
Place on the team Technical expert for the largest accounts Highly customer-facing, hands-on role, based in New York
Main work Building and deploying new integrations for customers Building LLM systems including agents, RAG, evals and monitoring
Systems to work with The ERP and HRIS the customer already uses The posting does not name customer systems
Requirements beyond code A strong customer experience from first contact to activation Comfort with ambiguity, strong written communication
Travel Not stated in the excerpt Occasional travel, depending on the project

The detail worth noting in the Senior posting is the customer journey it covers, running from first contact, through contract signing, to onboarding and activation. That means FDEs are involved before the customer signs, at a stage where listening matters as much as integration skills.

The AI Solutions posting leans toward AI products. Aced, a job aggregator, likewise describes Ramp’s FDE team as one that designs end-to-end AI solutions for its largest enterprise customers.

How to prepare if you are aiming for Ramp

Start with the technical bar, but put a time limit on it. Beyond the four fundamentals, the AI Solutions role needs evidence that you have built an agent or RAG system with evals and monitoring attached.

A sample project that automates a repetitive accounting task, with an eval suite recording where the agent went wrong and how you fixed it, says more than ten algorithm problems. It touches both financial data integration and the exact list of LLM skills the posting names.

Most of the remaining time should go into two or three tightly told discovery stories. Each needs an initial request, the questions you asked back, the final scope and the result. Practice telling them aloud, then write up a one-page design doc, because the posting asks for written communication too.

When you read an FDE posting at any company, look for three things. Whether they treat technical skill as a bar or a race, whether the main work is integration or AI products, and at what point on the customer journey the FDE gets involved. Those three answers decide what you should practice.

Ramp has made its selection criteria public. The advantage goes to candidates willing to practice the parts that never appear in a coding problem.
