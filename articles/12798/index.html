FDE or OPC? Two Career Paths for Individuals in the AI Era

Original title: FDE还是OPC？AI时代个人的两条出路

53AI · 2026-06-01 · 4 min read

Summary

This article compares two career paths for individuals in the AI era: becoming a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) hired by vendors like OpenAI or Anthropic at salaries of $500k–$1M to embed at customer sites, or running a One Person Company (OPC) that uses AI tools to handle sales, delivery, and operations solo. The author argues the real difference is risk preference and value ownership, not technical skill: FDEs trade time for stable pay and platform backing but stay dependent on their employer, while OPCs face volatile income with no ceiling and keep their brand equity. It suggests choosing based on temperament, or taking a middle path: work as an FDE for about three years, then go independent with accumulated know-how and credentials.

Key points

  • FDEs earn $500k–$1M salaries embedded at major clients.
  • FDE value depends on the vendor's brand; personal IP is hard to build.
  • OPCs run everything solo, with volatile income but no ceiling.
  • The core divide is risk appetite and who owns the value created.
  • Suggested path: three years as an FDE, then transition to OPC.

Full text

Introduction

In our previous article, "Presence Is the Moat: The FDE War Behind OpenAI's Acquisition of Tomoro," we took a deep dive into the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), a role that is reshaping the landscape of large model deployment. Today, let's place the FDE in a broader context and explore: what is the essential difference between it and the OPC (One Person Company) we discussed before? When the "organizational tentacle" collides with the "super individual," how should individuals at the center of the AI storm make career choices that maximize their interests?

Two Paths

In the AI era, the divergence in individuals' ability to monetize is more dramatic than in any other era.

On one side, Silicon Valley giants like OpenAI and Anthropic are frantically hiring FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers), offering annual salaries of $500,000 to $1 million and requiring you to essentially "live inside" client companies. On the other side, countless "One Person Companies" (OPCs) are quietly rising—one person taking on projects, delivering, and operating with AI tools, where earning a million a year is no longer a tech myth.

FDEs and OPCs both appear to be raising the water level of AI adoption, but in essence they represent two completely different survival philosophies for individuals.

FDE: The Organization's Tentacle

The FDE is the "elite technical mercenary" of the AI era, trading personal time for high-certainty, substantial income.

You are hired by a leading vendor like OpenAI or Anthropic and dispatched long-term to key client sites. Your task is to help clients stitch large model capabilities into their intricate business processes—tuning prompts, orchestrating agent workflows, doing system integration, and even driving organizational change. You earn a top-tier salary from a major company, wear a client's temporary badge, and navigate between two giants.

Income model: Fixed annual salary of $500,000 to $1 million plus equity incentives, with extremely stable cash flow.

Skill requirements: Technical depth + business understanding + cross-organizational communication. You need to understand both the underlying models and the client's outdated ERP systems, while also being able to hold meetings with business departments and coordinate interests with IT departments.

Core characteristics:

  • Strong dependence — Your core value is built on the vendor's brand premium and platform resources, making it difficult to visibly accumulate a personal brand.
  • Deep binding — Long-term deployment with a single top client gives you extremely deep industry know-how, but your technical vision is easily constrained by a single scenario.
  • Risk resistance — You enjoy the endorsement and benefits of a top-tier company and are relatively safe during economic downturns, but your career ceiling is clearly visible.

The FDE path suits people with solid technical skills who enjoy solving complex engineering problems, have a low tolerance for uncertainty, and are willing to trade time assets for stable, high returns.

OPC: A One-Person Company

The OPC is the "super individual" of the AI era, using technological leverage to unlock unlimited possibilities.

You don't belong to any organization; you alone are the brand, sales, delivery, and after-sales service. You use AI tools to amplify your personal productivity tenfold or even a hundredfold—using ChatGPT to write business proposals, Claude Code to architect code, and Midjourney to generate visuals, while you retreat behind the scenes as the "super curator," controlling quality and liaising with clients.

Income model: Project-based fees + subscription services + monetizing knowledge assets. Income fluctuates greatly, but there is no ceiling.

Skill requirements: Technical breadth + business acumen + extremely strong self-management. You need to keep up with the latest AI tech stack while also mastering pricing strategy, legal contracts, customer psychology, and personal brand operations.

  • Weak dependence — Your value is built entirely on your personal brand and track record of past deliveries. Clients trust you as a person, not the logo behind you.
  • Breadth first — Serving multiple clients simultaneously, drawing nourishment across industries, with a strong capacity for evolution and rapid iteration.
  • High risk, high reward — No base salary safety net when you wake up, but because you've completely shed organizational friction, your productivity ceiling is far higher than an FDE's.

The OPC path suits people with broad technical skills, business intuition, a desire for absolute freedom, the ability to embrace uncertainty, and strong self-drive.

The Essential Difference

The divide between FDE and OPC has never been about technical ability, but about risk preference and where your value belongs.

Time vs. leverage: An FDE sells "premium time," packaging 8 hours a day at a high price for clients, with income linearly tied to time. An OPC sells "delivered results," using AI agents to compress delivery time to the extreme, infinitely amplifying output per unit of time.

Depth vs. breadth: An FDE digs deep into one client for three years, becoming a veteran expert in AI transformation for a specific industry; an OPC serves ten clients a year, becoming a "specialist among generalists" through cross-industry collisions.

Security vs. freedom: An FDE has a major company for shelter—clients are provided, contracts are signed by the compliance department, and you just focus on doing the work; an OPC relies on themselves for everything, from finding clients and negotiating prices to chasing payments and handling after-sales—you must become a one-person army.

Brand ownership: An FDE's success ultimately becomes a case study for OpenAI or Anthropic, and you can't take the clients with you when you leave; an OPC's success is painted stroke by stroke onto their own résumé—clients trust your face, and wherever you go, your assets go with you.

How to Choose

There is no standard answer, only whether a niche fits.

  • Choose FDE: If you have specialized technical expertise, demand income certainty, are bad at or averse to sales, and want to leverage a major company's platform to access top-tier clients and challenges at the Fortune 500 level.
  • Choose OPC: If you have well-rounded technical skills, sharp business instincts, can tolerate seasonal income fluctuations, and dream of building a personal business empire that truly belongs to you.

Of course, there is also a middle path with minimal risk: become an FDE first, then transition to OPC.

Enter the industry's top clients as an FDE at a leading company, accumulating industry know-how and elite connections on the best platform with the highest salary. Three years later, strike out on your own armed with deep client understanding and impeccable success stories. Using other people's money to hone your own skills—this is the most elegant path to a dimensional strike.

Conclusion

Whether FDE or OPC, the core competitiveness of individuals in the AI era is, in essence, "translation ability."

Translating cold technical language into vivid business language, and translating a model's novel capabilities into commercial value for clients. The FDE is a giant's translator; the OPC is their own translator.

When AI completely erases the boundary between "one person" and "one company," the real divide is no longer where you clock in, but whether you possess the ability to independently deliver complete value.

For those who have this ability, choosing FDE or OPC is merely a matter of which trump card to play.
