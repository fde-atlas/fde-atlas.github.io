OpenAI invests $4 billion to compete for the enterprise market, dispatching engineers to help customers deploy AI

Original title: OpenAI 投資 40 億美元搶企業市場，派駐工程師協助客戶部署 AI

Full text

OpenAI is accelerating its push into the enterprise market, announcing the formation of a new company with over $4 billion in capital to help organizations adopt and scale AI applications, while also staffing this new venture through the acquisition of Tomoro.

The new company, named OpenAI Deployment Company, and its business will specialize in Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) who are embedded within customer companies to help them tackle complex problems in demanding environments. These FDEs work closely with business leaders, operations staff, and frontline teams at customer companies to identify where AI can deliver the greatest benefits, redesign the organization's infrastructure and workflows around AI, and turn these results into robust, long-term systems.

Through its newly acquired Tomoro, an applied AI consulting and engineering firm, OpenAI has given OpenAI Deployment Company approximately 150 FDEs and experts, who will spring into action as soon as the new venture launches. Tomoro established a partnership with OpenAI back in 2023, and its clients include Mattel, Red Bull, Tesco, and Virgin Atlantic.

Nineteen investment firms, consulting agencies, and system integrators have already joined in partnership with OpenAI's new venture. OpenAI said the new venture is led by TPG as the lead investor, with Advent, Bain Capital, and Brookfield as co-lead partners. The overall structure is a long-term committed partnership, with OpenAI holding majority equity and control in the new company.

After early success with its consumer-oriented products, OpenAI is now actively expanding its enterprise business. Rival Anthropic has won favor with its Claude models, establishing a strong position in the enterprise and developer markets.

OpenAI's focus this year has narrowed to enterprise customers and coding tools—an implicit admission that its previous strategy of broadly rolling out products has ceded some ground to Anthropic. At a company-wide all-hands meeting, OpenAI's Chief Executive of Applications, Fidji Simo, told employees that Anthropic's progress should serve as a "wake-up call," and said OpenAI must "deliver on productivity" to serve enterprise customers. "We can't afford to miss this opportunity by getting distracted by side quests," she told employees.
