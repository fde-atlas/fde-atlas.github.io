Forward deployed engineers arrived in 2026 to help deliver on AI’s promises

Channel Dive · 2026-11-25 · 2 min read

Summary

This Channel Dive roundup traces how "forward deployed engineer" went from Palantir jargon to one of enterprise AI's hottest job titles during 2026. OpenAI put $4 billion into a deployment company and acquired Tomoro; Google Cloud posted 59 FDE roles; AWS committed $1 billion; Microsoft followed with $2.5 billion and 6,000 field engineers. The model spread through the channel via Anthropic, AWS and Microsoft certification programs, with Accenture pledging 1,000 FDEs for Gemini Enterprise, Cognizant training 18,000 specialists, and EY investing $1 billion. Gartner estimates only about 2,000 FDEs exist while demand is roughly four times higher, and predicts about 70% of vendor-led FDE initiatives will be abandoned by 2028 due to rising costs.

Full text

As 2026 began, "forward deployed engineer" was mostly Palantir's insider jargon. But by November, it evolved into one of the hottest job titles in enterprise AI.

The stories below come from Channel Dive and CIO Dive between May and November, and they show just how quickly FDEs became a thing.

OpenAI invested $4 billion in a consulting firm and bought Tomoro to staff it. Google Cloud posted 59 FDE roles. AWS committed $1 billion to a dedicated FDE unit. Two days later, Microsoft answered with a $2.5 billion investment and a promise to put 6,000 engineers in the field.

All the AI innovators stepped up, copying one another's plans at the same voracious speed their models pilfered humanity's writing, art and photographs.

Next, the FDE model moved to partners. Anthropic, AWS and Microsoft built credentials so integrators and MSPs could train AI specialists. Accenture pledged 1,000 FDEs for Google's Gemini Enterprise. Cognizant committed to training 18,000 Google Cloud AI specialists. And just this past month, EY got in on the action, promising to invest $1 billion in an FDE initiative.

A brief history of FDEs

  • March 12Anthropic invests $100 million into the launch of the Claude Partner Network.
  • May 11OpenAI creates $4 billion OpenAI Deployment Company.
  • May 12Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian puts out a call for forward deployed engineers.
  • June 30AWS pours $1 billion into a dedicated forward deployed engineering organization.
  • July 2Microsoft launches Microsoft Frontier Company with a $2.5 billion investment aimed at embedding 6,000 AI engineers with customers.
  • September 8Accenture partners with Google Cloud to create the Gemini Enterprise Business Group and staff it with 1,000 forward deployed engineers.
  • November 19EY pledges to spend $1 billion to create an Assurance Forward Deployed Engineers practice within its Assurance risk assessment and auditing business.

Enterprises buying AI and quickly realized they can't get it into production on their own. FDEs are supposed to close that gap. But the rush to adopt AI created an immediate supply problem. In September, Gartner Senior Director Mukul Saha put the number of working FDEs at about 2,000. "Demand is probably quadruple that, so there aren't enough engineering resources available," he told Channel Dive.

The scarcity makes FDEs pricey, and that shows up in the forecasts, too. Gartner expects about 70% of vendor-led FDE initiatives to be abandoned by 2028 as costs mount.

The reporting below has three parts. The first five stories cover the arrival, as model builders and hyperscalers placed their bets. The next seven follow FDEs into the channel through partner programs, certifications and integrator build-outs. The final three look at what customers need, what the talent costs and whether any of it will work.
