Customer Site Data and Compliance Self-Check Checklist: Fifteen Must-Ask Questions Before On-Site Deployment

Original title: 客户现场数据与合规自查清单：驻场前的十五个必问项

FDE中国 · 2026-09-04 · 3 min read

Summary

FDEChina publishes a data compliance self-check list for forward deployed engineers, with fifteen items across five groups covering the data lifecycle: classification, deployment location and cross-border/intranet constraints, least-privilege access, audit logging, and contract/NDPA alignment. It is meant to be run before on-site work and before go-live. The article stresses the list only surfaces risks—final compliance judgments belong to the customer's compliance and legal teams, not the FDE. It targets constraints common in Chinese enterprise sites, such as data-residency requirements, dedicated-line whitelists, classified logs, and customer-signed destruction procedures. A concrete takeaway: add a data-flow path diagram to the next project kickoff agenda.

Key points

  • Fifteen checklist items in five groups covering classification, deployment, access, logging, and contracts.
  • The list surfaces risks only; compliance conclusions must come from the customer's legal team.
  • Addresses China-specific constraints: data residency, line whitelists, log classification, signed destruction.
  • Temporary access rights should auto-expire; extensions require re-approval.
  • Recommended action: add a data-flow path diagram to project kickoff meetings.

This piece provides on-site FDEs with a data compliance self-check list that works both before entering the site and before go-live, with the focus on asking the right questions up front. Five groups of checks cover the full lifecycle of data in a project: how it is classified, where it runs, who can touch it, what traces are left, and how the contract is written. The most common misuse is treating it as a substitute for legal review—the checklist helps you surface risk points, but conclusions must be confirmed by the client's compliance department and legal team; FDEs should not make compliance judgments on the client's behalf. Compared with generic security checklists, this piece targets common constraints in domestic enterprise environments: data cannot leave the domain, dedicated-line whitelisting, logs also requiring classification, and destruction processes requiring client sign-off—all recurring topics in actual on-site work. After reading, do one thing: at your next project kickoff meeting, add the "data flow path diagram" to the agenda. — FDEChina Editorial Team · Architect's Perspective

In on-site delivery, technical incidents can be rolled back, but compliance incidents often cannot. Most compliance problems arise not from malice but from "nobody asked": data ended up in some third-party online tool, a test environment used real personal data, or a temporary network egress was opened and forgotten. This checklist is organized into five groups and should be reviewed once at project kickoff and again before go-live; it helps you raise the questions, with final determinations subject to the client's compliance department and legal counsel. The checklist should be used together with the project kickoff template, with relevant conclusions written into the kickoff document.

Data Classification

  • □ Input data has been classified (e.g., public, internal, sensitive, highly sensitive), and for each level the answers to three questions are clearly defined: "Can it enter the model? Can it appear in logs? Can it be used in demos?"; the classification results are confirmed by the client, not determined unilaterally by the project team;
  • □ Fields entering the model context are minimized: mask what can be masked, pass only aggregate statistics instead of detailed records where possible, truncate where possible; every field entering the model has a stated reason for its inclusion;
  • □ Test and demo data contain no real personal data; if real data is genuinely needed for a demo, obtain written consent and limit who is aware of it.

Cross-Border and Intranet Constraints

  • □ The deployment locations of the model and storage are checked against the client's data-not-leaving-the-domain requirements: public cloud, dedicated cloud, or on-premises deployment is decided by the client's compliance stance, not by technical convenience; the architecture diagram annotates the physical and logical location of each component and is archived with the client;
  • □ Map the data flow path: which network zones and which third-party services a request passes through, draw a path diagram, and submit it to the client for confirmation;
  • □ Before using any external online service, confirm its data processing terms; do not process client data in third-party services the client has not approved;
  • □ In intranet or dedicated-line deployment scenarios, network egresses are restricted to a whitelist, and temporarily opened debugging channels are logged, have expiration dates, and have closure confirmations.

Least Privilege

  • □ Project members are granted data access permissions by role; access to production-grade data goes through approval and is logged; the permission matrix is archived with project documentation and updated whenever personnel join or leave the project;
  • □ Temporary permissions (debugging, troubleshooting, demo preparation) have expiration times and are automatically revoked upon expiry, without relying on someone remembering to revoke them; extensions before expiry require re-approval rather than automatic renewal;
  • □ Credentials are not shared or hardcoded; rotation mechanisms and responsible persons are clearly defined, and credentials are changed when personnel change; no member should have access beyond what their work that week requires.

Logging and Audit

  • □ Key operations are logged: who, when, which data was used, and what operation was performed; logs cannot be modified by project members themselves;
  • □ Log retention periods and storage methods match the agreement with the client; logs themselves may contain sensitive content and likewise require classified controls—they must not be treated as ordinary files that can be exported at will;
  • □ Audit records can be exported by project or by time period, enabling responses to the client compliance department's inspection requests without last-minute scrambling; actually perform a trial export once before go-live to verify the process truly works.

Contract and Confidentiality Alignment

  • □ The data usage scope agreed in the contract and its attachments matches the actual implementation: client data will not be used to train or improve any model service unless the contract explicitly permits it;
  • □ Confidentiality obligations cover all personnel who come into contact with the data, including outsourced and seconded staff; the contracting entity is verified against the actual operators;
  • □ Data return and destruction procedures at project end or contract termination are agreed in writing, with the destruction method confirmed by the client and certificates retained.

How to Use

We recommend using this checklist as two gates: review it before entering the site and have the client confirm the classification and deployment stance, then re-review it before go-live together with the pre-launch checklist. When you find an item that cannot be met, the correct action is to escalate to the responsible parties on both sides and legal, not to act first and report later. To check whether data commitments made during acceptance were fulfilled, refer back to the PoC acceptance checklist; for handover responsibilities at project close, see the handover checklist.
