Bridge story

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 2 min read

Summary

Full text

A bridge story is our name for a short account that connects your past work to work. It names a customer, internal or external, what you built for them, and what changed for them because of it. It is the evidence behind your “why FDE” answer: the reason says what you want, and the story shows you have already done a version of the job.

Why it matters in interviews

Leo Mehr, Ramp’s Director of Engineering, said on dearCC in June 2026 that software engineers who say they want to be closer to customers or users are a great predictor of FDE fit. Source 2Leo Mehr - Ramp's $44B Bet on Services (YouTube auto-generated English captions)PublisherBasil Chatha, YouTubeSource typerecorded talk or interview Expect the follow-up: “Whose idea was that, and what did you own after it shipped?”

A weak bridge story has no named customer, or has you relaying requirements someone else gathered. A strong one sounds like this: “Our finance team closed the month by hand in a spreadsheet. I sat with them through one close and found the bank export silently dropped reversed transactions, which no ticket had mentioned. I built a reconciliation job that matched on the bank’s own reference, ran it every morning, and got paged when it failed. The controller stopped staying late on close night.”

Tell the same story at three lengths: one sentence in “Why FDE?”, a short paragraph in “Tell me about yourself”, and the full version in “What customer-facing experience do you have?”. Keep a second story ready for “Give me another example.”

The lesson The bridge story, and the short ‘why FDE’ answer shows how to build yours and test it against follow-ups.
