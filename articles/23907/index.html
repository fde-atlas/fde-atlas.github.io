What is an OpenAI Forward Deployed Engineer? Differences from the Palantir-style FDE

Original title: OpenAIフォワードデプロイドエンジニアとは？Palantir型FDEとの違い

FDE総研 · 2026-09-17 · 9 min read

Summary

This Japanese article explains OpenAI's Forward Deployed Engineer role: embedding at customer sites to personally implement APIs and agents, spanning discovery, prototyping, eval design, production rollout, and feedback into the core product. It contrasts OpenAI-type FDEs (model evals, prompt design, weekly-to-quarterly cycles) with Palantir-type FDEs (data integration, ontology design, multi-year engagements), noting different failure modes: data quality for Palantir, reproducibility and hallucination control for OpenAI. Required skills include solo production-grade app development, eval design, and non-technical abilities like expectation management. For Japanese engineers, the author recommends building an end-to-end AI deployment result at one's current job before applying, and preparing to explain technical decisions in English.

Key points

  • OpenAI FDEs deliver working code, not proposals, iterating in days-to-weeks cycles.
  • Palantir-type FDEs focus on data integration and ontology; OpenAI-type on evals and prompt design.
  • Field feedback flowing back into the product is the economic rationale for vendors hiring FDEs.
  • Eval design separates strong candidates: converting 'looks good' into percentages is rare.
  • Aspiring FDEs should first complete a production AI deployment at their current workplace.

What does an OpenAI Forward Deployed Engineer do?

OpenAI's FDE embeds itself in customer companies' operations, personally implementing APIs and agents, and works alongside them all the way to business outcomes.

Although the title says "engineer," the role actually sits on the boundary between sales, consulting, and development. OpenAI opened its Tokyo office in April 2024 as its first Asian location and has been expanding its enterprise adoption support framework in Japan. The Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) emerged in this context.

What is an "implementing consultant" who embeds with customers?

FDEs deliver code, not proposals. They build prototypes in the customer's environment and reach agreement through working products.

Whereas a traditional solutions architect "draws the blueprint and hands implementation to the customer or an SIer," an FDE creates their own repository, touches the customer's data, and writes evaluation scripts. At OpenAI, the focus is mainly on embedding ChatGPT Enterprise into business operations and building custom applications using the API and Agents SDK.

From a practitioner's perspective, the hard part of this role is not the technology itself but "the speed at which you understand the customer's business." Being able to run a cycle of interviewing about business processes in three days, delivering a prototype in a week, and bringing improvement proposals in week two is what separates good from great—a reasonable way to view it.

From pre-sales to production operation: how far does the role extend?

The FDE's scope runs from pre-deal PoCs to supporting adoption in production, characteristically spanning the entire lifecycle of a project vertically.

Typical engagement phases are as follows.

  • Discovery: Interview the customer about business challenges and data assets, and translate them into problems solvable with LLMs
  • Prototyping: Build a working demo in days to two weeks to let the customer experience the value
  • Evaluation design: Agree with the customer on evaluation criteria (evals) for accuracy, cost, and latency
  • Production implementation: Raise quality to production standards, including authentication, logging, guardrails, and fallbacks
  • Adoption and scaling: Support internal rollout and feed insights back into the core product

The fifth item, "feedback into the product," is the decisive difference that separates FDEs from mere contract development. Points of friction found in the field return to headquarters as feature requests and are reflected in the next model or SDK improvements. This feedback loop is precisely the economic rationale for a vendor to employ FDEs directly.

How does the OpenAI-type FDE differ from Palantir's FDE?

The difference lies in the assets they handle. Palantir implements data integration platforms in the field; OpenAI implements foundation model APIs.

It was Palantir Technologies that popularized the FDE job title. The company established a model of embedding people inside customer organizations with two pillars: FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer) and Deployment Strategist. In Japan, Palantir Technologies Japan, established in 2019 as a joint venture with SOMPO Holdings, is active. The OpenAI-type FDE inherits this lineage but works with a fundamentally different technology stack.

How do the products handled and the field work differ?

The Palantir type centers on data integration and ontology design; the OpenAI type centers on model evaluation and prompt design.

From a field-level feel, the Palantir type has a long runway because it often starts from "the data isn't even connected." The OpenAI type, on the other hand, can build a demo on day one, which means expectations run ahead and it easily falls into the trap of "the demo worked but it's not usable in production." Keep in mind that building fast does not necessarily mean it's easy.

How do success metrics and organizational positioning differ?

The Palantir type tends to measure success by business KPI improvement; the OpenAI type by product usage growth and adoption.

Palantir's business depends on long-term platform usage, so its FDEs commit to transforming the customer's business itself. OpenAI, by contrast, centers on API and per-seat billing, so an FDE's contribution tends to be measured by "whether an implementation that gets used made it to production." As a result, the OpenAI-type FDE is evaluated on the speed of expanding across multiple projects, while the Palantir-type FDE is evaluated on the persistence to dig deep into a single project.

It's not a matter of which is better—the feel of the career is different. To put it simply: the Palantir type suits those who want to accumulate deep business knowledge, while the OpenAI type suits those who want to build a large repertoire of AI implementation skills.

What skills are required of an OpenAI-type forward deployed engineer?

What's needed, beyond implementation ability, is the ability to translate business operations into requirements, and the ability to move customer decision-making forward—three things in total.

You can't grasp the demands of this role just by tracing the words on a job posting. What actually matters is the following combination of technical and non-technical skills.

What technical abilities are needed to be competitive?

The minimum bar is being able to launch a production-quality application on your own and manage LLM output quality numerically.

Skill areaWhat you should be able to do
Application implementationIndependently build API integrations and web applications in Python/TypeScript
LLM application designAppropriately choose among RAG, function calling, and agent task decomposition
Evaluation (eval)Design ground-truth data and continuously measure accuracy and reproducibility
Production operationsImplement authentication, rate limiting, cost management, audit logs, and fallbacks
Data handlingUnderstand customer data confidentiality and retention policies and reflect them in the design
SecurityExplain and mitigate risks such as prompt injection and privilege escalation

The third item, evaluation design, is where people especially differentiate themselves. Surprisingly few people can convert "seems good" into "X%." Whether you can isolate whether the cause of poor accuracy lies in the model, retrieval, data, or prompt appears to be the dividing line of real capability.

What non-technical abilities are required?

The ability to narrow a customer's vague requests down to verifiable problems and secure stakeholder agreement in a short time is essential.

  • Business interviewing skills: Observe the staff's work and capture even implicit exception handling
  • Expectation management: Accurately explain to non-engineers what LLMs can and cannot do
  • Scope design: Reframe "automate everything with AI" into "one step of one business process"
  • Documentation and articulation: Leave the design and its limitations in a handover-ready form
  • Languages: For FDEs at global companies, coordinating with headquarters in English is part of daily work

I can't say for certain, but I've heard from multiple practitioners that most reasons candidates fail in interviews lie in this area rather than technical skill. Being able to code but going silent in front of a customer—or conversely, pandering to the customer and making unfulfillable promises—are both fatal for an FDE.

For a Japanese engineer aiming to become an OpenAI-type FDE, where should they start?

The shortest path is to build a track record of "deploying AI in a customer's actual operations and delivering results" at your current workplace before starting a job search.

FDE is a role evaluated on track record, and the weight given to certifications or educational history is relatively low. Since the absolute number of openings in Japan is limited, you'll want to broaden your targets beyond OpenAI and Palantir to include positions where you can work in a similar way.

How can you gain FDE-like experience in your current job?

The most transferable experience is choosing one internal business process and carrying it through alone, from requirements definition to implementation, evaluation, and adoption.

The concrete approach is as follows.

  • Pick one target process: Something with clear steps, such as inquiry handling, meeting minutes processing, or internal document search
  • Measure the current flow: Record volume, time required, and error rates before starting
  • Build a prototype in two weeks: Prioritize a state users can touch over perfection
  • Prepare evaluation data: Decide pass/fail criteria upfront rather than relying on subjective judgment
  • Take it to production: Design logging, permissions, and manual fallbacks for failures
  • Record results numerically: Compare against pre-project measurements and document both improvements and shortfalls

Once you've been through these six steps, you'll be able to answer nearly all the questions raised in an FDE interview. Conversely, experience that stops at the prototype stage is hard to get credit for. It's the process of getting into production—facing permission design and cost control—that hiring managers really want to hear about.

How can you broaden your target employers and career options?

Beyond applying directly to OpenAI or Palantir, AI implementation roles at consulting firms and operating companies are also places to gain similar experience.

  • FDE/solutions engineer at AI vendors: The most direct route, with the closest job title
  • AI implementation divisions at general consultancies and SIers: Easier to accumulate experience on customer-embedded projects
  • In-house AI promotion engineers at operating companies: You can create your own deployment track record as firsthand experience
  • Deployment support engineers at AI startups: Small teams mean you can own the entire process

FDE roles at foreign vendors often assume English interviews and coordination with headquarters, which tends to be the biggest practical barrier for Japanese engineers. Alongside technical preparation, it's highly worthwhile to practice explaining your implementation decisions in English.

Finally, one piece of on-the-ground reality. FDE may look glamorous, but in practice it's a continuous stream of unglamorous work: grinding out requirements in a customer's meeting room and rewiring data that won't connect. Even so, this role is growing because we've entered a stage where the value of AI is determined not by "model performance" but by "whether it's connected to real operations." I believe that, for the time being, supply of people who can handle that connection will not keep up with demand.
