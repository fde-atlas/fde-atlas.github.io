Forward-deployed engineer salary data

FDE Notes · 2026-10-13 · 37 min read

Summary

FDE Notes aggregates salary ranges only as disclosed by employers in their own job postings for forward-deployed and deployment-adjacent roles. Of 943 live roles, 260 publish pay across 58 companies. Among 239 US annual-pay roles, the median published range is $185,000–$250,000, with a median midpoint of $225,000; the full span runs from $90,000 to $850,000. The page excludes closed postings, never estimates missing pay, excludes equity and bonuses, and offers the full CSV dataset plus citation formats. Snapshot tracking began 2026-11-20.

Key points

  • 260 of 943 live deployment roles publish employer-disclosed pay, across 58 companies.
  • Median published range for 239 US annual roles: $185,000–$250,000; median midpoint $225,000.
  • Full span: $90,000 lowest lower bound to $850,000 highest upper bound.
  • No estimates: closed postings excluded, equity and bonuses not counted, small groups withheld.
  • Applied AI/ML roles median midpoint (~$255,750) exceeds solutions engineering (~$205,875).

Median published range

Across the 239 US roles posted with annual pay in US dollars, the median lower bound is $185,000 and the median upper bound is $250,000.

Half of those ranges sit between $160,000 and $300,000. Taking each range's midpoint and then the median of those midpoints gives $225,000 — that is the figure directly comparable to the single-number medians other sites publish.

Full span

The lowest lower bound is $90,000; the highest upper bound is $850,000. Spread this wide is normal for this role family: the title covers everything from a mid-level integration engineer to a director-level solutions architect.

How these numbers are computed

  • Open roles only. Closed postings are excluded — an expired range says nothing about the current market.
  • Employer-disclosed ranges only. A role is counted when the posting itself states pay. We never estimate, and we never fill gaps from similar roles.
  • Currency and period are not mixed. The aggregate uses US-dollar roles paid annually — 239 of the 260 disclosed ranges. The remaining 21 disclosed ranges could not be pooled — 5 in GBP, 7 in CAD, 3 in AUD, 1 in KRW, 3 in SGD, 2 in EUR — and are still listed below.
  • Each quartile is labelled by what it measures, because "median" is ambiguous for a range: the medians above are the median of lower bounds and the median of upper bounds, not the midpoint figure.
  • Groups too small to quote are withheld. Role-family and level splits need at least 10 disclosed ranges before a median is published; smaller groups are labelled or held back entirely. Those splits, with their sample sizes, live on the market data page.
  • Equity, bonus and benefits are not included. Neither is cost-of-living adjustment. We only see base pay, so that is all we report.

Why this page exists

Pay ranges are the least guessable part of a job search and the most often estimated. We already collect every posting, so publishing the disclosed ones costs us nothing and costs you nothing.

The number that matters most is the one in the first stat above: only a minority of employers publish pay. If you are hiring, that is your edge — a role with a published range gets read.
