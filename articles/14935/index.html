FDE interview glossary

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 6 min read

Summary

This article is an alphabetical glossary of roughly 60 terms used by interviewers and lessons in FDE interviews, each defined in one sentence with a pointer to the lesson that teaches it. Entries span engineering practices (idempotency keys, canary releases, latency budgets), data and SQL topics (data lineage, window functions, slowly changing dimensions), AI and evals (LLM-as-judge, prompt injection, RAG), compliance regimes (GDPR, HIPAA, FedRAMP, SOC 2), and roles and pay (deployment strategist, sales engineer, on-target earnings). It also defines interview-narrative terms such as bridge story, ownership audit, and tripwire, and notes company-specific usages like Palantir's FDSE title, called Delta internally.

Key points

  • Alphabetical glossary of about 60 FDE interview terms, each defined in one sentence.
  • Entries link each term to the lesson that teaches it.
  • Covers engineering, data/SQL, AI evals, compliance, and role/pay vocabulary.
  • Notes company-specific titles, e.g., Palantir's FDSE is called Delta internally.
  • Includes interview-storytelling terms like bridge story, ownership audit, and tripwire.

The words interviewers and lessons use, each defined in a sentence, with the lesson that teaches it.

A

  • Agent
  • A system in which a model chooses steps and tool calls to complete a task, within limits the design sets.

B

  • Backfill
  • Reprocessing historical data through a pipeline, ideally with the same idempotent code as the daily run.
  • Backpressure
  • A signal from a slow consumer that makes a producer slow down instead of overflowing memory or queues.
  • Bridge story
  • A short account that connects your past work to FDE work by naming a customer, what you built for them and what changed.

C

  • Calibrated commitment
  • A promise limited to what you control, with an owner, a date and the conditions stated for everything else.
  • Canary release
  • Sending a change to a small share of traffic and watching agreed metrics before rolling it out to everyone.
  • Circuit breaker
  • A pattern that stops calling a failing dependency for a while, then probes it, so failures do not cascade.

D

  • Data lineage
  • A record of where each piece of data came from and how it was transformed, so any number can be traced to its source rows.
  • Data residency
  • A requirement that data, including logs and backups, be stored and processed in a specific region.
  • Dead-letter queue
  • A holding place for messages that failed processing too many times, kept for inspection and replay.
  • Decomposition round
  • An open-ended interview in which you work out loud from a vague problem with several possible solutions to a concrete approach and a first working version.
  • Deployment strategist
  • A customer-facing role that works out the customer’s questions and scope beside FDEs; at some employers the title means a product-manager or quota-carrying role instead.

E

  • Embedding
  • A vector representation of text or other data used to measure similarity of meaning.
  • Exponential backoff with jitter
  • Retrying with growing, randomized delays so that clients recovering from the same failure do not retry in lockstep.

F

  • FedRAMP
  • The US government program that assesses and certifies a cloud service at an impact level before federal agencies may put their data in it.
  • Forward deployed engineer
  • A software engineer who builds and ships production systems inside a customer’s problem and environment, accountable to that customer’s outcome.
  • Forward deployed software engineer
  • Palantir’s title for its FDE role, called Delta internally; OpenAI and EY also post FDSE titles, each with its own duties.

G

  • Gaps and islands
  • A SQL pattern for finding continuous runs, and the breaks between them, in ordered data such as machine status.
  • GDPR
  • The EU regulation governing the processing and transfer of personal data.
  • Golden set
  • A curated, labeled set of examples, sampled to cover the cases that matter, used to evaluate a system the same way over time.
  • Guardrail metric
  • A metric watched alongside the success metric to catch harm or gaming, such as wait times by district when the target is the average.

H

  • H-1B labor condition application
  • A US filing that states the base wage, as a rate or a range, an employer will pay a sponsored worker in a job; it records no bonus or equity.
  • HIPAA
  • The US law whose Privacy and Security Rules govern protected health information and the vendors that handle it.
  • Hybrid search
  • Combining keyword and vector retrieval and merging their ranked results, for example with reciprocal rank fusion.

I

  • Idempotency key
  • A client-supplied identifier that lets a server apply a repeated request once, making retries safe.
  • Inter-rater agreement
  • How consistently two reviewers, or a reviewer and a model judge, label the same items, corrected for agreement by chance.

L

  • Latency budget
  • The total time a request may take, divided among the steps it passes through, measured at the slow tail as well as the median.
  • Launch criteria
  • Thresholds agreed with a customer before building that decide whether a system goes live.
  • LLM-as-judge
  • Using a language model with a rubric to grade outputs; the judge itself must be checked against human labels.

M

  • Model Context Protocol
  • An open protocol for exposing tools and data to models through servers, so agents can call a customer’s systems in a standard way.

O

  • OAuth
  • A standard for granting an application limited, revocable access to resources on a user’s behalf without sharing passwords.
  • On-target earnings
  • Base pay plus variable pay if targets are met; a posting that states pay as OTE includes incentive pay in the figure.
  • Ontology
  • A shared model of a customer’s business objects and their relationships, mapped onto the underlying data so people and applications use the same terms.
  • OpenID Connect
  • An identity layer on top of OAuth that lets an application verify who a user is and get basic profile claims.
  • Ownership audit
  • Rewriting a story so every decision has a named owner: what you decided and did, and what others did, credited to them.
  • Ownership language
  • Phrasing that names who acts, what they will do and by when, owning your part without blaming others or claiming control you lack.

P

  • Personal data
  • Information that identifies a person or can be linked to one, which privacy law and customer policy restrict.
  • Problem shape
  • A recurring structure of customer problem, such as unifying fragmented sources, that looks the same across industries.
  • Profit participation unit
  • A grant that pays a share of a company’s future profit distributions instead of stock, with no shareholder vote.
  • Prompt injection
  • Input that tries to override a model’s instructions, directly or hidden in retrieved documents, emails or tool results.

Q

  • Query plan
  • The steps a database chooses to run a query, read with EXPLAIN to find scans, joins and missing indexes.

R

  • Re-engineering round
  • An interview in which you learn, debug or extend code or a library you have never seen, against the clock and out loud.
  • Regression suite
  • Cases rerun on every prompt, model or code change to catch behavior that used to work and now does not.
  • Reranking
  • Re-scoring a short list of retrieved results with a slower, more accurate model before they reach the prompt.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation
  • Answering with a model that is given passages retrieved from a document collection as context.

S

  • Sales engineer
  • A technical role on or beside a sales team that helps win deals with demos and proofs of concept; the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics says employers usually pay it salary plus commissions or bonuses.
  • SAML
  • An XML-based standard for exchanging authentication assertions between an identity provider and an application.
  • SCIM
  • A standard protocol for provisioning and deprovisioning users and groups from an identity provider into applications.
  • Scope creep
  • Requirements growing through small, unreviewed additions without a matching change in time, people or budget.
  • Service level objective
  • A target for a measured level of service, such as availability or latency; the failure it allows is the error budget.
  • Single sign-on
  • Signing in once through a central identity provider and using that session across many applications.
  • Slowly changing dimension
  • A dimension table whose attributes change over time, and the technique used to record the change, such as a new row per version with valid-from and valid-to dates.
  • SOC 2
  • An auditor’s attestation report on a service organization’s controls against the AICPA trust services criteria.
  • Solutions architect
  • A customer-facing technical role that designs, advises and often builds proofs of concept; postings place it before the sale, after it, or both.

T

  • Token bucket
  • A rate-limiting algorithm that refills tokens at a fixed rate and allows bursts up to a capacity.
  • Tripwire
  • A case-specific failure mode a strong candidate surfaces before it sinks the plan, such as a metric each stakeholder defines differently.
  • Trust boundary
  • A line where data or calls pass between parts of a system that trust each other differently, and where authentication and validation belong.

V

  • Virtual private cloud
  • An isolated network inside a cloud provider where the customer controls addressing, routing, egress and private links to other accounts.

W

  • Walking skeleton
  • The thinnest end-to-end version of a system that performs one small real function across its main components, built first and then extended.
  • Window function
  • A SQL function computed over rows related to the current row, such as a running total, without collapsing them.
