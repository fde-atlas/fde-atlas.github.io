FDE Interview Questions | Selection Process and Case Interview Preparation

Original title: FDEの面接で聞かれること｜選考の流れとケース面接の準備

FDE Consulting · 2026-10-20 · 6 min read

Summary

Drawing on published job postings from Japanese companies hiring FDEs (JAPAN AI, SB OAI Japan, OpenAI Tokyo, JDSC), this article explains what FDE interviews assess and how to prepare. The author argues interviews test both implementation skill and the ability to structure ambiguous customer problems, and offers a five-step framework for case interviews, advice on presenting past experience with concrete numbers and production outcomes, guidance on AI-use rules during hiring, and suggested reverse questions for candidates.

Key points

  • All four companies' processes test both coding ability and structuring ambiguous problems.
  • Interviews assess five areas: problem decomposition, system design, customer communication, production delivery, and AI understanding.
  • In case interviews, clarify the question first; evaluators care about reasoning order over the final answer.
  • Describe experience via personal contributions, quantified impact, and whether systems reached production.
  • Confirm AI-tool rules with recruiters beforehand and ask four reverse questions about role scope.

The selection process for FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) differs from that of a regular software engineer. In addition to coding ability, candidates are evaluated on whether they can break down a customer's vague problems, make decisions while talking with the customer, and take what they build all the way to production use.

This article organizes what is asked in FDE interviews and how to prepare, based on the selection processes written in job postings from companies hiring FDEs in Japan. A list of hiring companies and how to read job postings is summarized in FDE Job and Career Change Guide, and the skills needed to become an FDE and how to build experience are covered in How to Become an FDE. The FDE role itself is explained in What Is an FDE? The Meaning and Work of a Forward Deployed Engineer.

FDE selection processes published by each company

Some job postings describe the selection process and even the interview content. Lining up these descriptions reveals what FDE selection is looking for. Extracted from job postings as of October 20, 2026.

Company and roleSelection process and requirements stated in the job posting
JAPAN AI (Forward Deployed Engineer)In order: document screening, coding test, interviews (4 to 5 rounds), offer. A reference check takes place before the final interview. Interviews assess understanding of AI/Agents, system design and implementation skills, structuring of vague problems, ability to explain in Japanese, and team fit
SB OAI Japan (FDE seconded from SoftBank)Practical discussions during interviews, including system design and case studies. Interviews and meetings are in principle held in person. Required: 5+ years of experience in software engineering or technical implementation, and experience driving projects including customer engagement
OpenAI (Forward Deployed Engineer – Tokyo)Both Japanese and English are required; submit your resume in English, and interviews include conversation in both languages. Cite 5+ years of experience, including work involving customer interaction
JDSC (FDE)Required: development experience as an engineer (2+ years) and experience organizing problems through dialogue with stakeholders even when requirements are unclear, and carrying them through to implementation. Experience as an FDE or on AI projects is not required

What the four companies' postings have in common is that they assess both implementation ability and the ability to organize vague problems. In my view, preparing for only one of the two is not enough.

Five things evaluated in FDE interviews

Translating the job postings' descriptions into what is evaluated in interviews, I see the following five things.

What is evaluatedExample of how it is asked in the interviewWhat to prepare
Ability to break down vague problemsYou are given a customer problem with unclear conditions and asked where you would startPractice thinking out loud in the order described in "How to Run a Case Interview" below
Ability to design and build systemsDesign a system using an LLM, covering data, permissions, and evaluationBe able to explain the architecture of something you built in 5 minutes
Ability to decide through conversation with the customerYou are asked how you handled a disagreement with a customer's representativePick one experience of gathering requirements, building a prototype, and iterating on it
Ability to take things to productionYou are asked what you did after a PoC to get it into production useNumbers on how long it ran in production and the volume of work it handled
Understanding of AIYou are asked about LLM errors and how much to delegate to agentsBe able to explain in your own words how to build evaluations and guard against errors

For the AI understanding part, what I wrote in How to Build LLM Evaluations (Evals) and Hallucination Countermeasures can be used directly as material to talk about in interviews.

How to run a case interview

In an FDE case interview, you are given a problem with unclear conditions, such as "A company wants to introduce AI into its inquiry handling. How would you proceed?" (I made up this question as an example). In my view, what is being evaluated is not the final answer but the order in which you think. Speaking in the following five steps makes it easy for the interviewer to follow your reasoning.

StepWhat to doExample of what to say
1. Clarify the questionAsk back whose problem it is, what the pain point is, and how it will be measured"Do you want to reduce handling time, or errors in responses?"
2. Break it into workflow and dataMap out the current workflow and the available data"Where do inquiries arrive, and where are past responses stored?"
3. Decide what to build firstChoose the first step, not everything at once"First we would only generate response drafts, and humans would send them."
4. Decide how to measureDecide how to judge whether what you built worked"We would look at the percentage of drafts used as-is, and handling time."
5. Discuss risks and next stepsTalk about errors, permissions, data handling, and the next phase"Since customer data will be involved, we would first confirm the AI's configuration for permitted use."

In my view, what you should avoid is skipping step 1 and starting with the solution. FDE work begins from the gap between the problem the customer first states and the problem that truly needs solving. In interviews too, the interviewer will read a candidate who clarifies the question with the first question as someone who would do the same in the field.

How to talk about your experience

In FDE interviews, your past experience is probed in depth. In my view, including the following three elements makes it easier to convey the skills FDEs need.

  • What you yourself did. Talk about what you decided and the code you wrote, not the team's results
  • Numbers. Talk about what changed and by how much, such as man-hours, handling time, and usage rates. If the numbers cannot be disclosed externally, the direction and magnitude of the change are enough
  • Production. Talk about whether it ended as a PoC or was used in production, and how long it ran

If you have no experience taking something to production, it is fine to talk about an experience where a PoC stalled. If you can explain why it stalled and what you would do now, that demonstrates your understanding of FDE work. I have summarized why PoCs stall in Breaking Out of AI PoC Stalls.

Check whether AI tools are allowed before the selection process

FDEs use AI as a tool, but whether AI may be used during the selection process differs by company. For example, Anthropic states the following in its guidelines for applicants.

SituationAnthropic's guideline
Resume and application questionsWrite the first draft yourself, then polish it with Claude
Take-home assignmentsUnless instructed otherwise, work on them without using Claude
Live interviewTake it without AI assistance unless instructed otherwise

If the coding test or take-home assignment doesn't state whether AI use is allowed, ask the recruiter before starting. I believe asking itself creates the impression that you're the kind of person who checks the rules on the customer's site before acting.

Things to confirm with your reverse questions

Reverse questions in an FDE interview are a chance to verify what the work will be like after you join. I recommend asking the following four.

What to askExample question
Endpoint of responsibility"Where does an FDE's work end: at PoC completion, at production deployment, or at changes in business metrics?"
Scope of implementation"Does the FDE write the production code themselves, or does another team?"
Distance from the field"How many days a week do you attend meetings at the customer's site, and which ones?"
How you're evaluated"Is an FDE's evaluation based on revenue, customer metrics, or the number of production deployments?"
