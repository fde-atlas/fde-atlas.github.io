Today's Takeaways

Today in China, a rare "three-party alignment" appeared:

  • Cloud vendor: An Alibaba Cloud VP publicly stated that "Alibaba Cloud doesn't make money from FDEs"; the role of FDEs is to drive product adoption. The business models he favors are "pay-for-results" and "long-term service contracts."
  • Local service provider: An on-site team of 40+ people in Qingdao broke through in the port industry and then replicated the model across multiple ports, with annual sales rising from RMB 31 million toward RMB 50 million.
  • SASAC: The next step for central SOEs' AI push is set as "focusing on the industrial sector, driving large-scale deployment of AI on production frontlines."

All three point to the same thing: the commercial value of FDEs lies not in person-days, but in whether they can sign "results + long-term" contracts.

Key Signals

Alibaba Cloud: Doesn't make money from FDEs, favors "pay-for-results + long-term service contracts"

  • Sources: National Business Daily; TMTPost
  • In an exclusive interview with National Business Daily, Alibaba Cloud VP Huo Jia said that "Alibaba Cloud doesn't make money from FDEs"; the team's core performance metric is whether they can drive product adoption, and FDEs should be "the glue and accelerator of product capabilities." He favors two commercialization models: first, pay-for-results, premised on the provider genuinely having delivery capability; second, long-term service contracts that customers are willing to sign. He warned providers not to expect to make big money from services themselves, and to think clearly about their moat.
  • A TMTPost feature the same day (co-planned with Alibaba Cloud) gave several named cases: Peking University International Hospital delivered 7 patient-service scenarios in 3 weeks, with intent-understanding and recall accuracy of about 90%, covering 1.5 million patients; PetroChina Kunlun Digital Intelligence used abnormal alarms in atmospheric and vacuum distillation units as an entry point, achieving 24-hour continuous operation in key production systems; CCTV Sports' World Cup AI viewing assistant launched in about a week. The project threshold is stated bluntly: business value must exceed 10x the total token cost.
  • Why it matters: This is the first time a leading Chinese cloud vendor has publicly positioned its own FDEs as a "cost center that drives products." The implication is clear: independent FDE providers can't compete with cloud vendors on person-day pricing; the moat can only come from industry judgment, results-based contracts, and long-term operations. "Value must exceed 10x token cost" can be used directly as a project gate. Caveat: all case figures are vendor-reported, with no independent endorsement from the hospital, PetroChina, or CCTV; "pay-for-results" is an executive's opinion, with no disclosed contract samples or proportions.

Qingdao Jiaoyin: A 40-person on-site team, breaking through one industry then replicating

  • Source: Qilu Evening News via 10jqka
  • Qilu Evening News reported on September 29 that Qingdao Jiaoyin Information Technology Co., Ltd. does enterprise AI agent deployment via on-site teams. Its port berth-scheduling agent considers about 134 factors; the company says it brings ports "RMB 20 million in net profit growth per year." Its port business covers 18 scenarios and has been rolled out to Tianjin Port, Beibu Gulf Port, Ningbo Port, and others. Another case is grain-and-oil group e-commerce reconciliation: processing 4.5 million orders corresponding to over RMB 600 million in sales, turning a monthly reconciliation that took 60 finance staff 45 days into daily same-day settlement. The team grew from 20 to 40+ people, with R&D making up 80%; 2025 sales were RMB 31 million, RMB 29 million through the end of July 2026, with full-year projections exceeding RMB 50 million.
  • Why it matters: This is a fairly clear sample of a small Chinese FDE provider: break through in one industry first, then replicate similar scenarios to customers in the same industry, with revenue growing through "scenario replication" rather than person-days. It corroborates Alibaba Cloud's judgment of "pay-for-results + long-term contracts." Caveat: the port is unnamed; the RMB 20 million net profit growth and revenue figures are all self-reported by the company, lacking calculation methodology and audits.

SASAC: Central SOEs' "AI+" hundred benchmark scenarios; next step focuses on industrial frontline scale-up

  • Sources: Xinhua; China News Service
  • A State Council Information Office press conference on September 28 disclosed: central SOEs have formed "a hundred benchmarks of strategically high-value scenarios," covering 20 industry categories; four data industry communities—energy, transportation, low-carbon, and finance—have aggregated over a thousand high-quality datasets; platforms such as "Xirang" and the "computing network brain" provide intelligent computing services in token form. The next step is to focus on the industrial sector and drive large-scale deployment of AI on production frontlines.
  • Why it matters: Central SOEs' framing has shifted from "building models and running demonstrations" to "frontline large-scale deployment"—exactly the stage that most demands on-site engineering capability. If the list of hundred benchmark scenarios is made public, it will be the most direct demand map for providers and talent development. Caveat: the press conference did not mention FDEs, nor did it give per-scenario investment, results, or implementing entities.

OpenAI and Anthropic: Enterprise "committed spend" becomes procurement currency in partner marketplaces

  • Sources: OpenAI DevDay 2026 recap; Baseten; Claude Marketplace
  • At DevDay 2026 (September 29), OpenAI launched a software marketplace: eligible enterprise customers can use a portion of their existing OpenAI committed spend to purchase approved partner software; the first batch of 32 partners includes Salesforce, ServiceNow, Harvey, and others, currently in beta. Earlier, on September 23, Anthropic launched Claude Marketplace, which, besides 2,000+ connectors, plugins, and agent products, also has a services partner category, naming Accenture, BCG, and Deloitte.
  • Why it matters: Model vendors' enterprise contracts are becoming "ecosystem accounts," moving the entry point for customers to purchase partner software and services up to the model vendors. By comparison in China, if Alibaba Cloud's and Tencent Cloud's partner FDE systems are integrated with marketplace credits, providers' customer acquisition entry points will concentrate further in cloud vendors' hands. Caveat: no procurement amounts or revenue-share ratios were disclosed; Anthropic's original text does not specify whether committed credits can be used to purchase services partners.

Additional signals: The National AI Application Pilot-Testing Base (Culture and Tourism) launched in Hangzhou on September 28, with 12 enterprises moving in (China News Service). The bid range for the Lanzhou LS AI middle-platform project is RMB 1.3–1.7 million, usable as a reference range for SOE AI agent platform pricing (Sina Finance). Zhejiang and Shenzhen have launched online solicitation for MIIT's AI application service provider resource pool.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Move pricing structures toward "initial delivery + monthly operations or results-linked fees": Cloud vendors have publicly said they don't make money from FDEs, so selling person-days will get harder for independent providers. Initial delivery builds trust; long-term operations and results-linked fees are the sustainable revenue.
  • Add a 10x gate column to your scenario screening table: Estimated business value ÷ unit cost per qualified task ≥ 10; skip scenarios below this ratio, or cut costs first.
  • Set product direction based on trial-and-error cost first: For businesses with low per-trial cost, AI handles "volume" and humans do filtering; for businesses with high trial cost, AI handles "precision" and humans do review. Decide this first, then choose between building a workbench or a Skill.
  • Take the "break through one industry, replicate to similar customers" path: Qingdao Jiaoyin's revenue growth came from replicating the same scenario across multiple ports. Delivery cycle, reuse ratio, and quality changes on the second customer are the metrics for testing whether a provider has truly accumulated reusable assets.
