What is the FDE, the most expensive new type of engineer in the AI era? Is it being hired in China?

CSDN · 2026-05-19 · 9 min read

Summary

This article explains the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role, tracing it to Palantir in the 2010s, where engineers worked on-site at sensitive clients; FDEs spend roughly 25% of their time coding, 50% on integration and debugging, and 25% communicating. It describes the recent AI-driven surge: OpenAI raised $4 billion at a $14 billion valuation for a deployment company and acquired Tomoro with 150 FDEs; Anthropic launched a separate FDE company with $1.5 billion targeting mid-market firms; Google Cloud built an FDE team under Thomas Kurian. In China, ByteDance, Ant and startups now post FDE roles, with ByteDance's Doubao FDE paying 525k–1.05M RMB annually. The author argues Chinese FDEs differ from Silicon Valley's in six ways—margins, talent incentives, promotion, product abstraction, culture, and pricing—and risk becoming on-site outsourcing, though AI adoption is narrowing the gap.

Key points

  • FDE originated at Palantir: engineers embedded at client sites delivering working code.
  • OpenAI raised $4B for a deployment company; Anthropic put $1.5B into a separate FDE firm.
  • ByteDance's Doubao FDE role pays roughly 525k–1.05M RMB per year.
  • Chinese FDEs risk becoming on-site outsourcing due to pricing and culture gaps.
  • The author sees FDE as a fast growth path for developers who want business exposure.

💡 This article breaks down a role that has been wildly discussed in AI circles lately: FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer). Where it came from, what it does, why OpenAI and Anthropic are throwing money at it, whether China is actually hiring now, and what it means for ordinary developers.

Recently I came across a job posting on a recruitment site called "Doubao AI Large Model FDE", posted by ByteDance Volcano Engine, in Hangzhou.

The salary listed was

35–70K × 15 薪

, which works out to an annual salary between

52.5 万 – 105 万人民币

.

I stared at the three letters "FDE" for a while and realized something: this is the most powerful new job category of the AI era, and it has quietly made its way to China.

What surprised me even more was that a similar role at Ant Group had a title that literally read "FDE Solutions Engineering Expert (AI) - Travel, Client-Facing, On-Site", not even hiding the words "on-site".

This is a far cry from my traditional impression of engineering roles.

Shouldn't a proper programmer be sitting at a desk writing code?

FDE = Forward Deployed Engineer

, literally translated as "Forward Deployed Engineer".

An FDE is an engineer stationed on-site at the client's company writing code.

It sits at the intersection of three traditional roles:

  • Software Engineer ✅
  • Solutions Architect ✅
  • Consultant ✅

But it differs from all three.

RoleMain deliverablesHow FDE differs
ConsultantPPTs, proposalsFDEs deliver working code directly
Solutions ArchitectArchitecture diagrams, PoCsFDEs also personally wire up APIs, debug, and deploy
Ordinary SWEFeatures in a repoCode written by FDEs goes straight into the client's business
An FDE spends roughly 25% writing code, 50% on integration and debugging, and 25% in meetings and communication.

In other words, the code an FDE writes may only account for a quarter of their working hours.

But that quarter is the key to whether an AI company can go from "selling models" to "selling deployment".

Many people think FDE is a term coined in 2026. It's not.

This playbook was proven by Palantir back in the 2010s.

Palantir served clients like the US military and the CIA, whose businesses had two characteristics:

  • The data was extremely sensitive, so client systems couldn't be moved to Palantir's side
  • The business was too complex to be explained by sales engineers alone

So they sent engineers directly to client sites, permanently stationed there.

Internally, Palantir called this role Delta, and paired it with an analyst role called Echo to form FDE teams.

By 2016, Palantir's FDE headcount had already exceeded that of ordinary engineers.

In Silicon Valley, this was hardly news.

The real turning point came in the last year or two, when FDE suddenly went from a "Palantir specialty" to a "standard fixture at AI companies".

For every dollar spent training a model, another dollar is needed to actually get the model running.

Over the past three years, the industry has been competing on model size and benchmark scores.

But enterprise clients gradually discovered an awkward truth:

They don't lack models; they lack someone to integrate the models into their own business.

The word "integrate" looks simple, but it's actually extremely heavy, because:

  • Every company's processes are different
  • Every company's data is scattered across a dozen systems
  • Every company defines "compliance" and "effectiveness" differently

The ordinary SaaS flow of "sign up → activate → use" simply doesn't work for enterprise AI.

So AI companies began aggressively competing for FDEs.

A few of the most visible moves:

  • On May 11, OpenAI announced the establishment of OpenAI Deployment Company
  • Private equity investment of $4 billion, at a valuation of $14 billion
  • Investors include TPG and Advent, with partners including McKinsey, Bain, and Capgemini
  • The minimum promised return rate to investors is 17.5%
  • Along the way, it acquired the UK AI company Tomoro, buying the people along with 150 FDEs
  • In early May, announced the establishment of an independent FDE company (the name hasn't even been announced yet)
  • Investors include Anthropic itself, Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, Goldman Sachs, and Apollo
  • Initial investment of $1.5 billion
  • Focusing on the mid-market, integrating Claude into business operations
  • Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian established an AI FDE unit under the go-to-market team
  • Total compensation for senior FDEs in the US can exceed $400,000
  • Paid in Google stock, aligning interests with the parent company
  • The interview process was compressed from 4–6 rounds to 2 rounds within 2 days

Note that the three companies take different approaches:

CompanyOrganizational placementInterest alignmentStyle
OpenAIIndependent companyIndependent company stockMore like high-end consulting
AnthropicIndependent companyIndependent company stockMid-market deployment

In other words, the FDE in China has already gone from "concept" to "a standard role in job listings."

But a word of caution: the FDE in China and the FDE in Silicon Valley are fundamentally not the same job.

There are 6 differences

DifferenceSilicon ValleyChina
Profit marginPalantir's gross margin is over 80%In China, 2B profits can hardly support million-RMB salaries
Talent preferenceFDE is a highly prestigious pathTop engineers prefer R&D roles at big tech companies
Promotion mechanismFrontline FDEs are core combat powerDo on-site work well and you get transferred back to HQ
Product abstractionHas accumulation methods like OntologyEasily falls into "one-off customization"
Organizational cultureHigh delegationLong approval chains, weak delegation
Pricing logicPay for resultsCustomers get pulled away by low-price solutions

Simply put:

The Silicon Valley FDE says "I'll help you solve a problem that creates hundreds of millions in value," while the FDE in China is easily treated by clients as "on-site outsourcing."

This isn't something FDEs can change on their own — it depends on clients accepting new payment models.

But with the arrival of AI, this gap is rapidly narrowing.

There are three reasons:

  • AI deployment is harder — clients can't handle it themselves, so they must bring in people
  • AI amplifies the leverage of excellent engineers — one FDE equals 3–5 consultants of the past
  • What AI brings to enterprises is "cost reduction" and "replacing manual labor" — the value space is enough to support FDE salaries

ByteDance Doubao's FDE role pays

35–70K × 15 薪

, which already shows that China's top AI teams are repricing this role at Silicon Valley talent price levels.

I've combined community discussions with my own impressions and summarized them into a table:

GroupMy take
New grads / 1–3 years into their careerThis is the best window for young people in the past decade. Regular SWE roles are shrinking while FDE roles are expanding, letting you get direct exposure to enterprise AI projects and grow very fast
Senior developersYou may initially feel "demoted" (frequent client changes, lack of belonging), but if you want to start a business in the future and get close to real business, FDE is the fastest way to see real demand
People wanting to move into AICompared to "building your own product," joining an FDE team is a low-risk path — you earn money while accumulating real deployment experience
People who don't want to travelStay honestly at your desk — FDE is not for you

I have a few personal judgments:

1. The next stop for regular SWEs isn't "writing better code" — it's "getting code to run inside clients' businesses."

Models will keep getting stronger, and the barrier to writing code will only keep dropping.

But "embedding AI into real businesses" is work that remains expensive, messy, and slow.

2. The essence of the FDE is "advanced engineering delivery for the AI era."

It's not sales, not pre-sales, not a programmer.

It's a composite role that combines all three capabilities.

3. The ceiling for FDEs in China depends on whether clients are willing to pay for "results."

If clients remain stuck on "paying by hours" or "paying by features," FDEs in China will remain stuck at "on-site outsourcing."

But AI gives clients a new way to evaluate value — paying based on labor cost savings or conversion rate improvements.

Once this path is proven, FDEs in China could form a high-salary career band just like in Silicon Valley.

Many people have been asking lately: where should I pivot in the AI era? My previous answer was "learn to code with AI."

Recently I've changed my mind a bit.

AI is already at 85 out of 100 on coding; grinding further yields diminishing returns.

What's truly in short supply is the person standing between the client and the model.

This person needs to understand the client's business, write working code, be familiar with a pile of AI tools, and handle a pace of "requirements today, launch tomorrow."

That person is the FDE.

It's not for everyone, but it really is a trend of the future.
