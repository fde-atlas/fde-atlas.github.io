From Coder to FDE? U.S. Stock Leader Pioneers a New Model for AI Deployment; Institutions: Computer Sector May See a Revaluation

① U.S. AI application leader Palantir rose more than 28% cumulatively this week, with its total market capitalization surpassing $380 billion; ② The company's second-quarter earnings report showed total revenue of $1.94 billion, above the market expectation of $1.8 billion, up 94% year-over-year; ③ CEO Alex Karp described the quarter as "beyond imagination" and said the sovereign AI wave makes him "extremely optimistic about the future."

STAR Market Daily, August 6 — Recently, the U.S. AI application leader Palantir has once again drawn market attention: this week, the company has risen more than 28% cumulatively, with its total market capitalization surpassing $380 billion, just one step away from breaking its previous high.

Palantir executive Danny Lutkus attributed this to the company's unique operating model: "Palantir's approach is (completely different from that of typical software companies) — standard solutions simply don't work for many enterprises."

Specifically, Palantir mainly builds systems that help enterprises integrate AI into their existing systems and data. And unlike the mainstream practice in Silicon Valley's software industry, Palantir not only delivers software to clients, but also dispatches "coders" to work long-term inside clients' offices, for periods ranging from several months to several years.

This model is what is known as the FDE.

FDE stands for Forward-Deployed Engineer: traditional outsourcing is mainly accountable for code and contracted workload, whereas an FDE must drive changes in clients' business metrics and distill project experience into reusable methods and product capabilities. The full workflow includes going on-site at the client, discovering real needs, completing solution validation, driving system launch, and gradually converting one-off deliveries into replicable methods and product capabilities.

Recently, Palantir released its second-quarter earnings report: total revenue of $1.94 billion, above the market expectation of $1.8 billion, up 94% year-over-year. The company simultaneously raised its full-year guidance, projecting full-year sales as high as $8.16 billion, above the market consensus of $7.7 billion. Adjusted operating profit is expected at $4.89 billion to $4.91 billion, above the previous forecast range's upper limit of $4.45 billion.

Company CEO Alex Karp described the quarter as "beyond imagination" and said the sovereign AI wave makes him "extremely optimistic about the future." Sovereign AI refers to the fact that major enterprises do not want to hand their data over to frontier AI labs like OpenAI or Google, striving instead for autonomous control over data privacy.

Citi analysts noted in a research report that Palantir has "further undermined the market's bearish thesis on intensifying competition in the AI industry," calling it one of the clearest beneficiaries of the wave of enterprise AI adoption. "Accelerating commercial demand proves the company is enjoying the same industry tailwinds as the leading native AI companies in the market." They added: "The sharp recovery of the U.S. commercial business has dispelled market concerns about slowing growth, and we expect the stock to see significant upside."

Guolian Minsheng Securities pointed out that the core value of the FDE lies in converting model capabilities into business results that can enter enterprises' production systems. The main difficulties in AI deployment today lie not only in model capabilities, but also in whether enterprises can identify real needs, complete scenario validation, integrate with legacy systems, and sustain operations. It bridges the value chain of "traditional software service capabilities — AI project deployment — productization."

The institution further stated that the FDE is poised to become one of the key factors in the revaluation of the computer sector in this cycle. As AI's involvement in software development deepens, the value of traditional software services built solely on code development and manpower is facing reassessment, and the focus of industry competition is gradually shifting to who can understand clients' businesses, drive AI into production environments, and continuously create business results. Competition among AI companies is currently shifting from competing on model parameters to competing on business deployment capabilities, and versatile talent that can understand both technology and business while owning project outcomes remains scarce.

It emphasized that the FDE is poised to become an important organizational form connecting model vendors, software service providers, and industry clients, allowing traditional computer companies' existing client resources, industry experience, implementation teams, and solutions capabilities to regain value in the AI era. When assessing the value of a company's AI business, beyond model and technical capabilities, one should also focus on whether it has an FDE team and whether it can distill project experience into replicable products and industry solutions.
