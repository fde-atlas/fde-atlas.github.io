What is Palantir Foundry? Features, Ontology, and How It's Used in FDE Practice

Original title: Palantir Foundryとは？機能・オントロジーとFDE実務での使い方

FDE総研 · 2026-08-10 · 14 min read

Summary

This article explains Palantir Foundry as an operational platform spanning data integration through business execution, not a BI tool. It details the ontology (object, link, and action types) as the shared core model, contrasts Foundry with Gotham and AIP, and walks through components like Actions, Functions, and the Ontology SDK. From an FDE practice perspective, it covers implementation steps, common pitfalls, and a learning path using official resources.

Key points

  • Foundry's value lies in shortening the distance from analysis to action, not analysis accuracy.
  • The ontology's three elements—object, link, and action types—let screens and AI agents share one business model.
  • Actions provide permissioned, audited write-back; Functions add business logic; OSDK enables typed external access.
  • FDE success is measured by transactions processed on-site, requiring consensus-building alongside technical skill.
  • Typical failures stem from misaligned business vocabulary, scope creep, and unclear roles versus existing BI tools.

What is Palantir Foundry?

Palantir Foundry is Palantir's platform for commercial enterprises that handles everything from data integration to executing business decisions in a single environment.

Palantir Technologies, the company that provides Palantir Foundry, is a US company founded in 2003. Its flagship product for commercial enterprises is Palantir Foundry, while Palantir Gotham targets the government and defense sector (Source: Palantir Technologies official website "Foundry" https://www.palantir.com/platforms/foundry/ / retrieved August 10, 2026). The job title FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) became widely known because implementing Palantir Foundry in customers' operations required a dedicated role. It is easiest to understand that the product's philosophy and the contours of the FDE role were shaped simultaneously.

The most common misconception about Palantir Foundry is that it is a "highly capable BI tool." The center of gravity of its design philosophy lies not in visualization itself, but in the operations that follow visualization. This difference shows up more in operational reality than in feature comparison tables. After deployment, look at who in the field opens which screen how many times, and how many items they process there.

What problems does Palantir Foundry aim to solve?

The main purpose of Palantir Foundry is to bridge the gap between data fragmented across departments and decision-making in the field.

In many companies, core systems, CRM, sensor logs, and Excel each live in separate places. Once data is brought into an analytics platform, it tends to fall into a state where "the numbers come out, but no one acts." Palantir Foundry is designed so that data does not remain merely an object of analysis. First, data is re-linked to business entities (objects) such as inventory, orders, and equipment. Furthermore, operations on those objects (approve, order, assign) are completed on the same platform.

Observing the field, Palantir Foundry is often valued not for "high analytical accuracy" but for "the short distance from analysis results to the next action." The value of eliminating the back-and-forth of looking at a dashboard and then placing an order in another system tends to appear, ahead of KPIs, as reduced operational burden in the field. Conversely, deployments that leave the back-and-forth in place tend to be judged as "just one more place to look."

How do Palantir Foundry, Gotham, and AIP differ?

Palantir Foundry is an operational platform for commercial use, Gotham targets government and defense, and AIP is an AI utilization layer that sits on top of both.

Palantir AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform) is not a separate, independent platform. It is a layer for running LLMs safely on top of the data and ontology held by Palantir Foundry and Palantir Gotham (Source: Palantir Technologies official website "AIP" https://www.palantir.com/platforms/aip/ / retrieved August 10, 2026). The growing number of job postings listing both "Foundry" and "AIP" reflects this two-layer structure as a given.

ProductMain targetPositioning
Palantir FoundryCommercial enterprisesOperational platform covering data integration through business execution
Palantir GothamGovernment and defensePlatform for government and defense
Palantir AIPUsers of Foundry and GothamAI utilization layer that runs LLMs on top of data and ontology

When evaluating Palantir AIP, you should look at the state of the underlying ontology rather than the LLM's standalone performance. If you layer AI on top while the business models it references remain vague, you end up back at the same problem of being unable to trace the basis of outputs. When AIP's name stands out in job postings or proposals, it is easiest to grasp the actual situation by checking who has defined the underlying business models, and to what extent.

How does Palantir Foundry differ from typical data platforms?

Unlike a typical DWH + BI setup, Palantir Foundry's scope extends to executing business operations.

PerspectiveTypical DWH + BI setupPalantir Foundry
Main purposeData accumulation and visualizationData integration through business execution
Data representationTables and columnsOntology (objects and links)
Write-backBasically done in a separate systemExecuted from within the platform via Actions
Permission designCentered on table/row levelGranular down to the object and property level
User profileData engineers, analystsBusiness users including field staff
Deployment modelCompanies assemble tools themselvesProvided as an integrated suite

This comparison organizes the direction of functional design; whether it actually fits your requirements varies by project. Palantir Foundry is an integrated suite, so proposals tend to involve partially replacing an existing modern data stack. Managing that adjustment is the core of an FDE's job. What to keep and what to replace cannot be decided by technical comparison alone; agreement is needed that accounts for existing investments and operational arrangements.

What are Palantir Foundry's main features and the role of the ontology?

The core of Palantir Foundry is the ontology, which serves as a common model that translates raw data into business objects and operations.

What are the main components of Palantir Foundry?

Palantir Foundry is an integrated suite with dedicated components across eight areas, from data connection to AI utilization.

AreaMain componentsRole
Data connectionData ConnectionIngesting data from external systems and databases
PipelinesPipeline Builder / Code RepositoriesTransformation processing via GUI or code (Python, SQL, etc.)
Data modelOntology ManagerDefining object types, properties, and links
AnalysisContour / Quiver / Object ExplorerInteractive analysis, time-series analysis, object exploration
App buildingWorkshop / SlateCreating screens for business users
Execution and automationActions / Functions / AutomateWrite-back processing, logic implementation, automated execution
AI utilizationAIP Logic / AIP Agent StudioLLM logic and agents that reference the ontology
External integrationOntology SDK (OSDK)Operating the ontology with type safety from external applications

Component names and functional scope are updated with each version, so exact specifications must be confirmed in Palantir's official documentation (Source: Palantir Docs https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ / retrieved August 10, 2026). The practical point of this table is that Palantir Foundry includes, within the same suite, not only the "ingest, prepare, view" layers but also the "execute" layer.

Memorizing component names is not a high priority. Rather, whether you can reframe the table as a vertical structure of "which layer, which role" directly affects architecture discussions in projects. Names may change, but the division of roles—connection, transformation, model, analysis, apps, execution—is unlikely to change.

Why is the ontology the core of Palantir Foundry?

Because the ontology converts row data into business vocabulary such as orders, equipment, and shipments, and serves as the common model shared by all features.

The ontology mainly defines the following three elements (Source: Palantir Docs "Ontology" https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/overview / retrieved August 10, 2026).

ElementRepresentsExamples
Object TypeBusiness entitiesOrders, equipment, shipments
Link TypeRelationships between entitiesRelationship between an order and a customer
Action TypePermitted change operations on objectsApprove, place an order, assign

Because of this trio, analysis screens, business screens, and AI agents can all share the same concept of an "order." Conversely, if the ontology is designed poorly, every screen and automation built on top of it becomes distorted. For this reason, in FDE practice, more time is often spent reviewing ontology design than implementing pipelines, since it reduces rework in later stages. Reviews cannot be closed by engineers alone; whether business stakeholders can converse daily using those names becomes the de facto pass/fail criterion.

When in doubt, ask: "Can this name be used as-is in the field's morning stand-up?" If not, it is a table name, not an object name.

What do Actions and Functions enable?

Actions handle write-backs with permissions and auditing, while Functions implement business logic, enabling business processing on the platform.

Actions are a mechanism that defines "who can change which properties under what conditions and how" as types, with permissions and audit logs attached. Functions are written mainly in TypeScript or Python, inserting validation or calculations before and after Actions. Combining the two allows you to move from the stage of "noticing things in BI" to "processing on the platform." When you want to operate the same ontology with type safety from an external application, the Ontology SDK (OSDK) is the entry point.

MechanismRolePractical use
ActionsWrite-back to the ontology (with permissions and audit logs)Completing approvals, ordering, and assignments within the platform
FunctionsImplementing business logic (TypeScript, Python, etc.)Inserting validation or calculations before and after Actions
Ontology SDK (OSDK)Type-safe operations from external applicationsWorking with the same ontology from existing applications

As a first impression, the success of Palantir Foundry projects tends to depend on how closely the write-back design can be aligned with the business side's approval flows. Being technically able to write back and the organization permitting write-backs are separate issues. Building consensus on the latter is also part of an FDE's job. If approvers, executors, and records can be traced by anyone, the burden decreases, including post-launch audit responses.

How is Palantir Foundry used in FDE practice?

FDEs work on-site with customers, using Palantir Foundry to handle everything end-to-end, from data connection to business app implementation and field adoption.

What kind of work do FDEs do with Palantir Foundry?

An FDE's work continues seamlessly, from requirements gathering to data connection, ontology design, app implementation, and on-site adoption support.

Palantir publicly lists Forward Deployed Software Engineer as one of its job roles, describing it as a position that builds software while directly engaging with customer challenges (Source: Palantir Technologies careers page https://www.palantir.com/careers/ / Retrieved: August 10, 2026). In Japan as well, there is a growing number of job postings for roles close to FDE, such as "on-site solution engineer" or "implementation engineer."

PerspectiveTypical data engineerFDE on Palantir Foundry
Main deliverablesPipelines and tablesScreens that field staff open every morning
成果の測り方 データの品質・鮮度 その画面で実際に処理された件数
Required level of expertiseTechnicalTechnical + business understanding and consensus building

The biggest difference from a typical data engineer lies in the definition of the deliverable. Because of this difference, the required skills broaden from purely technical to business understanding as well. It is easy to understand if you think of unused screens as an evaluation axis where, no matter how high the implementation quality, they do not count as results.

How does a Palantir Foundry implementation project typically proceed?

The typical flow is to narrow down to a single business issue, launch a small use case, and then gradually expand it horizontally.

  • Issue identification: Through field observation and interviews, select one place where decision-making is stalled. Do not start with company-wide optimization.
  • Data connection: Connect the minimum necessary sources with Data Connection and verify the actual state of freshness and missing data.
  • Pipeline development: Use Pipeline Builder or Code Repositories to shape the data so it can withstand business vocabulary.
  • Ontology design: Define object types, link types, and action types, aligning them with the business-side terminology.
  • Application implementation: Build field-facing screens in Workshop and set up write-back paths with Actions.
  • Adoption and horizontal expansion: Improve based on actual usage logs and expand to similar operations in adjacent departments.

Among practitioners, it is often said that projects that allocate sufficient time to steps 1 and 4 experience less rework in the later stages. However, the duration of each step depends heavily on the target business and data quality, and no evidence-based standard number of days can be given. When asked for an estimate, it is more realistic to agree in terms of scope—"which business processes will be completed on the platform, and to what extent"—rather than in days.

What pitfalls do FDEs tend to fall into on Palantir Foundry projects?

The reasons Palantir Foundry projects stall are often not technical difficulty but unsettled business vocabulary and scope creep.

There are three typical pitfalls. The first is a pattern where ontology naming is decided solely by the IT department and diverges from field terminology. The second is a pattern where the initial scope includes multiple departments, consuming time on consensus building. The third is a pattern where the division of roles with existing BI tools is left ambiguous, parallel operation continues, and a "which numbers are correct" problem arises.

PitfallSymptoms that surfacePreventive measures
Misaligned business vocabularyScreen item names do not resonate with the fieldReview ontology naming with field staff
Scope creepConsensus building takes time and the start is delayedLimit the initial scope to a single department and a single business process
Running alongside existing BIA "which numbers are correct" problem arisesDecide in advance where the authoritative numbers reside and how roles are divided

None of these are technically difficult, but once they occur, recovery takes time. The reason FDEs are said to need "implementation skills + on-site consensus-building ability" is precisely this structure. Note that the tendencies listed here are a summary of what the editorial team (FDE Soken / Operated by VACAN) judged to be generally plausible, and do not represent the track record of any specific project.

In what order should beginners learn Palantir Foundry?

Learning efficiency improves if you first understand the ontology concepts and then get hands-on with the implementation tools.

  • Understanding concepts: Use the Palantir Blog and official Docs to grasp the ontology concepts (object types, link types, and action types).
  • Data transformation basics: Practice data transformation with SQL and Python hands-on, even in environments outside Palantir Foundry.
  • Official tutorials: Go through the Pipeline Builder to Workshop flow once using the courses on Palantir Learn.
  • Practice app implementation: Pick one small business scenario and build it yourself, from object definitions to writeback via Actions.
  • AI integration: Review how to configure LLM logic that references the ontology, such as AIP Logic.

Some people say that if you skip Stage 2 and jump straight into Stage 3, you can complete the tutorials but cannot apply what you learned. The tool operations of Palantir Foundry itself are among the easier ones to learn. However, the ability to design the data transformations running underneath must be developed separately as a general-purpose skill.

How can you substitute for an environment where you cannot directly access Palantir Foundry?

Even without access to the product, ontology-style design thinking and practice in business interviews can be fully substituted in other environments.

What works well is to try writing a "business object design" rather than a "table design" using data at hand. Summarize entities, relationships, permitted operations, and who performs and approves each operation in a single table. This is almost the same approach as Palantir Foundry's ontology design, and you can practice it without a product license.

Item to write outCorresponding ontology elementExample
EntityObject typeOrder, equipment
RelationshipLink typeOrder and customer
Permitted operationAction typeApprove, assign
Performer and approverActions permission designWho executes and who approves

Furthermore, the core FDE skill of "defining problems in the language of the front line" can be trained simply by interviewing a neighboring department in your organization. While knowledge of Palantir Foundry as a product can be acquired after joining, a large part of business understanding and consensus-building experience can be accumulated beforehand. Even in selection processes, experience in spotting and acting on business bottlenecks is stronger material than proficiency in operating the product.
