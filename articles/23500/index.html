Generative AI certifications are a general term for certifications in which a third-party organization certifies knowledge, skills, and implementation abilities related to generative AI. They can be broadly divided into two types.

① Literacy certifications (business utilization and knowledge-based)

These certifications test knowledge of how AI works, how to use it, ethics, risk management, and more. No programming experience is required, and they target business professionals in all kinds of roles, including sales, marketing, HR, and planning.

Representative examples: Generative AI Passport, G Test, IT Passport (AI field)

② Practical certifications (engineer and technology-based)

These certifications prove technical skills such as deep learning implementation, machine learning system construction, and cloud AI service operation. They often assume knowledge of Python and mathematics, and mainly target engineers and data scientists.

Representative examples: E Certification (JDLA), AWS Certified AI Practitioner, Azure AI Engineer Associate, Google Cloud Professional ML Engineer

Background 1: AI adoption is accelerating across all industries

Generative AI tools like ChatGPT have permeated business operations, widening the gap between people who can use AI and those who cannot. Many companies encourage certification as part of employee training, and more than 120 companies use the Generative AI Passport for group exams, making certification acquisition an organizational movement.

Background 2: Deepening shortage of AI talent

According to a survey by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, an IT talent shortage of up to 790,000 people is predicted by 2030. Demand for people who can handle generative AI and data science is particularly pronounced, and visualizing skills through certifications has become an important metric in hiring and evaluation.

Background 3: Development of AI legal systems and the need for ethical literacy

In June 2025, an AI-related law was enacted in Japan, and companies are now required to establish AI risk management systems. Taking into account international regulatory trends including the EU AI Act, legal systems and ethics fields have been added to the exam scope of AI literacy certifications.

As of March 2026, there is no national certification specialized in generative AI. All current related certifications are private certifications. However, the "IT Passport Exam" promoted by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry includes questions in the AI and data science fields, drawing attention as a certification linked to national policy.

In addition, the G Test and E Certification offered by the Japan Deep Learning Association (JDLA) are aligned with the government's AI strategy, and the G Test is included in "Di Lite" defined by the Digital Literacy Association, so they are spreading with quasi-public standing.

The G Test covers a broad range of topics and requires answering 220 questions in 120 minutes, so strategic studying is essential. However, the pass rate is relatively high at 65-80%, and 30-40 hours of study is enough to pass.

Step 1: Grasp the overall picture with the official textbook (10-15 hours)

First, read through the JDLA-supervised official textbook (e.g., "G Test's Strongest Pass Textbook"). If you struggle with math (partial derivatives, determinants), the key is not to dig too deep into those areas but to proceed with just an understanding that "such concepts exist."

Step 2: Identify weaknesses with workbooks and mock exams (10-15 hours)

Use a workbook (e.g., "Deep Learning G Test Mock Tests") to identify areas with low accuracy. In particular, "AI use cases," "law and ethics," and "latest technology trends" are key areas that appear frequently in every exam.

Step 3: Last-minute preparation using social media and communities (5-10 hours)

If you search Twitter (X) or Qiita with the "#G Test" hashtag, you'll find many summary articles of key points posted by test takers. Reading these intensively during the final week before the exam allows you to efficiently fill in knowledge gaps.

The biggest advantage of self-study for the G Test: You can take the exam from home with reference books at hand (searching on screen is prohibited), so you can approach the exam while having built systematic knowledge during your preparation period.

For obtaining the E Certification, the most important thing is choosing a "JDLA-accredited program." There are multiple accredited programs required to gain exam eligibility, each differing in cost, duration, and content.

Three key points for choosing an accredited program:

① Cost: Programs vary widely, from 50,000 to over 300,000 yen. Choosing a course eligible for the Education and Training Benefit (up to 80% subsidy) can greatly reduce your actual burden

② Duration: There are intensive short-term programs (3-6 months) and long-term programs (about 1 year). Working engineers are often better suited to short-term programs

③ Support: Check the difficulty of the completion exam, the quality of the mentor system, and exam preparation support

Study schedule after completing an accredited program (6-8 weeks until the exam):

  • Weeks 1-2: Review weak areas from the program completion stage. Solidify math fundamentals (linear algebra, calculus)
  • Weeks 3-4: NN implementation exercises in Python (NumPy, PyTorch). Get used to fill-in-the-blank code question formats
  • Weeks 5-6: Use past questions and mock exams to develop a sense of timing. Review the latest methods in natural language processing and image recognition
  • Final 2 weeks: Focused practice on weak areas. Pay attention to managing your condition on exam day
