Cagan's Three Product Roles and the Missing FDE

Falkster.AI · 2026-11-06 · 1 min read

Summary

The article responds to Marty Cagan's November 6, 2026 post on three product manager roles, arguing that the forward deployed engineer (FDE) is missing from his framework. It claims an FDE is essentially a feature team product manager's job—building for one customer against a named problem with a deadline—plus an eval, with the vendor as employer. Citing Chris Taylor in TechCrunch, it notes about 17,000 FDEs in the US, demand up 2,100%, and only about 2,000 delivering real ROI. The eval is written by the FDE with customer experts supplying labels. The author proposes a Product OS framework defining five seats by ownership within one loop, with the FDE owning the customer edge.

Key points

  • Cagan's three PM roles omit the FDE.
  • FDE work resembles feature team PM work plus an eval.
  • About 17,000 FDEs exist in the US; demand rose 2,100%.
  • Only around 2,000 FDEs deliver real ROI.
  • Product OS defines five seats by loop ownership; FDE owns the customer edge.

Full text

THE SHORT ANSWER

No, and that is the problem with sorting the role by operating model. Marty Cagan's November 6, 2026 post says the project model produces the feature team product manager and the product owner, the product model produces the empowered team product manager, and under AI the product owner is being phased out, the feature team product manager's future is least clear, and the empowered team product manager is in demand and at the center of the eval. A forward deployed engineer is the feature team product manager's job, build to earn at one customer against a named problem with a date, with an eval added and the vendor as employer. Per Chris Taylor in TechCrunch, as reported on falkster.com, there are about 17,000 in the US, demand is up 2,100%, and about 2,000 can deliver real ROI. The eval is written by the FDE with the customer's experts supplying labels, which is how Colin Jarvis's team had 60 percent at scoping. The rewrite: the loop drives the seat, and the Product OS defines five seats by what each owns in one loop, with the FDE owning the customer edge.
