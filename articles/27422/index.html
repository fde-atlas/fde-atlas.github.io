From "Delivering Software" to "Co-Creating AI": Zhiyuan Interconnect's FDEs Bring Service to the Customer's Front Line

Original title: 从「交付软件」到「共创AI」：致远互联FDE，把服务做到客户一线

m.stnn.cc · 2026-11-14 · 6 min read

Summary

At its 2026 user conference, Seeyon (致远互联) launched an FDE ecosystem co-creation partnership with eight companies including 深信服 and 天天创科. The article explains its Forward Deployed Engineer model: teams work on-site with customers to co-define requirements, validate scenarios, and run AI capabilities long-term rather than delivering software for acceptance. Case data include CNPC Logging (5-day integration, a "1+9" platform structure), a manufacturing client (80%+ approval accuracy, 50% faster processing), and CNNC Machinery (review time cut from 30 to 10 minutes). Its first AI application competition produced 42 scenarios, 49 teams, and 88 reusable solutions. Three partners—天天创科, 未来式智能 (AutoAgents.AI), and 天赋智源 (Ai4X)—shared practices, agreeing that industry know-how requires platform, AI capability, and domain experience combined.

Full text

There is no standard answer for AI implementation; the answer lies on the customer's front line. On September 11, 2026, at the main forum of "Making Organizational AI Land — 2026 Intelligent Collaborative Operations Forum and Seeyon's 16th User Conference," Seeyon joined hands with Caizhi Technology, Fangcun Wuyou, Sangfor, Zhang Guanjia, Beijing Jizhi Technology, Beijing Future Intelligence, Shenzhen Tiantian Chuangke, Tianfu Zhiyuan, and Jiangxi Tongyou to jointly launch the "FDE Ecosystem Co-Creation Strategic Partnership."

The FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) service model refers to a team integrating "AI technology + industry business + value delivery" that goes deep into the customer's site, working with the customer to jointly complete requirements co-creation, solution design, scenario validation, and continuous operations, moving AI from "demonstrable" to "deployable, operable, and replicable."

Unlike the traditional project approach of "confirm requirements first, then develop back at the office, and finally deliver for acceptance," the FDE team does not take requirements back to work on; instead, it stays on-site with the customer to define requirements together: business experts, AI technology experts, and customer business staff work at the same location, validating and adjusting scenarios as they go. What is delivered is not a feature list awaiting acceptance, but AI capabilities that can run within the customer's organization and be operated sustainably.

Service upgrade: from "product-type" to "AI service-type"

In the AI era, "using AI to write reports and do analysis" has become an office routine. But organization-level AI implementation remains a different challenge: general AI tools can boost individual productivity, yet struggle to embed themselves into an enterprise's processes, permissions, data, and accountability systems; no matter how powerful a large model is, it does not understand the business rules an enterprise has accumulated over many years. One of the key problems organizational AI implementation must solve is how to translate business know-how into AI-executable capabilities.

This work needs to be done on the customer's site.

At the conference, Seeyon announced its AI strategic positioning — building an intelligent collaborative operations hub to make organizational AI land. The service approach was adjusted accordingly: the FDE service model is positioned as the "AI forward deployment center and the co-creation hub for organizational AI implementation." Around this positioning, the FDE team will ensure customers' AI value lands, drive asset accumulation to feed back into R&D, and deliver the AI-FDE methodology to partners.

Under the FDE model, delivery is not the endpoint but the starting point for the continuous growth of the customer's AI capabilities, with services shifting from one-off projects to long-term operations.

Currently, Seeyon's FDE has built an expert team covering key scenarios, whose capability structure comes from more than twenty years of composite talent reserves serving state-owned enterprises and government clients — a team that understands both platform technology and organizational authority, responsibilities, and process systems. It has also established an integrated FDE toolchain: built on the CoMi AI Platform, the code branch responds to customer needs on one end and feeds back into the platform on the other. In terms of methodology, AI end-to-end delivery has been standardized into four steps: AI opportunity discovery, AI solution design, AI quoting, and AI delivery.

Customer co-creation: making organizational AI truly land

In the CNPC Logging project, after FDE engineers completed an on-site IT status diagnosis, they finished integration validation in 5 days using the CoMi AI Platform and CoMi Work; nearly 30 systems required no modification, supporting unified construction across 13 branch companies, ultimately forming a "1+9" structure — one enterprise-level integrated AI platform plus nine intelligent scenarios. The head of the customer's IT department commented: "FDE co-creation has truly achieved the shift from 'project delivery' to 'capability building'... making our AI strategy truly land."

In the intelligent expense reimbursement scenario of a leading international manufacturer, the FDE team worked with the customer to organize the scattered experience in reimbursement review into unified rules, evidence, and review results, achieving an intelligent approval accuracy rate above 80%, a 40% reduction in rejection rates, 17,000 documents processed monthly, a 50% improvement in approval efficiency, and rules covering 118 major categories and 675 subcategories; in the CNNC Machinery project, the FDE team on-site connected over 50,000 knowledge sources, solidifying procurement review judgments that previously relied on personal experience into a verifiable process, reducing average review time per item from 30 minutes to 10 minutes, and cutting the rejection rate from 60% to 20%.

In addition, projects such as the Hunan Provincial Public Security Department's "AI Xiao Jing," the Xianyang Municipal Data Bureau's AI + public document library intelligent governance, the Deyang Municipal Government Office's AI + fair competition review assistant, Changhong Jiahua's AI + marketing intelligent assistant, and the Southwest Oil and Gas Field's AI + intelligent contracts have landed one after another. FDE customers now cover central and state-owned enterprises, government agencies, education and research institutions, financial institutions, manufacturers, and group enterprises. Data from the FDE Business Unit shows that pure AI projects undertaken by headquarters currently involve 11 customers.

Results review: 42 scenarios, 49 teams, 88 solutions

Seeyon's first AI Application Innovation Competition lasted three months, co-organized by Seeyon and the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology's Zhuji Program, with "challenge-based bidding" as the core mechanism: customers post the challenges, FDE teams take them on, and all challenges come from real business scenarios with real project budgets.

The competition ultimately produced three sets of numbers: 42 real business scenarios; 49 FDE co-creation teams composed of internal R&D, pre-sales, delivery, and regional staff along with external partners; and 88 reusable AI solutions that, after tiered certification, remain available year-round, forming a closed loop of "competition development — landing delivery — nationwide reuse — sustained revenue growth."

At the Delivery and FDE sub-forum, three partners — Shenzhen Tiantian Chuangke, Beijing Future Intelligence, and Tianfu Zhiyuan — shared their co-creation journeys.

Shenzhen Tiantian Chuangke

From doing projects to building a platform

Shenzhen Tiantian Chuangke has been deeply engaged in the industry inspection field for many years, having implemented four types of scenarios including ecological and environmental protection inspections and small-scale engineering supervision, with more than 5,000 AI sensing devices connected. Engineering Director Li Chengbin summarized the difficulties in four points: little information, mixed scenarios, many rules, and low fault tolerance. To address these, Tiantian Chuangke uses large-small model collaboration to form a judgment process of finding the basis first, then generating conclusions.

In its presentation, Tiantian Chuangke pointed out that customers need a complete closed loop from discovering problems to solving them, not single-point identification. This closed loop is achieved through three-party co-construction under the FDE model: Seeyon provides the collaborative portal and process engine, Tiantian Chuangke provides multimodal recognition and AI pre-review of reports, and the customer is responsible for scenario definition and landing validation. The partnership with Seeyon has driven its shift from doing projects to building a platform.

Beijing Future Intelligence

One modular platform, six scenarios running through in one go

Future Intelligence (AutoAgents.AI) introduced at the event that in September 2023 the company released its enterprise-level agent production platform "Lingda." What "Lingda" must cross is the "last mile" from large models to scenario applications: enterprises' legacy systems have inconsistent interfaces, and many intranet systems without APIs are hard to reach — no matter how strong the model's capabilities, it cannot enter the business scene.

The approach is FDE-style: Future Intelligence entered the customer's site together with Seeyon's FDE team, using a modular platform that can be combined on demand to connect systems — edge-side agents connect to API-less intranet systems from the client side, with modules for knowledge, orchestration, models, tools, and security governance independently pluggable, and full-stack adaptation to nine domestic chips and more than ten domestic models. Six scenarios thus ran through in one go, covering discrete manufacturing work-hour prediction and quoting, SEM defect analysis, and bidding and evaluation assistance, improving compilation efficiency by more than 100 times. These scenarios share the same pipeline — AI finds evidence, rules do the calculations, experts confirm. Three lessons: see the big through the small, co-build standards, and keep the pace transparent.

Tianfu Zhiyuan

Building digital employees deep into business systems

Tianfu Zhiyuan (Ai4X) focuses on industrial digital labor, providing job-role agent services for industrial clusters such as discrete manufacturing, process manufacturing, and energy. From the day it was founded, it has refused to make demo-version AI assistants, aiming instead to build self-driven general agents capable of entering enterprises' core businesses.

The difficulty lies in real quoting scenarios: for new part quotations at a leading aviation industrial enterprise, similar parts had to be manually searched among 20,000 historical drawings, process additions and deletions and work-hour estimates relied on veteran engineers' experience, finalized processes and prices were scattered everywhere, and when a similar part came again, everything started from scratch. Tianfu Zhiyuan entered with an FDE staffing structure — one job-role business expert plus one AI technology expert, supported by several AI digital employees, with the customer's real data involved throughout and every step acceptance-tested by the business side. The award-winning work, the "Intelligent Drawing-Based Process Work-Hour Prediction and Dual-Plan Quoting System," follows three principles: a deterministic engine makes the ruling, dual plans are compared, and humans stay in the loop. From receiving the data to deployment took about two weeks, with zero error in reproducing historical prices in the database.

"What Zhiyuan Interconnect's ecosystem needs most is delivery capability that goes deep into core business systems. OA serves as the task entry point and process carrier, while enterprise-grade AI agents act as executors embedded in business systems. The two are inherently complementary," said Tianfu Zhiyuan.

One company does multimodal inspection, one builds an agent platform, and one creates enterprise digital employees. Their paths differ, but their conclusion is the same: industry know-how cannot be replaced by general-purpose capabilities, and converting it into runnable systems requires the combined efforts of a platform foundation, AI capabilities, and domain experience.

Customer scenarios vary widely, and no single company can cover the know-how of every industry. Bringing partners into the FDE system—unifying methodology, sharing reusable assets, and jointly entering customer sites—transforms the service provider from a single company into a collaborative network. Supporting this system are five service items: opportunity sharing, project profit-sharing, dedicated support, conversion pathways, and customer benchmarks. From delivering software to co-creating AI, from "delivery means the end" to "delivery means the beginning," what is changing is not only the way services are delivered but also the collaborative relationship between software companies and their customers. AI implementation is a long-term endeavor, and Zhiyuan Interconnect will continue to collaborate with customers and ecosystem partners throughout the process.

(Zhiyuan Interconnect Dongtai Bao)
