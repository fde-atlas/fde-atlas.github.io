"The true value of an FDE lies in finding the issues that truly matter" — Ms. Onda of LayerX

Original title: 「FDEの真価は、本当に価値のあるイシューを見つけること」LayerX恩田さん｜FDEの走り書き

Summary

This Findy Media interview opens the "FDE no Soukaki" series with Takayasu Onda, a Forward Deployed Engineer at LayerX's Ai Workforce unit. He explains that LayerX modeled its approach on Palantir around 2020 but only published an FDE job description in July 2025 to popularize the role in Japan. The role pairs with Deployment Strategists, handling AI workflow building, LLM tuning, and accuracy evaluation, with the core skill being identifying which customer issues create real business value. LayerX's own platform, Ai Workforce, ships enterprise features like access control and storage integrations, letting FDEs skip generic development. He also discusses a leasing-industry solution, periods spent purely on platform development, and advice that candidates should verify role details beyond job titles.

Key points

  • LayerX referenced Palantir's FDE model around 2020 but published its FDE job description only in July 2025.
  • FDEs work with Deployment Strategists, owning AI workflow building, LLM tuning, and evaluation.
  • The core FDE skill is spotting which customer issues genuinely raise business value.
  • LayerX's Ai Workforce platform pre-builds enterprise features, letting FDEs focus on customer-specific problems.
  • Onda warns job seekers to confirm actual duties in interviews rather than trusting job titles.

The Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a new profession attracting attention in the AI era. Companies in Japan are increasingly hiring FDEs, but the reality of the role is still not widely known. FDEs embed themselves on the front lines with customers and bridge the last mile of putting AI into practice in society—what do they think about, and how do they approach problem-solving?

In this series, "FDE Scribbles," we follow the trial and error that the people running through high-uncertainty environments can only talk about now. For our first installment, we spoke with Takayasu Onda, Forward Deployed Engineer in the Product Department of the Ai Workforce Business Unit at LayerX, Inc.—arguably one of the pioneers of the FDE role in Japan.

What Is "Ai Workforce"?

"Ai Workforce" is an AI platform developed and provided by LayerX, specialized for enterprise companies. It learns a company's proprietary knowledge and operational know-how, creating an environment where "AI works" within the company.

> "Ai Workforce" service site (https://getaiworkforce.com)

Poured Our Soul into the Job Posting to Build Awareness of FDEs

— Your company published a Job Description for FDEs (job posting; hereinafter "JD") in July 2025, and you are a leading figure in popularizing FDEs in Japan. Please tell us about your thinking at the time you introduced the job title "FDE," which was still largely unknown.

Actually, since around 2020, when we were driving the blockchain business that became the predecessor of "Ai Workforce," we had been referencing Palantir, the US company that created the FDE model, and we even made declarations like "We will become the blockchain version of Palantir." I considered my own work to be FDE work since then, but awareness of FDEs in Japan was so low that I thought "no one will apply if we say FDE," so we used titles like "AI Workflow Engineer" and "LLM Engineer."

But at some point the conversation turned to "If it isn't trending yet, don't we need the determination to make it trend ourselves?"—and that's how we came to publish the FDE JD. At the same time, we appeared on LayerX's Podcast as well as external YouTube channels and podcasts, creating opportunities to talk about FDEs.*

When we first published the JD, it generated some buzz in VC circles, and from then on, I gradually got the impression that we increasingly heard people in SI, consulting, technical advisory, and similar fields—people who take pride in solving customer problems with technology—say things like "Maybe the work I'm doing is actually FDE work."

— Over the past year, awareness has steadily spread, and interest from engineers and consultants has grown. Was there anything you particularly paid attention to in expanding awareness?

We wrote the JD with great care so that our conception of the FDE model would be communicated correctly.

That's because in September 2024, Ted Mabrey, then Palantir's Global Head of Commercial, published an article titled 『Sorry, that isn't an FDE』. It criticized other companies for superficially imitating FDEs, and we didn't want to be seen the same way.

Our CTO Matsumoto told me to "get it out quickly," but I told him "If we're going to publish it, I want to do it properly," and we spent about two weeks carefully considering it.

The Strength of "Ai Workforce" FDEs: A Platform Honed In-House

— More companies in Japan are hiring FDEs, but the definition of an FDE and the role required seem to differ from company to company. What is an FDE in the Ai Workforce Business Unit?

In the Ai Workforce Business Unit, FDEs are the people who take technical responsibility for the production deployment and delivery of "Ai Workforce" while solving customers' critical business challenges.

On projects, they typically join from the initial stages of an engagement and work hand in hand with a Deployment Strategist (DS). While there is some overlap in their roles, the DS handles areas closer to consulting and project management—such as as-is analysis, to-be design, breaking down business processes, and overall project design—while FDEs support problem-solving from areas closer to engineering, such as building AI workflows and agents, tuning LLM algorithms, and evaluating accuracy. FDEs also personally carry out feature development of Ai Workforce itself that arises during the course of a project.

The work of an FDE spans a wide range, but the heart of the job is "discerning the issues that can most increase the customer's business value." Identifying where in the customer's operations a change would create value, and how to use LLMs to achieve fundamental improvement—that is the greatest contribution of value to the customer and lies at the core of the business.

— I see, that connects with how you often describe FDEs as being like an "external CTO." So what do you think is your company's FDE strength that others don't have?

Above all, it's the fact that we have a platform product called "Ai Workforce." Our main customers are major Japanese companies that represent Japan—finance, trading companies, and so on—and these enterprise companies have their own particular requirements.

For example, access permission design is essential, and system integration with storage services like SharePoint and Box, as well as export features to Word/Excel, are functions every customer needs.

"Ai Workforce" comes with these features—needed before solving problems with AI—built into the platform. An SIer would develop all of this from scratch, resulting in projects that take a year or more, but we can shortcut that and get straight to solving each customer's unique challenges. Our greatest strength is that FDEs themselves build the platform that empowers the FDE role.

— Please tell us about a success story where you were able to leverage these strengths.

The "Ai Workforce Leasing Solution" is a symbolic example of this. We generalized the use cases of leasing companies and implemented them into the platform as a solution. Because AI workflows specialized in asset management operations are pre-built, companies in the same industry can start using "Ai Workforce" right after implementation by simply configuring the fine details to fit their own business.

> "Ai Workforce Leasing Solution" (official Ai Workforce website)

— I see. Having generalizable workflows makes it a business model that scales easily. Conversely, what challenges or areas for growth do you see?

"Ai Workforce" is a product that has only been out for about two years, so it is truly in the phase of being nurtured going forward. Like the "Ai Workforce Leasing Solution," we want to increase AI workflows specialized in specific industries and operations, maturing it into a platform that can handle a wider range of use cases.

However, that evolution comes with difficulties. The pace of change surrounding AI and LLMs is so fast that the product we worked hard to develop could itself become a liability. If that happens, we will need the attitude of aiming forward relentlessly, even if it means breaking the product.

Right now, the world as a whole is still in the stage of trial and error over "how to turn LLMs into value." But waiting for the answer to be settled before systematizing would be too late. Commoditized solutions are never paid high usage fees. That is exactly why FDEs move faster and more than anyone else, discover issues the world has not yet noticed and how to solve them, and strive to implement them into products as quickly as possible.

For business growth, sometimes focusing exclusively on product development

— I heard that for the last three months, you have been primarily responsible for developing "Ai Workforce" itself. So even as an FDE, there are periods when you don't face customers and focus on product development.

Basically, I am assigned to customer projects, but I judged that the feature currently under development is directly tied to business growth, so I decided to focus on it.

It is rare to be devoted to platform development the way I am, but when FDEs find a feature the product needs in a customer project, they build it themselves and submit a pull request. That kind of behavior is expected of FDEs in the Ai Workforce business unit.

— So FDEs have diverse roles. Personally, which do you prefer: facing customers or polishing the product?

When you draw a vertical axis of "can do / can't do" and a horizontal axis of "want to do / don't want to do," I want to stay at the origin. I have come to think that I want to prioritize my work based not on subjective preferences, but on whether it contributes to business growth and benefits customers.

This is a value I have cultivated over the roughly six years since joining the company. Recently there is a rumor that I smile too little because I value objectivity so much (laughs), but my thinking has become constantly simple.

— Please tell us about your business strategy and what you are focusing on as an FDE.

As a business strategy, we aim to get deeper into customers' core businesses and critical operations to deliver greater value. The impact of improvement differs greatly between operations that occur only a few times a year and operations that form the core of the business. It also leads to business revenue, and aligns with what we FDEs want to do. The very act of FDEs discovering valuable problems and defining the themes to solve forms the foundation of the Ai Workforce business.

Personally, I also want to focus on improving the engineering for using LLMs with high accuracy and stability. Even now, when automating with AI workflows, there are still many cases that ultimately require human review.

For example, when having it write tech blogs, it may include unintended context; when reading quotations, it may misread dotted lines in boxes as commas and fail to read numbers accurately — there are still many scenes that require human eyes. LLMs are components that exhibit probabilistic behavior, so engineering is needed to control them automatically and with retry logic so they can be handled deterministically. By using technology to minimize the fluctuation in LLM accuracy as much as possible, I want to expand the areas where they can be trusted even in enterprise operations where mistakes are not tolerated.

— How do you hone these engineering skills?

After all, the most important thing is to be on projects and encounter real cases.

Honestly speaking, in past projects there have been cases where I could not break through problems as I hoped. However, our R&D team is re-challenging these unsolved problems for the sake of the product's development. There is so much to learn from such live issues.

On the other hand, for cases we have solved, we also pursue a patent strategy, obtaining patents. According to our IP team, the number of patent applications is growing rapidly right now, and what is valued most in patent registration is the specificity and reality of the problem — what problem was solved.

Building a track record and making "LayerX FDE" a brand

— I believe more and more companies will adopt the FDE model going forward. From your perspective, how do you view the current state of FDEs in Japan?

I am very happy that so many people have taken an interest. What worries me a little, though, is that from a job seeker's standpoint, it may be easy for situations like "I changed jobs, but it wasn't what I expected" to occur.

For example, even if an FDE job description is a position that simultaneously takes on three roles — platform engineering, solutions architect, and software engineering — depending on circumstances, the work can end up skewed toward one of them. My situation of working on platform engineering for the last three months falls into this category, and if such skew is not what the person intended, it can easily lead to a gap after joining.

Also, in our company, we consider it good for FDEs to develop features themselves and submit pull requests, but there are surely companies that have a division of labor where feature requests are passed to a PdM.

In other words, what I want to convey to job seekers is: don't judge by the job description title alone, and be more careful about aligning expectations during interviews.

Right now, the FDE role is a box of everyone's dreams, with each company stuffing its own ideals into it. But over time, patterns will emerge. When that happens, I want to solidify the branding that "LayerX's FDEs are different from the rest." To do that, we need real results to back it up, so for now we're focused on increasing our volume of work and building the "LayerX FDE brand" in both name and substance.

— Finally, could you share a message for those interested in FDE roles?

I feel that AI and LLMs are changing the work environment, and more people are rethinking their careers. Recently, we've received applications from people working at cloud vendors and consulting firms, as well as freelancers in roles like technical advisors.

There are two major attractions of working as an FDE at our company. First, since we have our own platform, there's a hands-on feeling of being able to develop things ourselves, not just submit feature requests. Second, you can hone the universal skill of identifying valuable issues—something that AI and LLMs cannot replace.

We want to grow together as a team with members from diverse backgrounds, supporting one another. If you're looking to expand your career, please don't hesitate to reach out.
