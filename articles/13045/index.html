Overview of the Transformer Architecture | FDE Learning Center

Original title: Transformer 架构概述 | FDE 学习中心

luoboask.github.io · 5 min read

Summary

This tutorial from the FDE Learning Center targets forward deployed engineers preparing for interviews, explaining Transformer architecture through the lens of inference optimization. It compares encoder-only, encoder-decoder, and decoder-only designs, explains why LLMs use decoder-only, and analyzes the two inference phases: prefill is compute-bound while decode is memory-bound and takes 85-95% of total time. It provides a KV Cache memory formula, worked examples with Llama 3 70B, and optimization techniques like GQA, quantization, and continuous batching, plus interview answer frameworks.

Key points

  • LLMs use decoder-only because training and inference are simpler and scaling is best.
  • Prefill is compute-bound; decode is memory-bound, dominating 85-95% of inference time.
  • KV Cache consumes 60-80% of inference memory; GQA shrinks it by the group count.
  • Worked example: Llama 3 70B decode step takes ~70ms on A100, GPU utilization under 1%.
  • Key optimizations: KV quantization, PagedAttention, chunked prefill, continuous batching.

Core Concepts​

Why FDEs Need to Understand Transformers​

Interviews won't ask you to derive the Attention formula by hand, but they will ask:

  • "Why does inference latency spike with long contexts?"
  • "What impact do GQA and MHA have on deployment?"
  • "Why is the decode phase memory-bound?"

Core principle: only by understanding computational characteristics can you find optimization opportunities.

Transformer Architecture Variants​

ArchitectureInput/OutputRepresentative ModelsWhy/Why Not Used for LLMs
Encoder-onlyText → representation vectorsBERTGood at understanding, not suited for generation
Encoder-DecoderText → textT5, BARTSuited for translation/summarization, but slow inference (requires both Encoder and Decoder passes)
Decoder-onlyText → textGPT, Llama, QwenAutoregressive generation is simple and efficient, and it performs best under scaling laws

Why do LLMs almost all use Decoder-only?

  • Simple training: only requires Causal LM Loss (predicting the next token), no additional alignment tasks needed
  • Simple inference: autoregressive generation, each step only needs the Decoder, no Encoder-Decoder cross attention
  • Optimal under Scaling Laws: the Chinchilla paper shows Decoder-only performs best at equal parameter counts
  • Flexible context: supports in-context learning, with strong zero-shot/few-shot capabilities

Complete Data Flow: From Token to Logit​

Attention Formula Explained​

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QK^T / sqrt(d_k)) V

Dimensions:

  • Q: after linear projection [batch, seq_len, d_model], reshaped to [batch, seq_len, num_heads, head_dim], then transposed to [batch, num_heads, seq_len, head_dim]
  • K: same transformation process as Q
  • V: same transformation process as Q
  • QK^T: [batch, num_heads, seq_len, seq_len] — the attention score matrix
  • softmax(...) normalizes along the last dimension (the key dimension)

Why divide by

sqrt(d_k)

？

If

Q

and

K

are random variables with mean 0 and variance 1, then

Q·K^T

the variance of each element is

d_k

. When

d_k

is large, the dot product values become very large, causing

softmax

to enter the saturation region (gradients close to 0), i.e., the

梯度消失

problem. Dividing by

sqrt(d_k)

renormalizes the variance to 1, keeping the softmax inputs in an appropriate range.

The Two Phases of Inference​

This is the most important concept in FDE interviews:

Prefill​

  • Processes all prompt tokens at once, computed in parallel
  • Complexity: Attention part O(seq_len^2), FFN part O(seq_len)
  • Bottleneck: compute-bound, GPU utilization can reach 80-95%
  • Executed only once, typically 5-15% of total inference time

Decode​

  • Generates tokens one at a time, each step depending on the previous step's result (autoregressive)
  • Each step's seq_len = number of generated tokens + 1 (growing step by step)
  • Every step must load all model weights to the GPU
  • Bottleneck: memory-bound, GPU utilization is typically only 10-30%
  • Executed output_length times, accounting for 85-95% of total inference time

KV Cache (Core Optimization)​

What It Is​

In the decode phase, the Keys and Values of all previous tokens are cached, avoiding redundant matrix multiplications in Attention for historical tokens that have already been computed.

Memory Calculation​

KV Cache 显存 = 2 × num_layers × batch_size × seq_len × num_kv_heads × head_dim × bytes_per_element

其中：
  - 2 是因为 K 和 V 各存一份
  - bytes_per_element: FP16 = 2, FP8 = 1, INT8 = 1

Considering the GQA case: in GQA models

num_kv_heads = num_q_heads / groups

, so the actual KV Cache is G times smaller than with MHA.

Example calculation: Llama 3 70B (GQA, 8 KV groups)

num_layers = 80
num_kv_heads = 8 (GQA: 32 Q heads / 8 groups = 4 Q per group)
head_dim = 128
batch_size = 32
seq_len = 8192
dtype = FP16 (2 bytes)

KV Cache = 2 × 80 × 32 × 8192 × 8 × 128 × 2
         = 2 × 80 × 32 × 8192 × 8 × 128 × 2
         = 2 × 80 × 32 × 8192 × 2048
         = 2 × 80 × 32 × 16,777,216
         = 2 × 80 × 536,870,912
         = 85,899,345,920 bytes
         ≈ 80 GB

The KV Cache typically accounts for 60-80% of inference memory. This is the core battleground for FDE optimization.

Quantitative Analysis of Why Decode Is Memory-Bound​

Taking Llama 3 70B as an example, the computation and data movement for a single decode step:

模型参数量: 70B (FP16 = 140 GB 权重)
每步 FLOPs ≈ 2 × params (前向传播) = 140 GFLOPs

假设 GPU: A100, 理论峰值 312 TFLOPs, 显存带宽 2.0 TB/s

计算所需时间: 140 GFLOPs / 312 TFLOPs ≈ 0.45 ms
权重加载时间: 140 GB / 2.0 TB/s = 70 ms

实际耗时 ≈ 70 ms（权重加载占主导）
GPU 利用率 ≈ 0.45 / 70 ≈ 0.6%

这就是为什么 decode 是 memory-bound：
  计算量极小但需要搬运海量权重，GPU 算术单元大部分时间在空闲。

Compared to prefill (seq_len=4096):

Prefill FLOPs ≈ 2 × params × seq_len ≈ 140 GFLOPs × 4096 ≈ 574 TFLOPs
计算时间: 574 TFLOPs / 312 TFLOPs ≈ 1.84 s
权重加载: 140 GB / 2.0 TB/s = 70 ms (只需加载一次)

GPU 利用率 ≈ 1.84 / (1.84 + 0.07) ≈ 96%

Prefill 是 compute-bound：计算量远大于数据搬运。

Deployment Optimization Directions​

  • Reduce KV Cache: GQA/MQA, KV Cache quantization, PagedAttention
  • Improve Prefill Efficiency: Chunked Prefill (process long prompts in segments to avoid KV Cache peaks)
  • Improve Decode Efficiency: Continuous Batching (dynamically merge decode steps from different requests)
  • Weight Optimization: Weight quantization (INT8/FP8/INT4), tensor parallelism

Interview Perspective​

How Interviewers Ask​

Q1: "Explain the two phases of Transformer inference and the characteristics of each."

Framework for a perfect answer:

  • Prefill: processes all prompt tokens in parallel, compute-bound, O(n^2) complexity, executed only once
  • Decode: generates tokens one by one, memory-bound, loads all weights at every step, executed output_length times
  • Decode accounts for 85-95% of total time and is the optimization focus

Q2: "Given a 70B model, batch=64, seq_len=32K, FP16, with GQA (8 groups), estimate the KV Cache size?"

Framework for a perfect answer:

  • Write out the formula: 2 × num_layers × batch × seq_len × num_kv_heads × head_dim × 2 bytes
  • Plug in Llama 3 70B parameters: 80 layers, num_kv_heads=8, head_dim=128
  • Calculation: 2 × 80 × 64 × 32768 × 8 × 128 × 2 ≈ 343 GB
  • Conclusion: it doesn't fit on a single GPU; you need multiple GPUs or a smaller batch/seq_len

Q3: "Why is decode memory-bound rather than compute-bound?"

Framework for a perfect answer:

  • Decode requires about 2 × params FLOPs per step, but needs to load all weights of params × 2 bytes
  • Take the A100 as an example: computation takes only 0.45ms, while weight transfer takes 70ms
  • The GPU's arithmetic units spend 99% of their time waiting for data to be transferred from HBM
  • Therefore, the optimization direction is improving bandwidth utilization (quantization to reduce data volume, tensor parallelism to share bandwidth pressure)

Q4: "Why do all LLMs use the Decoder-only architecture? Isn't Encoder-Decoder good?"

Perfect answer:

  • Decoder-only is simpler to train (only requires Causal LM Loss)
  • Inference only needs the Decoder, no cross attention required
  • Best performance under Scaling Laws
  • Supports in-context learning
  • Encoder-Decoder requires Encoder pre-encoding + Decoder generation + Cross Attention, resulting in high deployment complexity

Best Practices​

Tuning Recommendations​

  • batch_size: larger batches yield higher throughput, but KV Cache grows linearly. For a 70B model on A100, batch ≤ 32 is recommended
  • max_seq_len: set according to business needs; don't blindly set it to the model's maximum. KV Cache grows linearly with seq_len
  • chunked prefill: enable when prompts exceed 8K to avoid OOM caused by KV Cache peaks
  • continuous batching: always enable; dynamically merges decode steps from different requests, improving throughput 2-3x

Pitfall Guide​

  • Don't use MHA models for long-context inference (KV Cache explosion)
  • Don't allocate all GPU memory to KV Cache at once; leave 10-20% for intermediate activations
  • Monitor GPU utilization: below 20% during the decode phase is normal (determined by its memory-bound nature)
  • Choose H100 over A100 for inference: HBM3 bandwidth is 1.5x higher, directly benefiting decode
