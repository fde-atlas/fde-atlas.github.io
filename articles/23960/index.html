What's the Difference Between an FDE and an AI Engineer? Comparing Roles, Skills, and Suitability

Original title: FDEとAIエンジニアの違いは？役割・スキル・向き不向きを比較

FDE総研 · 2026-10-05 · 9 min read

Summary

This article compares FDEs and AI engineers, using Palantir's organizational model as a reference point. The key distinction lies in what counts as 'done': FDEs are accountable for changing the customer's actual business operations, while AI engineers are judged on model accuracy, latency, cost, and quality. The article contrasts their responsibilities, proximity to customers, and skill emphasis—FDEs need problem definition and client-facing skills; AI engineers need depth in model design and evaluation. It outlines a four-step path for AI engineers moving into FDE roles and notes that since 2025, companies like OpenAI and Anthropic also hire FDEs, though job content varies widely, so candidates should judge roles by actual duties rather than titles.

Key points

  • FDEs own customer outcomes; AI engineers own model and feature quality.
  • Palantir called on-site engineers 'Delta', the origin of the FDE role.
  • FDEs prioritize problem definition and client alignment; AI engineers prioritize evaluation and optimization.
  • Career switch path: gain customer exposure, document results, broaden technical range, screen employers.
  • OpenAI and Anthropic now hire FDEs, but role content varies—judge by duties, not titles.

Both FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) and AI engineers are roles that "make AI usable." For this reason, job postings alone make the differences hard to see. In reality, they differ greatly in what they are responsible for, how close they are to customers, and how they are evaluated. This article compares the roles, skills, and fit of the two, using the organizational model of Palantir, the company that popularized the FDE role, as a reference. The second half also outlines the steps for moving from AI engineer to FDE.

What's the difference between an FDE and an AI engineer?

An FDE is a role responsible for solving problems in the customer's environment, all the way to results. An AI engineer is responsible for building and improving models and AI features.

Even though their technologies overlap, they differ in "what counts as done." For an FDE, done means the customer's business actually changes. For an AI engineer, done means the model or feature reaches the required accuracy and quality.

Difference in scope of responsibility: "customer outcomes" vs "model and feature quality"

An FDE's responsibility is outcomes in the customer's business. An AI engineer's responsibility is the quality of models and AI features—the evaluation criteria are fundamentally different.

Comparison itemFDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)AI engineer
Main responsibilitySolve customer business problems and deliver resultsDesign, implement, and improve accuracy of models and AI features
Starting point of workProblems discovered in the customer's environmentProduct requirements and research themes

Commonly evaluated metricsAdoption and retention, customer business improvement, contract renewalAccuracy, latency, cost, reproducibility

Involvement in generalizationFeed insights from the field back into the productBuild features as generalized capabilities

Having touched both roles in the field, I feel the FDE's role is to "build the right thing for that particular customer." An AI engineer, on the other hand, builds "the right thing for many customers." Even when building the same LLM application, the criteria for prioritizing work differ.

Difference in workplace and proximity to customers

FDEs work embedded at customer sites or within customer data environments. AI engineers spend most of their time developing within their own team.

It is known from statements by people associated with Palantir that the company distinguished between engineers who embed with customers, called "Delta," and engineers who develop the product, called "Dev." The prototype of the FDE lies on the Delta side. This way of working involves being stationed in or frequently visiting customer offices or secure environments, handling everything from data integration to application building.

AI engineers also have occasions to hear customer feedback. However, in most cases, requirements reach them through PdMs or sales. FDEs get involved from the earlier stage of "what is the real problem," before requirements are even defined, making them one step closer to the customer.

How do required skills differ between FDEs and AI engineers?

Knowledge of Python and LLMs is common to both. On top of that, FDEs are tested on problem definition and customer engagement, while AI engineers are tested on depth in model design and evaluation.

Both are assumed to be "engineers who can code." The difference lies in whether you prioritize technical depth or breadth of scope and interpersonal skills.

Technical skills required across the board

The shared foundation for both is Python, SQL, cloud, and an understanding of how LLMs work. If this foundation is weak, neither role will accept you.

  • Programming: The ability to carry implementations through to the end in Python, TypeScript, etc.
  • Data processing: SQL, data pipelines, data quality checks
  • Cloud infrastructure: Experience building and operating on AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud
  • LLM usage: Prompt design, RAG, agent architectures, and evaluation basics
  • Security awareness: Access control, handling of personal and confidential data

Skills whose emphasis differs between FDEs and AI engineers

FDEs place greater weight on problem definition, business understanding, and building consensus with customers. AI engineers place greater weight on model evaluation and optimization.

In practical terms, the biggest differentiator for FDEs is the ability to bridge the gap between a "technically correct solution" and a "solution the customer's organization will accept." Even a highly accurate model won't be used if it doesn't fit the field's approval workflows or existing systems. I believe the ability to adjust for this is a major factor that separates AI engineers from FDEs.

Which Is a Better Fit: FDE or AI Engineer?

FDEs suit people who want to solve customer problems with their own hands. AI engineers suit people who want to dig deep into technology and pursue accuracy.

It's not a question of which is better. The difference lies in the situations where each tends to shine. Putting your own inclinations into words makes it easier to stay consistent, whether in a job change or an internal transfer.

Traits of People Suited to FDE

People who enjoy ambiguous situations and can narrow down problems through conversations with others are well suited to FDE roles. They find fulfillment in seeing visible results.

  • Comfortable forming hypotheses and taking action even when requirements aren't yet defined
  • Able to translate technical topics into language non-engineers can understand
  • Enjoys the cycle of shipping something working quickly, observing field reactions, and iterating
  • Can positively embrace changing environments, such as business trips or working at customer sites
  • Feels a sense of accomplishment in solving problems completely, rather than in the depth of a specific technology

Traits of People Suited to AI Engineer

People who enjoy digging deep into a single technical domain and improving accuracy or efficiency in measurable terms are well suited to AI engineer roles.

  • Enjoys following papers and new models and validating them hands-on
  • Finds fulfillment in designing evaluation metrics and stacking up improvements
  • Wants to secure dedicated time for focused development
  • Finds value in building generic, reusable systems
  • Prefers technical discussions within the team over customer interactions

How to Transition from AI Engineer to FDE?

Build on your technical foundation and accumulate a track record in customer engagement and business understanding. The shortcut is to reach a state where you can talk about "results" in your resume.

AI engineers already have the technical foundation FDEs need. What tends to be missing is experience defining problems in the customer's field and making implementations stick. Deliberately building this experience is the core of transition preparation.

4 Steps to the Transition

Proceed in this order: increase customer touchpoints at your current job, articulate your results, then apply to FDE openings. This keeps things manageable.

  • Increase customer touchpoints: Proactively volunteer to launch PoCs, support deployments, and join customer interviews
  • Build "problem → action → result" case studies: Record, to the extent you can share, what the AI features you worked on changed in the customer's business
  • Broaden your scope: Gain implementation experience beyond models, such as integration with existing systems, data infrastructure, and authentication/permissions
  • Read job postings carefully and apply: Compare "whether on-site work at customers is required," "the ratio versus product development," and "the industries served," and choose companies that match your inclinations

From 2025 onward, in addition to Palantir, AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic have been recruiting for FDE roles. Openings with the same title have also started appearing in Japan. However, even under the same "FDE" title, the actual work ranges from presales-leaning to implementation-leaning. I recommend judging by the job content rather than the title.

What's Evaluated in Your Resume and Interviews

In FDE selection processes, beyond technical depth, evaluators look at how concretely you can explain "how you defined the customer's problem and led it to a solution."

What's evaluatedExample of how to present it
Problem-framing abilityExplain the difference between the customer's initial request and the problem you actually solved
End-to-end implementationConcretely describe the technical hurdles you overcame to get it running in production
Building consensus with customersShow how you handled prioritization and managing expectations
Sharing knowledge backShow how you reflected lessons from individual projects into the product or team

In interviews, you may be asked in a case format "how you would approach this customer's problem." There, rather than starting with technology choices, first listing the assumptions to confirm and the stakeholders involved makes it easier to convey your FDE-style order of thinking.

Things to Check Before Transitioning

FDE is a role with major changes in working style. Check the frequency of business trips, the evaluation system, and subsequent career paths before joining.

  • Working style: Frequency of on-site work at customers and business trips, remote work availability
  • Evaluation system: How customer outcomes are evaluated, how product contributions are treated
  • Career path: Whether there are examples of moving from FDE to PdM, solution architect, or product engineer
  • Technical growth opportunities: Whether the environment risks becoming project-response-only, losing technical depth

FDE is not a "senior version of AI engineer." It's a separate career centered on turning technology into customer outcomes. From what I've seen in the field, people who moved to FDE while retaining their expertise as AI engineers tend to earn customer trust more easily. Rather than abandoning your current expertise, prepare with the intention of layering "the ability to solve problems in the field" on top of it.
