Summary

Written by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, this article compares FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) employers in Japan and the US using public job data as of spring 2026. US FDE job postings grew roughly 800% between January and September 2025, with OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, Sierra, and Salesforce hiring aggressively; total compensation reaches $350K–550K+ at frontier labs, with high equity components. Japan had 35 FDE positions (26 domestic, 9 foreign), centered on companies like LayerX (¥12M+), SB OAI Japan, and Ricerca, with salary ranges of ¥5M–25M. The article identifies four key Japan-US differences: pay (2–3x gap), coding requirements (100% vs 81% builder-type), English requirements (100% vs 12%), and evaluation criteria. It also promotes DeFactory's lab-type FDE model (average ¥1.35M/month) as a third option between full-time and freelance, and warns readers to spot companies using the FDE label for conventional SES staffing work.

  • US FDE job postings grew ~800% from January to September 2025.
  • Japan had 35 FDE positions in spring 2026: 26 domestic, 9 foreign.
  • Frontier lab FDE pay reaches $350K–550K+, heavily equity-based.
  • Japan-US gaps: pay, coding requirements, English, evaluation criteria.

In conclusion, the FDE market has grown rapidly to the point that job openings have exploded in the US, and a total of 35 positions can be confirmed in Japan as of spring 2026. It is an extremely new and dynamic market that emerged from zero in just about a year.

As the problem of generative AI implementations stalling at the "PoC (proof of concept)" stage has become apparent, demand for FDEs who work on-site and handle implementation through to adoption is expanding rapidly in both Japan and the US.

In the US, FDE job postings increased approximately 800% from January to September 2025, making it the "most watched role" as major AI companies rush to hire.

The trends in the US market are as follows.

  • 800% increase in job postings: FDE job postings grew roughly 8-fold from January to September 2025 (centered in Silicon Valley)
  • Companies hiring: OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, Sierra, Cohere, Adobe, Salesforce, UiPath, and others
  • Salesforce's large-scale hiring: CEO Marc Benioff has announced the hiring of 1,000 new graduates and interns (for AI projects such as Agentforce, with FDE being one of the core roles)
  • Background: Growing recognition that "demos win contracts, but production deployment retains customers"

As a professional opinion, the reason FDEs have surged in the US is clear. AI products do not deliver results on a "sell and done" basis, and companies have keenly realized that talent who embed themselves in the customer's operations and drive adoption through to production is indispensable. While not all of Salesforce's 1,000 hires are FDEs, this move symbolizes their investment posture toward AI implementation talent.

In conclusion, the biggest reason for the rapid expansion of FDE demand lies in a structural change in the market: "simply introducing generative AI does not produce results, and talent who embed themselves deeply in operations and handle implementation through to adoption has become indispensable."

The background to the surge in demand is as follows.

  • PoC fatigue and expectations for "working deliverables": Customers tired of consultants offering only strategic proposals have come to demand "implementation over discussion"
  • The on-the-ground gap in generative AI adoption: Companies wanting to integrate LLMs into their operations have surged, but projects frequently "end at PoC," and talent who can handle implementation is in short supply
  • Rising unit prices for enterprise AI: For large contracts with major corporations, "post-adoption usage support" has become standard, and the presence of FDEs affects retention rates
  • The spread of the Palantir model: Palantir's success with FDEs has become widely known, and companies are beginning to establish their own FDE organizations

From a professional perspective, the essence of the FDE role is "building what's needed on the spot and delivering results." The faster AI evolves, the more valuable hands-on implementation capability becomes compared to desk-bound strategy. This structural demand is not a passing trend and is expected to continue going forward.

There are four essential differences between FDEs in Japan and the US: "salary," "role (degree to which implementation is required)," "English requirements," and "evaluation criteria." Understanding these is the key to choosing the environment that suits you.

In terms of salary, while the median upper limit in Japan is about 15 million yen, foreign firms offer 20 million to 50 million yen, a difference of 2 to 3 times.

The salary range for Japanese FDEs is 5 million to 25 million yen (median upper limit 15 million yen), while foreign firms offer 20 million to 50 million yen (OpenAI's TC is about 50 million yen). Much of this difference comes from the equity granted by foreign firms, especially frontier AI labs.

As a professional view, choosing a foreign firm based solely on the headline figure is premature. Foreign compensation has a high equity component, and its value depends on the market and the company's performance. Whether you prioritize the stability of cash compensation or aim for upside through equity depends on your personal risk tolerance.

In terms of role, 100% of foreign FDEs are Builder type (implementation required), while in Japan it is 81%, meaning a small number of "FDEs who do not implement" exist.

At foreign firms, all FDE positions are implementation-focused, with writing code yourself to solve customer problems as a fundamental premise. In contrast, at Japanese firms, 81% are Builder-type (coding ratio of 70-90%), while the remainder include proposal- and coordination-focused roles.

A common real-world failure case is a mismatch where someone joins attracted by the "FDE" title, only to find the actual role is a coordinator who barely does any implementation, differing from the career they expected. Be wary of what we later describe as "SES-ization in name only."

Regarding English requirements, there is a large gap: foreign firms require it 100% of the time (effectively mandatory, bilingual level), while Japanese firms require it only 12% of the time.

In foreign FDE roles, collaborating with global teams and reading/writing English documents is a daily occurrence, making English proficiency effectively a mandatory requirement. Meanwhile, most Japanese FDE roles do not require English and center on supporting domestic customers in Japanese.

As a professional perspective, for those lacking confidence in English, Japanese FDE roles are a realistic option. Conversely, with English skills, you can aim for the high compensation at foreign firms. The English requirement, alongside annual salary, is a key dividing line between Japanese and foreign FDE roles.

In terms of evaluation criteria, foreign firms center on "commitment to customer business outcomes," while Japanese firms mix outcome-based and traditional delivery-based models.

At foreign firms, the mainstream is being evaluated on "results such as customer revenue and operational improvements." At Japanese firms, the reality is a mix of the original outcome-commitment FDE model and traditional SES-style "delivery/effort-based" evaluation.

As real-world insight, this difference in evaluation criteria touches on the essence of how you work. Outcome-based roles offer great autonomy and fulfillment, but also heavier accountability for results. Delivery-based roles are stable, but dilute the original FDE appeal of "building solutions." It is important to clarify for yourself which you seek.

Behind the emergence of the freelance FDE option lies a mismatch: "full-time FDE positions are rare and highly competitive, while companies want to leverage people with FDE skills only when needed."

As real-world insight, for people who "have FDE skills but aren't committed to being a full-time employee at a specific company," freelance FDE is an attractive option. In fact, rates for FDE projects remain at high levels.

In conclusion, to avoid failure when changing jobs to or joining an FDE company, it is important to see through "SES-ization that merely uses the FDE name," verify the actual conditions in interviews, and prepare the necessary skills in advance. Precisely because this is a new profession, a discerning eye is required.

Here, we explain specific points to note in order to avoid failure.

The most important thing to watch out for is companies that "use the FDE name but are in reality traditional SES (staffing provision)." Don't be misled by the name; you need to assess the actual nature of the work.

Here are the points for distinguishing "SES-ization that merely uses the FDE name."

  • Check the evaluation criteria: Is it based on working hours or on the client's business outcomes?
  • Scope of discretion: Can you get involved from problem discovery, or only perform assigned tasks?
  • Whether implementation is involved: Do you write code yourself, or is the work mainly coordination?
  • Proximity to the client: Do you get deeply involved on-site, or engage only through intermediaries?

As a common real-world failure example: there are cases where someone applied for an "FDE" job posting, only to find it was actually an SES role stationed at a client site doing assigned development work. Given that 19% of Japanese FDEs are non-Builder type, it is important to confirm the details of the work concretely.

In hiring interviews, you can prevent mismatches by always asking five questions about "role, evaluation, discretion, growth, and team."

The five questions to ask are as follows.

  • Role: "As an FDE, how far will I be involved, from problem discovery through implementation and production deployment?"
  • Evaluation criteria: "Is evaluation based on working hours or on the client's business outcomes?"
  • Discretion: "How much involvement can I have in technology selection and architectural decisions?"
  • Growth: "What skills and career can I build as an FDE?"
  • Team: "How does collaboration with the product team and on-site work style function?"

As a professional opinion: be cautious if answers to these questions are vague, or if you only get abstract responses like "we just want you to do your best on-site." The more clearly a company can articulate roles and evaluation, the more it truly understands what FDE means.
