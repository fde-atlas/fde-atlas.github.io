Forward Deployed Engineering: The Engagement

DataDank · 2026-10-31 · 2 min read

Summary

This first lesson of a six-part course walks through how a forward deployed engineer runs a customer engagement across seven stages: discovery, scoping, integration, build, deployment, adoption and handover, plus feedback to the product team. It gives a concrete two-week playbook: meet the sponsor and users, request data access immediately since approvals are slow, profile data in week one, and agree a narrow proof of concept with a measurable success number by week two. Common failure modes include solving the stated request rather than the real problem, late system access, poor data quality found too late, vague success criteria, and no customer-side owner after handover.

Key points

  • An engagement spans seven stages from discovery to feedback, which overlap and loop.
  • Request system and data access in the first days; approvals are often the slowest step.
  • Agree a narrow proof of concept with a success number by week two.
  • Send short weekly written updates covering progress, decisions, risks and asks.
  • Engagements derail from vague success criteria and no customer-side owner.

How a forward deployed engineering engagement runs from first meeting to handover: discovery, scoping, integration, deployment and adoption, with checklists.

On this page

A forward deployed engineer (FDE) is measured by one thing: whether the customer ends up with a working solution that people use. Getting there follows a fairly standard path. This course walks through it stage by stage. If you want to know what the role is first, see what a forward deployed engineer is.

The stages of an engagement

Stage Goal Main output
1. Discovery Understand the real problem, the people, the data and the systemsA short problem statement, a data inventory and a list of risks
2. Scoping Agree what a first version will prove and how success is measuredA one-page proof-of-concept plan with a success number
3. Integration Connect the customer’s APIs and data to the productReliable connectors, mappings agreed in writing, quality checks
4. Build Configure and extend the product for this customerA working solution on real data
5. Deployment Run it inside the customer’s environment, within their rulesA deployed, monitored system that passed their security review
6. Adoption and handover Make sure people use it, and that their team can run itMeasured results, training, documentation and runbooks
7. Feedback Feed lessons back to the product teamProduct requests backed by evidence

The stages overlap and loop: integration often reveals data problems that change the scope, and early users change the build. The skill is moving forward while keeping everyone informed.

The first two weeks

  • Day 1 to 3: meet the sponsor and the people who will use the solution. Write down the goal in their words and how it is measured today.
  • Day 2 onwards: request access to systems and data early, because approvals are often the slowest part.
  • Week 1: profile a sample of their data (see technical discovery) and list what is missing or messy.
  • Week 2: agree a narrow proof of concept with a success number (see scoping a proof of concept).
  • Every week: send a short written update: progress, decisions made, risks, and what you need from them.

What usually derails engagements

  • Solving the stated request instead of the real problem. Ask what decision or task the work supports.
  • Late access to systems, data or environments.
  • Data that is worse than promised, discovered too late.
  • A vague definition of success, so nobody can say whether it worked.
  • No owner on the customer side to maintain the solution after you leave.

Each later lesson tackles one of these.

Key takeaway

An engagement moves from discovery to scoping, integration, build, deployment, adoption and feedback. Start access requests and data profiling in the first days, agree a measurable proof of concept early, and write short weekly updates. Next: technical discovery.
