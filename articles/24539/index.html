19 FDE Career Transition, Portfolio, Interviews, and a 30-Day Action Plan

Original title: 19 FDE 职业转型、作品集、面试与 30 天行动计划

FDE中国社区 · 2026-07-11 · 4 min read

Summary

This article, part of a Chinese FDE community series, argues that an FDE portfolio must prove you can deliver an end-to-end closed loop, not just list tools like LangChain or Dify. It maps job-search keywords (Forward Deployed Engineer, Applied AI Engineer, Deployment Strategist, etc.), transition paths for five backgrounds, six types of portfolio evidence, a 3-minute interview narrative structure, common interview questions and evaluation dimensions, and a four-week 30-day action plan ending with a portfolio or client proposal.

Key points

  • Portfolios should show delivery evidence: discovery, design, demo, evals, rollout, retrospective.
  • Homepage should feature only 3–5 projects in a uniform structure.
  • Interview stories follow a 30/60/60/30-second scene-solution-eval-result format.
  • Most overseas FDE roles require 25%–75% on-site travel.
  • The 30-day plan moves from interviews to a demo, evals, and a retrospective.

Chapter Goals

Turn the outputs of this column series into an FDE portfolio, internal transfer materials, client proposals, or an enterprise AI deployment capability-building plan.

Opening question: What you need to prove is not that you "learned AI," but that you "delivered a closed loop"

Many job seekers' portfolios list tools: LangChain, Dify, Coze, OpenAI, DeepSeek, Milvus. Tools certainly matter, but what interviewers really want to confirm is: can you take a vague requirement, find the real workflow, design a minimal pipeline, handle permissions and failures, prove effectiveness with samples, and drive the project to user adoption.

So an FDE portfolio is not a tech-stack resume, but a set of delivery evidence. It should convince the reader: if you were dropped at a client site, you would know what to ask first, what to do first, what not to promise, and how to push a Demo toward a trial-ready system.

Career Paths

Backgrounds well suited to transitioning into FDE roles include:

  • Full-stack development.
  • Data engineering.
  • Algorithm engineering.
  • Solutions architecture.
  • Pre-sales and consulting.
  • Implementation and operations.
  • Product and customer success.

The key is not your previous job title, but whether you can prove you can turn real business scenarios into verifiable systems.

Portfolio Evidence

A qualified portfolio should contain verifiable evidence, not conceptual descriptions:

  • Scenario cards and flowcharts (proving discovery).
  • Architecture diagrams and tool lists (proving system design).
  • Demo recordings or scripts (proving build).
  • Golden Dataset and failure samples (proving evals).
  • Launch gate checklists (proving rollout awareness).
  • Retrospective documents (proving feedback and productization).

How to Organize Your Portfolio Homepage

Keep only 3–5 high-quality projects on your portfolio homepage, each following the same structure:

SectionHow to write it
One-sentence scenarioWho completes what task in what context
Current pain pointsDescribe time cost, errors, rework, or risk with data
AI intervention pointExplain why you chose RAG / Workflow / Agent
Delivery evidenceDemo, eval samples, Trace, launch gates
ResultsMetrics, trial feedback, failure retrospectives
Next stepsHow to move to production or productization

Don't turn every project into a long essay. The portfolio homepage does the filtering; the detail pages do the proving.

The 3-Minute Interview Narrative

Prepare a 3-minute version of each project:

  • 30 seconds on the scenario: Who the client is and what pain points the original process had.
  • 60 seconds on the solution: Why you chose this architecture and how you handled permissions and risk.
  • 60 seconds on the evals: Where the samples came from, what the metrics were, and how failures were reviewed.
  • 30 seconds on the results: Whether users adopted it and how to move to launch or productization next.

This structure keeps you from falling into a tool-listing monologue during interviews.

Portfolio Project Standards

A qualified project should include:

  • Business scenario and target users.
  • Current process and pain points.
  • AI intervention point.
  • Architecture choice.
  • Data and tool list.
  • Permission and compliance boundaries.
  • Golden Dataset.
  • Demo script.
  • Failure samples.
  • Launch and operations plan.

Interview Preparation

Common questions:

  • Given a vague client requirement, how do you break it down?
  • How do you choose between RAG, Workflow, and Agent?
  • What do you do when a tool call fails?
  • How do you evaluate an AI agent?
  • How do you explain model uncertainty to a non-technical client?
  • How do you handle sensitive data and permissions?
  • How do you feed client-site experience back to the product team?

Travel and Client Sites

Most overseas FDE JDs require 25%–75% client-site presence or travel. Be prepared in interviews to discuss:

  • How to build trust and momentum at a client site.
  • How to align business, IT, and compliance stakeholders.
  • How to deliver demonstrable results within a limited timeframe.
  • How to escalate field issues into the product roadmap.

TimeActionDeliverables

Key areas to fill in for different backgrounds

  • Technical learners: fill in LangGraph, LlamaIndex, APIs, Docker, and evals.
  • Product and solutions learners: fill in scenario evals, architecture diagrams, ROI, acceptance criteria, and business proposals.
  • Business learners: fill in knowledge organization, sample preparation, acceptance criteria, and operational feedback.

What to do after 30 days

After completing the first version of your portfolio, don't stop. Keep doing three things:

  • Add real feedback: Find 3 target users to try it out and have them point out what seems untrustworthy.
  • Add eval samples: Turn failure feedback into a Golden Dataset.
  • Add business expression: Rewrite the project into three versions: a client proposal, a job interview case study, and an internal project approval document.

FDE growth isn't about stacking up more demos, but about continuously bringing the same scenario closer to real delivery.

Hands-on task

Organize the deliverables from this column into a public or semi-public portfolio page.

Deliverables

  • FDE portfolio directory.
  • 30-Day Action Plan
  • Draft answers to interview questions.
  • Client proposal summary.

Common pitfalls

  • The portfolio only shows the UI, not the business closed loop.
  • Only talking about technical buzzwords, not customer problems.
  • No evals or failure samples.
  • No explanation of launch boundaries and risk controls.

Checklist

  • Does the portfolio prove you can deliver production systems?
  • Are there reusable templates and methods?
  • Can you answer follow-up questions from clients and interviewers?
  • Is there a concrete next-step action plan?
