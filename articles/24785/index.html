Summary

Written by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, this Japanese article explains why documentation matters in system development, what types are produced at each phase, and how to write them well. It argues documents serve three purposes: aligning client and vendor expectations, enabling smooth handoffs among multiple engineers, and supporting maintenance, operation, and personnel transitions after delivery. It walks through deliverables by phase—RFP, requirements definitions, basic and detailed design documents, source code delivery, test specifications and reports, and operation manuals—and closes with writing tips: keep text accessible, update documents promptly when specifications change, and avoid producing more than can be managed. The piece ends with promotion of DeFactory's development support services.

Key points

  • Documents align client expectations, smooth collaboration, and support post-delivery maintenance.
  • Phase-by-phase overview: RFP, requirements, basic/detailed design, testing, operation manuals.
  • Source code delivery requires care over copyright treatment.
  • Tips: readable writing, prompt updates on spec changes, avoid excess documentation.
  • Author is DeFactory CEO Shohei Tokumitsu; article ends with company promotion.

In system development, it is common to deliver not only the system being developed but also various documents alongside it. However, some people may wonder why documents need to be created during the system development process.

There are many types of documents, and the specific content of the documents to be created varies depending on the development company.

This article explains the necessity of documents in system development, as well as the types of documents and key points when creating them.

Documents are a type of deliverable created during the system development process.

So why do documents need to be created in the first place? The necessity of creating documents in system development mainly lies in the following two points.

In system development, it is important for the client and the development company to share the same vision of the final product as development progresses. If the system does not meet the client's requirements, wasteful rework and costs will occur, and the development itself can be considered a failure.

By sharing documents covering the budget, design, features to be implemented, and more, clients can entrust the development with peace of mind. Since the process up to basic design often involves close communication with the client, feel free to voice your opinions if anything differs from your expectations.

Some documents are for sharing with clients, while others, such as those related to programming, are intended for the development company's engineers and programmers.

By documenting technical aspects, such as which programming language to use and how to implement the programming, development can proceed smoothly.

In system development, many engineers and programmers are involved, not just one person. Since the technical staff in charge typically varies by phase, documents are necessary to ensure a smooth transition from, for example, design to development (programming).

Additionally, having documents in place makes it easier to share which parts need to be changed and how when specification changes occur during development.

System development does not end once the system is complete; issues and bugs discovered during actual operation must be fixed as they arise, and the system needs to be updated. When performing such maintenance and operation, using documents containing information about design and testing makes it easier to identify areas that need fixing.

Furthermore, after some time has passed since development, the development company's staff or team structure may change. Even in such cases, having documents makes it easy to grasp the overall flow of the system development, enabling smooth handovers.

From here, we will introduce examples of the documents created during system development, organized by development phase. However, what gets created varies depending on the development company and approach, so please treat this as a reference only.

System development does not begin abruptly; it starts when an order is received from a client. Therefore, while not part of the development process itself, the development request is the first phase in which documents are produced.

For web systems, the appropriate approach differs depending on whether you want an e-commerce site or a portal site. A document that outlines the purpose of the system development, the problems to be solved, constraints, and other overviews is the RFP (Request for Proposal). Based on the RFP's content, the development company considers what development methods and solutions to use, and at what budget they can take on the project.

Note that the RFP is created by the client (the ordering party), while the requirements definition document and subsequent documents are created by the development company.

[Content to Include in an RFP (Request for Proposal)]

System requirements, budget, purpose of development, operation methods, delivery deadline, etc.

The difference between requirements definition and specifications definition can be summarized as follows.

[Requirements Definition]: Defining the specifications that users demand, i.e., what kind of product to build.

[Specifications Definition]: Defining the specifications such as the features to be implemented and the requirements to be met.

In the requirements definition phase, interviews are conducted to understand what kind of system the client wants to develop, and the requirements are identified. For example, if the client says, "Given that in-store customer acquisition is stagnating, we want to build an e-commerce site to strengthen online customer acquisition," the requirements definition phase outputs the specific features and design they are looking for.

Based on this requirements definition, the specifications definition phase translates the requirements into feasible system specifications. In the e-commerce site example above, the specifications are determined by considering what kind of payment system is needed to solve the customer acquisition problem and how to integrate with other services.

The specifications definition document clearly states the requirements for development, including feasible features as well as projections for the overall cost, personnel, and development effort required.

Also, it is advisable to summarize not only functional requirements but also non-functional requirements in the requirements definition document.

Functional requirements are the minimum "functions" that the client requests for the system to be developed. Non-functional requirements, on the other hand, refer to aspects of system quality other than functional requirements.

Whether the system can be operated smoothly hinges on non-functional requirements.

[Content to Include in the Requirements Definition Document]

Requirements, specifications, overview of functions, costs, personnel, development effort, implementation methods, etc.

Basic design is the design of the system's appearance, such as screen layouts and button placement. For a web system, basic design involves considering things like what happens when a particular element is clicked.

The basic design document describes specific screen design, batch design, database design, and so on, assuming the scenarios in which the system will be used. For details on what to create during basic design, refer to the following article.

Related article: What Is Basic Design in System Development? Also Explains Creation Tools and Key Points

[Content to Include in the Basic Design Document]

Screen design, system design, batch design, external interface design, database design, ER diagrams, etc.

Detailed design determines how the content of the basic design document will realistically be implemented and programmed. For example, it describes the specific methods for realizing the web system layout and button functions defined in the basic design.

The detailed design document breaks down the content that is still only roughly defined in the basic design into detailed functions, and describes activity diagrams, sequence diagrams, the libraries and languages to be used, and so on.

Detailed design is explained in depth in the following article, so please take a look as well.

[Content to Include in the Detailed Design Document]

Activity diagrams, sequence diagrams, IPO (processing function descriptions), database design, ER diagrams

etc.

Development is the phase in which engineers actually perform programming according to the detailed design document. One document that should be created during programming is the source code deliverable.

Source code is a text file written when creating programs for building software and systems, describing the processing content, i.e., "what behavior the program should perform."

After the source code is delivered, the client will often operate the system themselves, so when delivering source code, make sure to carefully configure coding standards, management tools, and other settings.

However, source code may not always be considered a deliverable, so care must be taken regarding copyright handling.

Testing is broadly divided into three types: unit testing, integration testing, and system testing.

In unit testing, each function is tested individually; in integration testing, several functions are tested in combination; and in system testing, the behavior of the entire system is verified.

The test specification document summarizes the test items for each type, and the test report records the results.

[Content to Include in the Test Specification/Report]

Functions to be tested, test methods, test perspectives, results, etc.

The operations manual is a procedural document describing how to operate and run the completed system.

Since the development work is performed by the development company, the client does not know how to use the system. Sharing detailed operating procedures with the client's actual business in mind leads to smooth operation.

[Content to Include in the Operations Manual]

Business workflows, how to operate functions, operation methods, security-related information (access rights, passwords, etc.), recovery/troubleshooting procedures, etc.

There are several points to keep in mind when creating documents. To make the most of the benefits of documentation, it is important to be mindful of the following points.

Documents are shared with an unspecified number of people, including clients and development team members. Therefore, it is advisable to write them in a way that is easy for anyone to understand and to keep them concise.

Design-related documents in particular tend to become difficult to read because they inevitably contain many technical terms and technical content. To make them as readable as possible, it is also effective to establish formatting and writing rules for the entire document.

During system development, parts of the specifications often change. In such cases, the documents must also be revised, but if revisions are delayed, discrepancies with the actual development status will arise.

If there is a gap in understanding between the engineers and programmers doing the development work and other members, development may come to a halt.

To avoid unnecessary confusion, it is important to revise documents promptly when specification changes occur and share them with all stakeholders.

By the time system development is finished, a large number of documents may have been created. However, since documents are also used for system operation, maintenance, and updates, having a large number of them is meaningless unless they are properly managed.

Considering how the documents will be used later, it is best not to create more than necessary and to keep them organized in an easily manageable state.

In system development, documents mainly serve the following roles.

1. To align understanding between the client and the development company

2. To ensure smooth progress of development

3. For maintenance and operation after the system is completed

There are various types of documents, and what to create and how varies by development company. As the client, if there is any content or documentation you want to review later, it is a good idea to communicate this at the requirements definition stage.
