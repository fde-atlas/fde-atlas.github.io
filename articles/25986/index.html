What this seat owns

The spec and the build: Product Builders take an outcome to a working product in a day and run the agents that build it.

When building is cheap, the outcome is decided by the loop from customer to adoption. One loop needs one owner. The CPO owns the whole product outcome, and product, design, product marketing, and forward deployed engineering run it together. See the whole OS.

  • 01CustomerField signal from deployments, support, and usage, read daily. Measured in hops and days from finding to fix.
  • 02 · leadsSpecThe problem, the behavior, the claim, and the eval that says it works. Measured by the eval pass rate.
  • 03 · leadsBuildAgents and Product Builders take the spec to working software. Measured by the repair rate in use.
  • 04StoryPositioning and proof that buyers, sellers, and AI assistants read, shipped in the same release as the build. Measured in story lag, the days until assistants describe a change correctly.
  • 05AdoptionVerified jobs done on every path, with the cost per job beside it. The number moves, or the loop runs again.

  • Which of LangChain's three eval-driven steps is the PM's?Step one, and the reading in step three. As Divya Santhanam describes LangChain's eval-driven development on November 9, 2026, the loop is: write a short spec of what good looks like for one behavior, write the eval before you build and confirm it fails, then make the change and compare results row by row. The spec and the examples are the PM's work, because they say what the product should do and which cases matter. Engineering owns the harness that runs them. My prediction: by the end of 2027, at AI-native software companies, most rows in a product area's eval file will have been written by people outside engineering.
  • What do Ramp's 7,000 non-engineer PRs mean for PMs?They mean the build no longer waits on the PM, so the PM's day becomes a queue of decisions. As Rich Holmes reports in Department of Product on November 1, 2026, Ramp's CEO, Eric Glyman, says non-engineers shipped more than 7,000 pull requests last quarter, and about 11% of PostHog's October merged PRs were opened by its own product after a scheduled agent found something to fix. The PM's job is deciding which proposals ship, which die, and when the bar itself is wrong. My prediction: by the end of 2027, at AI-native software companies, most changes a PM approves in a week will have been proposed by an agent.
  • What does Salesforce look for when it hires AI PMs?Two skills and five traits. As Aakash Gupta reports from his November 3, 2026 conversation with Jayesh Govindarajan, who runs Salesforce's roughly 700-person Agentforce org, the core AI PM skills are AI prototyping and AI evals. The traits are agency, an agent-native mindset, skepticism, craftsmanship, and setting the bar, and the last one, can you say what good looks like, is the one he calls most important. None of it is about writing requirements.
  • What should the first lines of an agent-written PR description say?Three things, above a collapsed block that holds everything else: what changed, what was not checked, and what the reader is being asked to decide. Simon Willison's November 9, 2026 post folds the agent's full description inside a details element labeled with the model and effort level and puts a short human-chosen opener on top. By his own list the agent's essay holds the steps taken to verify, so the verification is now under the fold, and the five example openers he quotes all describe the change. The second line should come from the run's record, not the agent's prose. In my own repository 343 of 401 commits since September 1 carry an agent co-author line, and the run reports are the long ones: last week's five creator scan digests ran 66,349 to 82,798 characters with the list of what was not read starting as late as character 74,764. The November 9 digest is the first to lead with it.
  • Who owns the deployment agent when agents do the forward deployed build?Product, under the CPO. On November 10, 2026, IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise with a fleet of more than 6,000 AI agents and assets and forward-deployed engineers attached for qualifying engagements. Microsoft's Kevin Shatzkamer describes FDE teams augmented with coding assistants and deployment agents, and ServiceNow's chief product officer, Amit Zavery, said on October 28, 2026 that the company is building AI implementation agents that do a lot of the heavy lifting at go-live. Software that every deployment runs is a product, so it needs a product manager, a backlog, and a quality bar. The human forward deployed engineer keeps four decisions: the choice of workflow, the judgment of the customer's senior people, the bar for go-live, and the handback to the roadmap. Falk's number for the field is human hours per go-live, sorted into decision hours and gap hours, where every gap hour is a bug report against the deployment agent.
  • Did the 2026 midterms reject AI?No. The 2026 midterms rejected the bill for AI infrastructure, not AI. Voters in ten states decided 41 data center measures and went against development in 40 of them; only Harwood, North Dakota, said yes. NBC's exit poll put AI and data centers as a crucial or major factor for 56% of voters, but the AP Voter Poll found only about a quarter called it crucial, behind cost of living and corruption. The verdict was on who pays for power and what a town gets for hosting the building. Falk's read: that priced the compute moat and moved the scarce resource to adoption, which is a product problem.
