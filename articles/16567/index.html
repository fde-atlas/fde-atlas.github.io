With AI adoption, "on-site client deployment" is on the rise: what is OpenAI's FDE, also utilized by Mitsubishi UFJ?

Original title: AI導入で「客先常駐」が台頭、三菱UFJも活用するOpenAIのFDEとは

日経クロステック（Xtech） · 2025-11-14 · 3 min read

The article uses MUFG's partnership with OpenAI as a case study—ChatGPT Enterprise will roll out to all 35,000 employees of Mitsubishi UFJ Bank, and an LLM-based AI concierge chat feature will be embedded in customer-facing apps and digital banking. Full text

Engineers from IT vendors embed themselves in the business departments of user companies, gather frontline needs, and develop custom applications. This "on-site resident" system development model, familiar in Japan, is now being eagerly adopted by cutting-edge AI startups such as US-based OpenAI. A new job title, "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)," is becoming the driving force behind AI adoption.

OpenAI itself supports MUFG's AI adoption

"Together with the specialist team that OpenAI has organized for us, we will create new services for our customers." So said Tadashi Yamamoto, Executive Managing Officer and head of MUFG's Retail & Digital Business Unit as well as Group CDTO (Chief Digital Transformation Officer), at a press conference held on November 12, 2025 regarding the "strategic collaboration" between the company and OpenAI.

Through its partnership with OpenAI, MUFG plans to introduce "ChatGPT Enterprise" to all 35,000 employees of its subsidiary Mitsubishi UFJ Bank, and to implement features such as an "AI Concierge," a chat function based on OpenAI's large language model (LLM), in the smartphone apps and digital banking services that MUFG group companies offer to customers.

In this effort, the "MUFG project team" established by OpenAI will support AI education for employees, review MUFG's AI strategy and the status of its customer-facing AI services on a quarterly basis, and assist with the work of implementing AI into the smartphone apps and digital banking services.

MUFG's Yamamoto expressed his expectations for the initiative with OpenAI, saying, "It is greatly appreciated that OpenAI does not merely provide solutions but also works alongside us toward implementing services. We want to implement the latest AI technology in a timely and rapid manner. With OpenAI working alongside us, we can pour the right AI technology into our services faster than anyone else."

US IT vendors with strong products, like OpenAI, have traditionally tended to focus on providing their products, leaving labor-intensive tasks such as deployment at user companies and the accompanying additional development to the user companies themselves or to partners such as system integrators.

OpenAI engineers even write code for customers

Recently, however, as the case of OpenAI and MUFG shows, AI startups have begun embedding themselves in user companies and getting involved all the way through to application implementation.

In its November 2, 2025 article "The new hot job in AI: forward-deployed engineers," the British newspaper Financial Times (FT) reported that OpenAI began organizing teams of such FDEs in 2025 and plans to expand their size to about 50 people within the year. FT also reported that rivals Anthropic of the US and Cohere of Canada are likewise expanding their FDE teams.

"Forward" in FDE is a military term meaning "frontline," and "Deploy" likewise means "to station troops." An FDE is an engineer deployed to the frontline—the field—of a user company.

OpenAI is also recruiting FDEs in Tokyo. According to its job posting, the FDE's role is to "lead the deployment of cutting-edge models into complex production environments," "own technical delivery from prototype to stable release," "work closely with customer teams to understand their needs and see developed solutions through to deployment," and "contribute directly to code when there is a clear need, such as for progress."

In short, an FDE at OpenAI works in the field at user companies, eliciting requirements from business departments, designing systems, leading development from prototype to production, and sometimes even writing the program code itself.
