This report consists of two main parts. Part 1 covers overall market trends based on publicly available external data, and Part 2 covers primary data based on DeFactory's own track record of support. Since these are different types of information, we have deliberately separated them.

In 2026, FDE is shifting from an "emerging role" to an "industry-standard business model." The key developments in the first half of 2026 are as follows.

  • OpenAI: On May 11, 2026, announced the establishment of "OpenAI Deployment Company," a business entity specializing in AI implementation. Secured $4 billion in initial investment from 19 companies led by TPG, and through the acquisition of AI consulting firm Tomoro, approximately 150 FDEs joined from day one (Source: Official OpenAI announcement)
  • Anthropic: On May 4, 2026, announced the establishment of a new company on the scale of $1.5 billion together with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs. Embedding Anthropic engineers directly into mid-sized enterprises to support business transformation centered on Claude (Source: Official Anthropic announcement)
  • AWS (Amazon): On June 30, 2026, established a new internal organization specializing in FDEs and announced an investment on the scale of $1 billion (Source: TechCrunch)
  • Microsoft: On July 2, 2026, announced the establishment of "Microsoft Frontier Company." Investing $2.5 billion to build a system that deploys approximately 6,000 industry experts and engineers to client companies (Source: TechCrunch)
  • Salesforce: Announced the "FDE Partner Network" in April 2026 and launched it in full in the Japanese market on June 8 (9 initial domestic partners) (Source: Official Salesforce announcement)

The market's center of gravity is shifting from competition over "which model is smarter" to competition over "which company can enter the customer's front lines and turn AI into business systems actually used in production."

FDE-related job postings grew 729% year over year, from 643 in April 2025 to 5,330 in April 2026 (Source: Business Insider report).

According to a BCG survey, 74% of companies struggle to scale the value of AI adoption (Source: Boston Consulting Group "AI Adoption in 2024"). This implementation gap—where "PoCs succeed but production deployment doesn't progress" or "AI tools are introduced but the front lines don't use them"—is the background behind the demand for the FDE role.

Global: Palantir (the company where FDE originated), OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS, Microsoft, and Salesforce announced organizational expansion and major investments in 2026.

Domestic: LayerX (AI/LLM business division, with job postings offering annual salaries of 12 million yen and up), AI Shift, SmartHR (Enterprise Success Unit), the SoftBank × OpenAI joint venture "SB OAI Japan" (established November 2025), Loglass, ExaWizards, ANDPAD, Stockmark, InsightX, Taylor (YC S22), Members, renue, and others are recruiting for FDE positions. Consulting firms are also expanding organizations that go all the way to implementation, such as McKinsey's QuantumBlack and Accenture's FDE practice.

All figures in this report are based on actual projects that DeFactory supported in-house during the second half of 2025 through the first half of 2026. Due to the limited sample size, these are not statistics representative of the overall market and should be referenced solely as our own track record.

During the covered period, DeFactory closed 11 freelance FDE engagements. The average monthly rate was ¥1.35 million (range: ¥1.10 million to ¥1.80 million).

This figure is the average across the 11 engagements we actually closed, and does not statistically represent market-wide rates.

However, we hope it serves as a useful reference when placing orders, as it reflects actual transaction prices that reached negotiation and contract with client companies.

Definitions of "Individual Assignment" and "Tiny Team"

The classification of delivery models in this report is based not simply on headcount, but on whether the two functions of "problem discovery and requirements definition" and "implementation" are concentrated in a single person.

  • Individual assignment: A model in which a single FDE independently handles the entire process, from client interviews, articulating problems, and requirements definition through design, implementation, and testing
  • Tiny team: A small team of roughly 2 to 3 members in which dedicated members divide the roles of "consultant/PdM-type role handling problem discovery, requirements definition, and client liaison" and "engineer role handling design and implementation." In our actual track record, the typical composition is one consultant plus one or two full-stack engineers

The goal is not simply to increase headcount. The essence of the tiny team model is designing the setup so that a single person is not expected to simultaneously excel at two different specialties: "understanding the client" and "technical implementation."

Looking back at results by delivery model, an interesting pattern emerges.

Of the 9 engagements where full-stack engineers were assigned individually (one person per project), 2 failed to achieve the outcomes the client companies expected by the deadline.

In contrast, among the 2 engagements delivered by a multi-person "tiny team," no such shortfalls have occurred to date. *Note: Since the sample size is still small, this cannot be asserted as a general trend.

However, what the 2 unsuccessful engagements had in common was that a single person was expected to perform both "problem discovery and articulation" and "implementation" at a high level.

Based on this track record, DeFactory recommends multi-person delivery models over individual assignments, and our own delivery is structured around multi-person teams.

In projects DeFactory supported, the transition rate from PoC to production improved by roughly 2 to 3 times with FDE involvement. This figure is a practical estimate comparing projects DeFactory supported with projects before support or without support (based on interviews with client companies).

Failure case: Industry: telecommunications. Team: 3 members including one consultant and one full-stack engineer. The client's understanding of on-site issues and alignment on outcome definitions were insufficient, and the contract period was short, so the expected results could not be achieved within the planned timeframe.

Improvement case: Industry: telecommunications. Team: 3 members consisting of one consultant and two full-stack engineers. By leveraging agentic coding, the team shortened the planned development issues and reduced the internalization of development knowledge from the originally expected 6 months to 2 months.

Although our current track record data is limited, we introduce the industry-specific usage patterns we have observed through our support and evaluations (please refer to these as general usage patterns, not as performance figures).

  • Manufacturing: In many cases, production line and quality control data is fragmented, so an effective approach is to start by building a data integration platform and begin AI adoption with specific operations such as inspection and inventory management. Introducing image recognition AI into visual inspection processes is one representative usage pattern
  • Finance and insurance: A demanding industry with strict security and compliance requirements, where integration with existing core systems often becomes a challenge
  • SaaS and IT companies: Growing use cases include embedding AI features into their own products and receiving accompaniment support when establishing their own FDE organizations
  • Logistics and retail: An industry with many themes directly tied to frontline operations, such as demand forecasting and inventory optimization

While investment and hiring continue to expand across the overall market, as exemplified by so-called "PoC deaths," only a limited number of companies have been able to translate AI adoption into actual business outcomes.

DeFactory's own track record also shows a tendency for results to become unstable under individual-dependent delivery models.

Looking ahead to the second half of 2026, we make the following recommendations as key points for companies considering AI adoption.

  • Team design: Do not concentrate problem discovery and implementation in a single person; approach projects with a team structure
  • KPI design: Measure partner value by business outcomes such as operational efficiency, adoption rates, and speed of transition from PoC to production, rather than by hours worked
  • Contract duration: Accumulating domain knowledge requires a certain ramp-up period, and short-term contracts do not allow a full return on investment

In the first half of 2026, the FDE market continued to expand rapidly, driven by large-scale investments and a surge in hiring by major global players.

Meanwhile, DeFactory's own track record has revealed that delivery model design (individual assignment vs. tiny team) significantly influences outcomes.
