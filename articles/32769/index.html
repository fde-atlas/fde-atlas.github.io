The same job title, FDE, means a dedicated on-site engineer serving a single customer at Palantir, a deployment engineer responsible for production deployment and roadmap feedback at OpenAI, and the name of a certification program that trains partner engineers at Anthropic. In Korea, it splits into customer-site full-stack, infrastructure operations, and internal company automation types. When reading a job posting, look at who the role works for and whether the outcomes flow back into the product.

Key Takeaways

  • FDE is more like a career that varies by company. When reading a job posting, you should clarify who the role works for, what outcomes it leaves behind, and whether those outcomes flow back into the product.
  • The Palantir-style prototype structure is: dedicated service to a single customer, ownership of multiple problems, and turning problems that recur across multiple customers into platform components.
  • OpenAI's job posting states that the FDE owns the full process from discovery, scoping, design, and building to production deployment, with outcomes measured by adoption in production environments and evals-based feedback that can change the roadmap (base salary $185K–$300K, based on the job posting collected on 2026-11-24).
  • Anthropic's Frontier Academy does not expand its own workforce; instead, through a 4-day intensive course and a 12-week residency program, it trains engineers nominated by customer and partner organizations and grants the "Frontier Deployed Engineer" certification.
  • Korean articles propose a standard: what ends once a customer's needs are solved is SI, while what can distill reusable product patterns is FDE. However, some observations note that outcomes rarely flow back into the product in practice, so you should not rely solely on the wording in job postings.

Same Job Title, Different Careers: Why You Should Look Company by Company

This is the third article in the "FDE, Engineers Heading to the Field" series. The previous article summarized the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) as an engineer who enters a customer organization and turns the first use case into a pilot that actually runs. This article looks at how differently the same job title is defined at different companies.

Industry articles agree that there is no unified definition for this job title. Pragmatic Engineer's article (w4) writes that "every company has its own FDE," summarizing that some companies focus on closing sales, some on customer support, and others on contributions to the core product. An article on the Korean platform Brunch (w10) likewise calls the FDEs at Palantir, Google, and OpenAI "the same job title, different careers." The Candid newsletter (w9) points out that looking only at Korean job postings, the role can be divided into full-stack engineers who enter customer companies, MLOps engineers at industrial sites, and PMs who automate internal company operations.

Therefore, this article is based primarily on official job postings and company materials, using industry articles only to supplement what the postings do not cover. Job postings can change at any time; the following content is based on materials collected on November 24, 2026, and the originals should be re-checked before applying or using them as hiring criteria. Four criteria need to be examined for each company: who the role works for, what the main outcomes are, what the relationship with the product team is, and what capabilities are required.

Palantir: Prototypes Dedicated to a Single Customer

The FDE role originated at Palantir. Internally, Palantir calls FDEs "Delta," and until 2016, FDEs outnumbered regular software engineers (w4, w7). According to Paraform's article (w7), they join teams at government, financial, and commercial customers to get Palantir's own products such as Foundry and Gotham running in messy, siloed, and security-cleared environments. They consolidate scattered data sources, use custom code to handle exceptions the product team did not anticipate, and build workflows around how field personnel actually work.

The core of this structure is "dedicated service to a single customer." The Brunch article (w10) explains that Palantir assigns FDSEs (Forward Deployed Software Engineers) to a single customer, paired with a Deployment Strategist who bridges the gap between technical work and field priorities. Essentially, one team owns multiple problems for one customer end to end. The same is true of Palantir's definition relayed in the Pragmatic Engineer article (w4): the FDE's responsibilities resemble those of a startup CTO, with a small team taking end-to-end responsibility for important projects. The same article says Palantir expects FDEs to spend about 25% of their time on-site with customers, and hires candidates with only 1 year of experience after college. A former Palantir FDE recalls working on Airbus's final assembly line and in closed network environments isolated from the external internet.

Why choose such an expensive structure? According to the Brunch article's interpretation (w10), every customer project is effectively R&D. When the same structural problems recur across multiple customers, instead of solving them from scratch each time, they turned them into platform components such as ontologies, object models, permission systems, workflow engines, and provenance tracking. In other words, they pay the cost of deeply binding people to individual customers in exchange for platform maturity. However, the body text of Palantir's job pages could not be confirmed in the materials for this article, so this section relies on industry articles.

OpenAI: Workflows and Requirements Stated in the Job Posting

OpenAI's San Francisco FDE job posting (w2) introduces the team as "the team that turns research into production systems" and "where customer delivery meets core platform development." The FDE leads complex deployments of frontier models end to end, working with the most strategically significant customers. The workflow stated in the posting is, in order: discovery, technical scoping, system design, building, and production deployment. Outcome measures are adoption at the production stage, measurable workflow improvements, and evals-based feedback that can change the product and model roadmaps.

The specific responsibilities point in the same direction. Own multiple deployments from the first prototype to stable operation, build full-stack systems, and make trade-offs among scope, speed, and quality. Write code personally when necessary, distill proven patterns into tools, playbooks, and building blocks others can use, and relay field feedback on where the models perform well and where they fall short to the research and product teams. Requirements: 5+ years of engineering or technical deployment experience including customer-facing work; experience writing and reviewing frontend and backend production code in Python, JavaScript, and similar; experience building or deploying systems based on LLMs or generative models. The work arrangement is hybrid with 3 days per week in the office, with travel up to 50%.

The New York FDE manager job posting (w3) requires 10+ years of experience, including at least 2 years managing FDEs or customer-facing engineers, with travel up to 25%. Outcomes are measured by whether the team ships consistently and whether the signals communicated to the research and product teams are clear. The base salary ranges stated in the postings are: San Francisco FDE $185K–$300K, New York manager $302K–$385K, with equity separate. The postings note that these amounts may vary by location, skills, and experience.

Only two OpenAI job postings were confirmed this time, so the sub-directions within the FDE organization (such as teams building reusable solutions) could not be verified through documentation. According to the wording of the job postings, "organizing proven patterns into reusable tools" is part of the FDE's own responsibilities. The Pragmatic Engineer article (w4) states that OpenAI's FDE role was established as distinct from a professional-services-style Solutions Architect. The Brunch article (w10) writes that OpenAI formally established the "OpenAI Deployment Company" in May 2026, with 150 FDEs assigned to it.

Anthropic: Training Partner Engineers into FDEs

The Anthropic FDE learning path (w5) introduced in the previous piece is a roughly 9.5-hour self-paced course open to Claude Partner Network members, sharing nine courses with the developer path. What this piece focuses on is not the course structure but the audience. This path is not designed for Anthropic employees, but to enable engineers at partner companies to lead pilots at customer sites.

Claude Frontier Academy (w6) goes even further in this direction. It is a nomination-based program in which organizations nominate their best talent, with an initial investment of $100 million announced by Anthropic. They state that through the first program, FDE Residency, they will train 10,000 "Frontier Deployed Engineers" by the end of 2027. Note that the abbreviation is also FDE, but the full name is Frontier rather than Forward. The curriculum follows the medical training model. First comes a 4-day intensive course: the first three days are onsite, building a Claude system for a fictional company, from the first customer requirement through security review to handoff; on the fourth day, an assessment is conducted with a new scenario, and passing it earns the "Claude Resident Engineer" badge. Then, over 12 weeks, participants lead a Claude deployment within their own organization, receiving mentorship from cohort peers and support from Anthropic engineers, and after passing a final hands-on assessment they receive the "Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer" badge. The first badges are expected to be awarded in early 2027, and the program is currently in an early stage targeting select customers and partners.

The purpose of this design is to prevent FDE capability from being limited by the size of one's own workforce. Anthropic lists talent shortage as the most pressing problem for AI adoption, explaining that the key is scaling a few capable people into many (w6). The target audience is frontline engineers with solid fundamentals, who need experience building things with LLMs and experience helping others adopt AI. Anthropic does not refrain from hiring its own FDEs. The Candid newsletter (w9) says Anthropic's job posting describes the FDE as someone who ships Claude-based production applications together with strategic customers; the Brunch article (w10) writes that the posting requires LLM operations experience and eval framework experience, among other things. Training engineers outside the company gains scalability while choosing to control quality through evals and certification rather than direct hiring and management.

Other AI startups: Shorter cycles, blurrier boundaries

The Paraform article (w7) summarizes that AI startups like Runway and Greptile adapt the Palantir model to suit faster iteration cycles and small teams. This material contains no specific work details for Runway. For Greptile, which develops an AI code review tool, the article gives an example of an FDE's week: Monday, a technical discovery call with a prospective customer; Tuesday, building a proof of concept (PoC) against that customer's codebase; Wednesday, reviewing compliance requirements with the customer's security team. When a customer is comparing three competitors at once, a PoC sometimes needs to be delivered within a week, with a single engineer owning the entire customer relationship from the first call to production deployment. If Palantir accumulates depth over multi-year contracts, startups demand both speed and technical depth.

The Pragmatic Engineer article (w4) shows more varied models. Ramp operates roughly 15 FDEs organized into pods, and embeds FDEs into core product engineering teams, where they decide whether to do custom development or accelerate existing projects. They also work with the sales team to close deals. Healthcare startup Commure believes onsite work can account for up to 50%; industrial AI startup Matta requires finding solutions on factory floors; Lindy expects FDEs to build and maintain customers' no-code workflows while also serving as technical advisors.

There are caveats as well. The same article (w4) notes in a 2026 update that at most companies, FDEs rarely actually contribute to the platform, and in many places the role feels like "a Solutions Architect skilled in AI merged with a product engineer." This observation holds that the clear distinction from Solutions Engineers in 2025 had narrowed by 2026 hiring. On the other hand, the Paraform article (w7) draws the dividing line with Solutions Engineers and Sales Engineers by whether the FDE belongs to the engineering organization and is accountable for post-signing deployment outcomes.

South Korea: The line drawn by the Channel Talk job posting and three types

Channel Talk's (Channel Corp.) FDE job posting (w8) introduces the team as one that solves AX (AI transformation) problems with engineering methods, and the team closest to customers among Channel Talk's engineers. The work is to solve customer support and operations problems exposed at customer sites in a scalable way, through recipes or Copilot for the AI customer support agent ALF. The posting explicitly states that the work is not one-off customization per customer, but rapidly building prototypes that are field-validated and can be adopted by product teams, and lists organizing prototypes and recipes into reusable forms as a responsibility. The role is close to a Technical Project Manager, expecting 5+ years of experience, participation in projects for Japanese and global customers, and a desire to grow into a PM, AI Product Lead, or founder. The hiring process consists of resume screening, a 30-45 minute technical pre-interview, a 3-day take-home assignment, and three rounds of interviews; the assignment assesses how one analyzes problems and how one uses AI.

The Candid newsletter (w9), citing this job posting, proposes a criterion for distinguishing SI (system integration) from FDE: solving one customer's need and ending there is closer to SI; distilling reusable product patterns from one customer's problem is closer to FDE. The phrase "not one-off customization" in the Channel Talk job posting is an example of a company writing this criterion directly into its posting.

The same article (w9) divides Korean FDEs into three types. The first is the full-stack type embedded in customer companies, with Channel Talk and Enhans as examples: they enter the customer's company, solve problems on top of their own product, and look for patterns that can be productized. The second is the model and infrastructure operations type, represented by MakinaRocks: they are responsible for stably deploying and operating AI services in industrial settings, requiring skills such as MLOps, PyTorch/ONNX, Docker/K8s, and vLLM, so this is closer to SRE and MLOps than to a PM direction. The third is the internal business automation type, with Krafton's AI FDE as an example: rather than serving external customers, they are dispatched to internal business units to automate operations; anyone can apply regardless of experience or educational background, and their cost basis is internal productivity rather than external contracts. The article adds that there is also another type of FDE closer to the implementation and onboarding leads at foreign enterprise software companies.

At-a-glance comparison: building what, for whom

Organizing the preceding content by four criteria yields the following (based on materials collected as of 2026-11-24).

- Palantir: works for its own individual customers (government, finance, commercial). The main deliverables are data integrations and workflows running in customer environments. One interpretation holds that recurring field problems are absorbed into platform components (w10). The required capability is the ability to untangle ambiguous problems, and candidates can be hired with 1 year of experience after college (w4). - OpenAI: works for its own strategic customers. Deliverables are systems deployed to production and feedback based on evals. The job posting explicitly lists responsibilities such as conveying feedback to product and research teams and organizing patterns into tools and playbooks. It requires 5+ years of experience, full-stack production code, and LLM systems experience (w2). - Anthropic Frontier Academy: trains engineers nominated by customer and partner organizations, with participants leading deployments in their own organizations. Deliverables are deployments led within 12 weeks and certification. The materials do not explicitly mention feedback loops back to product teams. It requires LLM building experience and experience helping AI land in practice (w6). - Greptile and other AI startups: work for new and prospective customers, with deliverables being PoCs and custom integrations on a weekly cadence. Deployment experience is reportedly relayed to the product team almost in real time, while speed and the ability to handle security due diligence are required (w7). - Channel Talk: handles AX challenges for its own customer companies and across the company, with deliverables being prototypes and recipes that product teams can adopt. The company says it directly influences the product roadmap, and requires 5+ years of experience and business acumen (w8). - MakinaRocks: responsible for stable deployment and operation of models for industrial-site customers, requiring MLOps capabilities (w9). - Krafton: builds business automation for internal business units, with no requirements on experience or education (w9).

Laid out this way, two axes distinguish the companies. One is "who you work for" (your own customers, partner personnel, or internal business units), and the other is "whether deliverables flow back into the product." However, on the second axis, what job postings say may differ from actual practice, since one observation (w4) notes that in many cases, even when a posting mentions product feedback loops, the work still remains customer-specific implementation.

How to read job postings, common mistakes, and the next article

The Candid briefing (w9) suggests checking three things when reading an FDE job posting: who the customer is (external customer companies, internal business units, or enterprises), where results are measured (product adoption, contract expansion, model operational stability, internal cost savings, PoC conversion rate), and whether the results flow back into the product. If you are the hiring side, writing the answers to these three questions into the job posting is a way to narrow the expectation gap with candidates.

There are four common mistakes. First, looking only at the job title and preparing as if for a regular engineer interview. The Brunch article (w10) observed candidates who prepared mainly by grinding coding problems and memorizing terminology fail for unclear reasons, pointing out that the problem lies not in ability but in direction. Second, directly transplanting US salary figures to Korea. The Candid briefing (w9) warns that US data largely reflects the equity compensation of frontier AI companies and should not be applied directly to the Korean market. Third, conflating Anthropic's "Frontier Deployed Engineer" credential with the general "Forward Deployed Engineer" role. Fourth, treating the phrase "contributing to the product" in a job posting as actual practice. A better approach is to ask in the interview for recent examples of FDE deliverables being incorporated into the product.

For further study, start by reading the OpenAI FDE job posting (w2) to understand the workflow and performance metrics, then compare the different types in Korea through the Channel Talk job posting (w8) and the Candid briefing (w9). Next, learn how partners are trained through the Frontier Academy introduction (w6), and confirm the gap with reality through the 2026 update notes in the Pragmatic Engineer article (w4). FDE job posting checklist

Who you work for

  • Is the customer an external customer company or an internal business unit
  • Dedicated to one customer, or moving across multiple customers
  • Does it specify the proportion of on-site work and the scope of travel

Results and deliverables

  • How are results measured (adoption · contract expansion · operational stability · cost savings)
  • Is there a path for deliverables to flow back into products and tools
  • Can you ask for recent examples of FDE deliverables being incorporated into the product

Conditions to verify

  • Did you note the date you viewed the job posting
  • Did you distinguish whether the compensation is base salary or total compensation
  • Did you distinguish between Forward and Frontier Deployed Engineer
