On May 17, 2026, Business Insider reported that the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is becoming one of the most sought-after roles in the AI industry.

The title translates literally as "Forward Deployed Engineer," but a phrase that better fits the Chinese context would be "AI implementation engineer."

Data from Indeed shows that in April 2025 there were 643 job postings for related roles; by April 2026, that number had grown to 5,330, a year-over-year surge of 729%.

This role is neither an ordinary software engineer nor a traditional Sales Engineer.

A Forward Deployed Engineer is more like a "technical strike team" dispatched to the customer's front line, writing code while understanding the customer's business, and truly connecting models, APIs, agents, and internal enterprise systems.

OpenAI's FDE job posting shows that these engineers are responsible for the full deployment process, from requirements discovery and technical scoping to system design and production launch, working directly with the engineering and business teams of strategic customers.

The rise of the FDE comes against the backdrop of enterprise AI entering a period of "implementation anxiety."

Over the past year, many companies have purchased large models, launched AI tools, and piloted agents, but many projects have remained stuck at the demo and experimentation stage; once they enter production systems, they run into issues with permissions, data, processes, security, compliance, and stability.

What enterprises need at this point is not someone who can only present solutions, but someone who can solve problems on-site and directly deliver systems.

This is why companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, Stripe, and Google Cloud are all hiring FDEs.

According to Business Insider, the FDE role was first popularized by Palantir and has now returned to the spotlight as enterprise AI demand heats up, with salaries for such roles typically ranging from $170,000 (1.16 million RMB) to over $200,000 (1.36 million RMB).

Consulting firms have also started joining the talent grab.

In April, EY announced the launch of FDE roles in the UK and Ireland, positioned to help customers advance their highest-priority AI use cases from the experimentation stage to scalable, production-ready deployments.

A similar job description at QuantumBlack, a McKinsey company, also emphasizes that engineers need to embed themselves in customer environments and deploy and scale next-generation AI platforms under real-world constraints.

This hiring boom shows that the focus of talent demand in the AI industry is shifting.

Model researchers and algorithm engineers remain important, but what enterprises truly lack are people who can turn models into business systems.

AI is no longer just about demos and parameters; it is starting to be about deployment, delivery, and whether it can truly enter enterprise workflows.
