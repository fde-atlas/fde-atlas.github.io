ElevenLabs Forward Deployed Engineer Interview Experience (2025)

Aced (formerly Exponent) · 2026-04-13 · 2 min read

Summary

A candidate recounts their 2025 Forward Deployed Engineer interview loop at ElevenLabs, which took about two weeks end to end after an internal referral. The process included a recruiter screen, a timed coding assessment, live coding in a Google Doc, a customer-style case study, and a CTO/founder interview. Across rounds, interviewers repeatedly probed individual contributions rather than team achievements, and mixed behavioral questions into technical rounds. The coding and case rounds were demanding and signal-driven, while the founder round felt off-topic and vague about the role. The candidate passed everything and received a verbal yes, but ElevenLabs ultimately declined to extend an offer because it only wanted to hire in San Francisco and saw immigration risks, even though the candidate was open to relocating.

Key points

  • Five-stage loop over roughly two weeks: recruiter screen, timed assessment, Google Doc coding, case study, founder interview.
  • Interviewers consistently separated individual contributions from team-level work across all rounds.
  • Coding and case rounds were hard and signal-driven; the founder round felt off-topic and vague on role definition.
  • Candidate passed all interviews and got a verbal yes, but no offer followed.
  • Final rejection stemmed from SF-only hiring policy and perceived immigration risks, despite relocation willingness.

I passed the interviews and it was verbally confirmed, but when the process was finished they decided they only want to hire in SF. Even though I was open to moving, they saw some immigration risks that they didn’t want to continue.

Interview process

I got into ElevenLabs through a connection and the whole process moved pretty fast, about two weeks end to end. My loop was recruiter screen, a timed coding assessment, a live coding interview in a Google Doc, a customer-style case study, and then a CTO/founder interview. The consistent theme was that they wanted strong individuals, not people who hide behind team language, and they mixed behavioral questions into basically every round. The coding and case rounds were on the harder, more signal-driven side, while the founder round felt a bit off-topic and oddly fuzzy about the actual role definition. I passed the interviews and got a verbal yes, but I ended up not getting the final offer.

Recruiter screen

Overview

The recruiter screen was more clinical than most initial calls I get. She went pretty deep on experience and motivation, and stayed focused on asking about individual contributions, which I appreciated it because it gave me a clear read on what they care about.

Question types asked

Specific questions asked

Tell me about the biggest project you're proud of, and be specific about what you built yourself.

What exactly was your individual contribution, not just the team's?

Why are you motivated by this kind of role?

I think she was really testing whether I had strong individual ownership. I could feel her trying to separate "team built X" from "I built X," and that theme stayed consistent. The vibe was focused and pretty clinical, but in a good way. I came away thinking they want strong individuals who are proud of specific things they shipped themselves, not people who only talk in team-level abstractions.
