Cursor Forward Deployed Engineer Interview: Questions & How to Prepare

Clique Media Group Pty Ltd · 2026-07-22 · 13 min read

Summary

This article analyzes Cursor's five Forward Deployed Engineer job postings to infer what the interview likely assesses, since none of the postings describes the actual interview process. The role embeds with customer teams, owns discovery through production support, and explicitly states "This is not a demo role." The India posting retains original wording, while four rewritten postings emphasize production agents built on Grok and outcome metrics like revenue and hours saved. The author maps each responsibility to likely interview probes and recommends preparation centered on a real workflow you built, instrumented evals, a discovery story with measurable outcomes, and detailed incident debugging, rather than algorithm drills.

Key points

  • None of the five postings describes interview stages, so the author advises ignoring claims about a fixed FDE loop.
  • Discovery is the anchor responsibility: success metrics must be agreed before rollout and monitoring mean anything.
  • Rewritten postings (SF/NY, EMEA, Australia/Singapore, Japan) drop Cursor's name and stress agents on Grok plus business-outcome metrics.
  • Likely questions cover finding real bottlenecks, designing workflows live, evals and model regressions, and explaining tradeoffs to Staff+ engineers.
  • Preparation advice: build and instrument a real workflow in Cursor daily, and keep broken versions as evidence of debugging experience.

What does a Cursor Forward Deployed Engineer do?

A Forward Deployed EngineerAn engineer who embeds inside a customer's own engineering org to scope ambiguous problems and ship production-grade Cursor workflows a senior engineer keeps using after they leave. Press Enter for the full definition. (FDE) embeds with a customer's team and ships production systems that measurably improve how that team works. Every current posting states that this is not a demo role. You own the work from the first discovery call through launch, iteration and support after launch, and you are responsible for systems that work in the real world.

The responsibilities below come from the original posting, and the India posting still lists them almost word for word. Every likely interview question on this page starts from one of them.

  • Lead discovery with customer engineers to identify the real bottleneck and define clear success metrics.
  • Design and build Cursor-powered workflows: large-scale refactors, migrations, PR review loops, incident-to-fix pipelines, spec-to-implementation systems.
  • Ship a fast first version in days, then harden it over weeks with rollout plans, monitoring, and iteration on real usage.
  • Own production quality: tracing, evals, metrics, debugging model behavior, latency and cost tradeoffs, and failure modes.
  • Work directly with Staff+/Platform/Eng leaders, going deep in code while communicating tradeoffs and results clearly.
  • Turn what you build into reusable patterns and push improvements back into the core Cursor product.

The six run in the order the work happens, and the first one carries the rest. Discovery is where the success metric gets agreed. The rollout plans and monitoring in later bullets only mean something against a metric someone signed up to. Skip discovery and an engagement can look busy for six weeks, then fail to say what changed. In our view that failure is easy to miss, because a shipped workflow is visible on day one while a missing baseline only surfaces at the review.

Which Forward Deployed Engineer posting should I prepare from?

Prepare from the one you are applying to, because the current postings come in two versions. The India posting keeps the original wording quoted on this page: production-grade Cursor workflows, the six responsibilities above and much the same fit criteria, without the year counts.

The other four (New York/Remote/San Francisco, EMEA, Australia/Singapore and Japan) were rewritten, and their descriptions don't mention Cursor by name. They describe production agents and coding-agent workflows built "on Grok and our models", for messy business workflows as well as inside a customer's repo. Their responsibilities add the systems around the model, such as tools, retrieval and evals. They also say to measure the outcome that matters, such as revenue, cost, hours saved or cycle time, rather than seat adoption.

Both versions keep the same core: discovery, production quality, end-to-end ownership and reliability, with the line "This is not a demo role." If you apply to one of the rewritten four, we would add two things to your preparation: an agent you shipped outside a code repo, and an outcome you measured in the customer's own numbers.

What does the Forward Deployed Engineer interview assess?

None of the five postings gives interview stages, rounds or a timeline, so be skeptical of any claim that "the FDE loop is X". The bar the postings set is what you can prepare for. Their responsibilities and fit criteria point at a few things any format would sample, whatever the sequence turns out to be.

When a company doesn't state its process, build toward what every format has in common: a real workflow you built, a discovery story with a measurable outcome, and plain reasoning about production reliability. That preparation carries into a screen, a panel or an onsite.

A first version in days rewards cutting scope, and owning production quality rewards the opposite. There is no clean answer to memorize for the row that asks for both, so bring a case where the balance went wrong. A workflow that shipped in four days and then took three weeks to repair tells an interviewer more than a clean success, as long as you can name what you would have built differently on day one.

Company size changes which rows you can back with evidence. If you come from a fifteen-person startup, you have probably never run a staged rollout, because there was nothing to stage it across, and claiming one falls apart on the follow-up. Bring what you did run, such as a feature flag and one team on the new path for a week, with a number you watched. From a large org the risk runs the other way: you may have shipped inside someone else's rollout machinery and be unable to say which parts you built.

What interview questions should I expect?

The cards turn the responsibilities into likely question types, since Cursor publishes no question list for this role. Prepare a story for each, plus a live workflow where you can, and expect follow-up questions to push past your first answer. Most cards note how a strong answer differs from a weak one.

A time you found the real bottleneck on a team that assumed the problem was elsewhere. Strong answers name the metric that proved it and how they would know the workflow worked. Weak ones narrate activity with no measurable outcome.

Design a Cursor-powered workflow out loud, such as a refactor, a migration or a PR review loop, and build it end to end if asked. The postings name Python and JavaScript/TypeScript. Strong answers show a workflow you shipped. Weak ones describe the tool in the abstract.

Evals, tracing and metrics: how you would catch a model regression, weigh latency against cost, and handle a failure mode. Strong answers cite a specific eval or trace and a regression they debugged. Weak ones say "I'd add monitoring" with no specifics.

A fuzzy problem you turned into a shipped system, and a place you owned infra or prompt iteration nobody assigned you. Every posting asks for people who thrive in ambiguity and build end to end.

Explain a technical decision to a Staff+ or platform leader: the tradeoff, what you gave up and the result. Strong answers own the cost of the choice. Weak ones present it as free. Every posting pairs going deep technically with explaining tradeoffs and results to Staff+ and platform leaders.

The original posting's company blurb prized truth-seeking and shipping code on a flat, talent-dense team. Have a concrete reason you want the FDE role and a workflow you would want to build. Give specifics, not a sales line.

Production reliability is the theme the original posting keeps returning to: it appears in two of the six responsibilities and two of the six fit criteria. A weak answer reaches straight for tooling, usually tracing and a dashboard.

When a workflow that worked last week starts producing bad diffs, first establish whether the same input still reproduces the failure. A reproducible failure is something you fix in the prompt or the context. One that won't reproduce has to be detected before it can be fixed. That is different work and a different answer, so say which you checked first.

The last card looks like a throwaway, but the original posting gave you something to work with. Its company blurb called the organization flat and the team small and talent dense. It said Cursor liked people who were truth-seeking, passionate and creative, and who enjoyed spirited debate and shipping code. In a role embedded inside another company's engineering org, spirited debate is more than a personality note. It describes the moment a customer's staff engineer disagrees with your design in front of their team, so prepare an answer for that moment.

How do I prepare for the Cursor Forward Deployed Engineer interview?

An FDE ships production systems for a living, so the strongest evidence is a workflow you designed and hardened yourself: something an interviewer can probe and you can defend in detail.

  • 1Work on a real repo in Cursor every day. Run a large refactor, a migration or a PR review loop end to end with Tab, Agent and Plan modeA mode that makes no edits: it researches the codebase and produces an editable plan you review before any code changes. Press Enter for the full definition.. Read what changed in Cursor in 2026 so your workflow uses the current model lineup.
  • 2Instrument something. Add tracing, an eval or a metric to a workflow you built, then debug a model regression on purpose.
  • 3Rehearse one discovery story: a fuzzy problem you scoped into a measurable outcome and shipped. Name the bottleneck, the success metric and what you'd change next time.
  • 4Learn the enterprise money and rollout mechanics in how Cursor pricing and usage work, because FDEs ship inside customer orgs with real rollout plans.
  • 5If you haven't used Cursor much, start with the fundamentals and come back to these steps.

Step 2 depends on step 1. We would put the most work into step 2, but it can't come first: there is nothing to instrument until step 1 has produced a workflow running on a repo you know. Instrumentation sounds senior, so it is tempting to rehearse it early, as vocabulary attached to nothing. That falls apart on the follow-up, which asks which regression you caught.

Build one workflow you can break on demand, and keep the bad versions: the prompt that produced the wrong refactor, and the run where the agent edited a file it should never have touched. A clean final state is the least useful thing to bring, because there is nothing to ask about it. Write down what broke while you remember it.

Standard software-interview prep covers less of these postings than you might expect. In the original fit list, only one bullet is about coding. The other five cover customer accountability, ambiguity, AI-native workflows in production, end-to-end ownership and production reliability. A weekend on those is likely to pay off in more of the interview than a weekend of algorithm drills.

Each day, take one real task on a repo, such as a refactor, a migration or a PR review loop, and build it end to end in Cursor. Then attach one eval or metric, and name how you would catch the workflow breaking.

What qualifications does the Forward Deployed Engineer role require?

The original posting's six fit criteria work as a checklist, because each one names something you can show. They weight production experience and customer accountability over years alone.

  • 5+ years building software, coding in Python and JavaScript/TypeScript.
  • 2+ years customer-facing, leading discovery and accountable for customer outcomes.
  • Comfort in ambiguity: turning fuzzy problems into clear scopes and shipped systems.
  • AI-native workflows owned in production, not just prototypes, including debugging real model failures.
  • End-to-end ownership across frontend, backend, infra, and prompt iteration.
  • Production reliability experience: metrics, alerts, safe rollouts, incident response.

On experience, the four rewritten postings differ from that list in two ways. They ask for 5+ years in software engineering, machine learning engineering or data science, and they move 2+ years in a customer-facing role to a nice-to-have list. The India posting drops both year counts.

We would expect the fourth bullet to screen out the most applicants. Building AI-native workflows is common now. Owning one in production is rarer, and the posting adds debugging real-world model failures, which rules out a prototype you demoed once and never operated. Rehearse one incident in detail, including what alerted you and what you rolled back.

One line appears in every version: "This is not a demo role." If your background is presales demos without shipping and running production systems, build that experience before you apply. Expect the interview to look for code you shipped and kept working, not a slide about it.

Frequently asked questions

Does Cursor publish its Forward Deployed Engineer interview process?

No. All five current postings describe the role in detail, and none of them covers stages, rounds or a timeline. Train for what the postings describe, which is discovery, building and production reliability, not a rumored sequence of rounds.

Where is the Cursor Forward Deployed Engineer role based?

Cursor lists five postings. Their locations are San Francisco, New York or remote; London, for the EMEA posting; Australia or Singapore; Japan; and Bengaluru, New Delhi and Mumbai, for the India posting. The earlier Australia/Japan/Singapore posting and the original single posting have both been removed. Check the location line on the posting you apply to.

Which team does the Cursor Forward Deployed Engineer role sit on?

Solutions, in all five current postings. The original posting filed the role under Software Engineering (Forward Deployed Engineering), so check the team on the posting you apply to rather than on an older copy.

What should I build to prepare?

A real Cursor-powered workflow you designed and hardened, such as a refactor, a migration or a review loop, with tracing, an eval or a metric attached, plus a debugged model failure to talk through. If you apply to one of the rewritten postings, an agent you shipped on a business workflow helps too.

How is the FDE role different from a Solutions Architect or sales engineer?

Every version of the posting says plainly, "This is not a demo role." FDEs embed and ship production systems end to end (frontend, backend, infra and prompts) and own their reliability, not just presales demos. Bring a system you shipped and kept running, and be ready to explain how you ran it.
