OpenAI Deployment Company: what it means for FDE candidates

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 17 min read

Summary

OpenAI launched the OpenAI Deployment Company (DeployCo) in May 2026 as a majority-owned subsidiary with over $4 billion in initial investment, and agreed to acquire Tomoro to bring in roughly 150 Forward Deployed Engineers and Deployment Specialists. The article is a candidate-facing guide distinguishing DeployCo hiring from OpenAI's internal FDE team. As of September 2026, DeployCo lists FDE openings in the US, UK, EU, Singapore and Australia; the US role pays $170K–$400K, requires 4+ years of experience, is hybrid with up to 50% travel, and is framed as a founding player-coach role heading toward management. It cautions that the Tomoro deal had not been confirmed closed and that a reported $14 billion valuation came from media, not OpenAI. It also outlines interview prep around diagnosing value, choosing workflows with customers, and shipping to production.

Key points

  • DeployCo is a majority-owned OpenAI subsidiary launched May 2026 with $4B+ initial investment.
  • Tomoro acquisition (about 150 FDEs and Deployment Specialists) was agreed but not confirmed closed.
  • FDE openings span US, UK, EU, Singapore and Australia as of September 2026.
  • US role: $170K–$400K, 4+ years experience, hybrid, up to 50% travel, founding player-coach scope.
  • Interview prep focuses on diagnosis, workflow selection with stakeholders, and production delivery.

How an engagement starts

This is the part that matters most for your preparation, because it tells you what the work looks like on day one.

OpenAI says a typical Deployment Company engagement will begin with a focused diagnostic of where AI can create the most value, followed by a small number of priority workflows chosen with the customer’s leadership and operating teams. Source 1OpenAI launches the OpenAI Deployment Company to help businesses build around intelligencePublisherOpenAISource typecompany blog FDEs then work inside the organization to design, build, test and deploy production systems. Source 1OpenAI launches the OpenAI Deployment Company to help businesses build around intelligencePublisherOpenAISource typecompany blog

Read that as a job description and it asks for three skills:

  • Diagnosis. Finding where value is, across a whole organization, before anyone builds.
  • Choosing. Narrowing to a few workflows, with leaders and the people who run the work.
  • Shipping. Taking those workflows all the way to production, inside someone else’s systems.

What a diagnostic sounds like in the room

Here is a short illustration about a fictional customer, using our method, not OpenAI’s. A logistics company’s COO says: “We want AI across dispatch, invoicing and customer emails.” A weak response starts designing an . A strong one sorts the ideas first.

For each candidate workflow, ask four things:

  • Who owns it? A workflow with no owner never ships.
  • What does it cost today? Hours, errors or wait time the customer already measures.
  • Is the data reachable? Can you read the inputs this month, or does it start with a security review?
  • What happens when the model is wrong? Who catches it, and how bad is a miss?

Then say your choice out loud, with the reason:

“Of the ideas on the board, I’d start with invoice exceptions: the carrier invoices that don’t match the agreed rate. The finance lead owns them, her team already counts how many they clear each week, the invoices and rate cards sit in two systems we can read now, and a wrong suggestion gets caught by a clerk before anything is paid. Dispatch is bigger, but a wrong call there strands a truck, so it goes second.”

That answer shows diagnosis, choosing and a first version you can ship. Our lesson on going from discovery call to technical proposal teaches the full sequence, and stakeholders and success metrics covers the “who owns it, how is it measured” half. When you have picked the workflow, our post on scoping a vague AI request takes the next step: an eval set, human review and a launch criterion.

You can rehearse the same move in the free practice case, against a customer who holds back facts until you ask for them.

Source 15The Role of The Forward Deployed Engineer with Tom McLaughlin, FDE at The OpenAI Deployment CompanyPublisherMBN Solutions (YouTube)Source typerecorded talk or interview

This is one person’s view, not a published list of hiring criteria. It is still a cheap thing to prepare for. Have one concrete story for each skill:

  • Orchestrating agents: a task you split across several AI agents or tools, and how you checked their work.
  • Feedback loops: an eval or check that ran on every change, and a failure it caught.
  • Your own tools: a script or internal tool you built because the existing one was too slow.

The question on how you use AI coding tools is the natural place for the first and third stories. The evaluation harness question drills the second.

What changes in how you apply and prepare

Before you apply to an OpenAI FDE role

  • Find out which organization the role sits in: OpenAI or DeployCo.
  • Read the live posting for pay, travel and office days.
  • Write a separate “why” answer for that organization.
  • Prepare a diagnostic story: a time you chose what to build first across many requests.
  • Prepare a field-feedback story: something you saw at a customer that changed a product.
  • Prepare a reuse story: something you built once and turned into a shared component.
  • Know the APIs you have built with, and their limits.

Ask which organization, in these words. On the recruiter call: “Is this role on OpenAI’s Forward Deployed Engineering team, or with the OpenAI Deployment Company? And how does the team decide which customer engagements to take on?” The second question is useful either way: the answer tells you what the first months will look like.

Have the field-feedback story ready for both. The internal posting counts roadmap feedback as part of success, and DeployCo’s postings ask for it too. Source 3Forward Deployed Engineer - USPublisherOpenAI Deployment Company (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job postingSource 4Forward Deployed Engineer - UKPublisherOpenAI Deployment Company (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job postingSource 11Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) - SF | OpenAIPublisherOpenAISource typecompany job posting The field feedback question is where to practise it.

Tailor the “why”. For the internal team, anchor on its focus: turning research into production systems with a small number of customers. For DeployCo, anchor on the founding, player-coach scope and on standardizing what repeats across customers. Source 3Forward Deployed Engineer - USPublisherOpenAI Deployment Company (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job posting A two-sentence version might sound like this:

“I’ve shipped the same kind of document-triage workflow for three clients, and each time I turned the repeated parts into a shared library. A founding role whose job includes turning field patterns into reusable deployment building blocks is that same work at a bigger scale.”

Our why this company question has the full structure; fill it with the right organization’s facts and your own numbers.

Rehearse the first month. Both organizations embed you with customers, so expect some version of “what would you do in your first weeks with a new customer?” Work through the first-month plan question with the diagnostic above as your opening move.

Know the platform. You will be building on OpenAI’s products inside someone else’s systems, so be ready for the question “Which of our APIs or products have you built with, and what did you find limiting?” For the assessment itself, OpenAI’s interview guide says skills-based formats vary by team and may include pair coding, take-home projects or technical tests. Source 17Interview Guide | OpenAIPublisherOpenAISource typecompany hiring page Our post on the OpenAI FDE take-home sets out one routine that works whichever format you get; none of the reports it covers names the Deployment Company, and we found none that does.

  • Treating DeployCo as delivery only. Its postings ask for feedback to Product and Research, so bring a story that shows it.
