Skills and Qualifications Required for FDEs | Explained Through the Hiring Requirements of OpenAI, Anthropic, and Palantir

Summary

Based on official job postings from OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, and Databricks (as of October 2026), this article organizes the skills needed for Forward Deployed Engineers into three layers: production-quality software engineering, LLM/AI implementation including prompt design, agents, and evals, and customer-facing problem discovery. Experience requirements vary widely, from 1+ years at Palantir to 8-10+ years at Databricks in Tokyo, but all emphasize end-to-end delivery experience. No universal certification is required; Databricks asks for its own certification and Palantir's government role requires security clearance. The article closes with skill-building roadmaps for engineers from different backgrounds.

Key points

  • FDE skills form three layers: engineering, LLM implementation, and customer discovery.
  • Anthropic's London FDE role requires 4+ years and production LLM experience.

  • No mandatory certifications; bilingual skills matter for Tokyo roles.
  • Building an evals suite is a strong, recommended preparation step.

Written and reviewed by: FDE Talent Editorial Team / Primary sources such as official job postings and official announcements from each company are cited

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a hybrid role that requires, alongside the implementation skills of a software engineer, practical knowledge of LLMs and AI as well as the ability to uncover customer challenges. In other words, once you understand the structure of the required skills, you can deliberately steer your experience in your current role toward FDE work.

This article organizes the skill set required for FDEs into three layers, based on the job postings (as of October 2026) and public information of leading FDE-hiring companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, and Databricks, and explains how to acquire each skill and whether certifications are necessary.

Key Takeaways of This Article

  • The skills required for FDEs can be organized into three layers: ① production-quality software engineering, ② LLM/AI implementation, and ③ customer engagement and problem discovery.
  • Anthropic's London FDE posting requires 4+ years of experience in technical roles involving customer engagement, plus production experience with LLMs including prompt design, agent development, and evaluation frameworks (as of October 2026).
  • OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting measures results through production usage, business impact, and evaluation (eval)-based feedback, while SoftBank's FDE posting lists experience building evaluation infrastructure as a preferred qualification; experience building LLM Evals is therefore a strong talking point in FDE interviews (as of October 2026).
  • Experience requirements for FDE postings vary widely between postings (even among the Tokyo postings alone, Palantir and Databricks range from 1+ to 10+ years after graduation), and the postings from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Databricks all cite, alongside years of experience, experience building and shipping to production (as of October 2026).
  • There is no mandatory certification exam for FDEs; as of October 2026, among Tokyo-based FDE postings, OpenAI does not mention certifications, Databricks lists its own certification, and Palantir's government-facing posting lists security clearance as a requirement.

Think of the FDE Skill Set in Three Layers

Looking across the hiring requirements of these companies, the abilities required of FDEs can be organized into the following three layers. Excelling in just one of them is not enough; you are expected to exceed a certain standard in all three layers.

FDEに求められる3層のスキル
LayerContentExample hiring requirements as evidence
① Software engineeringFull-stack implementation skills, design with production operations in mind, and the ability to build things that work even in broken data environmentsPalantir's job posting describes the FDSE role as "close to being a startup CTO," explaining that it involves executing high-impact projects end-to-end with a small team and minimal supervision (as of October 2026)
② LLM/AI implementationPrompt design, AI agent development, building evaluation (Evals) infrastructure, and operating LLMs at production scaleAnthropic's London FDE posting requires "production experience with LLMs, including advanced prompt engineering, agent development, evaluation frameworks, and large-scale deployments" (as of October 2026)
③ Customer engagement and problem discoveryBusiness requirements gathering, identifying high-value application areas, building consensus from executives to frontline staff, and structuring ambiguous situationsIn an interview with The Pragmatic Engineer (August 2025), OpenAI described FDEs as a role that is "more hands-on than a solutions architect and deals with more ambiguous situations"

Anthropic's London FDE job posting (as of October 2026) describes the FDE's job as building production applications using Claude within customers' systems. Responsibilities include delivering artifacts such as MCP servers, subagents, and agent skills to customers, identifying reproducible deployment patterns and feeding that knowledge back to the product and engineering teams, and building long-term relationships with customers; travel to customer sites is frequent (25-50%). It is a role whose core is implementation, yet whose responsibilities also extend to building customer relationships and feeding insights back into the product.

① Software Engineering Skills

The foundation of an FDE is, above all, the engineering ability to "build something that works in production on your own." At customer sites, there is no polished CI/CD or clean data. The following abilities appear repeatedly in job requirements.

  • Full-stack implementation skills: able to write everything end-to-end, from API design and data pipelines to the frontend
  • Data engineering: cleansing, integrating, and modeling messy business data (equivalent to Palantir's "ontology design")
  • Commitment to production quality: code that can be "placed in a customer's production environment," including authentication, permissions, audit logs, and error handling
  • Development under constraints: adapting to non-ideal environments such as on-premises, closed networks, and legacy system integration

How Many Years of Experience Are Needed?

It varies by company. In job postings as of October 2026, Palantir's Tokyo government-facing FDSE requires 1+ years of professional experience after graduating from university, US fintech Ramp's Software Engineer, Forward Deployed requires 3+ years of software engineering experience (preferred), Anthropic's London FDE requires 4+ years in technical roles involving customer engagement, OpenAI's Tokyo FDE requires 5+ years of experience in engineering or technical deployment including customer engagement, and Databricks' Tokyo FDE requires 8+ years (the company's Tokyo Sr. Forward Deployed Engineer requires 10+ years). Alongside years of experience, what each company emphasizes is experience building and shipping. Ramp states that it values "what you built over where you learned it," and OpenAI cites experience scoping and delivering complex systems. Even if small, a track record of delivering a project from start to finish yourself becomes a strong asset.

② LLM/AI Implementation Skills

FDE job postings at generative AI companies list experience in this layer as a requirement. OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting cites experience building and deploying systems using LLMs and generative models, and SoftBank's FDE posting (seconded to SB OAI Japan) requires experience building or deploying applications using LLMs and generative AI (as of October 2026). What is being tested is not the "I've used ChatGPT" level, but experience integrating LLMs into production systems and operating them.

  • Prompt engineering: reproducible prompt design, context management, and control of structured output
  • AI agent development: designing multi-step tool-use workflows where the model calls external APIs, references databases, and writes to internal systems. Overseas commentary mentions frameworks such as LangGraph, LangChain, CrewAI, and DSPy, but MCP (Model Context Protocol) appears more often by name in FDE job postings; Anthropic's FDE role lists delivering MCP servers and the like to customers as a duty (counts in the note below)
  • Evaluation (evals) infrastructure: building evaluation suites that detect hallucination, performance degradation, bias, and lack of grounding before production. Overseas commentary calls this "a non-negotiable, must-have skill for FDEs in 2026"
  • RAG and fine-tuning: the standard patterns for connecting a company's own data, and the judgment of when to use which

Counting the body text of 517 job postings (79 companies, including non-engineering roles) whose job titles contain Forward Deployed, FDE, or FDSE, collected by our editorial team on October 16, 2026 from 133 public job boards (mostly overseas companies; 131 responses), 21 companies and 70 postings mentioned evaluation (evals, evaluation frameworks, etc.), and MCP was mentioned by 10 companies in 29 postings. Few postings name frameworks: LangChain appeared in 3 companies and 11 postings, DSPy in 1 company and 7 postings, and LangGraph and CrewAI in 1 company and 3 postings each. These are counts of terms appearing in the body text; no distinction was made between requirements and preferred qualifications.

Evaluation infrastructure in particular is placed at the core of the validation phase, "defining evaluation metrics together with the customer," even in how OpenAI described FDE engagements in an interview with The Pragmatic Engineer (August 2025). OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting as of October 2026 also states that results are measured by production usage, business impact, and feedback based on evals. That is because the technology to bridge the gap between what works in a demo and what is reliable in production is precisely the value FDEs deliver.

Even without experience putting agents into production, there are paths to apply. Anthropic's US Staff Forward Deployed Engineer (senior role) posting published on October 14, 2026 states that production agent experience is not required, but asks for extensive experience building and operating production systems and hands-on experience with LLMs and agents, counting personal projects and prototypes as experience. It also cites the judgment to use AI coding tools daily and to review and steer their output.

③ Customer-facing and problem-discovery skills

This is the layer that takes the longest to acquire and is the one scrutinized in selection. Specifically, it involves abilities such as the following.

  • Problem discovery: identifying the genuinely valuable problem behind what the customer articulates (at Palantir, this work is weighty enough to warrant a dedicated role, the Deployment Strategist)
  • Tolerance for ambiguity: being able to define one's own scope and move forward from an undefined state of requirements
  • Stakeholder communication: balancing value explanations to executives with collaboration with field engineers and business owners
  • Scope management: an approach of delivering a "minimal end-to-end solution" first, building trust, and then expanding

Do FDEs need certifications? What Tokyo-based job postings say

There is no mandatory certification exam to become an FDE. As of October 16, 2026, reviewing FDE job postings for Tokyo-based roles at three companies, the core of the application requirements was practical experience and language ability, not certifications. Some postings do mention certifications, but their content differs by company.

Where certifications appeared as requirements, it was either cases like Databricks, which asks for its own platform's certification, or cases like Palantir's government-facing role, which asks for eligibility to handle classified information (security clearance). In both cases, these are not certifications common to FDEs in general, but requirements specific to that company and role.

The same trend holds even including overseas postings. Of the same 517 postings as in the note above, 5 companies and 86 postings listed certifications among application requirements or preferred qualifications (including those requiring acquisition within 6 months of joining), and 79 of those were Databricks' own certification (Databricks Certification). The rest included MongoDB's own certification (3 postings), Cloudflare's CISSP, CCIE, and cloud certifications (2 postings), and security certifications in Palantir's US government postings (1 posting) (security clearance requirements are not included in these counts).

Cloud certifications such as AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure serve as supplementary proof that you can handle customers' cloud environments. However, what employers' job postings emphasize most is experience delivering projects all the way to production. If you are going to spend time studying, building an LLM-powered application to production-level quality and being able to talk through that process will be a stronger asset in selection processes than preparing for certifications.

In October 2026, provider-run training and certification programs for FDEs also launched. AWS published a training path for engineers at partner companies that offers a two-tier credential, "FDE Validated" and "FDE Advanced," based on hands-on verification in an AWS-provided environment (holding a valid AWS certification is a prerequisite), and Anthropic announced the "Claude Frontier Academy" to train Frontier Deployed Engineers (also abbreviated FDE). Anthropic's program is nomination-based from organizations. Both are mechanisms that certify hands-on skills on the vendor's own products and environments; none of the 517 job postings above mentioned these credentials.

How to build the skills: A roadmap from your current role

The three layers of FDE skills can be built deliberately at your current job before changing careers. Typical development paths by background are as follows.

現職別・FDEに向けたスキルの伸ばし方
Current roleExisting strengthsAreas to focus on
Web software engineerImplementation skills and production operationsOpportunities for direct interaction with customers (joining requirements-gathering sessions, pre-sales support), building LLM applications for production
SIer / contracted development engineerClient relations and development under constraintsModern development stacks, LLM and agent development, experience building evaluation platforms
IT consultantProblem discovery and consensus buildingExperience writing production-quality code with your own hands (personal projects or side work are fine)
Pre-sales / customer engineerTechnical explanations and demo buildingExperience carrying projects beyond PoC all the way to production deployment, data engineering
  • 01 Build one LLM-powered application to production-level quality (with authentication, logging, and evaluation)
  • 02 Implement an evaluation suite (Evals) for that application so you can quantitatively assess the impact of model and prompt changes
  • 03 Deliberately increase opportunities to talk directly with customers and users in your current role, and gain experience converting "requests → problems → implementation scope"
  • 04 Review your work history so you can describe results in terms of "how the customer's business metrics moved" rather than "technology"

Summary

What an FDE needs is three layers: (1) production-quality engineering skills, (2) practical knowledge of LLM and agent development and evaluation platforms, and (3) the ability to discover customer problems and build consensus. You don't need to have all of them perfectly in place before taking on the challenge; job postings, alongside years of experience, look for "experience delivering end to end." If you want to understand where you currently stand and what gaps you need to fill, we recommend taking stock through an FDE-specialized career consultation.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

QWhat programming languages do I need to become an FDE?

Rather than a specific language, "the ability to build things full-stack" is what matters. As of October 2026, Anthropic, OpenAI, Palantir, and Databricks all cite Python and languages such as TypeScript/JavaScript as examples in their job postings. Some postings list cloud experience as a requirement: Databricks' Tokyo FDE role asks for practical knowledge of at least two of AWS, Azure, and GCP, while SoftBank's FDE role asks for experience building systems in cloud environments.

QDo I need machine learning research experience?

In principle, no. What FDEs are asked to do is not to build models but to integrate existing foundation models into customers' production systems and operate them. Skills on the "applied" side, such as prompt design, agent development, and evaluation platforms, are what get tested.

QHow much English proficiency is required?

It varies by posting. As of October 2026, OpenAI's Tokyo FDE role requires fluency in both Japanese and English (resumes in English, interviews in both languages), Palantir's Tokyo government-focused FDSE role requires fluency in both English and Japanese, and Databricks' Tokyo FDE role asks for fluent Japanese and business-level English. Domestic SoftBank (secondment to SB OAI Japan) and JAPAN AI (Genie) list English only as a preferred qualification. Reading comprehension for English documentation is useful in any workplace.

QI have no experience with evaluation platforms (Evals). How can I gain some?

Even a personally developed LLM application provides plenty of experience. Try implementing a test case collection, automated evaluation with LLM-as-a-judge, and regression testing before and after prompt changes. Anthropic's FDE posting cites production LLM experience including evaluation frameworks, and SoftBank's FDE posting lists experience building evaluation platforms (as a preferred qualification) (as of October 2026), giving you concrete material to discuss in selection processes.

QDo I need certifications to become an FDE?

There are no required certification exams. As of October 2026, OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting does not mention certifications, while Databricks' Tokyo FDE posting lists its own certification (Databricks Certification) as one of its requirements. Even among the 517 FDE postings including overseas ones (compiled by the editorial team on October 16, 2026), most that mentioned certifications were Databricks' own certification. Cloud certifications serve as supplementary proof, but in selection processes, a track record of building an LLM-powered application to production quality is a stronger asset.
