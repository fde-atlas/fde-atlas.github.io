Why Are FDE Needs Surging? An In-Depth Analysis of Job Openings at 73 Companies in Japan

Original title: FDEニーズはなぜ急増している？国内73社の求人を徹底分析

AI Ops Manager · 2026-09-21 · 24 min read

Summary

Based on a September 2026 survey of 73 Japanese companies posting FDE jobs on their own career pages, this article analyzes why demand for Forward Deployed Engineers is surging. US FDE postings grew from about 200 in 2024 to over 5,200 in the first seven months of 2026 (Lightcast data), while Japanese announcements of FDE organizations and services clustered after April 2026. The author attributes the trend to AI vendors taking responsibility for making their products actually used in customer operations. Most posters are domestic AI/software companies (40), followed by SI/consulting firms (16). Jobs split into two types: deploying the vendor's own product, and product-agnostic AI implementation driven by customer needs. Among 50 companies listing salaries, median lower bound was ¥7.5M and upper bound ¥15M. The article also cautions that some new FDE roles are renamed existing work, and that job titles alone cannot distinguish FDEs from resident SES engineers.

Key points

  • 73 Japanese companies posted FDE jobs as of September 2026; 40 were domestic AI/software firms.
  • US FDE postings rose from ~200 in 2024 to 5,200+ in early 2026 (Lightcast).
  • FDE roles split into product-deployment type and product-agnostic, customer-driven type.
  • Median salary range across 50 firms: ¥7.5M lower bound, ¥15M upper bound.
  • Some FDE roles are renamed existing work; titles alone don't distinguish FDEs from SES engineers.

Behind the growing number of job openings for FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) is the fact that companies providing AI have begun taking responsibility not just for deployment, but all the way to the point where AI is actually used in customers' business operations. Based on job postings from 73 companies recruiting FDEs in Japan, this article explains the hiring companies, the roles they seek, and the realities of salaries.

Published date Last updated

Many people have probably started seeing the term FDE frequently over the past year. In the US, FDE job postings have grown by an order of magnitude since 2024, and in Japan as well, entering 2026, announcements of FDE organizations, positions, and services have followed one after another. Tracing the sources of these job postings and each company's explanations reveals that, behind this trend, AI sellers have moved toward taking on themselves the task of getting AI into a state where it is actually used in customers' business operations. When job postings from 73 companies recruiting FDEs in Japan were tallied as of September 2026, the most common posters were domestic AI and software companies (40 companies). Comparing the postings shows that FDEs fall into two types: those who deploy their own company's products to customers, and those who do not assume their own products and instead advance AI adoption in line with customer challenges, and the roles companies seek also differ.

The job postings of the 73 companies recruiting FDEs in Japan were each verified one by one on their respective recruiting pages. The hiring backgrounds written in the postings were also read to capture why each company has established this role. Some figures cited as evidence of FDE demand have spread with meanings altered from their original sources, so cited figures were traced back to their origins.

What Is an FDE?

In a previous article, "What Is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Salaries, Skills, Careers, and How Companies Secure Them", we explained in detail the job content, salaries, and career paths. To reconfirm the definition: an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who, while belonging to a company that has its own products, embeds themselves in the front lines of customer companies' operations and, alone or with a small team, handles everything from identifying challenges to implementing systems and establishing operations. This way of working was established by US-based Palantir and has become established as an independent job title alongside the spread of AI adoption.

However, in Japanese job postings, FDE is used in a broader sense than this original definition. Some companies do not have their own products, and some recruit FDEs for their own business divisions rather than external customers. In the previous article, this expansion was organized in the chapter "The Scope of FDE in Japan." Reading actual job postings makes the substance of this expansion more concrete.

How FDE Job Postings Are Growing

In the US, FDE job postings have grown by an order of magnitude in count since 2024, and in Japan, announcements of FDE organizations, positions, and services have been concentrated since April 2026. Since Japan has no statistics tracking trends in the number of FDE job postings, the expansion is read from the timing of each company's announcements.

US FDE job postings grew from about 200 in 2024 to over 5,200 in just 7 months of 2026

According to Lightcast, which handles labor market data, US FDE job postings grew from about 200 in 2024 to about 1,200 in 2025*1. In 2026, postings exceeded 5,200 in January through July alone, more than four times the full-year total for 2025.

Even so, Lightcast itself notes that demand is still small compared with existing technical occupations*1. The growth rate appears large partly because the starting number was small.

Japanese explanatory articles have also cited the figure that FDE job postings grew from 643 to 5,330 (a 729% increase year over year). This originally came from US-based Business Insider reporting on Indeed data, and what the magazine actually reported was growth compared to January 2025 levels (543% and 5,230%). The figures 643 and 5,330 are values converted from these growth rates into an index with the baseline set at 100, not counts of job postings. The 729% figure is the growth of this index from April 2025 to April 2026. The magazine appended a correction to the article noting that it had mistakenly written the index values as counts of job postings*2.

This is an example of talent development being announced at scale, following the establishment of new organizations and services.

Survey results on FDE job openings

Checking companies' own recruiting pages on September 20–21, 2026, 73 companies were posting FDE job openings in Japan, with the most frequent posters being domestic AI and software companies at 40. Next were SI, consulting, and contract development firms at 16, followed by 10 Japanese offices of overseas AI and software companies, and 7 postings aimed at in-house or group-internal work. Job titles vary

Even among postings recruiting FDEs, the way job titles are written is not uniform. The notations we confirmed include the following.

  • Notation omitting the "do": フォワードデプロイエンジニア (Forward Deploy Engineer)
  • Notation indicating internal roles: Forward Deployed Engineer (Internal), in-house FDE
  • Notation appending FDE to a Japanese job title: AI Solution Engineer (FDE/Forward Deployed Engineer)
  • Palantir's notation: Forward Deployed Software Engineer

Conversely, some companies recruit for similar work without using the name FDE. Within the scope of this survey, 10 major operating companies, including Mizuho Financial Group, Dai-ichi Life, Fast Retailing, and Tokyo Gas, were recruiting for roles that implement AI in their own operations under different names*17. Table 2 counts only companies recruiting under the name FDE, so if similar work is included, the number of companies recruiting is even larger.

Types of FDEs and the roles companies expect of each

Even under the same job title of FDE, comparing job postings reveals that the work involved differs considerably. The dividing line is whether the role assumes the company's own products. Categorizing by job descriptions in the postings, there are two types: FDEs who deploy the company's own products to customers, and FDEs who do not assume the company's own products and instead advance AI adoption in line with customer challenges. These two categories are a different cut from the five types introduced in a previous article.

FDEs who deploy the company's own products to customers

This work involves deploying the company's own AI products or cloud-delivered software to customer sites and bridging the gap between the product's standard features and business operations that differ by customer. In terms of the posters in Table 2, domestic and overseas AI and software companies are posting job openings of this type.

CADDi, which provides data platforms for the manufacturing industry, writes in its job posting: "Cases requiring deep integration with existing systems or advanced, customer-specific business requirements that cannot be solved by standard features alone are rapidly increasing"*18. ShareDine, which provides systems for recruiting, labor management, shift scheduling, and other operations for the food service industry, likewise states: "Business realities differ greatly from client to client, and essential problems cannot be solved by uniform feature provision alone"*19.

Some companies also require feeding the insights gained back into the product after solving customer problems. RevComm writes that it abstracts the insights gained from customer-specific responses and "works with PdM and development organizations to feed them back into product evolution" (PdM refers to product managers, who set product direction)*20.

FDEs who do not assume the company's own products and advance AI adoption in line with customer challenges

Starting from the customer's challenges, they select and use existing products where sufficient, and develop from scratch when nothing fits. In terms of the sellers in Table 2, SI, consulting, and contract development companies, as well as companies recruiting for their own or their group's business needs, post job openings of this type.

Boost Consulting writes, "We do not limit ourselves to a specific technology stack in order to select the optimal technology for each project"*21. ACES writes, "We do not simply build systems based on defined requirements like typical contract development," and states that they take on work from requirements definition, technology selection, and design*22.

There is also a movement of companies that select products partnering with software companies. Accenture announced FDE organizations and programs with Microsoft*6, ServiceNow*23, Google Cloud*24, and SAP*25, respectively. Salesforce Japan fully launched its FDE partner program in Japan in June 2026, listing nine initial partners including Fujitsu, NTT Data, and PwC Consulting*9. Meanwhile, SHIFT launched an FDE service that emphasizes selecting and implementing products that fit the customer without being tied to specific products*5.

The destination is not necessarily external customers. Money Forward is recruiting FDEs to join its own business divisions under the job title "Forward Deployed Engineer (Internal)"*26, and STRACT is recruiting them under the title "Internal FDE"*27. PayPay Card, KINTO Technologies, and Medley are also recruiting FDEs to implement AI into their own or their group's business operations.

STRACT writes in its hiring background that as the business grew, the volume and complexity of work increased proportionally, and until now this had been compensated by the ingenuity and effort of existing members. To grow further, it says that having engineers embed themselves in frontline business processes is essential, and it created this role for that reason*27. PayPay Card explains, "The operational challenges of each department are highly individual, and simply providing AI tools does not lead to sufficient adoption and establishment"*28. When a business company places an FDE in its own operations, it takes this form.

There are also roles that work with FDEs to decide what to build

FDEs do not necessarily work alone. In the job postings of Palantir and LayerX, alongside FDEs, they are recruiting for a separate role called Deployment Strategist (responsible for defining problems), who listens to and organizes issues at customer sites*29*30. LayerX's posting states that this role "pairs up" with FDEs to design AI agents*30. Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu also highlights collaboration between this role and FDEs in its announcement*7. Counting only FDE job postings would miss roles that cover part of the same work.

Is it just a rebranding of on-site resident SEs?

From the scope of work described in the job postings, some differences from SES (on-site resident contracts paid for hours worked) can be read. Of the 50 companies whose hiring backgrounds could be read in the fixed-point survey in the previous chapter, nearly half—23 companies—stated in their hiring backgrounds or job descriptions that they would take responsibility not just for trial implementation but through production operation and establishment into business operations. Of these, 21 are postings for working within external customers' operations. Nearly half of the postings place "making it actually used in business operations," rather than providing manpower, within the scope of work.

Both FDEs and engineers working under SES work at customer sites, so they look similar from the outside. In Japan, an article titled "Is 'FDE' Ultimately Just a Rebranding of On-Site Resident SEs?" has been published*31. The same article reports that Accenture practices contracts where compensation is based on predetermined outcomes rather than hours worked*31.

That said, job titles alone do not distinguish them. In January 2026, Intersect was recruiting FDEs with "SES experience welcome" in the title (posting ended in September). The body made no mention of SES, and the work involved taking responsibility for establishing the company's AI products in customers' operations*32. The title's "SES experience welcome" can be read as referring to applicants' career backgrounds. The differences between roles are compared in the "Differences from Other Roles" chapter of "What Is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Salary, Skills, Career, and How Companies Secure Them".

Why demand for FDEs is surging

As seen in Table 2, most companies recruiting FDEs in Japan are on the selling side of software and AI implementation services. Overseas as well, companies posting many FDE jobs are on the selling side. According to a tally by Plank, which handles the dispatch of FDE talent, companies with the most active postings include sellers of AI and data platforms such as Databricks, Palantir, Salesforce, and OpenAI (565 companies and 1,333 postings as of September 12, 2026, including locations outside the US)*33.

Both the growth of US job postings and the concentration of announcements in Japan can be read as a movement of sellers taking on themselves the responsibility of making AI actually used in customers' business operations.

Many postings seek roles that do not end with handing over products or technology

Of the 50 companies whose hiring backgrounds could be read in the fixed-point survey, 31 stated in their hiring backgrounds or job descriptions either that simply handing over products or technology is insufficient, or that custom development for each customer is necessary (22 companies), or that they would take responsibility not just for trial implementation but through production and establishment (23 companies). Fourteen companies fall into both categories. Both are the companies' own explanations in their postings.

Of the 50 companies, 11 also stated that they would feed back what is learned in the field into their own products or assets. At these companies, FDEs also serve as a pathway for improving products.

However, these are figures showing what each company seeks from FDEs, and do not directly indicate the cause of the increase in job postings.

Sellers began employing implementation specialists in-house

The movement had already begun in 2025. US FDE job postings grew to about six times the previous year's level in 2025*1, and in Japan, LayerX began recruiting FDEs in July*3. In December, AI Shift announced in its tech blog that it had established an FDE role. The company explains that its Business Tech role (established in 2024, covering both business and technology and interfacing with customers) became insufficient in technical depth as customers increased and projects became more complex, so it established FDE as a role with higher requirements*4.

In 2026, AI developers and cloud providers have begun building FDE organizations. In May, OpenAI launched a dedicated company to send FDEs to enterprises with an initial investment of over $4 billion *34. In June, AWS announced a plan to invest $1 billion in an FDE organization and deploy thousands of specialists into customer teams *35. OpenAI's aims are covered in the chapter "Why FDEs Are Needed Now" of "What Is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Salary, Skills, Career, and How Companies Can Secure Them".

The way of selling is also changing. In its announcement, AWS said engagements would be structured around goals and business outcomes shared with customers, rather than billable working hours *35. Sierra, a US company that provides AI agents, explained at the end of 2024 that outcome-based payment has become possible with AI agents, and its own business follows this model. However, it also notes that this is not always the optimal form *36. If compensation is tied to outcomes, the seller must take responsibility all the way to the point where the AI is actually used in the customer's operations.

Some companies write that their staff cannot keep up with the increase in implementation projects

Of the 50 companies in the periodic survey, 6 wrote that their staff cannot keep up with the growth in implementations and projects. LayerX wrote that "the number of implementation projects is increasing rapidly" *37, and Stockmark stated that "projects, including moving from PoC to production deployment, advancing RAG, and building AI agents, are surging" *38. A PoC is a trial implementation, and RAG is a mechanism that searches internal documents and other sources for use in AI responses.

Although neither company provides figures or timeframes, it can be read that at these companies, projects are growing faster than the people available to handle them.

Existing work was given the name FDE

On the other hand, at least some of the newly created FDE positions and job postings in Japan are cases where existing work, previously performed without a formal job title, was simply given the FDE name. Preferred Networks wrote in a job posting that "projects that go beyond PoCs and embed AI agents into the core of business operations are rapidly increasing." It went on to explain that the role of implementing generative AI on the customer's front lines was something "PFN engineers had implicitly taken on until now," and that this has now been explicitly defined as the FDE role *39.

Attention is also needed on where the people come from. On its news site, US-based Salesforce wrote that it built an FDE team from three internal teams and tripled it in six months. The company also wrote that 40-50% of placements into FDE roles are internal transfers, so the growth of its FDEs is not entirely due to new hiring *40.

Work that merely received the FDE name is not new demand. Even so, the fact that previously untitled work has been carved out as a distinct job and become the name of job postings and organizations is a change of the past one to two years. Research firm Gartner also cautioned in September 2026 about the trend of selling consulting services under the FDE name *41.

How to identify the FDEs your company needs

Where you source an FDE from is determined by the business problem you want to solve and what you want to retain in-house after the engagement ends. Table 4 summarizes what remains after the engagement and the situations each sourcing method suits.

How to source an FDEAfter the engagement endsSuitable situations
Bring in an FDE from a product vendorThe product remains; the FDE leavesThe product to adopt and the business problem it will solve are already decided
Engage an FDE from an SI or consulting firmThe built system remains; the FDE leavesYou want to delegate everything from product selection, or there are problems the product alone cannot solve
Hire an FDE in-houseThe people remain in-houseYou want to continuously rebuild operations with AI as the premise

Of the three options, only hiring an FDE in-house keeps the people themselves in the company after the engagement ends. After an externally sourced FDE moves on to the next engagement, the role of monitoring how the system is used and continuing to improve it falls to someone inside the company. Approaches to defining the scope of an external FDE's responsibilities are organized in the chapter "How Companies Can Secure FDEs" of "What Is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Salary, Skills, Career, and How Companies Can Secure Them". Contract forms and preparation before engaging an FDE from an SI or consulting firm are covered in the separate article "What to Know Before Outsourcing FDE Work | Decision Criteria and Preparation".

Looking at the surveys, while many companies use AI, more companies than in the US are not tackling business transformation as an organization, and AI's effects center on improving operational efficiency. In a survey by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications of managers and above at companies with 10 or more employees working on digitalization, 86.4% answered that they use generative AI in at least one business process *42. On the other hand, 27.0% answered that there are "no organizational initiatives" for business transformation through generative AI, far exceeding the 1.4% in the US *43.

IPA's (Information-technology Promotion Agency) survey shows a bias in the nature of the effects. Of the 853 companies that had already adopted AI or were trialing it and reported effects above a certain level, 91.6% cited improved efficiency or speed of operations as the effect. Only 3.9% cited increased sales or profits *44. This is as far as the survey goes, but trying to reach the sales and profit gains that lie beyond efficiency gains likely requires rethinking how operations are structured. That work tends to go more smoothly when there is someone embedded in the operations who monitors usage and keeps making improvements.

Whether recruiting an FDE in-house or bringing one in from outside, clarifying the following three points in the job description or the scope of the engagement makes it easier to avoid mismatching the role you seek.

  • What to use: whether to assume the adoption of a specific product, or to select products based on the problem and develop one if none exists
  • Whether to delegate problem definition: whether they enter from the stage of deciding what to build, or build to fixed requirements
  • How far implementation responsibility extends: whether they are responsible up to production operation and embedding into business operations

In Japan, there were 7 companies posting FDE job openings for their own or their group's business operations. Even within the scope of this survey, 10 major operating companies were recruiting for similar work, albeit under different names. Before deciding whether to secure AI talent through hiring, training, or outsourcing, the first thing to decide is whether to establish a role that works embedded in business operations and takes responsibility through implementation and adoption. Hiring FDEs in-house is one way of establishing such a role. The approach of preparing talent internally is organized, together with how to balance hiring, training, and external resources, in "A Complete Guide to FDE Development: How to Balance Hiring and External Resources, and How to Proceed with Development".

Summary

Behind the rapid surge in FDE job openings in the US and the string of announcements in Japan is the fact that AI vendors have shifted toward taking responsibility all the way to the point where AI is actually used in customers' business operations. Looking at the job openings from 73 domestic companies, FDEs fall into two types: FDEs who deploy their own company's products to customers, and FDEs who advance AI adoption tailored to customer challenges without assuming their own company's products, and the roles companies seek also differed.

The first thing your company can do is to choose one task where AI has been introduced but is not being used in actual operations. Once that task is decided, you can then choose which of the approaches in Table 4 to use for establishing a role that works hands-on to rebuild the process.

The FDE we often hear about lately is someone who does not simply hand over AI and walk away, but works inside the customer's business operations and takes responsibility through to actual usage. Advancing AI adoption in your own company likewise starts not with outsourcing and waiting, but with deciding where to place a role that works embedded alongside the business.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. How many companies are recruiting FDEs in Japan?

Companies posting job openings with FDE in the job title numbered 73, based on what could be confirmed on each company's recruitment pages (as of September 20-21, 2026). This does not include companies recruiting for similar work without using the name FDE.

Q. Is it true that "FDE job postings increased 729% year over year"?

The 729% figure is the growth in Indeed's job posting index (with January 2025 as the baseline) from April 2025 to April 2026, which Indeed provided to US Business Insider. The geographic scope of the index cannot be determined from the Business Insider article. The "643 postings → 5,330 postings" cited in Japanese articles is a value converted from the growth versus January 2025 (543% and 5,230%) stated in the same article, and does not represent the number of job postings. Business Insider has issued a correction to the article *2.

Q. Why have FDE job openings increased recently?

The background is that AI vendors have shifted toward taking on themselves the work of getting AI to the point where it is actually used in customers' business operations. In the US, the number of job postings grew to roughly six times the previous year's level in 2025 *1, and in Japan, announcements of FDE organizations and roles followed one after another from April 2026 onward. Some of the newly created roles announced in Japan are existing jobs that were given the FDE name.

Q. Do operating companies also hire FDEs?

Of the 73 companies, 7 were posting FDE job openings for their own or their group's business operations (including software companies). Among major operating companies, 10 were recruiting for similar work without using the name FDE, within the scope of this survey.

Q. What is the salary of an FDE?

Among the 50 companies that listed salaries in yen, the median lower bound of the 49 companies that listed a lower bound was 7.5 million yen, and the median upper bound of the 46 companies that listed an upper bound was 15 million yen (as of September 2026). Salary benchmarks and the reasons they tend to be high are organized in "What Is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? Salary, Skills, Career, and How Companies Secure Them".
