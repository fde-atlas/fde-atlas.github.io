What's the difference between an FDE and implementation, consulting, and pre-sales roles?

Original title: FDE和实施、咨询、售前有什么不同

圣路可 Fde · 2026-10-17 · 2 min read

Summary

This article contrasts pre-sales, consulting, implementation, and forward deployed engineers. Pre-sales closes deals, consulting sets direction, and implementation delivers features from requirement documents. FDEs, by contrast, own business outcomes: they start from on-site observation, often redefining the requirement itself, then codify rules, verify with samples, launch, and keep improving. Acceptance is judged by real usage and measurable impact, not feature delivery. An after-sales ticket case study shows how an FDE breaks a vague request into seven tasks, deciding what AI handles and what humans must confirm.

Key points

  • Pre-sales, consulting, and implementation each own only one stage; FDEs own business results.
  • FDEs start from field observation and often redefine the requirement itself.
  • Deliverables include rule libraries, sample verification records, permission schemes, and effect data.
  • Acceptance means real adoption and measurable impact, not just go-live.
  • FDEs hand over rules and methods so enterprises can continue improving after they leave.

Sales pre-sales closes deals, consulting determines direction, implementation delivers features according to requirement documents; an FDE is accountable for business outcomes: first judging whether the requirement itself is right, then writing the rules clearly, verifying with samples, going live, and continuously improving.

A one-table comparison

RolePrimarily accountable forTypical deliverables
Sales consultantHelping the client understand the solution and closing the dealDemos, proposals
Management consultingDetermining direction and prioritiesDiagnostic reports, roadmaps
Implementation engineerDelivering and launching features according to requirement documentsConfigurations, integrations, acceptance forms
Forward Deployed EngineerBusiness outcomes: first judging whether the requirement itself is right, then getting it running, verified, live, and continuously improvedWorking digital employees, rule libraries, verification records, permission schemes, performance data

Four concrete differences

  • Different starting point: Implementation starts from the requirement document; an FDE starts from on-site observation, often discovering that the requirement itself needs to be redefined.
  • Different deliverables: Implementation delivers configurations and acceptance forms; an FDE also delivers rule libraries, sample verification records, and permission schemes.
  • Different acceptance criteria: Implementation checks whether features are launched; an FDE checks whether they are actually used in the business and whether the results can be measured.
  • Different way of leaving: An FDE must hand over the rules and methods to people inside the enterprise, so the enterprise can continue improving after they leave.

An example

An after-sales supervisor proposes: "After a customer submits an issue, have the digital employee automatically decide whether it's a return, exchange, or repair." An implementation engineer would configure based on this sentence. An FDE would first shadow a veteran agent through tickets, pressing "it depends" into specific conditions, then break the requirement into seven small tasks, judging item by item which go to AI and which must be confirmed by a human. The first version only handles information organization, document retrieval, and reply drafts; refunds, compensation, and formal commitments are left to people with the proper authority. Frequently asked questions

Can an implementation consultant transition to FDE?

Yes, implementation experience is a great foundation. What needs to be added are skills in on-site observation, probing for rules, sample verification, and outcome accounting. The course trains methods and deliverable materials; it does not promise jobs, projects, or income.

Do consulting firms' AI projects count as FDE?

It depends on whether they go on-site and whether they are accountable for implementation results. Delivering only diagnostic reports and roadmaps is consulting; getting scenarios running, verified, and handed over to the enterprise is FDE work.
