Module 11: Behavioral mastery for FDEs

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 2 min read

Summary

This FDE interview-prep lesson turns Palantir's public hiring page into a concrete preparation list. Palantir says it asks about executing, challenging yourself, motivating others, making judgments, and failing; the article interprets what each topic must prove in customer-facing work and the likely follow-up probe (e.g., "What would have changed your mind?"). It cites Palantir's early-talent timeline and a December 2025 Reddit report from a Government-track candidate saying decomposition and learning rounds split into 15 behavioral and 45 technical minutes. A fictional engineer, Maya, illustrates building a story bank, with one-minute versions prepared alongside answers to the probes. Short written application prompts are covered separately.

Key points

  • Palantir's hiring page lists five behavioral topics; prepare a story for each.
  • A Reddit candidate report describes 15 behavioral plus 45 technical minutes per round.
  • Each story should anticipate a specific follow-up probe.
  • Practice one-minute versions of each story.
  • Fictional engineer Maya demonstrates the story-mapping exercise.

Full text

Palantir’s Getting Hired page, written for its candidates in general and not for forward deployed software engineers alone, says: “We’ll be asking you questions about how you’ve executed, challenged yourself, motivated others, and made judgments—as well as where you’ve failed in the past.” Source 1Getting Hired - Palantir CareersPublisherPalantir TechnologiesSource typecompany hiring page That sentence is a list of stories you need before the onsite. This lesson builds that list for each of your targets and maps it to stories you have; the evidence map covers how much weight each candidate report can carry.

Through this module you follow Maya, an integrations engineer who became the deployed engineer at a company that sells scheduling and payroll software to restaurant groups. She also shipped an assistant that answers managers’ scheduling-rule questions from a customer’s own handbook. Maya is fictional, and so are her employer and its customers.

What Palantir publishes

The page doesn’t say which interview asks which topic, so have a story for each, and be ready to tell one in the same hour as a technical problem. Palantir’s early-talent timeline describes its virtual onsite’s learning and decomposition interviews as “consisting of behavioral and technical questions.” Source 2Students | Palantir Careers (timeline image: RecruitingTimeline_V3.jpg)PublisherPalantirSource typecompany hiring page That graphic is for early-career hires, but one Government-track candidate wrote on Reddit, in December 2025, that their decomposition and learning rounds each split into 15 behavioral minutes and 45 technical ones. Source 3Palantir FDSE Full Interview Loop Process (post by u/Not_the_Sauron)PublisherReddit r/csMajorsSource typecandidate report on Reddit

Read each topic for what it must prove in customer work, and for the question that follows your answer. These readings and probes are ours, not Palantir’s.

  • Executed. A result at a customer, in their systems, under constraints you didn’t set. Expect: “What would have happened if you’d left halfway?” Practice: a project you owned end to end.
  • Challenged yourself. Something you couldn’t do at the start and could at the end. Expect: “What did you get wrong first?” Practice: outside your comfort zone.
  • Motivated others. People who didn’t report to you, often at the customer: their IT team, their store managers. Expect: “What was in it for them?” Practice: a group that didn’t report to you.
  • Made judgments. A call made before the facts were in: what you knew, and what you didn’t. Expect: “What would have changed your mind?” Practice: a decision with too little data.
  • Failed. Your mistake, what it cost the customer, and what you do differently now. Expect: “Who found it?” Practice: a failure that was your fault.

Prepare the probe’s answer with the story. Answering in about a minute builds the short version and its follow-ups.

The short written prompts on Palantir’s New Grad FDSE application, such as a time you changed your mind, are a different exercise, covered in the bridge story.
