Summary

This article from FDE総研 teaches job seekers without FDE experience how to self-assess whether they should apply to FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) postings. It proposes reading job listings in three layers—business content, must-have requirements, and nice-to-have requirements—starting with the verbs in the job description to gauge the mix of customer-facing work and hands-on implementation. It distinguishes three types of "no experience" (role, industry, implementation) and notes employers usually tolerate only one or two. Must-have items should be split into entry conditions versus ideal conditions; nice-to-haves into learnable versus not. Years-of-experience figures are a density gauge, not a hard cutoff, and depend on whether the stated role is broad (engineer) or FDE-specific. The piece ends with a five-step application decision process and resume-writing guidance.

Key points

  • Read postings in three layers: duties, must-haves, nice-to-haves.
  • Three types of inexperience exist; employers tolerate only one or two.
  • Split must-haves into entry conditions versus ideal conditions.
  • Experience years are a gauge, not a hard cutoff; check the stated role.
  • A five-step process and resume tips guide the final application decision.

Can beginners decipher the requirements of FDE job postings?

FDE Soken believes that even beginners can judge for themselves whether to apply if they divide job requirements into three layers: "must-have," "preferred," and "job description."

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer, a role that embeds at customer sites and handles everything from problem structuring to implementation and operational adoption) job postings tend to have more inconsistent wording than SWE or IT consulting postings. This is partly because Palantir popularized the FDE-type role, and even with the same title, the scope of responsibilities differs from company to company. Palantir's hiring information can be found on the official careers page, and making a habit of consulting primary sources reduces misreading.

The definition of "beginner" differs from posting to posting

According to FDE Soken's classification, "beginner" in a job posting falls into three types: "no FDE experience," "no industry experience," and "no implementation experience."

From the perspective of a practitioner at VACAN, in most cases the hiring side tolerates only one or two of the three. For example, in a posting aimed at people with engineering experience but new to the FDE role, someone with no implementation experience who applies as-is risks falling outside the intended audience at the document screening stage. The starting point is to identify which kind of beginner you are before reading the posting.

Type of inexperienceTypical situationWhat the hiring side tends to care about
No FDE experienceTransitioning from SWE or a system integratorWhether you have experience in customer dialogue
No industry experienceNo field knowledge in manufacturing, finance, public sector, etc.Whether you can demonstrate speed of learning
No implementation experienceBackground in consulting or PdMWhether you can write data processing or API integrations yourself

Read the job description from the "duties" section before the "requirements"

FDE Soken recommends reading the job duties section before the requirements section to grasp the ratio of customer interaction to implementation.

The requirements section is often filled in by HR with boilerplate text, while the actual work shows up in the verbs of the duties section. Count how many of the verbs "define requirements," "design," "implement," and "drive adoption" you have actually experienced. Practitioners tend to think that if you can claim more than half, the posting is worth considering even if some must-have requirements are unmet.

How do must-have and preferred labels determine whether beginners can apply?

FDE Soken's basic rule is: if multiple must-have requirements are unmet, hold off; if only one is unmet, apply while showing material that compensates for it.

Must-have and nice-to-have are, for the hiring side, the cutoff line and the bonus line respectively. However, practices vary by company, and some companies write their ideal profile into the must-have section. Don't give up based on the labels alone; you need an attitude of reading the underlying tendencies.

Read must-have requirements by dividing them into "entry conditions" and "ideal conditions"

FDE Soken recommends sorting the items in the must-have section into entry conditions, without which the work cannot function, and ideal conditions, which are desirable to have.

For example, "able to conduct business negotiations with customers in Japanese" is an entry condition. On the other hand, "experience building large-scale data platforms" may be an ideal condition at a company that starts with small projects. The guideline for sorting is whether the duties section contains a corresponding verb. If there is no corresponding verb, you can treat it as an ideal condition.

View preferred requirements by dividing them into "compensable" and "non-compensable"

From the perspective of a practitioner at VACAN, it is realistic to divide preferred requirements into those that can be made up within about six months of joining and those where the track record itself is required.

Example of preferred requirementEase of compensatingHow to handle it when applying
Experience using a specific BI toolEasy to compensateAttach records of self-study and personal verification
Certification for a specific cloud platformEasy to compensateClearly state your plan to obtain it
Customer negotiation experience in the industryHard to compensateExplain similar experience with concrete examples
Customer support in EnglishHard to compensateHonestly describe situations where you used it in practice

For preferred requirements you don't meet, it is considered more resilient in interviews to write your "current level" and "plan to close the gap" in your resume openly rather than hiding them.

How much do stated years of experience affect beginners?

FDE Soken recommends reading statements like "3+ years" not as a reason to give up on applying, but as a gauge of the density of experience being sought.

Years of experience is an indicator HR places to streamline document screening, and it is said that actual interviews tend to focus on substance. However, since the hiring side's discretion varies with company size and hiring phase, you cannot uniformly say "years of experience can be ignored."

Check whether the years refer to "total" or "within the role"

From the perspective of a practitioner at VACAN, "3+ years of engineering experience" and "3+ years of FDE experience" are different conditions; the former can be read as leaving the door open for beginners to FDE.

Check the wording in the following order.

  • Look at whether the job title immediately before the years is a broad term like "engineer" or "development," or a restricted term like "FDE" or "Forward Deployed."
  • If it is a broad term, consider whether your experience in customer negotiation and data integration can be counted toward the years.
  • If it is a restricted term, confirm whether adjacent experience can substitute, for example in a casual interview before the formal one.

Material to compensate when your years of experience fall short

FDE Soken believes that a shortfall in years can sometimes be compensated by presenting concrete examples of embedding in the field. However, this does not guarantee success.

Easy-to-use compensating material includes: experience where you personally listened to customer or internal user problems and solved them; experience where you learned an unfamiliar domain in a short period and produced a deliverable; and experience where you solidified requirements from an ambiguous specification. Writing in the order of situation, decision, result, and limitations makes it easier for the reader to understand.

Who should apply, based on conditions like years of experience, current role, and English ability?

FDE Soken judges whether to apply based on the combination of years of experience × current role × English ability, prioritizing above all whether your current role exposes you to both implementation and customer interaction.

When conditions conflict, priorities are needed. In FDE Soken's comparison framework, weight is placed in the order of: ① implementation experience, ② direct dialogue with customers, ③ English proficiency, ④ years of experience. This ranking reflects the judgment of practitioners at FDE Soken and VACAN, and does not represent each company's selection criteria.

Guidelines by combination of years of experience × current role

Priorities when English proficiency is high but years of experience are short

FDE Soken recommends that, even with high English proficiency, those with short years of experience should not make English their main axis, but instead build up implementation experience first or choose positions that assume training.

English is listed as a preferred requirement in many job postings, and becomes essential for foreign-affiliated companies or projects with overseas customers. However, even with high English proficiency, if you lack the implementation skills to deliver results in customer settings, you are more likely to struggle after joining. Conversely, even with moderate English proficiency, if you have substantial experience in implementation and customer dialogue, you can compete well in positions focused on domestic customers.

How do FDE requirements differ from other roles, and who is a poor fit?

FDE Soken distinguishes FDE from other roles by its requirement to combine both "dialogue close to being embedded at the customer site" and "implementation you write yourself."

FDE is known as a Palantir-style role, characterized by handling everything from discovering customer problems to implementation and operational adoption. Here we organize the requirement differences that tend to appear in job postings. Because there is wide variation between companies, this is only a guideline for interpretation.

Differences in requirements between FDE and SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer

From the perspective of VACAN practitioners, FDE job postings often combine requirements from multiple roles, and the key is identifying which axis carries the most weight.

RoleRequirements commonly seen in postingsMain difference from FDE
SWELanguages, design, code reviewLower weight on customer dialogue
IT consultantProblem structuring, proposals, document creationLower weight on implementing yourself
PdMPrioritization, requirements definition, metric designOften not responsible for on-site implementation and operational adoption
SIerRequirements definition, schedule management, customer negotiationUsually no product of their own and often works on a per-project basis
FDECustomer dialogue, implementation, operational adoptionCovers all of the above elements single-handedly

Characteristics of people who are a poor fit for FDE requirements

FDE Soken views people who find dialogue with customers burdensome, or who want to quietly implement fixed specifications, as a poor match for FDE job requirements.

Characteristics commonly considered a poor fit are as follows.

  • People who struggle to proactively keep asking questions in ambiguous situations.
  • People who want their scope of responsibility clearly delineated.
  • People who want to focus exclusively on either implementation or customer dialogue.
  • People who want to avoid business trips or work at customer sites (this varies by posting).

These are differences in aptitude, not in quality. If any apply, SWE or PdM positions may suit you better.

How to decide whether to apply, and how to reflect it in your resume?

FDE Soken recommends judging whether to apply through a 5-step process of mapping the verbs in a job posting to your own experience, and reflecting the result at the top of your resume.

Making the judgment procedural rather than intuitive keeps it consistent. The procedure, incorporating the perspective of VACAN practitioners, is as follows.

5 steps for deciding whether to apply

In FDE Soken's procedure, check in the order of job description, required, preferred, years of experience, and working style; if two or more required items are unmet, consider holding off.

  • Extract the verbs from the job description section and map them to your own experience.
  • Sort the required requirements into entry conditions and ideal conditions.
  • Divide the preferred requirements into those you can fill and those you cannot.
  • Check the role names for the years-of-experience requirement, and consider whether adjacent experience can count.
  • Check working style, such as location, business trips, and frequency of English use.

This judgment is only a guideline for applying. Final selection is decided by each company's hiring process.

How to reflect this in your resume

From the perspective of VACAN practitioners, writing both customer dialogue and implementation experience in the first three lines of your resume, in the order of situation, judgment, and result, makes it easier for readers to understand.

ItemExample of how to writePoints to note
SituationWhich problem of whom you worked onAbstract it within the bounds of confidentiality
JudgmentWhat you chose and whyDo not present others' achievements as your own
ResultDescribe the changes that occurred, within the scope of factsDo not write figures without basis
LimitationsWhat was lacking and what you learned nextWriting honestly tends to earn trust in interviews

If there is a required requirement you cannot fill, do not hide it in your documents; attach a plan to fill it. FDE Soken also recommends confirming how requirements are applied in a casual interview before applying.

FDE job requirements vary greatly between companies, and the organization in this article is only a general guideline for interpretation. When applying, be sure to check each company's job posting and official recruitment page.
