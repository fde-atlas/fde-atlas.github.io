What an FDE Should Leave a Discovery Call With

Falkster.AI · 2026-11-05 · 1 min read

Summary

Drawing on Salesforce's November 5, 2026 Ask Slackbot column, this piece argues that an FDE should leave a discovery call with better questions, not firm opinions. Treat the first answer as a draft, ask "what happens next" instead of "why," bring evidence, and admit unknowns. The author recommends requesting three records per opinion, having the customer's expert confirm the right answer on the call, and running the current product against real cases before the second call. Evals should be labeled by the customer, and divergence analysis belongs on a handshake page rather than scattered Slack threads.

Key points

  • Leave discovery with better questions, not firm opinions.
  • Ask for three records to back each opinion.
  • Have the customer's expert confirm answers on the call.
  • Run the current product before the second call.
  • Label evals by the customer; analyze divergence on a handshake page.

Full text

THE SHORT ANSWER

The first rows of the eval, labeled by the customer. Salesforce's November 5, 2026 Ask Slackbot column tells an FDE to treat the first answer as a draft, ask 'what happens next' instead of 'why,' bring evidence not opinions, admit what they do not know, and leave with better questions than they brought. The last part is where deployments get found out at go-live. A firm opinion is a compressed story about a pain; the record where the pain happened is the uncompressed version. Ask for three records per opinion, have the customer's expert say on the call what the right answer was, and run the product as it stands against them before the second call. That is how Colin Jarvis's team could say 60 percent at scoping. Run the column's divergence prompt, where the first ask and the final build diverged, on the handshake page rather than on five Slack threads, because my discovery-week ranker buried a three-call theme under a 31-call one and the three were the renewal.
