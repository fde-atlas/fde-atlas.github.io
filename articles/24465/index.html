Where Will the Next Generation of PIs Come From? | Switching the Development OS in an Era When Young Researchers' Scaffolding Is Disappearing

Original title: PIの担い手はどこから来るのか｜若手の足場が消える時代の、育成OSの切り替え

AX for Revenue Institute · 2026-07-17 · 16 min read

Summary

This Japanese theory piece by Yoichi Aso asks where the people who can truly master AI will come from, given that AI is automating the entry-level tasks young employees historically used as scaffolding to build judgment. Citing McKinsey, IMD, and HBR/BCG research, the author argues the world warns of the risk but offers no prescription because it still thinks in an efficiency-oriented training model. His answer: switch to an evolution-oriented development OS where Field intelligence grows only through deliberate frontline exposure and Crazy intelligence only through sanctioned deviation from convention, implemented via a four-layer talent hierarchy of revenue evolutionists, AX architects, FDEs, and revenue-evolution FDEs.

Key points

  • AI is automating entry-level tasks that served as young workers' developmental scaffolding.
  • McKinsey, IMD, and BCG warn of weakened talent pipelines but offer no concrete prescription.
  • The author proposes switching from an efficiency-based to an evolution-based development OS.
  • Field intelligence requires deliberate frontline exposure; Crazy intelligence requires permission to deviate.
  • A four-layer hierarchy (revenue evolutionist, AX architect, FDE, revenue-evolution FDE) implements this model.

The world warns of this as a risk, but offers no prescription. That is because it is thinking in terms of the logic of the mountain of efficiency. The AX for Revenue prescription is this: Field only grows through exposure to the front lines, and Crazy only grows through permission to deviate. Switch the development OS from the mountain of efficiency to the mountain of evolution. The talent hierarchy (Revenue Evolutionist → AX Architect → FDE → Revenue Evolution FDE) is the implementation form of that development OS.

This article is a THEORY piece, organized from the personal perspective of Yoichi Aso, examining where the talent for ② humans who master AI will come from in an era when young talent's scaffolding is disappearing. AI is shifting from efficiency to revenue creation. The humans who will carry out that transition will not emerge if left alone.

The most overlooked issue in discussions of talent in the AI era is the upstream question of where the talent for ② humans who master AI will come from.

In three-responsibility-granularities, I organized the three responsibility granularities, and in beyond-searching-for-superstarspi-five-lenses-complementary-teamcomplementarity-three-transformation-types, I organized how to build complementary teams.

But where, fundamentally, do the ② members who make up those complementary teams come from?

The world's major research institutions are increasingly alarmed by this question. Yet they have not been able to present a clear prescription. In this article, I write a prescription for this upstream problem from the perspective of AX for Revenue.

② Humans who master AI will not develop if left alone

Chapter 3 of WP-09 "Management of Co-creation with AI" states the following (AX for Revenue Institute, "Management of Co-creation with AI," Chapter 3): "Even if you design an organization that brings together ② humans who master AI, if that talent does not develop, the formation becomes a pie in the sky."

The ② talent is not the implementer of AI Sprint (efficiency AI). They are the co-creators of PI Injection, people who push AI beyond its learning range. They are the ones with the hands to develop general-purpose generative AI into revenue-generating AI for this company and this market.

As established in three-responsibility-granularities, if left alone, ② slides down into a degraded version of ① (autonomous AI agents). Into an "editor" who merely rubber-stamps AI output. Into a "passive user" who just runs AI. What the research of McGuire et al. (2024), Walton et al. (2025), and Liu et al. (2025) shows is a structure in which the more comfortable the co-creation with AI, the more easily one slides down the mountain of homogenization.

That is precisely why "keeping ② as ②" itself becomes an active management responsibility.

However, to fulfill this responsibility, ② talent must first be developing within the organization. No matter how hard management tries to bring together talent that has not developed, there is nothing to bring together. The question of this article lies here: where does ② talent come from?

The scaffolding for young talent is disappearing — McKinsey's observation

Chapter 3 of WP-09 references the observation by McKinsey's Turle et al. (2026). The firm's People & Organizational Performance Practice presents the following structure regarding early-career talent in the AI era (McKinsey, "Rethinking early-career talent in the agentic organization," 2026).

Research, data cleanup, basic coding, document drafting, preliminary analysis. These are tasks historically positioned as entry-level work, but they are being rapidly automated by AI. A lead recruiter at a global technology company stated, "Most of the tasks positioned as grade-level or entry-level responsibilities can be automated."

The problem is not the reduction of work itself. It is that the "scaffolding" through which young people cultivated judgment and a sense for handling exceptions is disappearing.

Why "scaffolding"? Young people have acquired the big picture of the work, the knack for judgment, and the sense for exception handling through repetitive tasks. By writing meeting minutes a hundred times, they grasp the structure of meetings. By repeatedly organizing data, they develop sensitivity to anomalies in numbers. Through repeated creation of research materials, they learn how to weigh information. These repetitive tasks functioned as "scaffolding," serving as the steps by which young people climbed up to higher-level work (judgment, exception handling, decision-making).

But as AI automates those repetitive tasks, this scaffolding disappears. An HR leader at a Fortune 500 company told McKinsey: "Employees used to learn through repetition. Now companies must deliberately design development."

The firm cites a triple impact as the risk for organizations that fail to respond: long-term weakening of the talent pipeline, diminished leadership development, and the loss of the apprenticeship-like layer that builds knowledge.

It's not just McKinsey. Professor Michael Wade of IMD projects that conventional middle management positions will be cut by 10-20% by the end of 2026, warning of a break in the development pathway (IMD "2026 AI Trends," 2025). In joint research with Harvard Business Review, the BCG Henderson Institute reports that in organizations that position AI as a teammate or employee, individual accountability declines and uncertainty about professional identity rises (HBR/BCG 2026). A co-authored essay by Sequoia's Roelof Botha and Block's Jack Dorsey, arguing for a fundamental redesign of organizational structure, also touches on the collapse of the premises underlying talent development.

The world's leading commentators uniformly express a sense of crisis. Yet none has arrived at a concrete prescription.

The world warns but has no prescription — why?

Chapter 3 of WP-09 places a crucial observation here: "The world warns of this as a risk, but offers no prescription."

Why is no prescription to be found? Because the world's commentators think about talent development through the logic of the Mountain of Efficiency.

The view of development under the Mountain of Efficiency logic goes like this: have people grasp the overall picture of the work through routine tasks; build "scaffolding" toward higher-level work through repetition; and place the fundamentals of development in the tradition of OJT (On the Job Training). This logic was the established "common sense of development" in Japanese and Western companies in the latter half of the 20th century.

But it does not hold in the AI era. Because routine tasks are automated by AI, the scaffolding itself disappears. Even if young employees are assigned to "tasks that can be delegated to AI," their opportunities to grasp the overall picture through repetition keep shrinking.

Lining up the countermeasures proposed by the world's commentators makes this structure clear.

The proposal to "give AI tools to young employees too" is correct, but if the way they are given is wrong, (2) slides into a degraded version of (1). The proposal to "strengthen AI literacy training" — training builds skills, but it does not build PI. The proposal to "deliberately increase decision-making opportunities for young employees" is partially correct but lacks the overall picture.

All of them presuppose the Mountain of Efficiency development OS: learn the routine, build scaffolding through repetition, climb to higher-level work. Because they think about "how to incorporate AI" within this logic, their prescriptions remain piecemeal.

What is needed is a switch to the Mountain of Evolution development OS. This is not a change in training menus but a switch in the fundamental premises of development.

Field grows from exposure to the front line; Crazy grows from permission to deviate

Chapter 3 of WP-09 states the core of this switch as follows: "Field can only grow from exposure to the front line, and Crazy only from permission to deviate."

Why doesn't LINK: pi-primal-intelligence grow through repetitive work? Look at the development conditions for each of PI's two elements and the answer becomes clear.

Field Intelligence is information that is not verbalized, not captured as data, yet undeniably present on the front line. A fragment of a part on the factory floor, the instant of handing over a business card in sales, a change in how customer service inquiries are phrased. Acquiring Field requires direct, hands-on experience of the front line (exposure to it).

In routine tasks and in work done indirectly through AI, opportunities to encounter primary information from the front line are lost. Meetings are summarized by minutes AI, sales data is visualized by analysis AI, and customer voices arrive neatly arranged on dashboards. Young employees who only receive pre-processed information cannot acquire Field.

Meeting customers face to face, standing on the factory floor, being on the front line of complaint handling — this kind of exposure develops Field. In the AI era, unless this exposure is deliberately designed, young employees will only drift further from the front line. The choice to "use the time freed up by AI to send young employees to the front line" will not happen unless management consciously makes it.

Crazy Intelligence is an internally driven, leap-making way of thinking that cannot be derived logically and is not suggested by the voice of the front line either. Like Dyson repurposing an industrial dust collector into a household vacuum, or 3M turning a failed adhesive into the Post-it.

Developing Crazy requires "permission to deviate from common sense." In routine work, deviation is treated as "error." Deviation from the manual, from assumed procedures, from a superior's instructions. Under the Mountain of Efficiency logic, these are suppressed as "quality variation."

But the seeds of Crazy lie within this deviation. An organizational culture that permits deviation (the Unconventionality factor of CULTURE7; see culture7-seven-factors) develops Crazy. In the AI era, unless management deliberately designs this permission to deviate, young employees will not produce Crazy. If it is not valued even when produced, the motivation to produce it disappears.

What both have in common is that they do not develop through repetitive work. Rather, they only develop outside of repetitive work. Thorough efficiency gains and developing the next generation of talent do not simply coexist. This is where a management design decision is required.

Switching the development OS from the Mountain of Efficiency to the Mountain of Evolution

The Mountain of Efficiency development OS and the Mountain of Evolution development OS can be summarized as follows.

The Mountain of Efficiency development OS teaches the routine, builds scaffolding through repetition, and uses OJT to climb to higher-level work. It is the common sense of development from the latter half of the 20th century.

The Mountain of Evolution development OS puts people on the front line (exposure for Field), permits deviation (permission for Crazy), and assesses how PI manifests (the five lenses of the POT Assessment covered in pi-five-lenses-complementary-team). It is the new development OS for the AI era.

Switching the development OS is not merely a change in training programs. It is a design change across the whole of management, encompassing organizational culture (CULTURE7), personnel evaluation systems (separation from appraisal), assignment logic, and career path design.

Here stands the executive's question: Are you developing your company's young talent under the Mountain of Evolution development OS?

Try rereading the world's warning (the disappearance of scaffolding) not as a risk but as a signal. The disappearance of scaffolding is at once a crisis and a signal to switch the development OS. If the Mountain of Efficiency development OS no longer functions, the only option is to switch to the Mountain of Evolution development OS.

"The disappearance of the next generation of talent" is not merely a warning. It is a matter of executive decision-making. Will you maintain the Mountain of Efficiency development OS, or switch to the Mountain of Evolution development OS? This decision goes beyond what individual young employees' efforts or the ingenuity of HR departments can determine. It lies in a domain that management must decide with clear intent.

The AX for Revenue Practitioner Hierarchy — Revenue Evolutionist / AX Architect / FDE / Revenue Evolution FDE

Beyond the Development OS of the Mountain of Evolution, AX for Revenue establishes a four-tier practitioner hierarchy.

TierPositioning
revenue-evolutionistExecutives and business owners who redesign business structures together with AI. The primary readership of the book "AI Revenue Evolution Theory"
AX ArchitectTransformation talent who design the capillaries of the AX Area on the front lines of business implementation. A multiplication of BA capability × AI capability
Forward Deployed ExpertAn engagement model that demonstrates its strength on the front lines of business implementation. A concept that AlphaDrive establishes in Japan
revenue-evolution-fdeA specialized engagement model that accompanies the implementation of AX for Revenue

These tiers are not a hierarchy where "higher is superior." They are complementary relationships with different roles. The Revenue Evolutionist holds the will of management, the AX Architect designs the capillaries of the business, and the FDE demonstrates strength on the front lines of business implementation. Each is complete in its own distinct role.

There is one positioning here that must never be omitted: respect for the Forward Deployed Engineer.

The Forward Deployed Engineer is a core engagement model on the front lines of technical implementation, established by Palantir Technologies in the 2000s and inherited by the world's leading AI companies, including Anthropic and OpenAI. The image of engineers writing code at customer sites while converting challenges into products represents one culmination of implementation in the AI era. I hold deep respect for this history and practice.

AlphaDrive stands on this respect for the Forward Deployed Engineer and, alongside it, establishes the Forward Deployed Expert as "a different horizon." The Forward Deployed Engineer, who demonstrates strength on the front lines of technical implementation, and the Forward Deployed Expert, who demonstrates strength on the front lines of business implementation, are not in opposition; they are different engagement models that each demonstrate strength in different arenas. Experts sometimes write code. Engineers sometimes step into business design. This is not a matter of superiority or inferiority, but a difference of horizons. This framing is covered in detail in separate-horizon-frame.

② The practitioners who master AI develop within this four-tier hierarchy. Initial practitioner candidates are discovered through the five lenses (pi-five-lenses-complementary-team). Complementary teams are formed by combining the three types (complementarity-three-transformation-types). Then, through exposure to the front lines and permission to deviate, they grow into practitioners (this article). Within the practitioner hierarchy, each walks the path of Revenue Evolutionist → AX Architect → FDE → Revenue Evolution FDE in their respective arena.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. If we deliberately assign young employees to "inefficient tasks," won't productivity decline?

Looking only at short-term productivity metrics, it may appear to decline. However, the Development OS of the Mountain of Evolution is an endeavor that consciously designs the trade-off between short-term efficiency and long-term practitioner development. The surplus capacity created by efficiency AI is deliberately redirected toward the space for young employees' front-line exposure and deviation. This allocation itself is a management decision. Should it be absorbed into careful, just-in-case work, or invested in practitioner development? Column ② of the book "AI Revenue Evolution Theory," "Parallel-Running Tactics," also connects to this topic of allocation.

Q2. How should "front-line exposure" be designed in the era of remote work?

The physical front line is not the only source of Field. Direct dialogue with customers, accompanying sales meetings, first-line contact in customer support, conducting user interviews. These all become opportunities for Field exposure regardless of format. What matters is not placing young employees in a position where they only receive curated information (dashboards, summaries, reports). Deliberately position them where they touch primary information. Whether remote or on-site is merely one design variable.

Q3. How can "permission to deviate" and "maintaining operational discipline" be reconciled?

The key to reconciliation is for management to explicitly separate the domains where deviation is permitted from the domains where discipline must be maintained. Clearly define the domains where deviation is unacceptable, such as safety, legal compliance, and quality assurance. At the same time, in the domains of ideas, proposals, and hypotheses, actively embrace deviation from convention. The Unconventionality factor and the Truthfulness factor (integrity and fairness) of CULTURE7 do not conflict. Design the organizational culture on multiple axes, treating the two as separate axes rather than opposing ones. What is needed is not company-wide uniform "freedom" or "discipline," but domain-by-domain design.

Q4. How does the practitioner hierarchy (Revenue Evolutionist → AX Architect → FDE → Revenue Evolution FDE) align with existing job grade systems?

The practitioner hierarchy is not a system of job titles or grades; it is a typology of roles. Independently of existing job grades (department manager, section manager, supervisor, staff), you assess where each role should be placed. A Revenue Evolutionist may concurrently serve as a department manager, and an AX Architect may hold a junior supervisory position. Separating job titles from roles is a prerequisite of the Development OS of the Mountain of Evolution.

Q5. Can the same prescription be applied to developing mid-career and senior employees?

The principle is the same. If mid-career and senior employees spend their time only on repetitive tasks, PI will not develop anew. The development conditions of front-line exposure and permission to deviate operate regardless of age or job grade. In fact, mid-career and senior employees are likely to possess Field accumulated in the past. If the organization creates opportunities to articulate it and pour it into AI, they can demonstrate strength as practitioners. This is not an issue for young employees alone; it should be considered as a shift to a development OS for all generations.

② The practitioners who master AI will not develop if left alone. In an era where the scaffolding for young employees has disappeared, the development function that repetitive tasks once provided cannot be restored unless it is consciously redesigned in a different form.

Field grows from exposure to the front lines, and Crazy grows from permission to deviate. Based on this principle, management shifts to a development OS, moving from the Mountain of Efficiency to the Mountain of Evolution. The development OS of the Mountain of Efficiency (OJT, skill maps, hierarchical promotion) was not wasted effort. It was the solid infrastructure of the Development OS of the Mountain of Evolution.

On that infrastructure, the next development OS is built by the will of management. AI moves from efficiency to the creation of revenue. The people who carry that transformation are only born when management holds the will to develop them.
