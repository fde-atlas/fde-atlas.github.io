Vibe Engines · 2026-09-03 · 25 min read

Summary

Vibe Engines has published a library of 209 full-length technical handbooks organized as 27 reading ladders rather than a flat list of cards. Topics span computing fundamentals, programming languages, databases, networking and auth, distributed systems, deployment and observability, and a large AI engineering arc covering transformers, prompting, RAG, agents, MCP, and fine-tuning. Each rung assumes the one below it, and every 'X vs Y' comparison is placed only after both subjects have been introduced, so comparisons are answerable in context.

Key points

  • 209 handbooks arranged as 27 sequential reading ladders
  • Covers systems, languages, data, networking, and distributed design
  • AI arc spans transformers, RAG, agents, MCP, and training
  • Comparisons appear only after both topics are covered
  • Each rung assumes knowledge from the previous one

Handbooks — frequently asked questions

Are the handbooks free to read?

Yes. Every handbook is free, full-length, and self-contained — no paywall, no sign-up, and no email required to read any of them.

Which handbook should I read first?

The Prompting Handbook — rung 1 of the Prompting and context ladder, and the only handbook here that assumes nothing before it. If you are an engineer looking for the technical floor instead, start at ML Fundamentals (rung 1 of "How models actually work") or System Design Fundamentals (rung 1 of the distributed-systems ladder).

Why are the handbooks in ladders instead of categories?

Because reading order changes what a handbook teaches. Each rung is written assuming the one below it, so a ladder can tell you what to read next; a category list cannot. It also puts every "X vs Y" comparison directly after both things it compares — RAG vs Fine-Tuning is the last rung of the retrieval ladder, because it is only answerable once you have read both sides.

Who writes these handbooks?

They are written by Saurabh Singh, a senior AI engineer, based on real production experience building agentic AI systems, evals, and large-scale services — not generic content.

How long does each handbook take to read?

Most run between 8 and 20 minutes, shown on each rung and filterable with the length pills. A handful of the older flagship handbooks are much longer — the Prompting, Senior AI Engineer and System Design Fundamentals handbooks are designed as a single focused sitting, with interactive widgets and quizzes throughout.

Do the handbooks track what I have read?

Yes, in your own browser. Marking a handbook complete fills in its rung and its ladder’s progress bar, and the catalog surfaces the next rung of whichever ladder you are furthest along. Nothing is sent anywhere and there is no account.

If there's a topic you'd like covered — or a rung you think sits in the wrong place on one of the ladders above — send it through. Genuine requests shape what gets written next.
