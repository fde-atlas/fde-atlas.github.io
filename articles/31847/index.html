Why breadth matters in engineering hiring and FDEs

FDE Times · 2026-10-08 · 3 min read

Summary

Writing on FDE Hub, Milos Mandic argues that AI is making execution cheap, so engineering value is shifting from specialist depth to breadth: the ability to judge whether output fits a given business. FDE roles show this shift first, since the job centers on judging AI output beside the customer. Hiring data supports the thesis: over 70% of recent FDE postings require customer discovery skills, with coding as mere baseline. The piece includes interview preparation advice and suggests organizing CVs by business domain rather than tech stack.

  • Paraform finds the supply of qualified FDE candidates has not kept up with demand. On the employer side, AWS has followed OpenAI and Anthropic in placing engineers inside companies

Writing on FDE Hub, Milos Mandic argues that breadth of experience, undervalued for years, is finally worth something in the engineering job market. His argument covers engineering hiring in general, not just FDEs. The FDE title is simply where the shift is most visible. It is an opinion piece with no data attached, but the core argument is clear.

AI is making execution cheaper, so value is shifting to the ability to judge whether output is right for the business in each domain.

Why was breadth undervalued? Mandic points out that job descriptions have always favoured people who go deep in one direction, for the simple reason that specialists are easier to assess. A broad profile has no ready-made yardstick, and hiring processes are built around what can be measured.

Cheap execution, expensive judgement

The piece’s central claim is that software engineering is still on the list of things that need judgement, but it is no longer the only item. AI leverage has a limit: a model only amplifies the curiosity, judgement and taste its user brings. It cannot supply what you lack. For FDEs this goes to the heart of the job, which is sitting beside the customer and deciding which output fits their business.

Mandic’s advice to hiring managers is to ask candidates how many domains they can tell right from wrong in.

What the hiring data says

Job posting data fits the argument. Perspective analysed 1,000 FDE job postings and found that the fastest-growing requirement is direct customer discovery, which appears in more than 70% of recent postings. Coding is only the baseline. The language used to separate candidates centres on understanding the customer.

How fast this wave is moving depends on how you count. Perspective records FDE hiring up more than 1,000% year on year as of early 2026. Paraform puts the growth at 350% from Q1 2025 to Q1 2026. Paraform also finds that the supply of qualified candidates has not kept pace with demand, and advises employers to focus screening on the two hardest qualities to measure: empathy for the customer and tolerance for ambiguity.

The demand side has added some big names. AWS has followed OpenAI and Anthropic in placing engineers to work inside companies.

Preparing for the new kind of interview

Question you may face What they are measuring How to show it
“How many domains can you tell right from wrong in?” Cross-domain judgement Describe 2–3 projects in different industries, each with one time you rejected output that ran but was wrong for the business
“What do you ask in a first meeting with a customer?” Customer discovery Lay out a sequence of questions that moves from the current workflow to the pain point, rather than starting from a solution
“The requirements are vague. What do you do first?” Tolerance for ambiguity Explain how you settle on one small assumption to test, instead of waiting for a full spec
“How do you use AI in your work?” The taste and judgement you bring to the model Give an example of fixing or discarding model output because you understood the domain better than it did

For Vietnamese developers who have done outsourcing work across many industries, this is an advantage that has long gone to waste. A CV that lists stacks looks like “knows a lot, but nothing deeply”. A CV organised by business domain, with the decisions made in each industry, matches exactly what the FDE market is looking for. When reading job descriptions, watch for phrases such as “discovery”, “customer-facing” and “ambiguity”. They signal that the company is hiring for judgement, not just someone to write code.
