What an FDE Leaves Behind at the End of an Engagement

Falkster.AI · 2026-11-19 · 2 min read

Summary

The article argues that handover documents record conclusions valid only for the conditions at writing time, so they become useless when customer priorities, contracts, or stakeholders change. It cites Marco Di Santo, who built a decision agent at LivePerson from video transcripts of himself reasoning through real decisions. The agent answers questions along five dimensions: customer size, contract structure, risk tolerance, regulatory environment, and proximity to renewal. Unlike a document, the agent holds the full decision tree across combinations, but must be built in the engineer's final week.

Key points

  • Handover documents capture conclusions, not judgment, and age quickly.
  • Marco Di Santo built a decision agent at LivePerson from video transcripts of his reasoning.
  • The agent works across five dimensions: size, contract, risk, regulation, renewal timing.
  • Engineers should record reasoning in their last week, before leaving.

Full text

The best handover document I have ever seen was thorough, accurate, and useless by week two.

It was thorough because the departing engineer cared. It was accurate because they wrote it from memory the week before they left. It was useless by week two because the situations it described were not the situations that came up.

A document is a record of conclusions under conditions that existed when it was written. FDE (forward deployed engineering) work produces constantly changing conditions. The customer's priorities shift, the contract renews under different terms, a new stakeholder inherits the relationship. The document does not update itself. The engineering judgment that would have navigated those changes has left the building.

What the decision agent captures

Marco Di Santo built a decision agent at LivePerson before leaving his role. The raw material was video transcripts of himself reasoning through real decisions. Not conclusions. Reasoning out loud.

The agent runs on five question types:

  • How does this recommendation change by customer size and strategic importance?
  • How does it change by contract structure (usage, seat, outcome-based)?
  • How does it change by the customer's stated risk tolerance?
  • How does it change by regulatory environment?
  • How does it change by proximity to renewal?

These five dimensions were not categories he was told to use. The transcripts revealed the structure he was already using implicitly. Every real decision he made could be explained by which combination of those five dimensions he was holding.

The document could not have captured this. The document gives you the answer for one combination. The agent holds the full decision tree.

What to build in the last week

The video transcript method works because it surfaces reasoning you would not think to write. An alternative approach: record yourself answering these five questions as if explaining to your replacement.

Start with the cases where you would slow down rather than move forward. Then the shortcuts that are safe and the ones that look safe but are not. Then what this customer says they want versus what they actually need. Then who belongs in every significant decision conversation. Then what would have to be true for you to recommend pausing the engagement.

No document holds those answers for 32 combinations of the five dimensions. A decision agent can. The constraint is building it the week before you leave, not the week after you are gone.
