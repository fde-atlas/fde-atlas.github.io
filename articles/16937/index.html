Insight 01

The Role of the Implementation Owner Who Delivers Results in AI Adoption

AI projects stall not at the strategy or specification stage, but at the "implementation stage." This article compares how FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) differ from IT consultants, traditional SEs, and outsourced development vendors.

While AI investment is accelerating, organizations frequently hit walls such as "the policy is decided, but AI never gets embedded into operations" or "projects end at the PoC stage." The cause is not a lack of strategy, but the absence of a process to translate strategy into implementation and make it stick on the ground.

The role that handles this implementation stage is the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer). Translated literally into Japanese, it means "an engineer deployed to the front line." It refers to a role that goes directly into the front lines of business—customer offices, factories, and stores—and takes end-to-end responsibility for AI, from design and development through to adoption.

IT consultants play an important role in organizing business challenges, formulating DX strategy, and creating roadmaps—showing the direction for AI utilization. However, AI projects actually stall at stages like the following:

  • The model is built but can't be integrated into operations
  • Data integration is more complex than expected
  • The front line won't accept the new operations

An FDE understands the strategy and then becomes an executor who writes code, builds infrastructure, adjusts the UI, and implements it on the ground, taking responsibility all the way to a working deliverable. It's easiest to understand as the difference between "someone who draws the picture" and "someone who makes it run."

Traditional SEs and outsourced development vendors proceed with development based on finalized specifications. In waterfall development, changes require additional work and contract modifications.

AI projects, on the other hand, generally have no correct specification from the start. Specifications themselves must be updated while looking at the data and observing the front line. FDEs iterate hypothesis testing on the ground from the stage where specifications are still ambiguous, searching for the optimal solution.

In conclusion, these three roles are not in conflict but represent a division of labor. IT consultants fit the entry point of strategy, SEs and outsourced development fit the development of standardized business systems, and FDEs fit areas like AI, where specifications keep evolving and front-line feedback directly affects quality.

Especially in phases like "the PoC is done but we can't move to production" or "we want to embed AI into our operations but have no in-house experts," the presence of an FDE who can bridge strategy and implementation makes the difference between success and failure.
