PropelX 2026 recap: delivery is the new Moat!

Rocketlane · 2026-09-30 · 13 min read

Summary

A recap of PropelX 2026, Rocketlane's professional services conference held in London on 10 September 2026, which drew nearly 300 PS, delivery and operations leaders—roughly triple last year's attendance. The central argument: customers no longer pay for effort, only for outcomes, making delivery the new competitive moat. CEO Srikrishnan Ganesan offered five predictions, including outcome-based pricing and go-lives measured in weeks. Case studies include Chargebee cutting data analysis from 7 weeks to 2 with 27 agents, Fin's $1M outcome guarantee, and Pendo's value milestones. Panels also covered failures, such as 100+ agents with shockingly low ROI and clients demanding discounts because AI is used.

Key points

  • Delivery is the new moat: customers pay for outcomes, not hours.
  • Ganesan predicts outcome-based pricing, week-scale go-lives, and compressed roles.
  • Chargebee cut a customer's data analysis from 7 weeks to 2 using 27 agents.
  • Fin's $1M outcome guarantee rewired its roles, scoping, and partner ecosystem.
  • Honest failures: 100+ agents with shockingly low ROI and AI-driven discount demands.

If you'd wandered onto the fifth floor of the Pan Pacific London on 10 September, you might have assumed a room full of PS leaders had quit professional services for puppy therapy. There was a puppy corner with its own fan club by mid-morning, a tarot room, fortune cookies with "bite sized wisdom for delivery pros," and a cold-brew cart for anyone whose third wind was running late.

Downstairs, though, the conversations were a lot more serious. Nearly 300 PS, delivery and operations leaders spent the day on one uncomfortable question: if AI is here, where are the outcomes? Across keynotes, panels and some very honest "here's what broke" sessions, the answer kept coming back to the same place. Customers have stopped paying for effort. They want results, and it's professional services teams that have to deliver them.

This PropelX 2026 recap covers the whole week at PropelX, Rocketlane's professional services conference in London: the side events, every session (with links to the full write-ups), the first Golden Comet Awards, what attendees took home, and the closing note from Srikrishnan Ganesan (CEO and co-founder of Rocketlane) on why this is PS's moment.

PropelX grew to roughly three times last year's size, and for the first time it became a full week of events rather than a single conference day. It followed the wider Propel community gathering in San Francisco earlier in the year.

  • Women in PS happy hour (8 September): Rocketlane's first-ever Women in PS gathering brought 27 PS leaders together over cocktails, with no agenda and plenty of conversation.
  • PS AI Build Day (9 September): co-hosted with GoCardless, the Build Day put 32 builders in a room with Anthropic, ElevenLabs, Lovable and Rocketlane's Nitro agents to build tools for onboarding, handoffs and customer communication. Flight cancellations and train delays pushed part of the group online, and it still worked. Sri called it "probably our best attended hackathon."
  • Customer dinner (9 September): an evening with Rocketlane customers before the main event, part of Rocketlane's community-first approach.

The banner at the Build Day read "Today, you build," which turned out to be a fair summary of the whole week.

Sri opened with the Wall Street Journal's clip of Claudius, an AI agent that went bankrupt running an office vending machine. That same month, the same model was helping Anthropic pass $1B in revenue. Same technology, very different results, and the difference is how it gets implemented.

From there, he laid out five predictions for professional services: outcome-based pricing, go-lives measured in weeks instead of months, compressed roles, more work moving pre-sale, and utilization fading as the main PS metric. Simon England (Partner at Garwood Growth), Adam Maze (Managing Consultant at Garwood Growth), Martin Roxby and Steve Beckley (Directors and Co-founders of J21A) closed the sessions with a board-level view, moderated by Heather Pertel (Head of Partnerships and Alliances at Rocketlane). Their message: "people don't want to pay for output and effort," embedded PS needs its own strategy, and every service should be mapped to automate, productise, enhance or exit. PropelX also hosted Rocketlane's first Golden Comet Awards for EMEA, celebrating the PS leaders and teams driving the shift from "projects delivered" to measurable outcomes. This year's winners:

  • AI Trailblazer in PS: Jacqui Morgan (Head of Client Delivery Transformation at Xceptor) and Sukanya Kuppuswamy (VP of Professional Services at Chargebee), for leading AI in PS delivery
  • PS Leader of the Year: Egor Diordichuk (Director of Professional Services at ABBYY), recognised for delivery leadership
  • PS Team of the Year: ICP , for delivery excellence

Egor summed up the mood afterwards: "Really proud (and still a little surprised!)." Jacqui shared her approach to AI in delivery on LinkedIn, and it's worth stealing: "I started with the problem, not the tool." It's a good reminder that AI transformation in PS starts with the problem.

The fifth floor was designed for the moments between sessions. There was the puppy corner (a clear crowd favourite), a tarot room with a custom 22-card deck predicting "what's in store for your career," fortune cookies, a Nitro cold-brew cart, a testimonial wall, an executive sky lounge and product office hours with the Rocketlane team. It was a small-scale version of the unreasonable hospitality Akbur talked about on stage.

It turns out PS leaders are very easy to find at a conference. You just check the puppies first. Katie Hatch (Head of Customer Experience at Alkimii) admitted as much: "If anyone was wondering where I disappeared to between sessions, the puppy corner is probably a safe bet." Several attendees also said the best part of the day was the community itself.

The sessions covered a lot of ground, but the same few ideas kept showing up in attendees' posts afterwards:

  • Go-live isn't the finish line. Tom Ford (Implementation at Codat) picked up Akbur's version: "go live is a state, not a date." Grant Millington (Director of Customer Success Engineering at Optibus) put it as "Go live is Day 0," a theme that echoes why go-live is the wrong finish line.
  • Hours aren't outcomes. "Hours do not equal outcomes," wrote Grant, and "outcome based pricing has arrived," as covered in outcome-based pricing.
  • AI with intent. Ronen Eyal (fractional Head of Professional Services) shared three insights from the day: "It's about the community. It's about value. It's about AI, but it's not about AI." That's the same point behind AI ROI in professional services.
  • PS is being promoted, not replaced. PS leader Neil Chandarana put it simply: "AI isn't replacing Professional Services. It's changing what great Professional Services looks like," which is where the outcome era is heading.

Duncan Meyers (Practice Manager at Outreach) managed to summarise the whole day in one line: FDEs, outcome-based pricing, "$2 hotdogs at a Michelin star restaurant" and a "room full of puppies." It's hard to argue with that as a professional services trends summary.

Sri closed the day by thanking the room for making PropelX what it is, and for the conversations that happened outside the sessions as much as inside them. He left everyone with three thoughts, and pointed people towards the PS Club to keep those conversations going.

Every decade or so, one function rises to the top. In the early 2010s it was marketing teams building inbound funnels, and then customer success took off as SaaS moved everything to subscriptions. Now, because of enterprise AI, professional services is firmly in that seat. Sri's message to the room was to take advantage of it and step up as the heroes of their organisations.

It won't just be tools that help customers reach an outcome. More competitors will say "we'll own and deliver the outcome for you." Fin's $1M guarantee is one example. Sri pointed to another in legal tech: while some tools help legal teams work faster, a startup is simply offering to do the contract work itself for $800 per contract. Rocketlane is trying the same with migrations, charging per successful migration. PS teams need to prepare for competition that sells results, not software, and for outcome-based billing.

Transformation won't happen by waiting for time to free up. Sri's advice was to deliberately make time, perhaps every quarter, for teams to catch up on what's changing, plan what they want to achieve, and build or experiment with what's out there. Rocketlane runs hackathons around the world and will help teams host one in their own city or office, in the same spirit as making time for work that matters.

He finished where the week started: "This is our moment to shine as a department, as a community."

And Rocketlane has already asked the question for next year: should PropelX go for 400+?

  • Delivery is the new moat. The team that proves the first outcome earns the right to automate the next one.
  • Outcomes beat hours. From Pendo's milestones to Fin's guarantee, PS is moving away from billable effort.
  • Intent beats volume. AI agents only pay off when they're tied to a specific customer outcome.
  • Go-live is day zero. Adoption, consumption and expansion are the new measures of PS success.
  • PS needs a strategy. Embedded PS is a business within a business, and it needs its own plan.
  • Define "done" as an outcome. Rewrite your best-selling package around a milestone and a customer KPI.
  • Audit your AI. Tie every agent to a customer journey step and an outcome, and retire the rest.
  • Fix your handoffs. Keep one living record of customer context from sales to customer success.
  • Raise the partner bar. Pay partners for the quality customers report, not just for the partnership.
  • Make time to build. Schedule a quarterly build day before the extra AI workload lands, as part of your annual PS plan.

PropelX 2026 was Rocketlane's professional services conference in London, held on 10 September 2026 at the Pan Pacific London. Nearly 300 PS, delivery and operations leaders attended, and the theme was "the outcome era," focused on how AI is changing professional services delivery. It's part of Rocketlane's wider Propel series for PS leaders.

The main themes were outcomes over effort, go-live as the start rather than the finish of delivery, outcome-based pricing, AI agents changing PS roles, forward deployed engineering, and the need for a clear PS strategy and business model in the AI era, all covered in Rocketlane's guide to AI in professional services.

Jacqui Morgan of Xceptor and Sukanya Kuppuswamy of Chargebee won AI Trailblazer in PS, Egor Diordichuk of ABBYY won PS Leader of the Year, and ICP won PS Team of the Year at Rocketlane's first Golden Comet Awards for EMEA, which recognise professional services excellence.

"Delivery is the new moat" means that as AI products offer increasingly similar features, the company that gets customers to measurable outcomes first gains the advantage. Rocketlane CEO Srikrishnan Ganesan argued at PropelX 2026 that this puts professional services at the centre of competitive strategy and time to value.

Session recordings and decks are being shared by Rocketlane after the event, with decks available at rocketlane.com/propelX/session-decks. Full written recaps of each session are linked throughout this PropelX 2026 recap, alongside earlier Propel event coverage.

The PS AI Build Day was a hands-on hackathon held on 9 September 2026, co-hosted by GoCardless and Rocketlane. Around 32 PS professionals built tools for onboarding, handoffs and customer communication using Anthropic, ElevenLabs, Lovable and Rocketlane's Nitro agents, including workforce agents.

Nearly 300 PS, delivery and operations leaders attended PropelX 2026 in London, roughly three times the previous year's attendance. The week also included a Women in PS happy hour with 27 guests and a PS AI Build Day with 32 builders, showing how quickly the PS community is growing.

The outcome era is the shift from selling and measuring professional services by effort, such as hours or sessions, to selling and measuring them by the business results customers achieve. At PropelX 2026, speakers described it through outcome-based pricing, value milestones and outcome guarantees, and new value realization measures.

Sri Ganesan left attendees with three takeaways: delivery is the new moat and the spotlight is on PS, competitors will increasingly offer to own and deliver the outcome for customers, and teams must deliberately make time for transformation, for example through quarterly build days and change management.

Rocketlane has signalled that PropelX will return, asking the community whether the next event should aim for 400+ attendees. In the meantime, the PS Club community, Women in PS events and regional hackathons continue the conversations, alongside Rocketlane's professional services delivery resources.
