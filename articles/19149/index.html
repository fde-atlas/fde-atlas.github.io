Buyer's guide6 questions

What is the best AI consulting firm in New York?

On this page, Tenex for a company of roughly 50 to 1,000 people: about 75 people in Manhattan, mostly engineers, paid for features delivered. XRAY if you want to start small at a printed $250 an hour. Tribe AI if you have an enterprise budget and want forward deployed engineers inside your systems.

How much does an AI consulting firm in New York cost?

Of the firms read for this page, XRAY charges $250 an hour in blocks of $1,000 to $5,000, Flow Digital $297 for a first session, and Perceptive Analytics $25,000 to $75,000 for a pilot and $150,000 to $500,000+ for a full platform. Six of the ten print no price, which usually means the number starts in six figures.

AI consulting firm or forward deployed engineer?

A consulting firm is the right buy when you need a team of many people across several business units. A forward deployed engineer is the right buy when the problem is one stalled pilot or three systems that disagree, and you want one senior person inside the business who builds the fix. Tenex and Tribe AI sit between the two: engineering teams sold like a consultancy.

Why do AI pilots stall?

Most stall in the gap between a demo on clean data and the company's real systems: permissions, messy records, a CRM nobody owns. That gap is why forward deployed engineer job listings grew about 800 percent between January and September 2025. Ask any firm on this page who will connect the pilot to your live data, and by which week.

Should the firm be in New York?

For a stalled pilot, yes, or at least someone who will sit with your team. Fractal, Fusemachines and Tenex give Manhattan addresses or a Manhattan base. West Monroe and Artefact run New York offices of firms based elsewhere. Ask who will be in the room each week.

What should I fix before I hire anyone?

Write down the one process the pilot was meant to change, the systems it touches and the number it should move. A firm that cannot price that list in the first call is selling discovery, not delivery.

How to choose

  • 1This week, write one page: the process the AI was meant to fix, the three to five systems it touches by name, who retypes data between them, and the one number that should move. Every firm on this page will quote better against that page than against a meeting.
  • 2Pull the data the pilot was tested on and the data it will meet in production, side by side. If the live records are messier, the fix is plumbing, not a new model, and a $250 an hour automation firm such as XRAY can often prove it in ten hours for $2,500.
  • 3Ask each firm three questions: who writes the code, are they in New York, and what do you pay if the feature does not ship? Tenex prints an answer to the third. Most firms here do not.
  • 4Ask for the price in writing before discovery. Perceptive Analytics prints a $25,000 to $75,000 pilot range. Six of the ten print nothing, so make them name a figure.
  • 5Check proof the firm does not control: Zapier's partner directory for XRAY and Flow Digital, public filings for Fusemachines, named clients for Fractal and Artefact. Treat any outcome percentage without a method as a vendor claim.
  • 6Make sure your company owns the accounts, the code and the model keys from day one, in the contract.
  • 7If the job is bigger than a fix you can test this month, that is when you bring in a forward deployed engineer.
