Distyl AI: FDE Player Dossier

Plank · 2026-06-14 · 14 min read

Summary

This dossier profiles Distyl AI, a San Francisco company founded in 2022 that applies Palantir's forward deployed engineer model to enterprise generative AI. Both co-founders spent roughly a decade at Palantir; CEO Arjun Prakash managed 30+ forward deployed staff there. Distyl bundles embedded FDE teams, its proprietary Distillery agentic platform, and applied AI research into multi-year contracts, promising production deployment within three months, with part of fees tied to client-defined outcomes. It raised $202M total, reaching a $1.8B valuation in September 2025 (a 9x step-up from its $200M Series A ten months earlier). Estimated 2025 ARR is ~$31M with ~179 employees. It claims 50+ Fortune 500 deployments, all in production, processing 1B+ decisions annually, though no customer has been publicly named.

Key points

  • $1.8B Series B valuation, Sep 2025, a 9x step-up from Series A
  • Both co-founders are ex-Palantir; model mirrors Palantir FDEs
  • Outcome-based contracts: fees partly tied to client business results
  • Claims 50+ Fortune 500 customers, all at production stage
  • All case studies anonymized; no customer publicly named

Distyl AI embeds forward-deployed engineers in Fortune 500 companies to build agentic AI on its Distillery platform, tying part of each project fee to client-defined outcomes. Founded in 2022 by two Palantir alumni, it has raised $202M and was valued at $1.8B in September 2025.

Their role in FDE

  • The play
  • Both co-founders came from Palantir, where CEO Arjun Prakash managed 30+ forward-deployed strategists and engineers and helped create Foundry. Distyl sells Fortune 500 companies multi-year deployments that pair embedded engineers and researchers with its own Distillery platform.
  • The economics
  • Part of each project fee rides on client-defined outcomes, on top of recurring Distillery licensing, and contracts can reach tens of millions of dollars. One case study: $200M+ in projected opex savings for a Fortune 100 telecom operator.
  • The edge
  • Distillery turns standard operating procedures into auditable, policy-aware agentic workflows and traces every decision to the exact knowledge state at runtime. Distyl reports a 100% production record and says its systems reach 150M+ end users.
  • The catch
  • Its partners are also its rivals. Distyl has been an OpenAI services alliance partner since April 2023, yet OpenAI's Deployment Company competes for the same Fortune 500 budgets, and Microsoft Frontier overlaps with it in the Azure co-sell channel Distyl also uses.

FDE model · approach · differentiation

What they do

What Distyl sells

Distyl sells multi-year deployments to Fortune 500 and Global 2000 companies. Each combines embedded forward-deployed engineers and researchers, the Distillery platform, and applied AI research on the client's workflows. An AI Strategist leads each engagement with a technical lead, and the team shares accountability for business results. CEO Arjun Prakash says Distyl delivers outcomes within three months.

The Distillery platform

Distillery turns enterprise standard operating procedures and subject-matter experts' knowledge into Routines: auditable, policy-aware agentic workflows. It has four layers: the Context Layer structures fragmented enterprise knowledge into a queryable semantic layer; in the Autonomous Solution Delivery Layer, non-engineers build and refine workflows in plain language; the Control Layer handles governance, tracing every decision to the exact knowledge state at runtime; and the Application Layer runs production deployment and monitoring. Products include Weave (autonomous system construction), Context Mesh (knowledge structuring), Journey (an AI voice layer), Personalization (1:1 customer engagement across app, web, chat and contact center) and Canary (self-improvement and monitoring). Distillery is SOC 2 compliant and listed on the Azure Marketplace.

Customers and use cases

Distyl's customers are Fortune 500 enterprises, Fortune 100 companies among them, in regulated, data-intensive industries. Use cases include contact-center intelligence and AI-native operations for telecom operators; prior authorization, contract management and remote patient monitoring for healthcare payers; and auto finance, fraud detection and underwriting in financial services. It also serves insurers, advanced manufacturers (supply-chain disruption analysis, root-cause resolution) and consumer goods and retail companies (order management). T-Mobile has been named publicly as a customer; published case studies identify other clients by Fortune rank and industry, such as "F20 Healthcare Payor" for a Fortune 20 healthcare payer.

Pricing

Part of each project fee depends on meeting business objectives the client defines, and recurring licensing fees pay for ongoing Distillery access. Contracts run over multiple years and can reach tens of millions of dollars. Distyl has disclosed no per-seat, per-token or time-and-materials pricing.

Models and cloud

Distyl builds on models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google (Gemini); OpenAI has been a services alliance partner since April 2023. Its cloud partners are Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud, which in April 2026 named Distyl an early and priority partner in its Gemini Enterprise transformation program. Since March 2026, Distillery has also integrated NVIDIA AI Enterprise software, including open Nemotron 3 Super models served through NVIDIA NIM microservices. Deployments run Distyl-hosted, in customer VPCs or on-premises, across AWS, Azure and GCP.

Team & org structure

Forward-deployed AI Strategists, paired with AI Engineers on every engagement, form the delivery core, backed by a small product and research group. Both co-founders, and several senior leaders, came from Palantir.

On The Org's chart (Oct 2026), AI Engineering (about 36 people) and AI Strategy (about 26) are the largest teams. Together, about 62 people run client engagements, each led by an AI Strategist paired with a technical lead. Platform development (about 11) and applied AI research (about 8) build Distillery; advisory staff and the Office of the CEO handle go-to-market and internal operations. Of the 25 roles open on Oct 31, 2026, ten are in research; the rest include lead engineering and strategist roles, infrastructure engineers for customer delivery, and an AI Engineer and an AI Strategist in London.

Where they lead · structural gaps · competitors

Market position

  • Where they lead
  • Fortune 500 and Fortune 100 enterprises in regulated, data-intensive industries such as telecom, healthcare payers, insurers and advanced manufacturers, on multi-year embedded engagements that can reach tens of millions of dollars. Distyl raised a $175M Series B in September 2025 and has partnerships with Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud and NVIDIA.
  • Structural gaps
  • Distyl requires customers to license Distillery and staff a multi-month embedded team, and contracts can reach tens of millions of dollars. That makes it a poor fit for startups, scale-ups and Series A–D companies, and for short, vendor-neutral engagements or work meant to speed up a client's own team.

Competes with

  • Palantir Technologies Specialist Both Distyl co-founders came from Palantir. The two compete for large-enterprise deals that pair forward-deployed engineers with a platform.
  • OpenAI Deployment Company Frontier lab OpenAI's forward-deployed unit competes for the same Fortune 500 agentic-AI transformation budgets. Distyl is also an OpenAI services alliance partner, so OpenAI is both a channel and a rival.
  • Ode (with Anthropic) Frontier lab Anthropic-aligned deployment specialist competing for agentic AI work in regulated industries.
  • Microsoft Frontier Hyperscaler Microsoft's forward-deployed unit; it overlaps with Distyl in the Azure enterprise co-sell channel, which Distyl also uses.
  • AWS Forward-Deployed Engineering Hyperscaler Forward-deployed engineers employed by AWS itself, competing for the same enterprise transformation contracts.
  • Accenture / EY (legacy consultancies) Incumbent management consultancies; Distyl pitches itself against them on technical depth and accountability for outcomes.
