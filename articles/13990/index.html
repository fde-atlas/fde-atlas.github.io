What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer? Role, Skills, Pay

Forward Deploying · 2026-11-02 · 3 min read

Summary

This article defines the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), a role that originated at Palantir: a software engineer who deploys products, usually AI products, into a customer's real environment with production code. It outlines four core responsibilities, a six-dimension skills scorecard, and warns that many re-titled pre-sales or solutions roles are not true FDEs. It closes with career paths and pay, noting FDE compensation sits at or above senior software engineering levels.

Key points

  • FDEs write production code that runs in customer environments, not demos.
  • Core duties: scoping, building on customer stacks, evals, and feeding product learnings back.
  • Six skill dimensions: production engineering, LLM systems, evals, data integration, customer judgment, communication.
  • The litmus test for a real FDE is shipped production code in a customer's environment.
  • Career paths lead to staff engineering, architecture, or leadership; pay matches or exceeds senior engineers.

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who works directly with a customer to deploy a product, usually an AI product, into that customer's real environment: their data, their systems, their permissions and their deadlines. The FDE writes production code, not slides, and is measured on what goes live.

Where the role comes from

Palantir built its business on engineers it sent to customers to make its software work on real data. The idea spread because the same problem now sits at the centre of AI: a model that performs in a demo still has to be connected to a customer's documents, tools and approvals before it earns anything. AI labs, AI-native startups and enterprise software companies have built Forward Deployed teams for that reason.

What a Forward Deployed Engineer does

  • Scopes the problem. Turns a vague request ("use AI on our claims") into something a system can actually do, and says no to the parts it cannot.
  • Builds on the customer's stack. Writes production code, mostly in Python or TypeScript, against the customer's APIs, data stores and identity systems.
  • Proves it works. Builds evaluation sets and monitoring so the customer can see quality, cost and latency, and so regressions are caught before users notice.
  • Feeds the product. Reports what broke in the field so the core product improves, which is why FDE teams sit close to product and engineering.

The skills that matter

We screen FDEs against six dimensions, and most hiring managers we work with use something similar:

  • Production engineering: code other people run and depend on, with tests and deployment.
  • LLM systems: retrieval, agents, tool use and structured outputs, and knowing when not to use them.
  • Evals and reliability: measuring quality instead of eyeballing it.
  • Data and integration: permissions, connectors, security reviews and messy data.
  • Customer judgment: prioritising, managing scope and earning trust with technical and non-technical people.
  • Communication and ownership: honest status, clear writing and follow-through.

Real FDE or re-titled role?

Because the title is fashionable, it is now attached to roles that are really pre-sales, solutions or support engineering. Those are valuable jobs, but they are different jobs. The quickest test is evidence: has the person written production code that runs inside a customer's environment, and can they explain what went wrong the last time they did it? If the honest answer is "I built the demo and handed it over", the profile is closer to a solutions engineer.

Career path

FDEs typically move into one of three directions: deeper engineering (staff or principal engineer on the core product), architecture (Forward Deployed or Applied AI Architect), or leadership (managing an FDE team, then heading the function). Many founders started as FDEs because the job teaches how customers actually buy and use software.

How much do Forward Deployed Engineers earn?

Pay sits at or above senior software engineering for the same level, because the role combines engineering depth with customer work. Base salaries vary widely by city and company stage.
