Evals - Beyond the Vibe Check | LangChain, Langfuse, Mercor, CoreWeave & Galileo

Forward Deployed · 2026-09-25 · 1 min read

Summary

A roundtable with eval practitioners from LangChain, Langfuse, Mercor, CoreWeave, and Galileo. LangChain reports roughly 89% of teams have observability in place, but only about a third act on the data. The discussion covers counterintuitive findings: online grading of agents can cost more than the agent itself, judge models fail in unnoticed ways, and one eval vendor advises shipping before writing evals. It also addresses manual trace reading and why the same agent takes a weekend internally but a year at a bank. Three of the featured companies were acquired in the past year.

Key points

  • 89% of teams have observability; only about a third use the data.
  • Galileo, Langfuse, and Weights & Biases were all acquired recently.
  • Grading agents while running can cost more than the agent.
  • Judge models are wrong in ways users never notice.
  • One vendor recommends shipping first and learning from breakage.

Full text

Everyone says they do evals. Almost nobody does. LangChain actually put a number on it: around 89% of teams have observability set up, and only about a third ever do anything with what it collects.

So I got five people who do this for a living in a room, from LangChain, Mercor, Galileo, Langfuse, and CoreWeave. Side note, three of those companies got acquired in the past year. Galileo to Cisco, Langfuse to ClickHouse, Weights & Biases to CoreWeave. The layer is getting bought up faster than most teams can figure out how to use it.

A few things I didn't expect going in. Grading an agent while it runs can cost you more than the agent does. The model you're using as a judge is probably wrong in ways you'll never notice. And the guy whose company sells eval tooling told the room to stop writing evals before shipping, just put it out and learn from what breaks.

We also got into the parts nobody blogs about. How many traces a person still has to read by hand. Why the same agent is a weekend project internally and a year of work at a bank. What happens when your users start doing things you never thought to test.

If you've shipped an agent and had no real way to know whether it was working, this one's worth your time!
