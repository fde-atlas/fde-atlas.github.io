Celonis FDE Role Analysis: Value Engineers and Deployment Engineers

Original title: Celonis FDE 岗位解析：价值工程师与部署工程师

FDE Times · 2026-11-25 · 5 min read

Summary

This article compares Celonis job postings and finds that the FDE title actually describes a value engineer role: owning the full customer lifecycle across landing, expansion, adoption, and renewal, and requiring native-level French or German. The hands-on technical work most engineers associate with FDEs sits under the Deployment Engineer title, which directs the Apollo agent to build data models, KPIs, and dashboards, and requires only English. The author advises reading posting descriptions and responsibility verbs carefully, learning PQL plus one SAP module, and verifying agent-generated numbers before trusting dashboards.

Key points

  • Celonis FDE postings describe value engineer work: land, expand, adopt, renew.
  • Deployment Engineer roles direct the Apollo agent to build data models and dashboards.
  • FDE roles require native French or German; deployment roles need only English.
  • Skills layer into SQL/Python, Celonis-specific PQL, and ERP process knowledge.
  • Engineers must verify agent output, e.g., row counts, before client meetings.

Key takeaways

  • The Senior FDE job posting in Paris calls the role a value engineer. Both it and the Munich FDE posting hand the full customer lifecycle to this role: landing, expansion, adoption, renewal.
  • The Deployment Engineer posting describes hands-on work: directing the Apollo agent to build data models, KPIs, and dashboards. It only requires English.

Celonis is hiring a "Senior Forward Deployed Engineer" in Paris. Read a few lines further down the posting, and the job changes its name: Value Engineer, the person responsible end to end for the customer value journey.

Meanwhile, the posting titled Deployment Engineer describes the work most engineers think of when they hear the letters FDE. The Lead Deployment Engineer posting says this role is directly responsible for deploying Celonis at customers. The Deployment Engineer posting assigns it the work of directing an AI agent to build data models and dashboards.

This matters because within the same company, two titles demand two different skill sets. Someone who picks a job by title alone may apply to the wrong role, prepare for the wrong interview, and only discover a year later that the job is completely different from what they expected.

At Celonis, the FDE title sits on the role that owns the full customer lifecycle

The Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) posting in Paris summarizes the responsibilities with four verbs: land, expand, adopt, renew. The "Forward Deployed Engineer – CPG/Retail" posting in Munich uses the same four words. This is the language of customer accounts, not of deployment projects.

The Munich posting never mentions the term value engineer. But the two postings use exactly the same verbs, indicating they describe the same type of role; Paris just spells out the name.

Expansion means broadening the customer's use of the product, and renewal means extending the contract. When these two words appear in a job description, it means the role is accountable for whether the customer stays and buys more—far beyond just getting the system running.

Celonis's own careers site confirms this understanding. An article introducing the value engineering team says value engineers are responsible end to end for the complete value journey of every Celonis customer.

Another article on the same site describes the daily work: creating demos, business cases, and value validation projects, translating the CEO's strategic goals into high-impact use cases.

The Paris posting is more specific about methods: the value engineer facilitates business value workshops, using Celonis to discover and validate process improvement opportunities.

The language requirements make the nature of the role very clear. The Paris posting requires native-level French; the Munich posting requires native-level German. Munich also explains why: this role facilitates workshops, presents to executives, and collaborates with stakeholders in German.

Deployment Engineers do the technical work

The Lead Deployment Engineer posting makes this role directly responsible for customer deployments, including facilitating requirements-gathering workshops. On the technical side, it requires SQL and Python. The enterprise resource planning (ERP) requirements are spelled out in detail: SAP MM/SD/PP, S/4HANA, APO/IBP, or comparable systems such as Oracle, Kinaxis, o9, and Blue Yonder.

The Deployment Engineer posting, published on Accel's hiring platform, requires Celonis's Process Query Language (PQL) in addition to SQL and Python, plus ERP knowledge.

The most telling difference is the language requirement. The Deployment Engineer posting only requires English, with other languages as a plus. For engineers who speak neither German nor French, this is the biggest distinction between the two titles.

When the agent builds the dashboards, your job is to verify

The Deployment Engineer posting states that the engineer directs Celonis's deployment agent, named Apollo, to build data models, process analyses, KPIs, and dashboards. If most of the building is done by the agent, the engineer's time shifts toward tasking the agent, reviewing its output, and explaining to the customer when the numbers go wrong.

Here is a hypothetical example. A customer has 1,000 order lines in SAP, with each order shipped in 2 deliveries. The agent doesn't group by order and joins the order table directly to the shipment table, yielding 2,000 rows. The on-time delivery KPI is then calculated per shipment rather than per order, yet the dashboard looks perfectly plausible.

An engineer who only knows how to write prompts for the agent will carry that number into the customer meeting. An engineer who understands data models will count the rows once before and once after the join, notice the number has doubled, and then ask why.

With a real customer, the first step is to verify a few numbers that business people know by heart—such as last month's total order count—before trusting any chart.

Workshops appear in the Lead posting and both FDE-titled postings; the difference lies in who sits across the table. Requirements-gathering workshops serve solution building; value workshops and executive presentations serve persuading the customer to keep investing.

What does process mining require of candidates?

Combining the three groups of postings, the skill requirements fall into three tiers. The base tier is SQL, Python, and the ability to build data models. The middle tier is PQL, a skill tightly bound to the Celonis product. The top tier is understanding how business processes run inside an ERP, from procurement and sales to production planning.

Backend engineers usually already have the base tier. If your German or French falls short of native level, the realistic path into Celonis is the Deployment Engineer role, which only requires English; before applying, learn PQL and study one SAP module in depth, such as MM or SD.

To reach the FDE title, you also need something technical documentation can't teach: facilitating value workshops in the customer's native language and the ability to build business cases.

Read job postings like you would read a contract

The first tip applies at any company: look for the role description in the body of the posting. Celonis puts "Value Engineer" right in the text of its Paris FDE posting; people who only look at the title will miss it.

Second, count the responsibility verbs. If the posting mentions building, data models, and dashboards, it's a deployment role. If it mentions expansion and renewals, ask the recruiter directly how success in this role is measured.

When applying for a Forward Deployed Engineer role, your resume should show that you know how to validate the output of automation. A good bullet point will make clear: what error you found in ERP data, how you found it, and what decision the client made because the numbers were correct.

At Celonis, the FDE title and deployment work belong to two different postings. Before applying, look past the title to see the actual work, then choose the role whose success criteria match how you want to be evaluated.
