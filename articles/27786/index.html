Today's Take

Today marks a day worth recording in the professionalization of the FDE role in China: the HICOOL Global Entrepreneurs Summit featured a dedicated "FDE Era: AI Industry Implementation" session and released a global FDE development research report—as a job category, the FDE has officially made it onto the agenda of a national-level entrepreneurship summit.

The consensus on-site shifted the primary challenges for Chinese FDEs from technology to three things: sustained business-side participation, the language gap between domain experts and AI engineers, and employees' passive resistance plus system/data breakpoints. The judgment on "standardization" was especially clear-eyed: it's not about turning every customer into the same product, but about shortening the second delivery for similar enterprises step by step from 30 days to 20, 15, 10, and 5 days.

On the same day, Shanghai Pudong advanced digital product launches into scenario-based supply-demand matchmaking with its "Digital Debut" initiative, while AWS's disclosed GoDaddy case provided a complete reference outcome: "governance first, a two-year migration, and agents embedded into existing workflows."

Key Signals

HICOOL launches an FDE session: China's FDE bottleneck shifts from technology to business participation, organizational resistance, and reuse speed

  • Source: on-site at the HICOOL Global Entrepreneurs Summit; People's Daily Overseas Edition report
  • On August 27, the HICOOL Summit featured the "FDE Era: AI Industry Implementation" session, including a keynote, two roundtables, talks by six guests, and the release of the global FDE development research report. The report presented on-site stated that 5,856 FDE and highly related job postings were filtered from nearly 20,000 listings (note: this is a research sample, not the global stock of such jobs).
  • Three key judgments from the on-site consensus: first, the primary difficulty in going from 0 to 1 is sustained business-side participation—without deep engagement from the business side, even the best engineers cannot obtain real business rules; second, there is a language gap between domain experts and AI engineers, and translating between these two languages is precisely the FDE's core value; third, employees' passive resistance and system/data breakpoints are harder obstacles than technology.
  • Why it matters: the discussion of FDEs in China has moved from "does this role exist" to "how to deliver, how to replicate, and how to be accountable for outcomes." The most valuable part is the definition of standardization—it's not about shaving all customers into the same set of requirements, but about shortening the second delivery for similar enterprises from 30 days to 20, 15, 10, and 5 days. This gives every FDE team a quantifiable self-check metric: is your "delivery reuse speed table" getting faster, or is every customer starting from scratch?

Shanghai's "Digital Debut": 8 digital companies move from product launches to scenario matchmaking

  • Source: Shanghai Municipal Government | Original link
  • On August 26, Pudong held the "Digital Chain Smart Manufacturing Debut in Pudong" event, where 8 companies collectively launched industrial software, AI computing power, high-quality data, intelligent agents, and energy digitalization solutions, and conducted on-site trials and cooperation matchmaking with scenario partners such as Sinosteel Engineering, Baosight Software, Humanoid Robot (Shanghai), and New Jinqiao Energy.
  • Why it matters: government industrial platforms are advancing from "showcasing results" to supply-demand matching and scenario validation—arranging scenario partner matchmaking directly at the launch event is a step beyond a simple product roadshow. But there is still a distance between "matchmaking" and "results": the official page did not disclose the number of trialing enterprises, project contracts, or adoption rates. This gap is exactly where the FDE fits: scenario definition, trial samples, access and data, acceptance criteria, and the translation work between vendors and clients—the government has built the stage, but who is responsible for turning "trials" into "results"?

GoDaddy: two years of governance-driven migration and embedded agents turn analytics from centralized queuing into self-service for everyone

  • Source: AWS official blog | Original link
  • AWS disclosed on August 26: GoDaddy completed its legacy BI platform migration in about two years, streamlining dashboards from 5,000+ down to 2,532, and reducing rendering time from up to 15 minutes to under 5 seconds. AI results: 7 custom chat agents generated nearly 2,000 queries in a single month, with an estimated annual savings of about 6,000 hours; all automation combined is estimated to save 15,000+ hours annually (a combined figure, including platform migration benefits).
  • Why it matters: the order of lessons from this case is clear—governance first, delete redundant assets, start from the biggest pain point, and embed agents into employees' existing decision points. Cutting nearly half of 5,000 dashboards was homework done before AI, not a result achieved after AI. GoDaddy also offers an important lesson: "adoption is a pull problem, not a push problem"—solve real team problems and usage will grow naturally; tools driven by administrative push drop to zero the moment the push stops. Note: the hours saved are customer/AWS estimates, and the case spanned two years—short-term replication should not be expected.

Takeaways for FDE Practitioners

  • Build a "delivery reuse speed" ledger: track the diagnosis, development, testing, and launch days for the 1st/2nd/3rd deliveries of similar scenarios, plus the proportion of reused assets. This is the core metric of an FDE team's ability to scale—if the second delivery isn't faster than the first, nothing has been accumulated.
  • Engineering acceptance and business acceptance are two separate checklists: all-green on functionality/permissions/migration/regression only qualifies you for a trial; continuous reruns with real users, real data, and real environments plus sign-off are what constitute production evidence. "Tests passed" and "business go-live" are two different things.
  • Subtract before adding AI: learn from GoDaddy—govern first, streamline first, find the biggest pain point first, then talk about agents. Layering AI on top of 5,000 chaotic dashboards only generates chaos faster.
  • Government-enterprise matchmaking events are opportunity grounds for FDEs: what government supply-demand platforms lack is precisely the role that "turns trials into results"—scenario definition, acceptance design, and translation between both parties are all billable professional services.
