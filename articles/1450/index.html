Scoping and Solution Architecture for AI Systems Projects

Educative · 2026-06-13 · 1 min read

Summary

This Educative lesson explains how a Forward Deployed Engineer turns discovery findings into a scope document and selects an appropriate AI architecture before building. The scope document is a written agreement between the FDE and the customer covering the POC phase—typically two to four weeks—defining what will be built, success metrics, data sources, dependencies, and risks. The author argues that fixing scope before the build is far cheaper than correcting mid-sprint, and that projects with clear scope documents rarely fail from misalignment.

Key points

  • Scoping converts discovery's problem definition into a concrete proposal.
  • A scope document is a shared FDE-customer agreement, not a product spec.
  • It covers the POC phase, typically two to four weeks.
  • Six elements: problem, success metrics, data sources, dependencies, risks, and boundaries.
  • Well-scoped projects fail for other, easier-to-fix reasons than misalignment.

Full text

Understand how to translate discovery findings into a detailed scope document that outlines the problem, success metrics, data sources, dependencies, and risks. Explore selecting the appropriate AI architecture and managing technical constraints to ensure alignment between the Forward Deployed Engineer and customer, enabling effective proof of concept delivery.

Discovery ends with a clear problem definition. Scoping converts that definition into a proposal. The proposal answers the following questions: what to build, which architecture fits the problem, and which risks need to be addressed before implementation begins. The scope document records those decisions and gives the FDE and the customer a concrete baseline to agree on before work starts.

Getting the scope right before the build begins is far cheaper than correcting it mid-sprint. A project with a well-written scope document rarely fails because of misalignment. It fails for other reasons, and those are far easier to address.

What a scope document does

A scope document is a shared agreement between the FDE and the customer. Its purpose is alignment. Both parties need to understand the same problem, the same success condition, and the same boundaries before the build begins. Without that agreement in writing, every conversation about progress introduces a new layer of interpretation.

A scope document is not a product specification. A product specification describes a future vision across months or quarters. A scope document describes what will be built in the next two to four weeks, what it will demonstrate, and how success will be measured. The scope document covers the proof of concept (POC) phase.
