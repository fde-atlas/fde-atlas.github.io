Summary

This article from FDE総研 argues that the outlook for Forward Deployed Engineers is strong, citing as evidence that Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic all actively recruit for the role, showing demand beyond a single company. It identifies four demand drivers: moving generative AI from PoC to production, corporate demand for in-house capability rather than outsourcing, the scarcity of engineers combining implementation and communication skills, and high agency. Does FDE have strong future prospects?

As far as current hiring trends show, FDE has strong future prospects, and demand will expand as production deployment of generative AI progresses.

Companies want to use generative AI in actual operations, and they want to build it in-house rather than leaving it entirely to outsourcing—as these two needs intensify simultaneously, the value of FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers), who can get into the field and carry work through to implementation, is rising. The current situation is that the bottleneck lies not in the technology itself, but in the process of turning technology into field results.

What is the basis for saying FDE has strong future prospects?

The basis is the fact that, in addition to Palantir, OpenAI and Anthropic are actually posting FDE job openings.

An FDE is an engineer who personally handles everything from workflow design to implementation and testing at the customer's site (Palantir official blog). This role, which consistently covers everything through "building and delivering," was established by Palantir and has in recent years spread to hiring at generative AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic.

When discussing future prospects, the most reliable evidence is "whether more companies are paying money for that role." The fact that job openings exist not just at one company for its particular circumstances but across multiple companies is itself one piece of evidence showing expanding demand. It can be said that FDE is at the stage of becoming established in the market as a job title, beyond just an internal Palantir designation.

What is behind the growing demand?

Behind it are two currents: the spread of generative AI and the need for internalization—companies wanting to stop relying on outsourcing.

Generative AI has reached the point where it works if you try it, but turning it into results in daily operations is a different kind of difficulty. Capturing vague on-site problems, translating them into working software, and seeing it through until it is actually used—people who can handle this entire sequence are still rare in the market.

Precisely because technology has become accessible, the differentiating point has shifted from "being able to use it" to "mastering it and turning it into results." There is a gap between the field and results that tool proficiency alone cannot close, and FDEs are increasingly sought after as the role that closes that gap with their own hands.

What assumptions are needed to avoid overestimating future prospects?

As a premise, there are no public statistics comprehensively covering the number of FDE job openings, and the basis for judgment is limited to the fact of each company's job postings.

What this article can show goes only as far as the verifiable fact that Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic are actually hiring FDEs. Predictions like "the market will grow X times in Y years" will not be written here, since no supporting figures can be confirmed. When discussing future prospects, it is safe to read by separating verifiable facts from interpretations drawn from them.

One more point: demand existing and anyone being able to enter the field are separate matters. Even if job openings increase, the required standard—the combination of implementation skill and dialogue skill—does not go down. Strong future prospects do not directly mean it is easy to get in. These two need to be considered separately.

The reasons for expanding demand can be organized into the four factors in the table below, all of which ultimately come down to the scarcity of talent.

Even among "engineers," code alone or dialogue alone cannot fully meet the field needs of the generative AI era. Precisely because people who can move back and forth between both are few, the market value of FDEs is rising.

How do the spread of generative AI and internalization needs have an effect?

The shift in corporate interest from "introduction" to "establishment and internalization" is a direct tailwind for FDE demand.

Many companies stumble in production operation even after succeeding in proof-of-concept (PoC). Between a working demo and a system someone uses every day lie unglamorous processes: adjusting business workflows, handling exceptions, and handing over operations.

What is needed there is talent that gets into the field, builds while making things stick, and ultimately transfers the know-how to the company. This role is exactly FDE's area of strength, and the more companies commit to internalization, the longer demand is expected to continue. An approach to avoiding getting stuck at the PoC stage is also organized in How to Introduce Generative AI.

Why is talent that can do "implementation × dialogue" rare?

Because implementation skill and dialogue skill require different kinds of training, few people possess both at a high level.

FDE's strength lies in combining implementation skill with the dialogue skill to dig into problems with customers. Even in Anthropic's FDE job posting, strong communication skills for conducting discovery with customers are listed as a requirement alongside high implementation skill (Anthropic official job posting).

OpenAI's Tokyo office also has FDE openings, and the spread of such postings is evidence of demand (OpenAI official). While there are a certain number of people with only one of the two skills in the hiring market, the supply of people who can move between both is thin, and that gap translates directly into scarcity value.

Why is high agency valued?

Because high agency is the evaluation axis that determines whether you can be entrusted with unprecedented, ambiguous projects.

On your first day in the field, problems are almost never laid out and waiting for you. Who is the decision-maker, which operations are truly stuck, what should you tackle first to have something to show a week later—you need to decide these yourself and start moving.

With a wait-for-instructions mindset, you cannot cross this starting point. Conversely, if you are someone who can move forward amid ambiguity, companies can entrust you with unprecedented projects outright. If implementation skill and dialogue skill determine "what you can do," then high agency is the factor that determines "whether you can be entrusted with things"—and demand concentrates on talent that has all three.

Will FDEs be replaced by AI?

FDEs are not on the side that gets replaced by AI; they are a role whose value rises as generative AI becomes more powerful.

FDE is not a role that gets replaced by AI, but a role that masters AI to deliver results. The more powerful the tools become, the more important are the people who apply them to on-the-ground challenges and turn them into value.

Why is FDE hard to replace with AI?

It is hard to replace because the role involves defining ambiguous problems, reaching agreement with customers, and carrying solutions through to adoption.

What generative AI excels at is work with a clear objective and clear inputs. The starting point of an FDE's work, however, lies in a state where "what to build has not yet been decided." Articulating unspoken on-the-ground frustrations as concrete problems, setting priorities with the customer, and taking responsibility all the way to putting what was built into actual operations—this process is the judgment of the side that gives instructions, and it does not disappear even as tools improve.

How do the stages AI excels at differ from the stages FDEs handle?

Generative AI is strong at tasks with clear objectives and inputs, while FDEs get involved from the stage where the objective itself is undecided.

The difference lies in where the stages they handle sit. Generative AI is a tool that accelerates what happens after you are told "build this." An FDE's work begins with deciding what that "this" should be, and ends with putting the finished product into operations so it keeps being used.

In other words, generative AI speeds up the middle implementation stage, while the stages before and after—problem definition and adoption on the ground—remain the FDE's responsibility. The more tools compress the middle, the relatively greater the weight the surrounding stages carry in determining results.

How does the evolution of generative AI affect FDEs?

The evolution of generative AI is not a threat to FDEs; it is a tailwind that gives them more tools for delivering results.

Faster implementation means more ideas can be tested in the same period, shortening the cycle of delivering to the field and observing reactions. An FDE's value is measured not by lines of code written but by how much the customer's operations changed, so the more efficient implementation becomes, the more time can be devoted to that core value. That is why the evolution of generative AI works to strengthen the FDE's position.

What kinds of careers can it lead to?

FDE experience opens up multiple paths, including tech lead, product or business roles, technical advisory, and going independent.

Experience combining both implementation skill and customer understanding is rare and valuable no matter which path you take. The ability to "build and deliver" is highly transferable, so career options rarely narrow.

What paths does FDE experience lead to?

After cycling between implementation and customer delivery as a field FDE, paths open up to tech lead, business roles, technical advisory, and independence.

FDE's strength is that it is not confined to a single specialty. Engineers focused only on implementation tend to struggle with technology becoming obsolete, while dialogue-only roles tend to struggle with reproducibility of results. FDEs who have cycled between both carry less of either risk and are well positioned to deliver value even when circumstances change. In an era where assumptions shift as fast as they do with generative AI, this "change-resilient experience" proves especially valuable.

What should you do to boost your future prospects?

The fastest way to boost your future prospects is to run implementation and customer delivery in parallel and build up solid experience in "building and delivering."

Avoiding imbalance between the two is key to cultivating rare value. Even if small, build something yourself, deliver it to the field, observe reactions, and refine it—the more you repeat this cycle, the more implementation skill, dialogue skill, and high agency are forged together.

Conversely, polishing only implementation will not grow your experience grasping problems with customers, and making only proposals will not develop your ability to see things through to completion. Continually trying new ways to use generative AI in the field is also effective for maintaining market value. Even within your current company, you can pick one internal operational problem, build a solution yourself, and see it through to adoption—this format lets you accumulate experience.
