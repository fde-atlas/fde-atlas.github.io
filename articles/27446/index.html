Today's Judgment

Enterprise AI is leaving the "demo" stage, and two productionization moves are happening simultaneously:

Platform side—Huawei Cloud AgentArts turns runtime, traces, evals, memory, optimization, and cost into a complete control plane, transforming agents from "development objects" into "governed runtime objects"; Industry side—OpenAI released Astra for Law, using the six-piece toolkit of "general model + professional index + industry instructions + enterprise knowledge + private evals + governance controls" to transform a general model into legal industry infrastructure, explicitly built jointly by its forward deployed engineering team with top law firms.

Policy context is advancing in parallel: the National Advanced Manufacturing Development Conference proposed deepening "AI + manufacturing." For FDEs, true completion is not the number of scenarios, but that evidence status, operational responsibility, and customer handover are all clearly documented.

Key Signals

Huawei Cloud AgentArts: Control plane completed across "runtime—traces—evals—memory—optimization—cost"

  • Source: Huawei Cloud product updates | Original link
  • Huawei Cloud's September 17 product updates show that AgentArts, in public beta, added/upgraded: cloud runtime instances, agent count and invocation/Token dashboards, trace analysis, tool/Skill/model optimization, lifecycle configuration, secure cloud browser, memory reranking and procedural memory; it also includes third-party agent evals, eval tags, Token efficiency assessment, and a Python SDK for third-party Metric/Trace integration.
  • Why it matters: Domestic platforms are turning agents from development objects into governed runtime objects—following Baidu and Tencent Cloud, Huawei Cloud has also made the control plane (runtime, traces, evals, memory, cost) a flagship capability. FDE delivery checklists should be upgraded in sync: instance specs, invocations and Tokens, failed traces, eval sets, memory versions, optimization recommendations, change approvals, and rollback conditions—a delivery checklist that only lists models and features has fallen behind platform capabilities. Note: these are public beta product features, not customer production results.

OpenAI releases Astra for Law: FDE team builds industry infrastructure jointly with top law firms

  • Source: OpenAI official | Original link
  • On September 17, OpenAI released Astra for Law: combining GPT-6 Astra with a legal retrieval index (covering over 230 million URLs, integrating CourtListener which covers 99.9% of published US case law) and legal analysis/writing instructions. Private validation set evals: on 200 US legal research questions, overall correctness was 54.0%, a 40% relative improvement over the 38.7% of "general model + web search." Key detail: OpenAI explicitly stated that its Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) team built agreement analysis, M&A due diligence, and IPO preparation tools jointly with law firms including Sullivan & Cromwell, Ropes & Gray, and Cooley, embedding the firms' own precedents, working methods, permissions, and review processes into the enterprise edition.
  • Why it matters: This is the official demonstration of the "vertical industry AI six-piece toolkit": general model → professional index/data → industry instructions and tools → enterprise proprietary knowledge and processes → private eval sets → permission governance and human review. Model capability is only the foundation; industry delivery capability comes from combining and continuously validating the other five layers. Equally important is the division-of-labor signal: even OpenAI needs its FDE team embedded on-site to co-build before entering law firm workflows—the last mile of industry specialization cannot be completed via API. Note: the 54.0% comes from a private validation set and was published by the vendor; even at the highest configuration, correctness is only around half—retaining human lawyer review in legal scenarios is not optional.

Policy context: On September 16-17, the National Advanced Manufacturing Development Conference proposed deepening "AI + manufacturing" and guiding enterprises' digital and intelligent transformation (MIIT)—the national direction for manufacturing AI continues to strengthen; project lists and acceptance results remain to be tracked.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Manage every project with the "four-way delivery status classification": any deliverable may only be in one of four states—"delivered and re-verified, historically verified, proposed/pending implementation, debugging not closed"—and status changes must be accompanied by new evidence; they cannot be promoted through copywriting. Writing "passed testing in the development environment" as "launched in the customer environment" is the fastest leak in delivery credibility.
  • Build a "nine-column delivery evidence table": asset/version/location → current status → original test evidence → re-verification in the customer's target environment → actual users → customer sign-off → maintenance responsibility → recovery drills → public disclosure boundaries. Any efficiency, accuracy, or launch conclusion must be traceable back to this table—this is also the qualification threshold for a project to become a "benchmark case."
  • Price industry solutions by the six-piece toolkit: apply the template directly to professional scenarios like legal, tax, and healthcare—model selection accounts for only a small portion of the quote; the professional index, private evals, and governance design are where the bulk of the value lies.
  • 54% correctness is an industry-level reminder: even the strongest model with the most complete index achieves only half correctness on legal research—human review and responsibility design in high-risk industries are always part of delivery, not an interim solution.
