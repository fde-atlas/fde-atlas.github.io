Summary

This Japanese article aggregates salary ranges for Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) from job postings in Japan and the US, while cautioning that these are advertised ranges, not actual pay, and definitions vary on overtime and bonuses. Japanese AI companies like LayerX (12M yen+) and JDSC (5M–25M yen) mostly advertise ranges above 10 million yen. The article attributes high pay to scarcity: per Financial Times, FDE job postings grew over 800% year-over-year in 2025 while candidates grew only about 50%. It advises building both implementation skills and customer-facing problem definition to reach the top of ranges.

Are FDE Salaries Really High?

They tend to be high. Among domestic FDE-related job postings, multiple companies list expected annual salaries exceeding ¥10 million. However, note that these are "expected salaries" stated in job postings, not statistics on actual pay.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role that brings products into customers' environments and delivers results while implementing them. It requires both software engineering and customer engagement skills, and while many people are strong in one or the other, those who meet both at a high level are limited. This scarcity forms the basis of the high offered ranges. The definition of the role itself is covered in What Is an FDE? The Definition, Skills, and Career of a New Role Originated at Palantir.

Why Are Expected Salaries in Job Postings Only "Guidelines"?

Because expected salary is the range a company can offer at hiring, which is different from the actual pay determined by grade, evaluation, and negotiation.

The expected salary in a job posting shows the upper and lower bounds a company can offer at hiring. The actual amount paid is determined by the grade set at joining, subsequent evaluations, and negotiations during offer meetings, so you will not necessarily receive the top of the range. All figures covered in this article are aggregates based on job postings and do not represent the distribution of actual pay—please read them with that premise in mind.

Furthermore, expected salaries may or may not include fixed overtime pay and estimated bonuses, and definitions are not standardized across companies. Keeping the following points in mind will help you avoid misreading the numbers.

Points to checkWhy they matter
Whether fixed overtime pay is includedIf included, the effective base salary is lower than the stated amount
Whether estimated bonuses are includedIf performance-linked, the amount paid varies by year
Which grade the top of the range assumesIf it assumes senior or lead roles, it won't be reached right after joining

When comparing ranges across multiple companies side by side, keep in mind that "they may not be measured on the same scale," which will keep you from being misled by the numbers. Please treat this article as well as a sourced aggregate meant to give you a sense of the market.

Where Does FDE Scarcity Come From?

Because few people meet both implementation skills and customer engagement at a high level, employers offer high ranges.

As production deployment of generative AI spreads, demand grows for "people who can build things and also define customer problems." There are many engineers who can only implement and many consultants who can only organize requirements, but FDEs stand at the intersection of the two. Employers actively offering high ranges is what pushes up the market rate.

This scarcity is also directly tied to how hard the role is to develop. Both implementation experience and customer-facing experience require accumulation in the field, and it is difficult to fill either one through short-term training alone. The priority of required skills is covered in detail in What Skills Does an FDE Need? The Priority of Skills That Really Work in the Field.

What Are Domestic FDE Salary Ranges?

Based on job postings, the core band exceeds ¥10 million. Ranges span from a lower bound of ¥5 million to an upper bound of ¥25 million. The source is each company's job posting, not actual pay.

How Should the Lower and Upper Ends of a Range Be Read?

The most realistic interpretation is that the lower end reflects mid-level roles and the upper end assumes senior or lead roles.

What stands out in the table is the breadth of JDSC's ¥5–25 million range. It shows that even within the same job posting, the offered amount can vary nearly fivefold depending on grade and experience. This does not mean "any FDE gets ¥25 million"—rather, it should be read as a design where the range of roles being recruited is broad, and depth of experience is directly reflected in the offered amount.

On the other hand, there are postings with high lower bounds, such as LayerX's ¥12 million and up or Stockmark's ¥10 million and up. In these cases, the recruitment is presumably premised on implementation experience above a certain level, suggesting that the entry level itself is high for this role. How to find openings and trends among hiring companies are covered in Where Are FDE Jobs? The Reality of Palantir-Style Hiring Companies and Selection Processes.

How much do FDEs earn in the US?

US salaries are even higher, with Palantir FDSE total compensation reported at a median of $211,000. Despite differences in exchange rates and cost of living, this far exceeds domestic figures in absolute terms.

How much is Palantir FDSE total compensation?

Total compensation is $171,000–$295,000 (median $211,000), and at the senior level it is reported to be in the $400,000–$500,000 range.

According to figures cited by SB Bit as levels.fyi data, Palantir FDSE compensation levels can be summarized as follows (SB Bit).

CategoryCompensation level
Palantir FDSE (overall)$171,000–$295,000 (median $211,000)
Palantir FDSE (senior)$400,000–$500,000 range

"Total compensation" here includes base salary plus stock compensation and bonuses. Note that its structure is fundamentally different from the expected annual salary shown in Japanese job postings.

Stock compensation is valued at the time of grant, and the actual amount received varies depending on the vesting period and the stock price during that time. Therefore, the median figure of $211,000 does not mean "that amount arrives in cash every year"; it is best read as a reference value combining base salary with future stock and bonuses. The difficulty of comparing it directly with domestic expected annual salaries is largely due to this difference in compensation structure.

How large is the gap between junior and senior?

Juniors earn $180,000–$250,000, while seniors earn $400,000–$630,000—more than double at the top end.

Job-change media aggregations also list the following levels (Mynavi).

Experience bandCompensation level
Junior$180,000–$250,000
Senior$400,000–$630,000

Two points stand out. First, the junior entry level is already high, starting at $180,000. Second, at the senior level the lower bound is $400,000, well above the junior upper limit. Between these two bands, a structure emerges in which accumulated experience translates directly into compensation. Conversely, this also means that years of experience alone will not reach the top band; consistently delivering results is the deciding factor.

Why do FDEs earn such high salaries?

Because demand far outstrips supply. Candidate growth has not kept pace with the surge in job openings. Two factors—scarcity and performance-based pay—underpin the market rate.

How different is the growth in job openings versus candidates?

Job openings grew more than 800% year over year, while candidates grew only about 50%, and this supply-demand gap is pushing up offered salaries.

The UK's Financial Times reported that in 2025, FDE-related job openings grew more than 800% year over year, while candidates grew only about 50% (SB Bit introduces the related statistics). While job openings swelled roughly eightfold, the talent pool grew only about 1.5-fold.

This growth of over 800% reflects the moment when generative AI adoption is shifting from the trial stage to production operation, creating a sudden need for people who can implement solutions while embedding them in the field. Developing such talent requires accumulated experience in both implementation and customer engagement, making it hard to increase the candidate pool quickly; the supply-demand gap is therefore unlikely to resolve soon. This structure is also why market rates are likely to remain high for the time being.

What changes when evaluation is based on "customer outcomes"?

Because performance is measured not by lines of code written but by how much customer problems were advanced, results translate directly into compensation.

FDE evaluation is not based on the number of features implemented or lines of code. It is placed on outcomes: how much the customer's business moved forward, how much faster decision-making became. This is why talented people who deliver results see their compensation grow more easily and are more likely to reach the top of the range.

In other words, implementation skills alone or customer-facing skills alone rarely reach the top—that is the hidden condition behind high salaries. If someone can implement but cannot define customer problems themselves, their evaluation stops at meeting specified requirements. If someone is good at customer dialogue but cannot build things themselves, the time to results depends on others. Understanding the top of the offered range as compensation for being able to handle both alone will sharpen your career planning.

How can you raise your salary as an FDE?

The shortest path is to build on implementation skills and hone the ability to define customer problems and turn them into results. The more balanced these two wheels are, the closer you get to the top of the offered range.

How do you balance implementation skills and customer engagement?

Securing the entry point through implementation first, then adding customer engagement experience, is a realistic and reproducible sequence.

With SWE implementation experience, you are more likely to get favorable terms from the start. Domestic job postings with high lower bounds, such as LayerX at 12 million yen and up or Stockmark at 10 million yen and up, are designed with this premise. Adding customer engagement experience brings you closer to the profile that can aim for the top of the range, such as JDSC at 25 million yen or Loglass at 20 million yen.

If you lean toward one side, deliberately compensating for the missing side will also pay off in salary terms. For implementation-oriented people: enter settings where you define problems directly with customers rather than receiving requirements handed to you. For engagement-oriented people: gain at least one experience of building a working product yourself. These two are concrete moves for moving from the middle of the range to the top.

How do you turn job posting figures into actual take-home pay?

Accumulating "customer outcomes" after joining is the essential condition for converting expected annual salary into actual take-home pay.

The expected annual salary in a job posting is merely a guideline at the entry point. As noted above, actual pay is determined by grade, evaluation, and negotiation, so at the offer stage it is worth concretely confirming where your experience falls within the range. After that, how much customer-side outcome you can accumulate after joining is what moves your next grade and offered amount.
