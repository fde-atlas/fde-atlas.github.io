Summary

Citing LinkedIn data, it says FDE hiring grew 42x over two years, far outpacing AI engineers (13x), and global FDE job postings rose 800% in 2025. This article introduces FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), an emerging AI role with surging demand and generous pay. FDEs are the key link between large AI models and specific business scenarios, requiring technical skills, product thinking, and communication abilities to turn AI technology into real business productivity. It's a great fit for engineers and product managers with ToB experience, product thinking, and strong communication skills. FDE is an important career direction in the AI era with broad market prospects.

Recently, one role in the AI world has been blowing up.

LinkedIn released a report in early 2026 saying that hiring for this role grew 42-fold over the past two years. What does that mean? AI engineer roles grew only 13-fold over the same period.

It's called FDE — Forward Deployed Engineer.

ByteDance is hiring, Tencent is hiring, and so is Zhipu. Early this year, Shanghai even launched the country's first FDE-focused training program.

Screenshot from a job platform

But what exactly does this role do? Why is it suddenly so hot? Who is it for?

1. FDEs Are the Ones Who Tackle AI's "Last Mile"

No matter how smart a large model is, it doesn't understand your company's specific business scenarios. FDEs exist to solve exactly this problem.

They're not pure programmers, not salespeople, and not customer support either — it's a three-in-one role:

FDE = a technically skilled engineer + a product manager who understands requirements + a delivery expert who can work on-site

Tencent's careers site puts it plainly: "Dive deep into industry scenarios, break down vague requirements into executable technical roadmaps, lead requirements analysis, technology selection, and architecture design, and deliver actionable AI solutions."

Shanghai Chuangzhi College put it even more precisely when launching its training program: they are both technology understanders and requirement translators; both an extension of R&D and the ones who drive real-world implementation.

In plain terms, it's about making AI stop being a "showpiece" and actually help businesses get real work done.

2. How Hot Is This Role? Just Look at the Money

LinkedIn data shows that over the past two years, companies added at least 1.3 million AI-related roles, and FDE is the fastest-growing of them all.

Looking at China:

  • ByteDance is hiring a "Doubao AI Large Model FDE" with a monthly salary of 35,000–70,000 RMB, 15 months' pay per year, up to 1.05 million annually.
  • Ant Digital Technologies is hiring a B2B FDE with a monthly salary of 40,000–60,000 RMB, also 15 months' pay.
  • Zhipu AI is hiring an FDE lead with a monthly salary of 60,000–80,000 RMB.
  • Tencent has opened FDE positions in both Wuhan and Shenzhen.

Screenshot from a job platform

Silicon Valley is even more extreme:

  • OpenAI offers FDEs an annual salary of $162,000–$280,000 (about 1.1–1.9 million RMB), plus equity.
  • Anthropic goes even higher: $200,000–$300,000 (about 1.36–2.03 million RMB).

Global FDE job postings grew 800% year-over-year in 2025 alone.

Now even traditional companies have started hiring FDEs — building their own teams and implementing AI in-house.

3. Why Do Big Tech Companies Desperately Need This Role?

The logic is actually pretty simple.

In the past, the large model competition was about "who has more parameters and higher benchmark scores." But things have changed — model capabilities are converging, and everyone's performance is fairly similar now.

Tan Yinliang, a professor at CEIBS, said something I find spot-on: "Technical leadership is becoming just the entry ticket. What really determines who wins the second half is whether you can turn models into productivity systems for enterprises."

Translated into plain language: having a good gun isn't enough — you also need someone to teach customers how to fight with it.

Big tech companies have discovered a problem: many enterprises bought AI but have no idea how to integrate it with their own business. How to feed the data? How to change the processes? How should employees use it? It's all a minefield.

If you only sell APIs, you quickly fall into a price war — you're cheap, and I'll be cheaper. But with FDEs, you're not just selling a model; you're selling the ability to "get things done for you," and that's far more valuable.

That's why the giants are spending big money to poach people — what they're fighting over is exactly this group of people who can help customers truly "get things done."

4. Who Is Suited to Transition into FDE?

Based on Tencent's hiring requirements and industry practice, these three types of people have the best shot:

Type one: Engineers with ToB project experience.

Tencent requires 3+ years of ToB delivery experience. When facing enterprise clients, requirements are always vague and complex — anyone who hasn't ground through requirements with clients simply can't handle it.

Type two: Technical people with product thinking.

There's a practitioner named Yasha who was originally a product manager for 8 years, then moved into development, and became an FDE two years ago. She said something I really like: the FDE role fully combines a product manager's business experience with a programmer's technical skills — you don't have to give up the accumulation from either side.

In plain terms, your past work wasn't wasted — both skill sets come into play.

Type three: "T-shaped talent" who can work on-site and communicate well.

FDEs need to be stationed at client sites long-term. What's it like there? Aging equipment, frustrated workers, noisy data — all messy problems you won't find in textbooks.

Tencent's job requirements include an interesting line: "Able to discuss ROI with non-technical executives."

Translated: you need to be able to explain, in language bosses understand, whether AI is actually worth it.

In its curriculum design, Shanghai Chuangzhi College summarizes the FDE talent profile as: versatile talent combining technical ability, industry knowledge, and implementation experience.

It sounds a bit vague, but translated it means: able to write code, understand the industry, and comfortable dealing with people.

5. Final Thoughts

For ordinary engineers and product managers, FDE may be one of our best paths to making money in the AI era.

It's hard for most of us to become top-tier algorithm engineers — that's one in ten thousand. But FDE only requires understanding the business, communicating well, and knowing how to implement — all skills that can be trained.

In the future, most enterprises will need FDEs to help them implement AI. We can join big tech companies to serve top clients, become in-house experts at traditional companies, or even start a small studio helping nearby small and medium-sized businesses deploy AI.

The market is big enough — the giants can't handle all the work themselves.

Honestly, FDE is demanding work, but it will most likely pay well.
