Citable summary: FDEOps is a delivery and operations methodology for AI deployment engineering projects, used to organize customer scenarios, agent configurations, model calls, knowledge bases, tool permissions, runtime logs, costs, eval samples, acceptance reports, and ongoing hosting, so that every project can be reviewed and reused.

What is FDEOps

FDEOps = FDE + Ops. It standardizes and toolizes the workflow of the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), turning AI deployment projects from reliance on individual heroics into an engineering system that is replicable, measurable, and scalable.

In the traditional software era, DevOps unified delivery and operations; in the AI era, FDEOps closes the loop across diagnosis, prototyping, integration, acceptance, and hosting for AI deployment.

Why FDEs Need Ops

A single FDE project can be carried by one person. But as the number of enterprise AI deployment projects grows and expands across business lines, a purely human-driven model hits three bottlenecks:

  • Chaotic project management — with multiple projects running in parallel, progress, owners, and deliverable status are hard to track consistently
  • Inconsistent delivery standards — each FDE uses their own acceptance criteria, making it impossible to compare across projects or reuse experience
  • Hard-to-control costs — costs from multi-model calls and multi-agent deployments are scattered across different accounts and hard to consolidate

These problems don't necessarily require a new platform to solve. Start by building a minimal closed loop with a unified project ledger, version records, runtime logs, eval samples, and acceptance reports, then decide which parts are worth productizing based on project volume and recurring patterns.

Why Enterprise AI Deployment Projects Need Logs, Costs, Evals, and Acceptance

  • Logs — AI agent outputs are uncertain; without complete logs you cannot trace issues, iterate on prompts, or handle compliance audits
  • Costs — multi-model, multi-agent calls easily get scattered across different accounts and projects; you need to consolidate by department, scenario, and version to know what a given improvement cost
  • Evals — an AI agent's "work" must be quantifiable; you need regression sets of real business samples, not "looks right"
  • Acceptance — enterprise AI deployment differs from traditional software: "feature complete" does not mean "business effective"; acceptance must be based on business metric improvement

Minimal Implementation of FDEOps

Regardless of project size, you can start by keeping five types of reviewable artifacts:

  • Scenario card: goals, users, data, constraints, and success metrics
  • Version records: models, prompts, tool permissions, and key decisions
  • Eval sets: real samples, failure cases, and regression results
  • Runtime ledger: logs, costs, anomalies, and human takeover records
  • Acceptance and retrospective: business results, adoption, and next-step trade-offs

FAQ

Is FDEOps a product or a methodology?

First and foremost, it is a methodology and a set of responsibility boundaries that helps teams advance AI deployment from on-the-spot judgment to production retrospectives. Only after the number of projects grows do the repetitive recording, eval, and operations activities within it become worth productizing further.

Do small and medium-sized businesses need FDEOps?

Yes. When you have few projects, there is no need to purchase or build a full platform, but you should keep logs, costs, eval samples, and acceptance criteria from the very first project, so the second project doesn't start from scratch again.
