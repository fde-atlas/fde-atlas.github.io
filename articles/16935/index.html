Salary, Hiring Companies, and Required Skills

Riding the wave of the generative AI boom, job openings for FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) are surging in the Japanese market in 2026. Which companies are hiring, what salaries are offered, and what skills are required—this article systematically organizes the latest hiring trends.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineering role that embeds on-site with customers and directly solves business problems through technical expertise. The role originated at Palantir Technologies, and its defining characteristic is that it is a hybrid of "engineer" and "consultant", taking end-to-end ownership from requirements definition through design, implementation, and deployment while working alongside the customer.

While a typical software engineer develops the company's own products at headquarters, an FDE is stationed at the customer's company, implementing and running the company's products on the spot to solve business problems.

Behind the rapid expansion of this role lies a structural reality of generative AI. While LLMs and agent platforms are extremely versatile, a large amount of implementation and alignment work is required before they can be adapted to a customer's specific business data, policies, and workflows. Demand for FDEs is growing as the role that fills this "last mile of AI implementation."

If you would like to learn more about the definition and responsibilities of the FDE role itself, please also see What is an FDE? A New Engineer Profile That Completes AI Implementation in the Field.

In the Japanese market in 2026, FDE job openings have increased significantly year over year. One research effort collected and analyzed 26 job postings from Japanese companies and 9 from foreign companies, 35 in total, and Indeed currently shows 277 Forward Deployed Engineer job listings.

Analyzing the contents of these job postings reveals clear trends. FDEs fall broadly into three types, and the "Builder" type—building AI systems at customer sites and deploying them to production—accounts for 81% of Japanese and 100% of foreign postings. In other words, nearly all FDE job openings in Japan are positions that "actually write production code." Beyond design and proposals, implementation skills that deliver working products are required.

Companies hiring include OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, Sierra, Cohere, Adobe, Salesforce, UiPath, Telexistence, and Firecrawl. Palantir currently operates its FDE roles along two tracks—"Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE)" and "Deployment Strategist"—and its Japanese entity is recruiting for similar positions as well.

Domestic AI and SaaS companies such as ExaWizards, LayerX, AI Shift, and ANDPAD have begun hiring for FDE positions. Also included are a CyberAgent subsidiary, SoftBank (in partnership with OpenAI), and "Akari," an AI unicorn valued at over 100 billion yen.

The differences between each company's FDE model are compared in detail in the articles on Palantir's FDE model and OpenAI's FDE model.

Salaries vary widely depending on the position and company. The standard range in Japan is around 8 to 16 million yen, but top foreign companies stand out. Based on actual job postings, the figures are as follows.

Seeing these figures, it is easy to understand why this role is described as the most talked-about job of the AI era, commanding the highest levels of compensation.

The technical bar is surprisingly realistic. Looking at the job postings of major companies as of 2026, the door is wide open to anyone who is a "full-stack software engineer who can write Python."

Particularly valued are skills in leveraging LLM APIs, building AI agents, implementing RAG, and building data infrastructure. Generative AI companies have even more specific requirements; in its job posting for Claude-based Forward Deployed Engineers, Anthropic explicitly lists generative AI-specific deliverables such as "MCP servers, subagents, and agent skills."

Just as important as technical skill is client-facing ability. As an "engineer who can talk to clients," an FDE is responsible for everything from articulating problems to implementing solutions, so communication skills are essential. The article Three Skills Every FDE Needs, which systematically organizes the competencies required of FDEs, is also a useful reference.

The market value of people with FDE experience is high, and their subsequent career options are broad. On the technical side, typical paths include moving to Solutions Architect, technical lead, or CTO; on the business side, to Deployment Strategist, Head of Customer Success, or product manager. As professionals who "understand both technology and business," they are in high demand in the job market.

The FDE is a new role that, despite originating at Palantir, has seen rapid growth in job openings in the Japanese market thanks to the generative AI boom. Positioned between engineer and consultant, salaries range from around 8 million yen domestically to the 50-million-yen level at top foreign companies, and the role demands professionals who combine Python-based full-stack implementation skills, LLM/agent building skills, and client-facing ability. As the "third option" of the AI era, this is a role whose prominence will only continue to grow.
