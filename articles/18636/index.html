AI Talent Development in Real Estate Management | How to Train Property Management Company Employees into In-House FDEs

Original title: 不動産管理のAI人材育成｜管理会社の社員を社内FDEに育てる進め方

To develop people who build AI systems at a property management company, start with employees who understand contracts and property specifics—such as those in condominium management front desks, rental management, or brokerage sales administration. Good use cases are high-volume, document-centric tasks like cross-checking move-out settlement estimates or drafting meeting minutes. Explanations of important matters and decisions on whether the tenant or landlord bears costs remain the work of licensed professionals and responsible staff.

Why will property management workloads grow heavier from here on?

Because buildings are aging and residents are getting older.

According to the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, the stock of condominiums for sale stood at about 7.236 million units at the end of 2025, of which about 1.588 million were 40 or more years old. This figure is projected to grow to about 5.036 million units in 20 years—roughly 3.2 times today's level. In condominiums completed before 1984, 55.9% of households had a head of household aged 70 or older. As a reason for considering bringing in outside experts as board officers, 42.3% of management associations cited "a shortage of people willing to serve as officers."

On the rental side, move-out settlements are a task prone to disputes. The National Consumer Affairs Center received 14,711 inquiries about restoration-to-original-condition disputes in rental housing in fiscal 2025. Whether you can promptly show the basis of an estimate affects trust with both tenants and owners.

How do you divide work between licensed professionals and the system?

The system handles only drafts and lists of checkpoints. What laws and regulations require licensed professionals to do, and decisions about who bears costs, are done by people.

TaskExample of system built by in-house FDEWhat humans decide
Explanation of Important Matters documentExtract relevant sections from management rules and survey materials, and create a draftFinalizing the content and the explanation by a licensed real estate transaction agent
Board meeting minutesCreate a draft from recordings or notes, and list decisions made and follow-up itemsReviewing the content and approval by the board chair
Inquiries from tenants and ownersClassify by type and urgency, and draft responses based on past handlingSending the response and deciding whether to make urgent arrangements
Restoration estimatesCompile contractor estimates into a list, flagging outlier unit prices and discrepancies with inspection recordsAllocating costs between tenant and landlord, and ordering work

For drafts of the Explanation of Important Matters document, having the system output which clause each item was extracted from alongside the text shortens the time licensed agents need to verify it.

What did we learn from testing with 5 move-out settlement estimates?

We prepared 5 fictitious estimates from 3 contractors (3 Word files, 2 Excel files) and asked an AI tool to process them. Neither wording nor formats were standardized.

The AI created a 19-line itemized list in 69 seconds, breaking down even narrative-style estimates into quantities and unit prices. We then asked it to compare the per-square-meter unit prices for the same work across contractors and flag anything inconsistent with the inspection notes; in 117 seconds, 12 of the 19 lines were flagged. For example, one estimate charged the tenant for full wallpaper replacement even though the tenant had lived there 6 years and 5 months and the inspection notes said "no major damage or stains." The per-square-meter price for the wallpaper replacement was 2,400 yen, 109% higher than the median for the same work.

The AI also noted that the 30% threshold it used to define an "outlier" was a provisional value it set itself. How to allocate costs is decided by the responsible staff based on contract special provisions and Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism guidelines. Note that this is the result of a single run using fictitious data.

How do you scale this across a company with many branch offices?

Build it first at one branch office, then collect 3 documents from each other branch to test before rolling out. Different contractors and formats mean the same system won't work as-is. Select 2 candidates per department. Because this industry sees frequent transfers between management companies, brokerage firms, and parent companies, establish rules from the start for handing over the system inventory and manual to successors. Starting outside the busy spring move-out season keeps the burden on field staff low.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it okay to have AI create the Explanation of Important Matters document?

AI handles only the draft and the list of extracted clauses. Finalizing the content and delivering the explanation remain, as before, the work of a licensed real estate transaction agent.

Can we put tenants' and owners' personal information into the AI?

In the first month of training, decide with the information systems department what data may be used and how to handle names by masking them. If you start with documents with names masked, there's no risk of having to stop midway.

Do we need to replace our rental management system?

No replacement is needed. The initial system reads existing documents such as estimates and meeting minutes and turns them into tables and drafts. Integrating with the system will be an engineer-type role focused on developing your information systems staff.
