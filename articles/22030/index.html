Summary

This long-form Japanese guide from DeFactory maps out how to become an AI professional in Japan by 2026. It distinguishes narrow definitions (engineers building machine learning models) from broad ones (people applying AI to business), aligning with METI's three-tier model and its estimate of up to 790,000 unfilled IT jobs by 2030. It compares five roles—AI engineer, data scientist, MLOps engineer, AI planner/consultant, and LLM app developer—with salary ranges (roughly ¥4.5M–¥15M+), freelance monthly rates, and required skills. The author argues Python plus SQL plus generative AI skills (RAG, agents) are the most versatile combination, that business communication skills drive pay gaps, and that going freelance after 2–3 years of experience is the most realistic high-income path, with rates 1.5–2x salaried equivalents.

Key points

  • AI talent is split into narrow (model builders) and broad (business appliers) definitions, matching METI's three-tier model.
  • Japan faces up to 790,000 unfilled IT roles by 2030; generative AI specialists are scarcest.
  • Five roles compared: AI engineer, data scientist, MLOps engineer, AI planner/consultant, LLM app developer.
  • Python + SQL + generative AI (RAG, agents) is the most versatile 2026 skill set.
  • Freelancing after 2–3 years of experience yields rates 1.5–2x salaried pay.

Behind the growing attention on the term "AI talent" is the rapid evolution of AI technology itself. The "third AI boom" began when deep learning significantly outperformed conventional methods at the 2012 image recognition competition (ILSVRC), and after the release of ChatGPT at the end of 2022, generative AI and LLMs (large language models) quickly became the main battleground for business implementation. The fact that corporate hiring and talent development have not kept pace with this rapid technological shift is what has led to the current severe talent shortage.

The narrow definition of AI talent refers to engineers whose primary work is designing, implementing, and improving machine learning models. Specifically, the following roles fall into this category.

  • Machine Learning Engineer (ML Engineer): Handles everything from preparing training data to model training, evaluation, and tuning
  • AI Engineer: Integrates machine learning models into web systems and mobile apps via APIs, implementing them as services
  • Research Engineer: Implements algorithms from the latest papers and applies them to in-house products or R&D

This tier requires proficiency in Python and TensorFlow/PyTorch implementation, as well as domain knowledge such as deep learning, natural language processing (NLP), and image recognition. They are highly scarce in the hiring market, and their salaries and rates rank among the top of all IT roles.

The broad definition of AI talent refers to people who can apply AI to operations and business even though they do not implement AI themselves. In the definitions by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry and the Cabinet Office, this "user tier" is also included in the broader category of AI talent.

  • AI Planner / AI Consultant: Plans and proposes AI-based solutions to clients' business challenges
  • Data Analyst: Analyzes data using BI tools and SQL to support management decisions
  • Prompt Engineer: Incorporates LLMs such as ChatGPT and Claude into business processes and optimizes output quality

This tier is easy to enter even without programming experience, and is a field where people from humanities backgrounds and business roles can thrive.

Also, broad AI talent is not a monolith; roles vary depending on the level of involvement within an organization. Executives handle decision-making and investment judgments regarding AI adoption, while general employees on the front lines function as "users" who incorporate AI tools into their daily work. The AI planners and data analysts introduced here serve as "bridges" that connect the two and drive AI adoption. This breakdown by level of involvement roughly corresponds to the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry's three-tier model (1-4) described later.

Strictly speaking, data scientists are part of the AI talent pool, while DX talent is a broader concept covering digital technologies in general, including AI. When reviewing hiring requirements, it is important to identify which tier is being sought.

In its "DX Report 2.2 (2022)," METI classifies AI talent into the following three tiers.

  • Expert tier: Highly specialized professionals who conduct R&D of AI systems and develop new algorithms
  • Professional tier: Engineers responsible for building, implementing, and operating machine learning models
  • User tier: Business professionals who drive business improvement using AI tools

In the job market as of 2026, the shortage of "professional tier" talent is the most severe, and hiring competition for professionals who can work with generative AI (LLMs) is intensifying.

Job Description

AI engineers play a central role in implementing machine learning models as products (such as web services and APIs). They can be described as a "bridge role," taking models built by data engineers and data scientists and integrating them into production environments as scalable systems.

Their main duties are as follows.

  • Implementing machine learning models (classification, regression, recommendation, NLP, etc.) in Python
  • Designing and training deep learning models using TensorFlow and PyTorch
  • Serving models as REST APIs (FastAPI, Flask, etc.)
  • Containerization and production deployment using Docker and Kubernetes
  • Model accuracy evaluation, tuning, and A/B test design
  • Developing LLM applications using generative AI APIs (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, etc.)

The table in section 2-6 shows ranges by role, but even within the same role and years of experience, actual salaries and rates vary widely. The four main factors behind these differences are as follows.

  • Depth of practical experience: The scale of projects involved in, and whether you have experience operating models in production environments
  • Presence of rare skills: Proficiency in technologies with few qualified professionals, such as RAG design, LLM fine-tuning, and MLOps

The required depth varies by role, but as of 2026, the combination of "Python × SQL × generative AI" is the most versatile weapon.

In the AI talent market, when technical skills are at the same level, professionals who possess the ability to accurately understand business problems and explain them to non-engineers are valued overwhelmingly higher.

The reason lies in actual practice. Many AI development projects fail not because of "technical problems," but because of "mistakes in defining business requirements," "misalignment with stakeholders," or "incorrectly set accuracy targets." Before using technology correctly, the ability to define "what it will be used for" is required.

The three business skills that are especially valued are the following.

  • Requirements definition skills: The ability to translate vague business problems into "AI-solvable problems"
  • ROI calculation and explanation skills: The ability to explain the cost-effectiveness of AI investments to management
  • Data ethics and risk explanation skills: The ability to accurately communicate risks of bias, privacy, and hallucination, and to build trust

When going independent as a freelancer, these explanation and proposal skills directly translate into "project rates." Improving not only technical skills but also communication abilities with clients leads to long-term growth in market value.

As corporate adoption of generative AI advances, the importance of knowledge that ensures "safe operation" is rapidly increasing, beyond just model performance itself. In particular, the following three points are increasingly being asked about in hiring interviews and project onboarding as of 2026.

  • Data quality management: Knowledge to detect missing data or bias in data used for training and analysis, and to prevent model accuracy degradation in advance
  • Security and information leak prevention: Managing the risk of inputting confidential information into LLMs, and designing internal access permissions
  • Copyright and rights handling of generated content: Understanding legal risks when generative AI output resembles existing copyrighted works, and experience establishing internal guidelines

This area is becoming an evaluation criterion not only for engineering roles but also for AI Planners and Data Scientists. Especially in AI projects in heavily regulated industries such as finance and healthcare, it is not uncommon for the presence or absence of governance knowledge to determine whether one can join a project.

For those starting with no experience, beginning with the "Generative AI Passport" or "G Test" and then aiming for cloud certifications (AWS ML, Google ML) once your role is decided is the most cost-effective path.

From here on, we'll explain a concrete roadmap for those aiming to become AI professionals with no prior experience. In recent years, efforts by working adults to continuously update their skills while employed are called "reskilling," the system of repeatedly moving between work and education throughout life is called "recurrent education," and the general attitude of lifelong learning is called "lifelong learning"—each has a slightly different nuance. Learning aimed at becoming an AI professional can be considered one of the prime examples of "reskilling." Follow the four steps below to steadily build your career.

Before starting a career transition into AI, it's important to first clarify which entry point you should take. Choosing the wrong entry point will cause your learning costs to balloon needlessly.

Engineering route (suited for people who)

  • Have programming experience (any language)
  • Have little resistance to mathematics or science/engineering fields
  • Are interested in "building" and "running" models
  • Target roles: AI engineer, data scientist, MLOps engineer, LLM application developer

Business route (suited for people who)

  • Come from business roles such as sales, planning, consulting, or marketing
  • Have no programming experience but are interested in business applications of AI
  • Are good at logical thinking, presentations, and creating documents
  • Target roles: AI planner, AI consultant, DX promotion specialist

Which route is right for you depends on your current work history, skill set, target salary, and the time you can devote to learning. The key is "don't try to do everything." Focusing on one role first, gaining practical experience, and then expanding into adjacent skills is the most efficient approach.

Regardless of route, traits commonly cited for "people suited to becoming AI professionals" are threefold: (1) curiosity that makes continuously catching up on new technologies and information feel effortless, (2) logical thinking skills to break down and organize ambiguous problems with no single correct answer, and (3) an attitude of learning not only one's own area of responsibility but also surrounding technologies and business context. Conversely, the desire to "work for a long time using only knowledge acquired once" is a somewhat poor fit for an AI career. Precisely because this is a field where technology changes rapidly, whether you can enjoy continuous learning itself is the first aptitude check.

Learning to become an AI professional often costs hundreds of thousands of yen even when using a school. To reduce the burden, it's worth considering the "Education and Training Benefit System" administered by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. Under this system, part of the course fees is paid out through Hello Work upon completion of eligible courses, and some data science and AI schools have courses designated as eligible.

Whether a course is eligible, and the benefit rate, differ depending on the system category (general education training, specified general education training, or professional practice education training), so be sure to check the latest list of eligible courses on the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's official website or at Hello Work before applying. Not all learning services introduced in this article may be eligible.

Once you've completed the foundational learning, proceed with obtaining certifications and building a portfolio in parallel.

Recommended certification path for the engineering route

  • First, systematize your overall AI knowledge with the "G Test"
  • Next, obtain "AWS Certified ML – Specialty" or "Google Professional ML Engineer"
  • If you aspire to be an LLM application developer, also consider the "TensorFlow Developer Certificate"

Concrete portfolio examples (what hiring managers value)

What hiring managers look for in a portfolio is not "high polish" but "high resolution in problem setting." Practical projects like the following are highly valued.

  • Participating in Kaggle competitions: Your participation record and ranking directly prove your ability
  • Building a RAG system: Publish on GitHub an LLM system that can search your company's product FAQs or documentation
  • Data analysis of actual work: Anonymize your own work data, perform EDA (exploratory data analysis), and publish it as a Notebook
  • Business automation tools using LLMs: Implement API apps for email classification, meeting minutes generation, code review assistance, etc.

It's important to publish your portfolio on GitHub and clearly state in the README "what problem you solved," "what you did cleverly," and "accuracy and results."

Once you have a portfolio and certifications, it's time to gain practical experience. There are mainly four options.

(1) Job change (the most standard route)

  • Job platforms: Search for AI engineers and machine learning engineers on LinkedIn, Wantedly, doda, Green, etc.
  • Key point: If you have no practical experience, a realistic path is to start with "assisting a machine learning team at a product-development company" or a "data analyst role" and get involved in AI projects within the company

(2) Using SES (System Engineering Service)

In SES projects, there are also positions you can join with 1–2 years of experience even if you started as a beginner. In particular, at SES companies specializing in AI/ML, there are cases where you can join projects while receiving educational support. In terms of ease of accumulating practical experience, this can sometimes be a lower barrier than changing jobs.

③ Side Jobs / Freelancing (Building a Track Record While Earning Income)

This method involves using crowdsourcing services (Lancers, CrowdWorks, etc.) or freelance agents to take on small-scale data analysis or LLM app development projects as side work. Since it can be done alongside your main job, you can build a track record while minimizing the risk of changing jobs.

④ Internal Transfer / Project Participation at Your Current Company

For those already working at an IT company or large corporation, requesting a transfer to an internal DX promotion department or data analysis team is the zero-risk option. There are many examples of people who, after obtaining the G Test certification, took the action of "creating an internal proposal for promoting AI adoption and submitting it to management," thereby building a track record for an internal transfer.

When looking at job postings, actual recruitment is generally not under the vague category of "AI talent," but under more specific job titles such as "Machine Learning Engineer," "MLOps Engineer," or "LLM Application Engineer." Before applying, it is also important to check the "development phase" (whether it is a PoC stage or a production operation phase) stated in the job description. The required experience differs: PoC-stage projects emphasize speed of hypothesis testing, while production-phase projects emphasize skills in stable operation and maintenance.

Those from Humanities / Non-IT Backgrounds

Those from SIer / Infrastructure Engineering Backgrounds

According to METI's "Survey Report on IT Personnel Supply and Demand (2019)," it is estimated that there will be a shortage of up to 790,000 IT engineers by 2030. At the center of this shortage are "advanced IT professionals" who can handle AI, IoT, and big data.

As of 2026, this supply-demand gap has become even more severe. Due to the rapid spread of generative AI (LLMs), corporate demand for AI adoption has grown several times compared to 2023, yet the number of people who can actually perform RAG design, LLM fine-tuning, and MLOps construction is extremely small.

This structural supply-demand gap is the biggest factor driving up the salaries and rates of AI professionals. In the freelance market as well, the average monthly rate for machine learning engineers is approximately 798,000 yen, placing it among the top of all job categories, with demand for AI and data engineers using Python driving overall salary levels.

Many people are concerned that "in an era where code is auto-generated by GitHub Copilot and Cursor, the value of engineers may disappear." However, when it comes to AI professionals, quite the opposite phenomenon is occurring.

The "judgment ability" to evaluate the quality of AI-generated code, identify problems, and correctly integrate it as a system will become the rarest and most valuable skill going forward. While AI will handle simple code writing, the following abilities are believed to remain human responsibilities even into the 2030s.

  • Problem-setting ability: The power to define "what should be solved" from a business context
  • AI system design and architectural decisions: Designing entire systems that combine multiple models and tools
  • Evaluation of quality, ethics, and risk: Reviewing bias, hallucinations, and security risks
  • Communication with stakeholders: The ability to "translate" between technology and management

The career path of "accumulating 2–3 years of practical experience as an AI engineer, then going independent as a freelancer" is currently one of the most realistic high-income routes in the AI talent market.

The reason is that many AI projects are designed on the premise of "project-based external procurement." For corporate AI in-house development projects, startup LLM service development, and SIer AI implementation support alike, more and more companies judge that outsourcing to experienced freelancers on a short-term, intensive basis is advantageous in both cost and speed compared to hiring dedicated in-house AI engineers.

This structure pushes freelance rates for AI professionals to 1.5–2 times the level of full-time salaries.

Note that this guideline of "2–3 years of practical experience" is the shortest route when building a career specializing in the AI field. Those who already have 5+ years of engineering experience but are new to the AI field are by no means behind. In fact, since they will add AI-related skills on top of their existing practical experience (a foundation in design, operations, team collaboration, etc.), there are many cases where they can reach high-rate projects in a shorter time than those starting from zero.
