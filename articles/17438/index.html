Delivery Lead: Delivery Lead

Original title: 交付负责人：Delivery Lead

Full text

This card explains what a management role looks like within an FDE team: a senior engineer taking it on, rather than adding an extra layer. Core breakdown: the difference between a Delivery Lead and a project manager lies in accountability for outcomes and the authority to make trade-offs—a PM focuses on planning and coordination, while a Delivery Lead is responsible for success or failure and can cut scope to preserve quality. In the FDE model, teams are small and feedback loops are fast, so management actions get compressed, and the focus shifts from "chasing progress" to "managing scope creep and customer expectations"—the two things that most commonly kill on-site delivery. One signal for judging whether this role is healthy: can the person veto a scope change without escalating through layers? Incremental judgment: most management content assumes engineering teams need a dedicated PM; this card gives the counterargument in the FDE context: dual reporting lines exhaust frontline engineers with translating information, and split technical judgment from management judgment. The solution is to designate a single decision-maker: technical trade-offs belong to the Delivery Lead, commercial commitments belong to the sales line, and both lines are made explicit face-to-face at the project kickoff meeting. Action suggestion: if you're leading delivery, cut next week's status meetings in half and use the saved time to review the scope change log for your current project. — FDEChina Editorial Team · Practitioner's Perspective

Definition

A Delivery Lead is a management role accountable for the overall delivery outcome of a project, covering scope management, schedule, risk, stakeholder communication, and commercial health, typically leading one delivery team or several parallel projects.

Details

The key distinction is with the project manager (PM): a PM focuses on planning, scheduling, and coordination, while a Delivery Lead takes full responsibility for delivery success and has trade-off authority—they can decide to cut scope, change the approach, or add resources. In a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) team, this role is often held by a senior FDE who both writes code and manages the project, because the small-team, tight-loop structure cannot accommodate a dedicated management layer. The FDE model also shifts this role's focus: delivery cycles are short and requirements change fast, so traditional "progress-chasing" management actions get compressed, and the real work becomes managing scope creep, managing customer expectations, managing escalation paths, and making judgment calls when technical solutions conflict with commercial commitments.

The most common misuse is forcing a technically clueless dedicated PM onto an FDE team, creating dual reporting: engineers exhaust themselves translating information for both sides, technical judgment splits from management judgment, and in a small team the biggest hidden cost is information translation and waiting on decisions. A healthier structure is a Delivery Lead coming from the engineering side, with commercial coordination handled by an account manager or project liaison.
