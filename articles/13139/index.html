FDE vs Solutions Engineer vs Software Engineer

FDE Council · 2026-10-29 · 7 min read

Summary

This FDE Council article compares forward deployed engineers, solutions engineers, and software engineers. The key distinction is accountability: software engineers answer for the product, solutions engineers for technical wins in sales, and FDEs for production systems a customer can run without them. Citing Lightcast and Levels.fyi data, it shows FDE postings surged from about 200 in 2024 to over 5,200 in the first seven months of 2026, with median US total compensation of $220,000 versus $198,850 for software engineers. It advises judging roles by what they are measured on, not by title.

Key points

  • The three roles differ mainly in what outcome each is accountable for.
  • FDEs write production code in customer environments and own adoption and handover.
  • Lightcast counts US FDE postings growing from ~200 (2024) to 5,200+ (first seven months of 2026).
  • Levels.fyi median total comp: FDE $220,000 vs software engineer $198,850.
  • Job titles blur; read the accountability, not the title.

In brief: the three roles share engineering skill but answer to different results. Software engineers answer for the product. Solutions engineers answer for technical wins in a sale. Forward Deployed Engineers answer for a production outcome a specific customer can run without them. Job titles overlap across employers, so read what the role is accountable for, not what it is called.

How do the three roles compare side by side?

The clearest difference is what each role is judged on. The table below shows the typical pattern. Individual employers vary.

How is a Forward Deployed Engineer different from a software engineer?

A Forward Deployed Engineer does the same core engineering, but inside a customer’s environment and with responsibility for adoption and handover. A software engineer usually builds for many customers at once from inside the product team.

Job posting data shows the difference. Lightcast compared skills requested in US FDE and software engineer postings (Lightcast, September 15, 2026). It found software engineering and communication common to both. FDE postings put more weight on artificial intelligence, workflow management and Python. AI and workflow management do not appear in the top 20 skills for software engineer postings. Lightcast sums it up: software engineers “generally focus on building software,” while FDEs “combine engineering with AI implementation, workflow design and direct customer problem solving.”

In practice, an FDE spends more time on work a product engineer rarely owns: discovery with non-technical stakeholders, fitting into unfamiliar identity, network and data constraints, and making sure people actually use the result. The FDE delivery lifecycle sets out those stages in order.

How is a Forward Deployed Engineer different from a solutions engineer?

A solutions engineer shows that a product can solve a problem. A Forward Deployed Engineer makes it solve the problem in production. The first role mostly works before a sale. The second mostly works after it.

The two roles share real skills. Both run discovery, translate business needs into technical terms and earn trust with customer teams. The split comes after the deal closes. A solutions engineer usually moves on to the next opportunity. An FDE stays to integrate with live systems, test quality against real cases, deploy securely, support adoption and hand over ownership.

Titles blur at the edges. Some solutions engineers build substantial integrations, and some FDE roles include pre-sales work. A useful test: if the role is measured on deals won, it is closer to solutions engineering. If it is measured on systems running in production, it is closer to forward deployed engineering.

What about solutions architects and consultants?

Solutions architects and advisory consultants typically design or recommend an approach. FDEs also build and operate it. A solutions architect may design how a product fits a customer’s systems, and a consultant may advise on the approach. An FDE is expected to write the code, ship it and stay accountable until the customer can run it. The Council’s role definition compares these neighboring roles too.

Do Forward Deployed Engineers earn more than software or solutions engineers?

Slightly, on median total compensation, but the ranges overlap heavily. On Levels.fyi, the US FDE median is $220,000, against $198,850 for software engineers. Solution architects also sit at $220,000 and sales engineers at $201,000.

Read these figures with care. Levels.fyi data is self-reported and skews toward larger, better-paying employers. The FDE sample is small. Levels.fyi has no separate solutions engineer page, so solution architect and sales engineer are the closest proxies.

Advertised pay shows a wider gap. Lightcast data reported by Fortune puts the median advertised FDE salary at more than $188,000, compared with roughly $145,000 for traditional software engineers (Fortune, September 3, 2026; sample size not stated). Posted salaries usually cover base pay only, so they are not comparable with the total compensation figures above. For FDE pay by level, company and city, see the monthly FDE Salary Report.

Why is demand for Forward Deployed Engineers growing so fast?

Employers adopting AI need engineers who can turn a capability into a working system inside messy real environments, and that is the FDE job. Lightcast counts about 200 US FDE job postings in 2024, around 1,200 in 2025 and more than 5,200 in the first seven months of 2026 (Lightcast, September 15, 2026). Fortune, citing Lightcast, reports FDE postings rose more than 1,000% from January to August 2026 versus the same period in 2025, while all tech postings grew 13%.

Scale matters here. Lightcast notes that demand “remains small compared with established technology occupations.” There are far fewer FDE roles than software engineer roles. They are simply growing much faster.

How can you tell which role a job posting really describes?

Read what the role is measured on, not its title. These five questions sort most postings quickly:

  • What does success look like? Deals, demos and technical wins point to solutions engineering. Systems in production and customer outcomes point to FDE work.
  • Who owns the code after launch? If you ship code that the customer relies on, and you support it until handover, the role is forward deployed.
  • Where does the work happen? Inside one customer’s systems and workflows suggests FDE. Inside the company’s own product codebase suggests software engineering.
  • Who do you report to? A sales leader is a strong signal of a pre-sales role. Engineering or a dedicated deployment team is more typical for FDEs.
  • Does the role include adoption and handover? Training users, writing runbooks and transferring ownership are core FDE responsibilities and rare elsewhere.

Employers can use the same questions to write clearer postings. The Council’s guide to assessing FDE skills shows how to turn them into a consistent interview process.

Which role fits you best?

Choose based on the work you want to own. If you enjoy building product depth for many users, software engineering fits. If you like discovery, storytelling and the pace of a sales cycle, solutions engineering fits. If you want to build, ship and stay with a customer until the system works for them, the FDE path fits.

The open FDE Council Professional Capability Framework lists 11 core domains an FDE needs. The free FDE self-assessment shows which of them you already cover. Software engineers often start strong on engineering domains. Solutions engineers often start strong on discovery and collaboration.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Forward Deployed Engineer a sales role?

No. An FDE may work alongside sales and may support an early deal, but the role is accountable for building and shipping a working system in production, not for closing revenue.

Can a solutions engineer become a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Yes, if they can show production engineering evidence. Solutions engineers usually bring strong discovery and communication skills. The gap to close is typically writing, testing, deploying and operating production code that a customer relies on.

Can a software engineer become a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Yes. Software engineers already have the core build skills. The gap is usually customer-facing work: discovery with non-technical stakeholders, working inside someone else’s environment, driving adoption and handing over ownership.

Is FDE the same job as solutions architect?

Not usually. Solutions architects typically design how a product fits a customer’s systems and advise on implementation. An FDE also builds, deploys and supports the solution in production. Some employers blur the two titles, so read the responsibilities.
