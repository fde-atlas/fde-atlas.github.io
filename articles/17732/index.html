How much does an FDE make? A breakdown of $550K total compensation

Original title: FDE 薪资多少？55 万美元总薪酬拆解

Summary

Key takeaways

  • $550K is the upper end of estimated annual total compensation at OpenAI and Anthropic. On Levels.fyi, the median total compensation for US FDEs is $215K.
  • Among 135 US roles, the median base salary is about $190K per year and is capped by level. Bonuses are typically 15%–25% of base salary.
  • At the top of the market, equity makes up 55%–70% of the compensation package, usually vesting over four years with a one-year cliff, and its value depends on how you value it.

The $550K figure often cited as the "FDE salary" is actually the upper bound of estimated annual total compensation at top AI labs. The same 2026 compensation survey from Aced (formerly Exponent) shows that the median base salary across 135 Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roles in the US is only about $190K per year.

These two numbers describe two different groups of people. The gap between them is not a matter of different parts of the same compensation package.

Even at these labs, what pushes total compensation to $550K is mostly equity. Equity vests annually, not monthly, and its value depends on how you value it.

If you are considering a move into an FDE role, or already have an offer in hand, misreading this structure can lead to two kinds of mistakes. One is expecting a monthly paycheck that no one ever promised; the other is choosing the offer with the bigger headline number, when its first-year cash and vested equity are actually lower.

$550K is the ceiling for a few, not the norm

Aced estimates FDE total compensation at OpenAI and Anthropic at $350K–$550K. These are two top companies, not the whole market. Levels.fyi, which collects self-reported compensation data, records a median FDE total compensation of $215K.

Perspective's 2026 compensation report, based on 1,200 data points from Levels.fyi and public job postings, shows an extremely wide range. At one end is Palantir's median of $215K; at the other, senior FDEs at Anthropic and OpenAI earning over $785K. Same job title, more than a threefold pay difference.

So $550K is best used to mark the top of the market, not to set expectations for a first offer. To know where you stand, you need to break the compensation package down layer by layer.

The cash layer: base salary is capped by level, bonuses are proportional

According to Aced, half of US FDE roles list base salaries between $160K and $220K (25th to 75th percentile). Perspective's salary negotiation guide notes that base salary is essentially capped by level, with limited room to negotiate upward without a promotion.

Bonuses are surprisingly predictable. Perspective found that FDE annual bonuses are typically 15%–25% of base salary, tied to performance, with targets generally met. On a median base salary of $190K, that works out to roughly $28,500–$47,500 in bonus, for total annual cash of about $218,500–$237,500.

A $190K base salary spread over 12 pay cycles comes to about $15,800 pre-tax per month. For a median-level FDE, this is the closest thing to a "monthly salary."

Public salary ranges also deserve a close read. Aced considers Anthropic's $280K–$320K range the highest-floor among US public ranges, but it already includes the target bonus and is not all guaranteed fixed cash.

The equity layer: the biggest share, the slowest to arrive

What inflates total compensation is equity. Perspective found that at the top of the market, equity now makes up 55%–70% of the compensation package, up from 35%–45% in 2024. Aced says that at OpenAI (paid in PPUs) and Anthropic (packages mostly in RSUs), equity can bring total compensation to roughly twice the cash component.

Equity does not arrive monthly. Perspective describes the standard structure at large labs as RSUs at private companies vesting over four years with a one-year cliff, and treats this arrangement as standard practice.

Suppose you are granted $800K worth of equity. It vests at $200K per year, but you get nothing in the first 12 months.

If you leave in month 11, you walk away with no equity. Work out what the cliff means for you before signing, not after.

One equity grant, multiple valuations

According to Aced, about 70% of FDE roles explicitly mention equity. But Aced notes that startups like Sierra, Ramp, and Perplexity grant substantial equity beyond the public ranges without giving specific numbers. To find out what it is worth, you have to ask directly at the offer stage.

At that point, Perspective's guide recommends evaluating private-company equity on a fully diluted basis, cross-checking both the 409A valuation and the preferred stock price from the most recent funding round. The guide's rationale is that preferred stock is typically priced at two to five times common stock.

For example: the recruiter says your equity is "worth $400K" at the latest round's price. If what you receive is common stock, a more realistic value might be only about $80K–$200K. For the same grant, the number on the slide and the money you can actually realize may differ by a factor of five.

Read your offer like a client contract

Perspective recommends that when comparing offers, you define total compensation as "cash plus first-year vested equity" rather than looking at headline numbers. That way all compensation packages can be compared on the same basis.

In the hypothetical example in the chart, offer A at $300K is mostly cash, while most of offer B's "$450K" is equity valued at the preferred stock price. Revalue B's equity at the common stock price, and its first-year value is only about $264,800–$334,250, possibly less than A.

This is also a core skill of the job. A good FDE does not take a client's numbers at face value, but probes how they were calculated, what data they are based on, and which assumptions they rely on. Apply the same client-research habits to your own offer: ask questions layer by layer, note the answers, then run the numbers yourself.

If you work in another country, don't compare these numbers directly, but the method still applies. Break down your local offer into base salary, bonus, and equity, ask the same questions as in the table, then compare on cash plus first-year vesting.

Since base salary is capped by level, your real negotiating leverage lies in the level you're assigned and your equity. When writing your resume or preparing for interviews, bring evidence that supports a higher level: deployments you've owned end-to-end, and the technical decisions you've made in front of customers.

The FDE market is increasingly paying compensation in equity. Those who benefit will be the ones who can read a cap table and vesting structure, not those who only remember the biggest number.
