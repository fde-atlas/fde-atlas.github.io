How to Become an FDE | Routes by Background and the Gaps to Fill

Original title: FDEになるには｜転向元別のルートと、埋めるべき差分

株式会社ウェステリア · 2026-11-16 · 9 min read

Summary

This Japanese article, based on job postings as of August 2026, explains how to become a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). It argues that FDEs need two abilities—shipping production software and defining problems with customers—and most candidates have only one, so preparation means filling the missing side. Requirements vary widely by company: Palantir's Tokyo FDSE role asks for one year post-graduation experience, while OpenAI's FDE role requires five years including customer-facing work. Entering directly with no experience is unrealistic; the realistic path is two to three years as a software engineer or IT consultant while deliberately building the missing skill. The article lists a 3-6 month preparation plan and notes interviews assess judgment under ambiguity, communication with non-technical stakeholders, and tolerance for travel (25-75% at Palantir, up to 50% at OpenAI).

Key points

  • FDE work combines production coding with customer-side problem definition; most candidates lack one side.
  • Entry requirements differ sharply: Palantir Tokyo asks 1+ year, OpenAI asks 5+ years including customer work.
  • SIer/consulting backgrounds mainly lack modern development practices and LLM implementation experience.
  • Zero-experience entry is unrealistic; build 2-3 years in engineering or IT consulting first.
  • Interviews test ambiguity handling, non-technical communication, and acceptance of heavy travel.

An FDE is a role where one person handles both software development and customer engagement. Most people have experience in only one of the two, so the question when considering a career change is not "can I become one?" but "which side am I missing?" In this article, after checking what job postings actually require as of the time of writing (August 2026), I'll organize what gaps you need to fill depending on your background.

The Short Answer

  • The years of experience required in job postings vary widely. As of the time of writing (August 2026), Palantir's Tokyo FDSE posting requires 1+ years after graduating from university, while OpenAI's FDE posting requires 5+ years of technical experience including customer engagement—the width of the entry point differs considerably by company
  • The gaps you need to fill depend on your background. People coming from development tend to lack experience working with customers and defining requirements, while those coming from consulting or SIer backgrounds tend to lack the ability to write production code and experience implementing LLMs
  • Going straight into an FDE role with zero experience in both development and customer engagement is not realistic. The fastest route is to first build 2-3 years of professional experience in one of the two roles

Years of Experience Job Postings Actually Require

There's an impression that "FDE is a narrow gate," but comparing job postings reveals that the width of the entry point differs considerably by company. As far as I could verify at the time of writing (August 2026), Palantir's Tokyo Forward Deployed Software Engineer posting lists 1+ years of professional experience after graduating from university as an application requirement. Meanwhile, OpenAI's Forward Deployed Engineer posting lists 5+ years of experience in engineering or technical implementation, including customer engagement.

This difference reflects what each company expects from FDEs. Palantir has an established playbook and is designed to hire young people and develop them. OpenAI's FDEs are expected to make independent decisions and build full-stack solutions in the customer's environment, so they hire people who are already complete from the start.

In other words, there is no universal answer to "how many years do you need to become an FDE?"—it becomes a question of choosing a company that matches your years of experience. If you're light on experience, aim for companies with an established playbook; if you're experienced, aim for companies with greater autonomy.

Gaps to Fill, by Background

What an FDE needs falls broadly into two categories: "the ability to see production software through to completion" and "the ability to define problems together with customers." Most people have one or the other. So preparation is about building the missing side in a short period.

As the table shows, many roles actually have an entry point. In particular, people who have deployed systems while working directly with customers at SIers or consulting firms are already doing half of an FDE's job. What's missing is modern development practices (Git, CI, testing, cloud) and experience implementing with LLMs. These two can take shape within a few months if approached deliberately.

Can You Aim for It with No Experience?

To be honest, going directly into an FDE role with zero experience in both development and customer engagement is not realistic based on the job requirements as of the time of writing (August 2026). Even Palantir's Tokyo posting, which has the widest entry point, requires 1+ years of professional experience after graduation, and most postings assume 3+ years.

However, being "inexperienced as an FDE" is a different story. Since few people have experience under the FDE job title itself, companies hire on the assumption of a career change from other roles. What's required is not a matching job title, but the two abilities from the previous section.

Therefore, the realistic route is to first build 2-3 years of professional experience as a software engineer or IT consultant, deliberately fill the missing side during that time, and then move into an FDE role. It may seem like a detour, but since this role requires making judgment calls in the field, without practical experience to inform those judgments, things will get tough after you join.

What You Can Prepare in 3-6 Months

If you have time before the job change, filling the gaps in the following order should be efficient. The list is ordered from top to bottom by impact in the selection process.

The first item is the most important. In FDE selection processes, what's tested is not raw technical skill but the experience of "defining someone's real business problem yourself and solving it with something that works." It doesn't have to be a company project. Even something on the scale of automating a friend's company's tasks with an LLM can be a talking point, as long as you carried it through from problem definition to production deployment.

  • Take something built with LLMs to the point where it's used in someone's real work — scale doesn't matter. Carry it through once from problem definition to production deployment and subsequent improvement
  • Become able to write from frontend to backend in Python or TypeScript — these two are the main languages requested in job postings
  • Build experience deploying and operating on your own with one of the cloud platforms (AWS / GCP / Azure)
  • Be able to explain RAG and agent architectures in your own words — not just how they work, but where they break down
  • English — if targeting foreign companies, at a level where you can handle technical discussions and customer meetings. Building experience using it in real work is the fastest way
  • Rewrite your resume from "what I built" to the form of "from what ambiguous state, how I defined it, and how it's being used in production"

What's Evaluated in Your Resume and Interviews

FDE selection doesn't end with just a coding challenge. The evaluation criteria that can be read from the job postings fall roughly into three categories. The first is whether you can make your own judgments in ambiguous situations and move forward. OpenAI's job posting lists phrases like experience scoping and delivering complex systems in uncertain, fast-moving environments, and the ability to make fast, sound decisions under pressure.

The second is whether you can communicate with non-technical people. From frontline customer staff to executives, you'll regularly need to explain things to people who don't speak the language of technology and reach agreement with them. Palantir's job posting also mentions the ability to collaborate in teams with a mix of technical and non-technical members.

The third is whether you can accept travel and change. The fact that travel ratios are explicitly stated in job postings (25-75% for Palantir's Tokyo role, up to 50% for OpenAI's FDE) shows that this is a role that assumes your lifestyle will change. This will also be confirmed in interviews. Rather than avoiding the topic, it's better to sort out in advance how much you can tolerate, which helps prevent mismatches after joining.

How to take the first step

If you wait until you're fully prepared, the hiring waves for this role will move on. In practical terms, the recommendation is to keep watching job postings while you prepare. The requirements in job postings are essentially "a list of skills the market is looking for," so as you watch them, it becomes clear what you need to fill in.

Also, even if you feel your experience is lacking, there's no problem with applying early. FDE is a role still in the process of being established, and companies can't afford to wait for finished candidates. Even if you're rejected in the selection process, knowing specifically what you're missing will improve the precision of your preparation.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • How many years of experience do I need to become an FDE?
  • It varies by company. As of this writing (2026-08), Palantir's Tokyo Forward Deployed Software Engineer posting requires 1+ years after graduating university, and OpenAI's Forward Deployed Engineer posting requires 5+ years of experience including customer-facing work. Broadly speaking, many postings will consider you with around 3 years of practical experience, and if your experience is limited, targeting companies with established frameworks is a realistic approach.
  • Can I become an FDE even with a humanities background?
  • The requirements in job postings are not about your degree, but about being able to write code and define problems with customers. Palantir's job posting lists backgrounds in computer science, mathematics, physics, etc. as "preferred," not required. There are also paths from IT consulting, so a humanities background itself is not an obstacle. However, you do need to be able to write production-level code.
  • Can I become an FDE even if I don't speak English?
  • Since a considerable number of FDE postings at domestic AI startups don't require English, it is possible. However, as of this writing (2026-08), postings at Japanese offices of foreign AI companies almost all require Japanese-English bilingual skills, and the highest compensation ranges are concentrated there. A realistic path is to build a track record at a domestic company, then fill in your English and target foreign companies.
  • Should I get certifications?
  • It's rare for FDE job postings to list specific certifications as requirements. Cloud certifications do serve as proof of foundational knowledge, but what's emphasized in selection is "experience defining things yourself from an ambiguous state and delivering them to production." Rather than spending time studying for certifications, it's more effective to add one more experience of building and operating something that actually works, even if small.
  • Does age work against me?
  • Since this role involves speaking on equal footing with customer executives, the depth of your work experience can directly become a strength. On the other hand, it's also a role with frequent travel and on-site work at customers, so lifestyle constraints need to be discussed. What matters more than age is whether you can demonstrate that you're continuously keeping up with technology (especially implementation experience around LLMs).
