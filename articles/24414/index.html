What the Anthropic-DXC Partnership Tells Us About a Path for Developing Existing Engineers into FDEs

Original title: AnthropicとDXCの提携から考える、既存エンジニアをFDEへ育てる道筋

株式会社本質 · 2026-09-17 · 3 min read

Summary

This article analyzes the multi-year global partnership Anthropic announced with DXC Technology on June 11, 2026, under which DXC will select engineers from existing development teams and combine Anthropic Academy training with internal education on customer core systems, aiming to train tens of thousands of Claude-certified FDEs. The author, from Japanese firm 本質, reads this as a model for converting existing field experience into AI-driven business improvement skills. For SES and SIer companies, the article proposes a three-step path: select candidates who can articulate exceptions in their own work; after shared AI basics training, have each trainee complete one real task end-to-end (e.g., drafting incident reports) with recorded evals; and assess trainees by the roles they can be entrusted with, from improving their own work to implementation and eventually customer-facing problem definition.

Key points

  • Anthropic and DXC plan to train tens of thousands of Claude-certified FDEs.
  • Candidates should be chosen for their ability to explain exceptions in their work.
  • After shared AI training, each trainee completes one real task end-to-end.
  • Trainees must record creation time, revisions, and reasons for failed evals.
  • Talent is tiered by role: self-improvement, implementation, then customer problem definition.

Full text

To increase the number of FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers), who should be targeted for training? For SES companies and SIers, the experience of engineers who understand customers' current business operations serves as a starting point for thinking about AI implementation.

Key Points of the Official Announcement

On June 11, 2026, Anthropic announced a multi-year global partnership with DXC Technology. DXC will select personnel from its existing development teams and combine Anthropic Academy training with its own education on customers' core systems. The plan is to train tens of thousands of Claude-certified FDEs.

The announcement also explains that DXC has been using Claude in its own operations ahead of customer deployments. The number of people to be trained is a future plan, not a count of completed trainings. For details, see Anthropic's original announcement.

Our Perspective on the Essence

We read this announcement through the lens of "transforming existing field experience into the ability to improve operations with AI." Even if you don't have an organization the size of DXC, it offers a useful reference for thinking about the order of training. Below is the approach we propose.

Look for training candidates among people who can explain exceptions in their work

When selecting candidates, in addition to how quickly they operate AI tools, look at whether they can articulate what is difficult in the work they handle. For example, in maintenance work, look for people who can explain incidents that don't proceed according to the manual, situations where they need to check with senior staff, and operations where a misjudgment can cause widespread impact.

With that knowledge, you can concretely consider which areas to delegate to AI and which require human judgment. In the first meeting, asking them to name "one task they repeat every week" and "one exception in that task that causes trouble" makes it easier to choose practical assignments. However, field experience alone doesn't guarantee they can handle design and implementation. Combine technical learning with reviews.

After shared learning, handle one task from start to finish

Once they have learned basic AI operations, how to handle information, and how to verify outputs, have each person choose a small task from their own work. For example, drafting incident reports. Start with reading the incident and organizing it into a prescribed format.

The first goal is to reach a state where creation time and revision details can be recorded. Check whether they are making definitive statements before the cause has been determined, and whether they have omitted necessary timestamps or the scope of impact. As training deliverables, have them submit, along with the prototype, the examples used for evaluation and the reasons for rejection.

When using customer data, confirm the environment and the scope of information available for that project. Until preparations are complete, evaluation procedures can be practiced using fictional incidents or information for which usage permission has been granted.

Evaluate personnel by the roles they can be entrusted with

It is unrealistic to entrust everyone who completes the training immediately with everything from setting customer issues to operations. First, can they improve their own work using AI? Next, can they implement and evaluate according to a design? Beyond that, can they determine issues and acceptance criteria with customers? Place these stages in sequence.

In addition to the number of completions, the training manager should record "which roles they can be entrusted with" and "what experience they should gain next." It is fine to have someone skilled in customer dialogue pair with someone strong in implementation to advance a project. Expanding their scope of responsibility through practice makes it easier for each person to grasp their next goal.

Honshitsu Co., Ltd. offers programs divided into stages, from starting to use AI, to implementation, to FDE development. If you would like to organize your own candidates and practical assignments, please visit Honshitsu's website.
