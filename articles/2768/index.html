Enterprise AI implementation faces three major challenges, and excellent Forward Deployed Engineers need three core capabilities

Original title: 企业AI落地面临三大难点，优秀前线部署工程师需具备三项核心能力

虎嗅 · 2026-02-12 · 3 min read

Summary

Drawing on his experience coaching an enterprise AI project, the author argues that enterprise AI adoption faces three hurdles: unclear customer requirements, unclear technical solutions (especially eliminating hallucinations through engineering), and unclear paths to product standardization. He contends that an excellent FDE must simultaneously be a business expert, an AI expert, and a product manager—rare combinations that make good FDEs scarce. He suggests cultivating FDEs from product managers or using joint teams where members complement each other's skills.

Key points

  • Enterprise AI adoption is blocked by unclear requirements, solutions, and standardization paths.
  • AI must eliminate hallucinations through engineering to meet enterprise accuracy demands.
  • Purely custom AI projects tend to lose money; standardization is key to profitability.
  • Great FDEs need business, AI, and product management skills at once.
  • FDEs can be developed from product managers or via complementary joint teams.

Recently, I've been deeply involved in coaching an enterprise AI project, and I've personally experienced just how difficult it is to become an excellent FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer).

At the current stage, there are three difficulties in enterprise AI implementation:

1. Unclear requirements

Customers often have high expectations for AI, but what kind of AI product do they actually need? What is the business logic behind this product? In truth, customers themselves are not clear about this.

For example, a customer may want to use AI to improve lead conversion rates, but how exactly? What is the underlying business logic? Customers often haven't thought this through.

2. Unclear solutions

Even if the business logic is clear, how do you implement it with AI? Nobody knows.

There is a key point here: enterprises have very high accuracy requirements. If hallucinations cannot be eliminated through engineering approaches, AI will be very hard to implement.

For example, different industries and different enterprises all have their own professional terminology and rules, and enterprises also have a large number of legacy software systems. How do you get AI to accurately understand these terms and rules? And how do you get AI to correctly invoke the corresponding software functions?

These are all brand-new territories.

3. Unclear path to standardization

Even if the AI solution is successfully implemented, AI projects still face their biggest hurdle: product standardization.

In fact, even abroad, purely custom AI projects easily lose money, and the key to turning a profit is product standardization.

However, many enterprise AI projects are exploring uncharted territory. Can they be standardized at all? How do you standardize them? These are major challenges.

Because of these three difficulties, an excellent FDE must possess three capabilities:

1. Business expert

Since customers themselves don't understand their own requirements, someone must sort out the business logic based on the customer's expectations.

For example:

Which is the key node for improving lead conversion rates?

For salespeople to do this node's work well, what kind of suggestions does AI need to provide?

To ensure the quality of these suggestions, what data needs to be provided to AI? What industry know-how needs to be communicated to AI?

In the traditional software era, although implementation consultants would also sort out business processes, they often stayed at the "process" level and never truly went deep into the "strategy" or "SOP" layers.

However, for enterprise AI to deliver results, the business logic must be sorted out at a much deeper level, which requires the FDE to have the knowledge and capabilities of a business expert. Otherwise, the research phase can easily drag on, or the AI system may end up as a mere "showpiece" that users simply abandon.

2. AI expert

For enterprise AI projects to succeed, the FDE needs AI technical skills on one hand, such as prompting, RAG, and Vibe Coding.

On the other hand, the FDE also needs engineering capabilities. For example, how do you build the semantic layer? How do you represent business rules? How do you trigger actions?

Since AI is developing so rapidly, talent with these capabilities is actually very scarce.

3. Product manager

The key to success in the FDE model lies in whether the project can be standardized.

This requires the FDE to act like a product manager, assessing from the very beginning whether the project has the potential for standardization—investing cautiously in projects that cannot be standardized, and for those that can, thinking about the path to standardization from the start.

As you can see, any one of these three capabilities is extremely rare on its own, yet an FDE must possess all of them simultaneously. This shows just how hard it is to be an excellent FDE—no wonder some people say the FDE is the CEO of the future.

So how do you cultivate qualified FDEs? I see two approaches.

The first is to select and develop them from product managers/project managers.

For example, the most common source of FDEs today is product managers, because relatively speaking, the product manager role is the closest to that of an FDE.

Product Planet has invited many heads of enterprise AI products to share their experiences in the community. In my view, they are all actually performing the functions of an FDE: being on the front lines, co-creating brand-new AI products with customers.

Another approach is to build a joint team.

For example, a delivery-focused FDE may lack product standardization skills, so you can assign a senior product manager specifically to oversee the project, thereby increasing the success rate of product standardization.

The AI project I've been coaching is such a joint team, which includes a high-level salesperson from a major international company who plays the role of business expert.

Everyone plays to their own strengths. As long as the first project is successfully implemented and the standardized product architecture is established, implementing subsequent projects will become much easier.
