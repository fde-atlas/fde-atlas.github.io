Today's Judgment

This issue focuses on one publicly reported story from China: Lenovo has established a dedicated FDE organization and disclosed pilot, delivery, and ongoing operations data from a home appliance manufacturer. It offers research leads spanning organizational arrangements to business metrics, but with an anonymous client and missing baselines and statistical periods, it still cannot be treated as a directly replicable promise of returns.

Key Signals

Lenovo discloses a manufacturing case with a three-week POC and delivery of two AI agents in three months

According to an October 27 report by Securities Daily, Lenovo published a case involving a large domestic home appliance manufacturer: the team went on-site to sort out inventory and demand planning issues, completed a POC in three weeks, and delivered two AI agents—inventory optimization and planning—within three months. Citing Lenovo's own figures, the report states that after ongoing operations, finished goods inventory dropped 70%, raw material inventory dropped 45%, order lead times shortened by 38%, and obsolete inventory dropped 50%.

The report also notes that Lenovo has set up a dedicated FDE organization, attempting to turn front-line field experience into reusable assets. What merits study is the connection between scenario analysis, validation, delivery, operations, and asset accumulation—not using the percentages from this single case directly as targets for other projects.

Time and evidence boundaries: The news was published on October 27; this issue reflects observations as of October 30. The client is unnamed, and the results data comes from Lenovo via media reports, with no independent confirmation or audit from the client; baselines, statistical periods, human intervention, costs, and failed tasks are all insufficiently explained. The improvements cannot simply be added together, nor can all of them be attributed to AI.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Verify the task first, then discuss replication. Clearly document the business owner, inputs and outputs, data and permissions, POC samples, and acceptance criteria.
  • Separate delivery timelines from results timelines. A three-month delivery does not mean all effects occurred within the same period.
  • Probe metric definitions. For inventory, lead times, and obsolete stock, record the baseline, calculation method, statistical interval, and other changes over the same period.
  • Leave capabilities on the client side. Delivery should include, beyond the system itself, operating rules, eval samples, maintenance responsibilities, and how the client can iterate independently.
