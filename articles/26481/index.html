Today's Judgment

Today's signals span three dimensions, but they all point to the same conclusion:

1. The competitive landscape at the AI tool layer is converging—Claude Code holds 54% of the enterprise AI coding market, Codex CLI has 5 million weekly active users, and a duopoly is established; Meta announced it is building the Meta Compute cloud service to compete head-on with AWS/Azure/GCP, and compute supply is also centralizing.

2. Policy is starting to draw the lanes for enterprise AI deployment—China's Cyberspace Administration issued dedicated agent policies, and the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology proposed promoting 500 typical scenarios by 2027; the EU AI Act's obligations take effect in August; the White House is negotiating voluntary release standards with the three major AI companies.

3. OpenAI's GPT-5.6 full release is delayed due to government review, with initial access restricted to vetted partners—access to frontier models is shifting from "whoever bids highest" to "whoever passes review."

Taken together:

  • Tools are getting stronger, cheaper, and more standardized—this is the real-world footnote to "the cheaper AI tools get, the more valuable the FDE becomes."
  • When tools are no longer the bottleneck, the bottleneck shifts to "who can, on-site at enterprises, wire tools into business processes, get through compliance review, and deliver measurable results."
  • The policy signals from the Chinese market are very clear: smart manufacturing, energy, and transportation are priority directions, and compute vouchers are lowering the barrier for SMEs—the FDE's customer pool is expanding.

Key Signals

Claude Code leads the enterprise AI coding market with 54% market share, establishing a duopoly in developer tools

  • Source: Neura Market / IntuitionLabs | Analysis link
  • According to Menlo Ventures data, Claude Code holds 54% of the enterprise AI coding market, growing 6x in 8 months. 135K GitHub Stars (leading).
  • Codex CLI has reached 5 million weekly active users and 41.8 million npm downloads (vs. Claude Code's 37.9 million). In the SWE-bench Verified benchmark, Claude Opus 4.8 leads with 88.6%.
  • Gemini CLI stopped offering free service to individual developers on June 18, and its market share is shrinking.
  • 90% of developers will use at least one AI coding tool in 2026.
  • Why it matters: AI coding tools are already the core productivity lever for FDEs. The Claude Code + Codex duopoly means FDEs need to master both tools' strengths—Claude Code excels at long-context understanding and code refactoring, while Codex excels at rapid prototyping. Tool standardization also means the FDE's competitive edge increasingly lies not in "knowing how to use tools" but in "what business problems you solve with them."

China's Cyberspace Administration issues dedicated agent policy; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology proposes promoting 500 typical scenarios by 2027

  • Source: Central Cyberspace Administration | Original link
  • The Cyberspace Administration of China and other agencies released the "Implementation Opinions on the Standardized Application and Innovative Development of Agents," covering smart manufacturing, energy, transportation, and other fields. It requires developing production management agents that dynamically optimize production scheduling, resource allocation, and process coordination.
  • The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology proposed promoting 500 typical application scenarios by 2027 and cultivating agents for key industries.
  • The "Interim Measures for the Management of AI Human-Interaction Services" will take effect on July 15, 2026.
  • Many regions across China are issuing compute vouchers to lower the barrier for SMEs to access intelligent computing power. Cities including Shanghai and Zhuhai have already launched programs, directing compute toward the real economy such as industrial manufacturing.
  • Why it matters: China's AI agents have entered a dual-track phase of "policy-driven + industrial implementation." Smart manufacturing and industrial internet are directions explicitly supported by policy. The compute voucher policy means the AI deployment barrier for SMEs is dropping—the FDE's potential customer pool is systematically expanding.

Meta announces it is building the Meta Compute cloud service, competing head-on with AWS/Azure/GCP

  • Source: Bloomberg / Hipther AI Dispatch | Analysis link
  • Meta is building a Meta Compute cloud infrastructure division to sell its surplus AI compute to external customers. Meta's capital expenditure surged from $37.2 billion in 2024 to $69.6 billion in 2025, with a median 2026 estimate of $135 billion.
  • At the same time, Meta laid off about 8,000 people (roughly 10%), while 7,000 others were internally transferred to AI roles.
  • Why it matters: Meta's open-source models (the Llama family) plus its own cloud service could offer enterprises an integrated "model + compute" solution. For FDEs, this means the enterprise technology selection menu has gained another major option—open-source models + Meta Cloud vs. closed-source models + AWS/Azure—and helping enterprises make this selection decision is itself a high-value service.

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 release delayed due to government review, raising the bar for frontier model access

  • Source: AIToolsRecap / Fortune | Original link
  • OpenAI has delayed the full public release of GPT-5.6 due to the US government's demands for early access and additional regulatory review. Initial access is limited to a small number of vetted partners, with broad availability expected in mid-to-late July.
  • OpenAI proposed ceding roughly 5% equity to the US government (about $42.6 billion at current valuation), and Sam Altman is actively negotiating with the White House.
  • Why it matters: Access to frontier models is shifting from "whoever bids highest" to "whoever passes review." This is especially important for FDEs in highly regulated industries (finance, healthcare, government)—helping enterprises obtain compliance approval for frontier models, or even helping them determine "we can solve this without frontier models," will become a differentiating capability.

2026 is defined as China's "Year of the AI Agent," with MCP/A2A protocols moving toward standardization

  • Source: Tsinghua University / Frost & Sullivan | Reference link
  • Industry consensus: the chat paradigm is over, and AI competition is shifting to agents that "get things done." Communication protocols such as MCP and A2A are becoming standardized, dubbed "the TCP/IP of the agent era."
  • The number of foundation models is converging: Baichuan Intelligence is focusing on healthcare, and 01.AI is pivoting to enterprise custom solutions. Ant Group launched trusted agent interconnection technology (ASL) and a security framework.
  • Frost & Sullivan has launched the call for the "2026 China AI Agent Best Practices Ranking."
  • Why it matters: China's AI agents are penetrating deeply from general scenarios into vertical industries. Enterprise customers' focus has shifted from "can it be done" to "what's the ROI" and "is the data secure." MCP/A2A standardization means improved interoperability between agents, and what FDEs must solve on-site at enterprises is no longer "can it run" but "how do multiple agents coordinate, how are they governed, and how do you account for the numbers."

Takeaways for FDE Practitioners

  • Tool proficiency is a basic skill, not a competitive advantage: 90% of developers are already using AI coding tools, and the market shares of Claude Code and Codex show the tool layer is highly concentrated. The FDE's differentiation lies not in "knowing how to use tools" but in "using tools to solve real business problems on-site at enterprises."
  • Watch the pace of policy implementation in China: The Cyberspace Administration's AI agent special policy, the MIIT's 500-scenario target, and compute vouchers together form a complete policy push. FDEs serving domestic enterprise customers should focus on directions with explicit policy support such as smart manufacturing, energy, and transportation, and proactively learn the business language of these industries.
  • Move compliance capabilities up front: The EU AI Act takes effect in August, GPT-5.6 was delayed by review, and China's AI anthropomorphic interaction management measures take effect on July 15—regulation is tightening in three directions at once. FDEs need to shift compliance review from "patching it up after delivery" to "considering it at the solution design stage."
  • Help enterprises do "subtractive selection": With Meta Compute joining the competition, the menu of technology choices facing enterprises has grown longer. The value of an FDE is not to enumerate all the options for enterprises, but to help them rule out unsuitable ones—"your scenario doesn't need frontier models," "your data volume doesn't justify building your own cloud," "your compliance requirements rule out these three solutions"—doing subtraction is more valuable than doing addition.
