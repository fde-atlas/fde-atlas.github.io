Today's Judgment

Yesterday's focus was on how to prove that business actions are trustworthy after AI agents go live; today's new developments happened further upstream and at a larger scale: the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs is procuring Agentic AI, data, and collaboration foundations through an enterprise agreement worth up to $1.6 billion; ITC Infotech is first deploying Gemini Enterprise within its own organization, forming a "Customer Zero—validate—distill blueprints—replicate to clients" delivery model; Cisco and AMD are bringing desktop local agents under four categories of controls: network, token cost, behavior, and security.

Together, these three signals show that enterprise AI scaling is no longer just about building more agents—it requires answering three questions at once: how budgets enter formal procurement, how implementation experience becomes replicable assets, and how agents scattered across the cloud and employees' devices can be uniformly observed and governed. For Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), the delivery target is expanding from a single workflow to a three-layer system of "contracted outcomes, replication blueprints, and operational controls."

Key Signals

U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs: Up to $1.6 Billion Procurement of Agentic Enterprise License Agreement

  • Source: Salesforce | Original link
  • On July 24, Salesforce announced that the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) awarded an Agentic Enterprise License Agreement worth up to $1.6 billion over 3 years through its distribution network. The contract consists of a 1-year base period plus two optional renewal years.
  • Delivery scope: Missionforce will connect workflows and systems across the department, providing a 24×7 virtual contact center, patient triage, access and care coordination, and handling highly sensitive business with FedRAMP High authorization and HIPAA-ready agents.
  • Existing operational foundation: VA Health Connect has handled over 40.6 million veteran calls; Slack has been deployed to more than 150 medical and outpatient centers; SQUARES has over 2,100 authorized users. These figures reflect the existing Salesforce foundation and do not represent new results added by this contract.
  • Why it matters: This is a strong signal that enterprise agents are moving from project budgets into formal enterprise-level agreements. What is truly procurable is not a chatbot, but a combination of compliance foundations, unified data, cross-system workflows, 24/7 service, and continuous delivery capabilities.
  • Evidence boundary: The $1.6 billion is the total contract ceiling, not confirmed revenue or amounts already paid; the contract includes Salesforce platform, data, collaboration, and agent capabilities, and cannot all be counted as "AI agent revenue."

ITC Infotech × Google Cloud: Service Provider First Becomes "Customer Zero," Then Replicates the Implementation Blueprint

  • Source: Google Cloud | Original link
  • On July 23, ITC Infotech and Google Cloud announced that ITC Infotech will deploy Gemini Enterprise end-to-end within its own organization, first validating and scaling it, then delivering the implementation methodology and technical blueprints proven through internal practice to clients worldwide.
  • The two parties will establish a joint Center of Excellence covering industry solutions, agent and multi-agent orchestration, data modernization, security governance, sovereign cloud, and workforce upskilling.
  • Delivery model: internal deployment → business validation → outcome measurement → asset distillation → client replication. This is closer to sustainable FDE productization than directly selling vendor demo solutions to clients.
  • Why it matters: A service provider's credibility is shifting from "understanding models" to "having run it themselves first." FDE services should also turn internal workflows and existing projects into Customer Zero: publicly documenting pre-transformation baselines, intervention actions, outcome figures, and replicable assets.
  • Evidence boundary: The announcement has not yet disclosed the number of people covered by the deployment, the first batch of workflows, time saved, revenue contribution, or the number of client replications. At this point, only the organization and delivery model can be confirmed; no quantified business results can be claimed.

Cisco × AMD: Local Agents Evolve from Standalone Tools into a Manageable Enterprise Operating Layer

  • Source: Cisco | Original link
  • On July 23, Cisco disclosed a joint enterprise architecture with AMD built on Ryzen AI Halo, elevating desktop local inference and agents from standalone capabilities into an observable, governable, and isolatable enterprise system.
  • Four categories of controls: the network side only opens necessary access; tokenomics distinguishes local from cloud inference and accounts for costs; the behavior side restricts high-risk actions such as file deletion and tool calls; in the event of a serious trust failure, the relevant agents or models can be isolated via the network.
  • Management can view the utilization, throughput, and energy consumption of each agent on individual devices, and identify which cloud workloads are suitable for migration to local execution.
  • Why it matters: The control plane for enterprise AI will no longer manage only cloud APIs, but also local models and agents scattered across employees' computers. FDEs will need to deliver a unified asset inventory, model routing, cost attribution, behavior policies, and isolation procedures.
  • Evidence boundary: This is a joint solution and architecture description; no official customers, deployment scale, or savings figures have been announced, so it cannot yet be claimed to have been validated in large enterprises.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Rebuild existing projects as "Customer Zero" case studies: First record the pre-transformation baseline, then document intervention actions, business outcomes, and replicable assets.
  • Service proposals should list not only features, but also contracted outcomes, delivery assets, and operational governance, making it easier to enter enterprise procurement and renewal processes.
  • Build a unified asset inventory for cloud and local agents: deployment location, responsible parties, data scope, executable actions, model costs, and isolation methods.
  • For high-ticket projects, prioritize designing annual or phased agreements, replacing one-off delivery checklists with quantifiable business outcomes and continuous governance.
