How will the U.S. midterm elections, AI infrastructure investment, and tariffs change FDE demand? Decision criteria for engineers

Original title: 米中間選挙・AIインフラ投資・関税でFDE需要はどう変わる？エンジニアの判断軸

FDE総研 · 2026-11-05 · 10 min read

Summary

FDE Soken examines how the 2026 US midterms, AI infrastructure spending, and tariffs could affect demand for forward deployed engineers. It argues election outcomes reach FDE demand through budget, regulation, and procurement channels with lags of several quarters to a year, so no immediate career moves are warranted. AI infrastructure investment creates construction and semiconductor jobs, not FDE roles; demand rises more when companies seek returns on investments. Tariffs mainly hit hardware costs, while cloud bills are often more sensitive to exchange rates. The piece advises companies to split costs into four categories and predefine review triggers, and engineers to build one portable, end-to-end delivery track record.

Key points

  • Election effects reach FDE demand via budget, regulation, and procurement with multi-quarter lags.
  • AI infrastructure spending creates hardware jobs, not FDE roles; ROI pressure drives FDE demand.
  • Tariffs directly affect hardware costs; cloud fees are often driven more by currency moves.
  • Companies should categorize costs and predefine which official announcements trigger plan reviews.
  • Engineers should build one industry-agnostic track record from problem definition through operations.

The causal flow organized by FDE Soken. There are time lags between policy execution and investment decisions at each stage

  • GPU and cloud compute resources become easier to secure
  • Projects moving from PoC to production increase, and demand for FDEs who handle implementation rises first
  • The speed of vendor selection and contracting becomes a competitive differentiator
  • Procurement costs for equipment and compute rise, prompting a review of adoption plans
  • The weight placed on FDEs who can design around existing assets and optimize costs increases
  • Rather than large new projects, narrowly scoped, business-specific implementations are prioritized

How do U.S. midterm election results transmit to AI infrastructure investment and FDE demand?

The U.S. midterms (all 435 House seats and about one-third of the Senate) move FDE demand through three channels: budget, regulation, and procurement, each with its own time lag.

As of November 5, 2026, when this article was written, vote counting and certification were still ongoing in many districts. For that reason, FDE Soken does not draw firm conclusions about increases or decreases in FDE demand from the election results. According to major reporting confirmed as of 12:00 JST on November 5, 2026 (10:00 p.m. ET on November 4) (CNBC updated at 8:15 p.m., CBS News at 10:22 p.m.), the Democrats were on track to secure majorities in both the Senate and the House. In the Senate, Democrats held at least 54 seats and Republicans at least 42, with two seats undecided: Alaska and Mississippi (in Mississippi, Democratic candidate Mr. Kolom led by a narrow margin, with the vote gap reported variously as 65 to about 1,000 votes). In the House, Democrats held at least 250 seats (CNBC), while CBS News projected a final count of 251 to 268 seats, with 9 districts where AP had not yet called winners. All figures are preliminary and not final. Seat counts vary by report. Results should be verified against official announcements from each state's election authorities as the primary source, and treated separately from media projections of winners. What follows is an outline of "how the results transmit" that applies regardless of which party holds the majority.

How long does it take for changes in congressional composition to reach budgets and regulation?

From a change in congressional composition to its reflection in actual budgets and regulation usually takes several quarters to years.

The new Congress convenes in January of the following year, but it takes further time for spending bills and regulatory legislation to pass and enter implementation. The more a program depends on the federal budget, such as government procurement or AI-related subsidies, the more clearly the election's impact shows, but the longer it takes to appear. Private capital expenditure is driven more by interest rates, stock prices, and corporate earnings outlooks than by the election itself. Engineers aspiring to be FDEs have little need to change career decisions based on election night news; what matters is the budget deliberations from the following year onward.

Do changes in AI infrastructure investment directly translate into FDE job openings?

Changes in AI infrastructure investment do not directly translate into FDE job openings. What matters for FDE hiring is whether "on-the-ground deployment after investment" increases.

Investment in data centers and GPUs creates jobs mainly in construction, power, and semiconductors. What FDEs do is embed the built infrastructure into customers' business processes and support them until results appear. In FDE Soken's view, FDEs are more likely to be consulted in the "phase when returns on investment are demanded" than during the expansion phase. When reading infrastructure investment headlines, it is practical to separately check whether deployment and operations budgets are attached.

How do tariffs change Japanese companies' AI procurement, cloud costs, and deployment plans?

The impact of tariffs on Japanese companies' AI adoption becomes easier to assess by breaking it into four cost items—hardware, cloud, labor, and currency—based on official announcements from USTR and METI.

Tariff-covered items and rates can change with each official announcement. For example, on February 20, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that IEEPA (the International Emergency Economic Powers Act) does not grant the president authority to impose tariffs (a statutory interpretation, not a constitutional ruling). As a subsequent substitute measure, tariffs under Section 122 of the Trade Act (reported as an effective 10%) were used, which reportedly expired on July 24, 2026, with tariffs under Section 301 of the Trade Act taking effect the same day. Japan is said to fall into a category where the combined rate with MFN tariffs is 12.5%. It is also reported that Section 232 tariffs have been partially applied to semiconductors. These are based mainly on secondary sources, so please verify against the latest announcements (the Federal Register and USTR releases). This article does not make estimates assuming specific tariff rates. What FDE Soken uses is a framework for distinguishing "which cost items are affected by tariffs and which are not."

Which costs are more and less susceptible to tariffs?

Physical hardware is most susceptible to tariffs, while the impact on SaaS and labor costs often remains indirect.

Cost itemTariff impactMain drivers of changeHandling in FDE projects
On-premise GPUs and serversCan be directly affectedCovered items, country of origin, tariff ratesSet validity periods on quotes
Cloud usage feesIndirectDollar-denominated pricing, exchange rates, price increasesBuild usage caps into the design
SaaS and AI licensesIndirectExchange rates and vendor price revisionsTrack contract renewal periods
FDE and implementation labor costsBarely affectedLabor market supply and demandEasy to absorb with a domestic setup

In some cases, exchange rates affect cloud costs more than tariffs do. So rather than lumping everything together as "prices went up because of tariffs," breaking down the causes at the invoice line-item level makes explanations to management more accurate.

Should the implementation plan be postponed, or scaled down and continued?

In most cases, rather than a full postponement, continuing with a narrower scope of validation keeps the cost of restarting lower.

As a general rule, the harder it is to forecast costs, the more it makes sense to put large-scale configurations involving hardware purchases on hold and focus on a single business challenge that can be validated on existing cloud infrastructure. However, this is a general tendency rather than an actual case study, and it may not apply to projects in regulated industries or those with data residency constraints. For phased implementation design, see "How to Implement FDE"; for cost benchmarks, see "FDE Cost Benchmarks and Rates".

How does demand for FDEs differ by years of experience, current role, English proficiency, and company size?

The FDE Research Institute examines the impact of policy changes through four assumptions: years of experience, current role, English proficiency, and company size. Among these, English proficiency and company size can change how external conditions affect you.

Even with the same "FDE" title, US-headquartered companies like Palantir and domestic companies differ in budget sources and customer bases. The table below shows trends by assumption, independently compiled by the FDE Research Institute. Read it as directionality, not as numbers.

How does risk split by English proficiency and company size?

The stronger your English, the easier it is to access US projects—but the more directly you are exposed to US policy changes.

In projects for foreign-affiliated or US customers, changes to federal budgets and export controls reach the front lines quickly. Projects for large domestic companies move on Japanese budget cycles and management decisions, so the impact of US politics is indirect. At startups, the funding environment has a large impact. The required English level is summarized in "Is English Necessary for FDEs?".

Are FDEs more susceptible to policy changes than SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, and SIer engineers? Who is a poor fit?

Using the FDE Research Institute's comparison axes—budget source, distance from customers, and contract duration—we find that FDEs tend to be more susceptible to policy changes than SWEs, and to pivot faster than SIers.

How does the impact differ by role?

The closer a role is to the customer's budget, the sooner the impact appears. Among these, FDEs sit close to both budgets and the front lines.

Because FDEs work on a per-project basis, they feel the impact early during contraction phases. On the other hand, they have the flexibility to quickly switch to customers' new priority challenges. For details, see "Differences Between FDE and Consultants" and "Differences Between FDE and SIers".

Who is a poor fit for FDE work during periods of policy volatility?

People who find frequently shifting assumptions burdensome tend to experience more stress as FDEs during politically unstable periods.

  • People who want to start only after specifications are finalized
  • People more interested in technology selection itself than in customers' business challenges
  • People who tend to take project interruptions or pivots as personal failures

These are tendencies in aptitude, not measures of ability. For self-assessment, use "Who Is Suited to Be an FDE?".

What should Japanese companies and aspiring FDE engineers prepare now?

The FDE Research Institute's recommendation, assuming the uncertainty as of November 2026, is for companies to adopt "a design that can continue even if scaled down," and for individuals to build "a track record that transfers across industries." We update the basis as official government announcements are confirmed.

What should decision-makers at Japanese companies check now?

Decision-makers at Japanese companies will find decisions more consistent if they break AI implementation plans into cost items and assumptions, and decide in advance the conditions for revisiting them.

  • Break implementation plan costs into four items: hardware, cloud, licenses, and labor
  • List the assumptions that depend on US policy (procurement sources, data residency, export controls)
  • Decide in advance "which official announcements would trigger a review"
  • Separate what to outsource to external FDEs from what to keep in-house

As a general rule, deciding upfront—as in item 4—"what will remain in-house after contracts end" is thought to speed up decisions when the external environment changes.

What track record should aspiring FDE engineers build now?

For engineers aspiring to FDE roles, the fastest path is to build one track record of carrying "from problem discovery through operations" to completion, independent of any specific policy or industry.

Because policy tailwinds shift from industry to industry, a track record portable across industries is an advantage. Internal business improvement projects are fine. Go through the full cycle—defining the problem, preparing data, implementing, and driving adoption among users—and be ready to explain both the results and the limitations; this makes you more likely to be evaluated favorably in interviews. Application requirements and procedures are only finalized on each company's official careers page, such as Palantir's. The overall career picture is summarized in "What Is an FDE Career?".
