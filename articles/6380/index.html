Bella · An Agent’s integrated marketing fieldwork

AIelsen Agent Field Notes · 16 min read

Summary

This field note documents Bella, an AI agent, supporting a full product launch for creators and small studios — from keyword research and competitor comparison through FAB development, page and FAQ copy, TVC script review, concept imagery, and post-launch operations. The central argument is that marketing quality comes from judgment carried across stages: an early correction to the competitor comparison (rebuilding it on shared category dimensions from full official specifications rather than search summaries) improved positioning, FAQs, and creative work downstream. The agent's role is organizing facts, flagging unverified claims, and turning feedback into actionable deliverables, with humans handling final production decisions.

Key points

  • Bella, an AI agent, worked across an entire launch, not a single demo.
  • A rejected competitor comparison was rebuilt on shared category dimensions from official specs.
  • FAB work translated features into benefits for creators, studios, and beginners.
  • Deliverables included keyword tiers, FAQs, livestream framework, and concept images.
  • Post-launch, the agent mapped user questions to content and maintained video versions.

01 / THE ASSIGNMENT

The reason to buy must be clear.

This project served creators and small studios. The product combined hardware, materials, software and multiple ways of creating, so the marketing challenge extended well beyond writing good copy.

Creators care about expressive freedom, small-business owners about orders and workflows, and first-time users about learning to use it. One specification sheet cannot answer everyone.

Product information, pages, FAQs, ads, livestreams and films are connected work. Feature names, limits on promises and core selling points need ongoing alignment.

Continuously organize product and market information, turn facts into marketing judgments and judgments into usable assets, then use team feedback and post-launch questions to revise the next round of work.

02 / THE WHOLE WORKFLOW

One body of work carried forward.

The workflow below is reconstructed from project records, with client and commercial information removed. Expand each stage to see its inputs, key judgments, actions and influence on the next step.

Turn a request for a keyword sheet into the starting point for later marketing work.

  • Inputs
  • Product materials, stage-specific objectives and a keyword task. Information was scattered across training material, documentation and conversations.
  • Judgment
  • Unless product boundaries, target users and adjacent categories are clear from the outset, keywords, FAB and later advertising can all drift off course.
  • Actions
  • Separate specifications, features, use cases, user questions and open items. Build a keyword matrix, then organize product specifications, user profiles and an initial FAB structure.
  • Deliverables and handover
  • A keyword matrix, product information sheet and initial selling-point structure became the shared foundation for later research, content and advertising recommendations.

Key value: turn an isolated request into a working brief that later tasks can keep using.

Move from search summaries and internal selling points to complete specifications and category standards.

  • Feedback received
  • The first competitor comparison covered too few dimensions. The problem went beyond missing rows: it relied too heavily on search summaries and used this product’s selling points to choose the comparison criteria.
  • Key judgment
  • A comparison that favors us is not necessarily the comparison customers need. Establish common category criteria first, then examine each option’s strengths and limitations.
  • Actions
  • Read complete official specifications and user manuals. Define category-wide dimensions before filling in each competitor, retain sources and unknowns, and cross-check conflicting data.
  • Deliverables and handover
  • A more complete competitor matrix and evidence of differentiation gave positioning, FAQs and creative reviews a stronger basis than an isolated search result.

Key value: feedback changed the standard for future research as well as the document.

Explain who benefits from a feature and why it matters.

  • Materials used
  • Verified features and specifications, competitor comparisons, and the tasks and concerns of different users.
  • Key judgment
  • The same capability can mean expressive possibilities for creators, a wider range of orders for studios, or a learning challenge for beginners. The benefit becomes meaningful when the audience is specified.
  • Actions
  • Map Features to Advantages and Benefits. Prioritize selling points, brand claims and evidence needs, listing unconfirmed technical and commercial points separately rather than turning them into promises.
  • Deliverables and handover
  • The FAB data layer, selling-point priorities, messaging structure and open-item list informed the page narrative, advertising angles and film presentation.

Key value: translate “what our product has” into “why this type of customer cares.”

Search captures intent, content builds understanding, and demonstrations answer questions.

  • Materials used
  • Keywords, target audiences, product differentiation and the stages from pre-launch to launch.
  • Key judgment
  • People at different buying stages need different material. Before orders open, transactional keywords and conversion messages also require a different approach from the live-sales stage.
  • Actions
  • Recommend tiers for brand, category-intent, transactional and competitor-related keywords. Expand the livestream structure around demonstrations, Q&A, brand introductions and interaction, and organize known limitations for pre-review communication.
  • Deliverables and handover
  • Advertising and SEO keyword tiers, a livestream execution framework and reviewer briefing points gave each channel a clear purpose beyond producing another piece of content.

Key value: connect content, the customer journey and channel actions in one working plan.

Every touchpoint shares the facts while answering different questions.

  • Inputs
  • The brand team’s baseline page copy, product material and user questions that needed answers during pre-launch.
  • Key judgment
  • The main page establishes value; the FAQ addresses decision barriers. Speed, post-processing time, consumable costs and maintenance consumption need more explanation than “easy to use.”
  • Actions
  • Organize questions around core capabilities, software workflows, accessories and sales policies. Flag specifications still needed from the product team, then use page selling points to align later advertising, film and FAQ language.
  • Deliverables and handover
  • Copy collaboration, an FAQ framework, missing-evidence requirements and consistent wording. Original brand copy retains its attribution; the Agent focuses on organizing, filling gaps and aligning assets.

Key value: help customers understand while reducing repeated explanations and contradictory messages across channels.

Check whether each selling point appears in the visuals, on-screen text and voiceover.

  • Inputs
  • The existing film script and page selling-point document. The original script already contained strong opening transformations and tabletop, family and art narratives.
  • Key judgment
  • Visual appeal alone is not enough. Some features appeared but were obscured by technical terminology; others were implicit in the visuals and would not automatically be understood.
  • Actions
  • Classify the script as clearly expressed, unclear, implicit or missing. For each gap, specify the issue, proposed change and visual treatment, prioritizing product understanding and reasons to buy.
  • Deliverables and handover
  • A structured review, prioritized changes and concept references turned “this needs to be better” into requirements the production team could discuss and act on.

Key value: retain the original script’s strengths while helping the film carry more of its marketing task.

Make settings, actions, shot details and emotion concrete.

  • Feedback received
  • The creative work needed a stronger story, emotion and a believable everyday atmosphere. The idea of cards coming to life needed to become more than a written description.
  • Key judgment
  • “Premium,” “spectacular” and “warm” are not shooting instructions. Specify who does what, what viewers see first and how an action makes a product capability understandable.
  • Actions
  • Iterate on tabletop gaming, family memories and tactile art. Produce second-round concept images and magical-card references that turn full-color models, personal creation and raised texture into visual proposals people can review.
  • Deliverables and handover
  • Concept images, storyboard recommendations and revision records gave the team a shared discussion object. The project team handled final filming, production and release.

Key value: turn abstract feedback into visible creative references people can point to and revise.

Carry the work from promotional content into answering questions and maintaining versions.

  • Inputs
  • Post-launch comments, common concerns, stage-specific communication needs and revision feedback on a milestone video.
  • Key judgment
  • When users ask for software demonstrations or information about compatibility, maintenance and running costs, slogans cannot replace evidence. Content needs to answer the questions most relevant to the current decision.
  • Actions
  • Group buying barriers and propose matching FAQs, demonstrations, updates and third-party reviews. Contribute to milestone-video iterations and turn version regressions into asset-completeness checks.
  • Deliverables and handover
  • Growth-operations recommendations, question-to-content mappings, video versions and reusable methods. Previously confirmed assets are retained through the next visual revision.

Key value: the product understanding built earlier continues to serve real user feedback.

03 / MARKETING JUDGMENT

judgment connects the stages.

Producing a table is straightforward. The harder question is whether it shapes the message, channels, page and shots, helping the team decide what to say first, to whom and with what evidence.

THE STRATEGY CHAIN

  • Define the category boundariesWhich capabilities are easily confused? Which differences need explaining?
  • Choose a specific audienceWhat matters to hobby creators, designers and custom-production businesses?
  • Identify buying reasons and barriersWhat can it do, can I learn to use it, and will it be difficult to maintain?
  • Set the order of informationEstablish the value before expanding on technology and evidence.
  • Turn decisions into channel and asset tasksPages explain, films demonstrate, FAQs answer and demos verify.

A real correction

“The competitor comparison needs more dimensions.”

Paraphrased from project feedback, not a chat screenshot or verbatim quotation.

After this feedback, Bella’s task was to rebuild the comparison method, beyond adding a few favorable specifications.

  • Source depth: Search summaries → Complete specifications and user manuals.
  • Comparison logic: Our selling points → Shared category dimensions.
  • Fact management: A single conclusion → Item-level sources and open questions.
  • Downstream effect: Positioning, FAQs and creative recommendations shared this more reliable working brief.

These anonymous examples follow the project’s working method; they are not direct copies of the client’s original table. Switch between three typical capabilities.

FFEATURE

Image-assisted model generation

Begin modeling with an existing photo or image, reducing the steps needed to start from a blank canvas.

AADVANTAGE

Connect several stages

Creation, model preparation and output form a workflow rather than a list of separate software names.

BBENEFIT

A starting point for a first creation

Give non-specialists an understandable first step that addresses their concern about whether they can model.

Show a continuous “photo → model → finished object” process, explaining which steps still need manual work. The film demonstrates the process; the FAQ explains the conditions of use.

FFEATURE

Automatic cleaning and temperature-controlled maintenance

The original draft grouped several maintenance technologies together, packing in information without making it easy to understand.

AADVANTAGE

Reduce maintenance risks such as clogging

Connect each technical mechanism to the problem it addresses, without implying that automation eliminates maintenance.

BBENEFIT

Less manual maintenance work

Studios care about preparation for the next job and continued operation, beyond the names of individual features.

A mechanism animation plus a clear statement of the practical benefit. Detailed FAQs explain cleaning modes, idle-period maintenance and consumable use, without promising that clogging can never occur.

FFEATURE

Multiple materials and modular workflows

Different substrates, accessories and production formats serve different custom-production tasks.

AADVANTAGE

Serve a wider range of production needs

Turn scattered sample displays into a process showing customer artwork, preparation and batch production.

BBENEFIT

Turn creative capability into a service

Help small studios see the custom services they could offer, beyond a table of attractive samples.

Specific order scenarios, workflow demonstrations, material conditions and cost explanations. The ability to serve a need is not a guarantee of profitability.

The project produced keyword tiers, FAQs, reviewer briefing points and a livestream framework alongside the main visual work.

Distinguish brand defense, category needs, competitor comparisons and transactional intent, matching information to the launch stage.

The main page explains the core value; FAQs add capability limits, maintenance, costs, accessories and policies.

Organize known limitations and applicable conditions before a demonstration, grounding reviewer communication in the actual product.

Arrange demonstrations, Q&A, brand stories and interaction into a running order, with clear asset and execution requirements.

04 / FROM CONCEPT TO FILM

visible in the proposal and the film.

The project owner reported that several recommendations and creative ideas entered final production. Four examples use original material to examine how scenes, actions and product benefits were organized, beyond visual resemblance.

01 Tabletop gaming: from printing a few models to creating a world

Product capability: full-color models + scene building / Marketing task: make creative value tangible

Stronger setting and emotion

Interaction, fantasy characters, a tabletop story and a warm atmosphere.

Models become part of an interactive experience within a complete tabletop setting.

An individual sample can show that printing is possible without explaining why someone would want the product. The models need to connect with users’ interests and relationships.

Building on the original father-and-son tabletop theme, Bella translated feedback about story, magic and character actions into second-round concept images for the team to discuss.

The tabletop world and interactive narrative remain, with terrain and groups of characters conveying creative freedom. The final film did not reproduce the concept’s fire-breathing effect scene for scene.

From showing an object to showing its use.Technical capability becomes understandable through the experience these models can create. Human interaction and the complete world connect the narrative.

02 Family memories: give software capabilities an emotional destination

Product capability: images to three-dimensional objects / Marketing task: make the process easier to understand and begin

The same value, a different execution

Bring everyday images, drawings and personalized objects together.

A family photo becomes a three-dimensional scene, making the result of modeling visible.

Software names and “AI modeling” alone are difficult to picture. Beginners need to understand where to start and what they can get at the end.

The review identified stacked software terms and recommended clarifying the creative process. The concept then placed family images, a child’s drawing and everyday objects into a use context.

The film shows taking a photo, generating a model and creating a three-dimensional family piece. It carries forward the value of turning personal memories into objects; its people and product forms do not replicate the concept exactly.

Image generation and the family narrative were already in the original script. This stage’s contribution was clearer expression and a more concrete setting. The solid-color patch in the frame removes small device information.

03 Tactile art: turn a technical specification into a reason to look closer

Product capability: raised texture and color / Marketing task: make the difference visually understandable

Setting + Detail evidence

The artwork, viewer and act of close inspection establish an art-viewing experience.

The magnifier and macro detail make “three-dimensional” visible.

Texture height alone is hard to imagine. The specification needs translating into surface relief, light and the act of looking.

Following the original art and close-inspection direction, Bella added scene references, texture close-ups and scale suggestions, making tactility a concrete visual requirement.

The gallery gives the object an artistic context; the magnifier emphasizes texture detail. The setting and close-up together explain why it deserves attention.

The setting gives meaning; the macro shot gives evidence.The creative work moves beyond “rich colors” to show how the pieces are created, displayed and appreciated.

04 Transparent material: make a missing point of difference visible

Product capability: combining transparency and color / Marketing task: complete the explanation of an important capability

From a review gap to a visual

Recommend adding a transparent model so an important capability is not omitted.

A transparent shell and colored internal structure make the combined capability immediately visible.

The review found that an important transparent-material capability was not clearly shown. A focus on full color alone would omit another area of creative possibility.

Add a transparent model and comparison-based visual references, turning an abstract material capability into an object viewers can see.

The released film includes a shot combining transparency and color. Its object differs from the concept reference, while communicating the same capability that needed a clearer explanation.

These comparisons show connected collaborative work: the original script, review, discussion, concepts and final production build on one another. Recommendations reaching execution demonstrate value; continuity across the campaign remains the central story.

and what viewers understand.

The TVC review compared page selling points with the film’s presentation. The “before” and “recommendation” descriptions below are anonymous summaries; observations were checked against the released film and page. Individual final lines are not simply attributed to one person’s original writing.

KEEP

Already clear

Retain strong shots and effective storytelling without changing them for its own sake.

CLARIFY

Present but unclear

Unpack technical terminology and explain why users should care.

MAKE VISIBLE

Implicit in the visuals

Add the necessary labels or actions rather than asking viewers to infer the meaning.

ADD

Not yet covered

Fill gaps in order of importance without turning the film into a specification list.

Before

Several software names and “AI modeling” were crowded into a single on-screen text element.

Recommendation

Show the steps from an image to a model to a finished piece, explaining the value for someone getting started.

Recommendation

Before

Texture specifications were present, but viewers lacked an intuitive sense of the thickness, surface and effect.

Recommendation

Add close-ups and references so the relief can be seen and the texture imagined.

Before

Several accessories and materials were implicit in the samples, but their individual uses were not explained.

Recommendation

Connect modules, materials and tasks so studios can understand the custom-production needs they could serve.

Before

The original script had a strong opening built on changing forms, but needed a clearer overarching message and emotional ending.

Recommendation

Use the opening to establish creative range and the ending to return to what people can accomplish, rather than repeating a feature list.

Release observation: the film opens with different creative forms and closes on the value of making creation available to more people. The final slogan differs from the proposed version and cannot be described as a verbatim adoption.

A recommendation may also appear on the page without entering the main film. Text-to-model generation, for example, receives a separate section on the page. A complete campaign considers how information reaches the right medium, beyond whether it appears in one particular film.

06 / AFTER THE LAUNCH

keep turning user questions into content.

Bella’s work continued after the campaign deliverables were handed over. Post-launch questions, feedback and new communication tasks fed into the same product understanding and content system.

01 / READ THE QUESTIONS

  • How does the software actually work?
  • What do consumables and everyday use cost?
  • Do the accessories, materials and modes suit my task?
  • What needs doing during idle periods and for maintenance?

02 / CHOOSE THE RESPONSE

  • Unclear operation → A continuous demonstration
  • Unclear information → FAQs and scope explanations
  • Insufficient trust → Hands-on tests and third-party reviews
  • Gaps in understanding → Updates, diagrams and focused Q&A

03 / UPDATE THE WORK

  • Identify additional shots and evidence needed
  • Bring recurring questions back into the content structure
  • Align the team’s external wording
  • Retain versions and the next review items

Those problems became reusable working requirements rather than being hidden.

Research quality

The first research round exposed gaps in dimensions and sources. The improvement was to define category criteria, read complete material, then resolve conflicts and unknowns, beyond adding more search results.

Communication quality

“Make it feel stronger” needs translating into shots, actions, props, space and emotion. Visual references gave the project owner and production team something specific to discuss.

Version quality

Milestone-video iterations included a regression in previously confirmed material. This led to content-completeness checks, version labels and human review before release.

07 / THE COLLABORATION MODEL

the team delivers together.

Integrated campaign capability means understanding and connecting several professional stages. Bella handles research, organization, recommendations and creative outputs; people and teams make key decisions and carry out final production.

BUSINESS OWNER

Set the business direction, supply and confirm product facts, judge priorities, give creative feedback, and decide trade-offs and external commitments.

CONTINUOUS MARKETING WORK

Carry context forward, research and verify information, build FAB and content structures, propose strategy, collaborate on copy, review scripts, produce concepts, follow feedback and capture reusable methods.

PRODUCTION & RELEASE

Provide existing ideas and content; execute product work, filming, design and post-production; then create and release final assets within project constraints.

The Agent’s business value lies in carrying information, judgment, assets and feedback across stages, reducing repeated explanations, messaging gaps and handover loss. It does not mean doing everyone’s job.

08 / WHAT CAN BE REUSED

can start from an established way of working.

The client’s material cannot be copied, but the working method can be reused. Breaking the campaign experience into modules that can be handed over, reviewed and reused retains more value than a few good prompts alone.

Editable Markdown template · No original client material

  • Brief and fact sheetObjectives, audiences, material, product boundaries, owners and open questions.
  • Research and competitive comparisonCategory dimensions, official sources, evidence of differences, conflicting information and unknowns.
  • FAB and information structureFeature—Advantage—Benefit—Evidence—Audience—Channel.
  • Campaign asset checklistThe individual jobs of pages, advertising, FAQs, reviews, livestreams and films.
  • Script and concept reviewSelling-point coverage and clarity, how shots provide evidence, and how feedback becomes changes.
  • Launch feedback and version updatesUser barriers, the next evidence required, content updates, asset completeness and version checks.
