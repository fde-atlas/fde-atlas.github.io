A PdM (Product Manager) is a role that defines a product's direction, roadmap, and priorities, and works across multiple disciplines—engineers, designers, business teams—to drive results. Often called the "CEO of the product," a PdM serves as the bridge between business and technology. For a more detailed explanation of the role, including the difference between PdM and PjM and why "PdM in name only" roles arise, see What Is a Product Manager? Roles, Differences from PjM, and Why "PdM in Name Only" Roles Arise.

A "freelance PdM" refers to an independent way of working that provides product management expertise under service contracts rather than as a full-time employee of a specific company. Styles vary widely depending on working days and scope of involvement, from dedicating to a single company to juggling multiple clients.

Note that there is a similar role, the "PM (Project Manager)," but while a PdM is responsible for "deciding what to build," a PM is responsible for "managing the process of how to build it"—a different role.

Multiple survey data points confirm growing demand in the freelance PdM market.

  • Average monthly rate of ¥939,000 (as of February 2026): According to Freelance Start's ongoing survey, the average rate for PdMs (Product Managers) is ¥939,000 (+¥11,000/+1.2% month-over-month), continuing to rise for six consecutive months
  • Top 5 in corporate adoption rate: According to an En Japan survey, PdM ranks in the top 5 at 33.8% in the ranking of roles for which companies use IT freelancers
  • Market expected to grow 1.6x in 10 years: The same survey shows the overall IT freelance market is expected to grow 1.6x over 10 years, and 60% of companies currently using IT freelancers say they want to increase usage going forward

There are three main reasons why the future looks bright for freelance PdMs.

1. Absolute shortage of product talent In Japan, the PdM talent market is less mature than those for engineers or sales, and the supply of high-quality freelance PdMs cannot keep up with demand. In particular, "those with growth-phase experience," "those with BtoB SaaS experience," and "those with experience integrating AI features" are chronically in short supply.

2. Acceleration of generative AI × product development Companies integrating generative AI into their products are rapidly increasing, and demand for PdMs of the AI era—who can decide "what to automate with AI" and "how to deliver value to users"—is exploding.

3. Increase in new product projects driven by DX initiatives As more companies pursue digitalization of existing operations and new business development, cases of outsourcing to external PdMs when no in-house PdM talent exists are increasing.

The greatest strength of freelance PdMs is the ability to take on multiple projects simultaneously. Below is a realistic portfolio example.

However, the above is an estimate based on contract rates. Actual take-home pay is the amount after deducting income tax, resident tax, national health insurance, national pension, and business expenses, so it is realistic to consider take-home pay as roughly 60–70% of the contract rate.

Freelance PdM skills are easiest to organize when viewed as a three-layer structure: "Business, Technology, and UX/Design."

Business Layer

  • Product strategy formulation (vision definition, identifying market opportunities)
  • KPI and OKR design and progress management
  • Competitive analysis and market research (both quantitative and qualitative)
  • Understanding business models and planning monetization strategies
  • Stakeholder management and presentations to management

Technology Layer

  • Basic understanding of system architecture (APIs, databases, cloud)
  • Understanding of development processes (Agile, Scrum, Kanban)
  • Evaluating technical debt and making prioritization decisions
  • Knowledge of applying generative AI and LLMs to products (particularly valued as of 2026)

UX/Design Layer

  • User research (interviews, surveys, usability testing)
  • Creating user stories and journey maps
  • Prototyping basics (knowledge of Figma operations)
  • A/B test design, analysis, and decision-making

Especially as of 2026, data analysis skills combining Google Analytics 4 and BigQuery, as well as knowledge of integrating LLMs and generative AI into products, are key differentiators that significantly influence rates.

In rate negotiation, the most persuasive factor is "quantifiable results." PdMs with achievements like the following tend to be highly valued.

  • Growth achievements: DAU/MAU improvement of X%, churn rate reduction of X%, LTV improvement, etc.
  • Launch achievements: Released an MVP in X months and acquired X initial customers
  • Organizational contributions: Built out the development process from a state with no PdM, improving release velocity by X%
  • AI utilization achievements: Released a generative AI feature in X weeks, demonstrating impact on X metrics
  • Domain expertise: Involved in X projects as a FinTech regulatory compliance PdM

Cases where freelancers struggle to land projects after going independent as freelance PdMs share common patterns.

  • Vague specialization: A pitch of "I can do anything" fails to resonate. Narrowing your strengths to a specific phase, domain, or tool makes it easier to match with projects
  • Failure to articulate achievements: If you cannot communicate "what problem you solved, how, and how the numbers changed" rather than just listing your responsibilities, you won't be evaluated highly
  • Dependence on a single agent: Registering with multiple platforms and increasing access channels to non-public projects is important
  • Undervaluing referrals: Many high-rate freelance PdM projects come from personal networks and referrals, not agents

To negotiate freelance PdM projects on favorable terms, a 1-2 page achievements document (profile sheet) is effective. Including the following elements is recommended.

  • Profile summary: Your specialty, preferred phase, and main tools in 3 lines or fewer
  • Achievement highlights: 3-5 projects in the format "problem → action → result (numbers)"
  • List of tools: Specific names of product management, data analysis, and AI tools
  • Availability conditions: Days per week, remote work availability, preferred industries, industries to avoid

Posting on X (formerly Twitter) and LinkedIn helps build your brand as a freelance PdM and can prompt outreach from startup founders and VCs. Examples of effective content include the following.

  • Product development insights (framework explanations, failure stories, anti-patterns)
  • Lessons from projects you were involved in (within the bounds of confidentiality obligations)
  • Original perspectives on AI and product trends
  • Practical tips on user research and data analysis

Pattern 1: Corporate PdM → Freelance PdM This pattern involves building 3-5+ years of experience as a PdM at a startup or corporation and going independent with the motivation of "wanting to apply my experience to more companies." It is the most standard transition path.

Pattern 2: Engineer → PdM → Freelance PdM This pattern involves transitioning to PdM with an engineering background, then moving to freelancing. Deep technical and specification understanding becomes a strength, making it easier to land high-rate projects.

Pattern 3: Consultant / Business role → PdM → Freelance PdM This pattern involves transitioning to PdM from a business-side career such as strategy consulting or business analysis, and leveraging a business strategy perspective as a freelancer. It offers strengths in enterprise DX projects.

To secure stable income as a freelance PdM, it is recommended to make the following preparations before going independent.

1. Land your first project as a side job before going independent If you acquire 1-2 side projects of 1-2 days per week while still employed before going independent, you can avoid a period of zero income. This is a safe transition method practiced by many freelance PdMs.

2. Inventory and quantify your achievements Re-articulate your past experience in the format "problem → action → quantified result." In particular, since PdMs are judged on "what you achieved" rather than "what you were responsible for," make sure you know your KPI changes in concrete numbers.

3. Deciding between incorporation and sole proprietorship The higher your monthly rate, the greater the benefits of incorporation. If you can expect a sustained monthly income of 700,000 yen or more, it is worth consulting an accountant about incorporating.

4. Pre-registering with freelance agents Registering your profile with major agents before going independent and understanding project volume and rate levels will help you hit the ground running after independence.

Registering with multiple agents is the norm Many freelance PdMs register simultaneously with 2-3 agents. Since each agent holds different projects, registering with multiple agents broadens your access to non-public projects.

Clearly communicate your "ideal project profile" at the first meeting By specifically stating your preferences for rate, working days, industry, and stage, the accuracy of project introductions from your agent improves. It is effective to state conditions numerically, such as "¥800,000+ per month, 3 days per week, BtoB SaaS, growth stage."

Access to non-public projects Most projects held by agents are non-public (not listed on job sites). Building relationships with agents to access non-public projects with less competition is a shortcut to securing high rates.

A. A PdM (Product Manager) is responsible for decisions about "what to build (WHAT)," handling product vision, roadmaps, and feature prioritization. A PM (Project Manager) is responsible for managing the process of "how to build (HOW)," mainly handling schedules, resources, and risk management. While their roles sometimes overlap, in the context of freelance projects the required skills and deliverables differ. PdM projects tend to have higher rates.

A. Even without a formal PdM title, you can secure projects if you have experience performing duties close to those of a PdM (such as specification planning, requirements definition, user research, and KPI management). Transitions from engineers, designers, or business analysts are not uncommon. However, since most freelance PdM projects assume "hands-on PdM experience," going independent with zero experience is difficult. We recommend first building a track record as a PdM in a full-time or side job before going independent.

A. Freelance PdMs with an engineering background have very high market value. Judging technical feasibility, communicating deeply with development teams, and understanding system design are major differentiators from business-side PdMs. In particular, there is strong demand for PdMs with engineering backgrounds in areas such as "implementing generative AI features," "Fintech products with complex backends," and "API-centric BtoB SaaS."

A. Yes, it is possible. In fact, many freelance PdMs work with multiple clients simultaneously, and some handle 2-3 companies at once. However, note that you need to check contracts for non-compete obligations (prohibiting simultaneous provision to competitors), strictly manage each company's confidential information, and manage your schedule so working days do not physically overlap.

The main approaches are as follows: ① Registering with specialized agents such as FLEXY, Levtech Freelance, and Relance (multiple registrations recommended), ② Acquiring inbound projects through outbound communication on X (formerly Twitter) and LinkedIn, ③ Networks and referrals from previous employers, communities, and conferences, and ④ Attending networking events in the startup scene. Since most high-rate projects are non-public, building relationships with agents is important.

By choosing blue-form tax filing, you can receive a special deduction of up to ¥650,000. If your income is high, incorporating (as a LLC/GK or corporation) instead of remaining a sole proprietor may optimize your tax burden. We recommend consulting a tax accountant.

Clearly stating your remote preference when registering with agents makes it easier to narrow down projects that match your conditions.

Freelance PdM is, as of 2026, one of the most market-valuable positions among IT professions. As the data shows—an average monthly rate of ¥939,000 and six consecutive months of increases—demand for product talent continues to rise structurally.

Here is a summary of the key points of this article.

  • Current market conditions: The average monthly rate for freelance PdMs is 939,000 yen (February 2026), rising for six consecutive months. PdM also ranks in the top 5 in corporate job-type rankings, with demand consistently high
  • Types of projects: The required role and rate range differ depending on the development phase (0→1, growth, scale) and domain (BtoB SaaS, AI, FinTech, etc.)
  • Rate benchmarks: 600,000–900,000 yen per month with 3–5 years of experience, and 900,000–1,200,000 yen or more per month for senior-level and above. Earning an annual income of 15–20 million yen by working with multiple clients is also realistic
  • Essential skills: Three-layer skills spanning business, technology, and UX/design. As of 2026, knowledge of generative AI utilization and data analysis skills (GA4, BigQuery) are especially valued
  • Project acquisition strategies: Focusing expertise on a specific phase or domain, articulating quantitative achievements, registering with multiple agents, and inbound leads through social media posting are effective
  • Independence roadmap: The safest path is to secure your first project through side work while still employed. At least 2–3 years of hands-on PdM experience is a guideline before starting side work

PdM skills—placing the product at the core of business growth and bridging business and technology from a user perspective—carry very high market value when going independent as a freelancer. In a business environment where DX and generative AI adoption are accelerating, a career as a freelance PdM is one of the most attractive options, simultaneously enabling diverse experience unattainable as a full-time employee and breaking through the income ceiling.
