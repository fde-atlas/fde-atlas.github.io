U.S. Big Tech Races to Embed AI Engineers Inside Client Firms

Seoul Economic Daily · 2026-11-03 · 2 min read

Summary

U.S. big tech firms are rapidly expanding forward deployed engineers (FDEs) who work on-site with clients to put AI into production, seeking stable revenue amid massive AI infrastructure spending. Microsoft launched Microsoft Frontier Company, planning to deploy about 6,000 FDEs with a reported $2.5 billion investment. AWS announced a $1 billion dedicated unit, and Google Cloud is hiring FDEs. Anthropic formed Odd with Anthropic, a joint venture with Blackstone and Goldman Sachs, while OpenAI's Deployment Company raised $4 billion at a $10 billion valuation. Full text

U.S. big tech companies pouring vast amounts of cash into artificial intelligence data centers are accelerating efforts to build stable revenue businesses. A leading example is the expansion of forward-deployed engineers, or FDEs, who work on site at client companies to help put AI systems into actual operation.

Microsoft in July set up a dedicated unit called Microsoft Frontier Company to deploy about 6,000 FDEs to customers using its cloud and other services, the Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported on the 2nd. The engineers are dispatched to client firms, where they form a single team with the client's own engineers to design, develop and maintain AI systems. Microsoft is reported to be planning to invest $2.5 billion (about 3.35 trillion won) in the unit.

Through the FDEs, Microsoft aims to redesign corporate clients' workflows and business processes to raise their use of AI. "The biggest changes from AI will happen in workflows and business processes," Rodrigo Kede Lima, president of Microsoft Frontier Company, said in an interview with Nikkei xTech. That requires FDEs who are versed not only in technology but also in the characteristics and regulatory structures of each industry, he said.

Holger Hürtgen, an FDE at consulting firm McKinsey, said a new large language model alone is not enough for a company. What matters is tailoring the model to the problem at hand to generate real business results, he said.

Beyond lifting corporate clients' use of AI, there is another reason big tech is entering the FDE market. Securing new revenue sources has grown more important as astronomical upfront AI investment proceeds. The drain on big tech's free cash flow has become a factor amplifying recent volatility in AI-related stocks. Dispatching FDEs to encourage companies to adopt their AI models and related services also offers a path to stable monetization.

Amazon Web Services announced in June that it would create a dedicated unit of its own, with investment reported at $1 billion. Google Cloud, the cloud business of Google, is also moving in earnest to hire FDEs.

Anthropic and OpenAI, the startups leading AI model development, have joined the trend. Both also share the trait of being headed toward initial public offerings. Anthropic this year set up Odd with Anthropic, a joint venture with Blackstone, Goldman Sachs and others to provide AI services. The company is tasked with introducing Anthropic's AI model Claude to mid-sized firms in industries such as finance, manufacturing and health care. Odd with Anthropic, formally launched on July 15 this year, also acquired Casper Studio, an AI consulting firm with on-site consultants.

OpenAI has created its own joint venture, Deployment Company, to sell AI to businesses. TPG, Advent International and SoftBank of the U.S. and Japan took part in its establishment, and Brookfield Asset Management and Bain Capital, among others, valued the venture at $10 billion and invested $4 billion.

Still, some worry that FDEs could encroach on the role of existing partner companies. If big tech takes on system construction and operation directly through FDEs, that could overlap with the business of system integration firms that have cooperated with them in opening up the market. In response, Google is taking steps such as setting up a $750 million fund for partner companies.
