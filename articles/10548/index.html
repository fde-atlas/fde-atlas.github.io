Recruiting demand is surging! What exactly does "the hottest job in the AI world" involve?

Original title: 招聘需求暴涨！“AI圈最火岗位”，到底是干嘛的？

Summary

A China News Service (Chinajingwei) report examines the explosive rise of the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role. A LinkedIn labor-market report cited in the article says new FDE positions grew 42-fold between 2023 and 2025, far outpacing the 13-fold growth of AI engineer roles. OpenAI, Anthropic, ByteDance and Ant Digital Technology are all hiring, with salaries reaching the equivalent of one million yuan or more; one US-based FDE reported headhunters offering $400,000 packages. Practitioners describe the job as sitting between pre-sales, product management and engineering: visiting customers, understanding their workflows and data, and personally building AI solutions that fit. It is not an entry-level role, typically requiring prior product or development experience. In China, however, million-yuan salaries are limited to top talent; junior FDEs earn roughly 20,000-30,000 yuan monthly. Interviewees compare its trajectory to the product manager boom of a decade ago and see it as a springboard for entrepreneurship.

Full text

Annual salary of a million, hiring demand up 42-fold! What exactly is the "hottest job in the AI world"?

BEIJING, June 5 (Luo Kun, Jiemian News) — AI has created a new job with an annual salary of a million. Platform data shows that over the past two years, the number of FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) positions has surged 42-fold. On social media, many netizens call the FDE "the hottest job in the AI world."

On recruitment platforms, hiring postings from some large companies show annual salaries for this role reaching over a million yuan. What exactly does an FDE do? Can you really easily earn a million-yuan salary?

Hiring demand surges, annual salaries reach a million

FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer. Yasha (a pseudonym), who works in Los Angeles, USA, has eight years of product manager experience, later transitioned into software development, and became an FDE two years ago.

She told Jiemian News that two years ago, the role was still "extremely niche." "At the time, on LinkedIn, only the company I work for now and one other company were hiring."

She says the FDE role fully combines her product manager business experience with her programming skills, without having to give up the accumulated experience of either side.

Nowadays Yasha frequently receives messages from headhunters. After just two years in the field, headhunters already consider her a "very senior" FDE. She revealed that headhunters are currently offering her an annual salary of $400,000 (about 2.71 million yuan) plus "fully remote" work to lure her into switching jobs.

LinkedIn's global labor market trends insight report, released in January 2026, shows that over the past two years, companies added at least 1.3 million AI-related positions, covering data annotators, AI engineers, and Forward Deployed Engineers. These roles didn't exist five years ago, yet they have now become an indispensable part of the digital economy. Among them, the number of new Forward Deployed Engineer positions grew 42-fold from 2023 to 2025, compared with a 13-fold increase for AI engineers.

Jiemian News found on platforms such as Liepin and BOSS Zhipin that companies including ByteDance and Ant Digital Technologies are hiring for FDE positions.

ByteDance's posting for "Doubao AI Large Model FDE" lists a salary range of 35,000 to 70,000 yuan per month, paid over 15 months a year, meaning a maximum of 1.05 million yuan. Ant Digital Technologies' B2B FDE role has a monthly salary range of 40,000 to 60,000 yuan, with 15 months' pay a year. Zhipu Huazhang's FDE lead position has a monthly salary range of 60,000 to 80,000 yuan.

However, interviewed practitioners believe that million-yuan salaries in China are concentrated among top-tier core talent, not the industry average.

Lai Juncheng (a pseudonym), a post-90s professional at an AI company in Shanghai, told Jiemian News that at his company, FDEs at lower job levels earn 20,000 to 30,000 yuan per month, while higher-level FDEs are mainly paid annual packages, mostly above 400,000 yuan.

According to Yasha, FDE is not an entry-level position in the US. "A fresh graduate can't do this job; it generally requires several years of experience as a product manager or developer. So the starting salary isn't low either — around $200,000-plus in the first year. In Silicon Valley, there's no ceiling. I've heard of packages of $500,000 (about 3.38 million yuan) a year, or even higher."

OpenAI's official website shows that its FDE role works with the most important strategic customers, leading complex end-to-end deployments of frontier models in production environments, with an annual salary of $162,000 to $280,000 (about 1.1 million to 1.9 million yuan) plus equity incentives, based in New York. In addition, Anthropic is also hiring FDEs, with an annual salary of $200,000 to $300,000 (about 1.36 million to 2.03 million yuan).

What does this job involve?

Lawted (a pseudonym), based in Shenzhen, was formerly a programmer at a major tech company. Not long ago, a logistics company approached him hoping he could use AI to optimize their business processes, so he transitioned into entrepreneurship and became an FDE.

"I found that their daily work was just repeating one task: a customer sends a PDF, they manually extract the tracking numbers and shipping addresses, then enter them into Excel," Lawted said. The company has 40 to 50 employees, of whom 20 to 30 do this kind of repetitive work.

He used AI coding tools to build a demo. A PDF that previously took a person several minutes to process was parsed by AI in seconds. He said that after seeing this efficiency improvement, the logistics company signed a letter of intent with him.

Nowadays, Lawted's day basically goes like this: in the morning he visits various companies to research whether their business processes are suitable for AI transformation; in the afternoon he stays on-site observing, seizing moments when staff take water breaks to ask questions; in the evening he organizes the day's requirements and does system development. "Before, I just sat in the office waiting for people to bring me requirements; now I run all over Shenzhen, looking for companies and bosses," he said.

Lai Juncheng defines himself as the "general manager who handles everything except coding and administration" at a software development company. He has worked as an implementation engineer, pre-sales, product manager, and project manager, and has fully experienced the entire process of a product from template to final delivery. "This role happens to integrate the three stages of pre-sales, product, and project," he said.

In Yasha's view, an FDE is a special kind of programmer. This role is not responsible for any specific functional module of the product; instead, it requires an overall understanding of about 70% of the entire product, then using that understanding to engage with customers, deeply understand their workflows and data structures, and ultimately integrate the product with the customer so it truly lands and gets used.

What are the barriers to entry?

"In the past, enterprise software was often a standardized product — I build it, and you use it as is. But now we've entered the AI era, and most products must be deeply embedded in the customer's business processes," Yasha said. At this point, you need someone who understands technology and can write code, to work on-site (or online) with the customer to analyze: which requirements can be implemented directly, which need to be worked around, and which can be turned into win-win solutions.

Yasha said this means an FDE cannot be a product manager who only communicates and then relays things back to programmers — that's too inefficient and prone to distortion. "You must have technical depth yourself, be able to make judgments, and even solve some on-site problems with your own hands."

In her view, the most important qualities for an FDE are strong analytical ability and patience. "Every day you face unclear requirements, like a tangled mess. The customer is already anxious, and you need to calm down, untangle the threads one by one, guide them to articulate the real problem, and then push implementation forward step by step."

Lai Juncheng is currently also responsible for hiring new FDEs for his company. When recruiting, he values two things most: first, learning ability, and second, the ability to see the essence of things. As for educational requirements, he said that team members who studied design or less commonly taught languages have all performed outstandingly.

On recruitment platforms, taking the aforementioned Doubao position as an example, the job requirements include a bachelor's degree or above, with computer science, communications, AI, and related majors preferred; a solid programming foundation (Python/Java, etc.), the ability to independently complete code development and debugging, and full-stack development capability; familiarity with the technical principles of large models, mastery of methods for evaluating large model effectiveness and performance, and familiarity with various Post-Training techniques; and familiarity with API calls for large-model-related products.

As for the future of FDEs, Lai Juncheng believes one can look at the trajectory of the "product manager" role when the mobile internet rose a decade ago. "It was hot for about five to ten years, then gradually cooled down. I think FDEs will go through a similar process. But along the way, top-tier 'super individuals' like Zhang Xiaolong and Zhang Yiming will surely emerge, profoundly influencing our lives."

Yasha, meanwhile, values the role more as a stepping stone to entrepreneurship. "FDEs have seen too many success and failure cases and deeply understand user pain points and the details of project implementation. In the AI era, the barrier to starting a business is getting lower and lower. If you have FDE experience, you'll know exactly what the market lacks and what product you should build."

Lawted, who had already received an admission offer from Harvard University, decided to temporarily focus on his entrepreneurial practice, seizing the one-to-two-year window for AI transformation of traditional enterprises. "Because AI is moving so fast. It used to take one or two years and several million yuan to build an enterprise management system. Now, with AI programming, we can deliver a customized system in two months."

Yasha said she is currently very optimistic about the prospects of the FDE role. But she also knows that AI is evolving so fast that no one can accurately predict the future of any profession. "All we can do is see clearly the direction it's heading and jump onto the next wave at the right moment."
