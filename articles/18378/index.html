FDE vs SES vs SI vs Strategy Consulting | An In-Depth Comparison of the Four Models

Original title: FDE vs SES vs SI vs 戦略コンサル｜4つのモデルを徹底比較

FDX株式会社 · 2026-04-29 · 11 min read

Summary

This FDX article compares FDE with SES, SI, and strategy consulting across eight dimensions, arguing the models differ in scope of responsibility and how deliverables are defined: SES supplies engineer hours, SI builds after specs are fixed, consultants only advise, while FDEs handle business redesign through implementation and operational handover. Using an 18–24 month TCO analysis of an AI implementation project, it shows FDE's higher monthly rate (3–5M yen) often yields lower total cost than a consultant-plus-SIer combination. It also clarifies that on-site presence alone doesn't make an FDE, notes Japanese procurement rules need new contract templates for FDE's quasi-delegated plus performance-fee structure, and offers a three-question decision flow for choosing a model.

Key points

  • SES, SI, consulting, and FDE differ in responsibility scope and deliverable definition, not just price.
  • FDE costs 3–5M yen monthly but often beats consultant+SIer on 18–24 month TCO.
  • FDE contracts typically combine quasi-delegation with performance fees tied to business KPIs.
  • On-site residency alone doesn't define an FDE; responsibility and outcomes do.
  • A three-question flow (redesign needed? who implements? spec risk?) picks the right model.

Key points (100 words): FDEs are easily confused with SES, SI, and strategy consulting, but they differ in scope of responsibility and how results are defined. This article compares the four models across 8 axes and presents a selection flow based on the need for business process redesign and the intent to build in-house capabilities.

Reading "What is an FDE" first will deepen your understanding of this article.

Summary (3 lines)

  • SES is responsible for "providing man-hours," SI for "building after specs are finalized," strategy consulting for "making recommendations," and FDE for "end-to-end coverage from business process redesign through operational handover."
  • They should be compared not by unit price but by business impact (ROI) and post-deployment TCO. FDEs often look expensive on unit price but cheaper on an 18–24 month TCO basis.
  • The right model is determined by three questions: "Does it involve business process redesign?" "Do you want to internalize it?" and "Who holds implementation responsibility?"

2. Essential Differences by Model

SES (System Engineering Service)

Definition: A contract that provides engineer man-hours to the client. Quasi-delegation is the mainstream form.

Strengths

  • Resources can be scaled up or down as needed, when needed
  • Relatively low unit price

Weaknesses

  • Responsibility for business understanding lies with the contracting party (the client)
  • Dialogue with executives basically does not occur
  • Fulfillment of man-hours is the definition of results; business impact is not evaluated

Suitable projects: Maintenance and operation of systems with finalized specs, reinforcement of steady-state development resources.

SI (System Integration)

Definition: A contract to build and integrate systems that meet specification requirements. Fixed-price contracts are the mainstream form.

Strengths

  • Implementation capability in specialized domains (core systems, cloud infrastructure, etc.)
  • Clear responsibility for spec compliance, deadlines, and quality
  • Risk-bearing for large-scale projects

Weaknesses

  • Does not engage in business process redesign before specs are finalized
  • Spec changes require cost and schedule adjustments
  • The operations phase tends to become a separate contract (maintenance contract)

Suitable projects: Large-scale system construction with settled requirements, implementation in specialized domains such as SAP and Salesforce.

Strategy consulting

Definition: A contract to structure management issues, make recommendations for business process redesign, and draft roadmaps. Quasi-delegation.

Strengths

  • Dialogue with executives and decision-making support
  • Cross-industry best practice knowledge
  • Methodologies for business process redesign

Weaknesses

  • Does not hold implementation responsibility ("the rest is up to you")
  • A divide easily emerges between recommendations and implementation
  • A separate vendor is needed for the implementation phase

Suitable projects: Post-M&A business restructuring, launching new businesses, drafting 3–5 year management plans.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)

Definition: An engineer role stationed at the client site that handles everything end-to-end, from business process redesign through implementation to operational handover. A combination of quasi-delegation + success-based compensation is common.

Strengths

  • Holds all three of business understanding × technical implementation × executive dialogue in one person/one team
  • Consistently handles everything from PoC to production operation without fragmentation
  • Can run internal capability building in parallel through Transfer Skills

Weaknesses

  • High unit price (comparable to strategy consultants)
  • Requires superhuman talent, and the talent market is thin
  • Does not fit existing SES contract formats

Suitable projects: Generative AI adoption involving business process redesign, implementation responsibility from PoC to production, integrated implementation of existing core systems × AI.

3. Diagram: 4-Model Comparison Matrix

A scorecard of 8 axes × 4 models. FDE is the only model that scores ◎ on all four axes: "business understanding," "technical implementation," "operational handover," and "executive dialogue." This is FDE's structural advantage.

However, FDE is not optimal for every project. For simple man-hour reinforcement, building after specs are finalized, or cases where recommendations alone suffice, choosing another model is more rational.

4. Differences in Cost Structure | Look at ROI, Not Unit Price

Unit price comparison (monthly, rough guide for senior level)

  • SES: 800,000–1.5 million yen
  • SI: Monthly equivalent depends on the project, as contracts are lump-sum
  • Strategy consulting: 3–6 million yen (partner class)
  • FDE: 3–5 million yen (2–4 million yen in hybrid configurations)

In simple unit price terms, costs rise in the order "SES → FDE → strategy consulting." But this is an inappropriate comparison.

What happens when compared over an 18-24 month TCO?

Let's compare using an example of a generative AI adoption project involving business redesign (18 months).

Strategy consulting firm + SIer combination

  • Consulting recommendations (3 months, 3-6 million yen/month): 10-15 million yen
  • SIer implementation (12 months, fixed-price contract): 50-80 million yen
  • Additional customization due to poor business fit: 10-20 million yen
  • Failed operational handover → reinvestment: 10-20 million yen
  • Total: 80-135 million yen, implementation period 18-24 months, degree of internalization achieved: low

FDE model

  • FDE team (4 people × 18 months, 3 million yen per person-month): 216 million yen → overestimated
  • In reality, one senior FDE + two mid-level FDEs working alongside internalization candidates: 80-120 million yen
  • Business fit is built in from the design stage, so no additional costs
  • Because internalization candidates work alongside, operational handover is smooth
  • Total: 80-120 million yen, implementation period 12-15 months, degree of internalization achieved: high

The numbers vary depending on project scale, but the FDE model has a structure where "the unit price is high, but additional costs and reinvestment are less likely to occur, and internalization assets remain," so in most cases it comes in below the strategy consulting firm + SIer combination over an 18-24 month TCO.

Characteristics of FDE contracts: Rather than simple quasi-delegation, "business KPI improvement" is often incorporated as performance-based compensation. For example, conditions such as "a performance bonus of X million yen if customer inquiry handling man-hours are reduced by 50%" are explicitly written into the contract. This aligns the interests of the FDE and the customer.

Points to note: Japanese procurement rules (especially the purchasing departments of large companies) are optimized for SES and SI contract formats, so FDE-type contracts require creating new templates. FDX Inc. supports this contract development.

6. The reality behind the misconception that "on-site = FDE"

In Japan, FDE is often understood as "on-site engineer at the client," but this is a misconception.

As Nikkei xTECH points out in "The American-style 'FDE' is similar to but fundamentally different from Japanese on-site work," the two only appear superficially similar and differ in essence.

7. Which model should you choose? | Decision flowchart

Three branch points lead to the optimal solution among the four models.

Question 1: Is business redesign necessary?

  • Yes → Go to Question 2
  • No (specifications are already fixed, existing operations are systematized as-is) → Go to Question 3

Question 2: Will implementation responsibility also be outsourced?

  • Yes (both business redesign and implementation responsibility outsourced end-to-end) → FDE
  • No (receive recommendations only, implementation in-house or by another vendor) → Strategy consulting

Question 3: Is the risk of specification changes high?

  • Yes (frequent specification changes, agile-style development) → SES
  • No (specifications fixed, delivery and quality are important) → SI

This flow is a rough guide, but most projects arrive at the appropriate model through these three branches. Hybrid cases (e.g., strategy consulting recommendations + FDE implementation, SI construction + FDE operational handover) also exist in practice.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

Q1. How is an FDE different from an "excellent SES engineer"?

A. They may look similar on the surface, but the scope of responsibility and the definition of results differ. For an SES engineer, the contractual responsibility is "providing man-hours," and business redesign, dialogue with management, and operational handover are not included in the performance metrics. For an FDE, business KPI improvement is the performance metric, and they take on PM decisions, engaging business stakeholders, and handover completion criteria as their own responsibilities. An individual excellent SES engineer can behave in an FDE-like manner, but as a contract structure, they are completely different things.

Q2. Is an FDE a quasi-delegation contract or a fixed-price contract?

A. A quasi-delegation (jun-i nin) contract is the baseline, and a hybrid contract combining it with performance-based compensation tied to business KPI improvements is common. The fixed-price contract structure (spec finalized → acceptance testing) is a poor fit for the nature of FDE work, which includes business process redesign.

Q3. Is an FDE cheaper than a combination of strategy consulting + SIer?

A. It varies by project size, but for generative AI implementation projects involving business process redesign, when compared over an 18–24 month TCO, the FDE model is cheaper in most cases. The reasons are threefold: (1) additional customization for business fit is less likely to occur, (2) reinvestment due to failed operational handover is less likely, and (3) in-house capability assets remain, lowering costs from the second cycle onward.

Q4. Doesn't the FDE model conflict with in-house development?

A. No, it does not. Rather, since the FDE model treats "Transfer Skills" as a core success metric, it has a structure that accelerates in-house development. A hybrid FDE approach (external FDE running in parallel with internal candidates) is the practical solution for advancing in-house development by the shortest route. See External FDE Utilization vs. Internal Development for details.

Q6. Can an SES engineer transition to an FDE role?

A. Yes, but additional skills are required. Specifically: (1) business understanding (domain knowledge of industry × business function), (2) ROI calculation and business language, (3) dialogue skills with executive management, and (4) stakeholder management. One must acquire capability areas distinct from SES, which can be completed with technical skills alone. See Organizational Integration and Development for details.

Q6. Will the procurement departments of large enterprises understand FDE contracts?

A. Large enterprises' procurement departments are traditionally optimized for SES and SI contracts, and FDE-style contracts (quasi-delegation combined with performance-based compensation) often require new contract templates to be established. Reaching consensus among executive management and adjusting contract formats with the procurement department can take 2–3 months. FDX Inc. also provides support for contract preparation.

Q7. Does a single FDE do everything, or does a team operate?

A. It depends on scale. For small projects (limited business scope, small data volumes), a single senior FDE may complete the work. For large projects, a team structure of "1 senior FDE + 2–3 mid-level FDEs + spot participation by specialists" is common. Even within the team, the principle is a dual skill set of "business understanding × implementation."

Summary

  • FDE, SES, SI, and strategy consulting are four contract models with different scopes of responsibility and definitions of outcomes
  • They should be compared not by unit price, but by business impact and 18–24 month TCO
  • The right model is almost determined by three factors: "need for business process redesign," "location of implementation responsibility," and "specification change risk"
  • FDEs have the greatest advantage in implementation projects involving business process redesign, but are not the optimal solution for every project
  • Japan's procurement rules are optimized for SES and SI, and FDE contracts require new contract templates
