An AI engineer is a technical professional responsible for designing and building machine learning models and developing and operating AI systems.

In recent years, the number of "freelance AI engineers" who work independently on a project basis has been increasing. With the spread of generative AI, more companies want to adopt AI but lack the talent, so demand for freelance engineers who can hit the ground running continues to rise.

So, what kind of engineers can command high rates?

Conditions for Achieving High Rates

  • Strong technical skills: Deep familiarity with the latest LLMs and generative AI, or deep expertise in a specific area (image recognition, NLP, etc.)
  • Ability to handle upstream processes: Capable of requirements definition, basic design, and negotiating with clients
  • Extensive track record: Experience delivering results on projects of similar scale and difficulty
  • Management experience: A proven record of leading teams as a PM or tech lead

To consistently earn 1.5 million yen or more per month, you need not only the ability to "write code" but also the ability to "move a team" and "solve business problems."

A realistic roadmap is to first aim for a level where you can consistently exceed 1 million yen per month (12 million yen annually), building up your track record and reputation along the way.

Because estimating deliverables in AI development is difficult, "hourly contracts" and "monthly contracts" are the mainstream.

For freelance AI engineers in Japan, an hourly rate of 4,000–6,000 yen is considered the typical market rate.

In the US, cases of hourly rates of $200–250 (about 30,000–40,000 yen) have been reported, showing that AI engineers command high market value globally as well.

When thinking in terms of hourly rates, "working hours" are something to watch out for.

At a monthly rate of 800,000 yen with 160 working hours, the hourly rate is 5,000 yen, but the same 800,000 yen over 200 hours works out to 4,000 yen per hour. Since required working hours vary by project, we recommend comparing rates by calculating the hourly equivalent as "monthly rate ÷ expected working hours." If you'd like to learn more about thinking in terms of hourly rates, also refer to Market Rates of 4,000–6,000 Yen per Hour and Strategies for Raising Your Rate.

Also, since freelance income is "proportional to working hours," income generally does not jump through raises or bonuses as it does for salaried employees. Understanding the limits of "selling your time" and consciously working to raise your rates and diversify your skills will lead to long-term income stability.

When discussing freelance engineer income, the concept you absolutely must understand is the "contract chain" (shoryu).

The contract chain refers to the path a project takes from the client company to the engineer. The more intermediaries (agents or prime contractors) involved, the more middleman margins are taken, reducing the engineer's take-home pay.

Below is a simulation by contract chain, using a project with a monthly rate of 1 million yen as an example.

Contract Chain PatternMiddleman Margin RateMonthly Take-HomeAnnual Income Equivalent

Second-tier subcontract (1 intermediary)15–20%About 800,000–850,000 yenAbout 9.5–10 million yen
Direct contract with end client0–10%About 900,000 yen or moreAbout 11 million yen or more

Even with the same skills and the same 1-million-yen project, a deep contract chain means 700,000 yen take-home, while a direct end-client contract yields 900,000 yen — a difference of about 2.5 million yen per year.

I understand the feeling of "I want to go freelance right now." However, it is also true that going independent without adequate preparation carries significant risk.

Here, I explain the four steps to going independent as a freelance AI engineer with no prior experience. Rather than thinking "independence = the goal," it is important to recognize that "continuing to secure work after going independent is the real starting point."

To win projects as a freelancer, trust that "this person can deliver results as an immediately productive contributor" is essential. For that, hands-on AI development experience at a company is the most reliable proof.

While it's not impossible to build skills on your own, learning from senior colleagues on the job is more efficient. How team development works, how to handle code reviews, communicating with clients—these are experiences that are hard to gain through self-study.

Even if your current job is unrelated to AI, there are paths to working on AI projects through internal transfers or changing jobs. Start by setting "1-2 years of practical experience" as your first milestone.

"Going independent right away" carries a high risk of failing to land projects, or of projects going wrong even if you do, which could damage your career.

The safest approach is to maintain your main job's income while trying freelance work as a side business.

The benefits of side work are as follows.

  • You can take on challenges while maintaining a financial safety net
  • You can get accustomed to client interactions and contract processes
  • You can determine whether you're suited to being a freelancer

However, before starting side work, be sure to check whether your current employer's work rules permit it. Some companies still prohibit side jobs.

For details on rates and how to find side projects in AI development, see Can You Earn from AI Development Side Projects? Rates and How to Land Them.

If at this stage you feel confident that "I could make it on my own," move on to the next step.

When landing projects as a freelancer, your portfolio serves as a "sales tool that proves your abilities."

The key to an effective portfolio is "including quantifiable results."

Good examples

  • "Improved image classification model accuracy by 15% over the previous version"
  • "Improved inference speed by 3x, enabling real-time processing"
  • "Built a chatbot that handles 1,000 inquiries per month"

Examples to avoid

  • "Participated in the XX project" (results unclear)
  • "Implemented machine learning in Python" (lacks specificity)

Publishing code on GitHub and sharing technical content on blogs are also effective. However, information about projects under confidentiality obligations must be anonymized and generalized. Violating an NDA (non-disclosure agreement) can ruin your career.

For landing your first project, using a freelance agent is effective.

The benefits of using an agent are as follows.

  • Sales support: Projects are introduced to you without you having to do sales activities yourself
  • Negotiation: Agents handle rate negotiations on your behalf
  • Access to unlisted projects: You can be introduced to high-rate projects not publicly listed

The realistic strategy is to secure steady work through an agent at first, and then gradually shift to direct contracts once you've built up a track record and trust.

After going independent, the most important thing is "consistently securing projects."

There are multiple ways to find projects, and the key is to use them according to your career stage. Here, we introduce five methods by stage, from beginners to advanced freelancers.

Crowdsourcing platforms like LANCERS and CrowdWorks are a suitable starting point when you have no track record yet.

The benefit is that "you can apply even with zero experience." While many projects offer low rates, accumulating positive reviews can gradually lead to bigger projects.

However, the reality is that it's difficult to maintain a high income through crowdsourcing alone. Once you've gained some experience, it's recommended to shift to agents or direct sales.

Some projects are labeled as AI work but are actually just simple data collection, or are unreasonably low-paying. Evaluating projects carefully is essential.

Sharing technical content on X (formerly Twitter) and LinkedIn becomes a long-term career asset.

"I learned something interesting from this LLM implementation, so I'm sharing it" or "I won a silver medal on Kaggle"—through this kind of regular sharing, once you're recognized as "an expert in this field," project inquiries will naturally start coming in.

Participating in technical communities (PyData, deep learning study groups, etc.) is also effective. Company CTOs and technical leads often attend, and projects can come from people thinking "I'd like to consult with that person who gave that presentation."

Connecting with people from other industries also broadens your chances of receiving unexpected project referrals.

What matters when choosing an agent is "what kind of contract chain their projects come through." Even if an agent has many projects, if they're mostly second- or third-tier subcontracted work, your take-home pay will suffer.

Direct contracts through connections from your previous job or proposal-based sales to companies are a way to earn high compensation without middleman margins.

The benefits of direct contracts are clear: with no agent margin, your take-home pay increases even for the same budget.

However, direct sales require "sales skills" and "proposal skills." Rather than talking about technology, if you cannot make proposals from a business perspective—such as "how AI can solve your company's challenges"—it will be difficult to turn opportunities into actual projects.

Realistically, we recommend a hybrid strategy: first build a track record and trust through agents, then gradually develop direct projects.
