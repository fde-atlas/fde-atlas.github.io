Forward Deployed Engineer at Thinking Machines Lab: Role, Salary, and Interview (2026)

fde.directory · 2026-11-25 · 6 min read

Summary

This article reviews Thinking Machines Lab's Forward Deployed Engineer (Tinker) posting in San Francisco. Its standout feature is a $350K–$475K base salary, matching its research engineer band and exceeding Anthropic's FDE range. The role is closer to ML infrastructure debugging—triaging failed fine-tuning and RL runs—than typical customer-integration FDE work, requiring LLM fine-tuning experience and Python/Rust skills. The author weighs this against risks: a reported $40B valuation against $100M+ run-rate revenue, roughly a third of founding staff departing, and rapid headcount growth.

Key points

  • Base salary of $350K–$475K matches the Tinker research engineer band exactly.
  • The work is debugging customer training jobs and building automation, not sales-style integration.
  • Requirements include hands-on SFT and RL fine-tuning, distributed systems debugging, and Python/Rust.
  • Founded by Mira Murati in 2025; reportedly raising at a $40B pre-money valuation with $100M+ run-rate revenue.
  • Risks include founder departures (Barret Zoph, Luke Metz, Andrew Tulloch) and 50% headcount growth plans.

Here is the number that makes Thinking Machines Lab’s FDE posting worth reading: $350,000 to $475,000 base. The bottom of that range is higher than the top of Anthropic’s posted FDE band ($200K–$300K). It sits just under OpenAI’s highest posted base ($385K). And it is identical, to the dollar, to the band Thinking Machines posts for its Tinker research engineers.

Most companies pay FDEs as a field role, somewhere between sales engineering and core engineering. Thinking Machines pays them like researchers. That tells you what kind of customer work it expects.

The company you’d be joining

Thinking Machines Lab was founded in February 2025 in San Francisco by Mira Murati, OpenAI’s former CTO, with John Schulman as chief scientist. In July 2025 it raised a $2 billion seed round at a $12 billion valuation, led by Andreessen Horowitz with Nvidia, AMD, Cisco, and Jane Street participating. In March 2026 Nvidia added an undisclosed investment and a multi-year deal for a gigawatt of Vera Rubin compute.

Two products matter here. Tinker, announced October 1, 2025, is an API for fine-tuning open-weight models. You write the training loop and Thinking Machines runs it on its own clusters. As of August 2026 it supports Qwen, DeepSeek, Kimi, gpt-oss, Nemotron, GLM, and the lab’s own models. Inkling, released July 15, 2026 under an Apache license, is a 975-billion-parameter open-weight model, followed by smaller variants in July and November.

The money is moving fast. TechCrunch reported in September 2026 that annual revenue run-rate was “over $100 million,” citing a source. In early November, Dataconomy and others reported that the lab was close to a $5 billion round led by Accel at a $40 billion pre-money valuation. As of this writing that is reported, not announced.

The contrarian part: Thinking Machines has lost people at the top. Barret Zoph and Luke Metz went back to OpenAI in January 2026, and Andrew Tulloch left for Meta. Dataconomy put the departures at about a third of the founding team. Sources don’t agree on exactly who left, but the pattern is clear. Wikipedia lists headcount around 100. Job aggregators counted 55 to 57 open roles this week, so the company is trying to grow by roughly half. Expect a lot of people who joined in the last six months, and processes still being invented.

What the role actually is

The posting is “Forward Deployed Engineer, Tinker,” based in San Francisco. Its one-line pitch is that you’ll “be the main point of contact with our customers.” The work it lists looks more like an on-call ML infrastructure engineer than a solutions architect:

  • Triage customer problems by “reading logs, reproducing failures, and tracing job execution”
  • Build tools and automation to “eliminate recurring failure modes”
  • Write documentation and cookbook recipes
  • Work with the research and infrastructure teams and feed what you learn into the Tinker roadmap

The requirements are where it gets specific. Thinking Machines wants hands-on experience fine-tuning LLMs with both supervised and reinforcement-learning methods. It wants experience debugging distributed systems and training infrastructure. And it wants proficiency in a backend language: “we use Python and Rust.” Five or more years of software engineering, ideally including customer-facing or deployment work, is listed as preferred rather than required.

That’s a narrow profile. Most FDE postings we track ask you to wire a model into a customer’s CRM, data warehouse, or identity provider. This one asks you to work out why a customer’s RL run diverged at step 4,000 on someone else’s GPUs. Compare Replit, whose FDEs integrate SSO and warehouses, or Databricks, whose FDEs mostly ship data and AI pipelines. At Thinking Machines the product is the training stack, so the customer’s problem usually is too.

The posting history suggests demand. The same title appeared on April 29, 2026 and expired on June 28, according to Career.com’s mirror. It’s now live again on the company’s Greenhouse board. Inference-focused job boards picked up the repost about four weeks ago.

None of these include equity. At the big labs, equity is most of total comp, as our salary guide shows. Thinking Machines’ postings say nothing about equity, so ask directly. If the reported round closes, you’d be buying in at $40 billion pre-money against a run-rate last reported above $100 million. That’s a valuation priced on research ambition, not current revenue. It also has an unusual governance structure: Wikipedia describes the company as a public benefit corporation where founding shareholders’ votes count 100 times those of regular shareholders. Ask what class of stock you’d receive and what your options are worth at the latest preferred price.

Benefits listed on the Tinker postings include unlimited PTO, paid parental leave, relocation support, and visa sponsorship. Aggregators show six H-1B approvals in fiscal 2025.

The interview loop

We found no first-hand candidate reports of Thinking Machines’ FDE interview, and we won’t make one up. Aggregator pages describe a generic recruiter screen, technical round, and behavioral round. They say their questions are “inferred,” not reported, so we don’t treat them as evidence.

Here’s what the company’s own materials show. The Tinker research-engineer applications ask for your GitHub, a portfolio, and “3 projects you’re proud of.” The research posting calls the work “technical-first,” with a major user-facing component. One aggregator says fewer than 5% of its own applications to the lab reached an interview. That’s a small sample, but it fits a lab that gets far more applicants than it can hire.

Prepare for what the posting stresses. Run something real on Tinker before you apply: a supervised fine-tune and a small RL loop, with notes on where it broke. Be ready to debug a failing distributed job out loud. And have a story about owning a customer’s problem from report to fix, because the role is built around that. Our interview guide covers the general FDE format.

Who this role is for

This seat suits an ML infrastructure engineer who has trained models, not just called them, and who would rather debug a customer’s training run than build a demo. The upside is pay at research-engineer levels, close collaboration with a strong research team, and real influence on Tinker. The risks are a valuation far ahead of revenue, a leadership team that has already lost several founders, and a company trying to grow by half in a short time. It’s also San Francisco only.
