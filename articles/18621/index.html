What to expect for Sierra's Agent Engineer interview and how Nora AI helps.

11 July 2026

Sierra is building a platform that helps businesses create more human customer experiences with AI, founded by Bret Taylor (Board Chair of OpenAI, former co-CEO of Salesforce and CTO of Facebook) and Clay Bavor (18 years at Google, most recently leading Google Labs). The company is primarily in-person out of San Francisco with growing offices across the globe, and it operates on five explicit values: Trust, Customer Obsession, Craftsmanship, Intensity, and Family. For the Agent Engineer role, that translates into a hiring bar centered on shipping production-grade AI agents that run mission-critical workloads in finance, healthcare, and commerce, not prototypes.

This role is a leadership-leaning engineering position: you will grow a team building performant, reliable agents, own the Agent Development Life Cycle (ADLC) end to end, and partner directly with large enterprises and startups to translate business needs into technical deliverables. Expect the interview to probe both hands-on technical depth (coding, debugging, agentic system design) and the ability to lead through ambiguity with high standards and customer obsession. Candidates consistently describe a well-organized, thoughtful process, though a few reported uneven experiences with individual interviewers.

Quick Stats

* Typical process: 4 to 5 rounds (recruiter screen, coding, take-home, onsite loop) over roughly 3 to 5 weeks

* Format: Video screens and coding rounds, plus a largely in-person onsite (San Francisco is the hub)

* Core focus: Coding and debugging, agentic system design, ADLC ownership, customer-facing technical judgment, behavioral and values fit

* Difficulty: Hard (avg 3.22/5 company-wide); the coding and debugging rounds are demanding and the bar for production-quality reasoning is high

What Sierra Looks For

* Engineers who can build and scale end-to-end production systems, not demos

* Strong problem-solving under ambiguity and fast-changing AI tooling

* A leadership style that blends high standards with empathy for both speed and quality

* Customer obsession: translating enterprise business challenges into shipped agent behavior

"recruiter screen, coding interview, take home, onsite with 3 parts: take home review, hiring manager call, debugging interview was pretty well thought out, was not super difficult things that you need to study for, but also tested what you can actually do" (Agent Engineer candidate, accepted offer)

What to Expect

A recruiter walks you through the role, the compensation range, and the full interview process. Expect questions about your background, your motivation for Sierra specifically, and your experience building or deploying AI/LLM systems. Sierra is an in-person company, so be ready to discuss location and in-office expectations early. Candidates describe recruiters as generally responsive and clear, and this is where you set the tone with a crisp pitch on why Sierra and why this role.

Example or Reported Questions

* "Tell me something about yourself."

* "What motivated you to apply to Sierra?"

* "Why choose computer science?"

* "Do you need sponsorship?"

Tips

* Have a tight two-minute story connecting your experience leading engineering teams and shipping production systems to Sierra's agent platform.

* Be direct and transparent about compensation expectations and your location or in-office flexibility; recruiters bring this up early.

* Rehearse the "why Sierra" and "tell me about yourself" answers out loud with Nora's Standard Mode so your pitch is confident and concise before the live call.

A live coding round focused on data structures, algorithms, and clean problem-solving. Reports describe a LeetCode-style question, often in Python (though TypeScript is welcome), that is not designed to be a brutal trick but does test whether you can actually build. Some candidates also see practical, API-oriented problems rather than pure algorithms. Communicate your reasoning as you go and prioritize a working, well-structured solution over cleverness.

* "A coding question focused on problem solving and algorithms."

* "Data structures question in Python."

* "Standard medium/easy to hard LeetCode-style question."

* "Debug issues around API calls."

* Practice medium-level data structures and algorithms in your strongest language, and be fluent in Python since it shows up frequently in these reports.

* Narrate your thought process and discuss trade-offs; interviewers evaluate how you reason, not just the final answer.

Most Agent Engineer candidates complete a take-home project before the onsite, which you will later present and extend live. This is where product sense and craftsmanship show up: strong candidates make clear, defensible design decisions and can justify them. For a role centered on the Agent Development Life Cycle, treat the take-home as a small production system, not a script. Think about reliability, extensibility, and how you would iterate toward customer needs.

* "Complete a take-home assignment, then discuss and extend it during the onsite."

* "What is a technical project you worked on, what went well and what didn't?"

* "Walk me through the design decisions you made and why."

* "How would you scale or productionize this in a real customer environment?"

* Write clean, readable code and document your assumptions; craftsmanship is one of Sierra's stated values.

* Anticipate extension questions by leaving your design flexible, and prepare to defend every decision with a business or reliability rationale.

* Use Nora's Technical Mode to rehearse walking through your architecture and answering "why did you build it this way" follow-ups out loud.

The onsite typically has three parts: a take-home review and extension, a hiring manager conversation, and a debugging interview. The debugging round asks you to find and fix issues in existing code, sometimes around API calls, under time pressure. For this leadership-leaning role, expect agentic system design discussion as well; other Sierra candidates were asked to design agentic services live (for example, using Excalidraw to diagram). The hiring manager conversation digs into how you lead teams, handle ambiguity, and partner with customers. Most people describe interviewers as kind and easy to talk to, though one report noted a disengaged interviewer, so stay composed regardless.

* "Design an agentic system for a given use case."

* "Design a subscription cancellation agentic service (draw it live)."

* "Tell me about a time you solved a technical challenge, and what you had to consider."

* For debugging, verbalize your hypothesis, isolate the fault systematically, and check edge cases; interviewers watch your diagnostic process.

* For agentic system design, structure your answer around the ADLC: pilot, deployment, evaluation, and continuous iteration, and mention eval frameworks, RAG, and prompt engineering where relevant.

* Prepare leadership and customer STAR stories in Nora's Behavioral Mode, and use Technical Mode to drill live system-design and debugging reps so both muscles are sharp.

Woven through the onsite (and sometimes a dedicated leadership chat), Sierra assesses values fit against Trust, Customer Obsession, Craftsmanship, Intensity, and Family. For an Agent Engineer expected to grow a team, be ready for questions on leading engineers, managing difficult stakeholders, and balancing speed with quality. Reports show a strong emphasis on how you overcome challenges and work cross-functionally with product and platform teams. Answer with specific, outcome-focused stories.

* "Walk me through one example in which you dealt with a difficult stakeholder."

* "Tell me about a time you had to manage difficult stakeholders."

* "Describe a time you worked with product/engineering."

* "What are the three things you look for in your next job?"

* Map two or three go-to stories onto Sierra's values, especially Customer Obsession and Intensity, and show measurable impact.

* Have a concrete example of translating a business need into a shipped technical deliverable; this is central to the role.

* Run full STAR rehearsals in Nora's Behavioral Mode to tighten your stakeholder and leadership stories before the onsite.

1) How many rounds are there?

Typically 4 to 5: a recruiter screen, a live coding interview, a take-home assignment, and an onsite loop (take-home review, hiring manager call, and debugging), with behavioral and leadership fit woven throughout. Some candidates also have a separate leadership or skip-level chat.

2) What topics are most common?

* Coding and debugging (data structures, algorithms, API-related problems, often in Python or TypeScript)

* Agentic system design, ADLC ownership, and behavioral or values-based questions on leadership and customers

3) How long does the process take?

Most candidates report roughly 3 to 5 weeks. Reports describe the process as efficient, well-organized, and thorough, with clear updates from recruiters, though a few candidates experienced scheduling delays or slow feedback.

4) How should I prepare?

* Drill medium-level data structures and algorithms and practice debugging existing code with API calls, in Python and ideally TypeScript.

* Study agentic system design and the ADLC, including eval frameworks, RAG pipelines, and prompt engineering, and be ready to diagram a design live.

* Prepare STAR stories tied to Sierra's values (Trust, Customer Obsession, Craftsmanship, Intensity, Family), focusing on leading teams and managing stakeholders.

* Use Nora's Technical Mode for timed coding, debugging, and system-design reps, Behavioral Mode for leadership and stakeholder stories, and Standard Mode to polish your recruiter-screen pitch and "why Sierra" answer.
