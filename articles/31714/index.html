Published September 8, 2026. Editorial.

Key takeaways

  • Postings carrying the title grew 1,165 percent year over year, comparing January to October 2025 with the same months in 2024, measured by Revealera across 1,000 postings.
  • Median advertised salary across those 1,000 postings was $173,816. Levels.fyi separately reports Palantir forward deployed engineer total compensation with a median of $278,000, read on 8 September 2026.
  • 68 percent of postings required travel, and 50 to 75 percent was typical where a figure was given. OpenAI's own postings state up to 50 percent.
  • The most-quoted number in this field, MIT NANDA's 95 percent of AI pilots showing no profit impact, is based on 52 interviews, 153 surveys and 300 deployments. Quote the sample with the number.

Almost every number published about this role is unsourced. Below is what we could verify, what it is based on, and what to distrust.

Demand and hiring

Postings up 1,165 percent year over year. Comparing January to October 2025 against the same months in 2024, measured by Revealera and published by Henley Wing Chiu on 18 November 2025. October 2025 was the highest posting volume on record in that dataset.

Caveat that matters: the same analysis found the title covers three different jobs, so part of the growth is adjacent roles being relabelled rather than new work appearing. Growth in a job title is not the same as growth in a job.

The three archetypes

From the same 1,000 postings:

  • Builder, 60 percent. Production engineers embedded with customers. 70 to 90 percent of time coding. Advertised range $140,000 to $250,000.
  • Sales engineer with implementation duties, 30 percent. 30 to 40 percent of time coding. Advertised range $120,000 to $200,000 plus commission.
  • Internal tools builder, 10 percent. No external customer. Advertised range $100,000 to $180,000.

This is the single most useful statistic on the page, and it gets its own breakdown.

Compensation

Median advertised salary $173,816 across the 1,000 postings.

70 percent mentioned equity. 8 percent mentioned commission or on-target earnings. None were quota-carrying. That last figure is the clearest test for separating the role from sales engineering.

Palantir forward deployed engineer total compensation: median $278,000, range $185,000 to $631,000. Self-reported to Levels.fyi, United States, read on 8 September 2026. Self-reported aggregator data includes only people who choose to report, so treat it as indicative.

What we could not verify: any specific compensation band for OpenAI or Anthropic forward deployed engineers. Levels.fyi does not have enough submissions to show a level breakdown for OpenAI's forward deployed engineer title. Ranges for the leading AI labs appear in many blog posts and we could not trace one to a primary source, so we do not publish them.

Experience and background

Experience targeted, from the 1,000 postings: 12 percent at zero to two years, 60 percent at three to five, 20 percent at six to eight, 8 percent at nine or more. The role is staffed mid-level.

Previous roles: 45 percent from software engineering, 22 percent from solutions or sales engineering, 15 percent from data engineering or data science, 10 percent from technical consulting, 8 percent founder or early employee.

Alongside that, First Round Review's February 2026 guidance reports that ten or more years at a large tech company can be a warning sign for this work, because the role needs people who operate without the support structure of a large company.

Skills named in postings

Languages and platforms: Python 66 percent, TypeScript 35 percent, AWS 32 percent, GCP 22 percent, Azure 18 percent.

AI competencies: AI agents 35 percent, LLM experience 31 percent, RAG 12 percent.

Soft skills: customer communication 47 percent, followed by problem-solving under pressure and adaptability across industries.

Duties named most often: working directly with customers 55 percent, building and deploying AI or ML systems 37 percent, integrating systems and APIs 32 percent.

Travel

68 percent of postings required travel. Where a figure was given, 50 to 75 percent was typical.

OpenAI's own postings for forward deployed software engineers state that up to 50 percent travel is required. That is the employer's own statement about its own role, which makes it a primary source for that company.

Who is hiring, and for which customers

58 percent of postings came from companies with 11 to 200 employees. The model is being adopted by growth-stage companies selling to large enterprises, rather than only by the well-known names.

45 percent sat on a dedicated forward deployed team rather than reporting into sales. Where the function reports covers why that number matters more than it looks.

79 percent did not specify a target customer industry. Among the 21 percent that did: financial services and banking 24 percent, government and defence 18 percent, healthcare 17 percent, insurance 17 percent, energy and utilities 13 percent.

Investment

AWS committed $1 billion in June 2026 to a Forward Deployed Engineering organization embedding thousands of engineers with customers, announced on its own newsroom. It stated the work would be priced on shared goals and business results rather than billable hours, and named customer self-sufficiency after the engagement as a design goal.

OpenAI had 22 open forward deployed and solutions engineering roles out of 311 total positions at the time a16z's Trading Margin for Moat was published on 4 June 2025.

The margin numbers behind the argument

From the same a16z piece, on the previous generation of enterprise software: ServiceNow's gross margin at IPO was 63.2 percent, Workday's 54.1 percent. By 2024, ServiceNow was at 79 percent and Workday at 75 percent. The early focus on services was the way in, and the software was the business model.

The most-quoted number, and its sample

95 percent of generative AI pilots delivered no measurable profit-and-loss impact. MIT's Project NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025", July 2025. Only 5 percent of integrated systems created significant value.

The sample: 52 executive interviews, surveys of 153 leaders, and analysis of 300 public deployments. That is real research, and it does not cover every company. Almost every article quoting this figure omits the sample size. Include it, and the number becomes a defensible signal rather than a slogan.

Numbers we deliberately do not publish

Stated plainly, because their absence is the point:

  • Any salary range for the leading AI labs. Widely published, not traceable to a primary source.
  • "Palantir employed more forward deployed engineers than product engineers by 2016." Repeated often, no primary source found.
  • A benchmark ratio of forward deployed engineers to product engineers. No credible published figure exists. Our page on sizing gives the calculation instead.
  • Any average engagement length or time to production. These vary with the environment and nobody has published a defensible distribution.

If you find a primary source for any of these, it belongs here. Until then, a page that says "unknown" is more useful than one that guesses.

What is a forward deployed engineer?

A forward deployed engineer is a software engineer placed inside a customer's organization to build and release production software there, against that customer's real data and real systems, and who stays accountable for the deployment until it is running. The title is often written FDE, and the variant FDSE means forward deployed software engineer. Palantir popularised the role and AWS, OpenAI, Anthropic and Google now use the title. Three tests separate it from the roles that resemble it: production code, the customer's environment, and ownership of the outcome.

What does a forward deployed engineer do?

A forward deployed engineer spends most of the week writing production code inside the customer's systems, and the rest of it finding out what the code should do by sitting with the people who will use it. A useful guideline from teams that run the model well is about 20 percent of the time facing the customer and 80 percent building. When that ratio inverts, the engineer has become an account manager and the deployment has stopped moving. Across 1,000 job postings, the three duties named most often were working directly with customers, building and deploying AI systems, and integrating systems and APIs.

The three jobs hiding behind the FDE title

Forward deployed engineer is one title covering at least three different jobs. An analysis of 1,000 postings carrying the title, published by Henley Wing Chiu in November 2025 using Revealera data, split them into a production engineer embedded with customers (60 percent), a sales engineer with implementation duties (30 percent), and an internal tools builder who faces no customer at all (10 percent). The three differ in how much of the week is spent writing code, how pay is structured, and who is accountable for production. Anyone hiring for the title, taking the job, or buying the service needs to know which one is being offered.

Why forward deployed engineering is growing

Forward deployed engineering grew because enterprise AI has an integration problem that nobody can solve from outside the customer's building. Three things happened close together: MIT NANDA reported in July 2025 that 95 percent of generative AI pilots produced no measurable profit-and-loss impact, a16z argued publicly in June 2025 that AI companies should hire teams focused on implementation, and in June 2026 AWS committed $1 billion to a forward deployed engineering organization. Job postings carrying the title grew 1,165 percent year over year. Each of those numbers deserves its caveat, and this page gives them.

Forward deployed engineering explained for executives

Forward deployed engineering means putting engineers inside your organization, or your customer's, to build and run a system rather than to advise on one. It costs more per engineer than any other delivery model and it is the only one that reliably gets an AI pilot to a production system. Three questions decide whether you should buy it: is the contract value large enough to pay for an engineer, do the environments differ from each other, and will anyone change the plan based on what the engineers find. Two noes means buy something else.
