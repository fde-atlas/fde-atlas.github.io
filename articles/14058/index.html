Forward deployed engineer salary: posted bands and filings

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 57 min read

Summary

This article compiles verifiable forward deployed engineer pay data: salary bands printed on US job postings and base wages filed in H-1B applications with the US Department of Labor, rather than unsourced estimates from prep sites. As of September 2026, US individual-contributor ranges span roughly $72,600 to $330,000, with LangChain's OTE reaching $380,000. Across 387 H-1B filings with forward deployed titles, the median offered base wage was $161,637. The piece lists posted bands for OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, Databricks, Google and others, and stresses that postings and filings measure different things and neither captures total compensation.

Key points

  • 364 of 415 US FDE postings published pay; ranges run $72,600 to $330,000.
  • Median base wage across 387 H-1B filings was $161,637.
  • Anthropic posts the highest band ($280K–$320K); Palantir among the lowest ($135K–$200K).
  • Posted bands and H-1B filings exclude equity, bonuses and sign-ons.
  • Levels.fyi self-reported median total compensation is $207,500 from 155 data points.

Postings and filings, the two public records in the brief above, answer different questions:

  • A posted band is what the employer says it will pay for one role, on the day you read it. It may be base, OTE or unlabeled, and it can span several levels.
  • An H-1B filing is the base wage an employer offered one sponsored worker. It has no bonus, no equity and no sign-on in it.
  • Neither is what FDEs receive in total. Only self-reported sites try to measure that, with small samples, and we cover them further down.

If you only need the number for a recruiter call, read the negotiation section below and the question What are your compensation expectations? first. The rest of this page is the evidence behind that answer.

Three things these tables hide.

One range can cover several levels. Scale AI’s postings say a posted range may span several career levels, and that pay is set during the interview process based on factors that include interview performance. The top of the band is not the target. All 61 Deloitte FDE postings in our count say it is “not typical” at the firm to be hired at or near the top of the range. To price your own offer, change the inputs to this function:

def one_year(base, bonus_pct, sign_on, grant, vest):
    bonus = base * bonus_pct
    first = base + bonus + sign_on + grant * vest[0]
    average = base + bonus + grant / len(vest)
    return round(first), round(average)

print(one_year(195_000, 0.15, 25_000, 360_000, [0.38, 0.32, 0.20, 0.10]))
# (386050, 314250)

A front-loaded vest and a sign-on make the first year look larger than any later one, and here the last year’s RSUs are the smallest slice of the grant. How the number gets set

This is our reading, not a process any employer publishes. The recruiter negotiates inside a band, the band follows the level, and the level comes out of the interviewers’ debrief, before the offer is written. What employers publish fits that reading: Scale AI’s postings say pay is set during the interview process, based on factors that include interview performance, and Google’s general hiring pages say every candidate is assessed using clear rubrics. So argue for your level in the loop, with stories that show that scope, and again on the recruiter call, using What level do you think you are, and why? as your model.

A script for the offer call

Say the offer is a base in the lower half of Palantir’s New York FDSE range, which runs from $135,000 to $200,000 as of September 2026. Source 15Forward Deployed Software Engineer (New York, NY) - Palantir on LeverPublisherPalantir Technologies (Lever)Source typecompany job posting Here is what to say, in order:

  • Confirm what the band covers. “Thanks, I’m excited about the role. Before I respond, is the posted range base, and is equity on top?”
  • Name a number and the reason. “Based on the scope we discussed, leading two customer deployments end to end, I’d like the base at one eighty-five. If you can get there, I’m ready to sign this week.” Say one figure, not a range, and say the last part only if it is true. One eighty-five, meaning a hundred and eighty-five thousand, sits above the midpoint of the posted range and below its top, and the scope you just named is your evidence for it.
  • Offer a second route. “If base is fixed at this level, can we look at the sign-on or the equity instead?”
  • Get it in writing. “Could you send the full breakdown in writing, with the vesting schedule and the deadline?”

Then be ready for three turns in the call:

  • If you hold a competing offer. “I have a written offer from [company] at $[X] base and $[Y] a year in equity. I’d rather join you. Can you get the base to $[Z]?” Name only an offer you hold in writing, with its real numbers.
  • If the recruiter says offers aren’t negotiated. “Understood, I’m not asking you to break the band. Can you tell me what evidence from the loop set the level, and what I’d need to show to be at the next one?” That moves the talk to the level, which is where the band comes from.
  • If the recruiter asks what you make now. “I’d rather anchor on this role’s range than on my current package, because the scope is different. The range you shared works for me.”

The last two follow our model answer to What are your compensation expectations?. Each line gives the recruiter something to take to the hiring manager: scope, not adjectives. A strong written offer names the level, the base, the equity (instrument, vesting schedule and value), any sign-on and its clawback, and the deadline.
