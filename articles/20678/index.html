What is the FDE salary in Singapore? A breakdown of Forward Deployed Engineer salaries

Original title: 新加坡 FDE 薪资多少？前线部署工程师薪资解读

FDE Times · 2026-10-20 · 5 min read

Summary

This article examines FDE salaries in Singapore and finds strong evidence of demand but almost no reliable public pay data. OpenAI, Singtel, and Field AI have all posted FDE roles there, and The Straits Times reported rising demand, yet none of these ads disclosed salary. The widely cited S$7,000–25,000 monthly range from recruiter Second Talent is borrowed from its machine learning engineer rate card, not measured FDE pay. The only real data point, a mid-level on-site Hiverlab role at S$7K per month, sits at the bottom of Second Talent's junior band, about 26% below its mid-level floor. The author advises treating published ranges as unverified hypotheses and evaluating unnamed-salary job ads by travel share, on-site requirements, experience thresholds, and deployment domain.

Key points

  • OpenAI, Singtel, and Field AI have all posted FDE jobs in Singapore, confirming demand.
  • Second Talent's S$7,000–25,000 monthly range is borrowed from ML engineer rates, not measured.
  • The only real data point is Hiverlab's mid-level on-site role at S$7K per month.
  • That figure is about 26% below the lower bound of Second Talent's mid-level band.
  • Evaluate salary-less ads via travel share, on-site status, experience, and deployment domain.

Key Takeaways

  • Demand for FDEs in Singapore is well documented: OpenAI, Singtel, and Field AI have all posted relevant roles, and The Straits Times has reported a sharp rise in demand.
  • The S$7,000–25,000 monthly range from Second Talent is borrowed from that firm's own machine learning engineer salary bands, not measured FDE salaries.
  • The only figure taken from a real job ad is S$7K for a mid-level role at Hiverlab, which happens to equal the lower bound of that firm's junior band.

The staffing firm Second Talent published a rates page that puts Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) salaries in Singapore at S$7,000 to S$25,000 per month. The same page also states that FDE is not a job title Singapore employers hire for in large numbers.

These two claims contradict each other. If few employers are hiring, where do these ranges come from? The answer is also on the page: the ranges are borrowed from the firm's machine learning engineer salary bands in Singapore. This is an analogy, not a measurement.

Whether you are weighing an offer in Singapore or just want to understand the market in this region, this contradiction directly affects your salary. The first number you see often becomes the anchor for the entire negotiation.

Putting the existing job ads, media coverage, and rates pages together, a pattern emerges: the evidence for FDE demand in Singapore is solid, but pricing is almost entirely absent from public sources. To get usable numbers, you have to collect them yourself.

Demand has left clear traces

On June 13, 2026, The Straits Times reported that FDEs are in high demand in Singapore. A syndicated summary of the article noted that recruitment consultancies have seen demand for the role rise sharply over the past two to three years. The summary did not include salary figures.

Job ads show more clearly who is hiring. OpenAI posted an FDE role requiring at least four years of experience, with travel up to 50%. The role was based in Singapore, showing that a leading AI lab is already recruiting FDEs locally.

Local companies have joined in too. Singapore telecom Singtel posted an FDE job ad dated May 6, 2026.

Field AI's ad adds another layer. Its Singapore FDE role sits within the Autonomy team, requires on-site work, and covers end-to-end deployment of autonomous systems in customer environments. In this region, FDE work is not just LLM software; it also includes robots and hardware in the field.

Pricing is almost entirely absent from public sources

None of the three ads from OpenAI, Field AI, and Singtel gave a salary range. Field AI's page explicitly states that salaries are not published. Singtel's role has been filled. OpenAI's ad is no longer accepting applications, and its job page only shows it was posted more than six months ago.

As a result, most Singapore FDE salary figures circulating in the market come from three types of sources: staffing firms, job aggregators, or a single ad. All three are useful, as long as you understand what each can actually prove.

What does the only real number tell us?

The job aggregator Semesteria lists an FDE role at Hiverlab in Singapore: on-site, mid-level, full-time, S$7K per month. The page does not give an exact posting date, and it is just one ad.

Compare S$7K with Second Talent's table: its junior band is S$7,000–9,500, and its mid-level band is S$9,500–13,500.

A role explicitly labeled mid-level lands exactly at the lower bound of the junior band, S$2,500/month below the mid-level lower bound, about 26% lower.

That gap is enough to decide whether you accept or reject an offer.

A single ad is not enough to dismiss the entire range. Hiverlab may be an outlier; or machine learning engineer salaries may simply not reflect what small local companies actually pay FDEs.

There is no data yet to determine which is true. Until then, the ranges circulating in the market should be treated as untested assumptions.

Audit a salary table the way you would audit customer data

On a customer site, a good FDE does not blindly trust the first dashboard someone shows them. They ask: where did the data come from, how many rows are there, and what date does it go up to. A salary table deserves the same three questions.

Apply them first to Second Talent's table. The numbers are borrowed from another role, not directly measured.

The ranges cover base salary only, not equity, so for companies that pay in stock, they say nothing about that portion of compensation. The table can serve as a rough reference, but it should not be your anchor.

Apply them next to Hiverlab's ad, and the result is the opposite. The number is directly measured, but it is a single data point with an unclear date. It gives you a real benchmark to compare against, but it says nothing about the whole market.

When there is no price, value the role by what the job description states

Ads that do not list salaries still contain verifiable details that directly affect what you should ask for. Up to 50% travel, as in OpenAI's ad, is a very different life from sitting in an office.

Working on-site with robots, as at Field AI, requires different skills than integrating APIs for language models.

So when reading a job description, look for four things: travel percentage, on-site or remote, minimum years of experience, and deployment domain. With these four details, you can compare two roles both titled FDE but actually very different jobs, even if neither ad lists a salary.

When writing your resume, Field AI's ad is the clearest guide: the role requires leading end-to-end deployment of autonomous systems in customer environments. If you are targeting roles like this, describe your experience the same way: what you deployed, where, under what constraints, and with what results.

If you have worked with devices or physical systems, state it explicitly, because Field AI's ad shows the market needs those people too.

Also pay attention to timing. Singtel's role has been filled, and OpenAI's has closed. In a market where recruiters report sharply rising demand but public ads are scarce, setting job alerts and applying early matters more than waiting for a perfect salary table.

Right now in Singapore, what is scarce is not just good FDEs but also salary figures with clear provenance. If you track a few dozen ads yourself and obtain a few real salary ranges, the data in your hands will be better than most existing salary tables.
