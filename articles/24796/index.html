The Difference Between FDE and Software Engineers, SIers, and SEs

Original title: FDEとソフトウェアエンジニア・SIer・SEの違い｜FDEジョブ

FDE Jobs · 2026-05-17 · 3 min read

Summary

This Japanese article compares FDEs with software engineers, SIer contractors, and sales engineers using a comparison table. Its central argument is that the decisive difference is the "endpoint of responsibility": SWEs are accountable until a feature ships, SIers until system delivery, and sales engineers until a deal closes, while FDEs remain responsible until their company's product is live and producing business results at the customer site. The article also contrasts FDEs with SIers (FDEs work on their own product and feed field insights back to product teams) and with sales engineers (FDEs handle post-sale implementation and operational adoption, not just proposals and PoCs). It closes with guidance on who suits each role and notes SIer backgrounds transition well into FDE roles.

Key points

  • The key differentiator across roles is the endpoint of responsibility: FDEs own outcomes through production adoption and business results.
  • FDEs go on-site to integrate and customize their own product for customer-specific problems, unlike SWEs who build in-house.
  • Unlike SIers, FDEs work on their own product and return field learnings to the product team.
  • Unlike sales engineers, FDEs carry post-contract work through to production and operational adoption.
  • People who enjoy both technology and customer dialogue fit FDE; SIer alumni are a common talent pipeline.

Key Points of This Article (30-Second Summary)

The biggest difference between FDEs and software engineers (SWEs), SIers, and sales engineers (SEs) is the "end of responsibility." While SWEs are responsible only up to "building" their company's product, FDEs take responsibility until the product is "delivered and delivers results" at the customer's site, spanning everything from pre-sales to operational adoption.

  • Difference from SWEs: whether you go out to customer sites (FDEs do, integrating and implementing according to customer-specific challenges)
  • Difference from SIers: rather than contracted work, you go deep based on your own company's product and feed insights back into it
  • Difference from SEs: you handle not just proposals and PoCs, but also production implementation and operational adoption after winning the deal
  • End of responsibility: FDE: production adoption and business results / SWE: feature release / SIer: system delivery / SE: winning the deal
  • Who it suits: people who enjoy both technology and customer dialogue, and want to take responsibility for their work being used in the field and delivering results

The Fundamental Difference: "Building" vs. "Delivering and Achieving Results"

Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), software engineers (SWEs), SIers, and sales engineers (SEs) are all technical roles, but their "end of responsibility" differs decisively. SWEs are responsible up to building the product, while FDEs are responsible until value is realized at the customer's site.

Difference from SWEs: Whether You Go Out to Customers

SWEs polish the product in-house. FDEs go out to customers, integrate and custom-implement their company's product to fit customer-specific challenges, and bring it to a state where it is actually used. While both write code, FDEs start by defining "whose problem is being solved" together with the customer.

Difference from SIers: Going Deep Based on Your Own Product

SIers develop systems on contract according to customer requirements. FDEs have the strong foundation of their own company's product and optimize it for customers to deliver value. They also work closely with product development teams and can feed insights gained in the field back into the product—a major difference from SIers. People from SIer backgrounds are a strong fit for FDE roles, and this is one of the classic career-change paths.

Which Role Suits You

If you want to "go deep and narrow with technology," SWE is for you. If you want to "dive into customer business outcomes with technology" or "want the satisfaction of seeing what you build used in the field," FDE is a good fit. Since FDEs experience both technology and business, an added appeal is the wide range of career options afterward (management, strategy, product).
