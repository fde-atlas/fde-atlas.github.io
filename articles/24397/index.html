What Is a Customer Engineer? | Two Meanings, Job Description, and Differences from FDE

Original title: カスタマーエンジニアとは｜2つの意味と仕事内容、FDEとの違い

FDE Consulting · 2026-11-13 · 8 min read

Summary

This article untangles the term Customer Engineer (CE), which means very different jobs in Japan. At domestic equipment companies like Fujifilm Business Innovation Japan, CEs install, maintain, and repair devices and IT infrastructure, with value measured by keeping customer operations running. At Google Cloud, Customer Engineers are technical sales roles who work with sales on demos, pilots, and pre-contract technical evaluation, then hand implementation to other teams. The author compares both with Solutions Architects and FDEs: maintenance CEs protect what exists, cloud CEs drive product adoption, while FDEs own defining, building, and shipping systems into production, judged by business metrics. It ends with advice for CEs aiming to become FDEs and a checklist for buyers choosing between the roles.

Key points

  • CE means device maintenance at Japanese equipment firms but technical sales at cloud vendors.
  • Fujifilm CEs handle installation, inspections, and repairs; their value is preventing downtime.
  • Google Cloud CEs need ~10 years cloud experience and hand off implementation after the deal.
  • FDEs differ by owning the problem definition through production, measured by business metrics.
  • The author suggests CEs build coding, production operations, and metric-measurement skills to become FDEs.

A Customer Engineer (CE) is a role that does technical work on the customer's side. However, what this title refers to varies greatly from company to company. At domestic equipment companies like FUJIFILM Business Innovation Japan, it refers to a technical role that installs, maintains, and repairs equipment and IT infrastructure at customer sites. On the other hand, a Google Cloud Customer Engineer is a technical role that works alongside sales to answer customers' technical questions and help them choose products.

Comparing job postings can be confusing because the same title means different things. This article organizes the job content of the two types of Customer Engineers based on recruiting information published by the Fujifilm Group and Google, and compares the differences with the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer). The FDE role itself is explained in What is an FDE? The Meaning and Job Content of a Forward Deployed Engineer.

The maintenance-focused Customer Engineer at domestic equipment companies

In my view, when people in Japan hear "Customer Engineer," the first thing that comes to mind is maintenance of multifunction printers and servers. FUJIFILM Business Innovation Japan divides its new graduate hiring into a Sales course, a Systems Engineer (SE) course, and a Customer Engineer (CE) course. The spelling is "Custmer" (カストマー), but it refers to the same role.

In an employee interview on the company's recruiting site, an employee is introduced who, after working as a CE for multifunction printers, is responsible for setting up, installing, and maintaining customers' IT infrastructure, including PCs, servers, networks, and cloud services. They handle not only the company's own products but also equipment from other manufacturers, making inquiries to manufacturers and requesting repairs on behalf of customers. The described daily routine involves checking repair requests in the morning, visiting customer sites to perform maintenance work, and setting up PCs at a kitting center in the afternoon.

A similar type of role exists in the medical field. A job posting for a Field Service Engineer at FUJIFILM Medical describes work that involves implementing, maintaining, and supporting medical equipment and IT systems at hospitals and clinics. The duties listed range from installing and maintaining digital X-ray diagnostic imaging systems to building medical imaging networks and performing regular equipment inspections. The required experience section states "Field service/customer service engineer experience welcome," showing that the title for maintenance-focused technical roles varies from company to company.

Here is the work of a maintenance-focused Customer Engineer, arranged according to my view.

TaskDescription
Installation and initial setupInstall equipment, connect it to the network, and get it ready for use
Regular inspectionsFind part degradation and configuration drift before failures occur
Handling failuresWhen called, go to the customer site, isolate the cause, and fix it
Liaising with other manufacturers' equipmentMake inquiries to manufacturers and request repairs on the customer's behalf
Providing information to customersListen to their problems and work with sales to lead to the next proposal

In my view, the value of this role lies in "keeping things running." The more a customer's business depends on equipment and systems, the greater the loss when they go down. A maintenance CE supports customers not by building things, but by keeping what already exists running.

The Customer Engineer at foreign cloud companies

At Google Cloud, a Customer Engineer is a technical sales role. The job posting for Senior Customer Engineer, Google Cloud in Tokyo explains that the role works with technical sales teams, serves as the customer's primary technical counterpart, understands business challenges, resolves technical questions and issues, and participates in proofs of concept and demos.

The responsibilities listed in that posting, translated into Japanese-style summaries, are as follows.

Responsibilities from the job postingSupplementary details
Own technical account planning and strategyDecide which areas each assigned customer will use the products in
Demonstrate product value through demos, pilots, and workshopsHave customers verify that the products work in their environment before signing a contract
Design cross-domain architectures and win deals on technical meritPropose solutions combining infrastructure, applications, data, and AI
After the contract, hand over the final implementation plan to the implementation teamImplementation is handled by the team responsible for deployment
Monitor the progress of the implementation plan and support launch and migrationContinue to advise even after the handover

The minimum qualifications are 10 years of experience with cloud-native architectures in customer-facing or support roles, and experience presenting to technical stakeholders and executives. There are also AI-focused positions. The job posting for AI Customer Engineer, AI Platform, Google Cloud (Japanese) in Tokyo lists as its work qualifying sales opportunities to drive AI adoption, identifying customers' technical concerns, and creating demos and presentations, with requirements of 6 years of experience in customer-facing or support roles and fluent Japanese.

In short, the CE at foreign cloud companies focuses on technical evaluation before the deal is won and on creating the implementation plan. This role largely overlaps with the one covered in What is a Sales Engineer?. The name differs by company, but in my view it can be considered the same position.

The difference between the two Customer Engineers and the FDE

Adding a Solution Architect and an FDE to the two roles discussed so far, here is a comparison. Since every company defines these roles differently, this is an arrangement based on my own view.

RoleMain period of involvementWhat they are responsible forHow the work ends
Maintenance-focused Customer EngineerAfter implementation, long-termThat the installed equipment and systems keep runningContinues until the contract period ends
Customer Engineer at foreign cloud companiesFrom before the deal is won through implementation planningWinning the deal on technical merit and the implementation planHands over to the implementation team
Solution ArchitectFrom before the deal is won through post-implementationDesigning and validating the architectureImplementation succeeds and usage expands
FDEFrom defining the problem through productionThe system itself that runs in the customer's businessUsed in production, driving measurable business results

The maintenance CE protects what has been built, while the cloud CE helps customers choose a product and hands it off to the people who build it. The FDE takes on, by themselves, the part in between: "deciding what to build, building it, and getting it into production." The job posting for an FDE at OpenAI in Tokyo states that the role works with strategically important customers and handles discovery, technical scoping, design, building, and production deployment, and requires directly modifying code when progress depends on it. Results are measured by production adoption and measurable impact on the business.

In my view, the three roles differ in "whose what they move forward." A maintenance CE moves forward the customer's daily operations, a cloud CE moves forward the adoption of their own company's products, and an FDE moves forward changes in the customer's business. No role is superior to another; they are simply needed in different situations.

When a Customer Engineer aims to become an FDE

For someone aiming to become an FDE from a maintenance CE, the strength is the very experience of working at customer sites. The ability to listen to people struggling in the field, isolate causes within limited time, and arrange things so the customer's business keeps running is directly needed in FDE work too. What tends to be lacking is experience writing code and building systems, and experience developing with AI models and APIs.

Someone aiming to become an FDE from a cloud CE has product knowledge and the ability to explain things to a customer's management. What tends to be lacking, in my view, is the part after handover to another team for implementation, that is, the experience of building something that runs in production yourself, operating it, and continuing to fix it.

The order in which to acquire these skills is, in my view, the following three steps.

  • Automate a small business task by writing the code yourself and running it in production
  • Measure the effect of what you ran in business metrics and explain it
  • Fix problems that arise in operation, and organize what you built into a form others can use

The path to becoming an FDE is summarized in How to become an FDE?, and what is asked in interviews is summarized in What is asked in an FDE interview.

How to assign work, from the perspective of the commissioning side

From the perspective of a company introducing AI or cloud services, the name "Customer Engineer" alone does not tell you what the person handles. In my view, checking the following three things before commissioning reduces discrepancies.

What to checkMaintenance CECloud CEFDE
Deciding together what to buildDoes not decide (maintains what is installed)Decides within the scope of product usageDecides from business issues
Writing code and building systemsDoes not buildUp to demos and validationBuilds things that run in production
Who fixes things after installationFixes under a maintenance contractProduct support or the implementation teamDecides the operational setup and hands over

If the goal is to keep equipment and systems running, a maintenance contract with a maintenance CE fits. If the product candidates are already decided and you want help with technical evaluation, consulting a vendor's CE is the fastest route. If you want to decide what to build and have no one in-house to build it, you need someone who will take responsibility through to something running in production.
