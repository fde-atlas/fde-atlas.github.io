Drawing on primary sources from Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic plus Japanese recruiting practice, it explains that FDEs embed at customer sites to implement products in production, unlike solutions architects or consultants. Since FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a new profession, the current reality is that searching in Japanese only turns up fragmentary information. On this page, we have compiled the questions most frequently received in our FDE-specialized career change and hiring support work, organized into four categories—fundamentals, working style, career changes, and hiring—in a Q&A format.

The answers are based on overseas primary sources regarding Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic, as well as our hands-on support experience in the Japanese market.

Key Takeaways of This Article

  • FDE stands for Forward Deployed Engineer, and in Japan it is generally pronounced "efu-di-i-e" (FDE).
  • There are no required certifications for FDE; what companies look for in selection is a track record of delivering projects end to end.
  • Even without hands-on FDE experience, if you have software development experience, you can transition to FDE from SIer, web development, consulting, or pre-sales roles.
  • While FDE job openings do exist in Japan, many are effectively FDE roles posted under different job titles such as "Solution Engineer" or "AI Implementation Engineer."

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

QWhat kind of profession is an FDE? In a nutshell?

An engineer who embeds themselves at the client's site and implements and deploys their own company's products (especially AI and data products) to fit the client's real business challenges. It is a profession born at Palantir, often described as a combination of "an engineer with implementation skills" and "a consultant who discovers problems."

QHow is FDE pronounced, and what is the full name?

The full name is Forward Deployed Engineer; in Japan it is generally pronounced "efu-di-i-e" (FDE). The name derives from the military term "deployed to the front lines."

QWhy has FDE suddenly become a hot topic?

Because the "last mile" problem—having a model but being unable to integrate it into business operations—became apparent with enterprise adoption of generative AI, and OpenAI and Anthropic launched FDE organizations one after another. US VC a16z called it "the hottest job in tech," and according to an analysis by Indeed and the Financial Times, FDE job postings increased by more than 800% from January to September 2025 (as cited in an official Salesforce article).

QWhat is the difference between an FDE and a Solution Architect (SA)?

The explanation from OpenAI's head of FDE is clear: "FDEs are more hands-on than SAs and deal with more ambiguous situations. SAs typically don't write code on customer infrastructure, but FDEs write production-quality code." Unlike SAs, who focus on advice and PoCs, FDEs take responsibility through production implementation and long-term accompaniment.

QWhat is the difference between an FDE and SES (client-site dispatch)?

Whether there is an in-house product. FDEs adapt their company's own product to the client's site and feed insights gained at the client back into product improvement. SES, on the other hand, provides engineers to a client's project under a quasi-delegation contract, with compensation paid for hours worked. Details are covered in the article "Differences Between FDE and SES, SIer, SE, and Consultants."

QWhat is the difference between an FDE and an IT consultant?

A consultant's deliverable is a recommendation (report), and the relationship ends when the contract period is over. FDEs differ in that they are embedded long-term as part of the client's team, implement solutions with their own hands, and feed those insights back into improving their company's product.

QWhat is the salary of an FDE?

Salaries offered for FDE positions in Japan generally fall in the range of 10 to 20 million yen annually, and job postings listing monthly salaries start from around 500,000 yen per month (as of October 2026). Tokyo positions at foreign AI companies may also apply global-level compensation structures. Monthly salary breakdowns and overseas levels are covered in the article "FDE Salary and Compensation." Because the range varies greatly depending on experience and company stage, please check the latest openings and specific market rates in a free career consultation.

QIs the FDE working style tough? How much travel and on-site work is involved?

Being on-site at client locations is an essential part of the profession; overseas examples indicate that Palantir engineers spend about 25% of their working hours on-site, and up to 50% at some companies. There are also cases of working in factories or closed-network environments. On the other hand, discretion is high, and the freedom to act like a startup CTO makes the role appealing to some people.

QWhat does the FDE career path look like?

At Palantir, many FDEs have transitioned to product development engineer roles. Because FDEs combine customer understanding with full-stack implementation skills, their careers tend to expand into positions at the intersection of technology and business, such as product manager, business owner, or founding lead of a solutions organization.

QWhat skills are needed to become an FDE?

Three layers: (1) production-quality software engineering (full stack), (2) LLM and AI implementation (prompt design, agent development, evaluation infrastructure), and (3) client-facing and problem-discovery skills. Details are covered in the article "The Skill Set Needed to Become an FDE."

Q: Do you need qualifications for QFDE?

There are no required qualifications. What companies look at is not credentials but "a track record of delivering end to end." If anything, cloud certifications (AWS/GCP/Azure) serve as supplementary proof that you can understand customer environments, but a portfolio of LLM applications built to production quality is far stronger evidence.

Q: Can you switch to FDE with no prior experience?

Since there are almost no people with hands-on FDE experience in the market, companies hire on the assumption that candidates will transition from adjacent roles such as SIer, web development, consulting, or pre-sales. Software development experience is a prerequisite, but "no FDE experience" is not a disadvantage. Now is actually the time to become an early adopter.

Q: Are there FDE jobs in Japan?

Yes. As of September 2026, OpenAI, Palantir, Databricks, SoftBank, LayerX, Sansan, JDSC, Money Forward, and others are recruiting FDEs in Japan. A survey aggregating public job postings in Japan (February 2026) confirmed 35 openings in total: 26 at Japanese companies and 9 at foreign companies. Many openings use different titles such as "Deployment Strategist" or "Applied AI Engineer," so searching by job title alone will cause you to miss them.

Q: Can new graduates become FDEs?

In the US, there are FDE openings targeting new graduates; Palantir recruits new-graduate Forward Deployed Software Engineers at an annual salary of $135,000 to $145,000. In Japan, as of September 2026, no full-time FDE openings for new graduates were confirmed, though LayerX and Fujitsu are recruiting FDE internships for students. Details are covered in the "FDE Career Guide" article.

Q: What are the benefits for companies hiring FDEs?

Three points: (1) filling the last mile of deployment raises order wins and retention rates, (2) discoveries at customer sites feed back into product improvements (at OpenAI there are cases where FDE insights led to API improvements), and (3) it develops people who can balance per-client customization with product standardization. Unlike outsourcing, the expertise accumulates in-house.

Q: How should you create FDE hiring requirements?

Start by documenting the job duties and their allocation in writing. Anthropic's job posting describes FDE duties concretely in terms of deliverables and also states the percentage of travel to customer sites (around 25%). Rather than demanding everything from one person, a design that splits roles into implementation (Delta) and problem discovery (Echo), as Palantir does, is also effective. Details are covered in the "FDE Hiring Guide."
