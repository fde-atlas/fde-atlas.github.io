Summary

This buyer's guide to website vulnerability diagnosis stresses that most services only identify vulnerabilities; fixing them is a separate task, and reports often go unaddressed when no one is assigned to remediate. It compares three approaches (tool-based scanning, manual testing, penetration testing), lists published prices from Japanese vendors (tool scans from ¥121,000 per run, manual-inclusive diagnoses from ¥200,000 for 10 pages, penetration tests from ¥1 million), and warns that page counts, login screens, and tax treatment must be aligned when comparing quotes. Providers are grouped into four types—tool, diagnosis firm, in-house, and FDE-style—distinguished mainly by who performs fixes. The author, whose company DeFactory sells FDE-style remediation, discloses this conflict of interest.

Key points

  • Diagnosis finds vulnerabilities; fixing them needs a separate owner decided before ordering
  • Published prices: tool scans from ¥121,000, manual-inclusive from ¥200,000 for 10 pages
  • Four provider types differ mainly in who performs remediation
  • Compare quotes with aligned page counts, login screens, and tax treatment
  • Author's company DeFactory offers FDE-style remediation—disclosed bias

Website vulnerability diagnostics cover only finding holes (vulnerabilities) in a site; fixing them is often not included. IPA (Information-technology Promotion Agency) also explicitly states that even if no vulnerabilities are found in a diagnostic, the site cannot be considered safe. So before requesting a diagnostic, decide who will fix the issues identified in the report. This article is for clients looking to outsource diagnostics and does not cover product comparisons of diagnostic tools.

[Disclosure] Our operating company, DeFactory, also provides FDE-type vulnerability response support (a model that involves working on-site through to remediation). This comparison was written based on public information, but please keep in mind that our own company is included.

A vulnerability is a flaw in a program or configuration that can be exploited by attackers, leading to information leaks or tampering. Website vulnerability diagnostics is a service that examines, within a defined scope, whether such holes remain in a site and compiles the findings into a report. The term "security diagnostics" is also often used with the same meaning for websites. For example, it examines whether entering malicious strings into input fields can steal database contents (SQL injection), or whether arbitrary processes can be executed in another user's browser (cross-site scripting).

Diagnostics fall into the following 3 types depending on the method.

Before outsourcing, there are 3 checks you can do yourself. Understanding your situation here makes it easier to write the items for your quote request (Chapter 6).

- List the software running your site (CMS, plugins, server OS) and check for outdated versions - Check whether unused pages or sites remain publicly accessible (if left unmanaged, even if holes are found, you won't be able to address them) - Check whether communications are encrypted (HTTPS) and whether easily guessable passwords are used for logins

IPA's "20 Guidelines for Safe Website Operation and Management" publishes a checklist of 20 items including these (source: IPA "20 Guidelines for Safe Website Operation and Management"). The same document recommends continuing external diagnostics periodically even after going live. Self-checks serve to reduce the entry points for problems found. However, they are not a substitute for diagnostics, so sites handling personal information or payments should consider outsourcing.

IPA's "Web Health Diagnosis Specification" is a document summarizing basic inspection patterns narrowed down to 13 items. It states that even if no vulnerabilities are found in these diagnostics, it does not constitute a declaration of safety (source: IPA "How to Build Secure Websites"). The same interpretation applies when free tools or simple diagnostics report "no issues."

The content of a diagnostic changes depending on "what is being examined." Before requesting quotes, determine which category your request falls into.

A request refers to a single communication sent to the site, such as pressing a button on a screen. Companies differ in whether they count costs by "page" or by "request."

A diagnostic that examines vulnerabilities accessible through screens and input fields; "website vulnerability assessment" generally refers to this. IPA's Web Health Check specification lists 13 items, including SQL injection, cross-site scripting, and flaws in authentication (login), session management (how login state is maintained), and authorization control (how permissions are divided after login). When diagnosing screens that require login, a diagnostic account must be prepared. Sakura Cyber Security's diagnostic tool allows one login-required location per diagnostic run. For sites with many screens requiring authentication, confirm the assumptions behind the quote.

Network diagnostics examine the servers and network equipment hosting the site itself. Even if the site's programs have no vulnerabilities, an outdated server OS could be an entry point. Open-source diagnostic software includes OpenVAS, used to automatically check server age and configuration flaws. When outsourced, pricing is often determined by the number of IP addresses. Published prices include ABURIDA at 40,000 yen per IP (44,000 yen including tax) and Cyber Security Cloud from 350,000 yen for 5 IPs (excluding tax).

WordPress is built from a collection of components made by others: the core, themes, and plugins. Therefore, diagnostics focus on checking whether component versions are outdated and whether any components with known vulnerabilities are included. Custom programs you added, as well as input fields such as contact forms, are examined using the same methods as website diagnostics. When requesting diagnostics from a company, tell them it is WordPress and the number of plugins in use. Updating or replacing components flagged in diagnostics can affect site behavior, so the need for someone to handle fixes is covered in Chapter 5. No price list for WordPress-specific diagnostics was found on the public pages reviewed this time. Costs depend on the scope: simple diagnostics that only check components can be cheap, while examining custom programs is quoted like website diagnostics based on page count and so on.

Guidelines for choosing a provider are as follows.

・You have your own programs or screens (login, contact form, member pages) → Website diagnostics ・Your company or a contractor manages the server OS and configuration → Include network diagnostics in scope ・WordPress with few custom programs → Focus on checking component update status. If there are custom programs or forms, examine those parts with website diagnostics ・Not sure which applies → Include your site's configuration (CMS type, server administrator) in the quote request and consult

Open-source free tools (ZAP (formerly OWASP ZAP), OpenVAS, etc.) can also diagnose sites and servers. Their strength is zero cost, but since diagnostics become in-house work, configuration, scheduling execution times, interpreting results, and triaging false positives remain with your company. IPA has evaluated three open-source tools for testing website vulnerabilities and summarized their characteristics and usage caveats in a report. Free tools are suitable when your company has development staff who can read the results and make fixes. If used by someone unfamiliar with interpreting results, the outcome tends to be either "too many findings to handle" or "no findings, so we're safe."

Three trends can be read from the table.

・Tool-based diagnosis costs roughly 100,000 to several hundred thousand yen, while diagnosis including manual work costs several hundred thousand to over 1 million yen ・The main factors that move the price are the number of pages (screens), the number of requests, and the number of IPs ・Since "up to how many pages" and "up to how many requests" differ by provider, comparing only the total price can lead to a wrong decision

Even with the same number of pages, the final price changes depending on the number of screens requiring login, whether a reporting session is included, and the conditions for re-diagnosis. For example, among "automated + manual" services covering up to 10 pages, CyberBusiness starts at 200,000 yen while CEC starts at 750,000 yen, a gap of more than 3x in published prices. Pages and requests are counted differently, so it cannot be simply compared with ABURIDA (440,000 yen for 10 requests). You cannot compare unless you request quotes from multiple providers with the same assumptions stated.

Three guidelines for applying this to your own case can be read from the published prices (these are readings of the table prices, not quotes).

・A corporate site of about 10 pages, checked first with automated tools → Tool diagnosis at 121,000 to 363,000 yen (tax included; Sakura, ITM) ・About 10 pages, wanting manual work added → Ranges from 200,000 yen (CyberBusiness) to 750,000 yen and up (CEC), varying by company ・Sites with logins (member pages, e-commerce) → There are examples where one login-requiring location counts as one diagnosis (Sakura), and additional costs may arise based on the number of login screens, so state the number of screens when requesting a quote

The published prices cover the diagnosis and the report. The cost of fixing the holes found is, in most cases, not included. No company publishing fix costs was found among the public pages of the 8 companies in the table. Re-diagnosis is free under certain conditions in some cases. CEC offers free re-diagnosis of automated diagnosis within 3 months after the diagnosis, and ABURIDA also offers free re-diagnosis within 3 months for findings rated Medium or higher. However, re-diagnosis is a "confirmation that it was fixed," not the fixing work itself. Fix costs should be estimated separately depending on who handles the fixes, covered in Chapter 5.

When the diagnosis is complete, you receive a report listing findings with severity ratings and proposed fixes. Everything after that is work outside the diagnosis service. If no one is assigned to fix the findings, the report will sit in a shared folder, no one will open it, and the holes will remain. IPA also recommends, after reviewing the results of a web health check, receiving a more detailed diagnosis or implementing countermeasures.

The person who fixes the findings in the report will be one of the following four.

The diagnosis company's "reporting session" is a venue for explaining the report's contents, not the fixing itself. ABURIDA's reporting session costs 150,000 yen (165,000 yen tax included) separately and is not included in the diagnosis price. If no one has been assigned to handle fixes, asking at the quote stage "can you handle fixes as well?" reduces the effort of searching again later. FDE-type costs vary depending on the diagnosis scope and fix scope, so they are quoted individually. The process in the case of DeFactory is described in 5-4.

Re-diagnosis is a diagnosis to verify "whether the hole was closed" after fixing. If you undergo re-diagnosis without fixing anything, you will simply receive the same findings. The order is "diagnosis → fix → confirm with re-diagnosis," so decide who will handle the fix stage before signing the contract.

Major fixes that change how screens are built, or fixes requiring coordination with the production company, may not finish immediately even after a hole is found. IPA's 20 guidelines list, as a countermeasure when a web application cannot be fixed promptly, introducing a WAF (Web Application Firewall, a mechanism that detects and blocks malicious communication) as "one of the effective means" to reduce impact until the fix is made. ABURIDA also states that it proposes introducing a WAF when fixes cannot be made before release. A WAF is not a means of eliminating vulnerabilities, but a means of reducing damage until the fix is made. Even after introducing a stopgap measure, decide on the person responsible for fixes and a deadline.

The flow when entrusting an FDE type, using DeFactory as an example (from its public pages): ・After the diagnosis, decide together, while considering business impact, which findings to fix first and which to defer ・Enter the site, fix the code, and hand over the fix methods to in-house members (technology transfer through pair programming) ・Contract is a quasi-mandate (committed to problem solving), basically 3 months; costs vary by diagnosis and fix scope, so quoted individually ・Entry point for consultation is a free hearing (about 1 hour); a proposal is made within 5 business days of the hearing, and kickoff takes place roughly 2 weeks after agreement You can consult even at a stage where the issues are not yet organized. When choosing, check the following six items in order.

  • Do the diagnosis target and methods fit your company? Align on which of a website, network, or WordPress is examined, and by tool or manually (1-1, Chapter 2)
  • Are the quote assumptions aligned? Compare with the number of pages, requests, and IPs aligned, and whether prices are tax included or excluded (Chapter 4)
  • Is the report's content understandable? Check with a sample whether it includes severity ratings, reproduction steps, and fix methods
  • Is there a reporting session and question support? Confirm whether a reporting session is offered and its cost, and the period for accepting questions after the diagnosis
  • How far can post-diagnosis fixes and re-diagnosis be entrusted? Confirm the scope of fix support, and the free conditions and period for re-diagnosis (Chapter 5)
  • Are the impact of the diagnosis and required advance procedures clear? Confirm whether the diagnosis will be performed on the live site or in a dedicated diagnostic environment. IPA's Web Health Check Specification (source) states that a diagnosis may cause the site to go down or unintended data to be registered, and requires obtaining prior permission from the server or data center administrator.

When requesting a quote, write the following items. This reduces the effort for the diagnostic company to reconfirm assumptions and makes it easier to compare the returned prices.

・Target of the diagnosis (site URL, number of network IP addresses, presence of WordPress and number of plugins) ・Number of pages, number of screens requiring login, number of forms ・Desired diagnostic method (tool only, or including manual testing) ・Diagnostic environment (production, diagnostic environment) and desired timing ・Report submission destination (for internal use, for submission to business partners) and whether a report meeting is needed ・Whether to request fixes as well, or diagnosis only ・Re-diagnosis conditions (number of times, period, cost) ・Whether prices are tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive

Also confirm whether you need to notify the hosting company or cloud administrator before performing the diagnosis. For consultations on fixes, Comparison and Selection of FDE Companies lets you compare types of outsourcing partners that handle fixes.
