What Is an AI Engineer? | Job Description and How It Differs from an FDE

"AI Engineer" has become one of the most common job titles in job postings. However, even when the title is the same "AI Engineer," some postings refer to people who train models, while others refer to people who integrate existing AI into products. The title alone doesn't tell you what the job actually involves.

In this article, I will clarify where the term "AI Engineer" came from, how it differs from similar roles, and what the work and required skills involve in the era of generative AI. Finally, I will compare it with the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), who goes into customers' operations and implements AI. A detailed definition of the FDE role is summarized in What is an FDE? The Meaning and Work of a Forward Deployed Engineer.

What is an AI Engineer?

An AI Engineer is an engineer who uses AI technology to build working products and business systems. In my view, the term is currently used in Japan with two overlapping meanings.

  • Broad meaning: engineers involved with AI in general, including both those who build machine learning models and those who integrate AI into products
  • Narrow meaning: software engineers who use generative AI models via APIs and integrate them into products and business systems

The person who popularized the narrow meaning was the American developer Shawn Wang (swyx), in "The Rise of the AI Engineer," written on June 30, 2023. He wrote that software engineers who turn AI APIs and open-source models into products would converge under the title "AI Engineer," and predicted that this would become the most in-demand engineering job of the next decade. The central claim is that what it takes to bring AI products to the world is not researchers but engineers.

The numbers from that time still exist. According to the same article, on the job site Indeed, machine learning engineer job postings were 10 times more numerous than AI engineer postings. swyx predicted that because AI engineers were growing so quickly, this ratio would reverse within five years.

I could not find public figures to verify whether the ratio has reversed, but the assessment of rapid growth aligns with other US surveys. In LinkedIn's US "Jobs on the Rise" published in January 2025, AI Engineer ranked first among the fastest-growing roles. The ranking is based on job growth for roles started between January 2022 and July 2024.

Differences from Similar Roles

Roles involving AI become easier to understand when divided by what they build and how far their responsibility extends. The following table is my own organization of this.

RoleMainly buildsFocusMain outcomes
AI ResearcherNew models and methodsPapers, new performanceResearch results
Machine Learning EngineerTrained models and the systems to operate themData, training, evaluation, inference infrastructureModels that achieve accuracy
Data ScientistAnalysis and prediction models, materials for decision-makingStatistics, analysis, business implicationsAnalysis usable for decisions
AI Engineer (narrow meaning)Applications and business systems that integrate existing modelsAPIs, retrieval, evaluation, production operationsProducts that get used
FDEAI implementations tailored to customers' operationsCustomers' sites, operations, dataMovement in customers' KPIs

The same person may hold multiple roles. Especially in small organizations, the line between machine learning engineer and AI engineer is unclear. swyx himself wrote that AI engineers sometimes go as far as fine-tuning models, and machine learning engineers sometimes build products on top of APIs. The table shows the focus of each role, not a classification of people.

What AI Engineers Do

The work of an AI engineer in the narrow sense is to build working systems using generative AI models as components. In my view, the work can be broadly divided into six parts.

  • Choosing models and usage suited to the problem. Deciding which model to use, with what settings, and how much to delegate to it
  • Designing prompts and input/output formats. Making the model answer in a fixed format and rejecting answers that don't conform
  • Building mechanisms to provide internal information. Searching internal documents and databases and passing them to the model along with the question (RAG)
  • Connecting to tools and systems. Enabling AI to read and write internal systems, and designing permissions and approvals
  • Building evaluations. Preparing test cases and criteria to measure answer quality, and re-measuring every time the model or prompts change
  • Operating in production. Monitoring cost, response speed, error reports, and logs, and continuously making improvements

Among these, I believe the biggest differences from a machine learning engineer's work are the fifth item, evaluation, and the fourth item, permissions. Instead of training models themselves, AI engineers take responsibility for measuring model errors and building systems where mistakes cause no harm. How to handle plausible errors is covered in Countermeasures Against Hallucinations, and how to handle instructions hidden in external documents is covered in What is Prompt Injection?

Skills Required for AI Engineers

The required skills fall into two layers: the fundamentals of being a software engineer, and technologies specific to generative AI.

LayerSkillContent
FundamentalsWeb APIs and backendsCalling external APIs, processing results, preparing for failures
FundamentalsDatabases and searchHolding internal data and quickly retrieving the necessary parts
FundamentalsTesting and production operationsVerifying that changes don't break things, and noticing anomalies through logs and monitoring
Generative AIPrompt and output designDeciding the format of answers and producing verifiable outputs
Generative AIRAG and retrieval designHow to split documents, retrieval methods, and access control by permission
Generative AIEvaluationCreating correct answers based on business criteria, and measuring errors and "I don't know" separately
Generative AISecurityPreparing for instructions hidden in external text, excessive permissions, and data exfiltration

In my view, the fundamentals come first. Generative AI technologies change quickly, and methods from six months ago can become outdated, but the abilities around APIs, databases, testing, and operations remain effective.

Differences Between AI Engineers and FDEs

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) overlaps almost entirely with an AI engineer in terms of technology. What differs is where they work and for whom.

FDE originated in the US with Palantir. Palantir's official blog explains its engineering roles by dividing them into "Dev," which delivers one feature to many customers, and "Delta," which delivers many features to a single customer. This Delta is the prototype of the FDE. Today, Anthropic also recruits engineers for its Applied AI team under the title of FDE. Based on data from Indeed, the Financial Times reported that FDE job postings grew by more than 800% from January to September 2025.

Points of comparisonAI EngineerFDE
Where they workIn-house development teamOn-site with the customer (attending business meetings and working alongside them)
What they buildAI features for their own company's products and systemsAI implementations tailored to that customer's operations and data
First taskStarting from fixed specifications or defined problemsStarting by working with the customer to decide what problem to solve
How results are measuredProduct quality and usageWhether the customer's business KPIs moved
Required skillsDepth of implementation, evaluation, operationsImplementation plus business understanding and building consensus with the customer

In my view, FDE is a role that combines AI engineer skills with the ability to immerse oneself in a customer's business and define the problems to solve. Someone who has built implementation and evaluation skills as an AI engineer and moves toward the side of talking with customers and defining problems is approaching the FDE role. The differences between FDEs and SES, SIer, and consulting firms are summarized in Differences between FDE and SES, and preparation for becoming an FDE is covered in How to become an FDE?.
