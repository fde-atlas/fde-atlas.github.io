AI Field Engineer vs FDE vs sales engineer: Fireworks

FDE Times · 2026-11-09 · 6 min read

Summary

In brief

  • Fireworks wants its AI Field Engineers to act as technical lead on co-sell deals while also building POCs and MVPs themselves and taking them to production.
  • According to Christian & Timbers, a recruiting firm, a sales engineer differs from an FDE in where responsibility stops: the sales engineer is done when the customer signs, while the FDE owns the production outcome.
  • Before applying, ask whether you will be measured by deal win rate or by a system that actually runs, because that decides your day-to-day work.

Fireworks AI is hiring a Field Engineering manager. The person in this job will track and improve the team’s win rate, velocity and POC cycle time, exactly the kind of scorecard a sales team uses.

Yet the same posting asks candidates to show they have built production software alongside customers, not merely advised from the sidelines.

These two requirements pull in different directions, and Fireworks does not hide it. For the AI Field Engineer – Strategic Partnerships role, the company accepts candidates with three or more years of experience in any one of four kinds of work: pre-sales, partner engineering, forward-deployed or technical consulting. In other words, it treats sales engineering and forward deployed engineering as two entrances to the same job.

If you are aiming for an FDE role, you will see more hybrid postings like this. The title on the posting does not tell you what you will be doing every day six months in. To find out, read the metrics and the pay terms closely, then ask the employer directly.

One role, two job descriptions

The description of the Strategic Partnerships role has two distinct halves. The first is commercial: the AI Field Engineer is technical lead on co-sell deals, meaning selling solutions to shared customers together with strategic partners. The second is building: making POCs and MVPs end to end with the partner’s engineering team.

The preferred qualifications make the second half even clearer. Fireworks wants someone with a track record of taking GenAI POCs from prototype to production-scale deployment. That is FDE experience, because a pure sales engineer usually stops once the demo has convinced the buyer.

The pay terms, however, lean towards sales. The published figure is an on-target earnings (OTE) range plus equity, the familiar format for roles where part of income is tied to sales results, unlike the fixed salary band of a pure engineering role. For candidates, this detail says a good deal about how the company will judge you.

The real boundary is the signature

So how do you tell whether a hybrid role sits closer to sales engineering or to forward deployed engineering? Christian & Timbers, a recruiting firm, argues that the two roles differ little in technical skill or in how much time is spent with customers. They differ in where responsibility stops. A sales engineer is done when the customer signs. For an FDE, responsibility continues after the signature.

Fullstack describes it along the same lines. Sales engineers and solutions engineers support the sales cycle before the close. FDEs work inside the customer’s environment, build a working solution and take it into production themselves.

An FDE interview preparation course on Educative sums it up with a yardstick: a sales engineer is judged by the buyer’s confidence, an FDE by a system that works in the customer’s environment.

Apply that yardstick to the Fireworks description and you see a role that spans both sides. The table below compares them criterion by criterion.

Criterion Sales engineer FDE AI Field Engineer at Fireworks
When the job is done When the customer signs When the system delivers results in production The team owns everything from technical evaluation to production adoption
What gets produced The buyer’s confidence A system running in the customer’s environment End-to-end POCs/MVPs with the partner’s engineering team
How performance is judged Signals from the buyer A working system Team metrics tracked by the manager: win rate, velocity, POC cycle time, utilization
Experience required Pre-sales Building and deploying with customers Pre-sales, partner engineering, FDE or consulting; preference for people who have taken GenAI POCs to production

The last column shows something neither posting says on its own. In scope of work, Fireworks describes an FDE. In the metrics the manager tracks for the team, it describes a sales engineering team, although the postings do not say whether each AI Field Engineer is measured in exactly the same way.

What a team is measured on is what it will do

According to the manager posting, Fireworks’ Field Engineering team owns technical evaluation and bringing customers into production. The evaluation process runs through four steps: discovery, architecture, POC, then a production plan. The team’s playbook includes a discovery framework, POC templates and reference architectures, the familiar tools of a deployment team.

But the metrics the manager must track and improve are win rate, velocity, POC cycle time and utilization. The manager also has to coach both account executives and Field Engineers. This team sits very close to the sales process.

Consider a hypothetical scenario. You lead a POC for a document extraction agent for a customer shared by Fireworks and a partner. You move fast, the demo looks good, the deal closes, and every team metric improves.

Three months later, the customer sends real traffic through it, latency rises and accuracy on messy data drops. The question now is who fixes it.

If you are the one fixing it, and that work counts in your review, you are doing an FDE’s job. If you have already moved on to the next POC because your calendar follows the sales cycle, you are a sales engineer who codes well.

Neither is better than the other. They are simply two different jobs, and the metrics will pull your time towards one of them. A metric such as POC cycle time rewards speed. Responsibility for production, by contrast, rewards care over the parts nobody sees in the demo.

How to apply for a hybrid role

Fireworks’ manager posting groups Field Engineering, Solutions Engineering, FDE and Pre-Sales together. That is good news: whichever track you are on, there is a way in. The condition is that your CV proves the half you are missing.

If you come from sales engineering, the weakness that will be scrutinised is what happens after the signature. A line such as “led many demos” is not enough. Describe a POC you followed through to production: how the real data differed from the demo data, what you fixed, and how the system is running now.

If you come from FDE work or software engineering, the weakness is on the other side. Fireworks needs someone who can act as technical lead in front of buyers. Show evidence that you have run discovery, narrowed a POC’s scope so the customer could decide faster, and understand why POC cycle time matters to a sales team.

In the interview, turn Educative’s yardstick into a question. At this company, is your success counted in buyer confidence or in a system running in the customer’s environment? Then ask who owns the system once the deal closes, and how many POCs have actually reached production.

If the employer answers clearly, that is a sign it has thought the role through. If the answers are vague, assume the OTE component will set the priorities in your calendar.

The AI Field Engineer title is not a bad one; it is simply honest that the role carries two jobs. You need to know which one wins when your calendar is full, and you should know it before you sign the contract.
