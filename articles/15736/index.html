On Flatness and Hierarchy

Fourth Age's Substack · 2026-05-21 · 8 min read

Summary

Fourth Age argues that succeeding with a Forward Deployed Engineering model requires first becoming the best place for FDEs to work, and that flatness is central to this. The company rejects tenure-based career ladders common at large tech firms. Instead, senior FDEs still build and deliver hands-on, junior FDEs lead from day one, and ownership expands with demonstrated impact rather than years served. Promotions, raises, and titles exist but serve to recognize value already delivered. Anyone can question decisions, including the CEO's. The author, whose firm has 60+ FDEs with 225 combined years of experience, claims flatness is what lets an FDE organization scale while staying differentiated.

Key points

  • The company treats top engineers as its first customers, and flatness is the product offered to them.
  • Senior FDEs stay hands-on in delivery, since leadership works through osmosis, not formal management.
  • Junior FDEs are expected to lead immediately, forcing rapid, non-linear growth.
  • Promotions and pay recognize impact already demonstrated, reversing the usual promotion cycle.
  • Open access to founders and the CEO removes organizational barriers; decisions can be overridden by anyone with better evidence.

At Fourth Age, our mission is to deliver massive value to enterprises via the Forward Deployed Engineering model. This worthy goal is however much easier stated than accomplished, and (at least nominally) shared by many. In order to succeed, we believe we must first succeed at another co-equal mission, which is to be the best place in the world to be a Forward Deployed Engineer. This means that our first customers are therefore really the top-tier engineers who have the capacity and relentless desire to go out and solve complex, real-world problems, and we have to be able to deliver the best product possible to those candidates and employees who make up this highly competitive market.

There are a variety of strategies and principles we adhere to in order give our best effort at delivering this “product.” Our north star always comes back to how we can provide opportunities to learn, grow, deliver increasing impact over time, and be recognized for delivering that value. As long as we are world-class at achieving those goals for our employees, we can guarantee that our people will be world-class at delivering for our customers.

This brings us to how we organize ourselves, which is one of the fundamental things that make us different. At most tech giants and large tech services companies, a career path is a pre-defined staircase: a year or two at one role before a pre-defined step up to the next role, and so on. Responsibilities, compensation, and the seniority of the person you report to all adjust accordingly. Most employees who are led through the same hierarchy without regard for their unique strengths (and weaknesses), interests, and experiences. Skipping a step or moving laterally is rare. Top performers may become managers, senior managers or principals faster, but at the end of the day, they are shepherded from one role to the next and guided linearly through the same sets of promotion criteria.

In contrast, we describe ourselves as a flat company, though it may not be clear from the outside what flatness means in our context or why its desirable. Below are some of the key points on what it means to eliminate hierarchy within the organization and why we value flatness as a core value.

No one is too senior to build

If we flatten our hierarchy and free ourselves of traditional pyramid structures, talking about “junior” and “senior” become highly relative terms. The reality is that we aim to hire the best and brightest regardless of prior expertise and we expect that everyone has a next phase of growth and development ahead of them. This is true whether an FDE is a new graduate or brings 10+ years of experience to the team.

This means that even our most senior FDEs are working side-by-side with their team to build and deliver for our customers. Partially, this is to ensure quality of execution. We know that it’s tempting to rely on the “base of the pyramid” for delivery (especially in a highly competitive market for senior talent), but this type of execution will never live up to the highest standards of delivery at the cutting edge of complexity. Therefore, we need our teams to reflect a flattened pyramid, with a high level of skills, strengths and experience sufficiently represented in small, agile teams.

This principle is also key to how we approach talent development. We know an FDE is smart, hard-working, technical, and highly motivated to deliver by the time they are hired. But raw talent is only part of what makes the FDE career path work. Ensuring our most senior FDEs are embedded within and across teams accelerates the pattern-matching abilities that newer FDEs need to develop and ensures each team can efficiently address even the most complex aspects of the work. Furthermore, this is a type of leadership that only works by osmosis. No amount of formal management, architecture diagrams, Jira sprint planning, or PowerPoint engineering can replace the day-to-day teaching that occurs when immersed in a problem alongside a full team.

No one is too junior to lead

The flipside of the above principle is that flatness only works if we have confidence in the skills and strengths that each FDE brings from day one. No one has all the answers regardless of tenure, as real-world problems almost never map neatly to tasks that lie within the FDE’s existing base of experience. Instead, we expect each FDE to do whatever they can to drive outcomes -- not whatever they are comfortable doing or assigned to do within the context of the project, but whatever their deployment requires that day. There is of course trial and occasionally error and a great deal of learning involved, but this is the point: the flat team structure allows (or forces) each FDE to constantly learn, grow and become more autonomous and confident in their overall set of capabilities.

This is a form of exposure therapy: FDEs must constantly push past pre-existing comfort zones to deliver what is needed, and in the process push the frontier outward of how they can be most effective and what they will find most rewarding. Keeping growth to a linear, tenure-based path would not only slow each individual’s path but would also constrain our customers to a much less ambitious vision of what each FDE can do.

More experience leads to more ownership and responsibility

This model leads to a highly virtuous cycle: the FDE ships as much and as well as they can, constantly pushing the limits of their knowledge and technical abilities, therefore learning and absorbing much more than if they were in a more structured and tenure-based role. Then, they can take on even more complex challenges at a greater scale for the next phase of the project or the next customer engagement. This speed of development leads to compounding growth that is a defining feature of Forward Deployed Engineering.

Compounding growth itself is an excellent goal but even this is wasted if not applied immediately and repeatedly to ensure that every FDE is using their growth to deliver more impact and value to their customers. This growth is inherently not linear or predictable. If we wait for someone to be at the company for two years or receive a formal promotion before empowering them to do more, we would be shortchanging our customers and our teams. We’d also be holding back exactly the highest-potential engineers who we worked so hard to find and hire in the first place, who happen to be the most likely to get frustrated by a lack of opportunity to continue pushing their areas of responsibility outwards.

Greater contributions lead to greater recognition

A flat hierarchy enables us to put each FDE in a position where they can have maximum impact. The result is that we effectively flip promotion cycles on their head: typically, an employee has to wait for a promotion to get new roles and responsibilities that are (at least theoretically) correlated with greater impact. Instead, our job is to give each FDE an opportunity to have more impact, and then use promotions, raises and bonuses to reflect that greater value they’ve proven they can deliver for our customers.

This leads to us retaining some trappings of hierarchy, namely progressive career paths with titles, transparent internal guidelines and compensation bands. However, our goal is for these sorts of internal mechanisms to serve flatness rather than rid ourselves of it. Seniority and other ways to recognize value creation should always be based not on pre-defined paths and managerial or political prowess but on demonstrated ability to create impact in the fullest variety of ways.

In a flat hierarchy, there is no promotion that’s too fast. Everyone’s shared incentive is to concentrate learning and development into the fastest possible cycles so that everyone achieves as much of their potential as possible, as soon as possible.

We are all peers

A final point is that flatness is not just about empowering everyone to deliver, but also about removing traditional organizational barriers that might slow us down.

Ideas for how we can be a more effective organization can come from anyone and everyone in the company. Everyone has an open door to sync and brainstorm or stress test ideas, from the newest joiner through to the co-founding team. Access is never gated through managers. This in turn means even the CEO can be questioned, and any decision or assumption can be overridden if there is new or contrary evidence for what’s best for our people and our customers. “Going rogue” (at least in small ways) becomes a norm that allows for creative expression and constant evolution of the business.

Flat and happy

We believe these principles are applicable to any organization trying to break free from more traditional structures to deploy engineers faster and more effectively, particularly when they are confronting critical demands to use data and AI to transform the world around them. To summarize, these principles are:

  • We are all builders (even our most “senior” FDEs)
  • We are all empowered to lead and deliver (even our most “junior” FDEs)
  • Everyone is enabled and expected to achieve continuous, non-linear growth
  • When accelerated learning leads to greater impact, it will be recognized accordingly regardless of tenure or title
  • The company is there to support you, not manage you

Flatness is a key feature of Forward Deployed Engineering, whether for 5, 50, or 500 FDEs (and beyond). A company of FDEs can only remain differentiated at scale if it can scale operations as a collective of autonomous units, made up of teams and individuals who are empowered to use their unique experiences and strengths to solve the challenges in front of them.

This is a window into our worldview at Fourth Age, where we leverage a world-class team of over 60 FDEs with a combined 225 years of experience at the time of writing. We are growing and the moment in front of us has never been more exciting. Reach out if you are interested in a supercharged career path in Forward Deployed Engineering or if you are looking for proven Forward Deployed Engineering that can transform the AI-driven enterprise.
