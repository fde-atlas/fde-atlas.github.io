Machine learning engineers are often confused with "AI engineers" and "data scientists," but the actual scope of their work is considerably broad.

Before applying to projects, it is important to check whether your skills align with the definition of the role.

A machine learning engineer is a specialist who implements machine learning algorithms to solve business problems.

The major difference from other roles is that the job doesn't end with "building a model"—it also covers integration into production environments and continuous improvement.

The main duties can be organized into the following five areas.

Area of WorkMain Content
Algorithm development and implementationSelecting and coding methods suited to the problem (linear regression, decision trees, deep learning, etc.)
Data collection and preprocessingCleansing raw data, feature engineering, visualization
Development environment and infrastructure buildingBuilding data pipelines, implementing cloud integration APIs, setting up MLOps
Model operation, maintenance, and evaluationAccuracy monitoring, retraining, hyperparameter tuning
Research on the latest technologiesReading papers, evaluating new libraries, attending external study groups

Some roles involve all five of these areas, while at large companies there are cases where engineers are dedicated solely to model development. When reading job postings, always confirm the scope of work.

Applying to projects without understanding the differences between the three roles makes mismatches likely. Let's organize them, including rate levels, in the table below.

The scope of work for machine learning engineers varies greatly depending on company size and team structure.

Large companies often have environments where engineers can focus on model design and implementation, with separate teams handling data infrastructure and frontend implementation. On the other hand, at startups and small AI teams, it is not uncommon for engineers to handle everything from infrastructure building to API integration, and sometimes even the frontend.

When choosing projects as a freelancer, confirming the "actual work content" rather than just the job title in the posting is the fastest way to prevent mismatches. Startup projects in particular tend to have a wide scope of responsibility, which is good for skill development, but there is also a risk that the breadth of your coverage may not be accurately evaluated when negotiating rates.

The image that "machine learning engineers earn high rates" is supported by the data. However, to reflect those rates in your take-home pay, you need to accurately understand the market reality and your own position.

According to aggregated data from projects listed on Freelance Start, the average monthly rate for machine learning engineers is 823,000 yen, with the highest rate at 1.82 million yen.

What is particularly striking is how rates have grown. In May 2025, the average was 834,000 yen, but by October of the same year it had reached 908,000 yen—an increase of over 70,000 yen in just six months.

Source: Freelance Start, "Machine Learning Engineer Freelance Jobs and Projects"

Three factors overlap behind this rise: a surge in projects driven by the generative AI boom, a chronic shortage of immediately deployable talent, and an increase in the use of external talent prioritizing speed as companies accelerate DX investment. The client-side need to "hand it to someone who can start moving right away, even at a high cost" continues to push rates upward.

There are four major project categories currently growing especially fast in the freelance market.

Service development using ChatGPT and Gemini APIs is seeing a rapid increase in orders, both for adding AI features to existing products and for developing business automation tools using LLMs. This is an area that is easy to enter if you have experience with Python and LLM APIs.

Building and improving MLOps pipelines refers to demand for continuous operations that go beyond just "creating" models, including CI/CD, monitoring, and retraining. Experience with tools like MLflow, Kubeflow, and GitHub Actions is directly valued.

Designing cloud-native ML platforms involves projects that build infrastructure using managed services such as AWS SageMaker, GCP Vertex AI, and Azure ML, and demand is high for engineers who can handle both infrastructure and ML.

RAG and agent development is a category that surged after 2025, requiring skills in designing and implementing retrieval-augmented generation and autonomous AI agents using tools like LangChain, LlamaIndex, and Dify.

Engineers who "have traditional machine learning skills while also being able to work with generative AI" are currently in the strongest position to choose from among projects.

A notable trend on the hiring and procurement side is the "shift from implementation to problem-solving."

Routine coding tasks are increasingly being automated by AI, and demand for engineers at the level of "being able to implement what they are told to build" is beginning to decline relatively. On the other hand, demand is rising sharply for engineers who "can accurately understand the business challenges clients face and proactively propose what to solve and how with AI."

This trend is clearly visible even in the projects we have supported. Clients are clearly offering higher evaluations and rates to engineers who can articulate "what will change in the business by adopting that model" rather than "which model to use."

This multiplication of "technology × business perspective" is the core of the BizDev skills explained in the next chapter.

Technical skill is a prerequisite. However, what separates engineers earning over ¥800,000 per month from the rest is not just technical ability. How you "use" and "present" your skills, and how you "choose" projects, ultimately determine your rate.

There are many technical skills to acquire, but prioritizing them is key.

Python is the de facto standard language in machine learning, with major libraries such as TensorFlow, PyTorch, and scikit-learn all running on Python. Bringing your Python skills up to a professional level is the first step.

You will need to choose libraries based on the use case. PyTorch and TensorFlow are often chosen for image recognition and natural language processing, scikit-learn, Pandas, and NumPy for data analysis and preprocessing, and Spark MLlib for large-scale distributed processing.

Being able to work professionally with at least one of AWS SageMaker, GCP Vertex AI, or Azure ML greatly expands your project options. Cloud vendor certifications directly help build trust when winning projects.

Building CI/CD pipelines for models using MLflow, Kubeflow, and GitHub Actions is a clear differentiator from being just a model developer.

In addition, a foundation in SQL, linear algebra, and probability/statistics is assumed as a given.

📌Pro Tip What gets you valued on high-rate projects is proving "ROI (return on investment)," not the "latest algorithms" Many engineers chase the latest deep learning models, but what management really wants is numbers: "How much will revenue increase and costs decrease by implementing this AI?" Even with a simple model, engineers who can articulate business impact in numbers are winning offers of over ¥1,500,000 per month. We are now in an era where connecting technology to business is valued more than the choice of technology itself.

It is true that the combination of "technical skill × a BizDev (business development) perspective" creates high market value. However, that alone will not increase your take-home pay. "Which supply chain you take projects through" is another axis that always comes into play.

Here is the reality we have seen firsthand on actual projects. Even on a project with a monthly rate of ¥1,000,000, if it goes through third- or fourth-tier subcontracting chains, the engineer may only receive around ¥600,000-700,000. Each intermediary company takes a margin, creating a large gap between the rate the client pays and what the engineer receives.

We can say this because we develop and operate our own products and have seen both the cost and value of AI implementation from both sides. The value of technology is not fairly evaluated unless you choose a supply chain with high transparency.

To maximize how much the value of "technical skill × BizDev perspective" is reflected in your take-home pay, choosing direct end-client projects with high transparency is a key point. When choosing an agent, we recommend checking two things in advance: "the ratio of direct end-client projects" and "whether margin rates are disclosed."

You can win projects as a machine learning engineer without certifications. However, early on when you have few accomplishments, they serve as "objective proof of skill," so obtaining them directly helps build trust when winning projects.

The JDLA G Test (Japan Deep Learning Association) is a certification covering general AI fundamentals. It requires no prerequisites and is held several times a year; having it during the period right after going freelance, when you are building your track record, adds persuasiveness to your portfolio.

This is the most difficult private certification in Japan related to deep learning, and completion of a JDLA-accredited course is required to take it. Passing it serves as proof of advanced implementation skills.

Google Cloud Professional ML Engineer and AWS Certified Machine Learning - Specialty are representative examples. Especially for projects involving MLOps or deploying models in production environments, whether you hold a cloud vendor certification can be a deciding factor in the selection process.

It signals that you understand the theoretical background of machine learning, and is useful if you also want to consider data scientist positions.

As for the order of obtaining certifications, the path "G Test → Python certification → Statistics Test Level 2 → cloud certification" is a realistic roadmap in terms of balancing cost-effectiveness with skill acquisition.

Continuing to win high-rate projects is harder than winning one. How you build trust, how you choose projects, and whether you have systems for self-management determine whether you can keep earning over the long term.

In the early stages of going freelance, how you present your track record determines whether you win projects.

Kaggle is one of the few platforms where you can objectively demonstrate your technical skill as a machine learning engineer. Even without top placements, simply publishing your competition participation records and implementation code on GitHub proves that you are "someone who actually gets their hands dirty."

GitHub is the simplest way to showcase your technical skill. Code quality, thorough comments, and readable READMEs convey your attitude as an engineer. Within the bounds of confidentiality obligations, publishing skills gained at work as personal projects is also effective.

Organize your portfolio so you can explain in one sentence "what problem you tackled, with what approach, and what results you achieved." Especially in selections for high-rate projects, "articulating business impact" is valued more than listing technical stacks.

Even at a low rate at first, delivering solid results and building trust with clients is the shortest path to your next high-rate project. Ongoing engagements and referrals grow from there.

There are multiple channels for finding projects. Understanding the characteristics of each and using them appropriately leads to a stable utilization rate.

Agents are the most standard channel in terms of both project volume and speed. However, as mentioned in 3-2, your take-home pay varies depending on the depth of the sales chain. When choosing an agent, use three criteria as your guide: the ratio of direct end-client projects, the transparency of margin rates, and whether the agent has engineering experience.

Our BizDev Tech service, a matching and sourcing support service for highly skilled IT professionals, focuses on direct end-client projects with high payout rates. Staff well-versed in technical fields including AI and ML are ready to propose the most suitable projects based on your skill set and desired conditions.

Communities form the foundation for long-term project acquisition. Many projects come through referrals or direct offers from networks built at study sessions and technical events. Sharing information via technical blogs and social media is also effective both for raising awareness and building trust.

Direct sales are difficult, but since there is no middleman margin, they offer the highest reflection rate in your take-home pay. Polishing your portfolio and carefully maintaining relationships with past clients are the shortcuts to direct projects.

For freelancers, surprisingly many cases of getting stuck involve factors other than skills.

In terms of self-management, schedule management is especially important when handling multiple projects in parallel. To maintain quality while preventing overwork, you need the habit of estimating the working hours for each project in advance and controlling your own workload.

Contract literacy is knowledge you should definitely acquire before going freelance. At minimum, there are four points to check: payment terms (monthly or two months later), the scope of confidentiality (whether you can feature the work in your portfolio), ownership of copyrights and deliverables, and settlement rules for mid-term termination. Starting work under an ambiguous contract becomes a breeding ground for trouble.

Continuous learning is especially essential in the world of machine learning. Accumulating small habits, such as reading one paper a week or trying one new library a month, helps you keep up with technology trends. Considering the pace of evolution in generative AI and LLMs, also make a habit of taking stock of your skill set every six months.

A. Going freelance with zero experience is not realistic. Machine learning projects require a high level of expertise and assume you can contribute immediately. We recommend accumulating at least 1-2 years of practical experience as a company employee, gaining implementation experience with Python and machine learning libraries, and gaining experience operating models in some product before going freelance.

A. The right time to switch is when all three of the following conditions are met.

1) Three or more years of practical experience with multiple projects; 2) experience with MLOps and production operation in cloud environments; 3) achievements you can demonstrate, such as Kaggle results or a portfolio.

In addition, securing six months of living expenses is a condition for a safe transition.

A. Use gaps between projects as a "period of aggressive investment." By simultaneously pursuing 1) catching up on new technical areas (generative AI, LLMs, etc.), 2) actively participating in Kaggle competitions to strengthen your portfolio, 3) raising awareness through technical blog writing and OSS contributions, and 4) building relationships with agents, you can increase your probability of landing the next project.

A. Machine learning projects are increasingly remote-work friendly, so it is entirely possible to acquire projects even if you live outside major urban areas. However, some large enterprise projects may require "visiting the office once or twice a month" or "on-site participation during important phases." If you live in a regional area, it is important to confirm in advance whether full remote work is possible and the frequency of office visits, and to actively highlight your remote communication skills.

A. Machine learning engineers should pay particular attention to the following three points.

1) GPU and cloud service fees for training can be recorded as business expenses; 2) technical books, online courses, and conference participation fees are also eligible as business expenses; 3) if you have contracts with multiple clients, be thorough about organizing invoices and contracts.

If your annual income exceeds 8 million yen, consider consulting a tax accountant as well.
