Summary

What kind of role is an Anthropic FDE?

An Anthropic FDE is an engineering role that goes into customers' environments and personally designs and implements production systems using Claude.

An Anthropic FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is responsible for integrating Claude into customer companies' business operations and getting it running in production. From around 2025, AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic began recruiting FDEs, and the job title originally coined by Palantir has come into use across the generative AI industry. Based on Anthropic's official job posting (listed on Greenhouse), this article organizes the job responsibilities, requirements, work style, differences from FDEs at other companies, and application preparation.

As the title "Forward Deployed" (positioned at the front lines) suggests, the main workplace is not the company's own office but the customer's side. Selling Claude is the role of sales and solutions staff. Anthropic FDEs handle what comes after, creating a state where Claude continues to be used within the customer's business workflows.

What do they do at customer sites?

They dig into challenges together with customers, then design, implement, and test workflows that incorporate Claude to build production applications.

The workflow that can be inferred from the job posting requirements consists roughly of the following four stages.

  • Discovery: Finding the real business challenges behind customer requests through dialogue
  • Design: Deciding the workflows, prompts, and agent architecture that incorporate Claude
  • Implementation and testing: Building in Python and making it work on the customer's systems
  • Production adoption: Eliminating exceptions and accuracy issues that arise in production so the system keeps being used

Looking at real-world implementation support, LLM-based systems often work in demos but stall when it comes to dealing with actual business data, permission management, and exception handling. Anthropic FDEs are presumably positioned to get past this final hurdle.

How does it differ from a Solutions Engineer or SWE?

The difference is the scope of responsibility. An Anthropic FDE doesn't stop at technical proposals; they deliver results with code they wrote themselves.

If an SWE's job is "delivering one feature to many customers," an Anthropic FDE's job is "fully implementing many features for the customer in front of them." The role is often mistaken for a meeting-heavy position, but the core of the work is writing code.

What are the Anthropic FDE job requirements?

The core requirements are four: Python implementation skills, LLM production operations experience, high agency, and discovery skills with customers.

Anthropic's official job posting lists the following four requirements.

What level are the technical requirements?

Python implementation skills are the foundation, with experience running LLMs in production required on top of that. Both assume a professional level.

The four requirements are best understood not as parallel items but as a stack. At the bottom is "Python implementation skills," on top of that "LLM production operations," and on top of that "high agency and customer discovery." The presence of customer-facing items does not lighten the implementation requirements. An Anthropic FDE must meet the same technical bar as an SWE, plus possess customer-facing skills.

What matters in LLM production operations is not whether you can call Claude. The question is whether you have experience operating systems end to end, including evaluation design, continuous prompt improvement, and handling agent failures.

What do the non-technical requirements assess?

They assess the ability to move forward on your own in ambiguous situations, and the dialogue skills to redefine problems together with customers.

While "high agency" sounds abstract, it manifests as concrete actions in the day-to-day work of an FDE. For example, building a small prototype before requirements are finalized to gauge customer reactions, or, when there's no internal precedent, verifying things yourself and gathering the materials needed to make a judgment. In application documents and interviews, the deciding factor will likely be whether you can concretely describe experiences where you took initiative beyond the scope of what you were instructed to do.

What is the working style and travel ratio for Anthropic FDEs?

Anthropic's job postings state 25-50% customer site visits, which is considerably higher travel burden than typical development roles.

The official job posting lists the customer site visit ratio as 25-50%. Simply put, this means spending a quarter to half of your working hours at customer sites. The role assumes a working style that combines in-person visits with remote development.

How does the weekly time allocation look?

While it varies by project, the basic pattern is alternating between customer conversations on-site and implementation and validation internally.

How does the travel burden affect your career?

While the travel burden is significant, it offers a valuable opportunity to learn industry domain knowledge and customer decision-making processes up close.

Working at customer sites exposes you to information not written in specifications, such as handling business exceptions, internal politics, and approval workflows. Since the lifestyle burden is not trivial, you should sort out family circumstances and living arrangements before applying. That said, being able to rapidly accumulate domain knowledge and customer-facing experience in a short period is a value unique to the Anthropic FDE role.

How does the Anthropic FDE differ from Palantir and OpenAI FDEs?

Implementing solutions at customer sites is common to Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic. What differs is the core technology involved and the working conditions.

Palantir Technologies popularized the FDE job title. Palantir established the approach of embedding people within customer organizations through two roles: FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer) and Deployment Strategist. OpenAI and Anthropic inherited this concept and applied it to foundation model deployment.

How do the technologies and products differ?

Palantir deploys data platforms at customer sites, while OpenAI and Anthropic deploy foundation models and agents.

If you're aiming to become an Anthropic FDE, mastering design approaches based on these primary sources will make it easier to communicate effectively both in interviews and in the field.

How do working conditions and language requirements differ?

Anthropic requires 25-50% customer site visits, while OpenAI Tokyo requires 3 days per week in the office, domestic travel, and Japanese-English bilingual skills.

OpenAI's Tokyo FDE job posting lists 3 days per week in the office, domestic travel, and Japanese-English bilingual ability as requirements (OpenAI official site). For Anthropic, the job posting indicates 25-50% customer site visits as a guideline for the working style.

Work locations and language requirements vary by position. Rather than assuming "this is always how it works for Anthropic FDEs," it's safest to check the job posting for the specific position you're applying to. That said, as a global company, you should expect internal documentation and design discussions to be conducted in English.

What should you prepare to become an Anthropic FDE?

The fastest path is to build a track record of running Claude in production and shape your customer problem-solving experience into a form you can present as achievements.

FDEs are evaluated on their track record, and certifications or educational history are relatively less important. A realistic approach is to prepare one experience that demonstrates each of the four job requirements.

What technical skills should you build up?

Gain end-to-end experience with agent development using the Claude API and evaluation design, in a production or production-like environment.

  • Write production-quality code in Python: Implement with tests, error handling, and logging included
  • Build agents with Claude: As Anthropic's "Building effective agents" recommends, start with simple configurations and add complexity only when needed
  • Build an evaluation system: Prepare test sets and measure accuracy every time you change prompts
  • Connect to external tools with MCP: Implement integrations with internal data and existing systems
  • Improve while operating: Have real users use it, and iterate on improvements based on failure cases

If you have no opportunity to put an LLM into production at your current workplace, it's a good idea to choose a topic with real users, even if small, such as an internal business improvement tool. This gives you experience you can speak to down to "whose work changed, and how."

What should you demonstrate in your application documents and interviews?

In addition to technical depth, concretely show your experience of redefining ambiguous problems yourself and carrying them through to results.

If you want to build experience while remaining an SWE, three things are effective: sitting in on requirements-definition meetings, attending customer interviews, and checking for yourself after release whether the product is actually being used in the field. Even if your job title isn't FDE, SaaS companies that implement generative AI into customer operations and AI implementation consulting firms have positions that consistently handle everything from requirements definition to adoption. Building a track record in such roles and then applying to Anthropic FDE is a realistic path for many engineers.
