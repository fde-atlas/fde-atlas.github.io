On-site Delivery: Embedded Engineer

Original title: 驻场交付：Embedded Engineer

FDE中国 · 2026-08-14 · 2 min read

Summary

This piece argues that "embedded" or on-site delivery describes only where an engineer works, not how they deliver or what they are accountable for, and therefore does not equal being a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). China's toB industry has had a two-decade on-site tradition of implementation consultants, outsourced operations, and custom developers. The real FDE differences are the toolset (code, evals, automation rather than product configuration) and closed-loop accountability for business outcomes. Long-standing on-site problems—scope creep, travel and staffing costs, single-person knowledge silos—persist under the FDE model and require structural fixes. The author advises removing "on-site" from job descriptions and judging responsibility structure before work location.

Key points

  • On-site describes location and collaboration density, not capability or accountability.
  • China's toB sector has two decades of on-site delivery tradition; it is not new.
  • FDE differs by using code and evals and owning end-to-end business outcomes.
  • Old on-site problems like scope creep and single-person risk persist and need structural solutions.
  • When writing JDs or interviewing, assess responsibility structure before work location.

Full text

This card sets the record straight about "on-site work": it describes location, not capability, and it certainly doesn't equal FDE. Core breakdown: on-site work only covers two variables—physical location and collaboration intensity—while delivery methods and accountability structures are two other independent variables. The most common misuse is the judgment "I'm on-site every day, so I'm already an FDE"—in China, large numbers of implementation consultants, on-site ops staff, and outsourced developers all work on-site, but their solution space is product configuration and their accountability is billable-hours delivery, which is not the same as the closed-loop accountability of a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). Incremental insight: most discussions focus on Silicon Valley AI companies, but this card puts on-site work back into China's toB tradition of more than twenty years: on-site work isn't new—what's new is that the on-site person now holds code and evals authority. Old on-site problems like travel costs, single-person dependencies, and boundary management also exist in the FDE model and are often underestimated: these problems must be solved structurally; relying on individual self-discipline won't cut it. Action advice: when writing a JD or self-introduction, remove "on-site" from capability descriptions and replace it with what you deliver and what you're accountable for. In interviews, this is also a good way to probe the other party's delivery maturity. Remember this order of judgment: look at the accountability structure first, then the work location—get the order backwards and you'll misjudge. — FDEChina Editorial Team · Practitioners

Definition

On-site delivery refers to a collaboration model where engineers work long-term at the client's premises, using the client's environment as their primary workplace to complete system construction and launch; Embedded Engineer is the English term emphasizing this embedded state.

Details

On-site work only describes "where the person is," not "how they deliver or what they're accountable for." China's toB industry has a twenty-plus-year tradition of on-site work: implementation consultants, ops outsourcing, and custom development staff all work on-site long-term. The similarity between a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) and on-site roles exists only at the physical level—the real differences are the engineering approach (solving problems with code, evals, and automation rather than in-product configuration) and the accountability structure (owning the closed loop of business outcomes). Writing "on-site" into a JD and assuming you've hired an FDE is the most common misuse in today's recruiting market.

The management difficulties of on-site delivery itself don't disappear with the FDE label: scope creep from the lack of a gatekeeper for ad-hoc client requests, travel and labor cost accounting, and knowledge silos and attrition risk from single-person dependencies. In practice, on-site arrangements should be paired with a shadowing period, knowledge transfer, and clear escalation paths—otherwise, the deeper the embedding, the harder the handover.
