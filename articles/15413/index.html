Google Forward Deployed Engineer: The Role and How to Prepare for the Interview

Prepare.sh · 2026-11-04 · 5 min read

Summary

This article from Prepare.sh surveys Google's forward deployed engineer (FDE) role, where engineers embed at customer sites to build agentic AI solutions on Google Cloud. It compiles documented details from job postings—levels FDE III through Partner FDE V, US base salaries from $174,000 to $364,000, and requirements covering RAG pipelines, multi-agent frameworks, and customer discovery sessions. Since Google publishes no FDE-specific interview process, the author clearly separates documented facts from a single unverified candidate account describing three rounds (AI/ML, DSA, Googleyness), and offers practice questions plus advice to demonstrate hands-on ADK and evaluation-pipeline experience.

Key points

  • FDEs embed at customers and ship bespoke AI agents on Google Cloud.
  • Three levels: FDE III, FDE IV, Partner FDE V, with base pay $174k–$364k.
  • Requirements stress RAG, vector databases, multi-agent frameworks, and discovery sessions.
  • No official FDE interview guide exists; the reported three-round process is unverified.
  • Stand out by building with ADK, an MCP server, and measured eval and cost metrics.

A Google forward deployed engineer (FDE) works inside customers and builds AI agents on Google Cloud. The FDE III posting calls the role "an embedded builder who bridges the gap between frontier AI products and production-grade reality within customers", with work to "code, debug, and jointly ship bespoke agentic solutions directly within the customer's environment."

Most of the roles we found sit in Google Cloud, with variants elsewhere. Google doesn't publish an FDE-specific interview process that we could read, so what follows is clear about which parts are documented.

Partner FDE differs in who you work with: it will "co-build with a strategic AI partner's forward deployed engineering team" and "Help partners to build their own agentic delivery capabilities". That's tied to a wider push. Google said in April 2026 that it's sending FDE teams to partner with firms including Accenture, Deloitte and PwC, and in October it announced an FDE academy for partners.

Other titles on the careers site: Forward Deployed Engineer in Google Public Sector, Geo Auto and Kaggle at DeepMind (a fixed-term contract), Senior FDE in Google Labs, and a Staff role in Developer AI in Australia. The live postings don't state a travel requirement, so ask.

What the postings ask for

The FDE III minimum requirements, besides the coding years:

  • "Experience architecting AI systems on cloud platforms (e.g., GCP)."
  • "Experience taking production-grade AI-driven solutions from conception to launch for customers."
  • "Experience leading technical discovery sessions with customers."
  • "Experience building pipelines for structured and unstructured data using both vector databases and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)-like architectures"

Preferred items include a graduate degree in a related field, "multi-agent systems using frameworks (e.g., LangGraph, CrewAI, ADK) and patterns (e.g., ReAct, self-reflection, hierarchical delegation)", and "LLM-native metrics (e.g., tokens/sec, cost-per-request)".

The day-to-day responsibilities mention "production-grade agentic workflows (e.g., multi-agent systems, model context protocol (MCP) servers)", "high-performance evaluation pipelines and observability frameworks", and turning field patterns into "reusable modules or formal product feature requests".

Products to know

Postings still say "Vertex AI", while Google's docs describe its successor. Know both names:

  • Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform: "a unified platform to build, deploy, govern, and optimize enterprise-grade AI agents", described as "an evolution of Vertex AI".
  • ADK: "the open-source agent development framework that lets you build, debug, and deploy reliable AI agents at enterprise scale."
  • MCP: "an open-source standard for connecting AI applications to external systems."
  • A2A: "an open standard for seamless communication and collaboration between AI agents." This appears in the Staff Developer AI posting, not the three above.

What's known about the interview

From the postings: "In most instances, this position requires in-person interviews as part of the hiring process." Plan for travel.

From Google, in general: Its structured interviewing guide, updated March 2026, says "we use the same interview questions, grade candidate responses on an identical scale". It recommends looking at "Role-related knowledge (RRK)", "Problem solving" and "Leadership", with "emergent leadership" for individual contributors, and it uses behavioral and hypothetical questions. That's a general guide and not an FDE rubric. Our Google interview guide covers the wider process.

From one candidate: A Medium post from April 2026 describes "3 interview rounds, plus an initial technical recruiter screening": "Round 1: AI/ML Interview", "Round 2: DSA Interview" and "Round 3: Googleyness Interview". Treat this cautiously. It's a single account with no level, location or outcome stated, and the post links to paid prep material. The same author's later post says its account "does not represent an official or universal Google interview process."

Ask your recruiter for the rounds at your level.

Practice questions

We wrote these from the postings, so they're practice and not reported questions.

"Lead a technical discovery session: a retailer wants an AI agent for customer service."

The posting names this skill directly. Start with the business: volume, top contact reasons, cost per contact and what a bad answer costs. Then the systems: where order data lives, how an agent gets access, who approves that. Then scope: "I'd start with order status, since it's high volume and read-only. Refunds come later, once we've shown it's safe, with a limit and human approval."

A strong answer ends with success metrics agreed before any build, such as containment rate and customer satisfaction against the existing channel.

Follow-ups: What if the customer's data is on another cloud? What if the legal team blocks access for three weeks? What's your plan B?

"Design a multi-agent system for this. When wouldn't you use one?"

The posting prefers experience with patterns such as "hierarchical delegation". Show the pattern, then the judgment. A coordinating agent could delegate to order, returns and policy agents, each with narrow tools. But multiple agents add latency, cost and more places to fail, so if one agent with a few tools meets the need, say so and start there. Move to several when tool sets or permissions clearly separate.

"Build a RAG pipeline for documents plus structured data."

From the minimum requirements. Cover chunking and parsing, including messy PDFs and tables. Cover embeddings and a vector store, and combine with keyword search for product codes and exact terms. Keep structured questions like "how many orders" going to SQL through a tool, not to embeddings. Enforce permissions at retrieval, so users can't get documents they couldn't otherwise open. Then evaluate retrieval and answers separately, because that tells you where a failure came from.

"How do you monitor this in production?"

The posting's "LLM-native metrics (e.g., tokens/sec, cost-per-request)" gives you a start. Add latency percentiles per step, tool error rates, retrieval hit rates, escalation rate, and a sampled quality score from your evals. Trace every request across its agent and tool calls, so one bad answer can be debugged. Set alerts on cost per request as well as errors, because a retry loop can look healthy on errors while spending heavily.

Coding

The candidate report above mentions a DSA round, and Google's posting asks for years of coding. Practice both. Our Google question hub has problems such as LRU Cache and Accounts Merge. See also our system design guide and AI engineer questions.

Behavioral

Use Google's general categories. Its guide gives this example: "Tell me about a time your behavior had a positive impact on your team." with follow-ups "What was your primary goal and why? How did your teammates respond? Moving forward, what's your plan?" Prepare stories on leading a customer through a hard decision, and on feeding what you learned back to a product team, since the posting asks for that.

How to stand out

Build something with ADK or a comparable framework, with an MCP server, an evaluation pipeline and cost numbers you measured. Be able to describe a discovery session you actually led. Those two pieces of evidence cover what the minimum requirements are really asking for.
