Summary

This article catalogs five Tokyo-related Palantir job postings (three Deployment Strategist, two Forward Deployed Software Engineer) verified on November 6, 2026, comparing required language skills, security clearances, experience, and work arrangements, and contrasting them with OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting. Three postings list Tokyo as the location; two list Washington, D.C. but state in the body that the role is based in Tokyo. Requirements vary notably: only the Government FDSE role specifies a minimum of one year of post-graduation experience, and the Commercial application form requires two 90-second YouTube video answers.

Key points

  • Three postings show Tokyo as location; two show Washington, D.C. but state Tokyo in the body.
  • Language requirements differ: business-level Japanese/English for Commercial, proficiency for Government roles, none for two postings.
  • Clearance requirements: two roles need active Top Secret plus SCI eligibility; one needs any security clearance.
  • Only the Government FDSE posting requires one year of relevant experience; the other four state no minimum.
  • The Commercial application form requires two 90-second YouTube videos and specifies no resume language.

For those considering Palantir's hiring in Japan, we have organized the requirements of five Tokyo-related job openings confirmed as of November 6, 2026. The positions are three Deployment Strategist (DS) roles and two Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE) roles. They fall into three listings with Tokyo in the location field and two listings that, while showing Washington, D.C. in the location field, explicitly state Tokyo-based work in the body text, with required conditions such as language and qualifications differing from listing to listing.

In this article, you can review the following in order: a comparison table of the requirements for the 5 job openings, official job postings and work locations, and conditions regarding language, clearance, and work experience.

The 5 Official Job Openings for Japan Hiring and Their Work Locations

On November 6, 2026, we searched "Japan" in the Open Positions section of Palantir's official careers site and found 5 job openings, and 3 openings using the Tokyo filter. You can view the official job postings via the links on each job title.

From each job page, you can proceed to the application form via the "Apply" button. We have only reviewed the contents of the application form for Japan Commercial, which we summarize in Points checked on the Commercial application form. The contents of the forms for the other 4 listings have not been verified.

The ones showing Tokyo in the location field of the official list are two Deployment Strategist listings and one Government-focused FDSE listing, three in total. Meanwhile, the remaining two listings show Washington, D.C. in the location field, but as roles supporting US government operations, their body text states at the beginning, "this role is located in Tokyo, Japan," clearly indicating that the work is based in Tokyo.

For the three listings with Tokyo in the location field, there is no additional mention of the work location in the body text. When determining the actual work location, you need to check three points: not only the location field display, but also the notes in the body text and the specified work style.

Regarding work style, the Japan Commercial DS and Japan Government FDSE are Hybrid, while the remaining three are On-site. The common text expresses a policy of valuing in-person collaboration, and while many teams are described as hybrid including one to two days of remote work per week, specific required office days are not stated. Remote work is treated as an exception, and listings designated as On-site are considered to require attendance at the office.

Note that "Go onsite" seen in the job descriptions refers to visiting client sites, which differs in meaning from "On-site" indicating the requirement to come into the company's own office.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a technical role responsible for everything from problem definition to establishing solutions in the field. We do not assert that it is the same role as the FDSE in the job titles.

Language, clearance, and work experience requirements differ by role

Japan Commercial's required fields ask for "fluent business Japanese and English" in reading, writing, and speaking. Japan Government's DS and FDSE require being "proficient" in both Japanese and English. Because the wording differs from posting to posting, it cannot be determined from the text alone whether these refer to the same level or correspond to a specific language certification standard. Note that the two Japan Forward Deployed postings contain no language requirements in their body text.

Clearance requirements are stated in three of the postings. The two D.C. postings require holding an active Top Secret clearance and being willing and eligible to obtain SCI. The FDSE (Japan Government) in the Tokyo section requires holding, or being able to obtain, a security clearance with no type specified. The two DS postings in the Tokyo section contain no clearance mention at all. The issuing country of the clearance, conditions for obtaining it, and applicant nationality requirements are not stated in the posting text.

The Japan Government FDSE posting requires at least one year of relevant work experience after graduating from university as a Requirement. The other four postings contain no stated minimum years of experience, but this does not mean experience is unnecessary. Also, none of the five postings explicitly states specific conditions regarding applicant nationality or work visas.

Deployment Strategists handle listening and connecting to business workflows; FDSEs handle design and implementation

In the job descriptions, DSs are responsible for hearing customer problems and connecting them to business workflows, while FDSEs lead system design and implementation. The three Deployment Strategist postings commonly list the following duties:

  • Go to customers' sites and listen to analysts' problems
  • Identify datasets and build data pipelines with Forward Deployed Engineers
  • Build workflows for customers
  • Train users so the product gets adopted
  • Report results to everyone from analysts to executives
  • Feed field insights back to product and design teams
  • Build demos
  • Scope new engagements in new industries and regions

FDSE is a technical role that understands customer problems and designs and implements end-to-end solutions. In the Japan Forward Deployed FDSE posting, system design and data integration using Postgres, Spark, Java, TypeScript, React, Python, AWS, and so on are given as examples for problems such as churn prevention and fraud detection. Japan Government's FDSE also handles design and implementation, but the technology stack described should be checked in each individual posting.

Team designations show the three Deployment Strategist postings as Echo and the two Forward Deployed Software Engineer postings as Delta.

Comparing the two companies, in addition to how language and clearance are handled, the treatment of experience years differs. Palantir's Government FDSE requires at least one year of relevant work after university graduation as a Requirement, while OpenAI Tokyo describes 5+ years as "a guideline for people expected to thrive," and the four postings in Tables ①–④ state no years. Also, the absence of a resume language specification for Palantir was confirmed only within the scope of the Commercial application form; the form specifications of the other four are unverified.

Commercial application form: required submissions and items to check

The Japan Commercial application form, first checked on October 27, 2026 and viewed again on November 6 of the same year (not submitted), requires attaching a resume, entering education history, and selecting languages used (this does not mean holding a degree itself is mandatory). It also asks whether you have legal work authorization in the country of employment and whether you require work visa sponsorship. No resume language specification is found, but this does not guarantee acceptance of Japanese-language resumes, and the existence of the questions does not guarantee visa support. The forms for the other four postings are unverified.

In the Japan Commercial form checked on November 6, 2026, video submission via an unlisted YouTube URL is required for two questions (each within 90 seconds, Shorts not allowed). The questions ask about two things: something important you created and why/how you made it, and Palantir-related content that made you think and why. The form also notes that videos reading from a script should be avoided and that videos should be deleted after the application process ends. Note that when this submission format began and the scoring criteria could not be determined from the form reviewed.
