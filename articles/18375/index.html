A Comparison of FDE Case Studies: Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Scale AI

Original title: Palantir・OpenAI・Anthropic・Scale AIのFDE事例比較

FDX株式会社 · 2026-05-13 · 15 min read

Summary

This article traces the FDE role from its origin at Palantir in 2003 to its adoption by Anthropic (2021), Scale AI (2022), and OpenAI's enterprise division (2023). It compares the four companies' org design, division of labor with adjacent roles (Deployment Strategist, Solutions Engineer), KPI design, and hiring bars, then distills five shared success factors and three pitfalls for Japanese companies adopting the model. Note the piece is published by FDX, a vendor of external FDE services, so it doubles as marketing.

Key points

  • Palantir pairs FDEs with Deployment Strategists so engineers can focus on implementation.
  • OpenAI splits pre-sales (Solutions Engineer) from production deployment support (FDE).
  • Anthropic FDEs emphasize safety: guardrails, audit logs, and multi-step agent design.
  • Common success factors: executive reporting line, product feedback loop, customer-outcome KPIs, internalization commitment, extreme hiring standards.
  • Japanese pitfalls: equating FDE with on-site presence, missing evaluation systems, procurement contract mismatches.

2. Case 1: Palantir Technologies | The Origin of the FDE

Overview

Palantir Technologies is a US company founded in 2003 that provides data integration platforms for government agencies and large enterprises. Because its main products "Foundry," "Gotham," and "Apollo" are deeply integrated into customers' business operations, Palantir has hired large numbers of FDEs as full-time employees since its early days.

Characteristics of Palantir FDEs

  • Deep business understanding: Attend customers' business meetings daily and understand the details of business processes
  • Coding ability: Build Foundry object definitions, data pipelines, and UI applications themselves
  • Scope of responsibility: End-to-end ownership from project strategy through implementation, operations, and ROI proof
  • Physical on-site presence: Often physically stationed at customers' offices, factories, and field sites (remote work is also advancing)

Division of Labor with Deployment Strategists

In addition to FDEs, Palantir has a role called "Deployment Strategist (DS)."

  • DS: Strategy planning, business redesign, stakeholder management, and internal lobbying at customers (writes little code)
  • FDE: Works alongside the DS and holds implementation responsibility. Handles code, data, and system integration

This division of labor is a rational role allocation to secure "time for FDEs to write code." When Japanese companies introduce FDEs, whether one person handles both roles or a DS + FDE pair is formed as at Palantir should be decided based on project scale.

KPI Design

Palantir uses customer outcomes directly as KPIs: reduced business workload by X%, found Y cases of fraud, increased throughput of a specific process by Z%. These numbers are incorporated directly into the evaluations of FDEs and DSs.

Comments from Palantir's CEO

CEO Alex Karp has stated in multiple public materials that "Palantir's competitive advantage is not technology, but the engineering organization itself that stays close to customers' operations." He has explicitly positioned the FDE organization as a differentiator against product-centric competitors (IBM, Oracle, etc.).

3. Case 2: OpenAI | The Accompaniment Model for Enterprise AI Adoption

Overview

OpenAI fully launched its Enterprise division in 2023. To bring APIs including GPT-4 to large enterprises, it placed two roles—Forward Deployed Engineer and Solutions Engineer—within its enterprise team.

Characteristics of OpenAI FDEs

  • Co-design of generative AI applications: Jointly build prompt design, agent configurations, and fine-tuning strategies tailored to customers' use cases
  • Production operation support: Implementation support from PoC to production, eval design, and operational monitoring
  • Cross-functional coordination: Liaising with product, research, and security teams

Division of Labor with Solutions Engineers

  • Solutions Engineer (SE): Technical pre-sales before and after sales negotiations, and initial PoC support
  • Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE): Accompanying the production deployment phase, embedding into business operations, and operational design

Simply put, an SE closes the deal, while an FDE embeds into the field after deployment—that's how the roles are divided.

KPI Design

The evaluation criteria are whether the customer's AI implementation made it to production operation, and how much it contributed to ARR (annual recurring revenue). Enterprise customers' renewal and expansion rates directly feed back into the FDE's evaluation.

Requirements gleaned from job descriptions

OpenAI's public job posting (Forward Deployed Engineer) requires the following.

  • 7+ years of engineering experience
  • Experience implementing LLMs/generative AI
  • Experience engaging directly with enterprise customers
  • Strong business acumen (ROI calculation, business understanding)
  • Adaptability to travel and on-site residency

Salary range is $300K–$500K + equity (equivalent to roughly ¥45–75 million + stock options in Japanese yen).

4. Case 3: Scale AI / Anthropic | The FDE model at AI labs

The Scale AI case

Scale AI is a company whose core business is data labeling and dataset construction for autonomous driving and AI models. Its FDEs embed within customer organizations, accompanying them through data pipeline construction, quality assurance, and model operation.

Characteristics of Scale AI FDEs

  • Deep data engineering: Full-stack design of customer data collection, preprocessing, labeling, and evaluation
  • KPI-based model performance: Model performance metrics such as precision, recall, and F1 score are built into the FDE's KPIs
  • Avoiding long-term lock-in: By helping customers build their own data pipelines, they promote self-sufficiency rather than dependence on Scale AI

The Anthropic case

Anthropic (the developer of Claude) has been expanding its Solutions / Forward Deployed Engineering teams for enterprise customers since 2024. They specialize in accompanying customers to embed the Claude API into enterprise business operations.

Characteristics of Anthropic FDEs

  • Emphasis on safety and alignment: Implementation design that minimizes operational errors, hallucinations, and misjudgments—not just AI that works
  • Deep agent design: Co-implementing multi-step agent designs with customers, rather than one-off prompts
  • Responsible AI deployment: Incorporating AI governance into implementations, including audit logs, guardrails, and human review integration

Common thread: FDEs at AI labs take on "product × customer operations × safety" as one integrated responsibility

Both Scale AI and Anthropic expect more from their FDEs than deployment support. They hold them accountable all the way through safely integrating AI into business operations.

5. Diagram: FDE organizational placement at major companies

Each major company places its FDEs in slightly different positions between "product teams ↔ customers."

  • Palantir: Embeds quite deeply on the customer side, with emphasis on fitting the Foundry product to customer operations
  • OpenAI: Positioned right in the middle between the product (API) and the customer, bridging generative AI implementation patterns
  • Scale AI / Anthropic: At the data and safety layers, building AI governance into implementations

When Japanese companies adopt the FDE model, being mindful of whether they are closer to the "Palantir type (deep operational embedding)," "OpenAI type (product fit)," or "Scale AI type (safety- and data-focused)" will determine FDE placement and authority design.

7. Five success factors common to major AI companies

1. Reporting lines directly under executive leadership

At all major companies, FDEs report directly to the CEO or a business unit head—not to IT departments or downstream implementation teams. This maximizes their decision-making authority within customer organizations.

2. Close collaboration with product teams

FDEs are on the front line of customer feedback. All major companies have mechanisms to feed FDE insights directly into product development. In other words, FDEs hold organizational authority to influence the product roadmap.

3. KPI-based customer outcomes

All major companies evaluate FDEs based on "customer outcomes" rather than "man-hours." Specific KPIs: customer business impact, production deployment rate, contract renewal rate, and ARR growth.

4. Explicit commitment to internal capability building

All major companies include "a state where the customer can operate independently" in the success criteria for FDE projects. Palantir calls this "Transfer Skills," and OpenAI calls it "Self-Service Excellence."

5. Extremely high hiring standards

At all major companies, the hiring bar for FDEs is stricter than for other roles. Engineering depth, business understanding, and communication skills—all must be at a top level. Palantir is said to require three qualities: exceptionally high intelligence, business understanding, and the ability to hold conversations with executives.

8. Three pitfalls for Japanese companies trying to imitate this

Pitfall 1: The misconception that "physical on-site presence = FDE"

When Japanese companies bring in external FDEs, many mistakenly believe that "as long as someone is physically on-site, that's an FDE." This is a typical failure pattern. The essence of an FDE lies in the scope of responsibility (from business redesign to operational handover), and physical on-site presence is merely one means of achieving it. Even a combination of remote work plus one or two on-site visits per month can function as an FDE arrangement if the scope of responsibility is clear.

Pitfall 2: Lack of an evaluation system

All major companies evaluate FDEs based on "customer outcomes," but Japanese HR systems center on man-hour-based and skill-based evaluations. Even if a company introduces FDEs, if the evaluation system is not aligned, FDE-style activities (business redesign, executive dialogue, internal capability building) will not be rewarded within the organization. As a result, the FDE ends up behaving just like an SES engineer.

Countermeasure: Formally institutionalize FDE-style positions within the company and establish a business impact evaluation system.

Pitfall 3: Mismatch of contract formats with the procurement department

The procurement departments of large Japanese companies traditionally only have contract formats for SES, SI, and consulting. FDE-style contracts (a combination of quasi-delegation plus performance-based compensation) require new templates to be prepared, and if the procurement department does not move, the deal stalls at the contracting stage.

Countermeasure: Directives from management to prepare contract formats, and leveraging external partners (lawyers and consultants) to support drafting contract formats. FDX Inc. also supports this contract preparation.

9. Best practices for Japanese companies adopting the FDE model

Taking into account the success factors of major companies and the organizational constraints in Japan, the following is a realistic adoption sequence.

  • Secure clear sponsorship from management (at or above the division head level)
  • Narrow down the target business domain (start with one domain, one project)
  • Prepare FDE-style contract formats (coordinate with the procurement department)
  • Select external FDEs (track record, industry knowledge, flexibility in contract formats)
  • Select internal capability-building candidates (at least one, ideally two)
  • Set an 18-month plan and KPIs for each phase
  • Pilot introduction of a business impact evaluation system

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1. How does a Palantir-style FDE differ from Japanese client-site staffing?

A. On the surface, they look similar in that both are "at the customer's site," but they are fundamentally different. A Palantir-style FDE holds end-to-end responsibility from business redesign through implementation to operational handover, and is evaluated on customer business KPIs. Japanese client-site SES engineers are responsible only for providing man-hours, and business redesign or executive dialogue is generally not included. Nikkei xTECH also notes that "the American-style FDE is similar to but distinct from Japanese client-site staffing."

Q2. How does OpenAI's Solutions Engineer differ from its Forward Deployed Engineer?

A. A Solutions Engineer (SE) handles technical pre-sales before and after sales meetings, mainly supporting up to PoCs. A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) accompanies the production deployment phase, focusing on business integration and operational design at the customer's site. Simply put, the division of roles is "the SE closes the deal, and the FDE stays engaged after deployment." The two roles are complementary, not in conflict.

Q3. Should Japanese companies imitate Palantir's division of labor between Deployment Strategist and FDE?

A. It depends on the project scale. For large projects (multi-year, multiple business divisions, on the order of tens of millions of yen per month), the DS + FDE division of labor is effective. For small to mid-sized projects, a "hybrid FDE" where one person handles both roles is more realistic. In Japan's talent market, people who can play both the DS-like and FDE-like roles are rare, but if such a person exists, having one person cover both is more efficient.

Q4. Compared to overseas FDE salary levels (tens of millions to 100 million yen), can Japanese companies afford to hire them?

A. FDEs at major overseas AI companies generally earn $300K–$500K plus equity (equivalent to 45–75 million yen plus stock options). It is difficult for Japanese companies to pay this range on their own, but by using external FDEs, they can access equivalent talent for a monthly fee of 3–5 million yen (36–60 million yen annually). This is one of the reasons external FDE services like FDX Inc. exist.

Q5. Can we bring in FDEs directly from Anthropic / OpenAI?

A. As a rule, both companies are vendors that provide APIs to customers, and their FDEs are resources for supporting the deployment of their own products (GPT / Claude). They are not offered to third parties as general-purpose AI implementation partners. Even for enterprise customers, FDEs are assigned only in connection with specific large contracts. If you want to leverage the FDE model in a general-purpose way, choose an independent implementation partner such as FDX Inc.

Q6. Is the FDE model effective for areas other than AI (DX, business reform in general)?

A. Valid. Palantir's origins lie in data integration projects that predate AI. It can be applied to any domain requiring business understanding × technical implementation × operational handover, such as BPR (business process reengineering), ERP customization, data platform construction, and SaaS integration implementation. AI is merely the main battleground for FDEs after 2024; the essence of the role is integrated implementation of business × technology.

Q7. Will the FDE role spread in Japan going forward?

A. Yes, it will spread. From 2025 onward, Japanese AI companies (AI Shift, Renue, Zix, Alphakt, etc.) began adopting it as a formal role, and media coverage (Nikkei xTECH, note) is increasing. It is becoming the mainstream model for enterprise AI adoption, and it is predicted that within five years, some Japanese SIers and consultancies will also start offering FDE-style services. FDX Inc. is one of the early entrants into this market.

Q8. How do Palantir Foundry and Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) relate?

A. Foundry is Palantir's core data integration and analytics platform, and FDEs are the implementers who build data pipelines, object models, and business applications tailored to customer operations on top of Foundry. Palantir's competitive advantage rests on two pillars: the Foundry product and the FDE organization that deeply adapts it to customer operations. The product alone is not sufficient. That said, if Japanese companies want to use a generic FDE model that does not depend on a specific platform, there is also the option of implementation support not tied to any particular product.

Q9. How does Anthropic's (Claude) Forward Deployed Engineer differ from those at other AI companies?

A. Anthropic's FDE (Applied AI / forward deployed engineering) is characterized by placing "safety and alignment" at the center of implementation when embedding Claude into enterprise operations. Rather than simply building AI that works, the business implementation includes guardrail design to minimize malfunctions, hallucinations, and misjudgments, audit logs, and built-in human review. A heavier emphasis on co-implementing multi-step agent designs with customers is another point of difference from OpenAI and Scale AI. However, the FDEs at both Anthropic and OpenAI share one thing in common: they are tied to the adoption of their own APIs (Claude / GPT). Whether a partner can work generically across products must be judged on a separate axis.
