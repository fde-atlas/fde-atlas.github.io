Today's Judgment

Automated R&D and content production may seem like two different types of tasks, but they face the same problem: as candidate outputs multiply, how do you decide which ones are worth keeping? This issue focuses on independent evals, version tracking, and collaboration workflows. The delivery recommendations below are distilled from public materials and do not represent independent testing of the products.

Key Signals

1. Baidu ERNIE Evolve: Beyond modifying code, an independent judgment is still needed on whether improvements hold up

On November 9, Baidu introduced ERNIE Evolve: given a code repository, an optimization objective, and constraints, the system runs isolated experiments, with independent evals and review deciding whether candidate changes are kept. The eval program is kept separate from the repository being optimized, and the review also checks for out-of-bounds changes and exploitation of eval loopholes.

What this means for FDEs: Automated iteration requires a stable acceptance baseline. When delivering, you should separately record candidate versions, the rationale for each change, eval results, and keep/kill decisions—never let the system that generates the results declare success on its own.

This is a vendor's description of an R&D system; it cannot be taken as evidence that ordinary enterprises can already run unattended R&D, nor can its R&D task results be directly extrapolated into customer business gains.

Source: Baidu's official introduction to ERNIE Evolve (November 9)

2. Weekly Watch: Mito's video production practice puts collaboration and task management alongside model calls

On November 6, Google Cloud published an article by the Mito team reviewing their production of TV commercials for Movistar. The team describes the management burden created by large volumes of generated assets, and how they adopted background asynchronous tasks, a shared canvas, and retention of old versions to support multi-party feedback and exploration of creative branches.

What this means for FDEs: Acceptance of a content production system should not stop at generating a single video. You should also check whether long-running tasks block operations, how failures are retried, whether versions are traceable, and whether customer feedback can be accurately linked to specific assets.

This is a customer team's own account on a vendor blog; the project took place during a previous World Cup and is not a new delivery in November. Asset volume and iteration counts do not directly prove quality, profit, or headcount savings.

Source: Google Cloud: Mito's content production architecture (November 6)

Takeaways for FDE practitioners

  • Separate "generating candidates" from "approving adoption," and maintain acceptance samples and eval rules independently.
  • For every change, keep the input, version, result, and owner on record, so that even failures can inform later judgments.
  • For long-running tasks, deliver progress querying, failure handling, and result traceability.
  • Measure final usable outcomes, human review time, and task cost—never substitute generation volume for business value.
