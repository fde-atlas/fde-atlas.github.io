Forward Deployed Engineer at Cursor: Role, Pay, and Interview (2026)

fde.directory · 2026-10-16 · 4 min read

Summary

This profile examines Cursor's forward deployed engineer hiring as of late 2026. Cursor had roughly six FDE requisitions open across the US, EMEA and APAC, including a Regional Director and an RVP, plus an India posting added in October. Unlike most FDE teams, Cursor's field org exists to convert bottom-up developer adoption into enterprise rollouts. The role requires 5+ years of experience and covers customer discovery, production agents, MCP servers, evals and reliability work. Cursor is the rare FDE employer whose product engineers already want to use. That is the strange part of this hiring story. Most forward-deployed teams exist because customers struggle to adopt a hard-to-use platform. Cursor’s tool spread bottom-up through individual developers, and the company is now building a field organization to convert that grassroots usage into enterprise-wide rollouts. By Aced’s count, Cursor had six FDE requisitions open across the US, EMEA and APAC as of September 2, 2026, including a Regional Director and an RVP of FDE. In October it added an India posting covering Bengaluru, New Delhi and Mumbai.

Two caveats, because this site prefers accurate to tidy. Cursor publishes no pay band on the postings we could read, so there are no salary numbers here, and Cursor publishes no interview process for the role. Everything below on those two points is labeled as what is and isn’t known.

The company behind the seat

SpaceX signed a definitive agreement to acquire Anysphere, Cursor’s maker, on June 16, 2026, in an all-stock deal with an implied equity value of $60 billion. Cursor announced the deal had closed on August 14, 2026, and said its policies and mission would not change, while promising access to a much larger GPU fleet for building cheaper, stronger models.

Business figures are murkier. Secondary coverage puts annualized revenue around $4 billion as of early June 2026, with enterprise at roughly 60% of revenue (our job board entry says ~65%; sources disagree and none cite filings, so treat both as estimates). The same coverage says Cursor passed $1 billion ARR in November 2025 and $2 billion in early 2026. If even the direction is right, this is a company that quadrupled revenue in under a year, and field engineering is how it keeps enterprise accounts from churning into a competitor’s bundle.

What the role actually is

Per the posting text as mirrored by the freehire job aggregator (the Cursor careers page itself blocks automated reading), the FDE leads customer discovery, finds bottlenecks, defines success metrics, then ships production agents, AI applications and coding-agent workflows. The listed deliverables are specific: tools, MCP servers, retrieval systems, rules, skills, CI gates and evaluations. You own production quality through tracing, evals, debugging, and latency-versus-cost tradeoffs, integrate with customer authentication, data and delivery systems, and then help the customer operate what you built.

Aced’s description of the work adds concrete examples: large-scale refactors, migration systems, automated PR-review loops and spec-to-implementation pipelines. In other words, you are building on Cursor, with Cursor, inside a customer’s engineering org. That is a distinct flavor from the frontier-lab seats we cover in Anthropic and OpenAI, where the product is a model API. Here the product is an agentic dev environment, and your customer’s buyer is a platform or developer-productivity leader.

The contrarian read: the Regional Director and RVP titles matter more than the IC reqs. A company hiring a management layer in parallel with its first ICs is not experimenting with FDE. It is building a regional sales-engineering-plus-delivery machine, and the early ICs will shape what that machine measures. Compare the Cursor structure with the pod-based model in our Cognition profile.

Compensation: what’s known

Nothing is posted. Aced lists comp as “not published” and says only that reported offers are top-of-market for AI tooling, with no numbers. Our job board lists Cursor as “Not disclosed.” Rather than guess, here is how to bracket it: Cursor reportedly competes with the same pool as frontier labs, so benchmark against the posted bands in our FDE salary guide, and push the recruiter early for base, equity and how SpaceX stock converts. Post-acquisition, equity is presumably SpaceX-denominated, which changes the liquidity question completely compared with a typical startup grant. Ask what you actually hold and when you can sell it.

The interview loop

Cursor has not published stages, rounds or a timeline for FDE. Third-party prep sites that list “questions” are inferring them from the job description, and one explicitly says so. I would not trust any specific round count you read online. What you can do is prepare against the posting, which is a reasonable proxy for what the loop screens:

  • A scoping story. A time you found the real bottleneck on a team and defined the success metric before building.
  • A shipped agent or AI workflow, including the eval harness and the regression you caught.
  • A reliability story: metrics, alerts, safe rollout, incident response.
  • Executive translation: explaining a tradeoff to a staff-plus engineer or a platform head.
  • Hands-on fluency with Cursor itself. The role is building workflows on the product, so arrive having built something real with rules, skills and MCP.

Who should apply

If you are a senior engineer with customer-facing time who has already built agent workflows, this is among the more legible FDE postings on the board: the requirements are specific and the ladder is visible. If you are earlier in your career, look at how to become an FDE without AI experience first; Cursor’s bar is explicit that it wants five years. In India, see our India FDE guide for how the three-city build-out compares.
