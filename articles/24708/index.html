Vietnam FDE Salaries: Local vs. Remote Roles Compared

Original title: 越南 FDE 薪资：本土与远程岗位对比

FDE Times · 2026-10-09 · 6 min read

Summary

Drawing on recruiter Second Talent's 2026 Vietnam FDE salary guide, the article reports that local junior FDEs earn 20–35 million VND per month—below many ordinary developers—while seniors earn 65–95 million and remote FDEs serving US, European and Australian clients earn 65–138 million or more. The claimed 60% pay gap tied to client-facing English comes from a recruiting firm and is unverified. The author also compares US postings (Palantir $125k–200k, OpenAI $185k–300k, Anthropic $280k–320k) and advises comparing base salaries separately from equity, then practicing scenarios like running client meetings solo to reach the higher pay bands.

Key points

  • Second Talent claims client-facing English can create a 60% pay gap for Vietnamese FDEs, but the figure is unverified.
  • Local junior FDEs earn 20–35 million VND monthly; seniors 65–95 million.
  • Remote FDEs for US/EU/Australian firms earn 65–138 million VND or more, exceeding local lead-level pay.
  • US FDE postings range from Palantir's $125k–200k to Anthropic's $280k–320k; compare base against base, equity separately.
  • To reach top pay bands, prove you can run client meetings alone rather than listing generic English skills.

Two Vietnamese engineers with the same technical background can still differ in salary by 60%. That is the view of recruiting firm Second Talent in its 2026 Vietnam FDE salary guide. In its interpretation, the bottleneck is not technical ability; the main factor is whether one can work with clients in English at senior-level roles.

The 60% figure comes from a company that sells recruiting services and has not been independently verified, so it is best treated only as a signal. Second Talent itself notes that Vietnamese companies are not yet hiring for the FDE title at scale. People actually performing this role are usually paid within the senior AI engineer and platform engineer ranges.

If you plan to pursue a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role from Vietnam, or from any non-English-speaking market, this directly affects where you should spend your time over the next six months. According to the recruiting firm's interpretation, the biggest factor widening the income gap is not learning another framework.

The FDE title premium is not as high as it sounds

Second Talent lists 2026 FDE salary ranges in Vietnam across four levels: junior at 20–35 million VND per month, mid-level at 35–65 million VND, senior at 65–95 million VND, and lead/principal at 95–120 million VND. All figures are fixed monthly salaries.

For reference, a TopDev report cited by VnEconomy shows that in 2024, the average monthly salary for Vietnamese developers was roughly $1,100–$3,000, or about 27 million to over 73 million VND, depending on skills and experience.

Comparing the two, junior FDEs sit at the low end of the overall range, partly below the 27 million VND line. Mid-level FDEs fall entirely within the overall range. Only at the senior level does the 65 million VND lower bound approach the upper bound for ordinary developers. The gap is real, but smaller than the impression created by the "FDE" title.

The two datasets are also two years apart. According to an ITviec report cited by VIR in January 2025, average salaries for Vietnamese IT professionals rose 27.9% in one year, mainly because more experienced people entered the market.

Therefore, part of the gap between the two datasets may simply be due to the different survey periods.

The ceiling is in remote contracts

The difference is most visible when clients are overseas. Second Talent's figures show that Vietnamese FDEs working remotely for US, European, and Australian companies earn 65 million to over 138 million VND per month. This range is not broken down by level. Its lower bound exactly matches the lower bound for local senior roles, while its upper bound exceeds the 120 million VND ceiling for lead/principal level.

Second Talent notes that for this group, clients require strong written English and several hours of working hours overlapping with the client each day.

Arc offers another reference point, based on self-reported expected salaries from Vietnamese remote developers, covering all developers, not just FDEs. The average is about $45,848 per year, and about $75,369 per year for senior developers.

Arc also observes that in non-English-speaking countries, people with good English typically earn above local market rates.

Why does English matter more for FDEs?

For a backend developer on a distributed team, most work can be done through pull requests, tickets, and asynchronous messages. As long as written English is clear enough, the job gets done.

FDEs can hardly work this way, because the role itself revolves around close collaboration with clients. So it is no surprise that Second Talent emphasizes client-facing English rather than general English. In its view, what separates candidates is not the technical interview, but a single conversation with a client.

Imagine two engineers building the same RAG pipeline for a logistics company in Australia. The first writes excellent code but needs a PM to accompany them to every meeting.

The second runs requirements-gathering sessions alone and tells the client directly: the existing data cannot support the feature they want, and a smaller component should be built first. Working with the second engineer, the client needs no intermediary, and therefore has reason to pay more for it.

Market demand points in the same direction. According to ITviec data cited by VIR, English was the soft skill employers valued most in the first half of 2025, with 40.3% of companies explicitly requiring it.

An earlier ITviec survey published in 2018 found that 85% of companies believed Vietnamese engineers' English was weaker than that of their foreign counterparts. That figure is outdated and cannot by itself explain today's gap; it only shows that English has long been seen as a weakness.

Don't compare your salary with Anthropic's on instinct

The US numbers are dazzling. According to job postings directly reviewed by Valletta Software on September 8, 2026, Anthropic lists a range of $280,000–$320,000 for FDEs. OpenAI lists $185,000–$300,000 base salary plus equity for FDEs in its business division.

The FDE role originated at Palantir, which lists a base salary of $125,000–$200,000.

Even the calculation methodology for these ranges differs. According to Aced, Anthropic's range already includes target bonuses; whereas Valletta notes that Palantir's equity and bonuses are not included in the range. So when comparing, match base salary against base salary, and list equity on a separate line.

The same rule applies to any offer you receive: ask clearly which currency it is paid in, how much is fixed, and how much is tied to performance.

A remote contract signed from Vietnam will not be paid at San Francisco salary standards. These figures only show where the FDE role is priced in the US market; they do not represent what you should expect.

How to reach the upper end of the range

Since the gap appears at the ceiling, the task is to prove you can stand in front of a client on your own, without a translator or a PM by your side. What you need to practice are the scenarios you will actually encounter at work: clarifying requirements through questions, pushing back on out-of-scope requests, and explaining why a demo went wrong. General conversational English is not enough.

When reading job descriptions, watch for phrases like "customer-facing" or "client-facing", timezone overlap requirements, or whether on-site visits to clients are needed. These indicate the company is paying for communication skills, which is exactly where you have room to negotiate.

Do not write "good English communication skills" on your resume. Instead, write a line with context: what kind of clients you worked with, in which country, which meeting you chaired, and what decision was reached. Recruiters need evidence that you have faced clients on your own.

In this market, the same code can command two different salaries. Which one you get depends on the meeting room where you talk to clients, not on the pull request.
