State of FDE Hiring 2026

Top FDE Jobs · 2026-10-29 · 13 min read

Summary

This October 2026 report from topfdejobs analyzes 1,465 verified forward deployed engineer postings at 499 companies, tracked from employers' own career boards between October 7 and 28. Demand has grown sharply: US postings rose from under 200 in 2023 to over 7,300 in the first nine months of 2026 (Lightcast data). The median posted base pay is $200k, with AI labs paying $93k more than consultancies ($262k vs $169k). The role is genuinely engineering work: 88% of postings require coding and only 2.4% carry a sales quota. Jobs cluster in four metros (SF Bay Area, New York, Washington DC, London) holding 45% of roles, and only 17% are fully remote. Startups post a third of roles; Palantir, OpenAI and Databricks lead. The report also flags title inflation: roughly one in six 'forward deployed' postings is not actual FDE work, echoing Gartner's warning about 'FDE washing'.

Key points

  • Census of 1,465 verified FDE postings at 499 companies, October 2026.
  • US postings grew from under 200 (2023) to 7,300+ (Jan-Sep 2026).
  • Median base pay is $200k; AI labs pay $93k more than consultancies.
  • 88% of postings require coding; only 2.4% carry sales quotas.
  • About 1 in 6 'forward deployed' postings is not real FDE work.

The big picture

Forward deployed is the hottest title in AI, and it's a real engineering job: more than 8 in 10 postings require writing code, and the median role pays $200k.

Between October 7 and October 28, 2026, topfdejobs tracked 1,465 forward deployed engineer postings at 499 companies, read from the employers' own career boards. Our editors reviewed every one against a written definition of the role, and we left out recruiter reposts, duplicate postings and roles that only borrow the title.

We put that census next to the best third-party data on the role: Lightcast's posting counts, Google search interest and the money that AI labs, cloud providers and consultancies committed to forward deployment in the past year.

Chapter 1

From Palantir niche to category

Palantir built its business on engineers it sent to work inside customer organizations. For a decade the role stayed mostly there. Then generative AI turned "make it work in production at this customer" into the hardest problem in enterprise software, and every AI company needed people to solve it.

Chapter 6

What "forward deployed" actually means

The title is fashionable, so we checked how often it's honest. We read every posting with "forward deployed" in its title and asked one question: does this person write code inside a customer's environment?

Nearly two-thirds of "Forward Deployed" postings are real FDE jobs

All 1,425 "forward deployed" postings we pulled in, by what they turned out to be

  • 64% Real FDE jobs (914)
  • 13% Not FDE work (182)
  • 16% Recruiter reposts (225)
  • 7% Copies of the same job (104)

Copies are the same role posted again (another city, another board). Recruiter reposts are agencies and aggregators advertising someone else's job.

Outlook

Demand keeps rising and the money is committed, but the people who build these teams disagree about how long the role lasts. Here is what they say, in their own words.

"Demand for forward deployed engineers is rising fast, and growing wages suggest employers are competing for a limited pool of talent."
"The reality is that experienced FDEs cannot simply be recruited at the scale clients need."
"From OpenAI's side, we should always be temporary."

Methodology

What counts as an FDE role. An engineer who writes production code inside or for a specific customer's environment. Sales engineers, solutions architects without hands-on build work, support, project management and configuration-only roles don't count, whatever the title says. Our editors review every posting against these written rules.

Window. Every posting that was live on an employer's board at any point between October 7 and October 28, 2026, including roles first posted earlier. We count postings, not openings: one posting can hire several people, and some employers post one role separately for each city (each city counts; an exact duplicate, same company, title and city, counts once).

Pay. Only pay from the employer's job board pay field or numbers written in the posting. We use the midpoint of the posted base range and leave out hourly, monthly and commission-only pay. Equity and bonus aren't included.

Labels. Travel, coding, quota, equity, seniority and skills come from a language model reading each posting, with samples checked against the posting text. Small shares (under 5%) carry the most error. 75 postings expired before we could read them; they count in the totals but not in any share that needs the posting text.

How we're likely wrong. We undercount employers without a public job board, employers on Workday and similar systems (which we only reach through Indeed, LinkedIn and Glassdoor, so large consultancies are underrepresented) and FDE work posted under unrelated titles. Our window is short, so we show no trends from our own data; growth figures come from Lightcast and Google. Employer types are our own classification.

Third-party data. Each chart names its source. We never combine providers in one chart, because they count differently.
