Solutions engineer vs forward deployed engineer: how the work differs

NeuraPath Academy · 2026-09-27 · 4 min read

Summary

This article contrasts solutions engineers with forward deployed engineers (FDEs). Solutions engineers support pre-sales: demos, security questionnaires, and proofs of concept, measured by deals won. FDEs take ownership after signing, writing production code in the customer's environment and being measured on outcomes in production. A hypothetical Indian logistics email-classification project walks through both roles stage by stage, showing how real data (30% Hindi or mixed-language emails) exposed gaps a clean POC missed. It also compares weekly workloads, metrics, reporting lines, how to read job postings, first-quarter expectations, and career paths.

Key points

  • Solutions engineers are measured on revenue influence and win rate; FDEs on production outcomes.
  • A POC succeeds when the customer believes; FDE work succeeds when it actually works on messy data.
  • In the example, 30% of real emails were Hindi or mixed-language, which the POC sample missed.
  • Job posting phrases like 'quota' or 'pre-sales' indicate solutions engineer; 'embed with customers' indicates FDE.
  • Key interview question: what do you still own after the contract is signed?

A solutions engineer is a technical specialist who helps sell a product: running demos, answering security and architecture questions, and building proofs of concept before a contract is signed. A forward deployed engineer (FDE) works after, and sometimes alongside, the sale, writing production code inside or next to the customer's environment so the product actually delivers results. The first is measured on deals won; the second on outcomes in production.

One customer request, followed through both roles

Take a hypothetical Indian logistics company evaluating an AI platform to classify and route incoming shipment exception emails. Here is who does what.

Stage Solutions engineer Forward deployed engineer
First call Understands the problem, maps it to product capabilities, runs a tailored demo Usually not involved, sometimes joins for a hard technical question
Security questionnaire Answers data residency, encryption and access questions with the security team May review integration-specific answers
Proof of concept Builds a two-week POC on 500 sample emails; shows accuracy on a clean sample May advise on what the POC will not prove
Contract signed Hands over to delivery; moves to the next deal Takes ownership
Integration Not involved Connects to the customer's mailbox, ticketing system and shipment database; handles their authentication rules
Real data Not involved Discovers 30% of emails are in Hindi or mixed Hindi-English and the POC sample had none; builds evaluation on real traffic
Go-live May attend the celebration call Runs the rollout, monitors errors, fixes what breaks in week one
After Renewal conversations Feeds reusable fixes back to the product team

The row about real data shows the core difference. A solutions engineer's POC succeeds when the customer believes the product can work. An FDE's work succeeds when it does work, on the customer's messy data, inside their constraints.

What each role's week looks like

A solutions engineer's week is heavy on calls: three or four discovery calls and demos, a security questionnaire, time building demo environments, and a forecast meeting with the sales team. Travel to customer offices is common in enterprise sales.

A forward deployed engineer's week is heavy on building: writing and testing integration code, debugging a failure in the customer's environment, a weekly status call with the customer's project owner, and a conversation with the product team about a feature the customer needs. See what a forward deployed engineer is for more on the role itself.

Deals won versus deployments that stay live

Solutions engineer Forward deployed engineer
Primary Revenue influenced, win rate, POC-to-close conversion Customer outcome in production, time to value
Secondary Deal cycle time, technical objections resolved Adoption, incidents, reusable components contributed
Failure looks like Lost deal Signed deal that never reaches production, or churns

Sales leadership versus engineering or delivery

Solutions engineers usually report into sales, to a head of solutions engineering or pre-sales, and work alongside account executives. FDEs usually report into engineering, delivery or a dedicated deployment team, and work with the product and engineering teams as well as the customer's technical staff.

Demo, POC or production: reading the posting

JD phrase Role it indicates
"Partner with account executives", "quota", "pre-sales" Solutions engineer
"Deliver compelling demos and POCs" Solutions engineer
"Respond to RFPs and security questionnaires" Solutions engineer or pre-sales architect
"Embed with customers to deploy and own production solutions" Forward deployed engineer
"Write production-quality code in the customer's environment" Forward deployed engineer
"Travel to customer sites for extended periods" Often FDE; ask how much
"Customer-facing engineer" alone Ambiguous; ask whether you own code after go-live

Ask in the interview: "After a contract is signed, what do I still own?" A solutions engineer's honest answer is "not much, you move on". An FDE's answer is "everything until it works".

Which suits you

If you enjoy explaining, persuading and switching between many customers a week, solutions engineering fits. If you enjoy staying with one hard problem until it works in production, and you are willing to write and debug a lot of code, forward deployed engineering fits better. Both need strong communication; FDE needs deeper engineering.

A first quarter in presales versus in the field

Solutions engineer: learn the product deeply enough to demo it without a script, shadow calls, then run your own demos by month two and a POC by month three. FDE: ship a small fix into a customer deployment in month one, own a workstream in month two, and lead an integration by month three.

Sales engineering leadership or field engineering leadership

Solutions engineers move to senior or principal SE, solutions architect or sales leadership. FDEs move to senior FDE, deployment lead, solutions architect or product roles. Frequently asked questions

Is a solutions engineer a sales role?

Usually it is a technical role inside the sales organisation. Solutions engineers, also called pre-sales or sales engineers, run demos, answer technical questions and design proofs of concept to help win deals. Their targets are often tied to revenue.

Can a solutions engineer become a forward deployed engineer?

Yes, if they build enough hands-on production skill: writing and testing integration code, debugging deployed systems and owning outcomes after go-live. The customer-facing experience transfers well; the engineering depth is usually the gap.
