What Questions Should You Ask in an FDE Casual Interview? Purpose-Based Question Examples, What You'll Be Asked, and NG Examples

Original title: FDEのカジュアル面談で聞く質問は？目的別の質問例と聞かれること・NG例

FDE総研 · 2026-11-20 · 12 min read

Summary

This FDE総研 article advises candidates on how to use casual interviews for Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roles as information-gathering sessions rather than informal screenings. Because the FDE title varies widely across companies—ranging from SES-like on-site work to presales—it proposes four axes (problem origin, implementation ratio, customer proximity, success metrics) with concrete questions to verify the actual job content. It also covers questions companies commonly ask and answer templates, questions to avoid (such as asking salary upfront), and how priorities shift based on experience, current role, English ability, and company size. It recommends recording answers within 24 hours and comparing multiple companies on the same four axes.

Key points

  • Use four axes—origin, implementation ratio, customer proximity, success metrics—to assess FDE roles.
  • Ask about the most recent project: who defined the problem, who implemented, whether it went to production.
  • Companies typically ask about motivation, customer-facing experience, and hands-on implementation.
  • Avoid questions answerable by research or salary-focused questions early on.
  • Record answers within 24 hours and compare companies on the same four axes.

The flow is to cover what you can learn from official information before the interview, and ask only about things not covered in official information during the interview.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) casual interview is not a selection process but an opportunity for information exchange. Even so, deciding in advance what to ask, what you'll be asked, and what criteria you'll use to judge afterward greatly changes how much information you get from the same 30 to 60 minutes. FDE is an engineering role that originated at Palantir, involving working closely at customer sites and handling everything from problem definition to implementation (What is FDE). In this article, FDE Soken (VACAN Technologies editorial team) organizes question examples, how to answer, things to avoid, and how to evaluate after the interview based on its four axes: "origin, implementation ratio, customer distance, and success metrics."

What questions should candidates ask in an FDE casual interview?

If you ask questions along FDE Soken's four axes (origin, implementation ratio, customer distance, and success metrics), it becomes easier to see the reality of an FDE position even in a single interview. The definitions of the four axes are summarized in What is FDE (FDE Soken's definition page). The job title "FDE" varies considerably from company to company. Some positions are close to on-site SES work, while others are close to pre-sales. For this reason, prepare questions that verify the actual nature of the work rather than the title.

What questions verify the substance of the work?

The substance of FDE work is easiest to verify by asking specifically, "In your most recent project, who defined the problem and who implemented it?" If you ask "What does the work involve?", you'll often get the same explanation as the job listing. Focus on one recent project and ask in the following order.

  • The customer's industry and the content of the initial consultation
  • Who redefined the problem from the consultation, and how
  • The time it took to produce the first working prototype, and who built it
  • Whether it went into production. If not, why

According to FDE Soken's perspective, the more concrete answers a company gives to questions 3 and 4, the more likely it is that the FDE role covers work through implementation. Based on the practical experience of VACAN Technologies CEO Tamaki, FDEs spend less than half of their working hours on implementation, with the rest spent on customer interactions (What is FDE). Therefore, a position described as having a nearly 100% implementation ratio may be closer to SWE than FDE. Conversely, a position with almost no implementation may be closer to IT consulting.

What questions verify evaluation and working conditions?

For FDE evaluation and working conditions, asking about three points—success metrics, frequency of on-site work and business trips, and how projects transition—reduces mismatches after joining. Since FDE is a role close to customer sites, the ratio of on-site to remote work varies greatly by company and project (for details, see FDE remote work, on-site assignments, and business trips).

  • "How many customers does a single FDE handle at the same time?"
  • "How often do projects transition? What triggers the transition?"
  • "For an FDE who was highly rated recently, what results were recognized?"

If the answer to the last question is only "utilization rate" or "low overtime," consider the possibility that the role is operated more as an on-site development position than as an FDE.

What are companies likely to ask in an FDE casual interview, and how should you answer?

In an FDE casual interview, companies mainly ask about three things: reasons for changing jobs, experience with customer interactions, and hands-on implementation experience, though they don't probe as deeply as in the formal selection process. Common formal interview questions and how to structure answers are organized in FDE interview preparation. In a casual interview, it's enough to briefly cover the "entry point" aspects of these topics.

What questions are commonly asked, and what formats work for answers?

Questions commonly asked in FDE casual interviews are best answered in a format of "one fact plus one reason" within one minute. Save longer stories for the formal selection process, and in the interview convey the outline of your interests and experience.

How should I prepare for questions I might get stuck on?

FDE candidates most often get stuck on "experience defining a customer's business problem yourself," but even a small-scale example is sufficient if you can speak about it as the person in charge. Improving internal tools, or re-listening to requests from other departments and changing the requirements, can also be presented as experience defining problems. People from SIers or IT consulting tend to be weaker on customer-facing topics, while those from SWE backgrounds tend to be weaker on implementation. Preparing one episode for your weaker side will keep you from panicking during the interview.

The priority of questions to ask in an FDE casual interview changes depending on years of experience, current job, English ability, and the size of the company you are applying to. How do the questions you should ask change based on years of experience and current job?

An FDE candidate's years of experience and current job are materials for deciding "which of the four axes to verify first." The basic rule is to ask first about the axis where your experience is thin in your current role. If you come from an SIer, verify implementation ratio first; from SWE, customer proximity; from PdM, both implementation ratio and success metrics. If you have fewer years of experience, asking about the onboarding system before the substance of the work makes it easier to imagine your life after joining.

How do the questions you should ask change based on English ability and company size?

The weight of English ability in FDE job openings differs greatly between foreign firms like Palantir and domestic startups, so ask specifically "where English is used." At foreign firms and large companies, verify where English is used: communication with headquarters, English documentation, or overseas customers. At domestic startups, the breadth of the role is more likely to be the key issue than English, so prioritize asking how much sales or customer success work you would also cover.

When conditions conflict, which should you prioritize?

According to FDE Soken's judgment, when conditions conflict, prioritize "ownership of implementation" first, then verify English ability and company size. For example, if you have "high English ability but few years of experience," rather than the percentage of projects using English, first ask whether there is a system where you can handle implementation together with senior FDEs. Since FDE is a role premised on being able to build working things in the field, thinking of English ability as a strength that becomes valuable after accumulating implementation experience makes it easier to set priorities.

In FDE Soken's view, if three or more of the four questions return "FDE-side" answers, you can consider it an FDE job opening true to its title. Even if answers contradict the job title, that does not necessarily make the opening bad. Judge based on whether it matches the work you want to do.

Who is not suited for FDE?

Those who find it hard to fit into an FDE role are people who want to build only after requirements are fixed, people who want to go deep into a single technology for a long time, and people who feel strong stress from coordinating with customers. In FDE work, the cycle of defining issues from vague consultations, building something small to show, and changing direction based on customer reactions repeats over and over. To imagine whether this working style suits you, it helps to ask in a casual interview about "a recent project where the direction changed significantly." For a detailed breakdown of fit, see People suited to FDE roles.

What questions should you avoid in a casual interview, and how do you evaluate companies afterward?

In an FDE casual interview, avoid asking about salary or screening results first, and within 24 hours after the interview, record the answers across the four axes to compare. Negotiating conditions is generally done around the time of the offer (FDE offer negotiation).

What questions should you avoid, and what are some NG examples?

In an FDE casual interview, avoid questions you could answer yourself through research and questions that only probe conditions upfront. Such questions tend to be perceived as a lack of genuine interest.

How do you evaluate companies after the interview?

After an FDE casual interview, the most reliable approach is to record the answers line by line across the FDE Soken's four axes and compare multiple companies in the same table. The steps are as follows.

  • Within 24 hours after the interview, note each company's answer in one line for each of the four axes
  • Record which axes were answered with concrete project stories and which were answered with generalities
  • Judge whether the axes where you are weak in your current role (e.g., implementation ratio for those from SIers) can be developed after joining
  • Note any concerns as questions to verify during the formal selection process
  • Compare two or three companies in the same table, judging by "the reality of the four axes" rather than the "job title"

In the FDE Soken's view, companies that answered two or more axes with generalities may not yet have a well-defined FDE role. For people who want to be involved from the ground up, such companies can actually be an opportunity. Don't judge them as simply good or bad; decide based on your own aspirations.
