FDE Methods and Tools

Original title: FDE方法与工具

圣路可 Fde · 2026 · 4 min read

Summary

This article presents a practical FDE methodology for AI projects, built around a 15-question self-test that identifies which of five stages is blocking progress: unclear business understanding, undocumented experience, undefined boundaries, untested samples, or poorly set permissions. It introduces a six-step workflow (observe, describe, map, simulate, verify, deploy), a one-page task specification that turns vague rules into checkable criteria, a red-yellow-green method for deciding which tasks AI can handle, and a four-level rollout path from historical validation to limited automation. Downloadable templates and a teaching example on after-sales tickets are included.

Key points

  • 15-question self-test diagnoses which of five stages blocks an AI project.
  • Six-step workflow: observe, describe, map, simulate, verify, deploy, each with a deliverable.
  • One-page task specification converts vague experience into checkable completion criteria.
  • Red-yellow-green method sorts tasks into automatable, human-confirmed, and off-limits.
  • Four-level rollout starts with historical data and grants AI permissions gradually.

From identifying blockers to step-by-step delegation, every step comes with a ready-to-use table. Take a 3-minute self-assessment first to see which stage your AI project is stuck at.

Which stage is your AI project stuck at?

Pick an AI scenario you are working on or planning to pursue, and answer 15 questions. The results will highlight the stage most likely to be the bottleneck and suggest next steps.

  • Five stages: business not clearly understood, experience not clearly documented, boundaries not clearly defined, samples not tested, permissions not properly set
  • 3 questions per stage; choose "matches", "partially matches", or "does not match"
  • No login required; results are shown instantly

The Six-Step FDE Workflow

The point is not memorizing terminology, but establishing a stable sequence of questions. Each step has a simplest possible deliverable that can be shown to the business owner for confirmation.

StepWhat the FDE doesSimplest deliverable
1 ObserveFollow business staff through the full process, recording waiting, repetition, rework, and risksBusiness observation map
2 DescribeTurn verbal experience into inputs, steps, outputs, exceptions, and completion criteriaOne-page task specification
3 MapDetermine what the AI platform can do, what needs human confirmation, and what it should not doRed-yellow-green capability table
4 SimulateRun the minimal process end-to-end with anonymized samplesSimulation log
5 VerifyCheck facts, rules, calculations, permissions, and exceptionsIssue list
6 DeployStart with a suggestion mode, pilot on a small scale, and delegate step by stepLaunch plan

One-Page Task Specification: Turning "It Depends" into Rules

When business staff say "it depends on the situation", that is not a mistake—it just means the judgment criteria still live in their heads. Use one page to get the business owner, operators, and delivery staff to confirm: they are working on the same thing, with the same completion criteria.

ItemHow to write it for an after-sales ticket assistant (teaching example)
Task goalHelp customer service make an initial assessment of tickets; does not replace supervisor approval
InputsCustomer description, order information, purchase date, product information, attachment status
ProcessingSummarize the issue, look up rules, check materials, identify risks, generate suggestions
OutputsIssue classification, suggested action, cited rules, missing materials, risk alerts, reply draft
ExceptionsEscalate to a human when information conflicts, rules do not cover the case, or safety and compensation are involved
Completion criteriaConclusions are backed by evidence, missing information is clearly stated, no high-risk cases are missed, no commitments beyond authority
What the business person saysWhat the FDE asks next
"This can usually be returned"What conditions does "usually" refer to? In which cases can it not be returned?
"The materials are more or less fine"Which materials are required? If which item is missing must it be sent back?
"Serious ones go to the supervisor"What words or signs count as serious? How quickly must it be escalated?
"Let AI handle it first"Does that mean generating suggestions, filling in fields, or executing system actions?

Completion criteria must be checkable. "The result looks professional" is not a criterion; "conclusions cite the corresponding rules, amounts are calculated consistently, and all high-risk tickets are escalated to humans" is.

The Red-Yellow-Green Method: Which Tasks Can Be Handed to AI

Clear rules do not mean the whole process can be automated. First break a one-sentence requirement into small tasks, then assign a color to each one, and only then talk about delegation.

Green: safe to pilot first

Highly repetitive, clear rules, results easy to check, errors recoverable.

Examples: summarizing tickets, searching the knowledge base, checking materials, generating reply drafts

Yellow: AI suggests, human confirms

Requires business judgment, involves customer commitments, mistakes have visible impact.

Examples: flagging high risk, suggesting returns/exchanges/repairs, filling in key ticket fields

Red: not handed to AI for execution for now

Involves money, permissions, legal liability, personal safety, or irreversible actions.

Examples: approving refunds, deciding compensation, modifying orders

When unsure about the color, ask yourself three questions

  • Can the AI's judgment be traced to a clear basis?
  • If it judges wrong, can a human notice and recover in time?
  • Does this action involve money, permissions, commitments, or safety?

Five Questions for Verifying Results

"Fluent writing" does not mean "business correctness". An AI output saying "purchase is within 30 days, suggest an exchange" reads smoothly and the rule seems right, but it misses the smoke the customer mentioned—so it is wrong from a business standpoint.

Check itemWhat to check
FactsWhether information not present in the input has been added
BasisWhether conclusions cite correct and valid business rules
LogicWhether high-priority risks override ordinary handling rules
CompletenessWhether missing materials and unjudgeable parts are pointed out
PermissionsWhether unauthorized commitments or system actions have been made

When information is insufficient, clearly stating "cannot judge, escalate to human" is also a correct outcome.

Four-Level Launch Model

Start with historical validation that never enters the real process; each level up requires meeting conditions, and human responsibilities adjust accordingly.

StageAI's permissionsHuman responsibilities
1 Historical validationAnalyzes historical anonymized data; does not enter the real processCompare against human results item by item
2 Suggestion modeGenerates classifications, risk alerts, and reply draftsReview and decide whether to adopt
3 Confirm to executePrepares content to write in allowed fieldsConfirm before anything is written or sent
4 Limited automationExecutes low-risk, recoverable, logged actionsMonitor anomalies and review periodically

Minimum Checks Before Launch

  • The data used has been authorized and properly anonymized.
  • The AI can only access materials the user themselves is authorized to access.
  • High-risk actions require human confirmation, and all key actions can be traced.
  • The business owner has confirmed the rule versions, exceptions, and responsible persons.
  • The team has agreed on how to pause on failure and how to restore the manual process.

Take a Real Task and Fill It In

When filling it in, first pick a real task; if sensitive materials are involved, only record where the materials are located and their authorization status—do not write raw data into the form.
