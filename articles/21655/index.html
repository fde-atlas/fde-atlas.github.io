Summary

This long-form Japanese guide by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, targets IT consultants at firms like Accenture considering going freelance. It reports monthly rate ranges (¥1.2–1.8M typical, ¥2M+ for strategy work), notes freelancers earn 1.3–2x their firm salaries, and warns the first 3–6 months carry the highest risk of project gaps. It covers pre-independence preparation (skill-sheet documentation, six months of savings, pipeline building), rate benchmarks by domain including an SAP boom driven by the 2027 ECC support deadline, AI-era positioning, and a three-layer sales strategy of multiple agents, direct end-client contracts, and long-term rate renegotiation cycles.

Key points

  • Freelance IT consultants typically earn 1.3–2x their consulting-firm take-home pay.
  • Monthly rates: ¥1.2–1.8M mainstream; strategy/business DX can exceed ¥2M.
  • SAP migration demand from the 2027 ECC deadline pushes rates to ¥2–2.5M for senior consultants.
  • Success hinges on preparation: documented personal track record, 6 months of savings, pre-secured client pipeline.
  • AI-era consultants win premium rates by translating vague client requests into architecture, requirements, and ROI estimates.

Conclusion: The three preparations you absolutely must make before going independent are: "① taking inventory of your individual track record and creating a skill sheet," "② securing cash flow and social credibility," and "③ pre-developing prospective clients and deal flow." By proceeding with these systematically starting 6 months before resignation, you can minimize the risk of losing projects immediately after going independent.

Conclusion: The first condition for success as an independent consultant is documenting "individual achievements that don't rely on your firm's brand." You need to articulate the projects from your firm days at the granularity of "what you personally did," and organize them into a skill sheet that agents and clients can evaluate.

Here is how to proceed with the inventory process concretely.

  • Project listing: List all projects you handled over the past 3-5 years in chronological order
  • Role clarification: Specify your role in each project, such as "PM," "design lead," or "implementation lead"
  • Quantifying results: Describe quantitative results, such as "reduced costs by XX billion yen" or "reduced work hours by XX hours"
  • Identifying deliverables: List documents you created, such as requirements definitions, proposals, and design documents
  • Frameworks applied: Note frameworks used, such as BPR, BPM, and Six Sigma
  • Technologies used: List your technology stack, such as SAP, Salesforce, and AWS
  • Client industry and scale: Describe the industry (e.g., finance, manufacturing) and revenue scale (within the bounds of confidentiality obligations)

What you especially need to be careful about is "describing within the bounds of confidentiality." Writing client names in full is not acceptable, but abstracted expressions like "a major financial institution (megabank class)" or "a listed manufacturer (revenue over 1 trillion yen)" are fine. In the agent industry, proving your track record with such an abstracted skill sheet is the standard accepted practice.

Conclusion: The first 3-6 months after going independent are when cash flow is tightest. Securing 6 months' worth of living expenses in cash and deposits, and applying for credit cards and a mortgage before going independent, are the keys to a stable life afterward.

The biggest point regarding social credibility is the reality that "after going independent, screening for credit cards and mortgages becomes stricter." Conclusion: To minimize the risk of losing projects after going independent, it is essential to start building relationships with agents and pre-developing prospective clients (past clients and internal connections) 3-6 months before resignation. If you "start sales from zero after going independent," there is a risk of a 3-6 month gap.

Here is how to move concretely in pre-development.

  • Agent meetings: Meet with 3-5 agents strong in upstream projects before resignation
  • Understanding your appropriate rate in advance: Hear from multiple agents about "your market value"
  • Adjusting your resignation timing: From agents: "There's a project starting in XX month, so plan your resignation backward from that"
  • Approaching former colleagues: Ask former colleagues who changed jobs about "project opportunities after going independent"
  • Maintaining client relationships: Maintain relationships within the bounds of confidentiality obligations
  • Building a personal brand via SNS and blogs: Start publishing before resignation
  • Speaking at industry events: Communicate your expertise externally
  • Trying small side jobs: Trial side projects within the scope permitted by your employer (be mindful of compliance)

In particular, we recommend starting "agent meetings" early, before resignation. If agents know you are "available from XX month," they will go out and find projects to match that timing, allowing you to bring the gap period as close to zero as possible. Conclusion: What is most in demand from IT consultants in the AI era is the ability to translate abstract client requests like "we want to introduce ChatGPT" or "we want to streamline operations with AI" into concrete architecture, requirements, and ROI estimates. This translation ability is the source of maximizing rate leverage.

Here is a typical interview framework for structuring abstract requests.

  • Business analysis: Which operations, which processes, and by how much do they want to improve efficiency
  • Data requirements: Types, volume, and quality of usable data
  • Accuracy requirements: Whether errors are tolerable or human review is mandatory
  • Cost estimation: Token billing, operating costs, development costs
  • Security requirements: Handling of confidential data, whether external APIs can be used
  • Governance requirements: Audit logs, bias countermeasures, accountability
  • Phased implementation plan: Roadmap from PoC → limited operation → full rollout

IT consultants who can immediately organize and present these perspectives are more likely to earn clients' trust that "leaving it to this person lowers the risk of failure."

Conclusion: IT consultants in the AI era can use generative AI not as an "enemy" but as a "powerful partner" to produce 2–3 times the output while keeping working hours the same. This in turn leads to "higher rates for the same number of working days."

Conclusion: A sales strategy that keeps high-rate projects flowing is built on a three-layer structure: "① using multiple high-class specialized agents," "② moving up the supply chain to direct contracts with end clients," and "③ cycling between long-term contracts and rate negotiations." Relying on a single agent and one-off projects will cause long-term rate growth to plateau.

Conclusion: The agents freelance IT consultants should use are not "general engineer-oriented" ones but those "specialized in consulting, PM/PMO, and upstream projects." The iron rule is to use multiple agents (3–5) in parallel to broaden project options and strengthen rate negotiation power.

Here are the criteria for selecting high-class specialized agents.

  • Quality of handled projects: Ratio of upstream consulting, strategy-related, and direct-under-CxO projects
  • Quality of clients: Transactions with enterprises, major SIs, and consulting firms
  • Industry knowledge of coordinators: Whether the coordinator has a consulting background or is a generic placement rep
  • Rate negotiation power: Track record of past rate increases, depth of negotiation support
  • Support for transitioning to direct contracts: Whether a relationship can be built even after the agent engagement ends
  • Specialty coverage: Abundance of projects in your specialty areas such as finance, SAP, and AI
  • Fee rate transparency: Disclosure of agent fees and margin structures

A portfolio of "placing weight on 2–3 agents and using 2 more as complements" offers an excellent balance between risk diversification and rate optimization. With a single exclusive agent, there is a risk that income stops the moment that agent fails to secure projects.

Conclusion: The ultimate strategy for maximizing a freelance IT consultant's rate is moving up the supply chain to "direct (prime-direct) contracts with end clients." By escaping the multi-layered subcontracting structure where 20–40% margins are deducted by agents and primary contractors, you can significantly increase take-home pay for the same number of working days.

Sales channels for winning direct end-client contracts are as follows.

  • Maintaining relationships with past clients: Regular contact even after project completion
  • Speaking at industry conferences: Building touchpoints with the CxO layer
  • Publishing books and blogs: Building a personal brand
  • Community participation: Joining CxO-oriented communities
  • Referral networks: Direct introductions from trusted acquaintances

Direct end-client contracts are not something you "build in a short period"; they are won as the result of personal brand investment over 5–10 year horizons. A realistic roadmap is to work mainly through agents right after going independent while building your personal brand in parallel.

Conclusion: To keep raising your rate continuously, it is important to become someone the client cannot let go of, extend contracts long-term, and establish a cycle of negotiating rate revisions every six months. Consultants who repeatedly leave after short stints risk having their rate reset with each new contract.

Let's organize the concrete actions needed to become an "indispensable presence."

  • Expanding business scope: Naturally extending into areas beyond the initial scope
  • Developing client internal talent: Taking on the role of "teacher" to support in-house development
  • Delving into clients' management issues: Providing management advice not limited to the IT domain
  • Continuously supplying industry knowledge: Regularly sharing other companies' case studies and industry trends
  • Handling emergencies: Proactively supporting during outages and troubles
  • Regularly presenting ROI summaries: Making "the benefits of hiring me" visible to management
  • Proposing the next theme: Suggesting the next project before the contract ends

In particular, the last item, "proposing the next theme," is an important sales activity that should be initiated three months before the contract ends. By presenting a roadmap such as "As the next step after the current project, we propose a project to solve your XX issue," you can transition seamlessly to the next contract without being dropped when the current one ends.
