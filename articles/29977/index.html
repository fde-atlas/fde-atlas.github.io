Job postings surge 800%! This year's hottest role in AI: Forward Deployed Engineer

①AI companies worldwide are currently racing to hire a rare breed of software developers—people who can both code and communicate with customers to accelerate the adoption of their cutting-edge AI technology. ②Reportedly, companies such as Anthropic, OpenAI, and Cohere are now recruiting so-called Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), currently the hottest new role in the generative AI enterprise space.

Cailianshe, November 3 (Editor Xiaoxiang) AI companies worldwide are currently racing to hire a rare breed of software developers—people who can both code and communicate with customers to accelerate the adoption of their cutting-edge AI technology.

Reportedly, companies such as Anthropic, OpenAI, and Cohere are now recruiting so-called Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), currently the hottest new role in the generative AI enterprise space. By deploying experts within enterprises to help customize AI models, these companies are working to generate more revenue.

Arnaud Fournier, head of Forward Deployed Engineers for Europe and the Middle East at OpenAI, said in an interview that the company formed its FDE team at the beginning of this year and expects to expand it to about 50 engineers by the end of 2025.

Anthropic, OpenAI's main competitor, also said that to meet customer demand, it will grow its Applied AI team—including FDEs and product engineers—fivefold this year.

Data from job platform Indeed shows that demand for hiring for these customer-facing AI roles surged in 2025. Between January and September this year, monthly job postings for FDE positions grew by more than 800%.

Why are FDEs on the rise?

Analysts say the rise in demand for Forward Deployed Engineers stems largely from the fact that companies across industries, from manufacturing to healthcare, are increasingly eager to adopt AI tools, yet often remain unsure how to use the technology and achieve a return on investment.

"A Fortune 500 bank's needs are often vastly different from those of a startup developing native AI products," noted Cat de Jong, head of Applied AI at Anthropic.

Nic Prettejohn, head of AI in the UK at data intelligence group Palantir, calls this model "internal product discovery"—"You need to build products that make customers exclaim, 'This is a game changer,'" he added.

Notably, if you trace the origins of the rise of the Forward Deployed Engineer role, it actually begins with Palantir, the currently red-hot AI data analytics company. Palantir, which provides AI data analytics platforms to the US Department of Defense and intelligence agencies, claims it pioneered the role nearly two decades ago; today, FDEs make up nearly half of its total workforce.

The concept originally came from the military—where soldiers are deployed forward to foreign countries. Palantir has sent FDEs to military bases in Afghanistan and Iraq, factory floors in the US Midwest, and oil refineries—the company typically dispatches two-person teams, internally codenamed "Echo" and "Delta": the former is responsible for understanding customer needs, while the latter has the technical ability to fulfill them.

Prettejohn said, "FDEs know that truly valuable software is not about elegant code or beautiful language... it only has value when it makes a real difference to the end customer."

Demand exceeds expectations

And right now, plenty of AI startups are undoubtedly trying to emulate Palantir's model. Aidan Gomez, co-founder and CEO of Cohere, pointed out that deploying engineers early in the customer signing process helps build lasting partnerships.

"We station engineers at the start of a project to ensure customers get solutions precisely matched to their needs, and then gradually scale down the team once the business is running stably," Gomez explained.

Although FDEs currently make up only a small share of AI teams, OpenAI says demand for the role has exceeded expectations.

OpenAI's FDE team once customized technical solutions for agricultural machinery manufacturer John Deere, helping develop more precise farming equipment—an effort that enabled farmers to reduce chemical spraying by 60% to 70%.

OpenAI's Fournier said, "We are gaining a deep understanding of the real needs of customers across different industries, and through joint experimentation and innovation, we turn these insights into momentum driving OpenAI's research and product development—all solutions are optimized based on real-world results."
