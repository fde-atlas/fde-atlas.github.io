How to Prepare for an FDE Interview: An 8-Week Plan

Rung · 2026-06-01 · 3 min read

Summary

Rung's guide argues that FDE interview prep differs from a standard SWE loop because the process is broader, and most candidates over-invest in algorithms while neglecting SQL, case structure, and communication. It proposes an 8-week plan: weeks 1-2 cover practical coding and SQL including window functions; weeks 3-4 cover cloud, deployment, and applied AI (RAG, agents, model serving); weeks 5-6 focus on integration debugging and consultant-style problem structuring; weeks 7-8 rehearse cases, STAR stories, and a full mock. Common mistakes include grinding hard graph problems, practicing silently, skipping the open-ended case round, and improvising behavioral answers. The core skill tested everywhere is scoping before solving and being presentable to a customer alone.

Key points

  • FDE loops are broader than SWE loops; allocate prep time by round weight
  • 8-week sequence: coding/SQL, cloud and applied AI, debugging, then cases and mocks
  • Frequent failures: algorithm over-grinding, silent practice, skipping case prep, weak STAR stories
  • Communication is graded in every round: narrate reasoning, state assumptions, name trade-offs
  • LeetCode alone is insufficient; the case round decides most offers

Preparing for a Forward Deployed Engineer interview is different from grinding a standard SWE loop, because the loop itself is broader. The key is to spread your effort across every round in proportion to how it is weighted, most candidates over-invest in algorithms and under-invest in SQL, case structure, and communication.

A structured 8-week approach

You do not need eight weeks of full days, a few short, focused sessions daily beats cramming. A sensible sequence builds fundamentals first, then the FDE-specific rounds.

Weeks 1 to 2: practical coding and SQL

Build fluency with data wrangling in a real editor, then move to SQL, joins, GROUP BY, and window functions in a live sandbox.

Weeks 3 to 4: cloud, deployment, and applied AI

Learn enough cloud and deployment vocabulary to reason about enterprise integration, plus applied-AI basics (RAG, agents, model serving).

Weeks 5 to 6: integration, debugging, and the consulting mindset

Practice tracing failures in unfamiliar environments and structuring problems the way a consultant would.

Weeks 7 to 8: cases, behavioral, and a mock

Rehearse the case round out loud with a framework, prepare STAR stories, and run a full mock before the real loop.

Common mistakes to avoid

Most preparation failures are not about effort, they are about aiming it at the wrong round. A few recur constantly.

Over-indexing on hard algorithms

The coding round is practical and it is only one part of the loop. Grinding Hard graph problems while skipping SQL and case practice is the classic misallocation.

Practicing silently

Every FDE round grades communication. If you only solve problems in your head, you will freeze when asked to narrate. Rehearse out loud, ideally to another person.

Skipping the open-ended round

Candidates avoid case practice because it is uncomfortable and unscored on most platforms. It is also where the most offers are won and lost, so do not leave it for the night before.

Neglecting your own stories

The behavioral and customer-judgment questions are predictable. Have three or four specific STAR stories ready so you are not inventing them live.

Prepare in the right proportion

The meta-skill graded across every round is the same: do you scope before you solve, and can an interviewer picture putting you in front of a customer alone? Practice narrating your reasoning, stating assumptions, and naming trade-offs, those habits transfer to coding, SQL, design, and case rounds alike.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to prepare for an FDE interview?

Most working engineers do well with a focused 6 to 8 week plan of short daily sessions across coding, SQL, systems and deployment, applied AI, and case practice, rather than cramming one area.

What should I study for a Forward Deployed Engineer interview?

Practical coding and data wrangling, SQL (especially window functions), cloud and deployment basics, applied-AI vocabulary (RAG, agents, model serving), open-ended case structure, and behavioral / client-simulation practice.

Is LeetCode enough to prepare for an FDE interview?

No. LeetCode helps only the coding round, which is a smaller and more practical part of an FDE loop. You also need SQL, systems and deployment, applied AI, and, most importantly, case and communication practice.

Can I prepare for an FDE interview in two weeks?

You can make meaningful progress in two weeks if you focus: prioritize SQL (window functions), practical coding, and rehearsing one case out loud, and skip the areas the specific role weights least. A longer runway helps, but focused, spoken practice beats a longer passive grind.
