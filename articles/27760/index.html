Today's Judgment

Today's three signals sit at three different evidence layers of enterprise AI industrialization: the National Data Administration proves supply-side scale and new business formats, United Water and Tencent Cloud show a route for co-building industry knowledge, and OpenAI's Brazil expansion demonstrates the next step in regional adoption.

Together they show that "enterprise AI is becoming an industry" — but each also stops at its own evidence layer: industry statistics are not enterprise adoption, strategic signings are not projects going live, and seat growth is not customer ROI. The value of the FDE is precisely to keep compressing macro growth, strategic signings, and organizational adoption down to concrete tasks, production acceptance, and business outcomes.

Key Signals

National Data Administration: Data industry reaches RMB 6.78 trillion in 2025, with token services and agents listed as new business formats

  • Source: National Data Administration | Original link
  • On August 28, at the China International Big Data Industry Expo, the National Institute of Data Development released the "China Data Industry Development Report (2026)": in 2025, China's data industry reached RMB 6.78 trillion in scale, up 15.7% year over year, with 482,000 data companies. Officially, token services and agent innovation were listed as new business formats to accelerate, with the judgment that AI's demand for data will keep growing and synthetic data will develop faster.
  • Why it matters: For the first time, the official framing lists "token services" and "agent innovation" side by side as nationally recognized new business formats — another signal, after "Token loans," that the token economy has entered policy discourse. But when citing it, the boundary must be held: RMB 6.78 trillion is the total scale of the data industry, not the enterprise AI, token services, or FDE market size — directly repurposing big numbers as your own segment's market size is the most common misdirection in industry analysis. The practical implication for FDEs: growth in model calls will push enterprise-side bottlenecks further toward data availability, licensing, quality, and results write-back — data supply must become task-oriented, acceptance-testable services, and the engineering in between is exactly the FDE's work.

United Water × Tencent Cloud: Writing water-utility expertise, Skills, and a data foundation into a standard-product co-build roadmap

  • Source: Securities Times | Original link
  • On August 27, United Water (603291) signed a strategic cooperation agreement with Tencent Cloud: the plan is to consolidate operational data, process expertise, and expert knowledge into knowledge bases, encapsulate a water-utility digital assistant with Skills and expert capabilities; unify governance of the group's business system data, advance intelligent dosing, optimized scheduling, and smart inspection, and turn the co-built results into a standardized product for the national water industry, while exploring overseas promotion.
  • Why it matters: This is a textbook industry FDE route — first define production tasks, then consolidate expert rules and data objects, integrate with core systems, and only then talk about cross-plant replication and productization. The real object of standardization is not the interface, but knowledge, Skills, APIs, evals, and acceptance packages. Such partnerships should be tracked using the "four-level evidence ladder for industry agents": strategic signing → first-scenario pilot → production acceptance → cross-organization replication — currently at level one; the remaining three levels are the real story to watch. Note: this is currently a signing and a plan, with no data yet on first plants, task success rates, or employee adoption.

OpenAI sets up commercial team in Brazil: enterprise seats grow 5x year over year

  • Source: OpenAI official | Original link
  • On August 27, OpenAI announced the launch of commercial operations in São Paulo: ChatGPT Enterprise seats in Brazil grew 5x year over year, entering OpenAI's top ten markets by number of business customers. The local team's mandate: help organizations select use cases, prepare people, establish governance, and scale effective practices.
  • Why it matters: A regional market moving from organic growth to establishing local commercial and delivery teams shows that the next step in enterprise adoption is not selling accounts, but use-case selection, organizational readiness, governance, and scaled replication — the four mandates OpenAI gave its local team are exactly an FDE's work list. Leading model vendors are proving with their own org structures: models reach globally, but deployment must be local. The same logic applies to Chinese vendors' moats — local relationships and on-the-ground industry capability form a delivery layer that APIs cannot replace. Note: seat growth is vendor-reported, with no absolute figures or customer ROI disclosed.

Takeaways for FDE Practitioners

  • Build "citation boundary cards" for macro numbers: total data industry scale ≠ enterprise AI market ≠ FDE market. Writing the scope clearly in proposals and presentations is a direct marker of professionalism — once a client spots number repurposing, trust is hard to repair.
  • Strategic signings are the start of the evidence chain, not the end: once knowledge bases, Skills, and industry brains are written into agreements, the next questions remain: which scenario runs first, who signs off, how metrics change, and whether the second organization can replicate faster. Use the four-level evidence ladder to track your own and your peers' projects.
  • "Turning industry knowledge into Skills" requires an asset card: expert experience, applicable operating conditions, input data, system actions, risk boundaries, human confirmation, test sets, and version owners — only when all eight elements are in place does industry knowledge turn from "a person's experience" into "a replicable asset."
