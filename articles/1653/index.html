FDE vs Solutions Engineer, Sales & Applied AI Engineer

FDE Interviews · 2026-09-17 · 2 min read

Summary

This article maps the Forward Deployed Engineer against adjacent roles using two axes: position in the customer lifecycle and how much production code the role writes. Solutions engineers and sales engineers operate pre-sale or at the sale boundary, focusing on demos, PoCs, and design, with light coding. The FDE works post-sale in production, codes most of the week, and owns the outcome. Applied AI Engineer is essentially the same job under a different title — FDE titles stress deployment depth, Applied AI titles stress evals. Customer success engineers protect accounts after go-live, while FDEs accelerate reaching production. Unlike consultants doing one-off installs, FDEs feed field learnings back into the product. The advice: judge job postings by responsibilities, not titles.

How the Forward Deployed Engineer role differs from solutions engineers, sales engineers, applied AI engineers, customer success engineers, and consultants — by lifecycle stage, mandate, and how much you code.

The one distinction that explains all the others

Every adjacent role can be placed on two axes: where it sits in the customer lifecycle, and how much production code it writes. The FDE is the role that sits post-sale, in production, and codes most of the week while owning the outcome. Hold that fixed and the rest fall into place.

FDE vs Solutions Engineer / Solutions Architect

Solutions engineers and architects live mostly in the late pre-sale and early post-sale window. Their mandate is to map product capabilities to a customer's requirements and design the solution — demos, proofs-of-concept, advisory. They code, but mainly for PoCs, and they're rewarded for design and advice rather than operational ownership. The FDE builds what doesn't exist yet and keeps it running; the SE sells and designs the vision of what could exist.

FDE vs Sales Engineer

A sales engineer is pre-sale and sales-first: provide the technical validation that closes the deal, then move to the next opportunity. Coding is light. The FDE picks up after the deal and is measured on whether the thing actually works in production months later.

FDE vs Applied AI Engineer

These are largely the same role under different titles. "Forward Deployed Engineer" (Palantir, OpenAI) tends to emphasize integration and deployment depth; "Applied AI Engineer" (Anthropic and many AI startups) tends to emphasize AI quality and evaluation rigor. Both embed with customers and ship production AI. If you're choosing between postings, read the responsibilities, not the title.

FDE vs Customer Success Engineer & Consultant

A customer success / customer engineer works post-go-live to protect and expand the account — onboarding, troubleshooting, ongoing support. The framing that sticks: FDEs accelerate getting to production; CS engineers protect and expand once you're there.

A professional-services consultant installs and configures software and makes one-off recommendations, often independent of product engineering. The FDE works with a customer long-term and routes insights back into the product, so a field solution can become a feature the whole user base benefits from — the opposite of a one-off customization.

Put this into practice

  • Evaluate the actual job description → Ask about implementation, account capacity, operating duties and who can authorize commitments.
  • Plan a transition from your current work → Find the evidence you already have and the gaps that need practical work.

FAQ

A solutions engineer designs and advises, mostly around the sale, and codes mainly for PoCs. An FDE works post-sale, writes production code most of the week, and owns whether the deployment actually works.

Largely yes — they're the same role under different titles. FDE titles lean toward integration/deployment depth; Applied AI Engineer titles lean toward AI quality and eval rigor. Read the responsibilities rather than the title.
