FDE representatives from across the country gather for the first time in Marina Bay! Translating AI into productivity that enterprises can understand

As AI moves from a technical concept to the industrial frontline, an emerging role known as the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is becoming the key force in bridging the "last mile" of implementation. On August 5, the national FDE Expert Exchange Conference was held at the National AI Application Pilot Testing Base (hereinafter the "Pilot Testing Base") in Dongguan's Binhai Bay New Area, attracting more than 50 industry representatives and establishing the first cross-regional collaboration and exchange platform for this emerging professional group.

The event was guided by the Economic, Technology and Finance Bureau of Dongguan Binhai Bay New Area, hosted by Shengen Ningji (Dongguan) Artificial Intelligence Technology Co., Ltd., and organized by HA7CH (the FDE community and incubator). The conference focused on policy and base resource introductions, real case sharing, industry methodology discussions, and the joint building of collaboration mechanisms. Through their hands-on experience, the experts explored one core question: how to make AI truly enter factories, workshops, and real production processes. Notably, this conference was one of a series of implementation activities under the "Smart Binhai Bay · AI Future" AI full-scenario literacy enhancement training program, and the first national FDE industry exchange conference.

What is an FDE? The "translator" that bridges the "last mile" of AI implementation

The FDE model was first scaled up by the American big data analytics company Palantir, whose core approach is to station engineers on-site at customers, embedding standardized products into enterprises' existing systems and workflows. Amid the wave of AI implementation, this model has been replicated by top global AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic. A LinkedIn report released in 2026 showed that between 2023 and 2025, 1.3 million new AI-related jobs were created worldwide, with demand for FDEs growing 42-fold.

Wu Mingze, head of HA7CH, offered a plain-language explanation of this emerging profession: "What a Forward Deployed Engineer does is complete the last mile of AI implementation—bringing AI out of large models and into real industries, enabling it to empower every process of an enterprise." With a master's degree in Design Engineering from Harvard and research assistant experience at Stanford's AI Lab, and having worked as an engineer at Alibaba, Tencent, and MiniMax, he began working in FDE-related roles this April.

On the relationship between FDEs and the more familiar OPC (One Person Company), Wu Mingze noted that both major AI vendors and individual OPCs are currently working on AI implementation and efficiency gains, but there is a clear market segmentation: FDEs at large AI vendors mainly serve large enterprises such as banks and state-owned enterprises, while individual OPCs are more flexible and excel at serving small and medium-sized enterprises.

In discussions among the attendees, the FDE role was given multiple interpretations—the "connector" between technology and business, the "translator" who goes deep into the frontline, and the "deliverer" who provides highly customized services at low cost. Though the emphasis varied, the consensus was clear: the core value of an FDE lies in entering the enterprise's site and turning vague business needs into implementable technical solutions.

On-site report: FDE hands-on experts reach a consensus—manufacturing is the "main battlefield" for AI implementation

At the conference, nine core speakers from different fields including industrial manufacturing, construction engineering, and cross-border e-commerce gave in-depth presentations and discussions.

The first half centered on the theme "Why enterprises need FDEs." Drawing on their practices in industrial manufacturing, construction engineering, cross-border e-commerce, and other fields, the speakers used real cases to demonstrate the "translation" value of FDEs—converting enterprises' vague demands for "cost reduction and efficiency improvement" into implementable technical solutions. Once enterprises saw the efficiency gains with their own eyes, their attitude shifted from initial wait-and-see to actively seeking more AI transformation opportunities. What FDEs bring is not just single-point efficiency gains, but systematic optimization of how enterprises operate.

The second half centered on the theme "How to become an FDE," with speakers exchanging views on capability building and growth paths for FDEs. They shared observations from the dimensions of industry ecosystem, technology transfer, and scenario implementation: an FDE must understand technology, but also business and communication; must be able to break down complex problems, and also design executable solutions. The value of an FDE lies in helping enterprises find the real problems and then matching the corresponding capabilities to solve them—this is the key role FDEs will continue to play in the future.

Compared with industries such as finance and the internet, manufacturing—with its thin margins and difficult transformation—is precisely the "main battlefield" where AI implementation offers the greatest room for efficiency gains and where FDEs can best demonstrate their value.

Positioned at a national opportunity: Binhai Bay unveils its "trump-card toolbox" for AI empowerment

Recently, some cities have issued policies that write FDEs and OPCs into official government documents and provide all-around support. This move marks that FDEs have entered the policy spotlight, becoming a key link in driving AI industry implementation.

In the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area, a practice of deep integration between FDEs and manufacturing is unfolding in Binhai Bay New Area. At the conference, an official from the New Area gave a special presentation on the innovation and entrepreneurship ecosystem, stating that Dongguan, a manufacturing capital renowned worldwide, is home to about 223,000 manufacturing enterprises, more than 14,000 industrial enterprises above designated size, and over 10,000 high-tech enterprises. This wealth of manufacturing enterprises provides a continuous stream of real scenarios and validation needs for AI implementation. These industrial scenarios, flowing with real data and real needs, are the "best testing ground" for AI implementation in manufacturing.

Wu Mingze has first-hand experience of this: "FDEs must go into enterprises. Binhai Bay New Area is backed by Dongguan's strong hinterland resources, with a great many manufacturing scenarios, a good environment, and proximity to the airport." In his view, this is both the center of manufacturing demand and a hub for FDE implementation.

This national FDE representatives exchange conference is a key step for Binhai Bay in promoting AI empowerment of manufacturing. Taking this as an opportunity, Binhai Bay is accelerating the connection of national expert resources with local industrial scenarios, converting technical capabilities into tangible productivity and injecting new momentum into the intelligent manufacturing upgrade of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area.
