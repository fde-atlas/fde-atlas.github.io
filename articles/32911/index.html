Forward deployed engineer roles by industry

Kernavique · 2026-11-20 · 21 min read

Summary

Kernavique reviewed forward deployed engineer job ads and public job-board feeds read on November 20, 2026, mapping which industries these roles serve. Government and defense showed the strongest evidence, led by Palantir with 28 of 48 industry-titled US postings. Healthcare and financial services followed, with employers including Tennr, Databricks, OpenAI, Rogo and Harvey. Insurance, legal and manufacturing had moderate evidence; energy and retail had little. Most ads name no industry at all—a third-party analysis found 79% unspecified. The article distinguishes three ways ads mention industries and notes that requirements center on clearances and citizenship rather than domain knowledge.

Key points

  • Government/defense leads: 48 titled postings, Palantir alone had 28.
  • Healthcare and finance follow, with Tennr, Databricks, OpenAI, Rogo and Harvey hiring.
  • Insurance, legal and manufacturing evidence is moderate; energy and retail is thin.
  • 79% of postings in a third-party analysis named no customer industry.
  • Ads demand clearances and citizenship more than domain expertise; pay ranges vary widely.

How to read the table:

  • Ads opened and read means we opened the ad and its text or title names the industry. An ad that names several industries is counted under each.
  • The feed count comes from 177 public Greenhouse, Ashby and Lever job board feeds read the same day. They held 298 US postings with “forward deployed” in the title, from 65 employers. The count matches words in the title only. Employers that post one title per city have more postings than distinct jobs. Amazon, ServiceNow and Deloitte are outside those feeds. Our method page explains the feeds.
  • The evidence labels are the ones our research notes gave each industry. “Strong” means several employers and readable ads. It does not mean the industry is large.

Many ads name no industry. Bloomberry, a third party, analyzed 1,000 postings (article updated January 25, 2026) and reported that 79% did not specify the customer industries. Among the 21% that did, it reported Financial Services/Banking at 24%, Government/Defense at 18%, Healthcare/Life Sciences at 17%, Insurance at 17% and Energy/Utilities at 13%. Our own reading points the same way: at least 11 of 34 employer ads we read for travel and work pattern named no customer industry.

Three kinds of ad name an industry. The difference matters when you decide where domain experience counts.

  • The employer serves one industry. Tennr in healthcare, FurtherAI in insurance, Eudia in legal.
  • A general vendor posts a role titled for one industry. Databricks, OpenAI and Palantir do this.
  • The ad lists several industries in one sentence. Anthropic, Scale AI, Turing and Domino Data Lab do this. Here the industry is context, not a requirement.

For the wider list of employers, see who is hiring forward deployed engineers.

Government and defense

This is the industry with the most evidence, and we cover it in full elsewhere. Our cleared roles briefing reads eleven ads from Palantir, Scale AI, Vannevar Labs, Dataminr and Anduril, quotes their clearance wording and sets out how official sources say a clearance is obtained. We do not repeat it here.

In the feed count, 48 US postings had a government word in the title: public sector, government, federal, gov, defense, intel or clearance. Palantir had 28, Anduril 6, Databricks 6, C3 AI 2, Scale AI 2, and Cognition, Cursor, Deepgram and OpenAI one each.

Seven more ads add detail beyond clearance.

  • C3 AI, Forward Deployed Engineer, Federal (Onsite - Lexington Park, MD). The ad says the work is full time on-site in a “secure government facility”. It requires US citizenship, an active Secret clearance and the ability to obtain further program access.
  • C3 AI, Forward Deployed Engineer - Federal, New Grad 2027. The role works directly with US federal customers. Experience with the federal government, especially the Department of Defense, is listed as preferred, not required. The posting shows $120,000 to $150,000 under the label “Virginia Base Pay Range”.
  • Deloitte, Palantir Associate Forward Deployed Engineer - GPS. The clients are federal, state and local government and public higher education institutions.
  • Accenture, Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer. The ad covers government and other clients (our paraphrase). It publishes a pay range for each of 14 jurisdictions and does not call any of them base: for the District of Columbia, $68,000 to $216,300, and for Virginia, $59,100 to $216,300. The page was still up but carried an end date of October 15, 2026.
  • Scale AI, Frontier Agents Engineer (Applied AI). The ad names US government agencies, including the Army and Air Force, among its customers.
  • Guidehouse, Forward Deployed AI Engineer. The hire must obtain a Federal Public Trust, which is not a security clearance, and US citizenship is required by contract. The annual salary range is $113,000 to $188,000, not stated as base.
  • Uare.ai, Forward Deployed Engineer (U.S. Government / IL5). US citizenship is required and an active clearance is a plus. The work is in person five days a week. Base pay is $130,000 to $170,000.

What the ads ask for. The constraints are about access, not subject knowledge: a clearance or eligibility for one, citizenship, and work inside a customer site. Only C3 AI’s new graduate ad, among those above, lists government experience itself, and only as preferred.

Where the roles are. In the feed count, the Washington DC area had 42 forward deployed postings from 10 employers, and 34 of them mentioned a clearance. Our Washington DC area page has the detail.

The Deloitte and Accenture roles are Palantir partner roles. Our briefing on Palantir Foundry forward deployed engineers covers that group.

Healthcare and life sciences

Two employers we read sell to healthcare and say so plainly.

  • Tennr, Forward Deployed Engineer (Post-Sales) and Enterprise Forward Deployed Engineer (Post-Sales). The customers are large health systems, national provider groups and enterprise partners (our paraphrase).
  • Infinitus, Forward Deployed Product Manager. The ad speaks of solving “real healthcare challenges”. It is a product manager role that works beside forward deployed engineers, not an engineer role.

Four more name healthcare in a list of industries.

  • Domino Data Lab, Forward Deployed Engineer, Intern. The ad describes “high-stakes environments” across healthcare, life sciences, finance and government, and names Johnson & Johnson, GSK and Bristol Myers among its customers.
  • Turing, Forward Deployed AI Engineer. The ad says Turing works with Fortune 500 enterprises across financial services, life sciences, healthcare, retail, automotive and CPG.
  • Anthropic, Staff Forward Deployed Engineer. A background in financial services, healthcare or life sciences, or another enterprise vertical, is listed as “nice to have”. Anthropic’s Applied AI Engineer, Commercial ad also lists healthcare among its customer industries.
  • Scale AI, Frontier Agents Engineer (Forward Deployed Engineering). The ad lists finance, healthcare, manufacturing, media, telecommunications and government.

Titles we saw but did not open. In the feed count, 14 US postings had healthcare, health or life sciences in the title: Databricks 7, OpenAI 5 and Palantir 2. The titles were “Sr. Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) - Healthcare & Life Sciences” at Databricks, “Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), Healthcare” at OpenAI in New York, San Francisco and Seattle, and “Forward Deployed Software Engineer - US Government - Federal Health and Civilian” at Palantir. Sierra’s careers page lists a “Software Engineer, Agent - Healthcare” role. Assort Health and Commure each list a forward deployed role; we did not open those ads.

What the ads say about domain knowledge. Less than you might expect. Tennr names the kind of customer. Anthropic welcomes the background without requiring it. Palantir’s Federal Health and Civilian posting sits under its US government line, and the feed record shows the same Top Secret wording as its other government ads: an active clearance or willingness to obtain one. Our reads did not return wording about health-privacy rules or clinical systems in any of these ads. That is a limit of the read, not proof the ads are silent.

Financial services

Our research notes rate this industry strong. Read the label with care: most of the finance-specific evidence is job titles and pay fields, and few full ads.

Ads we opened.

  • Turing, Forward Deployed AI Engineer. Financial services is the first industry in the sentence quoted above. A separate Turing ad with the same title in Los Angeles shows base pay of $200,000 to $250,000 plus equity. We read the industry sentence in the New York ad.
  • Anthropic, Applied AI Engineer, Commercial. Customers are in software, financial services, healthcare, retail, manufacturing and professional services. The ad shows “Annual Salary” of $280,000 to $320,000. The Staff Forward Deployed Engineer ad lists a financial services background as “nice to have”.
  • Scale AI and Domino Data Lab. Both list finance among several industries. Domino names UBS and FINRA among its customers.
  • Carlyle, Forward Deployed Engineer, Client Solutions Group. Carlyle is a private-equity firm, not a software vendor, and it advertises the role in its own name. The ad shows an anticipated base salary range of $160,000 to $190,000 for New York, with four days a week in the office.
  • A contract posting on Dice. A third-party firm, IT Trailblazers, posted a contract role titled Forward Deployed Engineer - New York. It describes a “regulated financial-services environment” and work with a bank’s fixed-income teams. The end client is not named, so treat it as weaker evidence than an employer’s own ad.

Outside job ads, AWS’s announcement of its forward deployed AI engineers names “regulated industries, financial services, and government” as where the group works.

Titles in the feed count. 23 US postings had a finance word in the title: Databricks 9, Rogo 7, Harvey 3, OpenAI 2, Coinbase 1 and Cursor 1. The word list is wide. It includes finance, financial, banker, credit, investor, equity, buyout, real estate, wealth, asset, trading and banking.

Insurance, legal, manufacturing, energy and retail

The evidence here is thinner. Our research notes rate insurance, legal and manufacturing moderate, and energy and retail thin. What follows is all of it.

Manufacturing and industrial: four ads, three employers.

  • Palantir’s Warp Speed internship ad is the clearest. It ties the program to manufacturing and the American industrial base, and says its engineers work with customers “from the factory floor to the board room”. A New Grad - Warp Speed posting in New York shows an estimated salary range of $135,000 to $145,000 a year in the feed record.
  • Palantir’s Deployment Strategist ad mentions manufacturing in a list of customer outcomes.
  • Scale AI and Anthropic each list manufacturing among several industries.
  • Databricks has a “Sr. Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) - Manufacturing” title in the feed count. We did not open it.

Insurance: one ad.

  • FurtherAI, read on Y Combinator’s job board, says it builds “AI Teammates” to automate insurance workflows.
  • Federato’s board had no forward deployed title among the 11 jobs we read. One Technical Program Manager role sits in a department named “Forward Deployed Engineering”.
  • WithCoverage advertises a Forward Deployed Engineer, Growth role in New York with an expected base pay range of $140,000 to $170,000 a year. Our notes file it under insurance, but we did not capture the ad’s industry wording, so we do not count it.
  • Sierra lists a “Software Engineer, Agent - Insurance” title. We did not open it.

Legal: one ad, and it is for a lawyer.

  • Eudia’s Forward Deployed Lawyer ad describes AI for legal work with “Fortune 500 legal teams”, and names Cargill, Coherent, DHL and Duracell as customers. The title asks for the profession, not for an engineer.
  • Harvey and Legora each list forward deployed roles in New York, and OpenAI has a “Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), Legal-SF” title. We did not open those ads.

Energy and utilities: no live ad.

  • We read no live employer ad that names energy or utilities as the customer industry.
  • The one ad we found is a copy of a closed Texture ad on an investor’s job board, posted June 13, 2026. Texture describes its product as an operating system for “the energy grid”. Its customers are small, member-owned electric utilities and the makers of the energy hardware they buy.
  • Palantir’s ads mention power grids as an example outcome, not as a customer sector. Nira Energy’s board showed no forward deployed title among the 4 jobs we read.

Retail and consumer: two ads, both lists.

  • Turing and Anthropic each name retail among several industries.
  • Sierra lists a “Software Engineer, Agent - Retail” title, and Databricks a “Sr. Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) - Retail” title. We opened neither.
  • Cresta’s Senior Forward Deployed Engineer (AI Agent) ad gives no sector label. It names United Airlines, Cox Communications and Marriott as customers.

Named once. Telecommunications and media (Scale AI, and a Databricks title covering communications, media, entertainment and games). Travel and hospitality (a Sierra title, and Cresta’s customers). Automotive and CPG (Turing). Professional services (Anthropic).

A note on two employers. All 31 feed postings with a communications, media, retail, manufacturing or digital native word in the title came from Databricks. One employer’s naming habit can look like an industry trend. Sierra splits its “Software Engineer, Agent” role across financial services, healthcare, insurance, public sector, retail and other verticals. Those titles do not say forward deployed; our briefing on the forward deployed AI engineer covers that neighboring title.

How to use domain experience in an application

This section is our reading of the ads above. No employer told us how it weighs domain experience.

  • Work out which kind of ad you are reading. At a single-industry employer such as Tennr or FurtherAI, every customer is in your field. In a vertical-titled role at Databricks or OpenAI, the field is in the title. In a list-of-industries ad, your field is one of several and may not shape the work.
  • Treat domain experience as an addition. Anthropic calls it “nice to have”. C3 AI lists federal experience as preferred. No ad we read says it stands in for engineering. The core requirement is on our forward deployed engineer role page.
  • Say what you worked on, in the ad’s terms. If the ad names health systems and provider groups, name the kind of organization you served. If it names a bank’s fixed-income teams, say which side of a bank you worked with.
  • Lead with hard conditions where they exist. For government roles, state your clearance level and citizenship at the top. Those lines decide whether the application is read.
  • Notice when the title asks for the profession. Forward Deployed Lawyer, Forward Deployed Banker and Forward Deployed Investor are not engineer titles. If you are the practitioner and not the engineer, those may be the closer fit. We read only the Eudia ad in full.
  • Check the pay basis before comparing. Harvey’s figure is labeled on-target earnings. Palantir’s is an estimate that is not called base. Databricks and Carlyle say base.

Engineers with experience in one of these industries can send Kernavique their details and résumé, and say which industry it is.

What a company in the industry should put in the ad

This is also our reading. It follows from what made some ads easy to place by industry and others impossible.

  • Name the industry and the customer. Tennr does it in one phrase. Bloomberry’s analysis found that 79% of postings did not. An engineer with ten years in your field cannot tell that the role is for them unless the ad says so.
  • Say whether domain experience is required or welcome. Anthropic and C3 AI both make the status clear. An ad that lists an industry without saying which leaves the reader to guess.
  • State hard conditions in plain words. C3 AI’s Lexington Park ad gives citizenship, clearance level and on-site work in a few lines. Guidehouse names the Public Trust requirement. If your industry has an equivalent condition, such as a background check or work only at the customer’s site, put it in the ad.
  • Describe the constraint, not only the sector. The contract posting we read says “regulated” and stops there. Engineers who know the field will want to know what that means for the work: what data they may touch, where the system must run and who signs off.
  • Publish pay with its basis. Say whether the figure is base, salary or on-target earnings, and which location it covers.
  • Choose the title with care. If the role is for a practitioner, say so, as Eudia and Rogo do. If it is for an engineer who will learn the field, say that.

In practice

For an engineer: your domain experience counts most where the ad names one industry, and least where it lists six. Sort the ads into those three kinds first, then check hard conditions, then pay basis. For a company: the ads we read rarely say what makes the industry hard to work in. One sentence on the customer and one on the constraint would set an ad apart from most of our sample. For both: government is the only industry here with enough readable ads to compare employers. For the rest, read each ad on its own terms.

What we left out

  • The full text of most vertical-titled ads. Databricks, OpenAI, Rogo, Harvey, Legora and Cursor postings were read as titles and pay fields in the feeds. We did not open the ads, so we do not describe their requirements.
  • OpenAI’s pay as shown on its own ad pages. The pages could not be read. The $185K – $300K figures are from the feed’s pay field.
  • Sierra’s vertical roles. Titles were read on its careers page. The ads were not opened, and the titles do not say forward deployed.
  • Clearance detail. It is in the cleared roles briefing.
  • Ads from Amazon, ServiceNow and every employer on Workday in the title counts. They are outside the 177 feeds.
  • Insurance and energy ads we could not open. Comulate, Sixfold, Gridsight, Singularity Energy and others appeared only in search results or returned errors.
  • WithCoverage’s industry wording, which we did not capture.
  • Any regulation by name. Our reads did not return wording about specific health, banking or insurance rules, so we name none. The reading tool shortens long passages, and an ad may say more than we saw.
  • Whether domain experience raises pay. Nothing we read measures it.
  • Industry sizes or growth. The feed counts are a convenience sample of mostly AI and technology employers. They do not measure any industry.

Questions

  • Which industries hire forward deployed engineers?
  • In ads and job titles read on November 20, 2026, government and defense, healthcare and life sciences, and financial services were the three industries named most often. Insurance, legal and manufacturing appeared in a handful of ads, and energy and retail in very few. Many ads name no industry at all, so this describes the ads we read and not the whole market.
  • Do I need industry experience to get a forward deployed engineer role?
  • The ads we read differ. Anthropic’s Staff Forward Deployed Engineer ad lists a background in financial services, healthcare or life sciences as “nice to have”, and C3 AI lists federal government experience as preferred. Government roles add hard conditions such as a clearance or US citizenship. No ad we read says domain experience replaces the engineering requirement.
  • Which employers hire forward deployed engineers for healthcare?
  • Tennr and Infinitus sell to healthcare organizations and say so in their ads. Databricks, OpenAI and Palantir each had postings with healthcare, health or life sciences in the title in our feed read of November 20, 2026. Domino Data Lab, Turing, Anthropic and Scale AI name healthcare in a list of several industries.
  • Which employers hire forward deployed engineers for financial services?
  • In our feed read of November 20, 2026, 23 US postings had a finance word in the title, from Databricks, Rogo, Harvey, OpenAI, Coinbase and Cursor. Turing, Anthropic, Scale AI and Domino Data Lab name financial services or finance among several industries. Carlyle, a private-equity firm, advertises the role in its own name. We read most of the finance-titled postings as titles and pay fields, not full ads.
  • Do industry-specific forward deployed engineer roles pay more?
  • Our sources cannot answer that. No ad we read states extra pay for domain experience, and the published ranges differ by employer, level, location and pay basis. Databricks shows the same range, $182,000 to $250,208, on its healthcare and financial services postings as on its general one. Compare ads on a like-for-like basis before drawing a conclusion.
  • What should a company in a regulated industry put in the ad?
  • In our reading, four things: the industry and the kind of customer, whether domain experience is required or only welcome, any hard condition such as a clearance, citizenship or work inside a secure site, and pay with its basis. The ads that were easiest to place by industry did the first of these in one sentence. This is our reading of the ads, not legal advice.
