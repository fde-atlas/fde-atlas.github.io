FDEs Are Everywhere: What B2B Leaders Should Expect

Forrester · 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Summary

This Forrester piece examines the rapid spread of forward deployed engineers (FDEs) and adjacent titles like marketing and customer success engineers in B2B AI. It argues that responsibilities vary widely across vendors, so buyers must distinguish genuine new capabilities from rebranded services. Effective FDEs pair technical skill with business understanding. Jasper's model pairs engineers with marketing practitioners, and a June 2026 Forrester survey found 53% of enterprise marketing leaders rely on technology vendors for content solution adoption, ahead of consultancies (35%), agencies (32%), and SIs (18%). The article flags misaligned incentives, blurred accountability across teams, and asset ownership risks, advising leaders to match expertise to work, define success in business terms, and build internal capabilities to sustain results.

New Titles Blur The Lines Between Engineering And Business Expertise

Forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) are everywhere, along with a growing collection of related titles, including marketing engineers, content engineers, and customer success engineers. But the titles don’t always tell buyers what expertise they’re getting.

Some vendors use FDEs to describe highly skilled engineers who work directly with customers to design, build, and deploy AI solutions. The responsibilities vary considerably across vendors, however, from hands-on software engineering and workflow development to technical advisory, implementation, and adoption support. These variations make it important to distinguish new capabilities from familiar services with new titles.

The distinction matters. Effective FDEs combine technical expertise with an understanding of how businesses operate, helping customers translate complex processes and requirements into working AI solutions. Adding “engineer” to a job title without changing the responsibilities or required skills creates confusion for customers and employees.

AI Vendors Expand Services To Help GTM Teams Get Results

AI vendors are investing in expertise to help customers turn technology into working business processes. As our colleague Ashutosh Sharma explains, FDEs close the gap between deploying AI technology and achieving measurable business results.

Jasper offers an example for marketers. The company is expanding its forward-deployed model by pairing engineering and marketing expertise. Engineers help build and integrate AI-enabled workflows, while marketing practitioners help apply the organization’s knowledge, processes, and standards to determine which work to redesign and where human judgment remains essential.

B2B marketing leaders already look to technology vendors for help adopting and scaling content solutions. According to Forrester’s B2B Marketing Online Community, Content Creation And Optimization Survey, June 2026, 53% of enterprise marketing leaders turn to technology vendors for help adopting or scaling content creation and optimization solutions. That’s well ahead of consulting firms (35%), agencies (32%), and systems integrators (18%).

Customer onboarding is another example because it already depends on technical and business expertise working together. Services teams configure, implement, and deploy the technology, while customer success (CS) connects that work to customer enablement and adoption. That requires coordination around implementation timelines, dependencies, technical decisions, and the customer’s path to value. Working directly with vendor specialists can accelerate implementation and help GTM teams translate their expertise into repeatable workflows.

FDEs Create New Questions About Value, Accountability, And Ownership

Embedding specialists with customers creates opportunities for AI vendors and their buyers, but their priorities may not always align. Depending on their business models, vendors benefit from increased technology consumption, broader adoption, stronger relationships, and insights that inform product development. Customers need measurable business outcomes, manageable costs, and workflows they can sustain without ongoing vendor assistance. These interests can reinforce each other, but leaders must ensure that increased technology consumption translates into greater business value.

FDEs also blur traditional responsibilities across engineering, professional services, and customer success. Implementing AI workflows requires technical integration, business process redesign, adoption, and ongoing optimization. These teams must work together rather than rely on sequential handoffs. FDEs can help design and implement AI workflows, while CS helps ensure that those workflows remain tied to changing customer goals, deliver measurable value, and evolve as the customer’s needs change. Customer-facing teams must coordinate these activities and establish clear accountability for results. For B2B organizations, that means connecting AI investments to improvements in marketing effectiveness, sales productivity, and customer outcomes.

Ownership and portability matter when vendors help build customized AI workflows. Business rules, prompts, evaluation criteria, and integrations may become embedded in a vendor’s platform. Buyers should establish who owns these assets, whether they can access or reuse them outside the platform, and what technical or contractual restrictions apply.

Make FDE Expertise Work For Your Organization

As AI vendors expand their forward-deployed teams, B2B leaders should establish expectations for the expertise, outcomes, and lasting capabilities these engagements deliver. Prepare to:

  • Match expertise to the work. Understand whether the FDE brings technical engineering skills, domain expertise, workflow design, change management, or some combination. A marketing team redesigning content and creative operations may need different expertise than a sales organization integrating AI into account planning or a customer success team improving onboarding.
  • Define success in business terms. Establish measurable outcomes before implementation begins, such as increased pipeline generation, improved customer retention, or greater renewal and expansion revenue. Evaluate whether AI workflows improve relevance, quality, and consistency of content that supports these outcomes. Increased content production or AI usage alone doesn’t demonstrate business value.
  • Build internal capabilities to sustain results. Assign an internal owner to maintain and improve AI workflows after the engagement. Make knowledge transfer part of the vendor’s responsibilities and ensure your teams can adapt workflows as business needs change.
