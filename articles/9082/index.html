Week 1: Lock in the Target Role and the Two-Layer Conversation Baseline

Original title: 第 1 周：锁定目标岗位与双层对话基线

FDE 成长手册 · 2026-08-25 · 10 min read

Summary

This is Week 1 of an FDE training handbook. It argues that the first week should focus not on AI tools but on choosing a target role and establishing a baseline in two-layer conversations: discussing strategy, priorities, and risks with business decision-makers while also reconstructing real workflows, exceptions, and system constraints with frontline staff. It lays out five steps: collecting 5–10 official job postings, tagging upward and downward conversation signals, running a diagnostic on a simulated customer-service scenario, evidence-based self-assessment, and producing six documents. The core method is converting a boss's strategic statements and frontline complaints into a chain of hypotheses requiring verification rather than jumping to technical solutions. All companies and data in the scenarios are synthetic teaching material.

Key points

  • Week 1 targets role selection and a dual-conversation baseline, not tool learning.
  • Collect 5–10 official job postings and tag upward/downward conversation signals.
  • In the simulation, ask executives five business questions; ask frontline staff to walk through a recent real task.
  • Use a translation board to flag unverified assumptions instead of picking sides.
  • Self-scores require evidence; no evidence means 0, and six documents must be completed.

Direct answer: Your first week as an FDE is not about learning a list of AI tools. It is about deciding which class of customer problems you intend to solve and establishing a capability baseline: you must be able to discuss strategic outcomes, priorities, and risks with business decision-makers, and also reconstruct real work, exceptions, and system constraints with frontline staff. Understanding both sides is not enough; you must convert what each side says into evidence that can be further validated.

Why an FDE must operate at two altitudes

Title "Why an FDE must operate at two altitudes"

The same project becomes a different problem depending on who is describing it:

Conversation partnerThe language they typically useWhat the FDE should probe forWhat this level alone cannot determine
Business decision-makersGrowth, cost, renewals, risk, strategic prioritiesWhy now? Which outcome must change? What is it competing against for resources? Who decides to continue or stop?Strategic wishes alone cannot reveal where frontline work is actually stuck
Frontline staffTickets, spreadsheets, copy-paste, approvals, rework, workaroundsHow was the most recent case completed? Which systems were used? Where does it fail? Who handles it when something goes wrong?Local pain points alone cannot determine how much resource the project deserves
FDEHypotheses, evidence, scope, constraints, and next validation stepsWhere do the two accounts agree, contradict, or lack evidence? What is the cheapest way to validate?The FDE must not fill in conclusions on anyone's behalf

When your boss says "use AI to cut customer service costs," that is a strategic intent, not yet a fact. When a frontline agent says "the system is hard to use," that is a problem signal, not yet a buildable requirement. The FDE's job is to connect the two statements into a chain awaiting validation:

战略意图  ↓ 需要业务数据验证业务结果与指标  ↓ 需要流程证据验证一线任务与失败点  ↓ 以后才进入技术方案系统、数据与工程约束

This chain is the backbone of this course. Week 1 only determines whether the target role requires you to do this work and what evidence you currently have; customer discovery does not truly begin until Weeks 2–4.

Look at the finished product first: a qualified target role card

Title "Look at the finished product first: a qualified target role card"

Below is a teaching simulation of a finished product. It demonstrates how to write one; it does not imply that "enterprise AI FDE" is the only correct direction.

# 目标岗位卡：B2B 企业 AI FDE（教学示例）
- 目标客户：有复杂知识、权限和审批流程的 B2B 企业团队- 目标问题：把尚未定义清楚的工作流问题转成可部署的软件或 AI 系统- 主要对话对象：业务负责人、一线用户、客户 IT / 安全、客户工程团队- 商业责任：发现高价值问题，解释投入、风险、优先级和采用条件- 一线责任：观察当前流程、绕路和异常，不把功能请求直接当事实- 工程责任：亲手完成集成、测试、部署和故障处理- 结果责任：上线后继续观察采用与失败，把重复模式反馈给产品- 当前证据：有 API 与部署项目；没有用户访谈记录，也没有业务指标决策记录- 最关键缺口：客户发现与跨角色沟通，而不是再学一个 Agent 框架- 选择节奏：24 周标准路线，每周 10 小时

A qualified role card will change your learning sequence. Even if the learner in the example can code, they should not spend the entire first month on model calls.

Step 1: Collect 5–10 target roles

Title "Step 1: Collect 5–10 target roles"

Do not just search job titles on recruitment aggregators. Different companies may use FDE, FDSE, Forward Deployed AI Engineer, Deployment Strategist, or similar names. Start by collecting samples from official job pages, then read the actual responsibilities.

Cover at least two types of companies or business environments. You can start from this tutorial's source matrix, but you need to re-verify that the links still work.

Step 2: Tag each role with "two-level conversation" markers

Title "Step 2: Tag each role with "two-level conversation" markers"

As you read each job posting, look for the following signals separately:

Upward conversation: can you discuss business and strategy

Title "Upward conversation: can you discuss business and strategy"

  • Identifying or prioritizing customer opportunities;
  • Defining successful outcomes rather than just receiving a feature list;
  • Discussing budget, timeline, risk, procurement, or organizational constraints;
  • Deciding with the owner whether to continue, narrow, postpone, or stop;
  • Distilling a customer delivery into product direction or reusable capability.

Downward conversation: can you get into the real work

Title "Downward conversation: can you get into the real work"

  • Interviewing or observing end users directly;
  • Understanding how data is produced and how it moves between systems;
  • Documenting manual handoffs, exceptions, rework, and workarounds;
  • Dealing with permissions, legacy systems, and on-site constraints;
  • After launch, observing whether anyone uses it and whether the work has actually changed.

If a posting only says "build relationships with executives" but mentions no user workflows or hands-on delivery, it may be closer to a sales, consulting, or strategy role. If it only requires executing already-defined technical tasks, it may be closer to product engineering. Mark the evidence first; don't rush to label the role.

Step 3: Run a two-level conversation diagnostic

Title "Step 3: Run a two-level conversation diagnostic"

This is not a formal customer interview; it just uses the same simulated scenario to check your default reactions.

Scenario

Title "Scenario"

The business lead of the fictional company "Beichen Collaboration" says:

"Customer service costs keep rising. We must roll out AI this quarter, ideally answering most tickets automatically."

A frontline customer service agent says:

"The current system is so hard to use; for every question I have to hunt for information everywhere."

All companies, people, and data are teaching simulations. There is no further evidence this week.

I have saved my baseline; show me the reference responses

Facing the boss: assess strategy first; don't rush to show off technology

Title "Facing the boss: assess strategy first; don't rush to show off technology"

Disqualified response:

"Sure, we'll use RAG plus an agent; once we connect the knowledge base, it can reply automatically."

The problem is that this sentence directly links "AI," "automated replies," and "cost reduction," skipping over current costs, strategic priorities, and risk boundaries.

A better first-round response:

"I won't assume automated replies are the right solution. This quarter, what most needs to change: unit service cost, response time, renewal risk, or team capacity? What metric is currently used to judge it? If speed improves but incorrect replies increase, which risk is unacceptable? Finally, who will decide whether to expand investment, based on what evidence?"

Use the following five questions to check whether you have entered the business layer:

  • Outcome: Which business outcome must change?
  • Why now: What is the triggering event and the opportunity cost?
  • Baseline: What are the current value, time window, and data sources?
  • Trade-offs: Which outcomes cannot be sacrificed for speed or cost?
  • Decision: Who makes the next decision, when, and based on what evidence?

These "five questions" are a teaching tool for this tutorial, not an official industry framework. In the first round, just mark each answer as known, to be verified, or unknown; don't fill the gaps with pretty numbers.

Facing frontline staff: chase the most recent real work; don't ask them to design the product

Title "Facing frontline staff: chase the most recent real work; don't ask them to design the product"

Disqualified response:

"If we built a smarter search and auto-reply feature, would you use it?"

This both leads the witness and only yields wishes. A better response is:

"Please open the most recent ticket that required looking up a policy, and walk me through it from the moment it arrived. Where do you look first? Which systems do you switch between? When do you stop to ask a colleague? How do you judge that what you found is the current version? If you find the wrong thing, who notices first?"

A frontline conversation should uncover at least:

  • A specific, recently completed task;
  • The systems and materials actually used, not the process as officially prescribed;
  • One normal path and one exception;
  • Existing workarounds, and why they exist;
  • How errors are discovered, their impact, and who handles them;
  • Claims that still need to be verified through logs or observation.

Put both sides on the same "translation board"

Title "Put both sides on the same "translation board""

At this point there is no interview evidence yet, so a qualified record should keep unknowns marked as unknown:

Boss's framingFrontline's framingCurrent statusConclusions that cannot be drawn directlyNext evidence needed
Customer service costs are risingEvery question requires searching everywhere for informationBoth are unverified statementsSearching for information is the main cause of rising costsCost breakdown, ticket logs, process observation
We must deploy AI this quarterThe current system is hard to useAI is a preset solution; "hard to use" is too vagueAn AI agent is the best solutionRecent tasks, alternatives, technical and procurement constraints
Want to auto-answer most ticketsNo indication yet of which tasks are suitable for automationRisk and task distribution unknownAuto-sending can safely reduce costsTicket classification, error impact, approval and permission rules

Your task is not to pick a side on this table, but to point out which connection most needs verification.

Step 4: Evaluate yourself using evidence only

Title "Step 4: Evaluate yourself using evidence only"

Return to the six-dimension capability matrix and focus on three dimensions:

Capability0-point signal1-point acceptable evidenceWhat higher levels require later
Customer discovery and product judgmentOnly read articles, no interview or question artifacts left behindCompleted simulated conversations, can distinguish facts from assumptionsReal interviews, process evidence, and scoping decisions
Communication and influenceSelf-rated as "a good communicator"Can explain the same problem to both the boss and frontline staffReal decision records, objection handling, and cross-role feedback
Software and data engineeringCan only run a demoHas a small viewable service, tests, and data processingDeployment, permissions, observability, and recovery in later courses

Do not give yourself a 2 or 3 just because you did one simulated conversation this week. At most, it proves you completed an exercise with guidance.

Write the gaps as observable actions:

  • Bad: Improve business thinking.
  • Good: Complete 3–5 cross-level interviews in weeks 2–4, connect one strategic goal and one frontline process with an evidence chain, and have a third party point out the assumptions in it.

Acceptance and failure fixes

Title "Acceptance and failure fixes"

Passing this week requires meeting all of the following:

  • Analyze at least 5 official postings that are still accessible, and not all from one company;
  • Every high-priority capability can point to job responsibilities or final Capstone evidence;
  • The target role card states the decision-maker, frontline users, and engineering responsibility;
  • Complete the cross-level stakeholder matrix and keep the unpolished conversation baselines;
  • All self-assessment scores have evidence links or a clear "no evidence";
  • Can explain in 90 seconds why you chose this direction and pace;
  • Can point out at least three assumptions pending verification in the dual-layer conversation translation board.
Common failuresDiagnostic signalsFix actions
Job table becomes a collection of technical buzzwordsOnly Python, RAG, cloud platforms, no customers or outcomesReturn to responsibility verbs, add "who is served, what changes, who owns adoption"
Treating the boss's words as business factsWrote ROI or growth targets without definitions or sourcesDowngrade to assumptions, add data sources, owners, and verification dates
Treating frontline complaints as feature requirements"The system is hard to use" directly becomes "build a chatbot"Change to recent tasks, actual steps, exceptions, and error impact
Overestimating yourself to choose 20 weeksUsing "have studied" instead of production and user evidenceDowngrade on the 0–3 scale, choose a pace you can consistently complete
Target role too broadWanting to do pre-sales, algorithms, platform, product, and delivery all at onceFix one industry, customer type, and primary work format

Independent transfer exercise

Title "Independent transfer exercise"

Change the scenario from "customer service" to "supply chain shortage handling". Do not design a system; only write:

  • Five business questions you would ask the business owner;
  • One recent task you would ask a warehouse or procurement employee to demonstrate;
  • Three evidence gaps likely to appear between the boss's and frontline's accounts;
  • Which capability gaps would prevent you from becoming a qualified FDE in this industry.

If your answer merely replaces "customer service" with "supply chain" without changing the decision-makers, business outcomes, processes, and cost of errors, it means you are still copying a template.
