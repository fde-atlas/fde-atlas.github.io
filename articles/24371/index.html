How to Become an FDE? Required Skills, Certifications (FDE Certification Exam), and 3 Paths for Those with No Experience

Original title: FDEになるには？必要なスキル・資格（FDE検定）・未経験からの3つの道

FDE Consulting · 2026-10-08 · 6 min read

Summary

Written from the perspective of someone who hires FDEs, this article argues that no certification is needed to become a Forward Deployed Engineer. What matters are three abilities—understanding the business, hands-on implementation, and structuring ambiguous problems—plus experience moving concrete numbers in the field. Two private FDE certifications launched in 2026 (Japan FDE Association and the IoT certification committee's FDE-CF) are too new to matter in hiring. The author maps three entry paths from adjacent roles (engineers, consultants, PMs/solutions engineers) and offers a 12-month plan: first produce quantified results, then a structured problem-solving case, then a small LLM system in real use, and only then study AI.

Key points

  • FDE hiring screens for quantified impact, not credentials.
  • Three abilities: Business, Technology, Creativity; structuring ambiguous problems is hardest to build.
  • Two 2026 private FDE certifications exist but no employer requires them yet.
  • Entry paths: engineers add business sense; consultants and PMs add implementation skills.
  • A 12-month plan builds measurable results first, AI knowledge last.

You don't need certifications to become an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer). What you need are three things: the ability to understand the business, the ability to implement things yourself, and the ability to structure ambiguous problems, plus experience using them in the field to move the numbers.

However, ending with "you don't need certifications" leaves you unsure of what to actually do. Here, writing from my perspective as someone who works in the field as an FDE and hires FDEs, I'll cover what is actually evaluated in FDE selection processes, the positioning of the two FDE certifications launched in 2026, three paths from adjacent roles, and a preparation plan to become ready to apply for FDE positions within a year.

The definition of the FDE role is covered in "What is an FDE? The Meaning and Work of a Forward Deployed Engineer", and how to find job openings in "FDE Job and Career Change Guide".

The Three Abilities an FDE Needs

We think of the abilities an FDE needs as falling into three categories: Business, Technology, and Creativity.

AreaContentQuestions to verify for yourself
Business (understanding the business)Understanding the customer's revenue structure, KPIs, and decision-making flows, and being able to speak about technical results in terms of ROICan you say which company numbers your work moved, and by how much?
Technology (technical implementation)Being able to get hands-on with everything from designing and evaluating LLMs and AI agents to full-stack development, integration with existing systems, and securityCan you build something that works in a week without a design document?
Creativity (structuring problems)Being able to break vague pain points down into solvable problems and determine what should be automated and what humans should decideCan you take a vague "things seem inefficient" complaint and split it into three concrete problems?

Of the three, Creativity is the hardest to develop. Technology can be learned, and industry knowledge comes with field experience. The ability to identify "what problem really needs solving" can only be trained by repeatedly being placed in ambiguous situations.

That said, no one has all three from the start. People coming from adjacent roles have one or two of them. Filling in what's missing on the job is the realistic path to becoming an FDE.

What Selection Processes Evaluate Is "Experience Moving Numbers"

When you read FDE job postings, certification requirements almost never appear. Instead, what comes up is years of experience, experience interacting directly with customers, and experience building systems that run in production. OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting requires five or more years of experience and proficiency in both Japanese and English (job posting).

We believe the three things you'll be asked about in selection are the following.

  • Which numbers you moved, and by how much. Work hours, revenue, retention rates, processing time. Can you answer in monetary amounts or percentages?
  • How you structured ambiguous problems. Starting from questions like "why are customers churning," what did you investigate, how did you break it down, and what did you choose as your countermeasures?
  • How you worked with people in the field. Experience gathering requirements, showing what you built, and fixing it. It's fine if the other party was another department within your company.

Technical depth comes after these three. Experience running an LLM-integrated system in production is a strong plus, but even without it, if you have the three above, you can make up the AI part on the job.

Should You Take the FDE Certification?

In 2026, two private certifications bearing the FDE name were launched.

CertificationOperatorContentStatus (as of September 2026)
FDE CertificationJapan FDE AssociationGrades 5 through 1. Explicitly states "This is not a national certification"Exam format and fees are being prepared
FDE Certification System (FDE-CF)IoT Certification Committee3 levels. Announced in June 2026Registration for the Level 1 beta exam opened September 3, 2026 (announcement)

Both are brand new, and we can find no job postings that require these certifications. Our take is this.

  • Useful for taking stock of your knowledge. As an entry point for systematically learning what FDEs do, the certification syllabus is helpful
  • Not a deciding factor in hiring. Certifications show that you "know," but FDE selection evaluates whether you have "moved" numbers
  • If you take one, do it while building a track record. Rather than spending time only on certification study, creating one experience of moving numbers in your current job makes for better application material

There is no national or public certification for FDEs.

Three Paths from Adjacent Roles

There are still very few experienced FDEs in Japan. That's why nearly everyone comes from an adjacent role. What you have and what you lack differ depending on your original role.

PathOriginal roleWhat you haveWhat you lackHow to fill the gap
1. From engineeringSoftware engineers, SEs, data scientistsTechnologyBusiness, CreativityTrack the business numbers of what you build. Proactively join meetings with customers and operations departments
2. From consulting and planningIT consultants, operations consultants, business plannersBusiness, CreativityTechnologyDon't stop at proposals; build small things yourself. Gain experience creating working things with AI coding tools like Claude Code and Codex
3. From PM and SaaS customer-facing rolesProduct managers, solution engineers, customer successBusiness, part of CreativityDepth in TechnologyExpand the scope of what you implement yourself. Take things all the way to production operation

Looking at hiring trends in 2026, path 2 is widening. Major SI and consulting firms such as Hitachi, NTT Data Group, and Accenture have begun building FDE organizations (FDE Job and Career Change Guide), and a flow is forming of consultants-turned-FDEs adding implementation skills.

A One-Year Preparation Plan to Become Ready to Apply for FDE Roles

This is a plan to assemble your "application materials" in one year while continuing your current job. The emphasis varies by your original role, but the order stays the same.

PeriodWhat to doWhat you'll have
Months 1-3Within your current job, pick one number and move it. Work hours, processing time, inquiry volume, anything. Whether or not you use AI doesn't matterOne accomplishment you can state as "reduced X by Y%"
Months 4-6Take on one vague pain point, structure it yourself, and solve it. Turn something that arrives as a consultation into the shape of a problemOne case you can explain from problem breakdown through countermeasures
Months 7-9Build a small system using an LLM yourself and get someone to use it. Internal routine work is fine. Track whether it was actually usedOne thing that works in production (something someone uses daily)
Months 10-12Organize your three experiences so you can articulate them with numbers and the reasoning behind your decisions. Compare against job posting requirements and fill in the gapsYour resume and three stories to tell in interviews

The key point of this plan is not to start with studying AI. What FDE interviews evaluate is not AI knowledge, but experience moving numbers and experience structuring problems; AI can be added afterward.

Materials for learning

As of October 2026, there are no Japanese-language books dedicated solely to FDEs. The following materials are closest to primary sources on the origins and philosophy of the role.

  • Dev versus Delta: Demystifying Engineering Roles at Palantir (Palantir official blog, 2019). The difference between Delta, the prototype of the FDE, and Dev, which focuses on product development
  • What are Forward Deployed Engineers, and why are they so in demand? (The Pragmatic Engineer, 2025). The history of FDEs and their spread at OpenAI and elsewhere
  • Trading Margin for Moat (a16z, 2025). An essay examining FDEs as a business model
  • 客先常駐と似て非なる「FDE」 (Nikkei Cross Tech). A concise overview in Japanese

If you want to learn more about the role in Japanese, "FDEとは？" and "FDEモデルとは？パランティアが生んだ「現場に入る」事業モデル" are also useful references.
