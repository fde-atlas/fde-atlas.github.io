Tech giants like Lenovo, Alibaba, and Tencent race to "issue certificates" as Forward Deployed Engineers continue to gain popularity | New Economy Watch

Full text

Cover News reporter Fu Wenchao

Today, the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) has become the "new darling" of the AI world and continues to gain popularity. In recent days, training providers across the country have launched FDE-themed training programs one after another, but the differences between their courses have exposed a current reality: the FDE role previously lacked a unified competency standard and a systematic training framework.

On November 6, news broke that the industry has welcomed its first certification—the China Electronics Standardization Institute of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and Lenovo Group jointly released an FDE training certification. Industry observers believe that the launch of this certification symbolizes the FDE role moving beyond "wild growth" and officially entering a year of "standard-setting." On recruitment websites, FDE is currently one of the most sought-after positions. According to statistics from Liepin Big Data Research Institute, from September last year to August this year, the supply-demand ratio for FDE talent in China was only 0.28, meaning four positions competing for one candidate.

On the industry side, companies such as Lenovo, Alibaba, and Tencent have each launched related certifications. Among them, the FDE training certification jointly released by Lenovo and the China Electronics Standardization Institute of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology is the industry's first training certification to receive authoritative recognition from both an enterprise and a professional institution for industry talent standardization.

According to reports, its curriculum is structured around three major series: enterprise AI design methodology, agent workshops and AI foundational capabilities, and project delivery, implementation and operations, with a total investment of about 120 hours, covering the complete chain from business scenario identification, solution design, on-site delivery, to continuous operations. The training investment is clearly tilted toward practice, with most of the time devoted to after-class exercises and integrated comprehensive training. Moreover, the hands-on exam explicitly requires completing designated tasks in a pre-configured environment and submitting evidence of both the process and the results.

FDE is a delivery-heavy, "composite" role; industry insiders liken the FDE to an "all-around player." When announcing the launch of the training certification, Lenovo also stated that an FDE's capabilities do not depend on tools but on the person themselves—for example, translating a customer's vague business needs into "what to do, what not to do, and who is responsible," and translating technical metrics back into business language.

It is understood that Lenovo has used agents to restructure its core enterprise value processes internally, with 60% of its business processes already taken over by agents and running automatically. It has productized and standardized its accumulated technical capabilities, building more than 200 verifiable, deliverable agent solutions externally, with as many as 128 in the manufacturing industry alone. At the same time, Lenovo has also established an FDE organization and launched the construction of a talent resource pool. Its personnel have extensive project experience in industries such as manufacturing, energy, government affairs, and finance, and can quickly convert general AI capabilities into quantifiable business value and ultimately complete delivery.

Delivery is a shared focal point of concern on the industry side. For example, Alibaba Cloud's public cloud ecosystem partner FDE system breaks down capabilities validated in on-site delivery into multiple assessable dimensions, such as architecture and task decomposition, tool invocation, and production reliability; Tencent Cloud's ADP FDE certification also focuses its assessment on implementation and delivery.

Two cases from different industries provide a window for observation. In the large-scale chemical industry, Alibaba Cloud and PetroChina Kunlun Digital Intelligence formed a joint FDE team: during the day, they sat in crews and control rooms running equipment and listening to experience, and at night they distilled industry experience into industrial Agent capabilities, reducing the time from detection to localization of typical anomalies from 30 minutes to under 2 minutes. At another large home appliance manufacturer, the Lenovo FDE team was embedded at the customer's site, completed a POC in three weeks, delivered two agents for inventory optimization and planning within three months, and distilled the proven logic into the customer's own "agent workshop."

These figures confirm the judgment of Dai Wei, Senior Vice President of Lenovo Group and General Manager of the China Solutions and Services Group, on the value of the FDE. He stated that the FDE is a verifiable line of delivery capability. The recent joint launch of the FDE certification with the China Electronics Standardization Institute of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology is intended to "take the methods and lessons Lenovo has accumulated on the front lines, calibrate them against standards, and then feed them back to the entire industry."
