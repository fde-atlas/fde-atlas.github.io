AWS Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) Role and Interview Preparation

Original title: AWS 前线部署工程师 FDE 岗位与面试准备

FDE Times · 2026-11-21 · 5 min read

Summary

The article explains that AWS uses the same "forward deployed" label for two distinct roles: the GenAIIC Deep Learning Architect, focused on research, design and evals, and the AWS Forward Deployed Engineer, who builds and operates production AI systems inside customer companies. The author suggests reading the last verb in a job posting to tell the roles apart, and offers interview preparation tailored to AWS's model of selling FDE work as fixed-price 45-day sprints tied to business outcomes.

Key points

  • AWS runs two different "forward deployed" roles under one label.
  • Deep Learning Architect ends at evaluation; FDE ends at operations.
  • FDE sprints are fixed-price 45-day engagements with metrics set in week one.
  • Senior FDEs must build reusable architectures across engagements.
  • On-call engineers fit FDE; eval-oriented candidates fit GenAIIC.

Key Takeaways

  • The Generative AI Innovation Center and the AWS Forward Deployed Engineering organization share the same roots, but they belong to different departments and hire different people.
  • The Deep Learning Architect role leans toward research, design, and evals, collaborating with scientists; the FDE role builds, deploys, and operates production systems inside customer organizations.
  • AWS FDE is sold in 45-day sprints at a known price, tied to business outcomes, so an FDE must lock down the goals in the first week.

The URL of the Generative AI Innovation Center page on AWS's official site now opens a different page: the sales page for AWS Forward Deployed Engineering. AWS states on the page that Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) services are sold in 45-day sprints, subject to FDE Terms, with no need to negotiate a statement of work.

This detail shows that AWS uses the same phrase to refer to two different jobs. "Forward deployed" now appears in at least two roles: Senior Forward Deployed Deep Learning Architect at the Generative AI Innovation Center (GenAIIC), and Sr Forward Deployed Engineer at the newly formed AWS Forward Deployed Engineering organization.

Apply to the wrong role, and you'll prepare for the interview in the wrong direction. The two roles differ in both collaborators and deliverables. For the FDE role, how the customer pays matters more for day-to-day work than the job title.

Two Departments, One Origin

On June 30, 2026, Amazon announced the creation of an independent Forward Deployed Engineering organization. Amazon said this organization builds on GenAIIC's experience: GenAIIC engineers have been building solutions for customers for about three years. The two teams are related, but they remain separate departments.

GenAIIC describes its mission as helping AWS customers deploy generative AI solutions. The department's Deep Learning Architect role is responsible for collaborating with scientists and AI/ML architects to research, design, develop, and evaluate generative AI solutions.

The Sr Forward Deployed Engineer job posting reads differently: embedding directly inside strategic enterprise customers to design, build, deploy, and operate production systems that use AI. The last word of each verb list is exactly where the two roles differ most: one ends at "evaluate," the other at "operate."

Operating means you're still responsible after go-live: the system breaks down at midnight, the customer's data changes format, inference costs exceed estimates. Evaluating means you have to answer which solution is good enough and why. Both are real skills; they're just developed in different ways.

How the Service Is Sold Determines How the FDE Works

Traditional consulting bills by the hour. AWS makes clear that FDE engagements revolve around shared goals and business outcomes, not hours worked. Each sprint has a fixed price, set by the team's location, and customers know the cost before committing.

The FDE team structure is also clear: one AWS product lead and one AWS technical lead, collaborating with one lead on the customer side.

AWS's official pitch: put the same engineers who build AWS's own AI into customer teams. The page title spells out the end goal: AI running in production and operated by the customer's team.

For engineers, these terms translate into a very specific rhythm. With no statement of work, there are no weeks of scope negotiation to serve as a buffer.

Fixed price plus committed outcomes means success metrics must be defined at the start of the sprint, because by day 45, no one will pay extra for "almost done."

Suppose an insurance company wants an AI agent that reads claims files and recommends payout amounts. In the first week, the FDE and the customer's lead agree on a number—for example, the percentage of claims that can be processed without human review—and identify the claims sample set used to measure it.

For the next four weeks or so, build and measure on real data. The remaining days of the sprint are used to push the system into production and hand it over to the customer's operations team.

Handover is the most underestimated part. If the customer's team can't run the system independently after day 45, the sprint has failed no matter how impressive the demo. So runbooks, dashboards, and incident documentation should be built alongside the code from week one.

The job posting also requires 30%–50% travel. Roughly applied to a 45-day sprint, that's about 13.5 to 22.5 days on-site with the customer, though actual travel arrangements may not align with a single sprint.

Weigh this number before applying, especially if your personal schedule is hard to adjust.

Read Job Postings Like a Hiring Manager

Don't just look at the job title. Find the last verb and who you'll be working with. From these two details you can infer whether the interview will focus more on experiment design and evals, or on your experience delivering systems to production and keeping them running.

The difference in verbs hints at how to prepare for each role. The table below offers preparation suggestions, not wording from the job postings.

What Else Does a Senior AWS FDE Need?

The Sr Forward Deployed Engineer posting includes an easily overlooked requirement: senior roles must create reusable architectures, patterns, and technical mechanisms that can be scaled across the entire FDE business. Delivering a single project isn't enough.

This fits the sprint model. Each sprint has a fixed price, so to do more within 45 days, you can't start from scratch with every customer. The eval data pipeline written for the insurance company in the example above, if turned into a template, could shorten week one of the next banking customer's sprint.

On a resume, "built system X for customer Y" only proves you can deliver. A stronger version: "extracted shared components from the project, reused them in later projects, and shortened startup time."

If you don't have such an entry yet, find code you've written repeatedly across your last two projects, turn it into a documented module, use it in your next project, and record how many days it saved.

Which Role Should You Choose?

If you're a software engineer with a few years of experience delivering production systems, have done on-call duty, and are used to working with other people's teams, the FDE role suits you better. The posting asks for five or more years of software development experience, not a research degree.

What you need to prove: you can quickly set goals and hand over a system that a customer team can run independently.

If you enjoy comparing models, designing evals, and working with scientists every day, the Deep Learning Architect role at GenAIIC is a better fit. In the interview, be prepared to explain how you judge whether a solution is "good enough," backed up with numbers.

Whichever you choose, practice planning a 45-day sprint. For services sold by the sprint and tied to outcomes, an engineer who can clearly articulate what to measure on day one and which team will run the system after the final day will likely walk into the interview with an advantage.
