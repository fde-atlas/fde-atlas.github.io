FDE vs Deployment strategist

Plank · 2026-09-11 · 3 min read

Summary

This Plank article compares two Palantir-originated roles. The forward deployed engineer writes production software — integrations, pipelines, applications on customer data — while the deployment strategist owns problem definition, workflows, domain knowledge, and stakeholder management without writing production code. Palantir deploys them as a deliberate pair, each accountable for the deployment. Outside Palantir, the split rarely survives: most companies fold both jobs into a single FDE req. Drawing on a first-party census of 1262 open FDE roles at 555 companies (observed 2026-10-18), the article shows 62% of postings ask for stakeholder management, 61% for travel or on-site work, and 54% for customer-facing skill — ranking just behind Python at 66% and above nearly all technical requirements. This absorbed strategist workload helps explain why FDE hiring is difficult. The piece closes with guidance: hire an FDE when the bottleneck is building; hire a strategist when the problem itself is undefined or adoption is the blocker.

Key points

  • FDEs write production code; deployment strategists own problem definition.
  • Palantir runs the two roles as an interdependent pair.
  • Most companies merge both roles into one FDE job.
  • 62% of 1262 FDE postings require stakeholder management.
  • Absorbed strategist duties make FDE roles hard to fill.

Both are Palantir inventions and both embed with the customer, but they split along the code line. A forward-deployed engineer writes the software: integrations, pipelines, applications against the customer's real data. A deployment strategist owns the problem around it — the workflow, the domain, the stakeholders, and whether the thing being built is the thing that should be built. Palantir runs them as a pair, and the pairing is the model.

Side by side

Forward-deployed engineer Deployment strategist
Writes production code Yes — that is the job No; may write queries, scripts, and configuration
Owns The system and whether it works The problem definition and whether it is the right one
Core skill Software engineering under someone else's constraints Domain fluency and stakeholder judgment
Typical background Software engineering Consulting, analytics, operations, or the customer's own domain
Failure mode Builds the wrong thing beautifully Specifies the right thing unbuildably
Palantir title FDE, or FDSE (forward-deployed software engineer) DS

What a deployment strategist actually does

A deployment strategist (DS) embeds with a customer to work out what should be built and how the organization will actually use it. The role is analytical and operational rather than engineering: mapping workflows, learning the domain deeply enough to argue with experts, managing stakeholders, and defining the problem the engineers then solve.

Where they overlap

At Palantir the two are deliberately interdependent and often indistinguishable to the customer, which is the point: one person in the room understands the domain and one understands the system, and they are both accountable for the deployment. Outside Palantir the split rarely survives. Most companies collapse both jobs into one FDE req and expect the engineer to carry the domain work too, which is a large part of why the role is hard to hire for.

What the hiring data shows

Plank's census harvests FDE-titled postings, so it cannot count deployment-strategist hiring directly. What it can show is where the strategist's work went. Across 1,276 forward-deployed roles at 547 companies, 62% of job descriptions now ask for stakeholder management, 61% for travel or on-site work, and 54% for customer-facing skill. Stakeholder management ranks second across every requirement measured, behind only Python at 66%, and customer-facing skill sits above every technical requirement except Python and APIs — the domain and relationship work outranks almost everything technical. The industry took the engineering half of Palantir's pairing and folded the strategist half into the same job description. That is a large part of why the role is hard to fill.

Which one do you need?

Forward-deployed engineer

  • The bottleneck is building the thing.
  • The workflow is already understood and someone needs to make it real.
  • You have domain knowledge in-house but no one to write the integration.

Deployment strategist

  • Nobody can yet articulate what should be built.
  • The blocker is organizational adoption rather than software.
  • The domain is specialized enough that a wrong problem definition is the main risk.

Questions people ask

  • What is the difference between an FDE and a deployment strategist at Palantir?
  • The FDE writes the software and the deployment strategist owns the problem and the customer relationship around it. Palantir deploys them together, so the engineer is not also expected to be the domain expert and the stakeholder manager.
  • What is an FDSE?
  • Forward-deployed software engineer — Palantir's fuller name for the engineering half of the pairing. It is used interchangeably with FDE and exists mostly to distinguish the coding role from the deployment strategist track.
  • Do other companies hire deployment strategists?
  • Very few. The title has stayed largely inside Palantir while the FDE title spread industry-wide. Most companies expect one person to carry both the engineering and the domain and stakeholder work, which raises the bar on the hire considerably.
