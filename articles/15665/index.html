Why Anthropic? · FDE recruiter screen question

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 2 min read

Summary

This article breaks down Anthropic's "Why do you want to work at Anthropic?" question from the Forward Deployed Engineer application. As of September 2026, the form asks for a 200-400 word answer that is highly weighted and will be defended verbally in interviews. The recommended structure has three parts: engage with a published principle like "Put the mission first" by naming its personal cost, describe your closest work matching the posting (building production applications with Claude, servers, sub-agents, skills), and explain why now, given these are founding FDE roles. When challenged, cite a published decision with a real cost, such as the September 2026 release of Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 with different safeguards, admit you cannot see internal deliberations, and link back to your own decision. The main trap is mission vocabulary with no personal decision behind it.

Key points

  • The written answer should be 200-400 words and must survive a skeptical verbal follow-up.
  • Structure: a principle engaged with personally, relevant work from the posting, and why now.
  • Defend with a costly public decision, like the Fable 5.1 / Mythos 5.1 split release.
  • Anthropic asks candidates to draft applications themselves, then refine with Claude.
  • The biggest failure is repeating mission language without a decision of your own.

How to answer

As of September 2026, the US application form asks “Why do you want to work at Anthropic?”, says the response is valued highly and notes that great answers are often 200-400 words. The written answer has three parts.

  • A principle you engage with, not recite. Anthropic’s published principles include “Put the mission first”, which calls the mission the final arbiter in its decisions. Source 2CareersPublisherAnthropicSource typecompany hiring page Engaging means saying what you think it could cost in your seat: a customer not getting what it asked for. Then give a decision of yours where you paid a similar cost.
  • The work, from the posting. The FDE postings describe building production applications with Claude inside customer systems and delivering servers, sub-agents and skills. Source 1Forward Deployed Engineer application formPublisherAnthropic (Greenhouse job board)Source typecompany job posting Name the closest thing you have built and the part that was hard.
  • Why now. The postings call these hires founding FDEs who help shape the forward-deployed motion. Source 1Forward Deployed Engineer application formPublisherAnthropic (Greenhouse job board)Source typecompany job posting Say what you have done early in something that others then built on.

The defense. The skeptical follow-up tests the first part, and “it’s written down” fails it, because a written principle is what the interviewer is doubting. Answer with a published decision that had a cost. In September 2026 Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1, the same model with different safeguards, and made Mythos 5.1 available only through trusted access programs. Source 4Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1PublisherAnthropicSource typecompany website Say why that counts as evidence to you, say you can’t see how it was decided, and point back to your own decision. For the competitor question, say yes or no, then say what would decide it.

Follow-ups

What the interviewer may ask next, once your first answer is on the table.

  • Every lab says it puts safety first. Why should I believe it here?
  • When would putting the mission first cost a customer something, and what would you do?
  • Would you take the same role at another lab?

Where answers go wrong

  • Repeats safety and mission language with no decision of the candidate’s own behind it, so the skeptical follow-up has nothing to push against.
  • Claims to know how decisions are made inside the company, from a forum post or a guess, and is corrected by someone who works there.

Answer this in two minutes

Write the answer you would say out loud. The clock starts with your first word.

Illustrative answer about a fictional project

Last year I recommended that a customer not let our model stop benefit payments on its own, and it cost us a quarter on the renewal. I lead integrations at a company that sells claims-processing software to state unemployment agencies.
