Today's Assessment

Today's three signals all point to the same trend: the infrastructure for FDEs in China is advancing along three fronts simultaneously.

  • EY China has begun recruiting directly under the title "FDE / AI Application Delivery Engineer," writing responsibilities that cover agents, RAG, permissions, logging, scheduling, and production deployment—the job market is starting to name this role.
  • Beijing's official policy interpretation has added financing, filing, funding, and OPC data, connecting forward deployed engineering to project funding, service vouchers, and outcome-based billing—policy tools are starting to provide project entry points.
  • The national standard for agent interconnection has entered its pilot phase, with 18 companies signing on and more than 50 adapting early—the protocol layer for cross-organizational collaboration is starting to take shape.

Taken together: FDE practitioners in China no longer face only the window of "conceptual awareness"—three actionable entry points have appeared simultaneously: talent, funding, and standards. What is still genuinely lacking are publicly available before-and-after numbers on enterprise transformations.

Key Signals

EY China Adds FDE / AI Application Delivery Engineer Position

  • Source: Niuke | Original link
  • On July 24, EY (China) Advisory Limited's Beijing branch opened campus applications for the FDE / AI Application Delivery Engineer position, with the window closing on October 24. The role covers enterprise-grade agents, RAG, intelligent Q&A, document review, NL2SQL, and data analysis assistants; it also requires participation in architecture, data flows, interfaces, permissions, logging, exception handling, task scheduling, file parsing, and report generation, and supports engineering upgrades from Demo and PoC to production deployment.
  • Why it matters: This is the first time a leading domestic consulting firm has written FDE directly into an official job title. It is not pre-sales, not implementation, not model invocation—it is a complete delivery role that takes applications from experimentation to production. The job responsibilities themselves are an FDE capability checklist: scenario decomposition, architecture design, system integration, permissions and logging, production deployment, and retrospective knowledge capture.

Beijing's Agent Policy Adds Official Industry Figures and Funding Details

  • Source: Beijing.cn | Original link
  • The policy interpretation released on July 24 added key figures: in the first half of 2026, Beijing's AI sector financing exceeded 95 billion yuan, accounting for more than 30% of the national total; the city has completed filing for 259 large models and registered 195 AI products. Eligible compute deployment, intelligent software upgrades, and "first model solutions" can receive up to 30 million yuan in support, with key projects eligible for up to 100 million yuan. Haidian District now hosts nearly 180 lightweight OPC companies, and the Beijing Economic-Technological Development Area has established 8 specialized OPC communities, gathering more than 500 super-individual innovators.
  • The policy interpretation explicitly connects Token-as-a-Service (TaaS), Agent-as-a-Service (AaaS), and Result-as-a-Service (RaaS), pushing billing from resource consumption toward value delivery.
  • Why it matters: The original policy text on July 23 wrote FDE into policy; today's interpretation added the money and the numbers. The Forward Deployed Engineer is no longer just a talent topic—it is entering an industrial environment supported by project funding, service vouchers, OPC communities, and outcome-based billing.

National Standard for Agent Interconnection Enters Pilot Application

  • Source: ITHome | Original link
  • A special meeting on July 21 launched the first batch of pilots, with 18 companies and research institutes signing on; more than 50 companies had already begun piloting earlier. The standard covers five major layers: identity, description, discovery, interaction, and tool invocation.
  • Why it matters: As agents move from standalone deployment to cross-organizational collaboration, identity authentication, permission auditing, and protocol interoperability become essential. The national standard pilot shows that China's agent ecosystem is shifting from "going it alone" to "networked collaboration"—the FDE's scope of work will expand from deploying individual agents to designing the interactions and governance between agents.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Use the EY job posting as a self-assessment checklist: Break down the job responsibilities in reverse—scenario decomposition, architecture design, system integration, permissions and logging, production deployment, retrospective knowledge capture—this is the market's current expectation of FDE capabilities. How many can you cover?
  • Watch Beijing's policy application details: Subsidy tools such as compute vouchers, token vouchers, and agent service vouchers can help SMEs lower the barrier to first-time AI deployment. But policy amounts are not the same as closed deals—keep tracking actual implementation progress.
  • Pay attention to how interconnection protocols affect delivery models: When agents need to interact under a unified protocol, the FDE's deliverables are no longer just a working agent, but also its identity, permissions, and interface design within the enterprise's agent network.
