What is an FDE? Differences from SES and outsourcing, and 4 perspectives for companies adopting them

Original title: FDEとは？SES・受託との違いと、導入する企業の4つの観点

株式会社アールストリート | R Street · 2026-08-21 · 8 min read

Summary

Japanese consulting firm R Street explains the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) model: engineers embed at client sites and take responsibility from problem definition through AI implementation to organizational adoption, working with real data and business constraints. The role originated at Palantir and has been adopted by major AI companies including OpenAI. Citing a 2024 BCG survey, the article notes only 22% of firms have generated value beyond pilots and 4% significant value. It contrasts FDE with strategy consulting (ends at recommendations), custom development (ends at delivery), and SES staffing (ends at labor provision), and offers four criteria for evaluating FDE providers: where responsibility ends, whether work happens in production conditions, whether adoption is designed, and whether capability stays in-house.

Key points

  • FDEs embed on-site and own outcomes from problem definition through implementation to adoption.
  • The role was established at Palantir and later adopted by OpenAI and other AI firms.
  • BCG's 2024 survey: only 22% of firms get value beyond AI pilots, 4% significant value.
  • FDE differs from consulting, custom development, and SES by responsibility extending to results and adoption.
  • Four evaluation criteria: responsibility endpoint, real-environment build, adoption design, in-house capability.

Definition

Engineering that embeds itself in the field

The FDE, as practical expertise that determines the success or failure of AI adoption, spread from US technology companies to the rest of the world.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) literally means "an engineer deployed to the front." It refers to a role that goes beyond staying at a development site and instead places itself in the customer's workplace, implementing software under real data and operational constraints and taking responsibility until results are achieved. The approach of driving customer transformation through such talent has been established as a support model known as Forward Deployed Engineering.

Importantly, this does not simply mean a "resident engineer." An FDE is not in the position of receiving requirements and building to them; rather, they take responsibility from understanding the field and defining the problems, through implementation, and all the way to embedding the solution in the organization. The essence of this model is combining a consultant's perspective with an engineer's implementation capability within a single team.

Immersion / Understanding the field

Going beyond analyzing documents, we grasp operations, data, and constraints on the ground, and identify the problems that directly connect to value.

Implementation

We build AI agents and workflows in the actual environment, taking them from the validation stage through to production operation.

Embedding

We design everything from operating structures and governance to talent development, leaving behind mechanisms that continue to function after the engagement ends.

Background: Origins and Context

Why FDE, and why now

The era of judging model performance is over; we have entered an era where the distance to results is what matters.

The FDE role was established by the US company Palantir in the field of data utilization (the role introduction on the company's official blog). This approach, in which engineers create value from inside the customer's organization rather than simply delivering software and walking away, became a core driver of the company's growth. Today, major AI companies including OpenAI have successively created similar roles (OpenAI's job listings), and it has come to be discussed as a standard methodology for AI businesses (Y Combinator "The FDE Playbook" by Bob McGrew).

Behind this lies a shift in where value is scarce. As the performance of generative AI rapidly became commoditized, merely being able to use an excellent model is no longer a differentiator. What separates outcomes is the ability to make AI work within the specific realities of one's own operations, data, and constraints, and to root it in the organization. In fact, a survey published by BCG in 2024 reported that only 22% of companies had produced any value beyond the proof-of-concept stage, and only 4% had produced substantial value (BCG "Where's the Value in AI?"). The expertise that closes this "last mile" is precisely the value of the FDE.

In Japan as well, from 2026 onward, everyone from major firms to specialist companies has been rolling out FDE-style support one after another (a Nikkei Cross Tech explainer). However, even when they use the same name, the substance of the support differs greatly from company to company. The following comparison is a starting point for telling them apart.

Comparison: How it differs from conventional support

Where does the endpoint of responsibility lie?

The difference from conventional support shows up in how far we define responsibility as our own.

Strategy consulting

Up to the recommendation

Strong at analyzing problems and presenting strategy, but implementation is handed off to other parties.

Contracted system development

Up to delivery

Responsible for turning specified requirements into reality, but the validity of the requirements themselves and post-deployment results are out of scope.

SES / resident engineers

Up to the provision of labor

People are placed on site, but direction and control rest with the customer, and the design of outcomes is not entrusted to them.

FDE

Up to results and embedding

We take on everything from problem definition through implementation, operation, and embedding in the organization as a single responsibility.

R-Street operates this FDE model according to three principles: "work backward from results," "take responsibility through implementation," and "leave capability behind in the organization." The principles are not slogans; they are reflected all the way down to contract design, how the team is structured, and the conditions for ending the engagement.

In Practice: What it looks like in the field

What does an FDE actually do on site?

More than any abstract methodology, the actual scenes of our engagements explain the FDE most accurately.

For example, at one company in the service industry, creating shift schedules for each location depended on long hours of manual work by managers. We verbalized the on-site rules and exceptions one by one, and put an AI agent that automatically assembles shift proposals into actual operation. Even when vacations are taken or schedules change suddenly, it reassembles only the affected portion rather than rebuilding the whole thing. Such operational subtleties cannot be designed into a system without entering the field.

In the accounting domain, we replaced daily processing with AI operations and moved to a structure where people focus on judgment and exceptions. There are also implementations that embed themselves into the work like a colleague, such as an agent that attends meetings and helps organize the points of discussion. What all of these share is the sequence: they begin with understanding the field rather than analyzing documents, and end with production operation rather than validation.

These initiatives are published on our business page as client case studies and our own internal initiatives.

Beyond FDE: The endpoint of the FDE

A good FDE makes itself unnecessary

What we call a successful engagement is a state in which improvement continues even without us.

The FDE model has a clear endpoint that becomes apparent the more you pursue it. A mechanism that only works if external experts remain on site is not yet complete. When the operation becomes the organization's own, when the people on the ground make changes themselves, and when improvement keeps emerging from within—only upon reaching that point can we say AI has become a foundation of the business.

It is based on this thinking that R-Street has made FDE talent development and training another pillar of its business. We systematize the methods we use in consulting engagements and cultivate FDE talent within client companies. In fact, one engagement that began with an intensive training program and expanded into implementation support, company-wide rollout, and infrastructure building is an initiative that symbolizes our model.

At its root lies our corporate philosophy: "creating a society where anyone can challenge themselves as many times as they want." Organizations that can run AI with their own hands can try small, fail, and try again. Keeping within the organization the ability to increase the number of attempts—that is the meaning we put into the FDE model.

Perspective: Points of Judgment

When considering FDE-style support

When choosing whom to engage, we recommend examining the design of the support rather than the label.

Where does the responsibility end?

Check whether the support is designed to end with proposals or validation, or whether reaching production operation is built into the contract and structure.

In what environment will it be built?

Results in a demo environment do not guarantee results in production. Whether there is a premise of building under real data and constraints makes the difference between success and failure.

Is there a design for adoption?

Operating structure, governance, usage guidelines. Check whether the scope of support includes building mechanisms that sustain value even after implementation.

Will capability remain in the organization?

After the support ends, who will continue improving? Check whether a path toward in-housing and talent development is presented.

How our support addresses these four points is published in our FDE Consulting guide, covering applicable situations, process, and fees and contract formats.

FAQ: Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about FDE

What is FDE?

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role and support model in which engineers embed themselves at the customer's site and take responsibility for everything from AI implementation to adoption, under real data and business constraints. It was established at US-based Palantir and has spread to major AI companies and the Japanese consulting industry along with the spread of generative AI.

Are Forward Deployed Engineer and FDE Consulting the same thing?

FDE is the name of the role; FDE Consulting refers to offering that model as a support service. It is closest to understanding it as consulting that takes responsibility through implementation.

How does it differ from ordinary consulting?

The endpoint of responsibility differs. Rather than ending with strategic proposals or concept formulation, it takes on implementation, putting into operation, and organizational adoption as a single engagement.

How does it differ from SES or resident engineers?

Although the resident format is similar, the role differs. SES is a contract providing labor under the customer's direction, while FDE designs everything from problem definition to outcomes as its own responsibility.

What kinds of companies is it suited for?

It is suited for companies that have run generative AI trials but have not reached production operation, and companies that want to root the capability in-house rather than leaving implementation entirely to external parties. Rather than company size, the will to achieve outcomes and in-house the capability determines suitability.
