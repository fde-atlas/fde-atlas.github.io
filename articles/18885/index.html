Full Text of the FDE Capability Benchmark (FCB) White Paper

Original title: FDE能力基准（FCB）白皮书全文

深圳市真实智元科技有限公司 · 2026-10-31 · 17 min read

Summary

Shenzhen-based 真实智元 (Zhenzhiyuan) publishes the FDE Competency Baseline (FCB), a white paper proposing a third generation of FDE assessment: delivery verification instead of knowledge exams or platform-bound certifications. It defines five competency dimensions (engineering implementation, scenario understanding, AI orchestration, field delivery, value closure) across three autonomy levels, anchored in a 15-cell behavior matrix that is tool-agnostic. Assessment centers on a 48-hour simulated delivery challenge where AI tools are allowed and required, with all usage logged. Output is an auditable evidence package—Git history, AI logs, four deliverables, defense video—rather than a certificate. Simulation caps at L2; L3 requires real-project evidence. The paper also covers governance commitments, disclosed conflicts of interest with its own training course, and a JD-frequency snapshot of 100 companies.

Key points

  • FCB targets AI orchestration ability, not AI knowledge or platform skills.
  • Five dimensions × three levels form a 15-cell, tool-agnostic behavior matrix.
  • 48-hour open-tool challenge requires AI use with full logging; caps at L2.
  • Deliverable is an auditable evidence package, not a certificate.
  • Appendix analyzes 100 FDE job descriptions; production delivery is mentioned in 98.

0 · Executive Summary

FCB answers three questions: what yardstick an FDE needs, what that yardstick measures, and why it can be trusted.

FDE competency assessment focuses on actual delivery: whether one can understand business requirements, build applications, inspect model outputs, and complete deployment and communication under constraints. FCB uses work products, process records, and a defense presentation as the basis of evaluation, and allows—and requires—documenting the use of AI tools.

FCB's answer: the object of assessment is AI orchestration capability (the ability to combine models, tools, data, prompts, and evals into reliable systems), and the form of evidence is a delivery dossier—a Git timeline, AI usage logs, four deliverables, and a recorded defense. The 48-hour simulated delivery challenge allows and requires the use of all AI tools, with a full audit trail. In one sentence: others test how much you know about AI, FCB tests what you can deliver with AI; others issue a certificate, FCB delivers an auditable chain of delivery evidence.

1 · Generational Positioning

There is no official paper standard for FDEs, but there is a de facto standard formed by the convergence of hiring JDs at major companies; certification products are already in a free-for-all. FCB's position is to establish a third generation on top of the two existing certification forms—the delivery-verification type.

As of the publication of this white paper, none of the four definers of the FDE role has launched an official FDE certification: Palantir's certification system covers only four tracks of the Foundry platform; OpenAI Academy badges are officially declared "not a certification"; Anthropic's role certifications are tied to the Claude product; Scale AI has no certification at all. The market is filled by two generations of forms:

FCB is a competency assessment framework developed and published by Zhenyuan for real-world use, intended as a reference for learners, training providers, and employers. It is not an official standard or industry-wide certification, and should be used in conjunction with specific roles, project contexts, and assessment scopes.

2 · Competency Model: Five Dimensions × Three Levels

The FCB competency model derives from a de facto standard formed by the convergence of job descriptions from four major FDE employers: production-grade coding + LLM productionization experience + embedded on-site customer delivery + high autonomy in ambiguous environments. The complete 15-cell behavioral anchor set has been published at the top of the previous section; this section presents the model's definitions and usage rules.

2.1 The Five Competency Dimensions

DimensionDefinitionObservation Focus
D1 Engineering ImplementationThe ability to turn AI applications into runnable software under real constraints (time, legacy systems, data quality)Code quality, system design, distance from production
D2 Scenario UnderstandingThe ability to translate "pain points the customer can't articulate" into "verifiable technical problems" in ambiguous business settingsQuality of requirement clarification, scope definition, translation between business and technical language
D3 AI OrchestrationThe ability to combine models, tools, data, prompts, and evals into a reliable systemContext engineering, eval awareness, engineering treatment of model uncertainty
D4 On-site DeliveryThe ability to drive delivery under time pressure, emotional pressure, and incomplete information at the customer siteCommunication cadence, prioritization trade-offs, demo and defense performance
D5 Value ClosureThe ability to translate technical output into financial and operational value recognized by the customer, leaving behind replicable assetsROI justification, adoption drive, knowledge retention

2.2 Three-Level Behavioral Anchoring (L1 / L2 / L3)

The basis for grading is not the amount of knowledge, but the radius of autonomy in one's work.

L1 · Apprentice

Completing tasks under supervision: working within clearly defined tasks and given scaffolding, escalating for help when facing ambiguity. Baseline for assessment: able to independently run through one slice end to end.

L2 · Independent

Independently owning a complete slice: independently responsible for a complete slice from requirements to launch in a real project, able to self-converge when facing ambiguity. The direct assessment target of the 48-hour challenge.

L3 · Leading

Defining problems and owning outcomes: defining problems in uncharted territory, guiding customers and internal teams through the entire journey, and being accountable for business results. Simulation environments alone are insufficient proof; real project evidence must be added.

Each cell in the dimension × level grid describes "what behaviors reviewers can observe in the evidence" (behaviorally anchored descriptions, BARS), 15 cells in total, all tool-independent—swap in the next model or the next toolset, and the cells still hold. The full 15 cells are in the "Five Dimensions × Three Levels Competency Matrix" at the top of this page.

2.3 Grading Rules and Counter-Indicators

  • Each dimension is graded directly against its behavioral anchors (Not met / L1 / L2 / L3); the overall grade uses a "five-dimension profile + minimum requirements for key dimensions" (in pilot calibration), not a single median aggregate—failing a key dimension caps the overall grade. For an FDE, there is no passing line for "weak engineering but great storytelling."
  • The 48-hour simulation environment caps the grade at L2; L3 requires additional real project evidence (employer/client work proof + project retrospective documentation + client confirmation). This is itself part of the model's validity: simulation is not reality, and FCB does not pretend it is.
  • Counter-indicators (triggering a downgrade review in any dimension): key decisions cannot be attributed during the defense ("AI wrote it, I didn't look closely"); eval reports contain only pass rates with no failure attribution; ROI figures lack source annotations.

3 · Assessment Design: The 48-Hour Simulated Delivery Challenge

One customer, one ambiguous requirement, 48 hours, four stages, four deliverables. Open book, open network, all tools allowed—and AI use is required.

3.1 The Four Stages

Stage One · Requirement Clarification

Deliverable: a Requirements Clarification Document: current state / goals / constraints / acceptance criteria / non-goals. One 15-minute customer clarification Q&A is available at T+4h.

Primarily assesses D2 · Focus: whether clarifying questions surface implicit assumptions

Stage Two · Working Prototype

A runnable prototype and a Git repository. Any model, framework, and scaffolding may be used; full traceability of submissions throughout.

Primarily assesses D1 + D3 · Focus: degradation paths for failure modes

Stage Three · Eval Report

Eval set, pass rate, failure attribution (model / retrieval / process), cost accounting.

Primarily assesses D3 + D4 · Focus: whether evals cover end-to-end or just single points, and whether attribution is honest

Stage Four · Customer Defense

A 15-minute live defense + a one-page ROI. Reviewers role-play the customer, raising challenges and on-the-spot changes.

Primarily assesses D4 + D5 · Focus: response structure for unscripted questions

Why 48 hours: it is the shortest time window that covers the complete loop of "evals → iteration → attribution." The time window itself is a stress test for D4—real on-site engagements never give you unlimited time. To clarify: 48 hours is the submission window for the assessment, not a requirement to work 48 hours straight—rest is allowed within the window, as is consulting public materials and using all permitted tools and materials (open book, open network). The assessment is judged on the quality of the final deliverables and full traceability throughout.

The following complete example illustrates how the four stages are assessed. The example is written according to the courseware methodology (the five-step delivery method: Discover → Prototype → Validate → Go live → Scale) and the structure of the capstone project in Chapter 30; the scenario is a fictional case for teaching purposes. Example · Illustrates the assessment method, not a leak

Customer Scenario (Fictional Case for Teaching Purposes)

A software company of about 800 people, with product manuals, delivery documents, and ticket records scattered across multiple systems: new hires take weeks of document digging to get up to speed, and frontline answers to customers are inconsistent. Management asks for "an enterprise knowledge assistant," IT worries about data permissions and liability for citation errors, and the frontline only cares that "answers must cite sources and stop making me dig through documents." As the FDE, you are responsible end to end: complete the full loop from requirement clarification to customer defense within 48 hours.

Five Steps × Four Stages

Challenge StageCorresponding Five-Step MethodExample Challenge Task
T+0–8h · Requirement ClarificationDiscoverCurrent state / goals / constraints / acceptance criteria / non-goals; one 15-minute customer clarification Q&A at T+4h to surface implicit assumptions
T+8–32h · Working PrototypePrototypeA single-pipeline enterprise knowledge base Q&A prototype (data in → retrieval → answers with citations), simulated API integration, full Git traceability
T+32–40h · Eval ReportValidateAn eval set of ≥30 items (including 10 hard cases), citation accuracy, failure attribution (model / retrieval / process), cost accounting

Deliverables Checklist

  • Requirements Clarification Document (current state / goals / constraints / acceptance criteria / non-goals)
  • Runnable prototype + Git repository (including simulated API integration and idempotency handling; empty placeholders with hardcoded return values are forbidden)
  • Evals report (eval set, pass rate and citation accuracy, failure attribution, cost accounting)
  • One-page ROI (numbers with source annotations, assumptions explicitly listed)
  • End-to-end evidence: Git timeline + AI usage logs + 15-minute defense recording

Scoring dimensions

Reviewers independently rate candidates using five-dimension behavioral anchors: Stage 1 primarily assesses D2 scenario comprehension, Stage 2 primarily assesses D1 engineering implementation + D3 AI orchestration, Stage 3 primarily assesses D3 + D4 live delivery, Stage 4 primarily assesses D4 + D5 value closure; the final assessment references the course's seven-dimension weights, where "eval set · metrics · attribution" is the largest single item—it is the dividing line that distinguishes an FDE from a demo engineer.

Pass and improvement criteria

  • Five one-vote-veto items (isomorphic to the course's final assessment): serious data leakage; executing high-risk actions without human confirmation; inability to roll back; inability to reproduce submitted results; inconsistency between the eval set and success metrics—if any of these occurs, the candidate fails regardless of total score.
  • Example acceptance requirements: citation accuracy must be ≥90% to be judged usable; the eval set must cover end-to-end scenarios rather than single points; any dimension that falls short must include attribution and improvement records.
  • Defense criteria: when facing on-the-spot variables (e.g., a client demanding early launch, a security team refusing to provide production data), the candidate must produce a viable revised plan within 10 minutes without sacrificing key security gates; key decisions must be attributable during the defense.

Real-challenge boundary: the scenario packages for the FCB real challenges are sealed separately, with release cadence covered in the governance commitments in Section 5; the situations, numbers, and thresholds in the examples are for teaching exercises only and do not constitute predictions of the real challenges.

3.3 Scoring and Review Mechanism

  • Scoring: the five capability dimensions are rated separately according to the 15-cell behavioral requirements matrix. The complete scoring rubric and scenario packages are still being finalized and will be released during the pilot period; please confirm the current rules and materials before participating in an assessment.
  • Review composition: each assessment is staffed with 2 human reviewers (one technical background + one delivery/business background) + 1 AI reviewer. The AI reviewer scores independently, serving to check consistency among human reviewers; any divergence of one level or more on any dimension triggers human re-review, and the AI has no final adjudication authority.
  • Anti-cheating stance: we do not guard against AI, but against "dishonest AI use"—the Git timeline proves work cadence, AI usage logs prove the quality of the orchestration process, and attribution questioning during the defense proves authorship. Concealing usage records is a more serious offense than using AI.

4 · Evidence Chain: A Deliverable Portfolio

FCB's output is not a certificate, but an auditable, verifiable, directly submittable evidence package.

  • Git timeline: complete commit history, proving the authenticity of work cadence—the first line of anti-cheating evidence.
  • AI usage logs: prompt-output-accept/reject records; log completeness is itself a scoring item.
  • Four deliverables: requirements clarification document, working prototype, evals report, one-page ROI.
  • Defense recording: full recording of Stage 4, for employer-side verification.

After the assessment, the candidate receives a "Job Capability Assessment Report" (five-dimension rating + cited review evidence for each dimension + validity statement) and the raw evidence package—the latter is designed as a portfolio that can be attached directly to FDE job application materials.

6.1 Conflict of Interest Disclosure

Standard-setting and assessment body: Zhenzhi Zhiyuan (真实智元) both sets the standard and operates the assessment, and also offers a course designed to this standard, creating an inherent conflict of interest. The checks and disclosures for this: the scoring system and 15-cell behavioral requirements matrix are public (the complete scoring rubric and scenario packages will be released during the pilot period); each assessment is staffed with 2 human reviewers (technical + delivery/business backgrounds) + 1 AI-assisted reviewer, the AI has no final adjudication authority, any divergence of one level or more on any dimension triggers human re-review, and L3 determinations are re-verified by the review committee against the same matrix; validity data (pass rates and rating distributions, reviewer divergence rates, employer feedback summaries) is published on schedule; the standard is reviewed and updated annually with public revision notes (see the governance commitments in Section 5). Meanwhile, as a public yardstick, FCB can be used independently by any third-party trainer, certifier, or employer, without reliance on this organization.
