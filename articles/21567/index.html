A Platform Engineer builds the common foundation developers use (deployment mechanisms, environment provisioning, monitoring, etc.) as an internal product, reducing developers' workload. This approach is called "Platform Engineering." The following is the author's own summary and not the definitions on project sheets.

RoleMain WorkCommon Terms in Job Listings (Author's Summary)
Platform EngineerBuilds common foundations for developers and shapes them into usable formFoundation, standardization, automation, Terraform
SREMaintains service reliability through both operations and softwareMonitoring, incident response, automation, lead
Infrastructure ConstructionDesigns and builds servers, networks, and cloud systemsDesign, construction, Linux, AWS, Azure
DevOpsPractices and mechanisms such as CI/CD that connect development and operationsCI/CD, IaC, GitHub Actions

Platform Engineering and SRE overlap considerably. The difference is that SRE's primary goal is "service reliability," while the Platform Engineer's primary goal is "a foundation developers can use without confusion."

There are 27 open projects overall with "platform" in the project name. However, 23 of these use the term mainly as the name of a business service or data platform, such as "logistics matching platform" or "EC platform." Their classifications are also backend development, PdM, data, QA, and so on, and they are not necessarily work building common foundations for developers. The SRE/DevOps classification had 4, and the infrastructure construction classification had 0.

Therefore, work close to Platform Engineering must be sought among projects in the SRE/DevOps and infrastructure construction classifications. So readers should search for projects by the substance of the work rather than the word "platform."

When going independent for the first time, it is easiest to gauge the market rate on job sites while pursuing direct sales in parallel. If you are unsure about going independent, you can ask questions in the following Q&A.

Q. I'm unsure whether to go independent. With my current experience, what monthly-rate projects can I apply for?

A. It's fine even if you haven't decided to go independent. We will ask about your career and tell you via chat the rate range of projects you can apply for and a rough guide to the timing of going independent.

Since the project titles do not include the words "platform engineer," write your resume so that the substance of your work comes through. Examples of what to write are shown below.

  • Infrastructure standardization: How many environments you unified using Terraform modules, etc., and how the creation time went from X hours to Y minutes
  • Developer tooling: How many teams and how many people used the deployment process automation you built
  • Reliability: How the number of incidents and time to recovery changed
  • Technologies used: Names of AWS, Kubernetes, and CI/CD tools, and the scope you were responsible for

Include at least one number for each accomplishment. An example of a completed sentence is: "Unified the configuration of 12 environments through Terraform modularization, reducing environment creation time from 3 hours to 20 minutes" (the numbers are examples).
