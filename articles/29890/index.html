State of Forward-Deployed Engineering 2026 (Original Data)

Adam Boudjemaa · 2026-07-21 · 3 min read

Summary

Adam Boudjemaa analyzed 519 FDE-family job postings from 57 employers, collected via the public APIs of Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, coded against a fixed rubric as a July 2026 snapshot. About 28% of postings labeled FDE or FDSE fail his broad coding test, while 98.3% explicitly require production coding and only 14.1% carry quota or commission language. The FDE title itself is just 36.2% of the family, and 58% of postings code to engineering/product reporting lines. The sample is employer-weighted: Databricks, Palantir, OpenAI and Snowflake supply roughly 59% of postings, skewing toward US, AI-native companies. Only 17.3% published pay ranges, with a median base floor of $175,000 and ceiling of $278,000. The author stresses these measure posting text, not actual work, and lists sampling limitations in detail.

Key points

  • 519 postings from 57 employers via Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby APIs, July 2026 snapshot.
  • 98.3% require production coding; only 14.1% include quota or commission language.
  • 28% of FDE/FDSE-labeled postings fail the study's broad coding test; the title is 36.2% of the family.
  • Databricks, Palantir, OpenAI and Snowflake supply about 59% of postings, skewing the aggregates.
  • Only 17.3% list pay ranges; median base floor $175K, ceiling $278K, median experience floor 5 years.

I collected 519 job postings in the forward-deployed engineer (FDE) family from 57 employers. One question guided the study: which responsibilities do these postings explicitly describe?

The postings come only from the public job-board APIs of three applicant tracking systems (ATS): Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby. I coded each posting against a fixed rubric. The aggregates describe the published July snapshot, not current vacancies. The dataset and methodology are free.

A companion study covers agent-engineering hiring. It uses a separate corpus and targets a different title family.

66 of 235 fail this study’s keyword-based test. Missing evidence in a posting is not proof of what the employee actually does.

meet the bar under a strict reading that also requires an explicit engineering or product reporting line

142 of 235 have the required signals plus an explicit engineering/product reporting line. An unclear reporting line also fails this stricter test.

require production coding

510 of 519 postings match the production-coding rubric. This measures posting language, not observed day-to-day work.

14.1%

carry quota, commission, or on-target-earnings language

73 of 519 postings contain those terms. Confirm the compensation structure directly with the employer.

What these roles are called

The FDE title itself is only 36.2% of the family (188 of 519 postings). The label is spread across a dozen names. FDSE means forward-deployed software engineer.

Who the postings say they report to

In this sample, 58% are coded as engineering/product and 7.7% as sales or go-to-market (GTM). Reporting lines are one signal to discuss with the employer, not a verdict on the role.

Limitations

  • Sampling frame: only companies on public Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby boards, from a hand-curated seed list. The sample skews to US, venture-backed, AI-native companies. It under-covers large enterprises on a private ATS (applicant tracking system) and staffing listings. It also under-covers EMEA (Europe, the Middle East and Africa) and APAC (the Asia-Pacific region). This is the state of FDE hiring among AI-native and infrastructure companies on public ATS boards, not the whole market.
  • Concentration: the four largest employers (Databricks, Palantir, OpenAI, Snowflake) supply about 59% of postings. Read every aggregate as employer-weighted, not evenly spread across the market.
  • Snapshot, not flow: point-in-time open roles are not hires. Trend claims need a second edition.
  • Self-reported text: requirements reflect what employers write, not what they enforce.
  • Derived fields (including the true-FDE bar) are coded by a keyword rubric applied to posting text; not human-adjudicated for every row.
  • Compensation coverage is partial: only the 17.3% of postings with public ranges, which over-represents US pay-transparency states.
