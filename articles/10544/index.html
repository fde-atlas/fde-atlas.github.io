FDE, a Super AI Opportunity

Original title: FDE，一个超级AI风口

腾讯云 · 2026-07-27 · 7 min read

Summary

The author argues that the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role is a structural opportunity in AI enterprise services: as models commoditize, value is shifting to people who can embed AI into real business workflows. Citing hiring data, the piece shows extreme demand, outlines the three capabilities a top FDE needs, and proposes a four-step growth path, ending with plans for an FDE training program.

Key points

  • FDEs deploy AI into client workflows, turning demos into working systems
  • Chinese tech giants pay FDEs up to 70k RMB monthly with 15-month salaries
  • Overseas FDE job postings grew 42x in two years, per LinkedIn data
  • Anthropic and OpenAI launched enterprise services firms the same day, both FDE-based
  • Top FDEs combine AI engineering, industry knowledge, and delivery skills

FDE in AI enterprise services implementation is a surefire opportunity.

Today, I want to write a dedicated piece to really talk through FDE with you.

Let me start with a judgment: The biggest AI dividend is rapidly shifting from people who know how to use AI to people who can make AI land in real businesses. And the person who can turn AI into business results is the FDE.

In our Pojue community's terms, that's the AI operator. Whether you're a boss or helping others with AI enterprise services, if you can achieve this, you're an FDE. I even think that for small and medium-sized companies wanting to become AI-driven, the best way is for the boss to become an FDE — that's roughly the role I play at Pojue.

Over the past six months, I've led Pojue through the first phase of its AI transformation. Currently, Pojue's AI transformation mainly runs along these lines:

1/ Obsidian/Feishu knowledge base to capture colleagues' skills and processes 2/ Codex for full-stack development and various skills 3/ Feishu CLI + Feishu AI employees for business operations

Our company now has over a hundred AI employees. Human colleagues and AI employees work together to deliver better results and more refined operational outcomes.

At the same time, around systematizing AI, we're building a skills/agent marketplace for Pojue members, and around productizing AI, we've developed an AI product-selling system, with plans to develop AI systems for several popular Pojue initiatives in the future.

Now, back to FDE:

Many people may not have heard of FDE. Its full name is Forward Deployed Engineer. The name is a bit convoluted, but what they do isn't complicated:

AI engineers are sent directly into companies, sitting alongside customers, turning AI from one demo after another into systems that actually work and embed into the customer's workflow.

I wrote about the FDE role in my previous piece, "The AI Enterprise Services Pyramid." In that piece, I divided AI enterprise services into three tiers, and at the top of the pyramid are the operators — the FDEs and the organizations that can run FDEs.

First, let's talk about just how in-demand this role is right now.

Let me share some data with you:

Domestic tech giants — ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent, Huawei — have all been scrambling for these people recently. Some are hiring FDEs focused on large models, with monthly salaries of 35,000 to 70,000 yuan, plus fifteen months of pay per year — that adds up to over a million yuan at the top end.

Overseas, LinkedIn data shows the number of FDE positions has surged 42-fold over the past two years, far outpacing the 13-fold growth of ordinary AI engineers. Indeed's data is even more direct: in April 2025 there were 643 related job postings, and by April 2026 that number had grown to 5,330 — a 729% year-over-year explosion. Senior FDEs in Silicon Valley now command total annual packages above $500,000.

In early May, Anthropic and OpenAI announced on the same day the establishment of their enterprise services companies. Both invested billions of dollars, and the core organizational model supporting them is the FDE model.

When a role becomes this expensive and grows this fast, it only means one thing: the market desperately lacks these people.

So why the sudden shortage? Behind it lies a major transformation that's underway.

1/ Models are no longer valuable; talent that can implement skills and agents is.

Over the past two years, AI model capabilities have improved rapidly. The models you can use today are far stronger than a year ago. Models are like electricity from a power plant — increasingly abundant. But no matter how much electricity there is, people can't use it directly from the power plant — unless someone runs the wires to your home, installs the outlets, and connects your appliances.

The bottleneck for enterprises has never been whether models are strong — it's that no one can wire this AI electricity into their business. The person who runs the wires and installs the outlets is the FDE.

2/ Tens of millions of companies have reached the point where they must transform.

Right now, from factories to clinics, from law firms to restaurants, virtually every industry and every company is being pushed by two forces: using AI to cut costs and boost efficiency, and using AI to create new business. Bosses have all heard how powerful AI is, and they're all anxious — but the vast majority don't know where to start, and they don't have anyone who can help them implement it.

This isn't the demand of a handful of tech companies — it's the common, urgent need of tens of millions of companies across every industry. You could say that in the future, every industry and every company will need its own FDE.

3/ People who can do this work are currently extremely rare.

A qualified FDE needs two capabilities:

1/ Genuinely understanding AI — at minimum able to build skills and agents, with AI thinking, and ideally AI programming skills as well.

2/ Understanding business and commerce — able to map a company's or boss's strategy down to business implementation, understand business decomposition and work SOPs, and identify the scenarios in work SOPs that most need AI employees and skills.

The FDE position requires both capabilities simultaneously. Most people only have one: the technically strong can't figure out the company's financials; those who understand business can't work with AI. What's worse, in China we don't yet have a system to cultivate such people — schools don't teach it, and there's no established path in the market.

Supply is nearly zero, while demand comes from tens of millions of companies. Even in a top national AI community and AI training organization like Pojue, I estimate no more than 2,000 members have the above capabilities.

At the same time, I believe the demand for FDEs and AI enterprise services implementation is not a passing trend. It's a new, structurally long-term scarce demand that has grown out of AI reshaping every industry. In the last Kondratiev cycle, the last technological revolution, the scarce resource was people who could write code; in this AI technological revolution, the scarce resource is people who can make AI produce results in real businesses.

Is this far removed from us?

Actually, many people in our community are already on this path.

In my previous two pieces on AI enterprise services, I mentioned cases like Xiao Lin, Lin Jie, Shi Tou AI, Hamo, Di Shu, and A Chao. Some have helped companies build AI customer acquisition systems and gained hundreds of paying enterprise clients; others have turned AI into an enterprise service product deployed across several companies in different industries.

What they're doing is essentially what FDEs do: landing AI in company after company, helping these businesses rapidly become AI-driven. They are the earliest prototypes of FDEs around us.

Recently, action-taker Shi Tou AI, leveraging FDE capabilities, signed enterprise contracts exceeding 1 million yuan. Another recent action-taker, Lin Jie, incorporated enterprise AI implementation into his AI one-person company training — a two-day, one-night course that generated 200,000 yuan in revenue from just two sessions, and he's currently negotiating multiple large FDE deals.

And as the wave of enterprise AI transformation rises, more and more people and companies will move in this direction. What we're seeing now is just the beginning of the opening act.

So what exactly can an FDE do for a company?

Simply put, it's translating the boss's statement "I want to use AI" into a system that actually runs.

Help the boss see clearly where AI can actually land in his industry and his company, so he doesn't waste money. Build the selected scenarios into a genuinely usable system with AI, turning processes that used to rely on manual grind into automation. Train employees to use the system and make it truly run — not just launch it and let it gather dust.

Going further, help the company sustain this business line long-term with annual contracts; and ultimately, package the proven playbook for an industry into a product, sell it to the entire industry, serve more bosses, or even build it directly into an AI SaaS. Every one of these things is what companies most urgently need right now — and what they're most willing to pay for.

The first capability: AI technical implementation — turning solutions into systems that actually run.

Someone who can independently build a product with AI coding tools, who can set up agents, skills, and automated workflows—and the stronger ones can also call APIs, connect data, build knowledge bases, and deploy systems to production.

Second, industry understanding—knowing where AI should actually be applied.

You can deeply grasp an industry's business processes, cost structure, and profit logic, and spot which part of the industry is the most labor-intensive and most worth replacing with AI employees.

Third, delivery capability—turning technology into results customers are willing to pay for.

You can talk with bosses as an equal, dig out real needs, translate pain points into solutions, deliver visible results, and distill your experience into a replicable methodology. This step is the key to turning the first two into results.

Technology, industry, and delivery—stack these three together, and you have a top-tier FDE.

How do you grow these three capabilities, step by step?

Step one: pick an industry you truly understand or are truly willing to go deep into, commit to it, and don't be greedy.

Step two: within that industry, find a scenario—using your own business or helping a friend's company for free—and personally run through a complete AI implementation loop, using skills and AI employees to produce a visible result.

Step three: distill the entire implementation process—the pitfalls you hit and the paths that worked—into a replicable methodology. This is the foundation for taking on projects, quoting prices, and leading people later on.

Step four: start by helping a company with one part of the process, then gradually move up to building entire systems, signing annual contracts, leading teams, and even turning your methodology into an AI product to compete in a market segment.

This path has four rungs: from someone who can use tools, to someone who can help one company implement AI, to an operator who can help an entire industry implement it, to someone who can lead a team to compete in a market segment.

Why is now the moment of opportunity and windfall?

Because this wave in China has only just begun, and the window is long.

AI transformation isn't a short-lived trend lasting a few months—it's a massive migration spanning years and covering every industry. Those who enter now won't just catch a short market wave; they'll reap the long-term dividends of the entire transformation cycle.

Meanwhile, most people are still stuck in the excitement of "I just learned to use another new AI tool." Only a tiny minority truly understand that the wind has shifted from using tools to implementing AI. This cognitive gap is your window.

If you're in a position to, dive into this direction now. A year from now, you'll have several real implementation cases, a methodology you've blazed yourself, and a group of enterprise clients who trust you.

Finally, let me share some reflections and the negative energy of breaking through.

FDE—doing AI implementation for Chinese enterprises—is, in my view, an extremely scarce and extremely valuable direction right now, with the dividends just beginning to open. And the company breaking through is Pojie itself, China's earliest AI training company, and even one of the earliest to offer AI enterprise services.

I don't want this insight to remain just an insight. I plan to launch an FDE Pojie Action, systematically guiding everyone toward the FDE direction and bringing out China's own FDEs, batch by batch. Of course, its prerequisite Pojie Actions are the Skills Action Camp and the Agent Action Camp.
