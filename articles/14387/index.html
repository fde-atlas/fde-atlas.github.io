Google Cloud customer engineer vs FDE

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 14 min read

Summary

Drawing on Google's own job postings, this article contrasts the Google Cloud Customer Engineer with the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). The customer engineer supports technical sales through demos and proofs of concept, then hands the delivery plan to implementation teams; the FDE embeds in the customer's environment to code, debug and ship agentic solutions, including eval pipelines and observability. The article compares pay structures (same components, but a larger bonus share for the CE), notes FDE postings list up to 50% travel, and summarizes interview reports: Google publishes no CE loop details, while Blind candidates describe a two-round FDE loop with a CTO role-play design round and a coding round of disputed format.

Key points

  • The customer engineer drives the technical win, then hands delivery off.
  • The FDE codes and ships agentic solutions inside customer systems.
  • FDE IV postings require 8 years of software development experience.
  • Blind reports a two-round FDE loop: design role-play plus coding.
  • Both roles share pay structure; CE bonus is a larger share of base.

You have a Google Cloud Customer Engineer posting open in one tab and a posting in the other. Both put you in front of customers, both sound technical, and the one you apply for decides what you spend the next month preparing to prove. In short, the customer engineer helps the customer decide and hands delivery on; the FDE does the delivery, inside the customer’s systems. For the wider comparison across every company, read the FDE vs sales engineer guide.

Customer engineer vs FDE at Google: the short answer

Google’s Platform Customer Engineer posting describes someone who partners with technical sales teams, runs proofs of concept and demos, drives the technical win, and then hands the final delivery plan to implementation teams. Source 1Customer Engineer, Platform, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting Google Cloud’s GenAI FDE postings describe an embedded builder who codes, debugs and jointly ships agentic solutions inside the customer’s environment, as opposed to a traditional advisory role. Source 2Forward Deployed Engineer IV, GenAI, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting

Put plainly: the customer engineer helps the customer decide, and the FDE builds the thing inside the customer’s walls. The first job hands the delivery plan to someone else to build. The second job is the building.

What the customer engineer posting describes

The Platform Customer Engineer posting reads like the arc of a sale: Source 1Customer Engineer, Platform, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting

  • Partner with technical sales teams to differentiate Google Cloud to customers.
  • Troubleshoot technical questions and roadblocks.
  • Run proofs of concept and demos, and architect cross-pillar cloud solutions for the customer’s business problems.
  • Drive the technical win.
  • Hand off: support pricing activities and move the final delivery plan to implementation teams.

The last line is the one to underline. The CE hands the final delivery plan to implementation teams; building it is another team’s work. Source 1Customer Engineer, Platform, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting

That shapes the stories a CE interviewer would want to hear, in our reading of the posting: a demo that changed a customer’s mind, a proof of concept scoped small enough to succeed, a technical objection answered before it stalled the deal. Those are real skills. They are not the same skills the FDE postings ask for.

What the FDE postings describe

Google uses the exact title “Forward Deployed Engineer” with numeric levels, such as “Forward Deployed Engineer IV, GenAI, Google Cloud” and “Forward Deployed Engineer III, Generative AI, Google Cloud”. Source 2Forward Deployed Engineer IV, GenAI, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting

The GenAI postings say you will move beyond high-level architecture to code, debug and jointly ship bespoke agentic solutions directly within the customer’s environment. Source 2Forward Deployed Engineer IV, GenAI, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting They also list building evaluation pipelines and observability frameworks for agentic systems, and turning repeatable field patterns into reusable modules or product feature requests. Set that beside the CE posting and the difference is where the hand-off sits. The CE posting passes delivery to implementation teams; the FDE postings put the engineer inside the delivery. Same customer, two jobs: a worked example

Here is one fictional deal, seen from both seats. A regional grocery chain wants an AI that answers store managers’ questions about stock and orders, using its inventory system.

The customer engineer’s week. You join the account executive in a meeting with the chain’s head of IT. The objection comes fast: “Our inventory data lives in an on-prem database and it isn’t moving.” You build a proof of concept on a sample export, showing the agent answering the questions managers really ask. You sketch how the pieces would connect, answer the security team’s questions, and the chain agrees to the technical case. You write the delivery plan and hand it to implementation.

The FDE’s week. You are embedded with the chain’s engineers. You find that the managers’ most common question depends on a nightly batch job that runs hours late on Mondays, so the agent gives confident, stale answers. You build the connector the plan assumed already existed. You write an evaluation set from real manager questions and catch the agent confusing “on order” with “in stock”. You ship to a few pilot stores, watch the traces, and turn the connector into a reusable module for the next retailer.

Same customer, same product. Ask yourself which of those two weeks you already have evidence for, because that is the evidence each posting asks for.

Pay: the same structure, different jobs

As of September 2026, the Platform Customer Engineer posting states US pay as a base range plus a bonus target plus equity plus benefits, without the word “quota”, and Google’s FDE IV, GenAI posting states US pay in the same shape. Source 1Customer Engineer, Platform, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job postingSource 2Forward Deployed Engineer IV, GenAI, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting

Both list the same parts, but the bonus target is a larger share of base pay in the CE posting than in the FDE IV posting, and the FDE salary guide has the figures. Source 1Customer Engineer, Platform, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job postingSource 2Forward Deployed Engineer IV, GenAI, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting What the bonus is tied to matters more than its name, so ask the recruiter:

  • “What is the bonus target tied to: company performance, team results or my own targets?”
  • “Does any part of it depend on deals closing?”
  • “Which level is this offer at, and what would the next level expect of me?”

A bonus tied to deals closing makes a role more sales-shaped, whatever the title says. The posting won’t tell you; the recruiter can.

Travel and where the work happens

As of September 2026, Google’s Applied AI FDE postings at levels III and IV list “Ability to travel up to 50% of the time” under preferred qualifications, call the role “high-travel”, and place delivery at the largest customers’ sites. Source 5Forward Deployed Engineer III, Google Cloud, Applied AI — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job postingSource 6Forward Deployed Engineer IV, Applied AI, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting

Read that carefully. It is a preferred qualification in one group of FDE postings, not a promise about every FDE role. We have no travel line from the Customer Engineer posting, so don’t assume it is lighter or heavier.

Ask both recruiters the same thing: “In a normal month, how many days would I spend at customer sites, and how long does one engagement last?” The lesson on travel, location and on-site time has the follow-up questions.

Which one fits you

These are our heuristics, not Google’s criteria.

The customer engineer is likely the better fit if

  • You like being in the room when the customer decides, then moving to the next account.
  • Your best stories are demos, proofs of concept and objections answered.
  • You would rather design across many products than own one codebase in production.

The FDE is likely the better fit if

  • You want to own the code that runs in the customer’s environment.
  • You have stories about debugging a system you did not control.
  • You can explain how you would measure whether an agent works, not only how it is built.

Exact words for “why FDE and not customer engineer?”

If you are moving from a pre-sales seat, expect this question in some form. A strong answer names the part of the work you want, with evidence you have done some of it:

“I’ve spent the last few years on the side of the work that wins the technical decision, and I’ve watched a lot of delivery plans go to implementation. The part I keep reaching for is after that: writing the code inside the customer’s environment and being the one who knows why it breaks. On my last proof of concept I stayed on through the first production release. That’s the job the FDE posting describes, so that’s the one I applied for.”

Swap the last two sentences for your own evidence, then back it up. Prepare the customer-facing experience question with a shipping story, not a deal story.

Common mistakes, and the fix

  • Telling a deal story in an FDE interview. Fix: end on what shipped, who used it and how you knew it worked.
  • Calling a proof of concept “production”. Fix: say what it took to go from the proof of concept to the version people used, or say plainly that you handed it off.
  • Assuming the CE and FDE loops are alike. Fix: we have no evidence either way for the CE loop, so ask your recruiter for both round lists.
  • Practicing with an assistant open. Fix: rehearse the way you will interview, because Google’s published policy says AI tools are not permitted during interviews. Source 7Our hiring process - Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany hiring page
