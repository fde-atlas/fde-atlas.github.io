Today's Judgment

For enterprise AI to move from isolated projects to organization-wide adoption, at least three things must be handled simultaneously: turning private knowledge and rules into executable assets, decoupling model selection and migration capability from business logic, and reusing proven capabilities across departments.

Today's three signals map exactly to these three layers: Kingsoft Office's Hundred-City Agent Program emphasizes the delivery sequence of "data and knowledge governance first, then scenario-specific agents, and finally business system integration"; Google Cloud pushes multi-model routing into the API gateway control plane, making model switching a platform capability; Shanghai's Hongkou District government released a preview of an FDE Enterprise AI Implementation Bootcamp—"FDE" has officially entered the vocabulary of local industry services.

Key Signals

Kingsoft launches Hundred-City Agent Enablement Program: emphasizes the "data—knowledge—scenario—system" delivery sequence

  • Source: Guangming Online | Original link
  • On August 21, Kingsoft Office launched the WPS Comate Hundred-City AI Agent Enablement Program, conducting scenario-based drills for enterprise digital transformation and AI business leaders; it publicly claims coverage of 11 domains and 26 application scenarios, serving hundreds of enterprises. Case figures cited by the media: a security company turned senior employees' experience into rules to achieve 24/7 data monitoring, saving millions of yuan annually; in contract pre-review, after tens of thousands of review emails' experience was written into a rule library, the cycle shrank from two days to 10 minutes.
  • Why it matters: Setting aside the vendor's case numbers (no named customers or independent audits, so they cannot be directly extrapolated), the most valuable part of this signal is the verifiable delivery sequence: data and knowledge governance first → then build dedicated agents around core scenarios → finally integrate with business systems. What enterprises buy is not a general-purpose assistant, but "the capability to sustainably operate after integrating private rules and existing systems"—the real moat is the rule library, not the model. This aligns perfectly with the FDE positioning of "making enterprises Agent Readable": expert experience can only be executed by AI once written as rules; the thicker the rule library, the deeper the moat.

Google Cloud pushes multi-model routing into the API gateway: model switching becomes a platform capability

  • Source: Google Cloud official | Original link
  • On August 22, Google Cloud released an update: API Gateway supports model routing tables, explicit routing rules, and default fallbacks defined via OpenAPI 3.x extensions, and transcodes OpenAI-compatible requests at the gateway before routing them to model endpoints such as Gemini, Claude, and GPT.
  • Why it matters: Model switching, default fallbacks, and traffic governance are becoming platform-layer capabilities rather than something each business application hardcodes itself. The implication for enterprises: decouple "business task contracts, test sets, permissions, and logs" from "underlying models" so you can migrate safely when pricing, capabilities, or compliance change. Every agent project should have a "six-item model routing and fallback table": task contract → primary model → alternative model → trigger conditions → same-sample quality testing → permissions/logging and human fallback. Note: a platform "supporting routing" does not mean the enterprise "automatically gets optimal cost"—routing rules still need to be calibrated with your own business test sets.

Shanghai's Hongkou District releases preview of FDE Enterprise AI Implementation Bootcamp: FDE enters local industry service vocabulary

  • Source: Shanghai Hongkou District government event preview
  • Shanghai's Hongkou District government released an event preview for an FDE Enterprise AI Implementation Bootcamp for enterprises—after the investment community defined FDE as "making enterprises Agent Readable," "FDE + enterprise scenarios" is now entering local governments' industry service systems.
  • Why it matters: This is a landmark signal in the professionalization of the FDE role: the demand side is no longer just tech companies and consulting firms—local governments have also begun treating it as part of enterprise digitalization services. For practitioners, this means two things: first, the FDE training market is opening up; second, government endorsement will accelerate awareness of this role among traditional enterprises. This is only an event preview for now; course outcomes and enterprise adoption data remain to be tracked.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • A capability only counts as replicable if it has a "cross-department asset card": capability name → departments where validated → applicable departments → data inputs → deployment prerequisites → owner → testing evidence → reuse status → failure/fallback. The hardest part of enterprise AI is not building the first feature, but making sure the second department doesn't have to restate its requirements from scratch—without an asset card, every department repeats the project initiation process.
  • A rule library is a longer-lasting moat than models: turning senior employees' judgment experience into AI-executable rules creates an asset that doesn't depreciate when models are swapped. Vendor case savings figures can only be referenced for their evidence structure; external citations must use data confirmed by your own customers.
  • Add a model routing and fallback table to every project: primary model, alternative model, trigger conditions, quality test set, fallback method—the model market changes fast, and business processes must not spiral out of control along with it.
