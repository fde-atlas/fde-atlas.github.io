Palantir FDSE Salary: Base Salary Range and Total Compensation

Original title: Palantir FDSE 薪资：底薪区间与总薪酬

FDE Times · 2026-10-27 · 5 min read

Summary

This article compares four Palantir FDSE job postings. Three FDSE roles—in Colorado Springs (Government), New York, and Seoul—all list the same base salary range of $135,000–$200,000, showing location doesn't change the posted band. A Chicago new-grad Commercial role caps at $145,000, which the author attributes to seniority level rather than lower Commercial pay. Total compensation also includes RSUs and signing bonuses excluded from posted ranges, so offers should be evaluated year by year. A Top Secret clearance is required for Government roles but brings no salary premium; engineers without clearance should target Commercial or untagged roles.

Key points

  • Three FDSE postings across cities share the identical $135,000–$200,000 base range.
  • The $145,000 cap on the new-grad Commercial role reflects level, not department.
  • levels.fyi estimates median FDSE total compensation around $208,000.
  • RSU value is uncertain; year-one pay can exceed year-two pay.
  • Top Secret clearance is an entry barrier, not a salary premium.

Key Takeaways

  • Palantir's Government role in Colorado Springs, its New York role, and its Seoul role all list the same base salary range: $135,000–$200,000 per year.
  • The New Grad - Commercial role caps at $145,000. This gap reflects seniority level and is not enough to conclude that Commercial pays less.
  • The Government role states that the base salary range excludes signing bonuses, benefits, and the future value of RSUs, and requires an active Top Secret security clearance.

Palantir has one Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE) opening in each of three locations: one in Colorado Springs for US Government work; one in New York with no business unit listed; and one in Seoul. All three list exactly the same estimated base salary range: $135,000–$200,000 per year.

The fourth opening is a Commercial new grad role in Chicago. Its lower bound is also $135,000, but the upper bound stops at $145,000.

If you are preparing to apply, or already hold a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) offer, this detail is worth a close look. Across these roles, neither the Government label nor the location changed the posted base salary range. The differences more likely come from three places: seniority level, the portion of compensation not captured in the posted numbers, and a requirement that technical skill cannot substitute for.

Three cities, one base salary band

The context of the three roles differs considerably. The Colorado Springs role belongs to the US Government; the New York role is hybrid with no business unit specified; the Seoul role is labeled "Korea Forward Deployed."

Even so, all three give the same range: $135,000–$200,000. The Colorado Springs and New York roles also state that total compensation may include restricted stock units (RSUs) and a signing bonus.

So at least on paper, location neither raises nor lowers the range. The range spans $65,000, with the gap between the upper and lower bounds approaching half of the lower bound. With a range this wide, the real question is where you land within it, not which city you apply to.

The biggest variation is in the upper bound. And the role with the lowest upper bound is not the Government role; it is the new grad role.

Is Commercial pay lower, or is it just the new grad level?

At first glance, it is easy to conclude that at the top of the range, Commercial pays $55,000 less than Government. But that conclusion does not hold. Of the four roles, the only one explicitly labeled Commercial is also the only new grad role, so two variables are entangled.

Another source supports this reading. Aced (formerly Exponent) compiled official job postings from September 2026, and its records show a new grad base salary range of $135,000–$145,000 for the NY/DC region, matching the Chicago role. In other words, $145,000 is the cap for the new grad level, not for Commercial.

The lesson from reading job descriptions: check the seniority label first, then the business unit label. If the Commercial division has regular FDSE openings, they most likely use the same $135,000–$200,000 range as the other three roles. No such posting is currently available to verify this, so it remains an inference, not a confirmed fact.

What the job postings leave out is what matters most

The Government role is explicit: the range is base salary only. This estimate excludes signing bonuses, benefits, and the future value of long-term incentives. The same posting also states that total compensation may include RSUs, a signing bonus, and future long-term incentives.

This is where many people compare the wrong things. Aced, citing levels.fyi data, puts the median FDSE total compensation at roughly $208,000, and explicitly notes this is an estimate, not a figure published by Palantir. levels.fyi calculates total compensation as base salary plus annualized stock plus bonus.

So putting $208,000 next to the $200,000 base salary cap and concluding "stock barely adds anything" compares two different things: one is a median of total compensation, the other is only a base salary cap.

In the UK, levels.fyi only gives a median FDE total compensation of £140,114, without breaking it down into base salary, stock, and bonus. With a figure like that, you only know the total, not how much is guaranteed cash and how much depends on the stock price.

A hypothetical offer shows why you need to calculate year by year. Suppose a base salary of $150,000, an RSU grant of $200,000 vesting evenly over four years, and a $20,000 signing bonus. Year one comes to $150,000 + $50,000 + $20,000 = $220,000. Year two drops to $200,000, because the signing bonus is paid only once.

This example also assumes the stock price stays flat. The future value of RSUs is excluded from the posted range precisely because it is uncertain. When you hear a first-year total, ask which part is cash and which part depends on the market.

A security clearance is an entry barrier, not a salary premium

The real dividing line is the entry requirement. The Colorado Springs role requires an active US security clearance at the Top Secret level or higher. The Seoul role requires an active Top Secret clearance and willingness to apply for Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI) eligibility.

A security clearance also does not show up as a pay premium: the Government role requires Top Secret, yet its posted base salary range is identical to the New York role. But without an active clearance, no matter how well you perform in deployments, the doors to both types of roles are nearly closed.

Most engineers worldwide do not have US security clearances and cannot obtain one through training. For them, the priority should be Commercial roles and roles without a listed business unit. The New York role shows that an FDSE role without a Government label still posts the full $135,000–$200,000 range.

Which numbers should you use to negotiate?

The base salary range spans $65,000, and RSUs and signing bonuses are on top of the posted figure. When talking to the recruiter, focus on three questions: which level you are placed at; what vesting schedule applies to the RSU grant; and whether the signing bonus is paid in one lump sum or in installments, and under what conditions.

The job posting answers none of these, so you have to ask directly.

Palantir does not explain what factors would push a candidate toward $135,000 or toward $200,000. The most plausible explanation for the gap between the new grad role and the regular FDSE role is level, and level is something you can prepare for right now. In your resume and interviews, do not just list the technologies you have used.

Explain which part of the deployment you were responsible for: who the client was, what decisions you made when no one assigned you work, and what the measurable outcomes were.

New grads should see the $145,000 ceiling for what it is: the ceiling of a level, not the ceiling of the career. Moving up to the regular FDSE band already has a public price tag, and that price tag is the same in Colorado Springs, New York, and Seoul.
