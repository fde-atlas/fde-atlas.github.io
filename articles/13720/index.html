The Forward Deployed Engineer: the book

O'Reilly Media · 2026 · 2 min read

Summary

This is an early-release announcement for O'Reilly's book The Forward Deployed Engineer. The author argues that FDE craft knowledge circulates mostly by word of mouth and recruiting copy, so the book documents it systematically. Chapters follow the shape of a real deployment: finding the real problem behind stated requirements, mapping systems as they actually are, building the smallest deliverable that proves the path, hardening and handing over, and navigating organizational politics. It targets practicing FDEs seeking vocabulary, engineers wanting to enter the role, and managers building an FDE function for the first time. The book assumes readers can build software but not that they have customer-facing experience.

Key points

  • Book is organized around a real deployment's shape, not technologies.
  • Discovery chapter covers finding actual decision-makers behind stated requirements.
  • First deliverable should be evidence, not infrastructure.
  • Exit design ensures deployments don't depend on the engineer.
  • Audience: practicing FDEs, aspiring ones, and first-time FDE managers.

The handbook for engineers who sit closest to the customer and have to make the software actually land: the deployment playbook, the discovery process, the failure modes, and the organisational politics nobody writes down.

01 What the book covers

Most writing about this role is recruiting copy. The craft gets passed around verbally and never written down: how you run discovery when the stakeholder is hostile, how you scope something you have never seen before, when to build the throwaway version.

That gap is what the book is for. It is organised around the shape of a real deployment rather than a list of technologies, because the technologies change and the judgment does not.

  • 01

    Finding the real problem

    The stated requirement is rarely the operative one. Who actually decides, how to find them, and how to run discovery when nobody wants to give you a straight answer.
  • 02

    Mapping the system as it is

    Not as the architecture diagram claims. Working with more systems of record than anyone admits, the critical spreadsheet, and the integration one person understands.
  • 03

    Building the smallest thing that proves the path

    Why the first deliverable is evidence, not infrastructure, and how that differs from everything product engineering teaches you.
  • 04

    Hardening, handing over, leaving

    Knowing which scaffolding to throw away, and designing an exit so the deployment does not depend on you.
  • 05

    The politics

    Where the role sits on an org chart, why it sits there awkwardly, and how to deliver bad news early without losing the account.

02 Who it is for

Engineers already doing the work who want a vocabulary for it. Engineers who want to move into it and cannot find anything honest to read. And the managers standing up an FDE function for the first time, who need to know what the role is actually for before they hire six of them.

It assumes you can build. It does not assume you have ever been in front of a customer.
