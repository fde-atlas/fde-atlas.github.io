Ownership audit

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 1 min read

Summary

This article introduces the ownership audit (also called the we-to-I audit), a method for rewriting interview stories so every decision has a named owner: first-person for what you decided and did, role-based credit for what others did. It targets vague collective “we” and passive phrasing that hides who decided. The author cites Leo Mehr of Ramp, on a July 2026 podcast, saying he assesses ownership by probing candidates' decisions; prep site Aced claims hiring managers prefer “I did” over “we did”, citing no source. After auditing, candidates should anticipate follow-ups on each “I”. If no personal decision remains, the fix is a different story, not better pronouns.

Key points

  • The ownership audit rewrites stories so each decision has a named owner.

Full text

An ownership audit is a pass over a story you plan to tell in an interview, rewriting it so every decision has a named owner: what you decided and did, in the first person, and what others did, credited to them by role. It replaces the collective “we” where you mean “I”, and passive phrasing such as “the decision was made to roll back”, which hides who decided. The audit, also called the we-to-I audit, is our method.

In FDE interviews

Expect behavioral questions and project deep dives to test what you personally were responsible for. Ramp’s Leo Mehr said, on a podcast published in July 2026, that he assesses ownership by asking candidates about the decisions they made and what they were responsible for. If the audit leaves you with no decision you made, the fix is a different story, not better pronouns.

The lesson The ownership audit: from ‘we’ to what you did walks through collective and passive phrasing, crediting the team fairly, and a before-and-after rewrite.
