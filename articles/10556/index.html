Domestic AI Office Productivity Race Intensifies: Tencent, Alibaba, and ByteDance Integrate Products and Compete for FDE Talent

Original title: 国内AI办公赛道竞争加剧：腾讯、阿里、字节三巨头产品整合并争夺FDE人才

虎嗅 · 2026-08-28 · 10 min read

Summary

The article analyzes intensifying competition in China's AI office software market. Within one month, Tencent merged QClaw into WorkBuddy, Alibaba launched Qianwen Office, and ByteDance folded Feishu, TRAE and Coze into Doubao Work, while all three began hiring FDEs at scale. The author reviews milestones from July 2025 to March 2026 and argues AI spreadsheets are the real battleground: they encode processes as data and can replace much traditional OA and lightweight business software. However, spreadsheets solve implementation, not process definition—enterprises often cannot articulate their own workflows, so FDEs must work on-site to turn vague processes into working systems. Since no giant can hire thousands of FDEs alone, the author expects investment in vertical AI firms and conversion of system integrators into an FDE ecosystem, making this ultimately a contest over ecosystem positions.

Key points

  • Tencent, Alibaba and ByteDance consolidated AI office products within a month and are hiring FDEs at scale
  • AI spreadsheets encode processes as data and threaten traditional OA and lightweight business software
  • Spreadsheets solve implementation but not process definition, which requires on-site FDE work
  • Giants will build core FDE teams, invest in vertical AI firms, and convert integrators into an FDE ecosystem
  • The winner will combine employee-facing products, FDE-led enterprise adoption, and ecosystem-scale replication

Behind this table is the result of fierce competition in China's AI office productivity track, as Tencent, Alibaba, and ByteDance completed strategic consolidation of their AI office products within one month:

  • Tencent merged QClaw into WorkBuddy;
  • Alibaba released Qianwen Office;
  • ByteDance merged Feishu, TRAE, and Coze all into Doubao and launched Doubao Work;

All signs indicate: with Agent technology maturing and market validation complete, the big players are all gearing up!

Next, the competitive landscape is quite clear, but as for how exactly this battle should be fought, what is being fought for, and how to achieve that result, the companies seem to have given similar answers:

  • At the top, integrate products as quickly and thoroughly as possible, polish the product experience, and make sure the products don't drop the ball;
  • At the bottom, a talent war around FDE is quietly heating up—ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent have all, coincidentally, begun large-scale hiring of FDE engineers;

So, what exactly is going on here? Let's review the history:

Key timeline of the past year

July 2025: Feishu Future Unlimited Conference

On July 9, 2025, the Feishu Future Unlimited Conference centrally released Knowledge Q&A, AI Meetings, Aily, and Feishu Miaoda, along with a major upgrade to Base (multidimensional tables). Base's monthly active users approached 10 million, with synchronized enhancements in capacity, dashboards, application mode, and AI fields.

This launch event already presented a closed loop of data—knowledge—collaboration—application:

  • IM, meetings, and documents collect process information;
  • Knowledge Q&A retrieves messages, documents, and knowledge bases with permissions;
  • Base carries structured data and lightweight workflows;
  • Aily and Miaoda help enterprises create AI and business applications.

On July 26, Coze Studio and Coze Loop were open-sourced, further expanding ByteDance's coverage in the Agent developer and evals space—though this area didn't generate much buzz afterward, and after OpenClaw exploded in popularity, the Coze ecosystem was once in a rather awkward position.

At this stage, Coze is still actually solving many concrete problems for enterprises, but people who use Coze carry the label of being "behind the times"—and mind you, only a year has passed...

August 2025: AI DingTalk 1.0

In August 2025, DingTalk released AI DingTalk 1.0. DingTalk ONE attempted to reorganize work entry points, DingTalk A1 and AI Listening Notes collected voice and meeting data, AI Search & Ask connected enterprise information, and AI Tables extended toward automation and business applications.

To be honest, I think several of the things Wu Zhao's team built at this stage were quite good, including the A1 recording device, which many people building Obsidian knowledge bases now love to use:

On August 20, WeCom 5.0 was released. Smart Search linked chats, documents, meetings, and emails; Smart Summarization handled group chats and work progress; Smart Bots supported enterprises in creating dedicated AI roles; and Smart Tables continued to strengthen data collaboration.

By this stage, all three platforms had IM, documents, meetings, knowledge search, AI tables, and intelligent assistants. Feature modules began to converge, and differentiation gradually shifted toward product experience, data structure, open platforms, and customer ecosystems.

Speaking frankly, at this stage the real fight was between Feishu and DingTalk—Tencent's products in this area were quite mediocre.

December 2025 – March 2026: Ecosystem building

On December 23, 2025, the AI DingTalk 1.1 "Mulan" launch event. DingTalk proposed Agent OS and showcased a matrix including DingTalk ONE, DingTalk Real, AI Search & Ask, Wukong, and the enterprise AI platform DEAP.

AI's role was upgraded from a content-assist tool to a task-executing entity, and DingTalk began building both an Agent runtime environment and an enterprise Agent management platform.

Then came the much-anticipated OpenClaw black swan. In early 2026, OpenClaw gave ordinary users an intuitive view of the possibility of Agents calling tools and continuously executing tasks, sparking a "self-distillation" craze, with all kinds of skills emerging one after another.

On March 9, WorkBuddy lowered the usage barrier with no deployment required, multi-model support, Skills, and mainstream IM integration, and from then on WorkBuddy entered a league of its own.

On March 17, DingTalk released Wukong, positioned as an enterprise AI-native work platform. Wukong can connect to DingTalk accounts, permissions, and applications, run in a controlled environment, and incorporate enterprise Skills, MCP services, scheduled tasks, and human confirmation into the execution process.

On March 27, DingTalk Workspace CLI was open-sourced, with the first batch covering capabilities such as AI Tables, Calendar, Logs, To-dos, Contacts, DING, and Attendance.

The next day, Feishu CLI was open-sourced, covering business domains such as messaging, documents, calendar, Base, email, tasks, and meetings through shortcut commands, curated API commands, and general API calls.

The concentrated emergence of CLIs confirmed a new premise: the future users of software include both humans and Agents. GUIs continue to serve human visual interaction, while CLIs, APIs, and MCPs form the action layer that Agents can invoke.

One thing worth adding here: the CLI reflects a platform's openness and level of investment—by comparison, Tencent's moves in this regard have been relatively few.

However, the incident of DingTalk's 75,000-word resignation letter then broke out. I don't know how it affected Alibaba's AI Office deployment; in any case, all three platforms entered a deeper phase of exploration in the AI office platform period.

Their respective advantages

Currently, WorkBuddy is without question the hottest star in the office space. Tencent has two core advantages here:

  • WorkBuddy has already captured the mindshare of the personal work Agent;
  • WeChat and WeCom hold enterprises' most important customer connections.

For example, in e-commerce, although WeCom isn't very pleasant to use, there's really no way to break out of this system. So I think Tencent's rough roadmap at present is:

While WorkBuddy occupies employees' attention, quickly shore up WeCom's weaknesses

Because there is currently a clear disconnect among the three systems of WorkBuddy, WeCom, and WeChat, and the authorization and permission experience of WorkBuddy connecting to WeCom isn't native enough.

Alibaba: DingTalk + Alibaba Cloud + industry ecosystem

Alibaba's advantage lies in enterprise business depth.

DingTalk has long accumulated a large number of industry applications; Alibaba Cloud has mid-to-large enterprise customers, implementation partners, and traditional software partners. So Alibaba is better positioned to handle complex workflows:

Who initiates; who approves; who executes; who accepts and verifies; which data can be modified; which operations require auditing.

Alibaba has certain advantages in the 2B battlefield, but its current product matrix is too complex—Qianwen Office, Wukong, DingTalk ONE, AI Assistant, and DEAP need further consolidation.

As for exactly how enterprises will pay for it, that's hard to say.

ByteDance: Doubao + Feishu + TRAE + Coze

ByteDance's biggest advantage is that Doubao has already captured the mindshare of C-end users. For example, when I conduct training and ask which AI tools people use, most participants say Doubao.

However, these users treat Doubao as a Q&A bot, not for office work, so ByteDance's advantage in the office space lies in product integration capability and the knowledge work experience.

Doubao has C-end users and a model entry point, Feishu has messaging, documents, meetings, knowledge bases, and Base, TRAE supplements professional execution, and Coze provides an Agent development system.

The data Feishu has accumulated is also particularly well suited for Agents to understand knowledge workers:

Which meetings I attended; which documents I recently modified; what stage the project has reached; which matters need follow-up; who is responsible for what work.

What ByteDance needs to supplement is depth in core business. Knowledge and collaboration data are abundant, but CRM, ERP, supply chain, finance, and production systems still need Volcano Engine, FDEs, and industry partners to keep connecting.

Now let's talk about something different:

AI spreadsheets matter a lot

With the above information in place, let me share some counterintuitive views. First, everyone can answer a question:

Do you think companies with a high level of digitalization prefer to use WorkBuddy, or companies with a low level of digitalization?

This question is actually quite misleading, because the data I've obtained from enterprise consulting shows:

Teams with a low level of digitalization prefer to use WorkBuddy...

Note that I'm talking about liking to use it, not using it well. Also, highly digitalized companies mostly use AI spreadsheets to solve most problems, and they run just fine, with no need at all for WorkBuddy to join...

This answer may differ from many people's intuition, because:

Enterprises really want a lightweight system for distributed multi-person data entry → centralized aggregation → unified analysis → permission-based querying

Excel/OA/low-code platforms have all been competing for this office share, and now the strongest players are Feishu Base and DingTalk AI spreadsheets.

For example, I once worked with a retail company that ran on a single Excel file for 8 years—until the number of SKUs exploded and the logic became so complex that the system truly couldn't keep running.

A large number of small and medium e-commerce companies, distributors, retailers, and foreign trade companies in China may operate in a similar way.

They use Excel to manage hundreds of customers, thousands of SKUs, sales orders, accounts receivable, and inventory, and annual sales of tens of millions is not unusual.

Because Excel has several very important characteristics:

Low learning barrier; fast to modify; data and business logic kept together; business staff can operate it themselves; no need to wait for the tech team.

On this basis, the more powerful Excel-replacement upgrade—AI spreadsheets—naturally becomes highly sought after. Why? Because:

Data itself is the process

So what Feishu, DingTalk, and WeCom are fighting over is certainly not Excel's small market share; they want to use AI spreadsheets to swallow up large amounts of traditional OA and lightweight business software.

Enterprise digitalization has always needed to accomplish a few things, and these are exactly what AI spreadsheets are best at:

Collecting data in a distributed way; merging and organizing data; displaying data by role; driving processes by rules; notifying relevant people at key nodes; and finally producing statistics and analysis.

With field permissions, linked tables, operation logs, automation, message notifications, dashboards, and app mode added, an AI spreadsheet is already a simple programmable database.

Connect it further with IM, docs, organizational relationships, and agents, and a basic AI Office takes shape:

IM handles communication, docs handle knowledge, AI spreadsheets handle business, and agents handle unified interaction

Personally, I believe that for most companies, this lightweight AI spreadsheet system is entirely sufficient as their digital foundation—and only those who have actually used it know how powerful it is.

The last mile

AI spreadsheets are great; they solve the problem of implementation, but not the problem of definition.

I've worked with many companies, and the biggest obstacle before buying an AI spreadsheet isn't not knowing how to use it—it's that they simply can't articulate what their processes look like.

A company wants to build a CRM, but how to divide customer stages, how to allocate leads, when records enter the public pool, who can see which data, which actions to hand to agents, and what metrics to use for final acceptance—all of these need to be defined by the company itself.

AI spreadsheets reduce the cost of code and configuration, but business process sorting, permission design, system integration, and result acceptance still need to be done by people.

For Tencent, Alibaba, and ByteDance, this problem becomes even more concrete:

How do you fit a standardized AI Office product into vastly different enterprise businesses?

Every company has its own CRM, ERP, financial systems, org structure, and SOPs. An office platform can provide IM, docs, spreadsheets, agents, and open APIs, but it cannot know in advance how a retail company manages inventory, or how a manufacturing company approves purchases.

If this problem isn't solved, WorkBuddy, Qwen Office, and Doubao Work will easily remain stuck at individual productivity:

Helping employees write documents; helping employees organize files; helping employees analyze data; helping employees summarize meetings.

To win AI Office, the three major platforms still need to accomplish three things:

Get enterprise data into their own systems; turn enterprise processes into actions agents can execute; convert individual productivity gains into verifiable organizational benefits.

This is why FDEs are starting to become important.

FDE = ground troops

AI Office is a very typical B2C product: the company pays, the employees use it.

Employees liking to use it only proves the product experience is good; for the company to keep paying long-term, it needs to see changes in business data, process efficiency, and operating metrics.

In other words: given the strength of the Big Three, it's hard for them to create much differentiation at the product level, so what will happen next is FDEs competing company by company on the map—it just depends on who lights up more in the end.

FDEs need to go on-site to each company in person, helping enterprises turn processes they can't articulate into systems that actually run

On this basis, Alibaba, Tencent, and ByteDance will also invest in many B2B companies—for example, those that previously did OA systems, or emerging companies doing AI customer service, GEO, and the like.

Their goal is certainly not to make money from these companies; their goal is to use these channels to do FDE work for them and promote their own AI office systems.

There is some business logic behind this.

The Big Three can hardly recruit thousands of FDEs on their own and serve companies one by one—that model is too heavy and hard to scale.

So they will most likely do three things at the same time:

Recruit core FDEs themselves to win flagship customers; invest in or partner with a batch of vertical AI companies; transform existing implementers and software vendors into an FDE ecosystem.

Several important types of players will be involved here:

Traditional OA, CRM, HR, and ERP vendors; implementation partners of Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud, and Volcano Engine; vertical AI companies in AI customer service, sales agents, GEO, etc.; consulting and solutions teams familiar with specific industries; and individuals and KOLs who control traffic, training, and customer resources.

These people are already on-site at enterprises; they know what systems customers use, where processes get stuck, and what bosses are willing to pay for. Having them promote their own business while carrying along their own Office suite—how delightful...

So this FDE battle will inevitably evolve into a fight for ecosystem positions:

Whoever can mobilize more traditional players, AI companies, and individual developers will light up the enterprise map faster

Conclusion

Looking back, the three companies' recent product consolidation and FDE hiring are simply the upper and lower layers of the same strategy.

WorkBuddy, Qwen Office, and Doubao Work are responsible for capturing the employee entry point; AI spreadsheets take on the enterprise's data and processes; FDEs then enter company after company, turning inarticulate businesses into systems that run, and distilling the experience into templates and industry solutions.

Tencent holds WorkBuddy and the WeChat customer ecosystem; Alibaba has DingTalk, Alibaba Cloud, and industry partners; ByteDance relies on Doubao, Feishu, and Base to advance knowledge work scenarios—each has its own advantages.

So what's worth watching next is no longer just product features. Whoever can attract employees with products, win enterprises with FDEs, and then scale through the ecosystem has the better chance of becoming the entry point for the next generation of AI Office.

This battle has only just begun.
