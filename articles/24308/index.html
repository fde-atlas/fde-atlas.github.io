The article outlines the three roles (Product Owner, Scrum Master, developers) and the workflow: product backlog, sprint planning, daily scrum, sprint review, and retrospective.   • Scrum is a team-based agile framework, named after rugby.
  • Three defined roles: Product Owner, Scrum Master, and developers.
  • Workflow uses sprints with backlogs, daily scrums, reviews, and retrospectives.
  • Benefits: quick problem correction, high productivity, predictable effort estimates.
  • Drawbacks: skill gaps among members and high communication requirements.

What exactly is "Agile development," a term often heard in product development?

"Agile" is an English adjective meaning "quick" or "nimble."

It is a management approach in which "design → development → testing" is repeated in small units of work, while developing based on user reactions and data.

Compared to "Waterfall development," a representative method of product development, it shortens the overall system development time through small units of work, enabling faster development, which is why it is adopted worldwide.

There are many methods within Agile development, but the representative ones include:

"Extreme Programming"

"Feature Driven Development"

And "Scrum development," which we will explain in this article.

As mentioned above, "Agile development" has three methods.

"Feature Driven Development"

A development method that emphasizes functional value (Features) for users, such as login and purchase functions

"Extreme Programming"

A programmer-centric development method that emphasizes engineers, designed with specifications, requirement changes, and feature additions anticipated in advance

Scrum development differs from the above two methods in its focus, with team-based development as its defining characteristic.

The name comes from the rugby scrum; it is a method in which the team works together as one, like rugby players forming a scrum, to tackle development.

Its characteristic is that a small team of members collaborates to achieve goals, working in units of short periods called "sprints" defined by the team.

Another characteristic is that Scrum development defines team lists called "backlogs."

These are work plans that clearly state the goals the team should achieve and the features to be implemented,

and can be described as a canvas for managing the progress of the overall plan.

There are two types of backlogs: the "Product Backlog" and the "Sprint Backlog." We will explain these in detail later in "How to Proceed with Scrum Development."

After setting the backlog, the team moves on to the stage of actually starting the work.

Scrum development has three clearly defined roles, each with defined responsibilities.

Here are the three roles in Scrum development.

Product Owner

The person responsible for product development. They oversee the entire product, making decisions such as "What features are needed?" and "What is the priority of this feature?", and are responsible for maximizing value.

Scrum Master

A coordinator responsible for ensuring the project runs smoothly. They check that all team members understand Scrum and are practicing it in development, focusing on bringing out the team's maximum performance.

Development Team Members

Everyone involved in development. Each member contributes to design, implementation, testing, operations, and more, delivering results as a team.

Even if members have areas of weakness, the Scrum team as a whole adjusts while aiming for all members to improve their skills in a cross-functional manner.

Scrum development projects proceed by repeating short cycles called sprints. Here, let's look at the basic flow.

Creating the Product Backlog

The "Product Backlog" is a list of prioritized features and technical improvement requests for the system under development, as well as issues and problems that currently need to be resolved.

It is constantly maintained, with the most important items displayed at the top of the list. It plays an important role in allowing the Product Owner to oversee the entire project and determine recent improvements and priorities.

Sprint Planning

Work is done in units of periods called "sprints" defined by the team. The team considers the goals to be achieved in the sprint, selects the "Product Backlog" items necessary to achieve the sprint goal, and creates an execution plan.

The combination of these three is the "Sprint Backlog" (the team's task list).

It is a planning document for developers that breaks down what can be achieved during this period into feasible task-level detail.

- Daily Scrum

In Scrum development, a meeting is held at a fixed time every day (the Daily Scrum). Here, daily progress is reported, and work is adjusted according to that progress.

Since Scrum development emphasizes communication, many reporting opportunities are provided, and issues arising within the team are shared as needed and addressed immediately.

- Sprint Review

On the final day of the sprint, the deliverables are tested and evaluated. The key evaluation point is whether they meet the criteria defined in the backlog.

Depending on the review results, specifications may be changed and the backlog may be created again.

- Sprint Retrospective (Review)

This is a meeting to reflect on the sprint. In Scrum development, development is never completed in a single sprint; sprints are repeated.

The good points and issues from the current sprint are analyzed and improvement measures are discussed. The results are then applied to the next sprint.

What kind of development is Scrum development suited for? To choose a method that fits your development, it is also important to deepen your understanding of the merits and drawbacks of Scrum development.

Here are three benefits of introducing Scrum development.

Benefit 1: Fast problem-solving

Because the team holds daily meetings (Daily Scrums), you can check the project's current progress and issues. This allows immediate course correction when problems arise.

Benefit 2: High productivity

Scrum development makes it possible to develop in shorter periods than conventional development methods.

In addition, because communication within the team is close, it is easier to grasp the skills of development members, improving both productivity and quality.

Benefit 3: Easy to forecast development hours

Because development is carried out in "sprints," which are particularly short units within agile development, work-hour estimates can be made accurately.

Development proceeds starting from the necessary features, and problems can be detected early, so there are fewer failures and forecasting is easier.

Here are the drawbacks of introducing Scrum development. More detailed failure examples and countermeasures are explained in "What Are the Drawbacks of Scrum Development? 3 Keys to Success Derived from Failures."

Drawback 1: Development quality varies by team member

Scrum development is characterized by teamwork. If there are large differences in the technical level of individual members, education for members becomes necessary, which takes extra time. {{26}Also, if member turnover and new hiring overlap, time is needed to set up new training and onboarding systems, which can disrupt the development rhythm.

Additionally, when member turnover and new hires coincide, it takes time to establish new training and onboarding systems, which risks disrupting the development rhythm.

Drawback 2: Strong communication skills are required

With many meetings, such as the daily Daily Scrum, strong communication skills are required.

In addition, during meetings, members need to share their issues concisely, which also requires strong verbal and information-processing skills.

Since it is a team effort, even if a member has strong specific skills, the team will not function well if there are members with poor communication skills.

Scrum development is a popular framework, but it has the drawbacks mentioned above and is not suitable for every organization. Whether it is suited to large-scale, complex development must be carefully judged in light of your organization's and company's culture. However, in development where services must be changed and users responded to flexibly due to today's rapid pace of change, it is also true that development speed increases dramatically compared to conventional development.

It can be said that it is well suited to development where specifications are not firmly fixed, for better or worse, and where speed is required while responding flexibly with an emphasis on user feedback.
