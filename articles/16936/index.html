A New Engineer Profile That Completes AI Implementation in the Field

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who does not stay confined to a development site but goes directly into the business operations of customer companies, taking end-to-end responsibility for AI design, implementation, and operation. The role was pioneered by Palantir and is rapidly spreading as a core position at AI companies including OpenAI. This article systematically explains "what an FDE is," covering its definition, origins, job responsibilities, and how it differs from other roles.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) literally translates to "an engineer deployed to the front line." It derives from the military term "forward deployed," and refers to engineers who are stationed at and closely embedded in customer sites far from their development base, taking responsibility for everything from identifying problems to implementation and operation.

In traditional software development, roles and physical distance were clearly separated: requirements definition was handled by the customer, while implementation was handled by the development vendor. FDEs deliberately remove this boundary. By having engineers deeply immerse themselves in the customer's business, they achieve a short improvement cycle of "noticing issues in the field, building on the spot, and improving on the spot."

The role of FDE is said to have been first systematized by the US data analytics company Palantir Technologies. Palantir served customers such as government agencies and major manufacturers who handled complex, highly sensitive data, and needed to provide "software optimized for each customer" rather than one-size-fits-all SaaS.

What emerged for this purpose was the FDE way of working: sending engineers into customers' sites to take on everything from understanding data structures to redesigning business workflows and implementing dashboards. FDEs are sometimes described as "strategists who can write code" or "implementers who understand the business."

With the spread of generative AI, the value of FDEs is being re-evaluated. OpenAI has positioned the Forward Deployed Engineer as one of its key hiring positions, defining the role as one that drives the successful adoption of enterprise ChatGPT and APIs. Companies seriously committed to enterprise AI, such as Google, Salesforce, and LayerX, have also begun hiring for FDE-like roles.

Behind this lies the reality that "even when AI can be introduced, getting it to take root in business operations is extremely difficult." Even as models and APIs become commoditized, final outcomes depend heavily on business data, business workflows, and how users actually use the system. It is precisely people who can adjust these in real time in the field who determine AI's ROI.

The work FDEs do in the field can be roughly organized into the following four areas.

  • ① Business understanding and interviews: Structurally grasp the field's tacit knowledge, decision criteria, existing workflows, and evaluation metrics.
  • ② Design and prototyping: Based on that understanding, design solutions encompassing AI, data infrastructure, and UI, and build working prototypes within days to weeks.
  • ③ Implementation and integration: Integrate with existing systems, data sources, authentication, and operational workflows, and bring the solution to a state where it can actually be used in the field.
  • ④ Adoption and improvement: Continuously improve features, prompts, UI, and operational design while gathering feedback in the field.

What is important is that these tasks are not divided among separate people, but are handled across the board by one person or a small team. With fewer meetings and handoffs, the improvement cycle becomes dramatically shorter.

Which roles is an FDE closest to? Let's compare it with commonly compared roles.

  • IT consultants: Focused on strategy planning and requirements definition; they do not usually write code and carry implementation through to completion. An FDE is a "strategist who can write code."
  • SEs and contract development: Develop according to specifications; they rarely go to create the specifications together with the customer. An FDE "enters the field before the specifications are even finalized."
  • Product managers: Decide product direction and priorities. An FDE differs in that they "get their hands dirty for the customer right in front of them."
  • SREs and infrastructure engineers: Their primary goal is stable system operation. An FDE's primary goal is business results (ROI).

A detailed comparison is provided in What's the Difference Between an FDE and an IT Consultant? The Role of the Implementation Lead Who Delivers AI Adoption Results.

To succeed as an FDE, you need three things: hard skills (implementation ability), domain knowledge (business understanding), and soft skills (adaptability). Technical skill comes from SEs, business understanding from consultants, and adaptability from PMs—the essence of the FDE's difficulty and value lies in one person spanning across the strengths of these separate roles.

For a detailed explanation of why these three skill sets matter, see The 3 Skills an FDE Needs: Driving Field AI with Implementation Ability, Business Understanding, and Adaptability.

Many AI adoption projects stall at the PoC (proof of concept) stage and end without ever moving to production. The cause is not technology, but the fact that "there is no one who can absorb the field's tacit knowledge and carry the project through to implementation." The consultant compiles requirements, the vendor develops according to the specification sheet, and the field says "this isn't usable"—and it ends there. This structural failure is analyzed in detail in Why AI Projects Stall at PoC and AI Goes Unused—and How to Avoid It.
