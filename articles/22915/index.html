How to Structure Healthcare FDE Handoff Contracts

FWD Deployment · 2026-11-02 · 9 min read

Summary

This guide explains how to structure contracts when a healthcare forward deployed engineering team exits. Its core argument: HIPAA governs PHI return or destruction under a BAA but says nothing about engineering handoff mechanics, so technical controls and any 30-60-90 day plan must be written into the services agreement. It recommends ending the services agreement and BAA on separate tracks, keeping the BAA alive until PHI is returned or destroyed with a signed certification, and detailing access revocation, knowledge transfer deliverables, and post-handoff support SLA terms.

Key points

  • HIPAA requires PHI return or destruction at BAA termination, where feasible.
  • End the services agreement and BAA separately; the BAA should outlast vendor PHI access.
  • HIPAA does not define account disabling or credential rotation; contracts must specify these.
  • A 30-60-90 day handoff is a contracting choice, with verifiable evidence per phase.
  • Tie final payment to accepted deliverables like runbooks and a signed certification.

Key points

  • At BAA termination, the business associate generally must return or destroy all PHI where feasible, and if that is infeasible, the BAA protections continue for as long as it keeps the data.
  • Ending the services agreement and ending the BAA are two separate decisions. Sequence them so the BAA outlives any vendor access to PHI.
  • HIPAA does not define account disabling, token revocation or credential rotation. Your services agreement has to.
  • A 30-60-90 day handoff is a contracting choice, not a HIPAA requirement. Tie each phase to evidence your team can verify.

When a forward deployed team leaves, the contract has to do three things: end the vendor's PHI access, make the vendor return or destroy the PHI it holds, and move working knowledge to your IT staff. Under HIPAA, at termination of a BAA the business associate generally must return or destroy all PHI where feasible. A 30-60-90 day handoff plan is a common way to organize the rest, but it is an operational choice, not a legal requirement.

What does HIPAA require when a forward deployed team leaves?

HIPAA sets the rules for the PHI. It does not set the rules for the engineering handoff.

A BAA must require return or destruction of PHI at termination where feasible. It must also extend its protections and restrict uses and disclosures when return or destruction is infeasible.

HHS also notes that HIPAA does not generally require a business associate to retain ePHI after services end. So you do not need to leave data with the vendor for compliance reasons. If a vendor wants to keep something, it needs a reason you have accepted in writing.

Should you end the BAA when the services contract ends?

Not necessarily, and not first. Treat them as two tracks with different end dates.

  • Track one, the services agreement or SOW: ends when the work and handoff are accepted.
  • Track two, the BAA: ends only after PHI is returned or destroyed, or after retention is documented as infeasible to return or destroy.

If you terminate the BAA while the vendor still holds data or access, you lose the cleanest place to enforce the return-or-destroy duty. Write the services agreement so the BAA survives until the vendor delivers a signed certification.

If the vendor will keep supporting the system after the team leaves and that support touches PHI, the BAA has to stay in force for that work. Do not let it lapse with the original SOW. This is the answer to the common question of whether a BAA covers ongoing remote support. It does only if it is still in effect and its scope names that support.

What does the HHS model BAA say about the termination process?

The HHS model BAA supports a workflow with distinct steps: written notice, a cure period for material breach, PHI return or destruction, and written notice if return or destruction is infeasible.

Step What the HHS model provides
1. Written notice Termination is triggered by written notice to the vendor.
2. Cure period For a material breach, the covered entity may terminate the BAA and related agreements immediately if the breach is uncured after giving the business associate 30 days to cure.
3. Return or destruction PHI is returned or destroyed where feasible, including PHI held by the vendor's agents and subcontractors.
4. Infeasibility notice If return or destruction is infeasible, the vendor provides written notice describing the conditions that make it infeasible.
5. Continuing protection BAA protections continue for as long as the vendor retains the information, and further uses and disclosures are limited to the purposes that make return or destruction infeasible.

The HHS sample provisions are model language, not a mandatory form. Adapt them to your architecture, subcontractors, backups, disaster-recovery copies, legal-retention duties and state requirements.

Which BAA termination clauses should you add?

Use the HHS language as the base and tighten it for an AI deployment. These are drafting suggestions for your counsel to review.

  • Scope of PHI. State that the termination process covers PHI stored in any form, including copies held outside the principal production environment. For an FDE team, name the places those copies tend to sit: laptops, notebooks, evaluation datasets, staging environments, prompt and output logs, and vendor ticketing systems.
  • Subcontractors. Require the vendor to cause its agents and subcontractors to return or destroy PHI too. The HHS model says the obligation should address PHI held by the vendor's agents and subcontractors.
  • Return versus destruction. Pick one per data category. A BAA may authorize destruction rather than return when both parties agree. Return makes sense for source data you need. Destruction fits working copies and test extracts.
  • Infeasibility notice. Require a written notice describing the conditions that make return or destruction infeasible, as the model BAA provides. Add a deadline for that notice and a duty to name the specific data set and location.
  • Limited retention. If the vendor keeps PHI for its own proper management, administration or legal responsibilities, it must retain only the PHI necessary for those purposes and protect it while retained. Ask for a list of what is kept and why.
  • Continuing safeguards. Require continued Security Rule safeguards on any retained PHI, and return of that PHI when it is no longer needed for the permitted purposes.
  • Termination for breach. Keep the right to terminate the BAA and related agreements immediately for an uncured material breach, with the 30-day cure period from the HHS model. Make sure the services agreement cross-references this so the engagement ends with it.
  • Certification. Add a signed attestation, from a named officer, that return or destruction is complete. Treat this as your own contract term, since the HHS materials describe the duty rather than a certificate.

How do you handle PHI access revocation during the transition?

HIPAA addresses PHI handling, return, destruction and continuing protections. It does not itself define technical steps such as disabling accounts, revoking tokens, rotating credentials or reviewing logs. Those controls have to be written into your services agreement and security procedures.

Here is a structure you can adapt. The timing column holds example targets to negotiate, not standards.

Step What to specify in the contract Example target
Access inventory Vendor lists every person, account, API key, service principal and environment with PHI access Before day 1 of handoff
Staged removal Remove individual FDE access as each duty transfers, not all at once Tied to each 30-60-90 phase
Credential rotation Rotate shared secrets, tokens and keys the vendor ever saw Same day as final removal
Log review Your security team reviews access logs for the engagement period Within a window you set
Subcontractor confirmation Vendor confirms in writing that subcontractor access is also closed With the certification
Break-glass access If the vendor keeps emergency support access, make it time-limited, approved per use and logged Per incident

For the access model itself, see our guide on how to securely grant forward deployed engineers access. The same least-privilege setup makes removal much easier later.

What does a 30-60-90 day knowledge transfer look like?

No HIPAA rule or primary source sets a mandatory 30-60-90 schedule. It is a contracting structure you choose, and its value is that each phase ends with something you can check. Adjust the lengths to the size of the deployment.

Phase Focus Evidence you accept
Days 1 to 30 Shadowing and documentation. Your IT staff watch the vendor run and change the system. Architecture, data flow and runbook drafts delivered. Access inventory complete.
Days 31 to 60 Reverse shadowing. Your staff run operations while the vendor observes and corrects. Your team handles routine changes and incidents. Vendor access reduced to advisory scope.
Days 61 to 90 Independent operation. The vendor is on call only. Final access removal, credential rotation, PHI return or destruction, signed certification.

Make acceptance a gate, not a calendar date. If your team cannot run the system by day 60, the contract should let you extend the overlap at a rate you agreed up front. Our piece on an embedded FDE team versus a project delivery team explains why embedded work needs this kind of exit plan.

What documentation should the vendor hand over?

Name each deliverable in the contract and tie final payment to its acceptance. A workable list for a healthcare AI integration:

  • Architecture diagram and data-flow map showing where PHI enters, is stored and leaves.
  • Inventory of every system, account and subcontractor touching PHI.
  • Runbooks for deployment, rollback, monitoring and incident response.
  • Model and prompt configuration records, plus any evaluation sets and their locations.
  • Known issues and open risks list.
  • Records of third-party controls and monitoring, so you can keep tracking them after handoff.
  • Return or destruction certification, and the infeasibility notice if one applies.

Hold back a share of the final fee until your team has run the runbooks itself. Pick the percentage in negotiation based on how much risk sits in the handoff.

How should you structure the post-handoff support retainer and SLA?

Retainer prices and SLA targets vary by vendor and deal, and no HIPAA rule sets them. Your job is to define the structure, then get quotes against it. When you compare vendors, our methodology page and firm profiles show what we look at.

Define these items before you ask for a price:

  • Scope: which systems, which environments, and whether support ever requires PHI access.
  • Severity levels: what counts as an outage, a clinical-workflow impact or a degraded model, based on your own operations.
  • Response and resolution targets: set by severity, with credits or termination rights if missed.
  • Access terms: on-demand, approved per use and logged, rather than standing access.
  • BAA status: in force for the retainer term, with the return-or-destroy process starting again when it ends.
  • Exit: a notice period and a repeat of the handoff steps above.

When should you get a lawyer involved?

This article is general information, not legal advice, and your counsel should read your BAA, state requirements and retention duties before you sign.

Common questions

What is the standard timeline for a healthcare deployment handoff from a forward deployed team?

No HIPAA rule sets a handoff timeline. A 30-60-90 day plan is a common contracting choice, and you should tie each phase to evidence such as documentation delivered, your staff running operations, and final access removal.

How do forward deployed operations handle PHI access revocation during transition?

HIPAA does not define technical steps such as disabling accounts, revoking tokens or rotating credentials, so your services agreement has to. Specify an access inventory, staged removal, credential rotation, log review and subcontractor confirmation.

What post-deployment SLA metrics should healthcare COOs require in FDE contracts?

Define severity levels, response and resolution targets by severity, remedies for missed targets, and how support access to PHI is approved and logged. The specific numbers depend on your clinical and operational risk and on the deal you negotiate.

Do Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) cover ongoing remote support after the FDE team leaves?

Only if the BAA is still in force and its scope covers that support. If the vendor will touch PHI during a retainer, keep the BAA in effect for that term, and apply the return-or-destroy process when the retainer ends.

How much do enterprise AI firms charge for post-handoff forward deployed support retainers?

Retainer pricing depends on the vendor, scope, coverage hours and access requirements, and we do not publish a benchmark figure here. Define scope and severity levels first, then request written quotes from each firm you are comparing.
