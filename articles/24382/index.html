Differences Between AI Talent Staffing and FDEs | How to Choose an External AI Engineer

Original title: AI人材の派遣とFDEの違い｜外部のAIエンジニアの選び方

FDE Consulting · 2026-10-24 · 6 min read

Summary

This article explains how Japanese companies without in-house AI engineers can bring in external help through three contract types: worker dispatch (haken), quasi-mandate (jun'inin), and contract for work (ukeoi), plus the newer FDE model. Drawing on the Worker Dispatch Law, the Civil Code, and Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare criteria, it argues the key distinction is who gives instructions and who is accountable for results. Dispatch is capped at three years and generally forbids selecting specific individuals. Mislabeling a quasi-mandate while the client directs daily work can create legal problems. FDE is a working style, not a contract type, suited to defining problems jointly and seeing solutions through to adoption.

Key points

  • Dispatch, quasi-mandate, and contract-for-work differ by who gives instructions and who owns results.
  • Legal classification depends on actual working conditions, not contract labels, per MHLW criteria.
  • Dispatch is capped at three years per worksite and generally prohibits picking specific workers.
  • Directing external contractors in detail under a quasi-mandate risks illegal dispatch findings.
  • FDE is a working style, not a contract type, fitting engagements from problem definition to adoption.

When a company without an in-house AI engineer tries to bring in people from outside, there are three main options: temporary staffing (dispatch), quasi-delegation (jun-i nin), and contracting (ukeoi). Recently, support under the name of FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) has also been added. All of these look the same in that "an external AI engineer works within the company."

The difference lies in who gives instructions and who is responsible for the results. Getting this wrong can result not only in the person not working the way you expected, but also in legal problems. This article uses the provisions of the Worker Dispatch Act and the Civil Code, as well as the standards of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, to organize how to use external AI talent. In situations requiring legal judgment, please consult your in-house legal team or experts.

The definition of the FDE role itself is covered in What is FDE? The meaning and work of a Forward Deployed Engineer, and a comparison with SES and SIers is covered in Differences between FDE, SES, SIers, and consultants. This article focuses on the judgment of the ordering party.

Dispatch, quasi-delegation, and contracting differ in who gives instructions

In dispatch, the receiving company gives instructions

The Worker Dispatch Act defines worker dispatch as having one's own employed workers work under that employment relationship, receiving instructions from another party, and working for that other party. A person hired by the staffing agency works under the direction of the client company. The content and methods of the work are decided and directed by the receiving company.

Operating a worker dispatch business requires a license from the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare. When accepting people through dispatch, verify that the other company holds a license.

In quasi-delegation and contracting, the receiving company gives instructions to its own people

Under the Civil Code, contracting is a contract where remuneration is paid for completion of work (Article 632). Quasi-delegation is a contract entrusting the handling of affairs that are not legal acts (Article 656), and it can also promise remuneration for results (Article 648-2). In both cases, it is the receiving company that gives instructions to the people doing the work.

What matters here is that the name of the contract does not determine this. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare states that whether an arrangement falls under worker dispatch or contracting is judged based on the actual circumstances, not the contract format, according to the "Standards Concerning the Distinction between Worker Dispatch Businesses and Businesses Conducted through Contracting" (Ministry of Labour Notice No. 37 of 1986).

Under these standards, even a contract in the form of contracting is deemed a worker dispatch business if the receiving company itself gives instructions on how to perform the work and manages working hours, and handles the work independently as its own business, separate from the client. One condition for independent handling is processing the work based on one's own planning, or based on one's own specialized skills and experience.

What to verify when accepting AI talent through dispatch

Dispatch is a quick way to add manpower if your company has someone who can direct AI work. However, there are several things fixed by law.

Period limit

The Worker Dispatch Act sets the period for which a client can receive dispatch services for work at the same business site at 3 years. This can be extended beyond 3 years after hearing the opinion of a labor union representing a majority of workers, etc. Separately, the same dispatched worker can be accepted for the same organizational unit for up to 3 years. Dispatch of workers employed indefinitely by the staffing agency is excluded from this period limit.

The law also expresses the principle that work through dispatch should be temporary and transient in nature. In my view, if you want to root AI capabilities in your company, a plan relying solely on dispatch is hard to sustain. You need to decide from the start how to document what was built and arrange handover, so things keep running even if the person changes after 3 years.

You cannot select the person

The Worker Dispatch Act requires the client company to endeavor not to engage in acts aimed at specifying the dispatched person. Temp-to-perm dispatch (shokai yotei haken) is an exception. Skill differences among AI engineers are large, but with dispatch you basically cannot select people through advance interviews. What the receiving side can do is write the required technical criteria concretely and communicate them to the staffing agency.

Is there someone in-house who can give instructions?

People who come through dispatch work under the receiving company's instructions. In other words, if there is no one in-house who decides what to build and how to proceed, the dispatched person cannot perform at full strength. In AI work, this is where I see the most stumbling. Choosing how to use AI, gathering data, and setting evaluation criteria are precisely the work of people knowledgeable about AI.

When commissioning external AI engineers under quasi-delegation

Under quasi-delegation, the receiving company gives instructions to its own people. The ordering party decides by contract what they want done and the scope to be entrusted.

The thing to watch out for is when the contract is quasi-delegation but the ordering party directly gives detailed instructions to the external person. Under the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's approach, judgment is based on actual circumstances rather than the contract name, so if this continues with a company that does not hold a dispatch license, it can become a problem. It is better to decide in advance how daily interactions will be divided.

How to choose between quasi-delegation and contracting, and how much AI development to outsource, is summarized in In-house or outsourced AI development.

FDE is a name for a way of working, not a contract type

FDE is not a contract type like dispatch, quasi-delegation, or contracting. It is the name of a role referring to a way of working: entering the customer's site, jointly defining business challenges, building AI systems, and refining them until they are actually used. The contract format differs by the supporting company.

In my view, the difference between dispatch and FDE lies in who decides what to build.

The table is my way of organizing the options. If you already know what to build and have someone in-house who can direct the work, bringing in help through dispatch or contracting is a better fit. If you are at the stage where you want to consult on what should be built, or want someone to see the project through to where the output is actually used, then support in the FDE form is more suitable.
