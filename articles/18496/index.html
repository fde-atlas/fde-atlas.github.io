Generative AI Support Services | Making AI Stick in Your Company

Original title: 生成AIの伴走支援｜使われないAIを社内に定着

株式会社Aiworker · 2025-11-30 · 12 min read

Summary

AIworker, a Japanese firm founded in 2024 by Takayoshi Sugimoto, offers an accompaniment service for generative AI adoption. Instead of training or advice alone, consultants regularly visit client workplaces, observe actual work, propose where AI can help, and build prompts, department-specific chatbots, and simple automations together with employees. Contracts run in 6-month blocks (monthly fees from 200,000 to 950,000 yen), aiming to leave behind a promotion structure, usage rules, manuals, effect records, and internal trainers. The company's own sales-call data found 38% of firms citing AI adoption barriers said usage never spreads beyond a few people, rarely blaming tool performance. A case at a ~70-person staffing firm raised lead conversion from 20% to 60% and cut 282 hours monthly across seven inside-sales staff.

Key points

  • AIworker's consultants embed at client sites and propose AI use cases from observed work.
  • 38% of firms reporting AI adoption problems said usage never spread beyond a few people.
  • A staffing-firm case: lead conversion rose from 20% to 60% in three months.
  • Seven inside-sales staff cut 282 hours of work per month.
  • Plans cost 200,000–950,000 yen monthly, minimum 6-month engagement.

What's happening in the field

Based on our sales meetings (May 2025 – October 2026), 38% of companies that mentioned barriers to AI adoption said "we introduced it, but usage never spread." In consultations, we often hear comments like the following.

Contracted company-wide, but only a few people use it daily

The methods of skilled users haven't been passed on to others.

Training surveys were positive, but things reverted within three months

After training, no opportunities were created to use AI in actual work.

The promotion lead has another main role and can only work on it in spare time

AI adoption hasn't been allocated any dedicated work time.

No decision on what to implement, and few use cases

Internal rules are unclear, so the scope of use remains narrow.

Unable to explain results to management, so no next budget

Time saved isn't being measured, so there's no case for continuing.

Among companies that reported their AI wasn't being used, only a small minority cited tool performance alone. What makes the difference is who runs things and how after implementation.

What we do in companion support

This is not support that ends with advice in a meeting room. Our consultants look at your actual work screens and documents and proceed through the following five steps in order. Even if you haven't defined your concerns yet, we'll find areas for improvement ourselves.

Observation of work processes

We observe workflows, interview the staff, and identify where AI can be used.

Us Create a list of use cases You Show us your work processes

Priorities and success criteria

We set the order starting with high-impact, easy-to-start tasks, and define upfront how many hours saved counts as success.

Us Propose the order and criteria You Decide the target tasks

Building systems on the spot

We build prompts, department-specific chatbots, and simple automations together, without interrupting work.

Us Build together and hand over the methods You Have your staff participate in the work

Department rollout and usage rules

We document skilled users' methods as procedures and hold study sessions so everyone in the department can perform the same operations.

Us Draft procedure documents and rules You Decide the rules internally

Measuring and reporting results

We measure time saved and compile it into a form you can present to management.

Us Create monthly reports You Share usage status

Each month, we compile the issues found and improvement proposals into a monthly report and deliver it to you.

Our strengths

There are many ways to outsource generative AI adoption. Here are three reasons we're the right fit for companies that want to escape the "introduced but unused" state.

Proposals based on observing your work

We find use cases ourselves, without waiting for you to ask

The less a company knows what to use AI for, the fewer questions come up in consultations. That's why we take the approach of observing your work and proposing use cases from our side.

Building together and handing over the methods

We don't stop at advice; we participate all the way to building the systems

If we simply build and hand things over, you won't be able to fix them when your work changes. We build together with your staff and hand over the methods. You can also choose an approach where we don't build anything and your employees build it themselves.

Consistent support from strategy through development and training

Tasks requiring development or employee training continue with the same consultant

Tasks requiring development to connect with internal systems are handed off to AI Agent Development, and if you want to develop employees who can build systems for their own departments, to Internal FDE Training, all with the same consultant.

Companion support case studies

Company names are withheld; only industry and size are shown. Figures are based on each company's own tallies and surveys conducted during the support period.

Launching an internal AI promotion office and expanding it department by department

ChallengeThere was a large gap between employees who used AI and those who didn't, and use cases hadn't been defined per department.
Support providedWe provided companion support for launching their internal AI promotion office. We established promotion leads for each department and created ways to use AI for sales meeting preparation, organizing candidate requirements, and personnel selection.
ResultsIn enterprise sales, the proposal conversion rate from first meetings rose from 20% to 60%, and meeting preparation time dropped from 1 hour to 15 minutes (over 3 months). A team of 7 inside sales staff cut 282 hours per month, and the rejection rate for contract applications fell from 62% to 18%.

Create usage rules and turn them into something used in daily work

ChallengeThe methods of skilled individuals were not shared with others, and there were no clear criteria for what information could be entered.
Support providedWe accompanied the client for 3 months with twice-monthly regular meetings, creating AI usage rules and supporting their use in daily work. The rules we created are now part of company policy.
ResultsOf 5 survey respondents, 4 use it almost every day, with an average usefulness rating of 4.6 (on a 5-point scale). Usage has spread to 25 employees.

Non-IT department employees build their own department's business apps

ChallengeThe goal was to enable non-IT employees who know the business to build systems themselves.
Support providedWe ran two terms of accompaniment: 1 hour weekly for 6 sessions each. The approach was for the consultants not to build anything, but for the employees themselves to create business apps and AI.
ResultsAll 11 departments (cumulative) completed working deliverables within the period. 120 people attended the related training.

Process and Timeline

We estimate about 12 months to full adoption. Contracts are in 6-month increments, and at each point you can review the results and decide whether to continue or stop.

Organizing Operations

We break down operations to clearly identify where AI can be used.

Using AI in Each Operation

Start using AI in the designated operations and reduce gaps between team members.

Months 3-6: Decision Point on Whether to Continue

Expanding to Judgment and Design

Go beyond drafting and summarizing, extending AI to judgment and design processes.

Months 6-9

Establishing AI as Part of Daily Operations

Ensure that even new employees can immediately use the same methods.

Months 9-12

Your company will allocate time for the promotion lead to attend accompaniment meetings and for team members to do the work as part of their duties. In preparation for review by your information systems department, we will provide answers to an information security checklist at the first meeting.

What Remains with Your Company After 6 Months

A system that stops working once outside people leave makes the investment pointless. At the 6-month mark, we leave the following six things so your company can run everything internally.

Promotion Structure

  • A state where the promotion lead and department representatives are designated, and AI use is allocated as work time

Internal Usage Rules

  • Rules written in language frontline staff can use to judge what information may and may not be entered

Use Cases and Procedures per Operation

  • A list of which AI procedures to use for which operations. New employees can get started just by reading it

Department-Specific AI and Prompts

  • Chatbots and prompts built during the accompaniment. These remain as your company's assets

Records of Results

  • Figures such as hours saved and reduced rejections, ready to use directly in budget approval for the next term

People Who Can Teach Internally

  • Employees who can transform their department's operations with AI. For further development, we also offer Internal FDE Development
