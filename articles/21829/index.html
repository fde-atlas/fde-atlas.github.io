What Is an Implementation Engineer? Explaining Average Salary and the Difference from FDE

Original title: インプリメンテーションエンジニアとは？年収相場とFDEとの違いを解説

デファクトリーNote｜事業開発/ソフトウェア/プロダクト開発ならDefactory株式会社 · 2026-08-28 · 16 min read

Summary

This Japanese-language article by DeFactory explains the Implementation Engineer role: deploying and configuring specific software or hardware in customer environments, handling system integration and testing. It is mainly employed by foreign IT vendors like Dell (which offers a VxRail-related DCS-IE certification) and global SaaS companies. Using first-party data from its BizDev Tech job site (August 2026), the article notes no freelance listings exist under the exact job title, but broader “implementation” projects (128 listings) average ¥801,000/month (median ¥735,000), while vendor salaried roles range from ¥5M to ¥13M annually. It compares the role with FDEs and GTM engineers, arguing implementation experience is a solid foundation for shifting toward the FDE career path.

Key points

  • Implementation Engineers specialize in deploying specific products at customer sites, not full-cycle development.
  • Dell's DCS-IE certification shows the role is established inside product vendors.
  • No freelance listings use the exact title; broader implementation projects average ¥801,000/month.
  • Salaried roles at foreign vendors range from ¥5M to ¥13M annually.
  • Compared with FDEs, development involvement is limited, but the experience supports an FDE career shift.

An Implementation Engineer is a technical role responsible for consistently handling the installation and configuration of software and equipment into client environments, as well as integration with existing systems and testing.

This role was established by foreign IT vendors and SaaS companies as part of their customer success strategies to ensure their products take root with customers, and is widely adopted by companies such as Dell, telecom carriers, and global SaaS providers.

In this article, we explain the job duties, required skills, and salary ranges of Implementation Engineers based on actual data from our job site "BizDev Tech, operated by our company", and also explore the differences from Forward Deployed Engineers (FDE), whose popularity is growing, as well as career paths.

An Implementation Engineer is defined as "a technical role specializing in system implementation for client companies." While a typical Systems Engineer (SE) handles a wide range of tasks from requirements definition to development and maintenance, the biggest difference is that Implementation Engineers specialize in the "deployment and rollout phase."

As this table shows, the Implementation Engineer is a specialized role whose scope of responsibility is narrowed down to "making a specific product work reliably in the client's environment."

The title "Implementation Engineer" is a job name that tends to be used in job postings by foreign IT vendors. Even in Google search results, this title frequently appears in Dell employee interviews and job postings in the telecom infrastructure and data center network fields.

Dell offers a specialized certification called "Specialist - Implementation Engineer (DCS-IE)" for its hyperconverged infrastructure product "VxRail," indicating that this role is established as an official career path within product vendors.

The work of an Implementation Engineer can be broadly divided into three areas: "installation and setup," "system integration," and "client interaction."

The core task of this phase is to actually bring the company's products into the client's environment and get them up and running. For software, this involves environment setup and parameter configuration; for projects involving hardware, it may also include transporting and wiring equipment and installing the OS.

Another important task is getting the product being deployed to work seamlessly with other systems already running on the client side. Since server configurations and data formats differ for each client, unexpected issues are likely to arise, and the speed of identifying root causes and responding directly affects evaluations.

In deployment settings, it is essential to pick up on the requests and concerns raised by client representatives on the spot, organize them from a technical perspective, and translate them into solutions. Part of this role's responsibility is also to proactively provide follow-up so that clients continue using the product, rather than simply finishing the deployment.

The required skills can be organized into three categories: "technical depth in the products handled," "interpersonal skills for engaging with clients," and "on-site problem-solving ability."

  • Technical skills: In addition to fundamentals of servers and networks, a deep understanding of the specific configuration and troubleshooting of the products handled (SAP, Salesforce, VxRail, etc.) is required
  • Interpersonal skills: The ability to draw out requests that clients cannot articulate and explain technical terms in plain language. In deployment projects, it is not uncommon for success or failure to hinge more on interpersonal skills than technical ability
  • Problem-solving skills: The practical ability to identify causes and respond on the spot to unexpected issues that arise during deployment

Since implementation work on-site rarely goes exactly according to specification, this role requires not only technical knowledge but also customer-facing skills and sound judgment in the field.

Although the term "implementation engineer" has several spelling variations, they all refer to essentially the same job duties.

  • Implementation Engineer (実装エンジニア): A notation using the Japanese translation of "implementation" (実装). There is no difference in meaning; companies simply use different names.
  • Implementation Service Engineer (ISE): A name that adds the word "service" to include ongoing customer support after deployment. It emphasizes the role of discovering and resolving technical issues together with customers and supporting continued use.

None of these titles have a unified certification system in Japan, and as with Dell's DCS-IE mentioned earlier, it is common for product vendors to establish their own certification programs. The very existence of multiple names suggests that this occupation has not yet been fully systematized or established in Japan.

When we searched our job site "BizDev Tech" for the exact terms "implementation engineer," "実装エンジニア," and "deployment engineer," no matching projects were found as of August 2026. This suggests that this occupation has not yet circulated as an independent job category in the freelance project market, and that hiring is centered on full-time positions at foreign-affiliated vendors.

Implementation engineers are not the only occupation adjacent to Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs). Here we compare two roles: FDEs, which share the common trait of "working deep inside the customer's field," and GTM engineers, which share the common trait of "systematizing the deployment and rollout phase with technology."

Implementation engineers and Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) have much in common in that both "work deep inside the customer's environment to make implementations successful." What is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? refers to a new occupation gaining attention in the AI era, in which engineers embed themselves at or near customer companies and take end-to-end responsibility from development through deployment and adoption.

The experience of "embedding yourself in the customer's environment to make implementations successful" as an implementation engineer overlaps considerably with the competencies explained in FDE required skills and career strategies, making it a solid foundation to leverage when considering a career shift to FDE.

The biggest difference between implementation engineers and GTM engineers is "where they embed themselves." What is a GTM engineer? refers to a role that uses AI, data analysis, and automation tools to build and optimize GTM (Go-to-Market) processes such as sales, marketing, and customer success from a technical standpoint. Unlike implementation engineers, GTM engineers build internal systems for their own company (or a client company) rather than for customer companies.

What they have in common is that both are valued for the implementation ability to "translate a specific business process into a form that actually works in the field." Experience as an implementation engineer can be leveraged as a career path into several adjacent professions.

  • Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE): A career that builds on implementation experience to expand scope into understanding client business and development. See What Is an FDE? Introduction Strategies for Enterprises of the Forward Deployed Engineer, a New Profession in the AI Era for details
  • IT Architect / Solution Architect: After gaining experience implementing individual products, move up to senior roles responsible for overall system design and architecture. Covered in What Is an IT Architect? Required Roles, Essential Skills, and How to Join High-Rate Freelance Projects and A Complete Guide: What Is a Solution Architect? Roles, Essential Skills, Salary, and the Path to High-Rate Independence
  • Going independent as a freelance engineer: After gaining hands-on experience in implementation and integration testing, another option is to go independent as a freelancer. For how to find projects, see 8 Ways for Freelance Engineers to Win Projects

Challenging this role without prior experience is possible, but foundational knowledge of servers and networks is a prerequisite. Many companies have training systems in place where new hires acquire specialized skills through training and certification for their assigned products (SAP, Salesforce, VxRail, etc.) after joining.

The names differ, but the job content is essentially the same. "実装" is the Japanese translation of "implementation"; companies simply differ in whether they use the English or Japanese term.

Full-time positions at foreign IT vendors show a salary range of 5 to 13 million yen. Direct market-rate data for freelance projects is still scarce, but adjacent "implementation" projects (based on data from our own BizDev Tech) show an average monthly rate of 801,000 yen.

It is hard to generalize, but FDEs have a broader scope that extends into development, and because their skills are applicable to areas like AI and data utilization, they tend to have greater room for rate increases as freelancers. See the market trends discussed in What Is an FDE for details.

This role suits people who combine interpersonal skills for carefully listening to client needs with the technical persistence to handle unexpected issues on the spot. It is also a good fit for those who enjoy deeply mastering a single product.

Openings are mainly at foreign hardware vendors like Dell, telecom carrier-affiliated companies, and global SaaS companies. Domestic SIers tend to recruit for similar roles under titles such as "implementation engineer" or "implementation consultant."

There is no unified national certification, but product vendors have their own certification programs. A representative example is Dell's "Specialist - Implementation Engineer (DCS-IE)" certification exam for VxRail.

As of August 2026, there are no projects listed under the exact title "implementation engineer" on our BizDev Tech platform. However, there are plenty of projects in the broader categories of "implementation" and "package implementation," listed at average monthly rates of around 650,000 to 800,000 yen.
