Decompose: design a system to coordinate disaster response for a city.

FDE Interviews · 2026-06-14 · 1 min read

Summary

This article analyzes one of the most open-ended system design prompts in Palantir's Forward Deployed Engineer interview canon: design a system to coordinate disaster response for a city. The prompt specifies no scale, no user, and no disaster type. According to the author, what separates passing answers is a design assumption about data that few candidates state explicitly. Candidates fail when they jump straight to the happy path; strong answers lead with where the obvious approach breaks, trace the failure back through the pipeline in order, and name the one metric the customer's executive sponsor actually cares about before proposing a fix.

Key points

  • The prompt gives no scale, user, or disaster type, testing open-ended design.
  • Most candidates jump to the happy path and lose the room.
  • Passing answers state a data design assumption few say out loud.
  • Lead with where the obvious approach breaks to show judgment.
  • Name the exec sponsor's key metric before proposing a fix.

The most open-ended prompt in the Palantir canon, no scale given, no user given, no disaster given. What separates passing answers is one design assumption about the data that almost nobody states out loud.

Lead with where the obvious approach breaks, because that is the judgment they are screening for — most candidates jump straight to the happy path and lose the room.

Then walk the failure back through the pipeline in order, naming the one metric the customer's exec sponsor actually cares about before you propose the fix.
