Summary

This guide by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, explains how freelance agents work for Japanese IT freelancers: they match engineers with clients under contracting arrangements, taking a 10-30% margin in exchange for job matching, rate negotiation, contract handling, and payment management. It compares major agents (PE-BANK, Midworks, Levtech Freelance, IT Pro Partners, TechStock) on margin rates, direct-client ratios, and average annual income (roughly 8.4-10.7 million yen). Citing market data, it notes Japan's IT freelance market reached about 1.18 trillion yen in 2025, up 1.6x over a decade. The author recommends registering with 2-3 agents and highlights cloud, data engineering, and generative AI skills as paths to monthly rates above 1 million yen. Note the article promotes DeFactory's own BizDev Tech service.

Key points

  • Agents take 10-30% margins but provide matching, negotiation, and legal protection.
  • Japan's IT freelance market hit ~1.18 trillion yen in 2025, 1.6x growth in ten years.
  • Key selection criteria: skill fit, margin transparency, direct-client ratio, payment terms.
  • Registering with 2-3 agents simultaneously is recommended; avoid duplicate applications.
  • Cloud, data engineering, and generative AI skills command monthly rates over 1 million yen.

When considering going independent as a freelance engineer, everyone feels anxious about "how to find projects." That's where "freelance agents" come in to solve this problem.

However, when you actually look into it, there are many services out there—Levatech Freelance, Midworks, IT Pro Partners, TechStock, and more—and many people say, "I don't know which one to choose." Since agents differ greatly in the number of projects they handle, their areas of expertise, margin rates, and support offerings, choosing the wrong one carries risks such as losing income you could have earned or only being introduced to projects that don't match your skills.

In this article, we systematically explain everything from how freelance engineer agents work to how to choose one, their impact on annual income, and the steps from registration to starting a project, all based on reliable data. We provide practical decision-making criteria for everyone from those using an agent for the first time to those considering reevaluating their current agent.

High-rate, direct-contract project information

A freelance engineer agent is a service that matches projects between client companies such as IT firms and startups, and freelance engineers. Unlike recruitment agents, its distinguishing feature is that it mediates "service contracts" between freelancers and clients.

The basic revenue structure is as follows.

クライアント企業 → エージェント（マージン差引）→ フリーランスエンジニア

The client company pays the agent a monthly rate (e.g., 1,000,000 yen), and the agent pays the freelance engineer the amount minus the margin (commission) (e.g., 800,000 yen). The industry-average margin rate is said to be around 10–30%, and whether it is disclosed varies by agent.

① Access to non-public projects

Most of the projects agents hold are non-public and not openly listed. Startups that are cautious about hiring engineers, as well as major SIers and enterprises, tend to prefer sourcing projects through agents to reduce hiring costs. Being able to access high-rate, favorable projects you couldn't find on your own is the greatest advantage of using an agent.

② Rate negotiation is handled for you

Negotiating rates on your own as a freelancer is psychologically difficult, and with little experience, you may end up agreeing to a rate lower than what's appropriate. Agents are well-versed in market rates and negotiate with clients based on your skills and track record, making it easier to secure fair compensation.

③ Projects are continuously supplied with zero sales effort

The biggest risk when starting out independently is "running out of projects." By registering with an agent, you can be offered your next project before your current one ends, minimizing income gaps (so-called "idle periods").

④ Avoid contract and legal risks

When freelancers contract directly with clients as individuals, there are risks of unfavorable contract terms, delayed payments, or non-payment. Agents conduct credit checks on clients and will step in to handle any issues should they arise.

⑤ Support with tax filing and invoice compliance

Some agents also provide advice on tax matters specific to freelancers (tax filing and compliance with the invoice system) and support with integrating accounting tools.

While there are many benefits, be aware of the following points.

  • Your take-home pay is reduced by the margin: 10–30% of what the client pays goes to the agent
  • Project options are limited to the agent's portfolio: It's important to choose an agent strong in your specific tech stack or industry
  • You may need to register with multiple agents: A single agent may not have projects that meet your desired conditions

There are dozens of freelance agents out there, and it's not uncommon to hear experiences like "I registered but there were no projects that suited me" or "The agent's staff was unhelpful and hard to work with." By choosing based on the following 5 key points, you can greatly reduce the risk of failure.

When choosing an agent, the first thing to check is whether your skill set matches the areas of their projects. For example, an agent strong in Python and machine learning projects and one strong in Java enterprise projects may both be "for IT engineers," but the quality and volume of their projects differ greatly.

Points to check

  • Total number of projects held (don't just look at numbers; ask how many projects match your skills)
  • Supported languages and frameworks (Python / Go / TypeScript / React / AWS / Azure, etc.)
  • Industries (SIer / product companies / startups / finance / healthcare, etc.)

The margin rate (commission) varies by agent, and some agents keep it undisclosed. Approximate margin rates for major agents are as follows (reference: sakufuri.jp margin rate comparison).

AgentApproximate margin rate
PE-BANK8–15% (varies depending on withdrawal frequency)
MidworksEffectively around 10–15% (including benefits)
Levtech FreelanceUndisclosed (estimated 10–20%)
IT PartnersUndisclosed (high payout due to 90% direct projects)
TechStockUndisclosed (high level, with average annual income of 9.35 million yen)

The lower the margin rate, the higher your take-home pay, but support may be simpler in return. It is important to judge not only by "low margin rate" but also by the balance between project quality and support content.

An often-overlooked issue when maximizing freelance income is the problem of multi-layered subcontracting structures. If a project brokered by an agent is actually a second- or third-tier subcontract of a major SIer, multiple intermediate margins are deducted from what the client pays, in addition to the agent's margin, significantly reducing the payout rate to the freelancer.

You can avoid this problem by choosing an agent with a high ratio of direct projects. IT Partners publishes that "90% of its projects are direct contracts with companies," making it a structure where intermediate margins are less likely to occur (reference: IT Partners official site).

The quality of an agent depends heavily on the quality of the assigned career advisor. Check the following at your initial counseling session.

  • Specificity of skill sheet feedback and project proposals (do they make concrete proposals like "There is a project called XX at YY yen")
  • Whether they are proactive about rate negotiation
  • Response speed (do they reply to inquiries within the next business day)
  • Whether they have specialized knowledge in the IT/engineering field (can they hold technical conversations)

Since income stability is important for freelancers, add the payment cycle (payment terms) to your selection criteria.

  • Month-end closing with payment at the end of the following month is standard (30-day payment terms)
  • Some agents pay on the 15th of the following month or two months later

Also, some agents, like Midworks, offer benefits that compensate for the company-paid portion of social insurance premiums. For freelancers whose out-of-pocket national health insurance premiums are heavy, such added value is worth including in your selection criteria.

In addition, the number of IT freelancers exceeded 350,000 as of 2024 and is projected to grow to around 450,000 by 2028 (reference: Levtech Freelance freelance population trends).

Behind this market growth are companies' promotion of DX (digital transformation) and a surge in demand for cloud, AI, and data utilization. Demand is particularly strong for engineers with skills in Python, Go, TypeScript, and cloud architecture, and projects with monthly rates of 800,000 yen or more are no longer rare even through agents.

For those using a freelance agent for the first time, here is an explanation of the standard steps from registration to actually joining a project.

First, register for free on the agent's official website. The main fields you will fill in are as follows.

  • Name, contact information, and place of residence
  • Current employment status (company employee / already freelance)
  • Desired start date
  • Skills, programming languages, frameworks, and years of experience
  • Desired monthly rate and work style (full-time / 3-4 days per week / remote preference, etc.)

Tip: Preparing your skill sheet and resume at this stage will make the subsequent interview go smoothly. Registering with multiple agents at the same time allows you to compare more projects.

After registration, your assigned career advisor will contact you to schedule an online interview. This interview mainly covers the following topics.

  • A review of your work history and skills
  • Your desired project conditions (industry, tech stack, working days, remote availability)
  • Your goals for going independent and career direction
  • Your current situation (still employed / already resigned) and desired start date

Information you should communicate during the interview:

  • Both your desired monthly rate and your minimum acceptable rate (as a negotiation range)
  • Commutable area and whether you prefer fully remote work
  • Development phases you excel at (requirements definition / design / implementation / testing)

After the counseling session, the agent will propose projects that match your conditions. For each project, the following information is provided.

  • Monthly rate and contract period (typically 3-6 month renewable terms)
  • Job description, technologies used, and development environment
  • Work location (fully remote / partial on-site / full-time on-site)
  • Client company's industry and size

If a project interests you, you proceed to the selection process. The selection flow varies by agent and client, but the common pattern is document review → 1-2 online interviews → notification of the result.

Since interviews are often decided in a single round, prepare your expected questions and questions for the interviewer in advance by checking Interview Preparation for Freelance Engineers.

After passing the selection process, you sign a service agreement with the agent. The main contract terms are as follows.

  • Service agreement (quasi-mandate or contract for work)
  • Upper and lower limits on monthly rate and working hours (e.g., 140-180 hours)
  • Payment terms (e.g., end-of-month closing with payment at the end of the following month)
  • Non-disclosure agreement (NDA)

Compliance with the Invoice System (since October 2023): The method of invoicing clients changes depending on whether you are a registered qualified invoice issuer (invoice-registered). Some agents can accommodate those not yet invoice-registered, so check in advance.

Even after you start working, a good agent will continue to provide follow-up support.

  • Follow-up contact within 1-2 weeks of starting work (checking for any issues)
  • Support for negotiating rate increases at contract renewal
  • Advance proposals for your next project (starting 1-2 months before contract expiration)

When to negotiate rates while working: The best time is at contract renewal (every 3-6 months). Many freelancers build up a track record while working and negotiate a monthly rate increase of ¥50,000-100,000 through their agent.

If you are unsure which agent suits you best, the most reliable approach is to actually register and check the quality and quantity of the projects they propose. Registration with agents is completely free, and registering does not obligate you to sign a contract.

As a common industry belief, registering with 2-3 freelance agents simultaneously is the most effective approach. The reasons are as follows.

  • Each agent holds different projects, so your options expand
  • Receiving project proposals from multiple agents makes it easier to understand market rates
  • Due to competitive dynamics, agents tend to provide more proactive support

However, note that applying to the same project through multiple agents can invalidate your selection process, so be sure to confirm with each agent before applying.
