A pattern you saw across customers · FDE behavioral question

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 2 min read

Summary

This FDE interview prep piece explains how to answer the behavioral question about carrying field learnings back into the product. It cites Google's GenAI FDE posting and Cursor's FDE postings, both of which ask FDEs to turn repeatable field patterns into reusable modules or product requests. The recommended answer structure has five parts: state the pattern, describe the moment you noticed it, present evidence before argument, show the path onto the roadmap, and report the result with your role. It stresses stating where the pattern stops, quoting OpenAI's head of forward deployed engineering Colin Jarvis, who said in a November 2025 Altimeter interview that generalizing too early was his team's biggest mistake that year. Common failures: generalizing from two loud customers, or stories ending at 'I told product and they built it' with no evidence or personal contribution.

Key points

  • Google and Cursor FDE postings explicitly require turning field patterns into reusable product improvements.
  • Five-part answer: pattern, noticing moment, evidence, roadmap path, result and your role.
  • Evidence should count across all customers, not just yours, and state where the pattern stops.
  • OpenAI's Colin Jarvis said generalizing too early was his team's biggest 2025 mistake.
  • Weak answers lack evidence, a proposal, or any part the candidate played in shipping.

How to answer

This is the question about the ’s second job: carrying what you learn in the field back into the product. Some postings write it down. Google’s GenAI FDE posting asks FDEs to turn repeatable field patterns into reusable modules or product feature requests. Source 1Forward Deployed Engineer III, Generative AI, Google Cloud — Google CareersPublisherGoogleSource typecompany job posting Cursor’s FDE postings ask FDEs to turn what they build into reusable patterns and push improvements back into the core product. Source 2Forward Deployed EngineerPublisherCursor (Ashby job board)Source typecompany job board A strong answer shows the whole path, from first noticing to the product change.

  • The pattern in one sentence. What several customers needed, and what each one was doing about it: the same script copied between deployments, the same workaround spreadsheet, the same ticket.
  • The moment you noticed. Concrete: “The third customer that month exported our output to a spreadsheet to do the same join by hand.”
  • The evidence, before the argument. A count across all customers, not only yours. What the workaround cost in hours, tickets or incidents. And where the pattern stops: the customers it doesn’t fit.
  • The path onto the roadmap. Who decided, what format they decide in, and the smallest product change that covers the most cases. Say what you offered to do: the spec, the first version, the migration.
  • The result, and your part in it. What shipped, what it replaced, and a number that moved.

Say where the pattern ends out loud. It shows you counted. Colin Jarvis, OpenAI’s head of forward deployed engineering, said in a November 2025 Altimeter interview that the biggest mistake OpenAI’s FDE team made that year was generalizing too early. Source 3Colin Jarvis | Head of Forward Deployed Engineering at OpenAI: Trust. Product. Impact.PublisherAltimeter Capital (YouTube)Source typerecorded talk or interview An interviewer who has seen a product bent around two customers will listen for that.

The trap is a story with no path: you noticed, you told product, it happened. Without the evidence and the proposal, the interviewer cannot tell your judgment from luck.

Follow-ups

What the interviewer may ask next, once your first answer is on the table.

  • Which customers did the pattern not fit, and what did you do for them?
  • Who owned the roadmap, and what did they need to see before they said yes?
  • What did you leave out of the product change, and why?

Where answers go wrong

  • Generalizes from two loud customers, with no count across the rest of the book and no view of where the pattern stops.
  • Ends at “I raised it with product and they built it”, with no evidence, no proposal and no part the speaker played in the build.

Answer this in two minutes

Write the answer you would say out loud. The clock starts with your first word.
