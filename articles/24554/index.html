04 The FDE Delivery Loop and Portfolio Standards

Original title: 04 FDE 的交付闭环和作品集标准

Summary

It describes the common three-person Pod (FDE plus Deployment Strategist plus domain expert) used in large overseas deployments, citing Salesforce and OpenAI DeployCo hiring patterns.   • DIVE framework: Diagnose, Identify, Validate, Embed.
  • Portfolios must show evaluation metrics, failure samples, and rollout plans, not demos.

Chapter Objectives

Build awareness of the FDE delivery loop and understand what a qualified FDE portfolio should prove.

Opening Case: Why a Portfolio Can't Just Contain Demo Screenshots

Many people transitioning to FDE roles build portfolios around "I built a RAG assistant" or "I made an Agent demo." The screenshots may look complete, but what interviewers and customers really care about is: where did this scenario come from? Who will use it? How is the process done today? Who reviews when the system makes mistakes? How is effectiveness evaluated? Who maintains it after launch?

If you can't answer these questions, the portfolio is just an AI application showcase, not evidence of FDE delivery. What an FDE portfolio must prove is not "I can call models," but "I can take an uncertain business problem and drive it to a verifiable, operable, reviewable closed loop."

The Delivery Loop

An FDE's job is not to "build a feature," but to complete a closed loop:

A diagram from the original is omitted here; see the original.

The corresponding actions can be summarized as: Discover → Prototype → Deploy → Productize → Feedback. The prototype phase validates hypotheses, the deployment phase enters real permissions and data, the productization phase accumulates templates, Skills, and components, and the feedback phase drives the next round of discovery.

Pod Collaboration Model

Large overseas deployment projects commonly use a three-person Pod of FDE + Deployment Strategist / Lead + domain expert:

RoleResponsibilities
FDEOn-site discovery, architecture, integration, evaluation, and rollout
Deployment Strategist / LeadValue path, organizational change, adoption, and escalation mechanisms
Domain expertProcess standards, Ground Truth, compliance, and business acceptance

Public job postings from Salesforce, OpenAI DeployCo, and others all reflect this "engineering + strategy + business" combination, rather than single-hero delivery.

Production Adoption and Reusable Asset KPIs

Acceptance of the delivery loop depends not only on model metrics, but also on:

KPI TypeExamples
Production adoption rateWeekly active users, task completion share, number of manually replaced steps
Reusable assetsScenario cards, Prompts, Golden Datasets, tool Schemas, launch gate templates
Business impactPerson-hours saved, error rate reduction, cycle time reduction, fewer risk incidents

An FDE should turn on-site customer experience into reusable assets, not leave it in personal notes.

Deliverables of Each Stage

StageKey QuestionDeliverable
Discover the problemWhich process is worth tacklingInterview notes, process diagrams, pain point list
Define technical scopeWhat to do and what not to doScenario cards, scope assumptions, risk boundaries
Design the solutionWhat architecture to usePoC architecture diagram, tool list, permission plan
Implement the systemHow to get the pipeline runningMinimal demo, code, logs, and task status
Evaluate effectivenessHow to prove it worksGolden Dataset, metrics, failure samples
Launch and operateHow to run it stablyLaunch gates, training, rollback, and feedback mechanisms
Feed back into productHow to reuse the experienceProduct requirements, templates, components, and retrospective documents

The DIVE Problem-Solving Framework

An FDE can use DIVE to connect consulting-style problem decomposition with engineering delivery:

StepEnglishKey Actions
DDiagnoseDiagnose the real bottleneck using interviews, process diagrams, and problem trees
IIdentifyIdentify high-value scenarios, data conditions, and success metrics
VValidateValidate hypotheses with PoCs, Golden Datasets, and weekly demos
EEmbedEmbed into business processes, permissions, training, and operations mechanisms

DIVE turns the closed loop from "discover the problem" to "feed back into product" into an executable method.

Three-Tier PoC Path

TierApproachSuitable Stage
L1 Platform demoOut-of-the-box tools like Dify, Coze, FastGPTValidating value, weekly demos
L2 Vibe codingQuick scripts + lightweight frontend and backendCustom logic, evaluation prototypes
L3 Engineering scaffoldLangGraph, gateways, CI, regression gatesBeta / production

Upgrade criteria: stable samples, clear permissions, observability, and business stakeholders willing to try it.

Weekly Demo Cadence

  • Kickoff to align on goals, scope, acceptance criteria, and risks.
  • Weekly demos showcase runnable increments, not PPT progress.
  • Continuously record failure samples and a change pool to prevent silent scope creep.

A Sample Delivery Cadence

Below is a 4-week PoC cadence that can serve as a timeline for a portfolio or customer proposal:

PeriodKey ActionsKey Deliverables
Week 1Interviews, shadowing, scenario cardsProcess diagram, pain point list, PoC scope
Week 2Sample data, architecture selection, minimal pipelineDemo v0, tool list, permission assumptions
Week 3Golden Dataset, failure samples, weekly demoEvaluation report, change pool, risk list
Week 4Trial, retrospective, launch gateAcceptance metrics, 30-day plan, portfolio summary

The point of this cadence is not "launch in 4 weeks no matter what," but ensuring the project has a checkable increment every week, preventing the team from losing focus in discussions, research, and ad-hoc requests.

Portfolio Standards

An FDE portfolio is not a collection of API call screenshots, nor a single-page demo. It should prove that:

  • You understand a real business process.
  • You can break vague requirements into a deliverable scope.
  • You can design system architecture and permission boundaries.
  • You can implement a minimal runnable pipeline.
  • You can design evaluation samples and acceptance metrics.
  • You can explain launch risks, rollback, and operations.

Portfolio Structure

Each portfolio project should include:

  • A one-sentence scenario.
  • The current process and pain points.
  • The AI intervention point.
  • A technical architecture diagram.
  • A list of data and tools.
  • Permission and compliance boundaries.
  • Evaluation metrics.
  • Demo screenshots or scripts.
  • Failure samples and retrospectives.
  • The next-step launch plan.

Hands-On Task

Pick a scenario and write a one-page portfolio summary for it.

Deliverables

  • An FDE delivery loop diagram.
  • A portfolio project outline.
  • A PoC brief that can be further expanded.

Common Pitfalls

  • Showing only model outputs, not the business process.
  • Showing only the tech stack, not acceptance metrics.
  • Showing only success samples, not failure samples.
  • Doing only a one-off demo, without explaining launch and operations.

Checklist

  • Does the portfolio convince customers that you can deliver?
  • Are there verifiable metrics?
  • Are there data, tools, permissions, and risk explanations?
  • Does it demonstrate product feedback and reuse value?
