AI led services: AI led services to generate up to $200 billion in new work by 2030: BCG report

The Economic Times · 2026-11-01 · 3 min read

Summary

A BCG report projects AI-led services will create $100-200 billion in net new work by 2030. The global services market should grow from roughly $1.6-1.7 trillion in 2025 to $2.2-2.4 trillion by 2030, with AI services adding $300-400 billion while about $200 billion of traditional revenue disappears. The report argues tech services firms lack not AI talent but role definitions: Applied AI professionals are only 10-15% of AI staff at traditional firms versus 90-95% at AI-native ones, and forward-deployed roles are under 5% versus 45%. It identifies 40 Applied AI roles that can be built by reskilling existing employees, cheaper than external hires that carry up to a 35% pay premium.

Key points

  • AI-led services could net $100-200 billion in new work by 2030.
  • Services market to grow to $2.2-2.4 trillion by 2030.
  • Forward-deployed roles: 45% of Applied AI staff at AI-native firms, under 5% elsewhere.
  • BCG maps 40 Applied AI roles reachable via internal reskilling.
  • External hires for these roles can cost up to 35% more.

Full text

ANI

Artificial intelligence (AI)-led services are expected to generate between USD 100 billion and USD 200 billion in net new work by 2030, with technology services companies able to meet most of the demand by reskilling their existing employees, according to a report by Boston Consulting Group (BCG).

The report, titled 'The Talent Is Already on Your Payroll. The Clock Isn't', said the global services market is expected to grow from around $1.6-1.7 trillion in 2025 to $ 2.2-2.4 trillion by 2030.

AI-led services are projected to contribute $ 300 billion to $ 400 billion to this growth. However, the report estimates that around $ 200 billion in traditional services revenue will be lost, leaving a net market opportunity of $ 100 billion to $ 200 billion.

The report said technology companies need professionals skilled in Applied AI, who can deploy, operate and maintain AI-based solutions in real-world business environments.

Applied AI professionals account for only 10-15 per cent of AI-related staff at traditional technology services companies. In comparison, they make up 90-95 per cent of the workforce at AI-native firms. The remaining 85-90 per cent of AI-related staff at traditional companies primarily use AI to improve the speed of existing tasks.

The report also highlighted a gap in roles that work directly with clients to develop and deploy AI solutions. Such forward-deployed positions account for around 45 per cent of Applied AI staff at AI-native companies, compared with less than 5 per cent at traditional technology services firms.

"Technology services firms do not have an AI talent shortage; they have a talent decision to make," said Sreyssha George, Managing Director and Partner at BCG and co-author of the study.

"The people who can deliver Applied AI are largely already on the payroll, and clients are moving from paying for hours to paying for outcomes. That changes how work is staffed and rewarded," George added. "Every quarter without defined roles is a quarter of real-engagement experience your people do not get."

BCG identified 40 Applied AI roles that can be developed from positions already available within technology services companies. For instance, full-stack developers and data scientists can move into forward-deployed engineering roles, while DevOps specialists can transition to AI Ops and data engineers can become semantic engineers.

However, the report noted that the effort required for reskilling varies across roles. Upskilling a data scientist to become a forward-deployed engineer requires three times the effort needed to train a data engineer for an AI data engineer role.

Building these skills internally could also help companies reduce costs. According to the report, organisations hiring externally for these specialised positions may have to pay a premium of up to 35 per cent compared with equivalent traditional roles.

The report grouped the 40 Applied AI roles into four categories: buying scarce anchor roles, building scale drivers, retaining the delivery core and watching early indicators.

It stressed that companies need a clear framework to define these jobs, including skill requirements, proficiency levels, pay bands and a dedicated job category.

"The decision that turns existing talent into supply is role architecture: a skill taxonomy, proficiency gates, bands and a dedicated job family, so that each Applied AI role has a title, a level and a band attached," the report said.

Synopsis

A recent report highlights the transformative impact of artificial intelligence-led services on the job market, predicting significant workforce expansion by 2030. It estimates the global services market will surge from $ 1.6-1.7 trillion to a remarkable $ 2.2-2.4 trillion. To leverage this growth, technology employees must undergo reskilling. Up to 40 new Applied AI roles may emerge, but organizations risk increased expenses by seeking external hires for these specialized jobs.
