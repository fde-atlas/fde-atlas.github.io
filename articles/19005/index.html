UAE FDE Salary: Base Pay and the Golden Visa

Original title: 阿联酋 FDE 薪资：基本工资与黄金签证

FDE Times · 2026-11-07 · 6 min read

Summary

This article argues that for FDE roles in the UAE, the split between base salary and allowances matters more than total pay. Since May 2024, the Golden Visa threshold of AED 30,000 per month applies only to base salary, and end-of-service gratuity is likewise calculated on base pay alone. Comparing three job postings—only Wave Group disclosed a range of AED 50,000-90,000 monthly—the author shows how two offers with identical totals can differ by AED 35,000 in gratuity over five years. It also covers one-year contract limits, tax residency pitfalls, and advises leveraging the scarcity of experienced FDEs to negotiate a higher base salary.

Key points

  • Only Wave Group's posting disclosed pay: AED 50,000-90,000 monthly.
  • Golden Visa requires AED 30,000 base salary, excluding allowances.
  • Gratuity is base-salary based; splits can differ by AED 35,000 over five years.
  • Visa and relocation support are typically subject to the final offer.
  • Scarce FDE talent justifies negotiating more pay into base salary.

Two offers with the same AED 50,000 monthly salary can differ in how they're split—and that split can determine whether you qualify for the Golden Visa and mean a AED 35,000 difference in end-of-service gratuity.

Key takeaways

  • Of the three UAE FDE job postings compared here, only Wave Group disclosed a salary: AED 50,000-90,000 per month. The other two either had no figures or just said "competitive."
  • Since May 2024, the AED 30,000 monthly Golden Visa threshold is based on base salary only, excluding allowances. End-of-service gratuity is likewise calculated on base salary only.
  • The UAE levies no personal income tax, but you may still owe taxes in your country of tax residency. Visa and relocation support often come with "subject to final offer" conditions.

Wave Group posted a senior Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role based in the UAE with relocation support and a clearly stated salary range: AED 50,000-90,000 per month. Of the three UAE FDE job postings compared below, this is the only one that gave a number.

But having a number only solves half the problem. In the UAE, the total compensation on an offer is split into base salary and allowances, and the two are treated differently. If you plan to stay long term, the two most important things are the Golden Visa for skilled talent and the end-of-service gratuity—and both are assessed based on base salary.

If you're an engineer considering an FDE role in the Gulf, the questions to ask aren't just "how much" but also "how is it split." Three real job postings and a simple calculation can show what that split is worth.

What the job postings said—and what they left out

Read closely, each of the three postings promises something different. Wave Group gave a salary range and noted that the relocation package includes a visa, but didn't distinguish base salary from allowances and didn't mention benefits.

SPsoft is hiring a Forward Deployed AI Engineer, on-site in Abu Dhabi. The posting lists no salary, but does list a fair amount of support.

According to SPsoft, the expected support includes a UAE work visa, employee health insurance, a first flight to the UAE, agency fees, and help finding an apartment—all subject to the final written offer. The role is a one-year contract, possibly renewable.

The third is a Dubai FDE role posted via Jobgether in June 2026, now closed, which only said the salary is competitive and offered equity opportunities.

So the most important part of negotiation happens after you pass the interviews.

Why base salary determines the Golden Visa

The Abu Dhabi Department of Economic Development (ADDED) Golden Visa page for skilled talent only states a minimum salary of AED 30,000 per month, with a salary certificate from the employer and six months of bank statements required, but doesn't say whether that figure refers to base salary or total compensation.

The answer is in a note on the UAE Golden Visa from immigration law firm Fragomen: since May 2024, applicants in this category must have a monthly base salary of at least AED 30,000, excluding any allowances. Whether the role is in Abu Dhabi or Dubai, the safe approach is to treat AED 30,000 as a base salary threshold.

An AED 50,000 offer sounds well above the threshold, but if the base salary is only 25,000, you still don't qualify. In the UAE, the salary split isn't an accounting detail—it determines whether you can get the Golden Visa.

Two identical offers, two different gratuity payouts

According to Gulf News, UAE end-of-service gratuity is calculated on the final base salary: 21 days of base salary per year of service for the first five years, then 30 days per year thereafter. The National confirmed the same calculation method. So allowances add nothing to your gratuity.

Suppose two offers, both AED 50,000 per month—the lower end of Wave Group's range. Offer A is split into 35,000 base salary plus 15,000 allowances; offer B into 25,000 base salary plus 25,000 allowances. The money hitting your account each month is exactly the same.

After five years, you've accrued 105 days of base salary. Assume the daily wage is the monthly base salary divided by 30 (confirm the actual conversion method with your employer).

On that basis, offer A comes to about AED 122,500 and offer B about AED 87,500. The difference is AED 35,000—exactly one month of offer A's base salary—yet you'd never notice any difference in your monthly income.

Offer B loses on another point too: a base salary of 25,000 falls below the Golden Visa threshold. With identical total income, one offer gets you both long-term visa eligibility and a larger gratuity, while the other gets you neither. These figures are hypothetical, but the calculation follows the rules described above.

Contract length also changes the outcome. A one-year contract like SPsoft's yields at most 21 days of base salary under the method above; the 30-days-per-year standard only kicks in after five years. If you accept such a role, ask up front what the base salary will be after renewal.

No income tax doesn't mean no tax questions

The National quoted an advisor saying that people working in the UAE will continue to pay no tax on their income. So the number on the offer is closer to take-home pay than an equivalent salary in a country with income tax.

But Wise points out that living in the UAE doesn't exempt you from tax obligations everywhere: what usually matters is tax residency, not nationality, and double taxation treaties exist between countries.

Gulf News uses British citizens as an example: if you remain a UK tax resident, you may need to pay tax on your worldwide income. Consult a tax advisor in your home country before signing, and only then compare the UAE offer with offers back home.

Three common mistakes when accepting an offer

First, looking only at total compensation and accepting a split where allowances dominate. Take offer B above: your monthly income is unchanged, but you lose Golden Visa eligibility and part of your gratuity. If the company insists on keeping the total the same, propose shifting some allowances into base salary.

Second, taking "expected" at face value. SPsoft's posting is explicit: visa, insurance, flights, and housing assistance are all subject to the final written offer. Anything not written into that document shouldn't be counted toward the value of the compensation package.

Third, if a job posting only says "competitive salary, including equity," don't just accept it without asking follow-up questions. When you encounter this kind of information, ask all your questions upfront: who pays the base salary, allowances, and visa costs, and what exactly the equity is.

Scarce talent gives you the leverage to ask for a higher base salary

UAE recruiting firm UMATR says engineers with genuine Forward Deployed experience remain rare in the region. The company says it successfully placed a candidate with only 1.5 years of experience, on the grounds that in a fast-growing AI market, execution ability often matters more than years of work.

That's your leverage when negotiating the salary split. When companies struggle to find people who have actually done FDE work, it's reasonable to ask for part of the allowances to be moved into base salary to cross the AED 30,000 threshold and increase your end-of-service gratuity: the monthly total stays the same.

But that leverage only exists if you can show work you've done on-site with customers, not just a title on your resume. Bring a concrete case to the negotiation: where you were embedded, which system you pushed to production, and what the results were.

Once you have an offer, treat the negotiation as a customer requirements interview with the recruiter. Find out what the base salary is and what the allowances consist of, and ask for visa, insurance, and relocation support to be written into the offer.

A good FDE never accepts a customer's requirements without asking where the real data lives. The same goes for offers from Abu Dhabi or Dubai: total compensation is the number on the slide, base salary is the real data.
