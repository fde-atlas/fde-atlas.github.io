Forward Deployed Engineer vs Software Engineer: What’s the Real Difference?

AWS Builder Center · 2026-11-09 · 8 min read

Summary

This AWS Builder Center article compares Software Engineers with Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), a role popularized by companies like Palantir. The core distinction is not coding volume but ownership: Software Engineers build and improve products, while FDEs work directly with customers to understand business problems and deliver working solutions in real environments. Using an AWS cloud migration example, the author contrasts their responsibilities, lists FDE skills across programming, cloud infrastructure, data/AI, and communication, and argues enterprise AI adoption is increasing demand for FDEs. The article also outlines how cloud and DevOps engineers can transition into the role, concluding the two roles are complementary rather than competing.

Key points

  • Software Engineers build products; FDEs solve customer problems.
  • FDEs gather requirements through direct customer interaction.
  • FDEs need programming, cloud, data/AI, and communication skills.
  • Enterprise AI adoption is driving FDE demand.
  • Cloud and DevOps engineers have a strong foundation to become FDEs.

The technology industry is constantly evolving. New tools, frameworks, programming languages, and engineering roles appear every year. But recently, one role has started getting more attention, particularly in the world of Artificial Intelligence and enterprise technology: Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). At first, it sounds like another name for a Software Engineer. Both roles involve writing code, understanding systems, solving technical problems, and building software.

But there is an important difference.

A Software Engineer generally focuses on building and improving software products, while a Forward Deployed Engineer focuses on taking technology directly into real-world business environments and solving specific customer problems.

So, what actually separates these two roles? And why are companies increasingly interested in Forward Deployed Engineers?

Let's understand.

1. First, What Does a Software Engineer Do?

A Software Engineer is responsible for designing, developing, testing, maintaining, and improving software applications and systems.

Think about an application you use every day, such as an online shopping platform, banking application, or food delivery service.

Behind that application, software engineers work on different components:

  • Developing frontend and backend functionality.
  • Designing APIs and database structures.
  • Writing clean, maintainable, and scalable code.
  • Fixing bugs and improving application performance.
  • Testing software before releasing new features.
  • Collaborating with product managers, designers, and other engineers.

Their primary responsibility is to build software that works reliably for its intended users.

For example, imagine a company developing an e-commerce platform.

A Software Engineer might build the authentication system, shopping cart, payment integration, order management system, and product recommendation features.

The engineer works on a product that may eventually serve thousands or millions of users.

In simple words, Software Engineers build the product.

2. What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Now, let's look at the relatively newer role.

A Forward Deployed Engineer is an engineer who works closely with customers to understand their technical and business challenges and then builds or adapts solutions to address those challenges.

The term became particularly associated with companies such as Palantir, where engineers work closely with customers and deploy technology into complex operational environments.

Unlike a traditional engineering workflow, where requirements are often passed through product managers and development teams, Forward Deployed Engineers frequently interact directly with the people experiencing the problem.

Their work may involve:

  • Understanding real business problems.
  • Working directly with customers and stakeholders.
  • Integrating APIs and third-party systems.
  • Building custom software and automation.
  • Deploying applications into customer environments.
  • Troubleshooting production issues.
  • Adapting existing products to meet specific requirements.

Let's take a practical example.

Imagine a healthcare organization that collects patient information from multiple systems. The data is scattered across different databases, applications, and platforms.

The organization wants a unified system to analyze information and improve its operations.

A Forward Deployed Engineer might work directly with the organization's technical team to understand the existing infrastructure, connect different data sources, build integrations, develop automation, and deploy a solution that fits the organization's actual workflow.

The engineer is not simply developing a feature and handing it over.

They are involved in understanding the problem, implementing the solution, deploying it, and making sure it works in the customer's environment.

In simple words, Forward Deployed Engineers take technology to where the problem actually exists.

3. Forward Deployed Engineer vs Software Engineer: The Real Difference

Although both roles require strong engineering knowledge, their responsibilities and day-to-day work can be quite different.

Area Software Engineer Forward Deployed Engineer
Primary focus Building software products Solving customer problems
Work environment Mostly internal engineering teams Often works directly with customers
Requirements Usually defined through product teams Frequently discovered through customer interactions
Development Product features and platforms Custom solutions and integrations
Deployment Product release cycles Often customer-specific deployments
Communication Primarily internal teams Customers, stakeholders, and engineering teams
Problem-solving Product and technical challenges Business and technical challenges
Success measurement Product quality, reliability, and performance Customer outcomes and successful implementations

One important thing to understand: these are general differences, not strict boundaries. Software Engineers can work directly with customers, and Forward Deployed Engineers can contribute to core product development.

The distinction is mainly about the problems they are expected to own.

4. A Real-World Example: Building vs Implementing

Let's understand the difference through a simple cloud computing example.

Imagine a company wants to migrate its existing application infrastructure to AWS.

What might a Software Engineer do?

A Software Engineer working on the application might:

  • Modify application code for cloud compatibility.
  • Improve API performance.
  • Optimize database queries.
  • Implement new application features.
  • Fix application-level bugs.

What might a Forward Deployed Engineer do?

A Forward Deployed Engineer working with the same organization might:

  • Understand the company's existing infrastructure.
  • Identify technical and operational challenges.
  • Design an appropriate cloud architecture.
  • Integrate existing applications with AWS services.
  • Automate deployment workflows.
  • Help implement monitoring and logging.
  • Troubleshoot issues during deployment.
  • Work with the customer's team to ensure the solution meets their requirements.

Notice something?

Both engineers may work with the same technologies, but their responsibilities start from different places.

One is primarily focused on developing and improving the software. The other is focused on ensuring that technology solves an actual problem in the customer's environment.

5. What Technical Skills Does a Forward Deployed Engineer Need?

This is where things become interesting.

A Forward Deployed Engineer cannot rely only on communication skills or technical presentations. The role requires practical engineering knowledge.

Depending on the company and project, the technical skill set may include:

Programming and Software Development

  • Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, or Java.
  • REST APIs and backend development.
  • Git and GitHub.
  • Debugging and software development fundamentals.

Cloud and Infrastructure

  • AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud.
  • Linux administration.
  • Docker and containerization.
  • Kubernetes and orchestration.
  • Networking, security, and infrastructure management.

Data and AI

  • SQL and database management.
  • Data pipelines and integrations.
  • Large Language Models (LLMs).
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).
  • AI agents and workflow automation.

Communication and Problem-Solving

  • Understanding customer requirements.
  • Explaining technical concepts to non-technical people.
  • Breaking complex business problems into technical solutions.
  • Working with different teams and adapting to changing requirements.

Not every FDE position requires all these technologies. The exact requirements depend on the product, industry, and customer environment.

But one thing remains consistent: an FDE needs to understand how different technologies work together, not just how to use them individually.

6. Why Is Forward Deployed Engineering Becoming Important?

One major reason is the growing gap between building technology and implementing it successfully.

Today, companies have access to powerful technologies such as cloud platforms, AI models, automation tools, and enterprise software.

However, having access to technology does not automatically solve business problems.

For example, an organization might purchase an AI solution, but integrating it with existing databases, internal applications, security policies, and business workflows can be challenging.

This is where Forward Deployed Engineers become valuable.

They help bridge the gap between what technology can do and what a business actually needs.

The growing adoption of enterprise AI is making this especially relevant. Businesses need engineers who can move beyond demonstrations and help deliver solutions that work reliably in real production environments.

7. Is a Forward Deployed Engineer Better Than a Software Engineer?

Not necessarily.

These roles are not competing with each other. They solve different kinds of problems.

A Software Engineer might spend months improving the performance, reliability, and scalability of a product used by millions of people.

A Forward Deployed Engineer might spend those same months working with a particular enterprise customer to build and implement a solution around its unique requirements.

Both require engineering ability, technical thinking, and continuous learning.

The right role depends on what kind of engineering work you enjoy.

If you enjoy designing systems, writing code, building products, and improving software at scale, Software Engineering might be a good fit.

If you enjoy solving unfamiliar problems, working with customers, understanding business requirements, integrating different technologies, and taking solutions into production, Forward Deployed Engineering might interest you.

8. Can a Cloud or DevOps Engineer Become a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Absolutely.

Cloud and DevOps knowledge can provide a strong foundation for moving into Forward Deployed Engineering.

Why?

Because implementing a real-world solution involves much more than writing application code.

You need to understand infrastructure, networking, security, deployment pipelines, monitoring, scalability, and troubleshooting.

For example, imagine deploying an AI-powered application for an enterprise customer.

You might need to:

  • Provision infrastructure on AWS.
  • Containerize the application using Docker.
  • Configure networking and security.
  • Set up CI/CD pipelines.
  • Integrate databases and external APIs.
  • Deploy and monitor the application.
  • Troubleshoot issues in the production environment.

These are areas where Cloud and DevOps engineers already have valuable experience.

However, to transition into an FDE role, they should also develop programming skills, API integration knowledge, system design fundamentals, and the ability to understand and communicate business requirements.

The goal is not simply to learn more tools. It is to become comfortable taking an entire technical solution from problem discovery to production.

9. The Future: Engineers Who Understand Both Technology and Business

I believe one of the most interesting changes happening in the technology industry is how we define engineering responsibilities.

For a long time, we have divided technical roles into specific categories: developers build applications, DevOps engineers manage deployment and infrastructure, data engineers manage data pipelines, and architects design systems.

These specializations remain important.

But real-world problems rarely fit into one category.

A customer might need an application that connects multiple APIs, processes data, uses AI, runs on cloud infrastructure, and integrates with existing enterprise systems.

Solving that problem requires an understanding of multiple engineering domains.

That is one reason Forward Deployed Engineering is an interesting career path to watch.

It encourages engineers to look beyond individual technologies and understand the complete problem they are trying to solve.

Final Thoughts

The difference between a Software Engineer and a Forward Deployed Engineer is not simply about writing more or less code.

It is about where engineering begins and what the engineer is responsible for delivering.

A Software Engineer primarily focuses on building and improving software products.

A Forward Deployed Engineer takes technology closer to the customer, understands real-world challenges, and works on delivering solutions that create measurable value.

Both roles are important. Neither replaces the other.

But as cloud computing, AI, and enterprise technology continue to evolve, engineers who can combine technical expertise with practical problem-solving and strong communication skills will have an interesting role to play in the industry.

Because at the end of the day, writing code is important. But understanding the problem behind that code is equally important.
