Case Files: See How Others Succeeded, and How Others Backtracked

Original title: 落地档案：看别人怎么做成，也看别人怎么走了回头路

Summary

This case file examines the Airbus and Palantir collaboration. A recurring fuel pump fault on the A380 had resisted investigation for roughly two years because sensor readings, maintenance records, flight logs and supplier batch data sat across a dozen disconnected systems. Palantir deployed engineers on site who worked with aviation engineers to agree on which parameters indicated pump anomalies, then unified the data onto a single timeline; reportedly, regularities pointing to the root cause emerged within about two weeks. The capability later became a platform feature extended to more fleets. Notably, no large language models were involved at this stage. The author cautions that this on-site model suits large enterprises and big contracts, not small businesses, and distills three transferable lessons.

Key points

  • Airbus spent about two years on an A380 fuel pump fault; Palantir found clues in two weeks.
  • Deployed engineers first aligned with aviation engineers on which parameters signal anomalies.
  • Data from a dozen disconnected systems was merged onto one timeline.
  • No LLMs were used; results came from data integration and expert judgment.
  • Lessons: small entry point, toolmakers beside users, replicate only after one success.

Full text

Airbus × Palantir: A fault investigated for about two years, a lead found in two weeks

About 2 years prior investigation time (reported)

About 2 weeks to find a lead pointing to the root cause

  • The starting point
  • A fuel pump on an Airbus A380 kept reporting faults. Sensor readings, maintenance work orders, flight logs, and supplier batch records were scattered across more than a dozen disconnected systems.
  • The approach
  • Palantir sent Forward Deployed Engineers on site, who worked with aviation engineers to first align on "which parameters matter for judging pump anomalies," then connected data from multiple systems onto a single timeline.

What changed, what didn't, and where it applies

  • What changed
  • Reportedly, the team found a pattern-based lead in about two weeks. This diagnostic capability was later built into platform features and extended to more fleets.
  • What wasn't solved
  • No large language models were used at this stage; it relied on data integration and the judgment of domain experts.
  • Applicability and takeaways
  • The playbook of large enterprises, big contracts, and on-site teams can't be copied wholesale by small and medium businesses. What can be borrowed are three things: start with a small entry point; put the tool builders next to the tool users; get one case working, then replicate.
