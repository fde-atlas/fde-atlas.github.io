Palantir Forward Deployed Engineer: Role and Pay

FWD Deployment · 2026-10-11 · 3 min read

Summary

This article explains Palantir's Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE) role, nicknamed "Delta", and its pay. Deltas sit in business development, serve a single customer, and are judged on that customer's results, working on data, AI, and custom applications on Palantir's platforms. They pair with Deployment Strategists ("Echos"), who lean toward product management. Posted salaries checked October 11, 2026 show FDSE roles in New York and Palo Alto at $135,000–$200,000, with Deployment Strategists at $110,000–$170,000. Palantir reported $4.475 billion FY25 revenue, up 56%, and runs a five-day AIP Bootcamp to get customers to a working use case.

The role

What a Palantir forward deployed engineer does

Palantir calls its version of the job Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE) and nicknames it "Delta". A 2019 Palantir post names Deltas and software engineers ("Devs") as its two largest engineering roles. Deltas sit in business development, support a single customer and are judged by that customer's results.

The current FDSE posting lists architecture work with other engineers, massive-scale data and AI, custom applications, and work with customer stakeholders up to executives. It asks for 1+ years of experience after college, strong coding in a language like Python, Java, C++ or TypeScript, and travel up to 25%.

Palantir pairs Deltas with Deployment Strategists, nicknamed "Echos". Palantir says Echos lean toward product management and Deltas lean technical, though the line often blurs.

Team: Business Development, alongside Deployment Strategists. In Palantir's words: "We pioneered this unique position, embedding talented engineers directly with our customers" (posting)

Best for: Governments and large enterprises with complex operational data

  • Founded 2003
  • $4.475B revenue, FY25
  • 5-day AIP Bootcamp

Palantir sells the Foundry, AIP, Gotham and Apollo platforms and pairs them with forward deployed software engineers, a job it says it pioneered. Its five-day AIP Bootcamp aims to get a customer from zero to a working use case. It reported $4.475 billion in revenue for 2025, up 56%.
