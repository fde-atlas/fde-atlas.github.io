Summary

Written by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, this article explains what cost items appear in Japanese system development quotes and how to review them. It breaks down costs across requirements definition, design, development, testing, deployment, equipment, and maintenance, noting that pricing is mainly calculated in person-months. Market rates range from 400,000–800,000 yen monthly for programmers to 1.2–2 million yen for senior engineers, and annual maintenance typically runs about 15% of development cost. The author advises checking scope of work, risk-related fees, contract type (ukeoi vs. jun-inin), and whether requirements are accurately reflected to avoid delays and disputes.

Key points

  • Development quotes are mostly labor costs calculated in person-months.
  • Cost items span requirements, design, coding, testing, deployment, and maintenance.
  • Programmer rates run 400,000–800,000 yen monthly; senior engineers up to 2 million yen.
  • Annual maintenance costs roughly 15% of the original development fee.
  • Reviewers should verify scope, risk fees, and contract type before signing.

In system development, estimates are mainly provided for the following items.

Requirements definition is the process of clarifying specific goals and issues so that engineers and programmers know what to do.

Meanwhile, requirements specification refers to the process by which people without specialized knowledge, such as business owners, communicate to technical staff what functionality they want developed. It is the stage of defining the specific requirements for the system, such as "We want to solve issue XX, so we want to implement functionality like YY."

Generally, requirements definition and specification take several days.

Related article: What's the Difference Between Requirements Definition and Requirements Specification? Concepts and Items to Include in Definition Documents

Design is the process of concretely designing the software's internals according to what was decided in the requirements definition.

Design is broadly divided into the following two types.

・Basic design (design of feature lists, data input/output, etc.) ・Detailed design (design of servers, databases, etc.)

Basic design and detailed design are further divided into external design and internal design.

External design concerns UI (user interface), such as design and appearance. Since the UI is the part users directly interact with, it is the part where they most readily perceive the system's usability and appeal. To achieve user satisfaction, it is advisable to secure a reasonable budget for UI design.

Internal design, on the other hand, is the design of the software's internal functions, carried out mainly by technical staff such as programmers and engineers.

Note that, like requirements definition and specification, design also takes several days.

This is the process of actually programming according to the design documents.

Development comes in two types: front-end development and back-end development.

【Front-end development】

Development of the parts visible to users. This refers to developing visual information, such as clickable elements and appearance.

【Back-end development】

Development of the parts invisible to users. This refers to substantive development, such as internal system processing and programming to implement specific features. 【Web system development】 ・Email forms: from 30,000 yen

【Examples】Forms, Orange Forms, Google Forms, etc.

・Customer management: from 200,000 yen

【Examples】Chikyuu, Salesforce Cloud, SATORI, etc.

・CMS: from 500,000 yen

【Examples】WordPress, Movable Type, FREE CODE, etc.

・EC sites: from 500,000 yen

【Examples】Amazon, ZOZOTOWN, Uniqlo, etc.

Note that after the requirements definition and design stages, it generally takes about 10 days, or up to about 20 days depending on scale, from contract to the actual start of development. Development effort itself varies with project scale, but taking MVP development as an example, it takes an average of about 2 to 3 months.

In web system development, costs for UI and other design work also arise. UI refers to usability, so understanding from a design perspective, not just a business perspective, becomes important.

・Visually easy to understand ・Fast page loading ・Clear guidance on what to do

Creating a well-designed service can also lead to a distinctive system, so this is a point worth carefully considering in development.

This is the process of testing to confirm whether the developed system works as specified in the design documents and whether there are any problems.

Testing takes about 2 weeks to 1 month depending on scale. If defects are found, they are fixed each time, and once no problems remain, the system is officially released (delivered).

Note that there are several types of testing, as follows.

・Unit testing: Testing that verifies program behavior at the module level (by component, page, etc.).

・Integration testing: Testing that verifies the behavior when modules that have passed unit testing work together.

・System testing (comprehensive testing): Testing that runs the entire program, which has passed the previous tests, under conditions simulating actual operation to confirm it works without problems. It checks from various perspectives—appearance, functionality, processing, security, etc.—whether the system is at a releasable level.

When the system is complete and actually deployed, initial setup is required. In addition, implementation support may be provided, such as training on usage and preparing manuals, to ensure smooth operation.

As described above, implementation and implementation support costs are also required when deploying a system.

When developing and operating a system, you may also need to purchase equipment such as hardware and servers, as follows.

- If development PCs are insufficient: prepare them through leasing or purchase

- For systems that run on the web: prepare web servers and web application servers. In addition, for infrastructure development such as server construction and storage provisioning, cloud services such as AWS and GCP may be used. These can also be considered part of equipment costs.

Even after development is complete, maintenance and operation costs are required to perform regular updates and fixes.

When actually operating a system, you may face sudden troubles or unexpected issues. Maintenance and operation work is essential to ensure continuous, normal operation.

Maintenance and operation work can be broadly divided into two categories: "improvement of the overall system" and "troubleshooting." Specific tasks include the following.

[Overall system improvement]

- Batch processing - System updates - Database tuning, etc.

[Troubleshooting]

- Handling unauthorized access - Fixing bugs - Responding to system failures, etc.

It is said that annual maintenance and operation costs typically amount to about 15% of development costs. For example, for a system completed at 5 million yen, 750,000 yen per year would be required.

Of course, this varies by project, but it is advisable to assume roughly 10% to 20% of development costs.

In addition to the above, travel expenses and schedule management costs may sometimes be included in the estimate.

Excluding equipment installation costs, most of the estimates explained so far consist of labor costs. In system development involving many people, it is impossible to simply say "X yen is needed."

This is because system development cost calculations are basically based on "person-month" rates.

A person-month refers to a unit of workload calculated by multiplying the following two factors.

"The number of engineers and programmers required for system development"

×

"The monthly labor cost per person"

For example, if development is carried out over 3 months by 5 engineers (monthly rate of 600,000 yen), this equals "15 person-months," calculated as follows.

5 people × 3 months × 600,000 yen = 9 million yen

Typical market rates for web system development costs are as follows.

[Market rates for web system development costs]

- Programmer, subcontractor/freelancer: 400,000-800,000 yen - Programmer, major company: 600,000-1,000,000 yen - Junior engineer: 600,000-1,000,000 yen - Mid-level engineer: 1,000,000-1,200,000 yen - Senior engineer: 1,200,000-2,000,000 yen

However, the required person-months vary by project, depending on the scope of systemization and the content of the system to be built. In addition, rates differ depending on the engineer's skill level, and if the development period extends beyond schedule, costs will increase accordingly.

When reviewing a system development estimate, simply checking the items and amounts is not enough.

Due to insufficient communication with the development company or changes in requirements, problems such as "delayed delivery," "integration errors," and "low-quality deliverables" often occur.

To prevent such problems, be sure to also check the following points.

System development involves various tasks, from requirements definition and requirement gathering to testing, release, and maintenance and operation. Whether the scope of work extends only to testing and release or all the way to maintenance and operation generally varies by project.

Therefore, in the estimate, confirm that your desired scope of work matches the development company's scope of work. Clarifying how far the development company will work also allows you to share the scope of responsibility. Also, confirm how the client will manage progress and how communication will be conducted. Deciding in advance whether to hold regular meetings, and whether they will be in person or handled by email, makes it easier to reduce gaps in understanding between both parties.

Development involves various risks. If major troubles or changes occur during development, development may not be possible within the initial budget alone. If items such as "trouble handling costs" or "correction costs" are included in the estimate, you know that costs for development risks are also included.

If items such as "correction costs" are not found, it is advisable to confirm what kind of support will be provided if risks arise.

It is also important to check the contract details to prevent problems from developing later.

In system development, contracts are generally made under either a fixed-price (contract for work) agreement or a quasi-mandate (consultancy) agreement, as follows.

- Fixed-price contract: a contract where compensation is paid for the completion of the system (work). - Quasi-mandate contract: there are two types, deliverable-based and effort-based, each as follows.

[Deliverable-based]: compensation is paid for the achieved results (completion of the system)

[Effort-based]: compensation is paid not for the results (completion of the system) but for the work under the contract

Under an effort-based quasi-mandate contract, costs are incurred as long as the work itself is performed, even if the system you commissioned is not completed.

Note that the conditions under which compensation is incurred differ depending on the contract details.

Also check whether the requirements and requests you communicated to the development company are reflected, such as whether the development content matches your wishes, stays within budget, and uses your desired development approach.

If the development content is not properly aligned, corrections and changes are more likely to occur during development. To optimize costs and prevent problems, it is necessary to check at the estimate stage that you are satisfied with the proposal.

Note that if you are considering getting an estimate, you likely already have a fairly clear idea of what you want to develop at that point. By fleshing out the software details as much as possible—such as what features should be implemented and what problems you want to solve—the development company will find it easier to provide an accurate estimate.

When reviewing a system development estimate, pay attention to whether the scope of work and responsibilities is clear, whether development risks have been taken into account, and make sure to thoroughly check for any unclear points. Also, be sure to review the contract details to avoid problems later on.
