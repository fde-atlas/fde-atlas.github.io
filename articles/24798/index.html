Summary

This article from FDE Jobs walks through a typical day for a forward deployed engineer, showing how time allocation shifts across project phases: requirements definition (dialogue-heavy), implementation (balanced coding and client interaction), and adoption (training and operational support). A sample implementation-day timeline runs from checking client data and building integrations or LLM/RAG pipelines in the morning, to client meetings and demos around midday, then implementing feedback and running evals, and ending with feedback to the product team. It also weighs the rewards—seeing your work used on-site and tied to business outcomes—against the strains of client-driven schedules, constant context switching, and juggling technical and interpersonal work. On-site, hybrid, and fully remote arrangements all exist, with remote and nationwide hiring increasingly common.

An FDE's Day: Back and Forth Between "Implementation" and "Customers"

A day in the life of an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) consists of going back and forth between time spent writing code and time spent talking with customers and stakeholders. Rather than a fixed routine, its defining feature is that the time allocation changes significantly depending on the project phase (requirements definition / implementation / adoption). Here, we introduce a typical day during the "implementation and delivery phase" as an example.

Typical Daily Timeline (Implementation Phase Example)

Time of DayMain Tasks
Early morningChecking customer environment data and logs, verifying the previous day's implementation, organizing priority issues
Late morningCustom implementation, API integration, building LLM/RAG pipelines
Noon to early afternoonCustomer meetings (progress sharing, spec alignment, demos), on-site interviews
Late afternoonImplementing requests from meetings, evaluation (Eval) and accuracy checks
EveningSharing feedback with the product team, updating documentation

This is just one example; during the requirements definition phase, interviews and design take center stage, while the adoption phase focuses on education, operational support, and improvement.

How Time Is Spent by Phase

An FDE's work shifts in focus as the project progresses.

Requirements Definition and Pre-Sales Phase

Centered on customer interviews, structuring issues, and building demos and PoCs. Dialogue to define "what should be built" carries more weight than code.

Implementation and Delivery Phase

As shown in the timeline above, implementation and customer dialogue are split roughly 50/50. This is the phase with the most hands-on time.

Adoption and Expansion Phase

Centered on onboarding, usage analysis, and improvements and feature expansion. The goal is to build a state where the product is used in the field and delivers results.

The Reality of Being an FDE: Rewards and Challenges

The reward is directly experiencing the satisfaction of seeing what you build actually used at the customer's site and contributing to business results. Because you engage with both technology and business, your career options also expand. On the other hand, there are burdens such as customer-driven schedules, frequent context switching, and balancing technical work with interpersonal work. This role suits people who find joy in "delivering maximum value in the field" more than those who simply want to focus on code. For more on suitability, see The Difference Between FDEs and Software Engineers.
