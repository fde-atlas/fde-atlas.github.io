Today's Judgment

Today's two signals, from policy and architecture respectively, point to the same upgrade in enterprise AI acceptance criteria:

Guangdong's industrial and IT system is using open competitive bidding to write AI innovation tasks into application and verification processes—tasks must have supporting evidence, and authorities must verify authenticity, completeness, and validity; AWS, meanwhile, has made the replaceable control plane across multi-model, multi-framework, and multi-cloud environments an explicit architecture problem for scale—avoiding vendor lock-in is not a slogan, but a concrete architectural choice.

Both point to the same conclusion: an FDE must deliver three things simultaneously—business results, an evidence chain, and exit capability. Getting the first workflow running is only the beginning; only when it can be verified and migrated does it become a true enterprise asset.

Key Signals

Guangdong organizes "open competitive bidding" for AI initiatives: policy now requires supporting evidence and verification

  • Source: Shantou Municipal Bureau of Industry and Information Technology ｜ Original link
  • On August 17, the Shantou Bureau of Industry and Information Technology forwarded a notice from the Guangdong Provincial Department of Industry and Information Technology, organizing applications for open competitive bidding on innovation tasks in AI and other areas. The key mechanism: application materials must include supporting evidence, which the competent authorities verify for authenticity, completeness, and validity before proceeding to the recommendation process.
  • Why it matters: The value of this policy lies not in "yet another AI subsidy," but in chaining "task guidelines → application evidence → authority verification → recommendation process" into a policy execution chain—the government side is also shifting from "submit a project name" to "speak with evidence." The implication for FDEs: enterprise AI scenarios can absolutely be broken down into biddable, verifiable task packages—each task with clearly defined goals, data conditions, acceptance criteria, and evidence methods—which both facilitates access to policy resources and makes internal project approval more rigorous.

AWS: Scaling enterprise agents hinges on a vendor-lock-in-free control plane

  • Source: AWS Official Blog ｜ Original link
  • On August 20, AWS published an article on architectures for scaling enterprise agents, discussing operating models in multi-framework, multi-model, multi-provider environments, and positioning framework-agnostic access and control plane practices as the core of avoiding vendor lock-in.
  • Why it matters: When even the cloud provider itself emphasizes "don't get locked in," it shows the enterprise agent market has entered a platform competition phase. When procuring agent platforms, the capability most easily overlooked in acceptance testing is not integration, but exit. Five exit acceptance criteria for enterprise agents: models are replaceable → Skills/connectors are exportable → business test sets belong to the enterprise → identity and logs are migratable → failures can be rolled back. Verify exit capability at procurement time to avoid discovering lock-in only after scaling. Note the boundary: this is AWS's official methodology—adopting AWS does not automatically eliminate lock-in—the acceptance logic applies equally to all platforms.

Implications for FDE Practitioners

  • Measure project progress with the "six-level AI delivery evidence ladder": real documents → sample volume → anomaly triage → quality scans → business sign-off → cross-period reuse. The first four levels prove engineering delivery, the fifth proves business acceptance, and only the sixth begins to prove organizational adoption. Generating a deliverable does not mean delivery succeeded—"generated, pending acceptance" and "accepted" are two different stages and must be reported separately in daily reports and briefings.
  • Break enterprise scenarios into biddable task packages: with all four elements in place—goals, data conditions, acceptance criteria, and evidence methods—you can both align with policy applications and make internal project approval clearer.
  • Run exit tests before procuring an agent platform: Can you swap models? Can you export Skills? Who owns the test sets? Can logs be migrated? How are failures rolled back? Ask these five questions clearly before signing—it is far cheaper than seeking remedies afterward.
