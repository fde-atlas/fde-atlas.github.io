What do people who regret an FDE career change have in common? Four key dimensions and a pre-offer checklist

Original title: FDE転職で後悔する人の共通点は？4つの軸と内定前チェックリスト

FDE総研 · 2026-11-19 · 10 min read

Summary

This Japanese article from FDE総研 argues that regret among people moving into Forward Deployed Engineer roles stems mainly from mismatches between pre-hire expectations and actual duties, not from workload itself. It proposes checking four axes before accepting an offer: role, workload, evaluation, and exit paths. It cites Anthropic job postings listing 25–50% customer-site visits as a workload benchmark, maps likely gaps by prior role (SE, sales engineer, consulting, ML engineer, SaaS developer), and provides a 10-item pre-offer checklist, advising candidates to delay acceptance if three or more answers remain vague.

Key points

  • Regret comes from expectation gaps, not workload itself.
  • Check four axes: role, workload, evaluation, exit paths.
  • Anthropic postings cite 25–50% customer-site visits.
  • Likely gaps differ by prior role, e.g. SE vs ML engineer.
  • Delay acceptance if 3+ checklist answers stay vague.

What aspects of moving to an FDE role are most likely to lead to regret?

FDE Soken divides career-change regret into four axes: role, workload, evaluation, and exit paths. For workload, it uses the 25-50% customer-site visits stated in the Anthropic job posting as a benchmark.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role popularized by Palantir. A small team brings the product to the customer's site and handles everything from problem definition through implementation to embedding it in operations. Most regret stems not from "the work was tough" but from a mismatch between pre-hire expectations and the actual scope of responsibility. FDE Soken believes this mismatch can be measured in advance along the following four axes. For an overview of the role, see What is an FDE?.

Role axis: What goes wrong when you join expecting a "development role"?

Palantir's FDEs are described as owning projects end-to-end, so joining expecting only implementation work makes regret likely.

The Pragmatic Engineer describes Palantir's FDE as "a role close to a startup CTO, owning difficult projects end-to-end in a small team." There is time to write code, but the FDE decides what to build. Reaching agreement with the customer and ensuring what is built continues to be used take up roughly equal weight. The more you want to focus on implementation, the more likely you are to feel "this isn't the job I expected."

Workload axis: Why is the ratio of business trips and on-site assignments overlooked?

Some Anthropic FDE job postings explicitly state 25-50% customer-site visits, so for an FDE career change you should ask for workload ratios as concrete numbers before accepting an offer.

Job postings often summarize this with phrases like "collaborating with customers" or "solving problems on-site," making actual travel frequency hard to see. Some, like Anthropic, state the ratio explicitly, but it varies widely by company and project. If you have family circumstances or location constraints, asking about "the busiest month" rather than the "average" can reduce the gap after joining. The realities of remote work, on-site assignments, and business trips are covered in How FDEs work.

Evaluation and exit axes: How do you verify how results are measured and your next career step?

Because FDE results tend to show up as on-site adoption, regret lingers unless you verify the evaluation metrics and the track record of where former FDEs have moved.

FDE results often show up as "whether the customer's operations changed," which may not fit evaluations based on lines of code or release counts. Whether evaluators directly observe on-site results and what roles former FDEs moved into are things you can ask interviewers directly. For a company-side view of evaluation frameworks, FDE evaluation systems is a useful reference.

How does the gap with FDE work differ by previous role (SE, sales engineer, consultant, ML engineer, SaaS developer)?

Viewed by previous role through FDE Soken's four axes, SEs tend to hit gaps on the role axis, consultants on implementation, and ML engineers on workload.

Even within FDE career changes, the axis where people stumble differs by previous role. How should those from SE / SIer backgrounds prepare for "specs not being handed to them"?

For FDE career changers from SIer backgrounds, requirements-definition experience is an asset, but the heavy responsibility of re-deciding specifications themselves tends to create a gap.

At an SIer, success means building exactly what was agreed upon in the specifications. In FDE, you work on the assumption that those specifications themselves will change along the way. The details of the difference are compared in Differences between FDE and SIer.

How do those from consulting or sales engineering backgrounds view the weight of implementation?

People from IT consulting or sales engineering backgrounds can leverage their strength in customer understanding, but in FDE they bear the responsibility of implementing things themselves and running them in production.

The biggest difference is that you are responsible for systems that keep running in production, not just proposals or demos for validation. Also check Differences between FDE and consulting and Differences between FDE and solution engineers.

What do ML engineers and those from SaaS development backgrounds struggle with?

FDE career changers from ML engineering and SaaS development have less anxiety about the technical side, but tend to struggle with on-site work at customers and the volume of bespoke support.

The more you want to go deep in a specialty, the more the breadth of technologies and time spent on field support feel like a burden. Differences from AI-related roles are organized in Differences between FDE and AI engineers.

Do years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size change whether an FDE career change is a good fit?

FDE Soken believes that of the four conditions, the combination of years of experience and the size of the destination company most strongly determines regret in an FDE career change.

If English ability is high but years of experience are short, what should take priority?

FDE candidates with strong English but limited experience should first verify whether they can carry out implementation on their own in the field, before anything else including English.

English may be a prerequisite for FDE roles at foreign companies. However, FDE Soken sets the priority order for decisions that prevent regret as "1) Can you implement on your own? 2) Experience coordinating with customers 3) English ability." This is because even if you are hired for your English, it is easy to get stuck on evaluation criteria if you cannot operate independently in the field. The level of English required is covered in detail in Is English ability necessary for FDE?.

What should people changing careers by age group or from non-technical backgrounds watch out for?

People in their 30s or 40s or from non-technical backgrounds aiming for FDE can reduce career-change regret by changing the axis they verify according to their own conditions.

It becomes easier to decide if people in their 30s check the role axis, those in their 40s check rank and the exit axis, and those without experience check the training structure before workload. Each is explored further in FDE career changes in your 30s, FDE career changes in your 40s, and How to become an FDE without prior experience.

What kind of person is less likely to regret an FDE career change?

People who enjoy defining ambiguous problems themselves and carrying them through from implementation to adoption tend to be less likely to regret an FDE career change.

It suits people who feel a sense of reward at the moment a customer's business changes, and who want to use both technology and communication. On its official website, VACAN Technologies states "Proposal and implementation, in one team," and introduces itself as a Forward Deployed Engineering team (official website). Whether you can empathize with this philosophy is also a gauge of your fit with FDE.

What kind of person is not suited to FDE?

For people who want to go deep in one technology under fixed specifications, or who want to avoid travel and customer-facing work, SWE or SIer is a better fit than FDE.

Being unsuited is not a matter of ability but of preferred working style. Differences from each role can be compared in Differences between FDE and SWE and Differences between FDE and PdM. The nature of the workload is organized in Is FDE tough?, and how to judge fit in People suited to FDE.

What should you confirm before accepting an FDE offer to prevent career-change regret?

FDE Soken recommends verifying 10 items across 4 axes in interviews before accepting an offer, and holding off on acceptance if 3 or more items have vague answers.

"Hold off if 3 or more" is a guideline FDE Soken established as an editorial judgment (evaluation date: November 19, 2026). For application and selection procedures themselves, please check each company's official recruiting page.

What should you ask in the interview?

In interviews with FDE offer holders, it is important to get answers backed by numbers and track records, not examples, on the 4 axes of role, workload, evaluation, and exit.

  • Role: In the most recent project, how far did the FDE's responsibilities extend, from problem definition to production operation?
  • Role: Who is responsible for deployment to production (the work of actually running it) and maintenance?
  • Role: In reaching agreement with the customer, what can the FDE decide?
  • Workload: What percentage of time is spent at customer sites, on average and in the busiest month?
  • Workload: Can you choose whether to be stationed on-site or travel, before assignment?
  • Evaluation: What are the evaluation metrics? Is adoption at customer sites counted as a result?
  • Evaluation: Does the evaluator directly observe the customer's on-site outcomes?
  • Exit paths: What roles have employees moved into after their FDE experience?
  • Exit paths: Are paths such as returning from FDE to product development or moving into PdM or leadership roles formally established as systems?
  • Overall: If anyone left the FDE role in the past year, what were the main reasons?

What items should be put in writing in the offer letter?

In an FDE offer letter, it is important to confirm the job title, grade, work location, and how travel is handled in writing, and not to accept based on verbal explanations alone.

Rather than relying solely on verbal explanations for workload ratios and transfer conditions discussed in the interview, keeping a written or email record helps prevent discrepancies later. How to approach negotiating conditions is covered in Negotiating an FDE Offer, and what to ask during the selection stage is covered in Common FDE Interview Questions.

How should you decide when an answer is vague?

If any items come back with vague answers in an FDE interview, the practical approach is to write out the unclear points on that axis, request a follow-up interview, and discuss extending the acceptance deadline.

At companies where the FDE organization has just been established, systems may not be in place and they may be unable to answer. In that case, treat the "no answer" itself as a sign that discretion is high but so is risk. Then, comparing it against your own tolerance will lead to a choice you are less likely to regret. A map of the FDE career as a whole is summarized in FDE Career Guide.
