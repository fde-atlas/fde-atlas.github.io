What Is an AI-Native Services Firm?

MarsLab · 2026-08-17 · 2 min read

Full text

A definition of the category MarsLab operates in, why it emerged, and how it differs from a software vendor, a consultancy and a staffing firm.

An AI-native services firm (also called an AI-native services company) delivers the outcome itself rather than selling software that helps someone else deliver it. It embeds engineers inside a client's operation, rebuilds that operation around AI systems, and is accountable for the result. The distinction is ownership of the work: a SaaS vendor ships a tool and leaves the operating problem with the customer; an AI-native services firm takes the operating problem.

  • Why the category emerged

    Spending on services has always dwarfed spending on software. Sequoia Capital's "Services: The New Software" puts it at roughly six dollars of services for every dollar of product. Until recently that gap was unreachable for a small team, because services scaled with headcount. Capable models changed the ratio between people and output, which made it possible to sell the outcome rather than the tool.

  • How it differs from a consultancy

    A consultancy delivers a recommendation and hands over a document; the client then finds someone to build it. An AI-native services company delivers a running system in production and stays through adoption. The test is simple: at the end of the engagement, does the client hold a plan or a working system?

  • How it differs from a software vendor

    A software vendor sells the same product to every customer and asks each to adapt around it. An AI-native services company builds around the operation as it actually runs (the existing ERP, the existing floor process, the existing field routine) because the constraint is rarely the model and almost always the integration.

  • How it differs from staffing

    A staffing firm supplies people by the hour and the client carries the risk of the outcome. An AI-native services company is accountable for the result, which is why the engagement is scoped to a system in production rather than a number of billed hours.

  • The Forward Deployed Engineer model

    The delivery model most AI-native services companies use is the Forward Deployed Engineer, a pattern Palantir originated: engineers work inside the client's operation rather than at a distance from it. It exists because the hard part of applied AI is not the model; it is knowing which twenty minutes of a plant manager's day actually costs money, and that knowledge is not available remotely.

  • What to ask one before hiring it

    Ask for named clients rather than anonymised tiers, and for the figure the engagement moved. Ask who owns the system and the stack at the end. Ask how long it takes to reach production, and whether that means a prototype or a system the business depends on. An AI-native services company that cannot answer those with specifics is selling consulting with a new label.
