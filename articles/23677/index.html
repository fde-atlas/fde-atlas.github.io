Summary

This Japanese guide from DeFactory explains the freelance CPO (Chief Product Officer) market for product professionals considering independence. It argues demand is rising from post-funding startups and large enterprises launching DX products. Freelance PdM rates average 87–89万 yen per month, while CPO-level strategic engagements can exceed 100–200万 yen. The article contrasts advisor-type and project-type contracts, outlines rates by working days (from monthly advisory at 30–80万 yen to full-commitment above 150–250万 yen), and details required skills: strategy design, data-driven decision-making, and stakeholder communication. It stresses quantifying achievements and concrete generative AI use cases in skill sheets, and recommends registering with multiple freelance agents for access to unlisted high-rate projects.

Key points

  • Demand for freelance CPOs is growing among funded startups and DX-focused enterprises.
  • Freelance PdM rates average 87–89万 yen monthly; CPO-level work can exceed 100–200万 yen.
  • Contracts split into advisor-type and project-type; multi-client parallel work can yield 150–250万 yen monthly.
  • Key skills: translating business strategy into product roadmaps, data-driven decisions, cross-functional communication.
  • Quantified generative AI achievements on skill sheets improve win rates; multiple agents help access unlisted projects.

"I want to go independent as a freelancer by leveraging my experience as a CPO" or "I want to learn how to work as a startup advisory CPO on the side"—demand like this has been rising rapidly from 2025 to 2026.

Professionals with CPO (Chief Product Officer) expertise in overseeing product strategy are in high demand among startups that have completed fundraising and large enterprises launching DX products. The market rate for freelance PdM (Product Manager) projects is around 870,000 to 890,000 yen per month, and freelancers with CPO-level strategic experience can secure projects exceeding 1 to 2 million yen.

This article provides a systematic explanation for product professionals considering independence, covering the role of a freelance CPO, market rates, required skills, and how to polish your skill sheet using generative AI.

High-paying, direct-contract project information

A CPO (Chief Product Officer) is the top executive responsible for overseeing the strategy, development, and improvement of a company's entire product portfolio. In Japanese, the role is called "Saiko Product Sekininsha" (Chief Product Officer) and is positioned as a member of the executive team (CXO).

The main responsibilities of a CPO are as follows.

  • Defining product vision and strategy: Translating the corporate vision into the product and formulating mid- to long-term roadmaps
  • Managing the product roadmap: Prioritizing feature development and managing progress
  • Managing the product development team: Leading teams spanning PdMs, designers, and engineers
  • Market research and customer analysis: Continuously analyzing competitor trends, user needs, and technology trends, and reflecting them in decision-making
  • KPI design and ensuring business results: Taking ownership of business metrics related to the product, such as DAU, retention rate, and revenue contribution
  • Collaboration with the executive team, including the CEO and CTO: Serving as a bridge between the business side and the development side

While the CTO is the "owner of technology," the CPO is the final decision-maker on "what to build (What & Why)." While the CTO handles "how to build it (How)," the CPO defines "why the product should exist in the market."

While a full-time CPO commits deeply to one company's management, the greatest characteristic of a freelance CPO is the ability to work with multiple companies in parallel. Combining three companies at 2 days per week each can result in a monthly income exceeding that of a full-time position.

Demand for freelance CPOs is particularly strong among startups at a stage where "hiring a full-time CPO is premature, but support is needed for designing and executing product strategy," with angel-stage to around Series B companies being the main clients.

Behind the growing demand for freelance CPOs are the following three structural changes.

(1) Need to strengthen products after startup fundraising Companies that have passed the seed to Series A stages expand their engineering teams, but frequently find themselves without CxO-level talent to oversee product strategy. This creates demand for freelance CPOs to join as "strategic partners."

(2) Launching DX products at non-IT large enterprises When large enterprises in traditional industries such as manufacturing, finance, and retail bring SaaS-type digital products in-house, they often lack talent who can formulate strategy with a product mindset. An increasing number of these companies bring in external freelance CPOs on a limited-term basis to establish product organizations.

(3) Supporting the growth of product organizations Companies that already have PM teams but whose growth has plateaued have advisory needs of the consultative type, asking for "an outside perspective to review product strategy."

Projects requiring CPO-level strategic involvement can go even higher, with some advisory contracts exceeding ¥1-2 million per month.

From a freelance consultant's perspective, achieving an annual income of ¥20 million generally requires working full-time at a monthly rate of about ¥1.6 million, and professionals with CPO-level experience, track record, and networks have the potential to reach this level.

Requests to freelance CPOs vary greatly depending on the company's stage and challenges. Understanding the actual project types helps you clarify which position you can contribute from.

This is the most common project type. Startups from seed through post-Series A, addressing challenges such as the following.

  • Developing product differentiation strategies based on competitive analysis
  • Revising roadmaps toward PMF and resetting priorities
  • Supporting onboarding of hired PdMs and designing evaluation systems
  • Preparing product KPIs and materials for investors

Startup projects are characterized by their sense of speed and direct connection to management. Since you support decision-making while working directly with the CEO, responsibility is high but so is discretion, and many projects are highly rewarding.

Support for large companies in manufacturing, finance, and distribution when launching in-house products (BtoB SaaS, internal DX tools, etc.).

  • Establishing product organizations (PdM roles) and recruitment support
  • Introducing agile development processes and transforming team culture
  • Stakeholder alignment and streamlining decision-making processes
  • Designing collaboration frameworks with external vendors

Large company projects tend to have higher monthly rates and longer contract durations, but on the flip side, they require the ability to handle slow decision-making and internal politics.

Support for redesigning products with slowing growth or pivots involving business model changes.

  • Structuring challenges through user interviews and data analysis
  • Redefining the product's value proposition
  • Developing strategies for transitioning from BtoC to BtoB, or reverse pivots
  • Roadmaps for executing new strategies while minimizing impact on existing customers

Pivot and rebranding projects have hard-to-define durations and are sometimes compensated on a project basis. The sense of achievement upon success is extremely high, and a "track record of turning a product around" becomes a powerful portfolio item for your subsequent freelance career.

What is most demanded of freelance CPOs is the ability to translate business strategy into product design. Rather than simply prioritizing features, you need the logical structuring skills to explain "why this feature is needed now" by connecting it to market data, competitive analysis, and customer needs.

Specifically valued skills:

  • Linking OKR and KPI design with product goals
  • Practicing user story mapping and Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) theory
  • Creating PRDs (Product Requirements Documents) and highly precise specification development
  • Applying competitive analysis frameworks (Porter's Five Forces, positioning maps, etc.)

Product decisions should start not from "what engineers want to build" or "what designers think is beautiful," but from customer problems indicated by data. Freelance CPOs need the ability to independently conduct data analysis, form hypotheses, and run validation cycles.

Quantitative data: Behavioral analysis using Google Analytics, Mixpanel, Amplitude, etc., A/B test design and evaluation, cohort analysis, funnel analysis

Qualitative data: Designing, conducting, and analyzing user interviews, creating customer journey maps, designing NPS and CSAT surveys and reflecting them in improvement initiatives

People who can "derive decisions from data," rather than merely "read data," are rare, and this is a key differentiator that determines freelance rates.

The CPO is a role that "touches every department" within a company. Engineers, designers, sales, marketing, and executives all communicate in different languages. Freelance CPOs face the additional handicap of being "external talent," so they need communication skills to build trust quickly.

Particularly valued skills:

  • Regular reporting to executives and how to drive decision-making
  • How to communicate requirements to development teams (defining non-functional requirements and acceptance criteria)
  • Designing consensus-building processes among conflicting stakeholders
  • Managing team motivation and fostering a feedback culture

The most important factor in winning high-rate freelance CPO projects is "articulating your track record."

Hiring managers and CEOs evaluate concrete business results such as the following.

  • "Experience developing a roadmap that improved DAU by X% in X months"
  • "Track record of redesigning onboarding that improved churn rate from X% to X%"
  • "Experience leading initiatives that improved paid conversion rate by X% through new feature releases"
  • "Experience creating product presentation materials for investors in a Series A round"

Expressions like "led and contributed with numbers," rather than "was involved in," directly demonstrate your market value as a freelance CPO.

Since 2025, the use of generative AI in CPO and PdM work has shifted from "optional" to "standard equipment." There is a significant difference in work efficiency and decision quality between CPOs who can leverage generative AI and those who cannot.

To differentiate yourself with your skill sheet (resume) when winning freelance CPO projects, the following points are important.

(1) Describe your track record with generative AI concretely

Writing quantifiable impact is effective, such as "Reduced PRD creation time by 50% using generative AI" or "Shortened the initiative planning cycle from two weeks to three days with ChatGPT-based user interview analysis." Vague statements like "knowledgeable about generative AI" are not valued.

(2) Clarify the product phase and your role

Clearly indicate which phase you excel at: "0→1 MVP launch experience," "1→10 growth experience," or "10→100 scaling experience." Since hiring companies look for talent that matches their current phase, this helps prevent mismatches and increases your project win rate.

(3) List the tools and frameworks you have used

Because recruiters refer to these when narrowing down candidates via search, accurately list tool names such as the following.

  • Product management: Notion, Linear, Jira, Confluence
  • Data analysis: Google Analytics, Mixpanel, Amplitude, Looker
  • User research: UserTesting, Dovetail, Maze
  • Design collaboration: Figma, FigJam
  • AI tools: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, GitHub Copilot

(4) Put a results block backed by numbers at the top

Place a 3-5 line bullet list of "summary achievements" at the beginning of your skill sheet, structured so that readers immediately judge "this person can deliver results."

When looking for projects as a freelance CPO or PdM, relying solely on personal sales activities (social media presence and referrals) risks gaps between projects. Using a freelance agent offers the following benefits.

  • Access to non-public projects: Much of the advisory demand from startups and operating companies is not posted on job sites and is introduced only through agents
  • Rate negotiation support: Agents negotiate with companies on your behalf to secure appropriate rates
  • Contract and legal support: They assist with reviewing service agreement terms and checking intellectual property clauses
  • Ongoing project proposals: They continue introducing your next project without gaps after your current one ends

To go independent as a CPO, the following are generally required: "5+ years of practical experience as a PdM," "experience in overall product strategy planning and team management," and "a proven track record of contributing to business results (DAU, revenue, churn rate, etc.)." Even without full-time CxO experience, you can win freelance CPO projects with a career where you have effectively played a CPO-equivalent role, such as "PM → Senior PM → PdM → Head of Product."

While not mandatory, understanding basic engineering concepts (APIs, databases, and system architecture concepts) is important for earning trust as a freelance CPO. Being at the level of "can't implement, but can estimate technical difficulty and workload" allows you to quickly build trust with development teams. For CPOs from non-engineering backgrounds, positioning yourself to compensate with strengths in business strategy, user research, and data analysis is effective.

Freelance rates tend to be higher. Full-time CPO annual salaries typically range from 15 to 30 million yen (large enterprises and listed startups), but cash compensation excluding stock options is often around 10 to 15 million yen. On the other hand, securing 1.5 to 2 million yen per month as a freelance CPO across multiple companies can reach an annual income of 20 to 30 million yen. However, you lose full-time employee benefits such as social insurance, retirement allowances, and stock options, so it is important to judge based on overall economic rationality rather than a simple comparison.

Yes, it is possible. In fact, starting with side work is a recommended route, as it lets you build a track record as a full-time CPO while getting a feel for independence without taking risks. A common path is to start with monthly advisory roles (2-4 times per month, 300,000-600,000 yen per month) or one-day-per-week side projects, then transition to full-time freelancing once you have accumulated enough achievements. However, be sure to check your employer's work rules (prohibitions on side work and non-compete obligations) in advance.

The most common issue is "difficulty making decisions in an environment lacking internal trust." Unlike a full-time CPO, a freelancer is outside internal politics and interpersonal relationships, so important information may not reach them, or decisions may lack follow-through. As a countermeasure, it is important to conduct frequent 1-on-1s with the CEO and CTO early on and clarify "who you are working for" before taking action. Also, leaving clear deliverables (documents and reports) and making your work "visible" in short cycles accelerates building trust.

There is no official certification dedicated to CPOs, but the following qualifications and certifications can serve as supplementary evaluation criteria during hiring.

  • Product School Certification (CPO Certification): A credential from a global product management education institution
  • PMI-ACP (Agile Certification): Demonstrates practical knowledge of agile development
  • Google Analytics Individual Qualification (GAIQ): Proof of data analysis skills
  • AWS Certification (e.g., SAA): Reinforces technical product knowledge

While "product track record and numbers" are valued more than the certifications themselves, holding certifications can be a plus when dealing with overseas companies or working on projects with major SaaS companies.

Summarizing the freelance CPO market as of 2026, the following points emerge.

  • Demand is growing: Demand for product strategy talent is surging in post-funding startups and enterprise DX projects
  • Monthly rates of over ¥1-2 million are realistic: Exceeding the freelance PdM market rate (¥870,000-890,000), you can aim for projects paying over ¥1 million with CPO-level strategic involvement
  • High flexibility in working arrangements: From a one-day-per-week advisory role to full commitment of 4-5 days per week
  • Generative AI usage is a differentiator: Demonstrating a track record of using generative AI for user research, PRD creation, and data analysis lowers hiring competition
  • Using agents is a shortcut to securing projects: Access to non-public high-rate projects and rate negotiation support lead to stability after going independent

Experience as a CPO or Head of Product overseeing product strategy carries very high market value in the freelance market. As a first step toward independence, we recommend quantifying your track record in numbers and starting with agent registration and a rate assessment.
