What is Forward Deployed Engineering? Meaning, Process, and Differences

Original title: forward deployed engineeringとは？意味・進め方・違い

FDE総研 · 2026-10-20 · 11 min read

Summary

This Japanese explainer from FDE总研 defines Forward Deployed Engineering as an approach where engineers embed at customer sites and carry software through to actual business use. It traces the term to Palantir, whose Forward Deployed Software Engineers worked on government and enterprise deployments. The article outlines a four-phase cycle (discovery, prototyping, production, feedback to product), contrasts FDEs with contractors, solutions engineers and consultants, and argues that generative AI's last-mile integration problem is driving demand—OpenAI and Anthropic now hire FDEs and a16z has written on the role. It closes with required skills and steps for aspiring FDEs.

Key points

  • FDE originated at Palantir, where engineers were stationed at customer sites to find and solve problems directly.
  • Work runs in short iterative cycles: discovery, prototyping, production deployment, and feeding learnings back into the core product.
  • Unlike contracted development, success is measured by actual business use, and field learnings are expected to flow back into the product.
  • Generative AI adoption creates a last-mile customization problem; OpenAI, Anthropic and a16z have highlighted rising demand for FDEs.
  • FDEs need full-stack and data/AI implementation skills plus problem framing and stakeholder coordination abilities.

Forward Deployed Engineering is an approach in which engineers embed themselves in the customer's workplace and deliver software to the point where it is "actually used in operations." This article organizes the meaning and origin of the term, the workflow, how it differs from traditional development, why it is attracting attention, and the skills required, from the perspective of engineers who want to work as FDEs.

What is Forward Deployed Engineering?

It is an engineering approach in which engineers deeply embed themselves in the customer's workplace and consistently handle everything from problem discovery to implementation and operational adoption.

"Forward Deployed Engineering" is a term often used alongside the job title "FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)," which carries out this approach. It doesn't end with simply building software and handing it over. The engineer themselves takes responsibility for the software actually working within the customer's operations and producing results. It is not merely a job title, but refers to the philosophy of "bridging the gap between the product and the customer's workplace through engineering."

Etymology and Origin: The "Frontline Deployment" Concept Born at Palantir

"Forward Deployed" derives from the military term for frontline deployment. As a job title, it originated from Palantir's deployments for governments and enterprises.

In the process of deploying data analytics platforms to government agencies and large enterprises, Palantir Technologies established a system in which engineers were stationed on the customer's side. The company's recruiting information and official blog explain that this role was called "Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE)" internally and operated separately from engineers developing core products. Rather than waiting for specifications inside the office, they stand at the customer's "frontline," pick up problems, and build solutions on the spot. This attitude is embodied in the term "Forward Deployed."

Who is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)?

An FDE is a software engineer who sits at the same desk as the customer to hear about problems and writes code on the spot to deliver something that works.

The roles of people who call themselves FDEs vary slightly from company to company, but their common characteristics can be summarized as follows.

From the perspective of someone working in the field, it is easiest to understand an FDE as "the product engineer closest to the customer." They are neither a salesperson nor a consultant; it is a role premised on getting hands dirty and delivering results.

How does Forward Deployed Engineering proceed?

It runs through four stages—problem discovery in the field, building and validating small, going to production, and feeding back into the product—in short cycles.

It does not proceed through each phase once in sequence like a waterfall. The hallmark of Forward Deployed Engineering is repeatedly moving back and forth through the same stages while observing the customer's reactions.

The Typical Four Phases

In most cases, the process moves through field understanding, prototyping, production deployment, and feeding back into the product, going back and forth through each stage many times.

  • Field understanding (Discovery): Through interviews with operations staff, field observation, and reviewing existing data, identify "what the real bottleneck is."
  • Prototype: Combine existing products and internal tools to build something "touchable" in days to weeks, and validate it together with the customer.
  • Production deployment: Set up permission design, data integration, monitoring, and operational procedures, and refine it to a quality usable in daily operations.
  • Feedback to product: Propose internally that requests and customizations that repeatedly appear across multiple customers be incorporated as features of the core product.
PhaseKey questionTypical deliverables
Field understandingWhat needs to be solved to change operations?Problem map, data inventory
PrototypeWill this solution actually be used?Working demo, validation notes
Production deploymentCan it be used stably every day?Production environment, operations manual

How is a week in the field structured?

A hallmark of how FDEs work is that dialogue with customers, implementation, and coordination with the internal product team are all mixed within a single week.

How the schedule is arranged varies greatly depending on the project stage. As a rough image, the allocation often looks something like the following.

How time is spent (illustrative)Description
Customer interactionsRegular meetings, interviews with on-site staff, demos and retrospectives
ImplementationBuilding data pipelines, UI improvements, designing prompts and tools for AI agents
Internal coordinationSharing requests with the product team, exchanging knowledge with other FDEs
Operations supportHandling incidents and inquiries, monitoring usage

In practical terms, things often move at a pace where "the code you write today is touched by on-site staff tomorrow." On the other hand, the sheer amount of context switching can be exhausting. Deliberately carving out focused time for implementation is essential.

How is Forward Deployed Engineering different from traditional software development?

The biggest difference is that you start building on-site before requirements are finalized, and success is measured not by "delivery" but by "whether it was actually used in the business."

In traditional contracted development or in-house product development, requirements definition and implementation are often handled by different people. In Forward Deployed Engineering, the same person handles both.

Differences from contracted development and SI

Contracted development focuses on correctly building agreed-upon specifications, while FDEs discover and refine the specifications themselves in the field.

The biggest difference is where the learnings flow. In contracted development, customer-specific knowledge tends to stay siloed within each project. In Forward Deployed Engineering, feeding field learnings back into the core product is an explicitly expected part of the role.

Differences from solution engineers and consultants

While pre-sales engineers and consultants often stop at proposals and design, FDEs write production code themselves and take responsibility for it.

Solution engineers and sales engineers are also technically knowledgeable roles that work with customers. However, their primary role is generally pre-sales technical validation and proposals. IT consultants focus mainly on business process design and decision support, with implementation often handled by a separate team.

FDEs go into the field both before and after the deal is signed, and write code that runs in production. From the field's perspective, you will often feel that "reaching agreement with a working screen, not a proposal deck" is the distinctly FDE way of working. In return, FDEs themselves bear responsibility for technical decisions.

Why is Forward Deployed Engineering gaining attention now?

The adoption of generative AI has spread the problem of "having a model but failing to embed it into the business," raising the value of people who can implement on-site.

A concept long used at Palantir, it became widely known as a job title around 2025. Behind this lies a structural shift happening where AI is being adopted.

The "last mile" problem of generative AI and agent adoption

LLMs and AI agents are highly versatile, but the final customization to fit each customer's data and workflows determines success or failure.

Large language models can do a lot out of the box. But to use them in business, you need customer-specific work: connecting to internal data, permission management, integration with existing systems, evaluating output quality, and more. This "last mile" is hard to close with documentation and support channels alone, which is why engineers who can go on-site and implement are in demand.

In fact, AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic have posted job openings for Forward Deployed Engineers. Venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) has also discussed the FDE as a notable role at startups. Ironically, the more capable AI becomes, the more visible the difficulty of "how to embed it into the business" becomes, and the FDE role exists precisely to take on that challenge.

What it means for startups and Japanese companies

Companies that can not only sell products but also support customers on-site until results are achieved are increasingly chosen, and FDE-style organizations are being considered.

From the perspective of companies considering adopting FDEs, Forward Deployed Engineering offers the following benefits and points of caution.

PerspectiveWhat to expectWhat to watch out for
Customer valueResults come early after adoption, making churn less likelyPer-customer customization increases maintenance burden
Product developmentFirst-hand field information reaches the product quicklyRules for feeding learnings back are needed to avoid being driven by individual requests
Talent and organizationDevelops people who understand both technology and businessHard to hire, and training takes time

In Japan, much of this overlaps with areas traditionally covered by SIers and in-house IT departments. So rather than seeing it as an entirely new role, it may be easier to picture adoption as "existing field capabilities plus a product-oriented mindset."

What skills does Forward Deployed Engineering require?

Beyond coding ability, it requires understanding the customer's business, articulating ambiguous problems, and driving stakeholders to act.

Rather than excelling in just one area, maintaining all of these skills at a consistently high, balanced level is likely the surest path to results as an FDE.

Technical skills: full-stack plus data and AI implementation ability

More than depth in a single area, broad implementation ability to build working systems single-handedly, from data integration to UI and infrastructure, is valued.

Business and Interpersonal Skills: Problem Definition and Building Trust

The ability to translate customers' words into business challenges, agree on priorities, and move things forward has the greatest impact on an FDE's results.

In the field, even when a customer says "I want this feature," the real problem often lies elsewhere. What an FDE needs is not to implement requests as-is, but the ability to dig into "why it is needed" and articulate the problem to be solved. In addition, there are many situations where you must coordinate the interests of stakeholders with different positions, such as the customer's business staff, IT department, management, and your own product team. The ability to explain technically correct things in words the other party understands forms the foundation of trust.

Steps to Become an FDE from Scratch

Rather than aiming for the FDE title right away, a realistic path is to accumulate "customer-facing implementation experience" in your current workplace.

  • Build full-stack fundamentals: Gain experience building small applications end to end on your own, from database to screen.
  • Increase opportunities to talk directly with business users: Get involved in improving internal tools or development close to handling inquiries.
  • Gain experience integrating generative AI into business: Try applying RAG and AI agents to actual business workflows.
  • Learn to describe results in terms of "usage" and "impact": Be able to explain not what you built, but whose work changed and how.
  • Apply for FDE positions or new internal roles: Look for opportunities at AI companies, SaaS companies, and companies driving DX.

Adding a field perspective, people suited to being an FDE are those who "can enjoy situations with no clear answer." How well you can treat situations like undecided specifications, dirty data, and conflicting stakeholder opinions as positive challenges matters as much as technical skill, as you will often find.
