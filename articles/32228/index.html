This guide lists seventeen firms that sell forward deployed engineering to companies in Europe. Each one uses the words forward deployed, or describes engineers placed inside a client's team, on its own pages, in its job ads or in its directory text. Most of the work is on AI software. Every page used here was read on 21 October 2026. Each sentence tells you where its fact comes from: the firm itself, a national company register, a news report, or a partner directory.

A forward deployed engineer, often shortened to FDE, is a software engineer who works inside the client's organization, with the client's people and on the client's systems, for as long as the work lasts. The guide to forward deployed engineering explains the role in full, and what a forward deployed engineer is gives the short definition. This guide is about the firms that a company in Europe can hire to supply one. A separate list of US forward deployed engineering firms covers the United States.

Why European companies look for this help

Eurostat, the statistics office of the European Union, found that 19.95 percent of EU enterprises with 10 or more employees used at least one AI technology in 2025. Among enterprises that had considered AI and decided against it, the most common reason was a lack of relevant expertise, given by 70.89 percent [34]. An outside engineer who works inside the company is one way to supply that expertise. Why European companies hire FDE firms sets out the full Eurostat figures, by country and by company size.

Rules matter too. The European Commission states that the EU's AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024 and became applicable on 2 August 2026, with some exceptions [36]. This guide does not explain the law. It does give you questions to ask each firm about where your data is kept and who can see it.

How each entry is written

Each entry gives the facts, then two short lines. Fits names the buyer the firm suits, based on what the firm, a register or a reporter says. Pick someone else if names the case in which another firm would suit you better.

Words such as "by its own account", "the firm says" and "its page says" mark a fact that comes only from the firm. A named register, such as Companies House or the Polish National Court Register, or a named outlet, such as BusinessCloud, marks a fact from outside the firm. How the European FDE firms in this guide were chosen explains every test and every source.

The firms

1. Reveneau

Reveneau offers forward deployed engineers. Its offices are at 435 Utah Street in San Francisco and on Floor 33, 909 Mainland Street, in Vancouver. Reveneau lists no office in Europe, so a European buyer should ask where the engineers on its project would work and in which time zone. The rest of this entry is Reveneau's own account of how it works. Reveneau says AI writes 100 percent of its code. By its own account, every change has to pass a large eval suite before it is released. An eval suite is a set of automated tests that checks whether the software does what its specification asks, and Reveneau says it writes its suite from the specification. Reveneau says the suite is graded by Jev, and that Jev makes the suite run ten times faster than its previous grader did. Reveneau says it uses AI in place of hiring more engineers and puts that saving into the prices its clients pay. It says it takes responsibility for the whole project, through production and after release. Production means the live system that real users rely on each day. Anthropic's directory did not list Reveneau when all 321 entries were searched on 20 October 2026 [1].

Fits: a company that wants embedded engineers whose code is written by AI and tested against the specification before every release, by Reveneau's own account, and that can work with a team based in North America. You can contact Reveneau to describe the work.

Pick someone else if: you need a firm with an office in Europe that you can visit, or your rules require a partner that Anthropic's directory lists.

Choosing between them

Start with three questions. Who owns the firm? Where will its engineers sit, and in which time zone? And how will the work end? European FDE firms owned by larger companies covers the first. European FDE firms and US firms with European offices covers the second. Handover and exit from a forward deployed engagement covers the third.

The size of your company matters too. Eurostat found that 55.03 percent of large EU enterprises used AI in 2025, against 17 percent of small ones [34]. Some firms aim at the largest buyers: Distyl AI at Fortune 500 companies, per Crunchbase News, and Devoteam at industrial companies, by its own account. Others describe small teams: Twistag one or two engineers, and Netguru one embedded engineer. Language can matter as well. SFEIR's offer page and Polynom's job ad are in French, and accilium advertises a role for an engineer with German.

What none of them publish

No firm on the list publishes a rate card, a day rate or a price list for forward deployed engineers on the pages read on 21 October 2026, so ask each firm for a written quote. Netguru says its work is billed on time and materials and shares rates on a first call about the scope [2]. Polynom calls its offer an operated service "indexed on realised value". SFEIR's homepage shows a card headed Projet Preuve, French for proof project, with the figure 40k€ after two less-than signs, beside the line "Votre premier livrable IA en production en 8 semaines", meaning your first AI deliverable in production in 8 weeks. The page does not explain the figure, so this guide does not treat it as a price.

The closest public figures are official pay data. The UK's Office for National Statistics gives a median gross pay of £56,914 a year in 2025 for full-time programmers and software development professionals. Statistics Sweden gives a median of SEK 53,500 a month in 2025 for software and system developers.
