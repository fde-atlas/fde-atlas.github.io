Do forward deployed engineer jobs require a security clearance?

FDE Dispatch · 2026-11-04 · 13 min read

Summary

FDE Dispatch hand-reviewed 651 forward deployed engineer and deployment strategist postings from an October 24, 2026 public-board snapshot to answer whether FDE jobs require security clearances. Most do not: only 143 postings (22.0%) state a clearance, U.S. citizenship, or U.S.-person/export-control rule, while 508 (78.0%) are silent. Palantir dominates the clearance language, accounting for 70 of 113 clearance-rule postings; excluding Palantir, only 66 of 546 postings state any rule. Of the clearance rules, 41 require an active clearance at hire, 64 require eligibility or willingness to obtain one, and 8 treat it as a plus. Separately, 18 postings require U.S. citizenship and 30 cite ITAR/EAR export-control limits. The article breaks results down by role type, location, employer, and clearance level, and advises candidates to read exact wording and ask recruiters whether a seat sits on a cleared program rather than self-selecting out.

Key points

  • Only 22.0% of 651 postings state a clearance, citizenship, or export-control rule.
  • Palantir accounts for 70 of 113 clearance-rule postings; outside it, rules are rare.
  • 41 postings require an active clearance; 64 only require eligibility to obtain one.
  • 18 postings require U.S. citizenship; 30 cite ITAR/EAR export-control limits.
  • Advice: read exact wording and ask recruiters about cleared programs before self-selecting out.

Active required vs eligible to obtain

Active required means the posting says you must already hold an active or current clearance at hire (41 postings). Examples include active Secret or Top Secret lines and active TS/SCI with polygraph. One of the 41 (LangChain) also accepts a prior clearance.

Eligible or obtain means you must be eligible for, able to obtain, or willing to undergo a clearance or government vetting (64 postings). Wording such as active or eligibility and willingness to obtain counts here, because you can apply without holding an active clearance today.

Preferred means clearance is a plus only (8 postings).

For U.S. seats, a clearance is sponsored by an authorized government agency or cleared contractor. Individuals cannot submit a personal clearance application on their own; see the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency. Read the requirements section for the exact wording, and ask the recruiter whether the seat is on a cleared program.

U.S. citizenship and export control as separate gates

18 postings explicitly require U.S. citizenship. 16 of them also state a clearance rule; 2 require citizenship alone. 30 postings state a U.S.-person or export-control hiring limit (ITAR / EAR / export-controlled technology language that restricts who can be hired). Employer-discretion export-license boilerplate that does not restrict the candidate is counted as noise only.

Combined citizenship or export-control under this taxonomy: 47 postings. The visa sponsorship guide counted 46 under one citizenship_or_export_control bucket with a different keyword set and coarser label. Differences are explained in the method note; the visa guide figures are unchanged. For work authorization and sponsorship stance, use that guide.

Official references for export rules: U.S. Department of State Directorate of Defense Trade Controls (ITAR) and Bureau of Industry and Security (EAR).

Government and defense work with no stated rule

27 matched postings describe government, public-sector or defense customer work without stating a clearance, citizenship or U.S.-person rule. A posting can name defense or public-sector customers and still set no written clearance requirement. Read the full posting, and ask whether a later customer program needs a clearance.

Engineer vs deployment strategist

Engineer-title texts: 87 of 566 state a clearance rule (25 active, 56 eligible or obtain, 6 preferred). Deployment-strategist texts: 26 of 85 (16 active, 8 eligible or obtain, 2 preferred). Strategist clearance statements in this pull cluster on Palantir U.S. government seats.

Practical next steps

Employers can sponsor a clearance investigation; you cannot apply for a U.S. clearance by yourself (DCSA). For work authorization and visa sponsorship stance, use the visa sponsorship guide. Method and data

Population: 566 engineer-title posting texts pulled October 25, 2026 and 85 deployment-strategist posting texts pulled October 28, 2026 from the October 24, 2026 public-board FDE snapshot (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday). Management titles from that snapshot are not in this corpus. Keyword seed: security clearance, clearance, cleared, TS/SCI, Top Secret, secret, polygraph, US citizen(ship), citizen, US person, ITAR, EAR, export control, export license, FedRAMP, IL2-6, government, public sector, DoD, Department of Defense or War, federal, defense, national security, intelligence community, military, SC, DV, BPSS, CMMC, NIST 800, sovereign, warfighter, classified. Every one of the 366 matches was hand-classified.

Compared with the visa guide's 46 citizenship_or_export_control postings: this guide's citizenship-or-export total is 47, using a broader keyword seed and finer taxonomy. It counts Palantir Warp Speed U.S. Person Required seats and drops Reflection AI employer-authorization export boilerplate and the Together AI Singapore citizenship seat from the U.S. citizenship/export total. The visa guide figures are unchanged.

Compared with the certification guide's 106 postings that mention a security clearance: all 106 are in this guide's 113 clearance-rule postings. The certification guide seeded on certif, accredit, credential and clearance, so it missed 7 postings that name TS/SCI with polygraph, a government background investigation or vetting without the word clearance. That guide's figures are unchanged. Postings that name public sector only as one example of preferred regulated-industry background are counted as noise here, so government work with no rule covers seats whose own work is for government, public-sector or defense customers.

Limits: keyword matching is approximate; hand review is what produces the published counts. Postings close; check the url before relying on a row. Employers that post only on LinkedIn, Indeed, or other applicant systems are missing. Evidence links were live-checked November 4, 2026 in Pacific Time.

Questions people ask

Do forward deployed engineer jobs require a security clearance?

Most FDE postings in this corpus do not ask for a security clearance. Across 651 engineer and deployment-strategist posting texts, 508 postings (78.0%) are silent on clearance, U.S. citizenship, and U.S.-person or export-control rules after hand review. Of the 113 that state a clearance rule, 41 require an active clearance, 64 require eligibility or willingness to obtain one, and 8 only prefer a clearance. 70 of those 113 clearance postings are from Palantir.

How many FDE postings require U.S. citizenship or U.S. Person status?

18 postings explicitly require U.S. citizenship (16 of them also state a clearance rule; 2 citizenship-alone). 30 postings state a U.S.-person or export-control hiring limit. Combined, 47 postings carry citizenship or export-control language under this finer taxonomy. The visa sponsorship guide counted 46 under one citizenship_or_export_control bucket with a different keyword set; those visa figures are unchanged.

What does eligible for a clearance mean for an applicant?

Eligible or obtain wording means you do not need to hold an active clearance when you apply, but you must be able to be sponsored and cleared for the seat. U.S. clearances are sponsored by an authorized government agency or cleared contractor; individuals cannot apply for a personal clearance on their own (Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency). Read the requirements section for the exact level named, and ask the recruiter whether the seat sits on a cleared program.

Do deployment strategist postings ask for clearances the same way?

Of 85 deployment-strategist texts, 26 state a clearance rule (16 active required, 8 eligible or obtain, 2 preferred). Of 566 engineer-title texts, 87 state a clearance rule (25 active, 56 eligible or obtain, 6 preferred). Strategist clearance statements in this pull are concentrated at Palantir government seats.

How should candidates read government or defense customer wording with no clearance line?

27 matched postings describe government, public-sector or defense customer work without stating a clearance, citizenship or U.S.-person rule. Those postings set no written clearance requirement. Read the full requirements section, and ask the recruiter whether any customer program later needs a clearance. Pair this page with the visa sponsorship guide for work-authorization wording.
