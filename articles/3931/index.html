Transcript: Palantir's Shyam Sankar on enterprise AI autonomy, defense reformation, and why the forward deployed engineer can't be cargo-culted

TBPN Digest · 2025-04-17 · 27 min read

Summary

In a TBPN interview, Palantir CTO Shyam Sankar argues enterprise autonomy is the most valuable AI application, comparing the long tail of edge cases to self-driving cars. He cites results like halved sepsis deaths at Tampa General and AIG underwriting dropping from three weeks to under an hour. He says models are commoditizing, so Palantir stays LLM-agnostic and builds evaluation tooling, while internal teams build AI FDEs. He traces forward deployed engineering to a culture of extreme ownership that cannot be cargo-culted, and argues big projects need individual champions like the WWII dollar-a-year men.

Key points

  • Enterprise autonomy is the top customer topic; edge cases, not model power, are the bottleneck.
  • Results: sepsis deaths halved at Tampa General; AIG underwriting cut from three weeks to under an hour.
  • Model layer is commoditizing; Palantir runs multiple LLMs with constant evals for safe migration.
  • Forward deployed engineering stems from total ownership of implementation and can't be cargo-culted.
  • Big efforts need individual champions, echoing WWII's dollar-a-year men and Somerville's Pentagon build.
