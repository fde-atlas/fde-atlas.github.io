AWS posts its fastest growth in five years! Yet Amazon's stock plunges 7% after hours due to excessive AI spending

Original title: AWS 創近五年最快成長！Amazon 卻因 AI 支出過高，股價盤後重挫 7%

INSIDE · 2026-10-30 · 3 min read

Summary

Amazon's AWS grew 39% year over year to $46 billion in Q3, its fastest growth in nearly five years and above analyst estimates. But heavy AI and data center spending pushed free cash flow to negative $42.4 billion over the past twelve months, with capex of $77.26 billion. Amazon projects $220 billion in capex for 2026, and CEO Andy Jassy says 2027 spending will be significantly higher, claiming AWS could reach $1 trillion in annual revenue within five years. A soft Q4 revenue forecast and ad revenue growth of 12%—the first miss in seven quarters—added to investor concerns, sending shares down as much as 7% after hours.

Amazon's cloud business AWS posted third-quarter revenue of $46 billion, up 39% year over year—its fastest growth in nearly five years—but the massive capital expenditures it is making to capture the AI market have become investors' biggest concern. Amazon's free cash flow over the trailing 12 months through the end of September was negative $42.4 billion, and its shares fell as much as 7% in after-hours trading following the earnings release.

According to Reuters, Amazon's AWS revenue reached $46 billion in the third quarter, beating analysts' estimate of $45.61 billion, with 39% year-over-year growth slightly ahead of the market's expected 38.2% and up from 37% in the second quarter.

AI Capital Expenditures Keep Climbing, With Even More Spending Expected Next Year

However, Amazon's spending on AI and data centers is also rising rapidly.

Over the trailing 12 months through the third quarter, Amazon's free cash flow was negative $42.4 billion, with capital expenditures totaling $77.26 billion during the period—about $20 billion higher than market estimates. By comparison, Amazon's cash outflow in the previous quarter was $7.6 billion, while in the third quarter of last year it still generated $14.8 billion in cash flow.

Dave Wagner, portfolio manager at Aptus Capital Advisors, said free cash flow was the "ugliest part" of the earnings report—Amazon's operations still generate substantial cash, but capital expenditures have almost entirely swallowed it.

Amazon expects capital expenditures to reach $220 billion in 2026, and to keep rising next year.

Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said on the earnings call that capital expenditures in 2027 are expected to be "significantly more" than this year, but did not disclose a specific figure.

Jassy also emphasized to investors that the current investment strategy is paying off. He said AWS could become a business with $1 trillion in annual revenue within as little as five years, and noted that the money the company has invested in AWS capabilities and infrastructure is already starting to show results.

Both Amazon and Alphabet are maintaining their existing AI capital expenditure plans, while Microsoft lowered its own spending estimate from $175 billion to $170 billion on Wednesday.

Q4 Guidance Slightly Below Expectations, Amazon Shares Plunge as Much as 7% After Hours

Beyond AI spending, Amazon's fourth-quarter guidance also raised concerns in the market.

Amazon forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $240 billion to $247 billion. Although analysts' average estimate of $244.51 billion falls within that range, the midpoint of roughly $243.5 billion is still slightly below market expectations.

Jesse Cohen, senior analyst at Investing.com, noted that the free cash flow and the weak guidance together dragged down market confidence, describing Amazon's third-quarter earnings report as a "double disappointment."

Amazon shares had already fallen 4.3% in regular Thursday trading, closing at $243.79, and the after-hours decline widened to as much as 7% after the earnings release.

Tech stocks broadly fell that day as well. Intel warned that weak PC revenue could persist into the first half of next year, and its shares plunged 23% after hours; Alphabet shares slumped 12% in regular trading, marking the largest single-day percentage drop in its history.

Ad Revenue Misses Expectations for the First Time in Seven Quarters, Jassy Denies Impact from AI Shopping Agents

Amazon's advertising business also fell short of market expectations this quarter. Third-quarter ad revenue grew 12% year over year to $19.6 billion, below the expected 18% growth and missing analysts' forecasts for the first time in seven quarters.

That growth rate is significantly lower than the 26% in the second quarter and the 22% in the same period last year.

Jassy said he remains satisfied with the advertising business's performance and attributed part of the impact to Prime Day being moved to the second quarter this year. He expects fourth-quarter ad revenue growth to return to nearly 20%.

He also denied that AI shopping agents have affected Amazon's ad revenue. This quarter, Amazon blocked Meta's Muse agent, barring it from automatically shopping on behalf of consumers on Amazon's website.

Holiday Shopping Season Faces Consumer Pressure, AWS Becomes Investors' Focus

Amazon's fourth-quarter outlook also shows that the U.S. retail market still faces consumer pressure. Rising gas prices and living costs have led some consumers to trim their shopping budgets.

Still, the National Retail Federation estimated earlier this month that overall U.S. holiday season sales will grow 4.2% this year.

Retail still accounts for the largest share of Amazon's revenue, but as the company pours vast sums into building data centers and competing in the AI market, AWS has gradually become a key gauge for investors assessing Amazon's growth prospects. This quarter, AWS delivered its fastest revenue growth in nearly five years.
