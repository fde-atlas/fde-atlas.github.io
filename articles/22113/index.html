Summary

Crack ML Interview's prep guide for forward deployed engineer (FDE) interviews explains the role—engineers embedded with customers, shipping production code and integrations—and how it differs from product, solutions, and ML engineering roles. It outlines a typical five-round loop (decomposition, practical coding, deployment system design, AI/LLM applications, behavioral and customer role-plays), with 79 reviewed practice questions, guides for 13 companies, and 504 open roles. The authors are candid that public FDE interview data is thin: only 17 questions and 3 candidate reports come from actual FDE loops (OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, Scale AI, and others); the rest is editorial inference from related engineering loops.

Key points

  • FDEs work inside customer environments, judged on customer outcomes and reusable patterns.
  • Typical loop: recruiter screen, technical screen, then four to five onsite rounds.
  • Only 17 questions and 3 forum reports come from verified FDE interviews.
  • Reported questions include OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir, Scale AI, and Google.
  • AI companies test model APIs, retrieval, agents, and evals, rarely deep training theory.

What a forward deployed engineer does

An FDE is a software engineer assigned to customers rather than to a product area. The work is to make the product produce a real result for one customer: understand their problem and data, connect the product to their systems, write the code and workflows that are missing, get it into production, and bring the patterns that repeat back to the product team. The role is common at companies whose product is a platform that needs fitting to each customer, which today includes most companies selling AI systems to enterprises.

Builds for One customer at a time, inside their environment Every user of the product Prospects, before and during the sale A model-backed product or platform

Our inference (editorial)

The shape most loops follow

  • Recruiter screen: background, motivation for FDE work, travel.
  • Technical screen: practical coding, often with an API or a data file.
  • Onsite or virtual onsite, usually four or five of: decomposition, practical coding, deployment system design, ai / llm applications, behavioral & customer.
  • Some companies add a take-home or a presentation of a past project.

Inferred from the role's responsibilities and from the rounds reported for software and AI engineering loops at the same companies. Treat it as a planning aid, not as any company's official process.

FAQ

  • What does a forward deployed engineer do?
  • A forward deployed engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who works directly with a customer to make a product deliver results in that customer's environment: scoping the problem, integrating with their systems, writing production code and workflows, and feeding what they learn back to the product team.
  • Is FDE the same as FDSE or "deployment strategist"?
  • Titles vary by company. Forward deployed software engineer (FDSE) and forward deployed engineer usually describe the same engineering role. Some companies pair it with a less technical deployment or strategy role that owns the customer relationship; read the job description for how much code the role writes.
  • How is the FDE interview different from a software engineer interview?
  • Expect fewer pure algorithm puzzles and more practical work: decomposing a vague customer problem, coding against messy data and APIs, designing a system inside a customer's constraints, and customer role-plays. The bar on code quality and communication is high.
  • Do forward deployed engineers need machine learning?
  • At AI companies, applied knowledge matters: building on model APIs, retrieval, agents and evaluation. Deep model training theory is rarely the focus. Our AI application round guide covers what is usually expected.
  • How should I prepare in two weeks?
  • Read the five round guides, then spend most of the time doing: two or three practical coding questions with APIs and data, two deployment design questions, a decomposition case out loud, and a prepared story bank for the behavioral round. Use the AI interviewer for timed mocks.
