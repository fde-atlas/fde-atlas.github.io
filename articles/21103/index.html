The Three-Step Standard Delivery Playbook for Enterprise AI Projects: A Full Closed Loop from Project Kickoff, Build, to Launch Handover

Original title: 企业级 AI 项目标准交付三部曲：从立项、构建到上线交接全闭环

FDECareer.work · 2026-10-26 · 8 min read

Summary

This FDECareer practical guide argues that over 40% of enterprise AI pilots die after the demo because teams lack a production-grade delivery standard. It describes a three-stage adoption model (individual productivity, focused business pilots, organization-wide multi-agent loops) and a delivery methodology in three phases: scoping with quantified acceptance criteria and data-security red lines; production build featuring an Agent Harness state machine with checkpoint recovery, a golden evaluation dataset with automated regression, and multi-model failover gateways; and launch covering offline/VPC deployment, security audits, tracing, and handoff of runbooks. Success is defined solely by whether the client team can operate the system independently without the FDE.

Key points

  • Over 40% of AI pilots stall after the demo stage.
  • Enterprise AI adoption follows three evolution stages.
  • Deterministic rules and LLMs must have strictly divided roles.
  • Acceptance metrics are agreed upfront: accuracy, latency, fallback rate.
  • Launch success means client teams can self-operate without the FDE.

Why do more than 40% of pilot projects led by people with "AI Architect" titles on the market ultimately die after the demo, never making it into the customer's production environment? The answer lies in the dividing line repeatedly validated in our 2026 China Enterprise AI Delivery Supply-Demand Panorama Report and Overseas Top AI Companies' FDE Hiring Structure Map: what enterprises pay for is never the prompts in a Notebook, but a production closed loop that is independently verifiable and delivers quantifiable revenue, achieved under legacy systems (see the later IM and Legacy ERP Integration in Practice), strict data security red lines, and demanding service level agreements (SLAs).

As the 4th installment of our hands-on series, this special topic breaks down the full set of delivery standards for enterprise AI, from breaking through cognitive barriers to engineering implementation. The content combines strategic foundation selection for large models, a three-stage penetration evolution model, and the three-step delivery methodology of "scoping, production build, and launch handover."

Core Stages of the Three-Step Delivery and Guide Navigation

Enterprise AI project delivery follows the standard closed loop of the three steps: "scoping, production build, and launch handover." The core objectives, deliverables, and corresponding in-depth hands-on guide index for each stage are as follows:

Breaking Through Cognitive Barriers: The Strategic Foundation for Enterprise AI in the Era of Large Models

Before advancing technical implementation, you must first clear the three fatal misconceptions at the cognitive level among enterprise decision-makers and technical teams, and accurately identify the real pace of enterprise AI evolution and the foundation for technology selection.

The Three Fatal Misconceptions Enterprises Have When Embracing AI Today

  • Misconception 1: Treating large models as "chat toys" with no serious business scenario expectations: Many enterprises confine large models to generic scenarios such as internal employee chitchat and copy polishing assistance. Such pilots have no clear input-output contract, do not directly solve core business pain points, and can neither generate measurable business returns (ROI) nor build organizational confidence for sustained investment.
  • Misconception 2: Blindly building private large models and heavy-asset compute infrastructure, falling into an input-output disaster: When business scenarios are still unclear and the data flywheel has not yet been established, some enterprises spend huge sums purchasing compute servers and deploying massive general-purpose models privately from scratch, ultimately turning them into a sunk-cost disaster due to excessive maintenance costs, poor scenario fit, and disconnected model updates.
  • Misconception 3: Training everyone to write prompts, but with no actual change or accumulation in business processes: Efforts stop at organization-wide prompt engineering training; the techniques employees learn are scattered across personal computers. Once employees leave or prompts become obsolete, the enterprise has formed no reusable, auditable systematic assets, and business pipelines remain in a traditional, inefficient state.

The "Three-Stage Evolution Model" of Enterprise AI Penetration

Enterprises adopting AI never achieve success overnight; they must go through three evolution stages: from "scattered individuals" to "core restructuring" to "systematic closed loop":

  • Stage 1: Personal productivity tools (single-point efficiency gains, scattered individuals): Employees spontaneously use public large models to help with daily tasks like writing emails and looking up code. This stage has no system integration or enterprise-grade data governance; efficiency gains are scattered across individuals, with risks of data leakage.
  • Stage 2: Pilots in local core business flows (process restructuring, immediate results): The FDE steps into core scenarios with clear boundaries and quantifiable pain points (such as automatic after-sales ticket triage, automated approval of internal access permissions, and initial review of key clauses in non-standard contracts). Through deterministic state machines with Agent Harness safeguards, it achieves automated document flow and end-to-end audit trails, producing clearly visible cost reduction and efficiency gains.
  • Stage 3: Organization-level agent networks and business closed loops (capability accumulation, commercial closed loop): A cross-departmental multi-agent collaboration network that connects ERP, CRM, knowledge bases, and warehousing systems, forming an autonomous decision-making closed loop and a self-evolving data flywheel, accumulating into the enterprise's core engineering moat.

A Minimalist Decision Guide for Technology and Toolchain Selection

  • General-purpose large models vs. open-source models vs. industry vertical models: For high-level intent recognition and complex planning tasks, prioritize commercial flagship large models; for highly confidential data centers and strong compliance scenarios, use locally deployed open-source large models (with fine-tuned LoRA weights); for industry vertical scenarios, attach a private knowledge base via RAG on top of a base model rather than blindly training from scratch.
  • Avoiding pitfalls in the actual implementation of knowledge bases (RAG) and agents: Static policies and product specifications go to vector hybrid retrieval (Dense + BM25); dynamic business document flows and actions that modify system state must be executed by agents with deterministic state machine guardrails—never let the model directly call write APIs without protection.
  • Cutting-edge field observation: Leverage super ecosystems to form real connections: Avoid force-pushing obscure, self-built standalone apps. The most vital way for enterprise AI to land is to seamlessly embed into WeCom, DingTalk, or existing collaborative office workbenches, reaching users as lightweight bots or native cards.

Stage 1: Scoping & Acceptance

The first step in real enterprise delivery is "subtraction": extract quantifiable metrics from vague business complaints, and draw strict boundaries around data security and determinism.

Mapping the Business Status Quo and Uncovering Real Pain Points

Requirements often begin with a single complaint like "review is too slow" or "customer service response is not timely." The FDE needs to go deep into frontline business pipelines to reconstruct the real personnel collaboration chain:

  • Observe the entire process of each business task from trigger, opening the system, human judgment, to waiting for others' approval;
  • Distinguish the real proportions of "objective system latency" versus "cross-departmental communication waiting";
  • Establish an end-to-end ticket flow baseline to identify the pilot process best suited as the entry point.

Division of Labor Between Deterministic Rules and Large Model Capabilities

Resolutely reject "LLM-izing everything." Enterprise-grade systems demand extremely high determinism and traceability:

  • Deterministic rule handling: Employee identity verification, organizational affiliation checks, software license inventory queries, hard monetary threshold judgments, etc., are all determined by traditional code, databases, and business rule engines;
  • Large models are responsible for: Extracting unstructured application justifications, identifying abnormal risk deviations, and aligning information elements across multiple systems with semantic normalization;
  • Business decision authority: The model must never overstep its authority to issue business change commands; named-person approval or system rule authentication must always be retained.

Data Permission Boundaries and Security Compliance Red Lines

  • Map out the hierarchy of data assets, defining fields the model may read and fields it is strictly forbidden to touch;
  • Deploy a dedicated security vault that automatically removes personally identifiable information (PII, such as ID numbers, phone numbers, and real salaries) before data enters the large model's context;
  • Strictly limit the scope of context injection to prevent cross-departmental leakage of confidential information caused by unauthorized retrieval.

Agreeing on a Quantifiable Acceptance Metrics System

At the kickoff stage, agree on acceptance metrics in writing with the customer's key stakeholders, including:

  • Accuracy and coverage: Field extraction accuracy ≥ 95%, automatic handling coverage for routine tickets ≥ 70%;
  • Response latency and circuit breaking: End-to-end average processing time reduced by more than 60%, with per-step timeout circuit breaking as a fallback;
  • Fallback human takeover rate: The rate of automatic downgrade to human agents for extreme unknown anomalies ≤ 5%.

Stage 2: Prototype to Production Build (Build & Evaluate)

Once scope and metrics are set, enter the production-grade system build stage. The core of this stage is building an industrially highly available Agent Harness state machine engine and a complete evals system.

Enterprise-Grade End-to-End Layered System Topology

Enterprise AI production systems must be layered and decoupled—from access, gateway, orchestration, and models to legacy system bridging—each layer with independent testing and fault isolation capabilities:

Building an Agent Harness with Checkpoint Recovery

Large models are non-deterministic and subject to occasional network jitter; running them directly in bare processes is a cardinal sin in enterprise delivery. FDEs must equip agents with a robust Harness runtime container:

  • Long-transaction state machine scheduling: Break down complex delivery workflows into ordered atomic states (e.g., receive application → eligibility pre-review → named approval → simulated provisioning → receipt archiving);
  • Circuit breaking for infinite loops and overflow: Set step limits (Max Steps) and Token budget monitoring; once an infinite loop, prompt injection attack, or reverse hallucination is detected, immediately interrupt and trigger an alert;
  • Checkpoint snapshot storage and idempotent retries: Persist every state transition to local SQLite or Redis. If a network packet loss occurs when calling a third-party system, the system recovers the already-submitted state via the original request key, never blindly issuing duplicate charges or duplicate provisioning operations.

Constructing a golden-standard business eval set and automated pipeline

  • Collect and synthesize no fewer than 100 sets of real business samples covering typical positive cases, boundary edge cases, permission-attack counterexamples, and concurrency conflicts;
  • Build a Golden Dataset eval suite that automatically runs full regression whenever prompts are tuned, model versions are updated, or Harness logic is adjusted;
  • Code is only allowed into the canary release environment after the automated regression pass rate reaches the preset baseline (e.g., above 98%).

Multi-model primary-backup failover gateway with timeout retries

  • Integrate an enterprise-grade multi-model intelligent gateway with health probes and concurrency quota management;
  • Dynamic primary-backup degradation: When the primary model cluster hits HTTP 429 rate limits or response timeouts (e.g., over 8 seconds), the gateway seamlessly switches within milliseconds to a local private backup model or a rule-based fallback template;
  • All external system calls strictly follow idempotent design, combined with exponential backoff and jittered retry mechanisms.

Phase 3: Launch Monitoring and Operational Handoff (Launch & Operational Handoff)

Launch success is not defined by "running through on day one," but solely by "whether the customer's technical team can operate autonomously long-term without the FDE."

Full-lifecycle decision flow and gate checkpoints of the delivery trilogy

Three rigorous quality checkpoints are set across the entire process; launch is never forced if gates are not met:

Offline classified server rooms and Virtual Private Cloud (VPC) isolated deployment

In government-enterprise, financial, and classified industrial scenarios, FDEs must handle stringent network isolation environments: Virtual Private Clouds (VPC, a logically isolated dedicated network built by enterprises in the cloud) and offline classified server rooms have become the compliance foundation of enterprise data security:

  • Provide fully offline runtime image packages, model weights, and dependency wheels with no external network dependency;
  • For physical one-way network gates and enterprise VPC isolated dedicated lines, configure one-way data penetration proxies and signed receipt confirmation services;
  • Verify that all system functions operate normally throughout the full lifecycle with the external network cable physically disconnected.

White-box security compliance walkthrough and penetration audit

  • Prompt injection defense: Deploy an input compliance filtering gateway at the system boundary to strictly prevent privilege escalation attacks such as "ignore previous instructions";
  • RBAC enforcement and data isolation walkthrough: Strictly verify the permission scope of approvers and operators, forbidding any role from issuing unauthorized high-risk write operations beyond its level;
  • Security white-box walkthrough checklist: Produce a complete penetration test report, a port exposure inventory, and a key storage audit trail.

End-to-end observability (Tracing) and anomaly alerting

  • Introduce distributed tracing to fully record, for each user request, the input business payload, model inference latency, intermediate reasoning steps, external tool call parameters, and final return values;
  • Set key performance metrics and anomaly thresholds; for events such as timeouts, model degradation activation, and manual intervention dispatch, notify frontline operations staff in real time via WeCom/DingTalk Webhooks.

Delivering the operations manual and engineering asset handoff

  • Write a standard operations Runbook for the customer's ops team, including SOPs for common troubleshooting, offline backup and cold recovery steps, and a model degradation switch guide;
  • Hand over a local drill toolkit requiring no external network or paid API keys, ensuring the customer's internal developers can reproduce all test scenarios and extend them further;
  • Hold a formal engineering asset acceptance review meeting, with both parties signing off that quantified business metrics have been met, completing the autonomous operations handoff.
