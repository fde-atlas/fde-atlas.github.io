Forward deployed engineer H-1B salaries: what filings show

FDE Interview Prep · 2026-09-25 · 27 min read

Summary

Using DOL LCA disclosure data for H-1B filings certified October 2024 to June 2026, the author analyzes 387 filings with "forward deployed" in the title: median offered base wage $161,637 (lower bound of ranges), 25th–75th percentiles $145,000–$190,000, from 119 employers. The post stresses what LCA data omits—bonus, equity, actual hires—and warns that range lower bounds understate offers, citing Anthropic filings sharing a $300,000 ceiling. Palantir's Deployment Strategist filings (106, median $138,500) require a separate search, and "deployment engineer" titles mostly describe unrelated roles.

Key points

  • 387 forward-deployed H-1B filings show a median base wage of $161,637.
  • LCA filings exclude bonus and equity and don't prove anyone was hired.
  • 157 of 607 filings used wage ranges; lower bounds understate offers.
  • Anthropic's two filings share a $300,000 ceiling; its posting lists $280,000–$320,000.
  • Palantir's 106 Deployment Strategist filings (median $138,500) are missed by title searches.

You want a real number for what forward deployed engineers are offered, and an H-1B filing carries a wage that the employer itself files with the US government. Maybe you’ll need sponsorship yourself; maybe you just want a figure nobody made up. This post is the H-1B part of our forward deployed engineer salary guide: what the filings record, what they leave out, and how to use them without being misled.

The short answer: in H-1B filings certified from October 2024 to June 2026, 387 had “forward deployed” or “forward-deployed” in the job title, and their median offered base wage was $161,637, taking the lower bound of any filed range. It leaves out bonus and equity, it covers only sponsored workers, and a certified filing is not proof that anyone was hired.

What an H-1B filing records, and what it doesn’t

Before a US employer can sponsor an H-1B worker, it files a labor condition application, or , with the Department of Labor. The Department of Labor’s record layout says the public file holds the LCA data as the employer or its representative provided it, plus the department’s decision, and defines the wage fields as the wage for the sponsored worker at the first worksite. Source 6Public Disclosure File: LCA (H-1B, H-1B1 and E-3), Form ETA-9035, FY2026 record layoutPublisherUS Department of Labor, Office of Foreign Labor CertificationSource typeUS Department of Labor filing dataHow we used this data

What one filing tells you:

  • the employer’s legal name and the job title it chose
  • the occupation code it filed under
  • the worksite city and county
  • the offered wage, as one figure or a from-to range, with its unit
  • the prevailing wage for the job and, when the employer used the Department of Labor’s OES wage survey data, a wage level from I to IV

What it does not tell you:

  • bonus, equity, sign-on or commission, because the form has no field for them
  • the level on the company’s own ladder
  • whether the worker was hired, or whether one worker had several filings
  • anything about people the employer did not sponsor

What a lower bound hides

Say “offered base wage”, not salary, and count filings, not people. In H-1B filings certified from October 2024 to June 2026, employers stated a range rather than one offered base wage on 157 of the 607 filings in the wider pool of forward deployed and deployment titles. That question moves the talk to level and equity, the two things no filing shows.

Why a filing is not total pay

An FDE offer can have more lines than base, as the Palantir posting shows. A filing captures only the first of them. Two things it can’t carry:

  • Equity. A filing has no field for grants, vesting or refreshers. Our post on FDE equity: RSUs, options and what to get in writing covers how to value the grant.
  • Time. A filing records an offer when it was filed, not raises or bands since.

Filings, posted bands and self-reports side by side

A filing is one past offer to a sponsored worker. A posted band is the range stated for a role on a date. Levels.fyi is self-reported pay from whoever submitted. Posted bands are the easiest to find. In our September 2026 count of postings on the job boards we checked, 364 of the 415 US-located FDE postings published a pay figure. If you are the one being sponsored

Three Department of Labor rules work in your favor:

  • The wage floor. The employer must pay you at least the greater of what it pays similar workers and the prevailing wage for the job and area, under the wage rule for H-1B employers. So check 
    PREVAILING_WAGE
    and 
    PW_WAGE_LEVEL
    on your LCA as well as the offered wage.
  • Your copy. The employer must give you a copy of the certified LCA no later than the day you start work, under the notice rule for H-1B employers.
  • The public file. The employer must keep the LCA in a public access file with the documentation of the wage.

Check that the wage, title and worksite on your LCA match your offer letter.

What to ask for in writing

A filing gives you one line of an offer. When the verbal offer comes, ask for the rest in writing:

“Thank you, I’m excited about this. Before I respond, could you send the full offer in writing: base, level, the equity grant with its vesting schedule, any bonus target and what it’s measured on, and the sign-on terms? I want to compare the whole package, not just the base.”

If the recruiter asks for your number before there is an offer, don’t lead with a filing figure. Say something like: “I’ve read the posted range and the public filings for this title. I’d like to understand the level you have in mind first. Can you share the band for that level?” Our free question What are your compensation expectations? has a full model answer, and What level do you think you are, and why? prepares you for the level conversation that follows. Do H-1B salary figures include stock and bonus?

No. An H-1B filing states the base wage an employer offers a sponsored worker, as a single wage or a range, for the first worksite. Bonus, equity and sign-on are not part of it, so read a filing figure as base pay only.

Why is the H-1B figure lower than Levels.fyi for the same company?

They measure different things.
