Summary

Written by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, this article uses Japanese government and Freelance Association data to analyze freelance engineers' hourly rates. Freelance engineers average 4,000–5,000 yen per hour, roughly 1.3–1.5 times the 2,800–3,100 yen implied rate for salaried employees, though freelancers bear their own insurance and expenses. Rates vary widely: under 3 years' experience brings 2,000–3,000 yen, while 6+ years reaches 4,000–6,000+ yen. Scarce languages like Go, Scala, and Python command higher rates, as do roles like data scientists, ML engineers, PMs, and IT consultants (6,000+ yen). To break the 5,000-yen ceiling, the author suggests rare skills, management/CTO experience, client trust, data-backed negotiation, and agents with short supply chains. It closes by promoting the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) model, where pay reflects business outcomes rather than hours, removing the structural ceiling of hourly billing.

Key points

  • Freelance engineers average 4,000–5,000 yen/hour, about 1.3–1.5x salaried peers.
  • Experience thresholds at 3 and 6 years mark major rate jumps, up to 6,000+ yen.
  • Scarce languages (Go, Scala, Python) and senior roles (PM, consultant, ML) pay the most.
  • Breaking the 5,000-yen wall requires niche skills, management experience, and negotiation.
  • The article promotes FDE work, paid on business outcomes instead of hours.

To understand the hourly rates of freelance engineers, comparison with salaried employees is essential.

Here, we clarify the hourly rate gap between freelancers and salaried employees based on public data. In conclusion, the average hourly rate of freelance engineers is about 1.3 to 1.5 times that of salaried employees.

For engineers with 5+ years of practical experience only

High-rate, direct-contract project information

The average hourly rate for freelance engineers is 4,000 to 5,000 yen.

According to the Freelancers Association's "Freelance White Paper 2023," the most common answer among engineers and technical development freelancers for "the hourly rate you keep in mind" was "4,000 to under 5,000 yen."

Converting this hourly rate to monthly income, working 160 hours per month yields 650,000 to 800,000 yen, or 8 to 10 million yen annually.

However, this is only an average. Depending on years of experience and the technologies involved, rates range widely from around 2,000 yen to over 6,000 yen.

The hourly rate of salaried engineers, converted from annual income, is approximately 2,800 to 3,100 yen.

According to the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "2024 Basic Survey on Wage Structure," the average annual income of system consultants and designers was about 8.2 million yen, and software developers about 6.55 million yen.

Assuming a typical system engineer's annual income of 5.5 to 6 million yen and 160 working hours per month, the hourly rate comes to about 2,800 to 3,100 yen.

Compared to the freelancer's hourly rate of 4,000 to 5,000 yen, this is a difference of about 1.3 to 1.5 times.

There are two main reasons why freelancers' hourly rates are higher than salaried employees'.

  • The cost structure on the corporate side
  • Skill matching on the freelancer side

First, the corporate cost structure. When companies hire freelancers, they don't need to cover social insurance premiums or welfare costs. Hiring full-time employees incurs many costs beyond salary, but ordering from freelancers carries no such burden, making it easier to add that margin to compensation.

The other reason is skill matching on the freelancer side. Freelancers can choose projects in their areas of expertise or high-rate projects. Unlike salaried employees, the situation of "doing work you're not good at for the same salary" rarely occurs, and by focusing on projects that leverage their strengths, they can raise their hourly rate.

However, there are caveats. Freelancers bear social insurance premiums and expenses themselves, so a general rule of thumb is that "you need 1.5 to 2 times the income you had as a salaried employee."

Also, many freelance projects are quoted at a "monthly rate." Even for an 800,000-yen-per-month project, if you work 200 hours, the hourly rate is 4,000 yen; if 160 hours, it becomes 5,000 yen. If you don't confirm the maximum working hours before signing, you may find that "the hourly rate was actually low."

Pro Tip | From the field at BizDev Tech, operated by our company In one of our recent projects, we closed a deal for a full-stack/TypeScript (end-client) project at a monthly rate of 800,000 yen, equivalent to 5,000 yen per hour (7+ years of development experience). Shallow-deal-flow, direct end-client projects tend to offer higher hourly rates even at the same skill level.

Freelance engineers' hourly rates vary greatly depending on years of experience.

Here, we explain hourly rate ranges in the following three stages:

  • Less than 3 years of experience
  • 3 to 6 years of experience
  • 6 or more years of experience

"3 years" and "6 years" are the turning points for raising your hourly rate.

The hourly rate for freelance engineers with less than 3 years of experience is around 2,000 to 3,000 yen, which translates to a monthly rate of 300,000 to 500,000 yen.

At this stage, engineers are beginning to understand object-oriented programming and basic development workflows. It's difficult to complete everything from design to testing alone, so they often proceed with development while receiving support from senior engineers.

Many freelance projects require "3 or more years of practical experience" as a qualification. With less than 3 years, the projects you can apply for are limited, so it's realistic to first accumulate foundational experience as a full-time employee.

The early period can be challenging, but the skills built during this time directly lead to higher rates later.

With 3 to 6 years of experience, hourly rates reach 3,000 to 4,000 yen, with a monthly rate of roughly 500,000 to 650,000 yen.

Three years of practical experience is "the turning point where the number of projects you can apply for increases dramatically." You reach a level where you can work independently in basic design and team development, and your market value rises sharply.

At this stage, engineers with a specialty approach 4,000 yen per hour. For example, strengths such as deep knowledge of a specific framework or experience building cloud infrastructure give you an advantage in rate negotiations.

Around 5 years of experience, 800,000 yen per month (5,000 yen per hour) is one benchmark. Engineers with in-demand skill sets begin to exceed 5,000 yen per hour around this time.

For senior engineers with 6+ years of experience, hourly rates of 4,000 to over 6,000 yen are the norm. Monthly rates of 800,000 to over 1,500,000 yen are not uncommon.

According to the Freelancers Association's "Freelance White Paper 2023," the majority of engineers and technical developers with 6+ years of experience reported hourly rates of "4,000 yen or more."

Engineers in this tier can often handle upstream processes such as requirements definition, project management, and consulting, and this added value is reflected in their rates.

Rates above 6,000 yen per hour are mainly offered by well-funded large corporations and mega-ventures. Startups also tend to offer high-rate projects right after funding rounds.

Many freelance engineers feel they "hit a ceiling at around 5,000 yen per hour."

Simply accumulating years of experience passively makes it difficult to break through this barrier. You need to deliberately build up specialized skills and management experience.

The following three strategies are effective for breaking through the 5,000-yen-per-hour barrier.

  • Work experience at well-known companies (mega-ventures, major SIers, etc.)
  • CTO/tech lead experience, management experience
  • Acquisition of specialized skills in rare fields (AI/ML, blockchain, etc.)

According to METI's "Survey on IT Human Resource Supply and Demand," Japan is expected to face a shortage of up to approximately 790,000 IT professionals by 2030. Demand for advanced IT talent will continue to rise, so honing your expertise leaves ample room for raising your hourly rate.

Pro Tip | From the BizDev Tech Front Lines For our CTO project (end-client project), we have a track record of closing deals at a monthly rate of 1,000,000 yen, equivalent to 6,250 yen per hour (15+ years of development experience). For engineers with both management experience and technical skills, securing projects paying over 6,000 yen per hour is realistic.

The leading high-rate languages are Go, Scala, and Python.

Go typically commands 800,000–1,000,000 yen per month (5,000+ yen per hour). Its high parallel processing performance has led to rapid adoption in backend development, but since few engineers can work with it, high rates are maintained.

Scala also commands 800,000–1,000,000 yen per month, with high scarcity in specific use cases (large-scale data processing, etc.).

Python commands 700,000–900,000 yen per month. Demand is surging due to the AI and data analysis boom. If you work with Python as a machine learning engineer or data scientist, you can aim for even higher rates.

Pro Tip | From the BizDev Tech Front Lines For our server-side/Python project (end-client project), we have a track record of closing deals at a monthly rate of 900,000 yen, equivalent to 5,625 yen per hour (20+ years of development experience). Experienced Python engineers have high market value.

The leading mid-rate languages are Java, PHP, and Ruby.

Java commands 600,000–700,000 yen per month (approximately 3,750–4,375 yen per hour). There is stable demand in the enterprise sector. It remains a primary language in finance and large-scale mission-critical systems.

PHP also commands 600,000–700,000 yen per month. There is stable demand in web backend development, with abundant WordPress and Laravel projects.

Ruby commands 700,000–800,000 yen per month. Ruby on Rails projects remain firmly in demand, especially among web startups.

While demand for these languages is stable, the talent pool is also large, so rates have settled. They are suitable options for engineers seeking a large number of projects and stable work.

Pro Tip | From the BizDev Tech Front Lines For our server-side/Ruby project (end-client project), we have a track record of closing deals at a monthly rate of 800,000 yen, equivalent to 5,000 yen per hour (15+ years of development experience), and for our server-side/Java project (prime contract), a monthly rate of 750,000 yen, equivalent to 4,688 yen per hour (7+ years of development experience).

If you want to aim for high rates, you should choose not "popular languages" but "languages where talent is scarce relative to demand."

For example, JavaScript and Java are popular among engineers, but since many engineers can work with them, rates tend to be hard to raise. On the other hand, Go and Kotlin have talent shortages relative to demand, so even at the same skill level, rates are higher.

Language choice is part of your career strategy. While keeping your current strong language as a foundation, it is wise to learn languages used in high-rate fields (AI, cloud, mobile, etc.).

Hourly rates for freelance engineers also vary significantly depending on the role (job type) you take on.

According to the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Basic Survey on Wage Structure," even among company employees, there is a gap of approximately 1,650,000 yen in average annual income between system consultants/designers and software developers. The tendency for roles involved in upstream processes to command higher rates applies to freelancers as well.

Data scientists and machine learning engineers are the highest-paying roles.

Their average hourly rate exceeds 6,000 yen, and monthly incomes of over 1 million yen are not uncommon. While demand has surged due to the AI boom, professionals capable of handling these roles remain extremely scarce, which keeps rates high.

METI's "Survey on IT Personnel Supply and Demand" also predicts that the shortage of AI talent will become even more severe toward 2030.

In addition to specialized knowledge in statistics and machine learning, professionals who can understand business challenges and propose solutions are especially valued.

For backend and frontend engineers, hourly rates of 3,000–5,000 yen are the market standard, which translates to monthly incomes of around 500,000–800,000 yen.

Since the frontend talent pool is relatively large, rates tend not to reach exceptionally high levels. Even so, the average hourly rate exceeds 3,000 yen, which is higher than the average for salaried employees.

For backend engineers, those with advanced skills such as cloud-native development and high-traffic processing often command hourly rates above 5,000 yen. Experience designing and building on AWS or GCP gives you an advantage in rate negotiations.

This is a role where rates vary greatly depending on the depth of your skills.

PMs (project managers) and IT consultants are high-rate roles at 5,000–9,000 yen per hour. Monthly income ranges from 800,000 to 1.5 million yen, and top performers can exceed 2 million yen.

IT consultants take on management-oriented responsibilities such as formulating corporate IT strategies and proposing business process reforms. Because they require industry knowledge and communication skills in addition to technical ability, barriers to entry are high and rates remain at a high level.

PMs earn around 700,000–1.5 million yen per month. Management skills that lead development teams to project success are highly valued.

Pro Tip | From the BizDev Tech Frontlines At our company, we have a track record of closing PM engagements (end-client projects) at a monthly rate of 900,000 yen, equivalent to an hourly rate of 5,625 yen (15+ years of experience). PMs with technical backgrounds have high market value.

Once you understand the market rates, the next step is to think about "how to raise your hourly rate."

Here, we introduce four methods you can put into practice right away.

If you want to aim for high rates, you should master technologies that are in high demand and short supply.

According to METI's "Survey on IT Personnel Supply and Demand," shortages of AI and cloud talent are predicted to worsen going forward. Building skills in these fields will give you an advantage in rate negotiations.

Obtaining related certifications (AWS certifications, Google Cloud certifications, etc.) is also effective. Certifications serve as objective proof of your skills and persuasive material for clients.

A realistic strategy is to build on your area of expertise while expanding your skills into adjacent high-rate fields. The accumulation of "technical skill improvement × track record × appropriate self-promotion" leads to higher income.

The shortcut to raising your rate is building trust with clients.

Prompt and attentive communication, meeting deadlines, and delivering more than expected. Thoroughly executing these basics leads to ongoing engagements and proposals for rate increases.

Striving to deliver "work above your rate" to raise client satisfaction creates room for your next negotiation.

A negotiation like "If you value the results this time, could you reconsider my rate going forward?" only works when there is a relationship of trust.

As a freelancer, your rate will not rise unless you negotiate. It is important to have the mindset that "if you don't ask, it won't go up."

The key to negotiation is presenting evidence, not emotions. Be familiar with public data and market rates like those introduced in this article, so you can say things like "Given my skill set and years of experience, the market rate is XX yen."

Timing is also important. Project milestones, contract renewals, and immediately after delivering results are good times to negotiate.

By proceeding carefully with solid evidence, you can build a win-win relationship.

Using freelance agents is an effective way to efficiently find high-rate projects.

The Freelancers Association's "Freelance White Paper" also shows an increasing trend in the proportion of work obtained through agents. In particular, high-rate, favorable projects are often only available through agents.

When choosing an agent, the key point is the proportion of "direct end-client projects" (projects with fewer intermediary companies between you and the client). The shallower the transaction chain, the lower the margin, and the larger the engineer's share.

Compare multiple agents and choose one that matches your skill level and desired conditions. For high-skilled professionals with 5+ years of experience, choosing an agent specializing in high-rate projects makes securing engagements above 5,000 yen per hour a realistic goal.

As an option for engineers who want to move beyond hourly-rate-based freelance development and achieve a working style where compensation is determined by "contribution to results," FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is attracting attention. Since FDEs are compensated based on customer business outcomes rather than working hours, it is a role that allows you to exceed the structural ceiling of hourly rates that comes from selling time.
