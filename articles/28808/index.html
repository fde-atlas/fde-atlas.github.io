NCS iFDE Triples Its Hiring—What Should FDE Interviews Ask?

Original title: NCS iFDE 扩招三倍，FDE 面试该问什么

NCS plans to add more than 200 AI deployment engineers within two years, and not all of them will come from the job market.

At the end of October, NCS launched its AI Mission Centre while committing to grow its iFDE team from 100 to more than 300 people. iFDE is NCS's name for its Forward Deployed Engineers (FDE). The figure comes from NCS's own press release; it is merely a company-set target and has not yet been delivered.

The new engineers will come from two sources: existing employees receiving reskilling, and external hiring. Liew, a spokesperson quoted in NCS's press release, called the expansion an investment in the company's "most valuable asset."

NCS Chief AI Officer Edward Chen spoke at the launch event, but none of the coverage mentions who will be responsible for these new engineers.

For those looking to move into an FDE role, the program shows that internal reskilling is one path into the field, though here only existing NCS employees can take that route.

The program is also tied to a larger goal. Liew said: "We want Singapore to be the place the world comes to see AI deployed at scale." If that goal is achieved, how NCS organizes its iFDE team will be worth watching for engineers in other countries too.

Tripling the team: the hard part is mentorship

Broken down by month: 200 new hires over 24 months, an average of more than 8 per month, meaning at least two newcomers for every existing iFDE.

This math is not part of NCS's plan, but it points to the risk that matters most to job seekers. By NCS's own definition, iFDEs work directly inside client teams, so newcomers will likely have to learn on the job at client sites. If new hires are deployed to the front line too quickly, their titles will grow faster than their skills.

So when you encounter a team expanding like this, don't just ask how many people they're hiring. Also ask about the ratio of internally transferred employees to external hires, who will work with you on your first project, and how long it will be before you can independently own part of the client work.

Where does "vendor-neutral" get tested?

NCS describes the iFDE as an AI expert with industry knowledge who is vendor-neutral and works directly inside client teams. This is NCS's own framing and may not fully match the industry's general understanding of the role.

The commitment is worth reading closely, because NCS also has its own platform. Sunshine.core is positioned as a "sovereign-ready" platform for building, running, and governing enterprise-grade agents; put plainly, it targets customers who need autonomous control over their data and systems.

The two are not necessarily in conflict: a company can have its own platform while still choosing tools based on each client's situation. The test lies in specific cases. When a client's problem is better suited to another tool, can the iFDE say so directly? Ask the employer for a real example that actually happened.

Industry knowledge means understanding the cost of every type of error

The "industry knowledge" in NCS's definition only becomes clear in concrete scenarios. Suppose you are responsible for a system that detects foreign object debris on airport runways; this is a hypothetical example, not an NCS project.

A false positive may send the operations team out to inspect the runway, while a false negative is a safety hazard. So alert thresholds cannot be set based on model metrics alone; you need to sit down with operations staff and understand how costly each type of error is.

This is exactly what your resume should reflect. Pick a domain you already know well, such as healthcare data, operations systems, or manufacturing.

Instead of listing frameworks, clearly describe the constraints real users imposed, the technical decisions you made as a result, and how you validated the outcomes.

Headcount targets are not production systems. The number that will truly matter in two years is how many of these engineers have delivered systems into the hands of clients' real users.
