Handling a sustained heavy workload · FDE interview question

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 2 min read

A practice prompt we wrote. No company or candidate report names it, so it carries no company tag.

How to answer

The interviewer wants to know whether you will still be effective months into a hard stretch, and whether you will say so before you aren’t. LeadDev reported, in July 2026, that Piotr Kraus, an ex-Palantir , said constant travel makes the role susceptible to burnout a lot earlier than others. Source 1The rise of the forward-deployed engineer (FDE)PublisherLeadDevSource typenews report So the strong answer does not pretend you never get tired.

Build the answer around one real stretch:

  • The load, made concrete. How long it lasted, the customers or workstreams, the travel, and what made it heavy. “Busy” tells the interviewer nothing.
  • The habits that held. Habits someone else could copy, aimed at the switching, not just the hours: a one-page state note per customer that you reread before every call, customers batched by day instead of by whoever pinged last, and travel days kept free of meetings.
  • The boundary, and the time you defended it. What you stopped doing or said no to, and who you told.
  • What didn’t work. The habit you dropped, the week it slipped, what that cost. This part makes the rest believable.
  • What you watch for now. Your early warning sign, and when you raise it with your lead. Then say what you’d need to hold that pace for a year, framed as a question you’d ask them, not a condition.

Say the uncomfortable part plainly: “By the second month I was answering messages at midnight and my code reviews got sloppy; my lead noticed before I did.” Then say what you changed.

Two traps. “I thrive under pressure” answers nothing and invites the follow-up you least want. The opposite is a list of limits with no sign you have carried load. Pair every boundary with what you delivered while holding it.

Follow-ups

What the interviewer may ask next, once your first answer is on the table.

  • What was the first sign you were running out of room, and who noticed it first, you or someone else?
  • What did you say no to during that stretch, and what did it cost?
  • If this role asked for that pace for a year, what would have to be different?

Where answers go wrong

  • Says “I thrive under pressure” or “I just push through”, with no habit, boundary or cost, so the interviewer learns nothing about how the candidate will be doing months in.
  • Lists only limits and conditions, with no evidence of load carried and work delivered, so it reads as someone who will struggle in the first hard quarter.
  • Offers hours worked as the proof (“I worked every weekend for months”), which tells the interviewer they measure themselves in hours, not outcomes.

Answer this in two minutes

Write the answer you would say out loud. The clock starts with your first word.

Illustrative answer about a fictional project

The longest stretch was about five months at a supply-chain software company, when three customer go-lives landed on top of each other: a frozen-food distributor, a pharmacy chain and a furniture retailer. I was the only deployed engineer on two of them and the second engineer on the third.
