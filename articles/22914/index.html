How to Hire Forward Deployed Engineers: 4 Routes Compared

FWD Deployment · 2026-10-28 · 7 min read

Summary

This guide compares four ways to bring in forward deployed engineers: hiring in-house, engaging an FDE firm, using a platform vendor's engineers, or sourcing through a talent marketplace. It uses OpenAI's job postings as the benchmark role definition, covering discovery, technical scoping, system design, building, and production rollout. Pricing guidance borrows FAR Subpart 16.2 definitions: firm-fixed-price contracts suit well-defined deliverables, while time-and-materials fits uncertain discovery work. Acceptance criteria should mirror OpenAI's own measures—production adoption and measurable workflow impact. Regulated industries like healthcare need extra scope items such as HIPAA, auditability, and human-review workflows. The author recommends writing the scope first, then choosing the route, and notes legal questions like IP and employment classification fall outside the sources reviewed.

Key points

  • Four routes exist: in-house hires, FDE firms, platform vendor engineers, talent marketplaces.
  • Scope engagements against OpenAI's five-stage role definition, from discovery to production rollout.
  • Use firm-fixed-price for clear deliverables; time-and-materials for uncertain discovery work.
  • Judge success by production adoption and measurable workflow impact, as OpenAI does.
  • Regulated-industry scopes should add HIPAA, auditability, and human-review deliverables.

You hire forward deployed engineers by choosing one of four routes (your own hires, an FDE firm, a platform vendor's engineers, or a talent marketplace) and then writing a scope that matches what the role does: discovery, technical scoping, system design, building and production rollout. Pick a pricing structure based on how clearly you can define deliverables. A fixed price needs clear acceptance criteria, and time-and-materials fits uncertain discovery work.

What does a forward deployed engineer actually do?

OpenAI describes its Forward Deployed Engineering team as partnering with customers to turn research breakthroughs into production systems. The same posting says FDEs lead complex, end-to-end deployments of frontier models in production with strategic customers.

The posting lists the work as discovery, technical scoping, system design, building and production rollout, done with customer engineering and domain teams. It also expects an FDE to own technical delivery from prototype through stable production and to make trade-offs among scope, speed and quality.

If you are trying to hire AI engineers or hire AI developers, this is the difference to keep in mind. The role is defined by the integration work, not by model skills alone. OpenAI's healthcare posting describes integrating AI applications and agentic systems with enterprise APIs, data platforms, electronic health records, claims systems and operational tools. Hiring your own

An in-house hire gives you one person who stays with your systems. The cost is that you must write the job to cover the whole range OpenAI lists: scoping, system design, full-stack building, embedding with teams and trade-off decisions. Engaging an FDE firm

A firm supplies engineers under a contract rather than an employment relationship. Dplyz, for example, places senior engineers inside a business to connect its data, build products and run AI agents and workflows in production. It offers three ways to work: an embedded team, project delivery or technical leadership. As of October 2026, Dplyz doesn't publish a price list, so ask for a written quote.

Using a platform vendor's engineers

Some platform companies employ FDEs who work with customers on the vendor's own technology. OpenAI's postings are an example of the role as a vendor describes it. Ask whether the engagement covers only the vendor's product or the surrounding systems you actually need connected.

Sourcing through a talent marketplace

A marketplace gives you access to individual engineers. The scoping burden then moves to you, because the OpenAI role includes writing the scope, sequencing the work and owning delivery. Confirm who does those jobs before you sign.

How do you scope the first engagement?

Use OpenAI's published duties as your outline. Its Forward Deployed Software Engineer role requires detailed scopes of work and project plans for both proofs of concept and full production deployments. Ask your provider for both documents, not just a demo plan.

  • Discovery. Name the workflow, the teams involved and the systems the work touches. Write down what "done" means for the workflow itself.
  • Technical scoping. List the enterprise APIs, data platforms and operational tools the build must connect to.
  • System design. Have the engineer present a design before building starts, including trade-offs among scope, speed and quality.
  • Build. Require hands-on work with your technical teams. The software engineer posting says engineers code side-by-side with the customer's team on customer infrastructure.
  • Production rollout and handoff. Define who runs the system after launch. OpenAI's healthcare role explicitly includes productionization and handoff.

Separate the proof of concept from the production deployment in the plan. They are different scopes in the OpenAI posting, and they can carry different pricing.

Which pricing model should the contract use?

The Federal Acquisition Regulation is a public-sector rulebook, but its definitions are a clear vocabulary for private deals. Under FAR Subpart 16.2, a firm-fixed-price contract provides a price that is not adjusted based on the contractor's cost experience. The same subpart states that time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts are not fixed-price contracts.

The FAR Part 16 guidance describes a time-and-materials contract as acquiring services using direct labor hours at specified fixed hourly rates and actual material cost.

Structure How it works Fits when
Firm fixed price Price is not adjusted for the contractor's cost experience Deliverables and acceptance criteria can be specified clearly
Time and materials Direct labor hours at specified fixed hourly rates, plus actual material cost Discovery and integration work is still uncertain

A practical split follows from that. Price a well-defined production build as fixed, and run uncertain discovery as time-and-materials. The sources do not give a private-sector cap, staffing ratio or recommended duration, so set those with your provider and have a lawyer read the contract. What evaluation criteria should you write into the deal?

OpenAI evaluates FDE success through production adoption, measurable workflow impact and evaluation-driven feedback that affects product and model roadmaps. You can borrow the first two directly as acceptance criteria.

  • Production adoption: the system is running in production and your teams use it.
  • Measurable workflow impact: pick the metric before the build starts.
  • Reusable patterns: OpenAI expects FDEs to codify reusable patterns and design abstractions that improve delivery quality across engagements. Ask what documentation or code you receive.
  • Direct code contribution: OpenAI expects FDEs to contribute code directly, so check that the people you pay for do the same.

No public benchmark tells you whether an FDE pays for itself across industries. Your own workflow metric is the only reliable test.

What should a regulated-industry scope add?

OpenAI's healthcare FDE posting shows how much a regulated scope can add. It lists protected-health-information, HIPAA, privacy, security, authorization, governance and auditability requirements.

It also requires customer-specific evaluations, validation evidence, human-review workflows, escalation paths and launch criteria. Put each of those into your scope as a named deliverable with an owner, even if your industry is not healthcare.

The sources reviewed do not cover employment classification, tax treatment, security clearances, intellectual property, data-processing terms or immigration for a US engagement. Settle those with counsel before work begins.

How do you choose between the routes?

Start with your scope, not the route. If you can write the five steps above in detail, any route can be tested against them. If you cannot, pick a route that includes discovery and technical leadership, such as an embedded team or technical leadership engagement from a firm, and price that phase as time-and-materials.

Common questions

Are forward-deployed engineers in demand?

OpenAI publishes several forward deployed roles, including a general FDE, a healthcare FDE and a forward deployed software engineer. The sources reviewed do not include market-wide demand data, so one employer's postings are not a measure of the whole market.

Is a forward deployed engineer worth it?

It depends on your workflow and what you can measure. OpenAI judges FDE success by production adoption and measurable workflow impact, and no public ROI benchmark applies across industries.

Which companies have forward-deployed engineers?

OpenAI lists forward deployed engineering roles in its job postings, and Anthropic also uses the title. This is not a complete list, and our company profiles cover more providers.

What does forward deployed software engineer mean?

In OpenAI's posting, it is an engineer who works hands-on with a customer's technical teams and codes side-by-side on customer infrastructure. The role includes writing scopes of work and project plans for proofs of concept and full production deployments.

How do you scope a forward deployed engineering team engagement?

Follow the role's stages: discovery, technical scoping, system design, building and production rollout with handoff. Write separate plans for the proof of concept and the production deployment, and set acceptance criteria such as production adoption and a measured workflow result.
