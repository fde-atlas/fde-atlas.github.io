Summary

This article from DeFactory analyzes freelance embedded-engineer rates in Japan using two sources: listings on its own platform BizDev Tech and case emails received between June and October 2026. Of 1,810 open listings, only 10 were classified as embedded development, but adding terms like automotive, equipment, and robotics raised the count to 21. The 20 listings showing monthly rates averaged ¥678,000 (median ¥700,000, range ¥600,000–¥1,000,000). Among 33 received emails, the 20 with rates averaged ¥684,000, topping out at ¥800,000. The article also covers work arrangements, required skills, and search strategies.

Key points

  • Only 10 of 1,810 BizDev Tech listings were classified as embedded; keyword expansion raised this to 21.
  • Listed rates averaged ¥678,000/month, median ¥700,000, with one ¥1,000,000 robotics role.
  • Received case emails (33 total) showed a ¥684,000 average; none exceeded ¥800,000.
  • Work split: 11 on-site, 6 hybrid, 4 fully remote; hardware-focused jobs usually require on-site work.
  • C++ appeared in 15 of 21 skill lists; only 3 listings used the word 'embedded' in their titles.

Many embedded engineers working as employees are considering taking on freelance projects. Of the 1,810 open projects on our job site BizDev Tech, only 10 are classified as "embedded development." However, embedded work is often listed under other names such as "control," "automotive," "equipment," or "firmware," and upon reviewing project names and descriptions, 11 similar projects were found, bringing the total to 21. Of these, the 20 listing monthly rates ranged from 600,000 to 1,000,000 yen, with an average of 678,000 yen and a median of 700,000 yen per month. We received 33 embedded systems project emails between June 9 and October 19, 2026, and the average rate of the 20 with listed rates was 684,000 yen per month, nearly identical to BizDev Tech (Chapter 7). Based on these 21 projects and the 33 received emails, this article examines project contents, rates, work arrangements, skills, how to search by project naming, and the decision to go independent.

The 11 on-site listings involve work where actual hardware or verification environments are on-site, such as semiconductor equipment, multifunction printers, network equipment, and in-vehicle gateways. Control software for equipment must be debugged on real hardware, so being on-site is necessary. The multifunction printer verification listing states "no remote work" in its listing. The FA equipment listing states that it includes on-site setup and operation training at the delivery destination, with domestic and international travel.

On the other hand, the 4 fully remote listings were projects involving firmware-cloud integration, IP camera edge gateways, consumer appliance apps, and robot-AI integration. Since the robot project involves real hardware, one cannot say "projects with real hardware are always on-site." However, this is a tendency among the 21 listings and cannot be stated definitively.

Therefore, readers searching with fully remote as a condition should assume that projects involving real hardware are mostly on-site, and broaden their search to include cloud-integration and app-oriented projects.

On your skill sheet, writing four items—languages, the phases you handled, the equipment and OS you worked with, and communication protocols—makes it easier to match against the required skills on project listings.

So readers should list equipment, OS, and communication protocols on their skill sheet, in addition to languages.

The headings of embedded project pages and job pages write the same job type in the following different ways (confirmed among top Google search results on October 19, 2026).

  • Embedded/Control Engineer
  • Embedded Engineer
  • Embedded/Control Systems Engineer
  • Control/Embedded Engineer
  • Firmware Engineer

"Embedded" and "control" are often used as a pair. Control software for equipment, automotive ECUs, and PLC sequence control may be labeled "control" on project listings, but you can apply with embedded experience. If you see headings like "embedded" or "firmware" on other sites, you can treat them as different names for the same job type.

Searching embedded projects in the following order minimizes missed listings.

    • Viewing with job category set to "Embedded Development" | 10 projects
    • Add terms like equipment, automotive, control, robot, and firmware to project names and skill fields (increases to 21 projects)
    • Look at projects whose body text mentions technology names like RTOS, AUTOSAR, ESP-IDF, ROS2, or Linux

If you have a specialty, you can search from the start using that field's terminology (for automotive: ADAS, AUTOSAR, ECU).

Nine projects list the contract type in the "Type" field of the project listing: 7 are quasi-mandate (jun-iin), 1 is dispatch (haken), and 1 is talent-based (freelancers not allowed). 17 projects state the number of interviews: 15 require one, and 2 require one to two.

Before contracting, confirm the following points on the project listing.

  • Contract type (quasi-mandate, dispatch, talent-based). Talent-based contracts cannot be taken by individual freelancers
  • Settlement range (e.g., 140–180 hours, 140–200 hours). All 12 listings that state it have a lower limit of 140 hours per month
  • Where the actual hardware or verification environment is located (whether you need to be on-site)
  • Whether business trips are required (stated in 2 projects: FA equipment and automotive evaluation/verification)

As preparation, before going independent, confirm the conditions and monthly rates of projects you'd like to take across multiple listings.

Project listings don't reveal how tough the work itself is. What you can confirm is that 11 of 21 projects are on-site, the settlement range is 140 hours or more per month, and some projects involve business trips.

There are only 10 projects in the current Embedded Development listings, so you can't say projects are always available. Since other sites also have projects, compare the number of active listings across multiple sites before going independent.

Our company, Defactory, receives project information emails from IT staffing agencies and other sources. From a source separate from the projects listed on BizDev Tech, the figures in this chapter are based on counting those received emails.

To summarize the results: among the emails received from June 9 to October 19, 2026, embedded systems projects totaled 33 after consolidating duplicates. The average of the 20 with listed rates was 684,000 yen per month (median 663,000 yen), with a minimum of 550,000 yen and a maximum of 800,000 yen.

The scope of the tally is as follows.

  • Target: Of the roughly 33,000 project emails received from June 9 to October 19, 2026, the 208 emails whose subject line or project name contained the terms "embedded (組込み/組み込み)", "firmware", "microcontroller", "RTOS", "automotive ECU", "AUTOSAR", "FPGA", or "PLC"
  • Excluded: Emails introducing the engineer themselves, emails bundling multiple projects into one, and non-embedded projects
  • Consolidation method: Resends of the same project and the same project received from different companies were counted as one (208 emails became 33 projects)
  • Monthly rate: The amount in the rate field of the email body; where a range was given, the midpoint of the range was used. Since BizDev Tech uses the lower end of ranges, figures may differ for the same project. The 13 projects whose rates could not be read were excluded from the average
  • Check adding "control" and "robot": Recounting with "control (制御)" added to the target terms yields 41 projects (25 with rates, average 709,000 yen). Of the 8 additional projects, many, apart from camera control middleware, cannot be called embedded, so they are not included in this chapter's figures.

These are projects as of the time of receipt and are not necessarily still open.
