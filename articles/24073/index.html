FDE Case Interview Prep | How to Solve 4 Sample Questions with Scoring Rubrics and a Practice Plan

Original title: FDEのケース面接対策｜例題4問の解き方と採点ルーブリック・練習計画

FDE総研 · 2026-11-13 · 12 min read

Summary

FDE Soken proposes preparing for forward deployed engineer (FDE) case interviews using a 5-axis, 20-point rubric derived from Palantir's job description. The article argues these interviews test reasoning rather than a single correct answer, scoring problem decomposition, customer understanding, data design, implementation judgment, and communication. It walks through four practice cases (crowd visualization, demand forecasting, data integration, government AI adoption), identifies common weaknesses by candidate background (e.g., SWEs struggle with customer understanding, IT consultants with implementation judgment), and lays out a 4-week plan of 12 problems targeting an average self-score of 15/20.

Key points

  • Rubric scores 5 axes at 4 points each: decomposition, customer understanding, data design, implementation judgment, communication.
  • Case interviews differ from consulting (goes beyond recommendations to what to build) and SWE interviews (requirements must be elicited from the customer).
  • Recommended answer flow: clarifying questions, decomposition, minimal 2-week version, risks, 30-second executive summary.
  • Weak axes vary by background: SWEs fail on customer understanding; IT consultants on implementation judgment.
  • Practice plan: 12 cases over 4 weeks, recorded and self-scored next day, targeting 15/20 average.

  • Week 1: Learn the framework — memorize the 5 steps and solve Examples 1 and 2 without timing yourself (goal: 3 points on problem decomposition)
  • Week 2: Solve within the time limit — work through Examples 3 and 4 plus 2 self-made questions, 45 minutes each, and record yourself (goal: 12 points total)
  • Week 3: Shore up weak axes — self-score, focus on your lowest-scoring axis, and redo 3 questions (goal: 3 points on the weak axis)
  • Week 4: Mock interviews — ask someone to play the interviewer and solve 3 unseen questions (goal: 15 points average)

The 4 weeks, 12 questions, and 15-point average are guidelines set by FDE Soken, not standard values or passing criteria based on cited sources. Adjust them according to your current role and years of experience.

Based on the job description on Palantir's careers page, FDE Soken recommends practicing the FDE case interview on 5 axes with a 20-point maximum.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineering role that embeds with customers on-site and handles everything from problem definition to implementation and operations. It is a role popularized by Palantir; see What is an FDE for details. FDE hiring processes often include, in addition to coding interviews, a case interview that asks "how do you break down an ambiguous business problem and what do you build?" Candidate accounts mention that Palantir's FDE selection process includes an interview called "decomposition" (this is not an official Palantir announcement).

This article compiles the scoring rubric created by FDE Soken (evaluation date: November 13, 2026), how to solve the 4 example questions, countermeasures by precondition, common stumbling blocks by professional background, and a practice plan.

What Is Evaluated in an FDE Case Interview?

Under FDE Soken's 5 axes based on Palantir's job description, the FDE case interview is scored on 5 perspectives including problem decomposition.

A case interview is not a test of finding the right answer. It is a setting where, over 30 to 60 minutes, you demonstrate how you would act as an FDE facing a customer's problem. Since there is no single correct answer, interviewers look less at the answer itself and more at how you think and why you make your judgments.

What Does FDE Soken's 5-Axis Rubric Look At?

FDE Soken's 5-axis rubric scores problem decomposition, customer understanding, data design, implementation judgment, and communication at 4 points each, for a total of 20 points.

Evaluation axis1 point (insufficient)4 points (sufficient)Example behaviors the interviewer looks for
① Problem decompositionTries to solve the problem as statedBreaks it down in the order purpose → metrics → key issues, and prioritizesConfirms "what must be resolved for success" in the first 5 minutes
② Customer understandingLumps all users together as "users"Distinguishes frontline staff, managers, and decision-makers, and can articulate each one's pain pointsAsks who looks at the screen when, and what they decide
③ Data designStops at "collect data and analyze"Concretely identifies data sources, granularity, update frequency, and missing dataShows required tables and keys verbally or with a diagram
④ Implementation judgmentOnly sketches an ideal overall pictureSeparates the minimal configuration deliverable in 2 weeks from what can be deferredDraws the line: "start with manual operations, automate once results appear"
⑤ CommunicationExplains in technical jargonSpeaks in the order conclusion → reasons → risks, adapted to the listener's positionCan deliver a 30-second summary for decision-makers at the end

How Does the FDE Case Interview Differ from SWE and Consulting Interviews?

The FDE case interview differs in that it tests both the "correctness of implementation" of SWE interviews and the "structure of key issues" of consulting interviews within a single case.

Consulting firm case interviews often end with market sizing estimates or strategy recommendations. The FDE case interview requires you to go further, into "so what exactly do you build and how." Conversely, SWE system design interviews usually have requirements defined in advance. For FDEs, the evaluation starts from eliciting requirements from the customer.

From the perspective of practitioners involved in on-site deployments at VACAN, the difference in FDE-like qualities tends to show in how you respond when asked in the second half of the case interview, "would frontline staff really use this system every day?"

How Do You Solve the FDE Case Interview Example Questions?

FDE Soken's 4 example questions are built from 4 common patterns FDEs encounter in the field: "demand forecasting, operations visualization, data integration, and AI introduction."

The following 4 questions are fictional cases created by FDE Soken for practice. They do not reproduce questions from real companies. Each question is meant to be solved using the 5 steps: "clarifying questions → decomposition → minimal configuration → risks → summary."

Examples 1 and 2: How to Solve the Congestion Visualization and Demand Forecasting Cases

For Examples 1 and 2, the core of the solution is to first pin down, as an FDE, "whose decisions, at what time, does this data support?"

Example 1: "A commercial facility operator asks you to reduce congestion in its dining floor. How would you proceed?"

  • Clarifying questions: Who is struggling with congestion — visitors, stores, or the facility operator? Is the success metric wait time, sales, or the number of complaints?
  • Decomposition: Break it into 3 parts: "understanding the actual congestion," "providing information to visitors," and "operator countermeasures (seating capacity, staffing)."
  • Minimal configuration: First check whether existing information sources, such as POS data or entrance cameras, can measure congestion levels. Defer introducing new sensors.
  • Risks: Privacy (handling of video footage), delays in data updates, and differences in each store's willingness to cooperate.
  • Summary: State confidently in 30 seconds: "In 2 weeks, we'll trial congestion display using existing data and measure impact by the number of complaints."

VACAN is a company whose business is visualizing crowd/congestion information. From the perspective of VACAN practitioners, the deciding factor in evaluation is not how the technology is chosen, but an approach that first verifies "whether people who see the display change their behavior."

Example 2: "A food manufacturer's logistics department wants to reduce stockouts and waste at the same time"

In Example 2, what is being tested is whether you can first articulate that stockouts and waste are in tension with each other. Rather than starting with talk of a high-accuracy prediction model, an approach that agrees with the customer on "which side of the demand forecast error to err on to minimize loss" tends to be rated highly.

Examples 3 and 4: How to solve the data integration and AI adoption cases

In Examples 3 and 4, whether you can state clearly "what you would discard in the first two weeks" as an FDE greatly changes the score on rubric item 4, implementation judgment.

Example 3: "A manufacturing client has three factories, each using a separate production management system. They want to see the company-wide operating status."

  • Before connecting all three factories, narrow it down to one factory and one metric (for example, equipment utilization) and show a screen
  • Confirm early on that the definition of "operating" may differ from factory to factory
  • First check with the data owner (the IT department or the plant manager) about how to obtain access rights

Example 4: "The mayor has said they want to introduce generative AI into a municipality's counter services"

In Example 4, what is tested is whether you notice that the request itself is "introducing AI." A response that first confirms counter wait times, the types of inquiries, and who bears responsibility for incorrect answers, and then also puts non-AI improvement measures on the list of options, tends to be rated highly on rubric items 2 (customer understanding) and 5 (communication).

How does FDE case interview prep change based on years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size?

According to FDE Soken's analysis, for FDE case interview prep it is effective to shift the focus by years of experience and the practice method by English ability (for the reality of English interviews, see FDE English ability).

Because the axes you need to strengthen depend on your starting conditions, it is more efficient to concentrate time on your weak axes rather than practicing all five axes evenly.

How does FDE case interview prep change based on years of experience and current role?

Engineers with less than 3 years of experience should focus on practicing ① problem decomposition from the FDE Soken rubric, while engineers with 10 or more years should focus on the decisiveness required for ④ implementation judgment.

Starting conditionAxis to strengthenPractice focusRelated article

3-9 years of experience② Customer understanding / ⑤ CommunicationAssume three stakeholders with different positions and speak to each appropriatelyFDE careers

New graduates and students① / ③Practice decomposition using familiar work (e.g., a part-time job) as materialBecoming an FDE as a new graduate

When English ability and target company size conflict, how should FDE case interview prep be prioritized?

If you are applying for an FDE role at a foreign company, prioritize solidifying the pattern of ① problem decomposition from the FDE Soken rubric in Japanese before strengthening your English.

When conditions conflict with each other, FDE Soken's view is to prioritize in the following order.

  • First solidify the thinking pattern (① problem decomposition). If you practice in English without a pattern in place, you will stumble on both language and thinking, and progress will be slow.
  • Strong English but short experience: Being able to speak English is a strength, but it cannot compensate for shallow content in a case interview. Compensate for limited experience by solving many examples and building up a "repertoire of questions to confirm."
  • Long experience but lacking confidence in English: For foreign companies, memorize only the fixed phrases of the 5 steps in English (e.g., "Let me clarify the goal first.") and use the content you practiced in Japanese as is.
  • Company size: At large companies and foreign firms, a methodical, by-the-book approach tends to be valued, while at startups you are more often expected to go as far as "what I would build this weekend."

Where do people from SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer backgrounds stumble in FDE case interviews?

According to FDE Soken's comparison, former SWEs tend to stumble on ② customer understanding and former IT consultants on ④ implementation judgment; the axis where people stumble differs by prior role (for role differences, see FDE vs SWE).

Who tends to be rated poorly in FDE case interviews?

In FDE Soken's view, people who want to settle on a single correct answer, and people who prefer solo implementation over dialogue with the customer, tend to be rated poorly in FDE case interviews.

Performing poorly in a case interview does not mean you lack ability as an engineer. It only suggests that the FDE way of working may not be a good fit for you. For example, people who:

  • Feel strong stress about taking action in ambiguous situations
  • Value time to focus on design and implementation above all else, more than meetings with clients
  • Are satisfied as long as their work is technically correct, even if it never gets used

If any of these tendencies sound familiar, before starting case interview preparation, we recommend reading People suited to FDE and The hardships of FDE to check your compatibility with the role itself.

How many weeks and how many practice sessions are needed for the FDE case interview?

The FDE Soken practice plan is designed so that over 4 weeks you solve 12 example problems, self-score using the FDE Soken rubric, and aim for an average of 15 points (out of 20). (See the FDE learning roadmap for the overall learning plan.)

The figures of 4 weeks and 12 problems are benchmarks set by FDE Soken for the practice plan; they are not a guarantee of passing. It assumes 3 problems per week, an amount that is easy to keep up even alongside your regular job.

How to structure the 4-week practice plan

The 4-week practice plan is structured so that in week 1 you learn the framework, in weeks 2-3 you solve example problems, and in week 4 you do mock interviews.

What to watch out for when practicing alone

When practicing alone, recording your answers and re-scoring them the next day using the FDE Soken rubric helps you get closer to the interviewer's perspective.

  • Always speak out loud: Thinking only in your head does not let you practice dimension ⑤ of the rubric, communication.
  • Score the next day: Right after solving, you tend to be lenient with yourself. Wait a night, then listen to the recording again.
  • Create material from familiar work: Thinking about problems at your current workplace or stores you frequent as "What would I do if I joined as an FDE?" gives you an endless supply of practice problems.
  • Do at least one mock interview with another person: The experience of being asked follow-up questions by an interviewer is hard to replicate alone. If you use a job-change agent, also see Recruiting agents strong in FDE.

Alongside the case interview, preparing to demonstrate your practical skills through your work history and projects will strengthen the persuasiveness of the interview as a whole.
