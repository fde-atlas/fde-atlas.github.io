How to Become an FDE? A 12-Month Roadmap from Zero Experience, Explained by Career Background

Summary

This Japanese guide lays out a 12-month, four-phase roadmap for engineers with no FDE experience to become job-ready: build one production-quality LLM app (including evals and documented architecture decisions), create customer-facing experience within your current job, reframe past achievements as business outcomes, then prepare for technical, case, and practical interviews. It diagnoses readers into four starting profiles and offers shortcuts: web engineers with LLM work experience or presales with PoC experience may need only 3–6 months. Citing Tokyo job postings from Palantir, OpenAI, and Databricks (as of October 2026), it advises those without software development experience to first gain it, and recommends applying early with a portfolio rather than waiting for perfect preparation.

Key points

  • Four phases over 12 months: production LLM app, customer exposure, outcome-based storytelling, interview prep.
  • Tokyo postings require 1–10+ years of development experience depending on company and seniority.
  • Web engineers with LLM experience or presales with PoC experience can shorten the path to 3–6 months.
  • Personal projects count: Anthropic and Exa accept prototypes as LLM experience.
  • Apply in waves of 2–3 companies and use feedback to refine later applications.

"I want to become an FDE, but I don't know where to start" — this is a question we frequently receive in our FDE-specialized career consultations. The three-layer skill set required of an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) — engineering, LLM implementation, and customer-facing work — may look broad, but if you have software development experience, you can build on your current role's experience in a structured way and aim to be ready for selection interviews within 12 months.

In this article, we break down the path from FDE novice to FDE career change into a 4-phase, 12-month roadmap, and concretely show shortcuts by career background and to-do lists for each phase.

Key Takeaways of This Article

  • The path to becoming an FDE can be organized into four phases: ① building an LLM app to production quality, ② gaining customer-facing experience in your current role, ③ reframing your track record in terms of customer outcomes, and ④ mastering the playbook and entering the selection process.
  • The roadmap in this article is designed to take an engineer with no FDE experience to the selection stage in 12 months; the editorial team believes web engineers with professional LLM app experience or presales engineers with PoC-building experience can shorten it to 3–6 months.
  • As of October 2026, FDE job postings in Tokyo from Palantir, OpenAI, and Databricks require 1 to 10+ years of practical experience in development or similar fields after graduation (10+ years for Databricks' Sr. Forward Deployed Engineer), so for those without professional software development experience, gaining experience in a development role first is the fastest route.
  • Some FDE job postings, such as those from Anthropic and Ramp, accept experience in related roles like consulting or presales even without FDE experience; rather than waiting for perfect preparation, applying once your portfolio is ready and getting feedback lets you adjust your preparation direction sooner.

Phase 0 (First 2 Weeks): Diagnose Where You Stand

The roadmap is not the same for everyone. First, diagnose your current position using the two major FDE capability axes — "implementation ability" and "customer-facing ability" — and decide which one to prioritize filling.

Phase 1 (Months 1–3): Build One LLM App to "Production Quality"

This is the top priority common to all types. As of October 2026, Anthropic's London FDE posting lists production LLM experience including prompt design, agent development, and evaluation frameworks as a requirement, and SoftBank's FDE posting lists experience building or deploying applications using LLMs and generative AI. On the other hand, Anthropic's US Staff FDE posting and Exa's London FDE posting state that personal projects and prototypes also count as LLM experience, so this can be demonstrated through personal development. The key is to finish it at "production quality" rather than stopping at a demo.

  • 01 Choose a topic from "a real business problem": pick a challenge with actual users, such as repetitive tasks at your own or an acquaintance's workplace (this proves your ability to define problems like an FDE)
  • 02 Build it end to end: include authentication, API design, prompt management, error handling, and logging. For connecting to external tools and data, try one mechanism that appears in job postings, such as MCP (Model Context Protocol) (Anthropic's FDE role lists delivering things like MCP servers to customers as a duty; few postings name specific frameworks like LangGraph)
  • 03 Implement an evaluation infrastructure (Evals): a test case collection, automated evaluation via LLM-as-a-judge, and regression testing before and after prompt changes. Get to the point where you can explain "how you measure accuracy"
  • 04 Document your architectural decisions: write in the README "why you chose RAG" and "which parts you protected with deterministic code." This becomes the walkthrough material for interviews

Phase 2 (Months 3–6): Create Customer-Facing Experience in Your Current Role

Customer-facing ability cannot be learned from books. Before changing jobs, deliberately create experience "directly interacting with customers/users and translating their requests into problems" within your current role.

  • Implementation-oriented people: volunteer for requirements gathering, user interviews, and shadowing presales support. Treating another internal department as a "customer" for process improvement also works
  • Field-oriented people: within one of your "build what you're told" projects, take the initiative to identify a problem and make a proposal (even if the proposal isn't accepted, the process itself is something you can talk about in interviews)
  • Communication-oriented people: since you're already strong in this phase, double the time you spend on Phase 1 implementation
  • All types: inventory your past achievements in the format "customer problem → your proposal → implementation → business outcome (numbers)." For how to write your resume, see the related article "FDE Career Change Guide"

Phase 3 (Months 6–9): Assemble Your Portfolio and Prepare to Enter the Market

  • 01 Polish the Phase 1 LLM app into a demo where "the value comes across in 5 minutes" (video + README + architecture diagram)
  • 02 Rewrite your resume for FDE roles: present your achievements in terms of customer outcomes, and describe in the most detail your experience delivering end to end
  • 03 Start researching the job market: include not only "FDE" but also job postings for FDE-like roles under different job titles, such as Applied AI Engineer (Anthropic and OpenAI's Tokyo openings), Deployed Engineer (Cognition's Japan opening), and Software Engineer, Agent (Sierra)
  • 04 In a meeting with an FDE-specialized agent, get an objective diagnosis of your current market value and skill gaps (this is the point where course correction is easiest)

Phase 4 (Months 9-12): Applications and Selection Preparation

The selection process varies by company (each company's publicly available selection process is summarized in the related article "FDE Interview Preparation"). Preparation is easier to manage if divided into three parts: technical interviews, case interviews, and practical assignments. The key points on the roadmap are the following three.

  • 01 Internalize the "framework" for case interviews: practice the five steps out loud: articulating the problem → taking stock of the data → proposing a minimal scope → agreeing on success metrics → surfacing risks upfront
  • 02 Practice walkthroughs using your Phase 1 app: talk about "why this problem," "how you evaluated it," and "what you cut"
  • 03 Apply in waves of 2-3 companies at a time: reflect the feedback you get from each round of selection into the next wave. Save your top choices for the second wave or later so you can approach them when you're comfortable with the process

Summary: Don't Wait for Perfection—Polish While You're in the Market

The path to becoming an FDE comes down to four phases: "① Build an LLM app to production quality → ② Create client-facing experience at your current job → ③ Reframe your achievements in terms of customer outcomes → ④ Learn the framework and enter the selection process." FDE is a new role with few experienced practitioners, and some job postings target experience in roles other than FDE. Rather than waiting for perfect preparation, applying at the Phase 3 stage and getting feedback lets you correct your preparation direction sooner. FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q Is it possible to switch to an FDE role in less than 12 months?

Yes. According to our editorial guidelines, web engineers with professional LLM app experience and pre-sales professionals with PoC building experience can do it in 3-6 months. Since FDE is a role with few experienced practitioners, we recommend entering the market as soon as you have the core proof in place (production-quality LLM implementation + achievements you can present in terms of customer outcomes).

Q Can I work through this roadmap while employed?

This roadmap is designed to be pursued while employed. As a guideline, the Phase 1 personal development takes about 5-10 hours per week for 3 months, and Phase 2 is an approach of creating opportunities within your current job. Quitting your job first to prepare carries significant risks both financially and psychologically, so we don't recommend it.

Q Do I need certifications or online courses?

Not required. In OpenAI's interview guide common to all roles, they state that they don't select based on certifications or titles, but want to know about the person's background and what they can contribute. What gets evaluated in FDE selection is also not a certificate of completion but "something that works + the ability to talk about it." As learning materials, Anthropic offers free courses covering APIs and MCP through "Claude Academy," and OpenAI offers courses on Codex and APIs through "OpenAI Academy" (as of October 2026). Even if you use courses, position them not as knowledge input but as a means to complete your Phase 1 app. Cloud certifications can serve as supplementary proof. Trends in FDE certifications are covered in the related article "Skills and Certifications Needed for FDE."

Q I'm still unsure where to start.

The first step is to "pick one real business problem and write a README outlining an app that solves it with an LLM" (takes about 2 hours). Once written, it becomes the design document for Phase 1. If you'd like to design it together starting with a diagnosis of your current position, please use our free career consultation.
