What is an FDE (Definition)

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who embeds themselves in the customer's field, implements software and AI into actual business operations, and runs them until results are achieved.

"Forward Deployed" originates from military terminology. Rather than building a product in the rear (your own office) and handing it over, you deploy to the front line (the customer's field) and build something that works on the spot—a name that describes the working style itself.

How does it differ from an ordinary engineer?

The decisive difference is the direction they face.

  • Product engineer: builds one feature for many customers
  • FDE: builds as many capabilities as needed for a single customer

It also differs from consulting. Consultants leave behind advice and documents, but implementation is the customer's job. An FDE works hands-on in the field, and their job extends all the way to leaving behind a working system.

Why is it getting attention now?

The trigger was AI.

A survey by an MIT research project (NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025") reported that 95% of corporate generative AI pilots have not produced measurable profit-and-loss impact. The cause is not model performance, but the absence of people who can "implement" AI amid messy operations, legacy systems, and field constraints.

As the answer to this, FDEs have rapidly risen in prominence. Palantir achieved results with this role, and OpenAI and Anthropic are also expanding their hiring.

In June 2026, Amazon was reported to have launched a billion-dollar-scale FDE organization, and the press positioned this as "a move following OpenAI and Anthropic." Source: TechCrunch, June 30, 2026

Competition in the AI industry has shifted from "building models" to "deploying them in the field and turning them into results."

If you want to receive business outcomes as deliverables—there is also a "Specialized Business AI-BPO" service where you simply hand over a task and receive the deliverable. Consultations are free.

What an FDE Does

  • Enter the field: understand operations, data, and constraints that never appear in documents, as a stakeholder
  • Implement into operations: run AI agents and the like on the field's real data and real workflows
  • Operate until results are achieved: measure effects and keep fixing until they appear
  • Leave behind a system: make it run even without the person, then move on to the next task

What matters is step 4. Excellent FDEs do not settle permanently in the field. Finding repeating patterns and turning them into systems or products is the finished form of the FDE role.

FDEs in Japan

The supply of FDEs is short even globally, and in the US large companies compete for them with generous compensation. In Japan as well, movement began in 2026.

The engineer job-change service Findy created a new FDE job category (about 50 listings out of roughly 6,000—nearly zero six months earlier), and there has been a succession of developments including fundraising by firms specializing in FDEs and Salesforce's rollout of an FDE partner network in Japan.

We have organized the full picture of who started what, with sources, in Japan's FDE Market Map 2026. Even so, the pool of people who can take on FDE work at the scale of mid-sized companies' operations remains thin.

Can FDEs Be Trained?

If supply is thin, can they be trained? The scarcity of FDEs is often explained as residing in "versatile individuals who can speak to both technology and business." Under that view, FDEs can only be obtained through hiring.

Our view is different. The true source of an FDE's scarcity is not individual versatility but whether a playbook exists. How to enter the field, how to interview about operations, implementation procedures, measuring effects, how to leave things behind as a system—if these are articulated, most of the FDE's work can be reproduced.

We also believe the order is reversed. Rather than teaching engineers business understanding, it is faster to teach AI implementation to people who can speak to the business. The advent of AI agents keeps lowering the barrier to implementation, and the center of difficulty has shifted from "can you build it" to "do you know what to build."

People with experience in sales or field operations learn implementation along the playbook, while experienced engineers move to reviewing design and quality. From our on-the-ground experience, FDE is becoming a role that can be trained in this way.

We ourselves use this playbook in our daily fieldwork. In fact, Gomumari has worked on-site at a major manufacturer on a company-wide scale, handling everything from inventorying operations across about 50 departments, through implementation, to leaving behind dedicated tools that let the field run on its own.
