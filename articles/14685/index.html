Forward Deployed Engineer interview: rounds, take-home and prep

FDE Times · 2025 · 3 min read

Summary

This FDE Times piece maps the forward deployed engineer interview loop: coding appears, but the decisive round is usually a 60-minute decomposition of a vague customer problem with an interviewer who withholds information. It details company-specific formats (OpenAI's week-long take-home with a recorded walkthrough, Palantir's debugging and unfamiliar-API rounds, Databricks' notebook task), what recruiters screen for from roughly 1,000 postings, and pay data: median base around $190,000 across 135 US postings, with $550,000 packages limited to OpenAI and Anthropic via equity.

Key points

  • The core FDE round is code-free customer problem decomposition.
  • OpenAI's take-home grading may weigh the decision walkthrough over code.
  • Palantir grades process via debugging and unfamiliar-API rounds.
  • 47% of postings require direct customer work; CVs need verifiable results.
  • Median US FDE base pay is about $190,000; $550k is a two-company ceiling.

FDE interview loops look like software engineering loops on paper, with a coding round, a system or case round and behavioural questions. What changes is the weight: most loops add a round where you break an open customer problem into steps, and many add a take-home you must defend decision by decision.

The pieces below are grouped in the order a candidate meets them. Each group starts with the piece to read first.

How the loop works

  • The FDE interview round that matters most has no code in it Coding survives at Distyl AI and Databricks, but the weight falls on up to an hour with a mock customer who withholds information on purpose.
  • The FDE interview round that matters most has no LeetCode answer At Databricks the 60-minute decomposition round, built on a vague business goal, is seen as the core of the loop; coding gets one separate round.
  • FDE interview prep: enough DSA to pass, then focus on decomposition Databricks asks you to write a dictionary-processing function in a notebook. OpenAI sets an implementation task split into several parts. Palantir gives you 60 minutes of decomposition on a problem that leaves out information on purpose, and it does not care whether you know Dijkstra by heart.

Take-home and project stories

  • In OpenAI's FDE take-home, the walkthrough may matter more than code You get about a week to ship code, a running app and a recorded video, and the grading seems to turn on tying each choice to the customer.
  • In FDE interviews, the trade-off question is the main question Interviewers are grading judgment, so a strong answer names the constraint, the options, the choice, its cost and how you explained it to the client.

Company loops

  • Companies hiring FDEs now Every company with open FDE roles, its role count and posted pay.
  • Two Palantir FDSE rounds grade your process, not your speed One hands you up to 1,000 unseen lines giving wrong output, the other an unfamiliar API; a small drill set on a laptop turns the method into reflex.

What recruiters screen for

  • Code gets you an FDE interview; customers decide who gets hired Across about 1,000 FDE postings, most demand Python and full-stack skills, while 47% say the hire must work directly with customers.
  • Your FDE CV needs a number with a baseline, not a stack list Recruiters for forward deployed roles look for a deployment result they can check, such as “six weeks down to nine days”, not tool names.
  • An FDE portfolio repo should show where the agent fails An agent right 80% of the time gets three runs in a row right only 51% of the time, so evals built from real failures beat a polished demo.
  • FDE postings rose 800% in 2025. Not all of them are FDE jobs Ignore the title and read what you may build and where your code ends up; five tests expose demo and support roles in disguise.

Prepare

  • Alex Xu's 30 system design chapters skip ML entirely The book still earns its place for FDE candidates, because its four-step answer framework mirrors a first working session with a client.
  • Educative's FDE course targets the interview, not AI beginners Its syllabus puts the first question to a customer ahead of the first design, the step that LeetCode-trained candidates tend to skip.

The offer

  • FDE salary: what job posts actually pay Posted base-pay ranges from US job posts, by seniority, company and city. Updated daily.
  • Palantir hires FDEs with one year of experience, starting at $135,000 At the top, OpenAI's FDE manager role pays $345,000 after eight years, and from mid-level up equity pulls total pay away from base.
  • The $550,000 FDE figure is a two-lab ceiling, not a typical salary Median base pay across 135 US FDE postings is about $190,000; what lifts OpenAI and Anthropic packages higher is equity that vests yearly and is hard to value.
