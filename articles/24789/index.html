What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)? | In One Line

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is an engineer who embeds deeply at the customer's site to implement and integrate their company's software and AI products, and stays alongside the customer until the solution takes hold in operations and delivers results. In short, it is a role where one person combines both "the builder" and "the one who delivers and enables use," taking responsibility for bridging the "last mile" between the product and the customer's operations.

While a typical software engineer focuses on the development of their company's product itself, an FDE commits to "seeing it through to working" at the customer's site. They handle everything end to end—from requirements gathering, data integration, and custom implementation, through PoC to production deployment, to operational improvement and expanded usage—spanning from pre-sales to customer success, which is their defining characteristic. This article covers the overall picture of the profession, while the detailed differences from adjacent roles are explored in Differences Between FDEs and Software Engineers, SIers, and SEs, and the required capabilities in The Complete Guide to Skills Needed for FDEs.

FDE is a profession established by US-based Palantir Technologies, and today leading AI companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Sierra, and Cohere are strengthening hiring for it as a core position. Which companies hire FDEs and how is organized in Guide to Companies Hiring FDEs. Actual job openings can be found on the FDE Jobs Overview Page and Job Listings.

Why It Is Attracting Attention Now

As enterprise adoption of generative AI and LLMs accelerates, a challenge has emerged worldwide: "We can build a PoC (proof of concept), but it cannot deliver value in production." FDEs, who bridge the gap between products and customer operations, saw demand surge in 2025–2026 as the profession that fills this "last mile." You can also see real examples in FDE Category Jobs.

The Meaning of the Name "Forward Deployed"

"Forward Deployed" is originally a military term meaning "positioning troops at the front line." The name embodies the idea of sending engineers not just to headquarters (the in-house development base) but to the "front line"—the customer—to create value on the spot. In other words, FDEs are engineers whose measure of success is not the product's completeness, but "whether it is actually useful at the customer's site."

The Essence of an FDE in One Line

In one line, an FDE is "an engineer who works backward from the customer's problem, shapes their company's product into something that works on-site, and doesn't leave until results are achieved." Neither technical skill alone nor customer-facing skill alone is enough; the premise is having both at a high level. The specifics of these "two wheels" are broken down in the article on required skills.

Why FDEs Are Called a "Rare Profession"

There are many engineers strong in implementation, and many business professionals strong in customer relations. However, people who can both "write code that works in production" and "engage directly with executives and frontline staff to build consensus" are limited. Because FDEs stand at this intersection, the talent pool is small, making the role highly scarce—and as a result, typical salaries tend to be set high.

The Origins of the FDE | Established by Palantir, Spread to AI Companies

The profession that popularized the FDE is US-based Palantir Technologies, which provides data analytics platforms to government agencies and large enterprises. Palantir grew not with a "sell the product and done" model, but by embedding engineers at customer sites to solve problems together and feeding those learnings back into the product. This "engineer who embeds deeply in the field" is the prototype of the FDE.

In recent years, as enterprise adoption of generative AI and LLMs has spread, leading AI companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Sierra, and Cohere have adopted this model and positioned FDEs as core hiring roles. This is because AI products require an extremely heavy process of adapting "generically built models" to "individual companies' business operations," and the people who handle this have become the rate limiter of business growth. Each company's hiring trends are covered in detail in Guide to Hiring Companies.

Palantir's "Embedding in the Field" Model

Palantir's approach is characterized not by delivering a generic product as-is, but by tailoring it to each customer's specific data, business workflows, and decision-making processes. FDEs were responsible for deeply understanding the customer's business and shaping the product into a state where it is actually used on-site. This model of "creating value through the back-and-forth between product and field" has been inherited by today's AI companies.

Spread to OpenAI, Anthropic, and Others

LLMs have the characteristic of being "smart, but not fitting individual companies' business operations out of the box." Site-specific customization is essential—connecting to internal data, designing evaluations, setting guardrails, and integrating into business workflows. That is why OpenAI and Anthropic place great importance on FDEs who create value at the customer's site. Job pages by hiring company also organize points of entry into these companies.

Spread Within Japan

In Japan as well, FDE-like positions are increasing at the domestic offices of foreign AI companies, professional services divisions of foreign SaaS vendors, and solution divisions of domestic AI implementation support firms and SIers. Even without the title "FDE," many organizations recruit for equivalent roles under names such as AI Implementation Engineer, Solution Engineer, or Deployment Strategist.

What FDEs Actually Do | Explained by Phase

An FDE's work consists of two pillars, "technical implementation" and "customer-facing engagement," with the focus shifting depending on the phase of the project. Here we break the work down into three phases—pre-sales, implementation/delivery, and adoption/expansion—and explain what the role involves concretely. If you want a more detailed picture of day-to-day activities, reading this alongside the article on differences from a job-comparison perspective will help you understand the role more fully.

Pre-Sales and Requirements Definition Phase

The FDE leads the technical side of sales meetings, hears out the customer's business challenges, and designs "what can and cannot be solved with our product." The key in this phase is engaging with both decision-makers and on-site staff, structuring the challenges, and assessing feasibility. Building demos and PoCs to make the value "visible" and supporting the decision to adopt is also part of the FDE's role. {{6} Hearing about the customer's business, data, and existing systems

  • Interview on the customer's business operations, data, and existing systems
  • Structuring challenges and distinguishing solvable from unsolvable problems
  • Designing and building demos and PoCs, and agreeing on evaluation metrics (success criteria)
  • Presenting technical risks and assumptions, and proposing a realistic implementation scope

Since this phase overlaps with technical sales (pre-sales), it is an area where people transitioning from solution engineer roles tend to thrive.

Implementation and Delivery Phase

The FDE integrates the customer's data and existing systems with the company's product, building it into a state where it can actually be used in the field. Core tasks include custom implementation in Python, API integrations, building LLM/RAG pipelines, evaluation design, and implementing guardrails. The FDE's job is to raise quality from "it works for now" to "it can withstand real business use."

  • Connecting and preprocessing internal data, and index design (LLM/RAG-related jobs)
  • Integration with APIs and existing systems (Python/API-related jobs)
  • Designing and improving output quality evaluation, and optimizing prompts/retrieval
  • Addressing enterprise requirements such as security, permissions, and auditing

This depth of implementation is the decisive difference separating FDEs from general customer success roles. The required tech stack is laid out concretely in the skills guide.

Adoption and Expansion Phase

The job doesn't end with implementation. The FDE monitors usage, creates a state where the field "actually keeps using" the product, and broadens the scope of use. Extending the success of the first department to other teams and driving contract expansion is all within the FDE's scope. Whether the FDE can deliver results here directly affects the customer's continued and additional investment—and in turn the FDE's own evaluation and salary.

  • Monitoring usage logs and KPIs, and proposing improvements
  • On-site training and maintaining operational documentation
  • Discovering additional use cases and rolling them out (expansion)
  • Acting as a bridge that feeds field feedback back to the product team

Feedback Loop to the Product Team

FDEs also serve as "information conduits" that channel insights gained in the field back into product development. Challenges that recur in the field are a sign that they should be implemented as product features rather than one-off custom work. FDEs who can run this feedback loop well become growth engines for the entire business, not just implementation staff. Career growth is also discussed in how to become an FDE without prior experience.

A Typical Week for an FDE (Illustrative)

Actual activities vary by project phase, but a typical example would be "requirements gathering and demos at the customer site early in the week, implementation and data integration mid-week, and evaluation, improvement, and reporting late in the week"—a mix of coding time and time spent talking with people. This differs significantly from pure development roles, where most of the day is spent on implementation.

Problems FDEs Solve | The "PoC Valley of Death" and the Last Mile

The fastest way to understand the value of FDEs is to look at "what happens without one." In AI adoption, failures are endless: PoCs generate excitement but never reach production, or systems go to production but go unused. FDEs are the ones who fill this "valley."

What Is the PoC Valley of Death?

The "PoC Valley of Death (Pilot Purgatory)" refers to a state where a proof of concept succeeds but the project never advances to production and is left hanging. It worked in the demo environment, but when applied to real data, real business processes, and real users, quality and operations break down, stakeholders burn out, and the project quietly dies—a very common pattern in AI adoption.

Typical Reasons PoCs Never Reach Production

(1) The gap between clean demo data and messy real-world data; (2) partially optimized PoCs that cannot be integrated into existing business workflows; (3) vague output quality criteria that make it impossible to judge "usable or not"; (4) security, permissions, and operational structures left as an afterthought. FDEs eliminate these issues starting from the design stage.

What Is the Last Mile?

The "last mile" is the "final gap" that remains between a generic product (especially LLMs) and the specific business of the customer in front of you. No matter how intelligent the model is, no value is created without connecting it to internal data, embedding it into business workflows, and building mechanisms for evaluation and improvement. Closing this gap in the field is the FDE's core mission.

Why the Need for FDEs Has Grown in the LLM Era

Traditional SaaS could be deployed largely through configuration, so on-site implementation engineers were not always essential. Generative AI, however, "behaves probabilistically," "requires connection to business knowledge," and "is hard to evaluate for quality"—properties that make per-company customization unavoidable. In other words, the spread of LLMs itself is driving demand for FDEs who create value in the field. This rising demand is also reflected in the growth of AI implementation engineer jobs and FDE jobs.

The Business Impact Created by Having FDEs

When FDE works, PoC-to-production conversion rates rise, deployment lead times shrink, churn decreases, and contracts expand. For AI companies, FDE is not a "cost" but an "investment that generates revenue and retention." That is why companies are aggressive in acquiring scarce FDE talent and tend to offer generous compensation (See: FDE Salary Benchmarks).

How FDE Differs from Other Roles | Comparison with SWE, SIer, SE, Consultants, and CEs

Because FDEs "also implement" and "talk with customers," they are often confused with several adjacent roles. In a nutshell, the difference is that for an FDE, the endpoint of responsibility is the customer's outcome, and to achieve it they write production code themselves. FDE vs Software Engineer (SWE)

SWEs are responsible for the features and quality of their own company's product, and their direct contact with customers is limited. FDEs also have implementation skills, but their focus is always on "how value emerges in the customer's actual business in front of them." Moving from SWE to FDE increases the weight of defining "whose code is this, and for what purpose," relative to the amount of code written. A detailed comparison is developed in the article Differences Between FDE and SWE.

FDE vs SIer and Contract Development

SIers build systems based on customer requirements, but the endpoint of responsibility tends to be "delivery per specification." FDEs set "achieving outcomes," not building to spec, as the endpoint, and solve problems using their own company's product as a weapon. People from SIer backgrounds can transition to FDE easily by leveraging their experience in requirements definition and customer negotiations, but shifting to a product mindset is key.

FDE vs Sales Engineer (SE) and Pre-sales

SEs/pre-sales are responsible for winning deals (the technical side of sales), and while they may build PoCs, production implementation is generally handed off to another team. FDEs enter through pre-sales but then carry the work all the way through production implementation and adoption. The decisive difference is not "propose and finish" but "build what you proposed yourself." It is also a natural next step as a career path from Solution Engineer jobs.

FDE vs Consultant

Consultants excel at strategy and recommendations, but as a rule they do not build production systems themselves. FDEs possess consultant-like skills such as problem decomposition and consensus building, but in the end they build something that works with their own hands. An FDE is not someone who "draws the picture" but someone who "draws the picture and turns it into something that works." People from consulting backgrounds can transition by leveraging their ability to structure problems, but acquiring implementation skills is a prerequisite.

FDE vs Customer Engineer (CE)

CEs focus on stable post-deployment operations and technical support, while FDEs are more deeply involved in the "launch" phase from pre-sales through production implementation. The two roles are continuous, and it is common to see career paths extending from Customer Engineer to FDE, and from FDE to Deployment Strategist.

Skills Required for FDE | The Two Pillars: Implementation Skills and Customer-Facing Skills

What is required of an FDE is combining "technical implementation skills" and "customer-facing skills" at a high level. Neither alone is sufficient. Here we organize the key points along two broad axes. For a comprehensive explanation, see the Complete Guide to Skills Required for FDE.

Technical Implementation Skills

The ability to write code that works in production is a prerequisite. The following areas are particularly valued:

  • Full-stack implementation skills in Python and similar languages (Python/API jobs)
  • API integration, existing system integration, and data pipeline construction
  • LLM/RAG implementation, evaluation design, and improvement (LLM/RAG jobs)
  • Handling enterprise requirements such as cloud, security, and permissions
  • Solution architecture design (Related jobs)

Customer-Facing Skills

With the same weight as technology, the ability to move people and organizations is tested.

Mindset and Behavioral Traits

For FDEs, ownership is crucial: stepping into unprecedented problems with the sense that "I will solve this myself." Even in ambiguous situations, they frame the questions themselves, test by getting hands-on, and move things forward by involving stakeholders—this attitude of "taking ownership of problems brought to them" is often valued even more highly than technical skills.

English Proficiency (for Foreign AI Companies)

When aiming for an FDE role at a foreign AI company, English is often needed for communication with headquarters teams and reading documentation. The required level varies by company, but English ability broadens the range of jobs you can apply for and is advantageous in salary negotiations.

There is no official statistic in Japan that can definitively state "how much does an FDE earn" as a single number. The verifiable facts are the disclosed ranges from actual job postings published by US-based Anthropic and Databricks. The Anthropic "Forward Deployed Engineer" job posting discloses an annual base salary of $280,000–$320,000, and the Databricks "Sr. Forward Deployed Engineer" job posting discloses $182,000–$250,208 (both confirmed as of September 22, 2026). When equity compensation (RSUs) is added, total compensation trends even higher.

Don't compare on base salary alone

A distinctive feature of FDE compensation is that RSUs/stock options make up a large share on top of base salary. At growing AI companies, the equity portion can multiply several times over in a few years, so always compare on total compensation. The detailed reasoning is explained in the FDE salary guide.

Why FDEs are highly paid

The reason is clear. FDEs are required to have the rare combination of "technical implementation skills" and "customer-facing ability," and they bear accountability for results directly tied to revenue and contract renewal. Because such hard-to-replace talent removes business bottlenecks, companies will offer high compensation to secure them.

Factors that raise your salary

The keys to maximizing your salary are: (1) a track record of involvement through to production deployment and adoption, (2) rare technical skills such as LLM/RAG and data integration, (3) experience negotiating with and expanding enterprise customers, and (4) English proficiency. In particular, case studies showing that you "didn't stop at PoC but contributed to the customer's business outcomes" greatly strengthen your negotiating power. For how to build a track record, see How to become an FDE with no prior experience.

Companies hiring FDEs | Cutting-edge AI, foreign SaaS, and domestic firms

Companies hiring FDEs fall broadly into three types. Since the required experience and working style differ for each, being aware of the type before applying makes your search easier. Details for each company are organized in the guide to companies hiring FDEs.

Cutting-edge AI companies (Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic, etc.)

These are the originators and driving force of the FDE model. Positions center on implementing cutting-edge LLM products at enterprises and government agencies, with high difficulty and high autonomy. High standards are demanded on both technical and customer-facing fronts, and total compensation including equity tends to be generous. Entry points by hiring company can be found on the job postings page by company.

Foreign enterprise SaaS

FDE-like positions are also increasing in professional services/solutions engineering departments of enterprise-focused SaaS companies. The role involves implementing and integrating the company's product to fit the customer's operations and supporting them through to adoption; these are often posted as professional services jobs or solutions engineer jobs.

Domestic AI implementation support firms, SIers, and startups

In Japan, AI implementation support companies, SIer solution divisions, and AI startups are seeking FDE-type talent. They are often recruited under titles like "AI implementation engineer" or "solutions engineer," and a notable feature is that entry-level opportunities for those without prior experience are relatively easy to find. You can search via AI implementation engineer jobs and startup/tech industry jobs.

Where FDEs thrive, by industry

FDEs are not confined to a specific industry; they deliver more value in domains where data and business knowledge carry heavy weight. Fields such as finance, manufacturing, healthcare, and government/public sector have high barriers of regulation, legacy systems, and domain knowledge, so FDEs who can build solutions on-site are especially valued.

How to become an FDE with no prior experience | Paths by previous role

FDE is not a role where large numbers are trained through new-graduate hiring; career changes from adjacent roles are the mainstream. If you have either "implementation experience" or "customer-facing experience," you have a foundation to build on, and the basic strategy is to shore up whichever side is lacking. A concrete roadmap by previous role is explained in How to become an FDE with no prior experience.

From software engineer

Since you already possess the greatest asset—implementation skills—you can move closer to FDE by supplementing the often-missing "customer engagement and requirements definition." The shortcut is to proactively volunteer for cross-team coordination and pre-sales-type activities and build a track record where you can articulate "who the implementation is for." For comparison, the difference between FDE and SWE is a useful reference.

From SIer or sales engineer

Experience in customer engagement and requirements definition is a major advantage. By strengthening implementation skills—LLM/RAG, Python, and so on—so you can "build it yourself" on-site, you can transition into an FDE role that goes beyond just making proposals. It's a good idea to check the technical standards required in Python/API jobs and LLM/RAG jobs.

From consultant or pre-sales

Since your strengths lie in structuring problems and building consensus, acquiring implementation skills lets you evolve from "someone who draws the blueprint" to "someone who draws it and makes it run." The key to the transition is to build something yourself and deploy it to production, even if small, and turn it into a portfolio.

How to build a track record and portfolio that gets you noticed

The most highly valued track record is one where you "didn't stop at PoC but deployed to production and contributed to the customer's (or your own company's) results." Summarizing your scope of responsibility, challenges, approach, before/after, and outcomes on a single page becomes a powerful weapon both in interviews and in salary negotiations. For job hunting, see FDE jobs overview and the FDE category.

The future of FDEs | Why the role will keep growing

In conclusion, FDE is seen as one of the fastest-growing roles over the next several years. As AI adoption shifts from the "stuck at PoC" phase to "production operation," the importance of FDEs who can deliver full value on-site only increases. AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic are ramping up FDE hiring, and in Japan as well, foreign AI/SaaS firms, consultancies, and SIers are strengthening their recruitment.

Structural reasons demand keeps growing

The more capable LLMs become, the more—not less—important the work of adapting them to real-world operations becomes. Smarter models have broader use cases, and the broader the use cases, the more customization to each company's operations is required. As long as this structure holds, demand for FDEs who create value on-site will continue to grow steadily.

FDE career paths

Career paths from FDE are diverse. You can lead challenging projects as a Senior FDE or FDE Lead, oversee deployment strategy as a Deployment Strategist, or expand into roles such as Product Manager or business unit head. FDEs with deep on-the-ground insight also excel in product and business decision-making.

Will AI Take Over FDE Work?

While AI will assist with some implementation work, the core of the FDE role—eliciting customers' ambiguous challenges, building consensus, and taking responsibility until results are achieved—is fundamentally about moving people and organizations. In fact, as demand grows for making AI "usable in the field," FDEs are a profession whose value increases with the spread of AI.
