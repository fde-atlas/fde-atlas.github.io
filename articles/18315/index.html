■ Background of BizCodeX-FDE's Launch

From "building" to "delivering customer outcomes"

The spread of generative AI is changing the role required of engineers. Deciding what to build, designing and developing with AI, reviewing it, and delivering it into production—. An engineer who handles all of this alone is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer).

The FDE is said to be a role born at Palantir, the US software company specializing in data platforms, and its defining feature is that the goal is not "delivery" but "customer outcomes." FDEs enter customers' business sites, structure undefined problems, validate small-scale with technology, take responsibility through results and adoption, and feed the knowledge gained back into their company's products and standards.

Growing FDE hiring in Japan and abroad

FDE job openings are rapidly increasing both in Japan and overseas. In the US, Indeed's FDE job posting index reached +5,230% compared to January 2025 (April 2026, index; per Business Insider reporting). In Japan as well, Findy, Inc. created a new FDE job category on its job service "Findy" in April 2026, and an FDE job board aggregating companies' career pages showed 74 openings from 47 companies (as of September 21, 2026). In a survey of 243 IT engineers conducted by Levtech, Inc. (May 2026), 68.3% expressed interest in FDE roles and 72.0% answered that "demand will increase."

In fact, AI and technology companies in Japan and abroad, including OpenAI, Salesforce (Tokyo), Google Cloud (generative AI, Japanese/English), CyberAgent, Inc., and CADDi, Inc., have published FDE job openings. These companies' postings call for roles spanning from discovering customer problems to technical scoping, system design and building, production deployment, and feeding on-site knowledge back into products. *The job information referenced is publicly available; there is no partnership or collaboration between our company and these companies.

Generative AI tends to stall "just before production"

On the other hand, generative AI adoption often stalls at the prototype (PoC) stage. Even if a technology is proven to work in a prototype, there is a shortage of people who can go as far as deciding which business processes and decisions to embed it in, and how it will change whose work. Moreover, customers themselves often do not yet know where AI would be effective, making it difficult to start with the traditional approach of gathering requirements and placing orders.

BizCodeX-FDE is an advanced professional school designed only for engineers with practical experience, created to address these challenges. On top of students' current work experience, it builds up "upstream processes, AI-driven development, and production design" to train FDEs who can deliver AI all the way into production operations. Entry is selective, and at the exit, instructors judge each student individually on deliverables, demonstrations, transfer, and reflection, ensuring a standard that holds up in the field.

■ Four Features of BizCodeX-FDE

1. Learn the three in-demand market areas in the order of an FDE's work

The curriculum follows the FDE work cycle (FRAME: understand the field and decide / BUILD: build and verify with AI / DEPLOY: deliver into production / RETURN: measure and feed learning back into the next step).

① Upstream processes and consulting:

KPI trees and business cases, MVPs and validation plans, Japanese sales channels and in-house development support, and more. In the requirements definition exercise, students create deliverables such as business flow diagrams (As-Is → To-Be) from a customer's request, and instructors judge pass/fail.

② AI-driven development:

How LLMs work, context engineering, harness engineering, eval loops (evals), AI agent design with permissions and stop conditions, MCP and tool integration, RAG design and evaluation, prompt injection countermeasures and guardrails, and leveraging AI agents such as Devin. In the code review exercises, you will point out defects in AI-generated code with justification tied to the relevant lines.

③ Production design and security:

ADRs, trade-offs among quality attributes, failure design with retries and idempotency, zero trust / authentication and authorization, containers, IaC, and deployment in closed networks, observability and audit logs, staged releases and rollbacks, and more. In the architecture review exercises, you will find gaps in architecture diagrams against business requirements.

2. An "integrated exercise" where you lead all phases of a fictional client project

As a capstone, you receive a request from a fictional company played by the instructor—"we want to streamline sales with generative AI"—and lead the entire process from observation and problem definition to outcomes and MVP, prototyping and evaluation, production design, and rollout and assetization. Along the way, conditions such as budget change and objections arise, and you produce ten required deliverables including a Mission brief and an Architecture/Security pack. In a final presentation (15 minutes) and an individual presentation (5 minutes), you explain your decisions, the options you discarded, and the remaining risks. *All case materials are fictional companies and projects.

3. Hands-on learning: 20% lectures, 20% exercises, 50% application to a fictional project, 10% reviews

Rather than just reading, half of each unit's time is devoted to applying the content to a fictional client project (the allocation is a guideline for material design). Daily reports and exercises receive direct feedback from the instructor, given concretely in the order of "observed facts → impact → good decisions → decisions to improve → next actions to try," tied to specific lines of deliverables and records of conversations. In the student-exclusive learning system "BizCodeX-FDE LMS," you can use slide materials, the requirements definition, code review, and architecture review exercises, daily reports with instructor comments, and a growth dashboard that recommends the next exercise based on detection and quality rates—all in one place (under development and subject to change; video lectures, weekly and monthly reports, and interview exercises with the client-role AI are planned to be added).

4. An application-based entry and strict completion criteria judged individually

Enrollment is application-based (no documents required) and limited to engineers with at least one year of professional experience. Completion is not granted merely by submitting deliverables. The instructor judges each person individually across four perspectives: deliverables, demonstration, transfer, and reflection. You must pass graduation gates A through D in order, with a standard of 70 or higher out of 100 on the rubric. "Critical defects"—such as designs that handle personal information without authorization or designs that feed AI output into critical business decisions without verification—require resubmission regardless of score. The instructors still work in the field at major companies, handling everything from upstream processes to development, production design, and AI-driven development, and they continuously reflect that practical knowledge and the latest AI trends into the seminars, materials, and exercises.
