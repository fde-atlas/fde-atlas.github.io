Reddit's Internal FDE Role: AI Enablement Responsibilities and Salary

Original title: Reddit 内部 FDE 岗位：AI Enablement 职责与薪资

FDE Times · 2026-11-07 · 5 min read

Summary

Reddit is hiring two internal FDEs (AI Enablement), at Senior and Lead levels, serving sales, marketing, business development, finance, and the AI Platform team rather than external customers. Both job postings require handing off maintainable workflows to function owners and reducing those teams' long-term dependence on the FDE team. Recurring needs go to the AI Platform Hub, and successful solutions must be packaged into reusable agents, templates, and connectors. The expected pace is prototypes in days and production deployment within weeks. Base salary ranges are $180,200–$252,300 for Senior and $194,600–$272,400 for Lead. The article argues this signals that FDE value increasingly lies in what keeps working after they move on.

Key Takeaways

  • Reddit is hiring two FDE (AI Enablement) roles, at Senior and Lead levels; the Senior role lists sales, marketing, business development, finance, and the AI Platform team as its internal customers.
  • Both job postings require handing maintainable workflows over to functional owners and reducing these teams' long-term dependence on the FDE team.
  • Recurring needs go to the AI Platform Hub; results that work well in one department should be packaged into reusable agents, templates, and connectors.

Reddit is hiring two Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), one Senior and one Lead, both tagged "AI Enablement." Their customers are not external. The Senior role lists sales, marketing, business development, and finance, plus the AI Platform team, as its internal customers.

What's notable is how the postings describe "getting the job done." Both the Senior and Lead postings require engineers to hand maintainable workflows over to functional owners and reduce these teams' long-term dependence on the FDE team.

The role was originally defined as sitting next to the customer; now the hiring requirements ask to make the customer need it less and less.

This detail is even more striking in a market context. Citing the Financial Times, PYMNTS reported that monthly job postings for FDE roles grew more than 800% between January and September 2025.

When a job title spreads this fast, how each company defines it determines how you'll be judged. Reddit has written handover capability directly into the job description.

The customer becomes a colleague down the hall

Reddit keeps the "embedded in the front lines" part of FDE work, just with a different target. The Senior role describes working closely with internal functions, embedding with teams when necessary to understand how they work, and building trust with each team's AI champions and domain experts.

So customer-needs research skills remain fully applicable. The difference is that you're interviewing a finance specialist doing month-end reconciliation, not a technical lead at a partner company.

The table below places the external-customer FDE model common in industry onboarding materials side by side with Reddit's job postings; the structure hasn't changed, only who is being served.

DimensionExternal-customer FDEReddit's AI Enablement FDE
CustomerThe customer's organizationSales, marketing, business development, finance, AI Platform
Who takes overAfter value is validated, the customer's own engineers take overOwners of each business function
Where common needs goTo the product team, so the solution can serve other customersThe central AI Platform Hub
Success criteriaThe customer owns the solutionWorkflows are maintainable and long-term dependence on the FDE team is reduced

The second row is the hardest. A guide from services firm And Above notes that the common handover model is for the customer's engineers to take over after the solution has proven its value.

At Reddit, the ones taking over are business function owners, not necessarily engineers, so you mostly can't just toss over a codebase.

A department running independently is what counts as done

The postings also set a tight cadence: prototypes within days, and if feasible, high-priority workflows in production within weeks. A hypothetical example shows how heavy this cadence becomes when combined with the handover requirement.

The finance team spends significant effort each month reconciling invoices against advertising contracts one by one. You work with them for a few days, then build an agent: it reads invoices, compares contract terms, and flags discrepancies.

If it were a demo project, you could wrap up once the agent runs successfully on last month's data. By Reddit's requirements, that's only half done. The real question is: three months later, when you've moved on to support the marketing team, who on the finance team can tell when the agent goes wrong, who knows how to shut it down, and how should it be adjusted when the contract template changes?

The answer to that question determines how you build from day one. You'll choose an architecture that non-engineers can understand, write documentation for finance specialists rather than engineers, and designate someone accountable for the process.

"Maintainable" is something you need to define yourself

The postings use only one word: maintainable. They don't spell out how. But if an AI workflow is to keep running after the developer leaves, a few things are almost indispensable. You should proactively propose them rather than wait to be asked.

Back to the reconciliation agent. A minimal test set could be a few dozen invoice-contract pairs whose correct answers finance already knows, re-run every time you modify a prompt or swap models. Approval thresholds could specify that any discrepancy above a certain amount must be signed off by a human before being booked.

What remains is visibility. The process owner should be able to open a page and see how many items the agent flagged this week and how many were overturned by humans. When someone asks why a particular payment was approved, the finance team must be able to point to a complete record, so design logging as a formal feature.

One solution per team, common needs centralized in one place

This model has an obvious risk: every department ends up clutching its own pile of scripts, with no one consolidating them. Reddit counters this with two requirements. First, FDEs must route recurring needs, bottlenecks, and infrastructure requests to the AI Platform Hub.

Second, results that prove effective in one department must be packaged into something the whole company can reuse: agents, skills, templates, connectors, playbooks, or platform patterns.

Suppose you discover that both finance and sales need to read contract PDFs. The right move is to tell the Hub this is a common need and package the first parser as a connector, rather than writing another parser. It's precisely this feedback loop that distinguishes an FDE from someone who writes scripts on demand.

This is an internal version of a familiar part of FDE work. A guide from You.com treats identifying which custom solutions could help other customers as an important part of FDE work. At Reddit, these lessons accumulate into an internal platform, and the platform's users are also your colleagues.

Senior vs. Lead: same handover requirement, different levels

The sentence about handover appears word for word in both the Senior and Lead postings. In other words, a higher level does not exempt you from the handover responsibility; it's core to the role at both levels.

If you're comparing the two levels: on Greenhouse, the Senior role's base salary is $180,200 to $252,300, and the Lead role's is $194,600 to $272,400. For reference, Levels.fyi records a median total compensation of $220,000 for US FDEs, though total compensation and base salary aren't directly comparable.

Your resume should reflect the handover

The practical takeaway is that you don't need to join an AI lab to accumulate FDE-style experience. If some department at your company is doing repetitive work by hand, that's a complete exercise: sit with them, prototype quickly, get it into production, then hand it over.

On your resume, write the full loop, not just the build. A sentence like "Built a reconciliation agent for the finance team, established a test set and approval thresholds, and handed it over to the team lead to run independently" is more persuasive than ten lines listing frameworks.

When reading a job description, watch for phrases like "transfer," "function owners," "reduce dependency," and "reusable": they signal that the employer values durable operation, not demo effects. In the interview, be ready to tell a story about deliberately making others no longer depend on you.

This story is harder to tell than saving a project by firefighting in the middle of the night, but it answers exactly the question posed in Reddit's job posting. If this practice spreads, a large part of an FDE's value will lie in the things that keep working well after they move on to other teams.
