Summary

This Japanese guide maps the fast-growing FDE job market in Japan. It cites Indeed data showing FDE postings up over 800% between January and September 2025, and about 729% year-over-year as of April 2026. It lists hiring companies including OpenAI, Palantir, Anthropic, Sierra, LayerX, Loglass, and Hitachi (planning 1,000 FDEs), categorizes postings into three job types, and details five things to check in a job listing. It also covers interview criteria, common prior roles, and contract-work considerations.

Key points

  • FDE postings surged: +800% Jan–Sep 2025, ~729% year-over-year by April 2026.
  • Foreign firms (OpenAI, Palantir, Sierra) and Japanese firms (LayerX, Loglass, AI Shift) are hiring in Tokyo.

  • Check five things: coding responsibility, end of accountability, on-site presence, language/clients, and compensation structure.

Job postings for FDEs (Forward Deployed Engineers) have surged over the past two years. According to a report by the UK's Financial Times analyzing data from the job site Indeed, postings grew by more than 800% from January to September 2025. Follow-up data from Indeed shows that the number of postings in April 2026 was up 5,230% compared to January 2025, and up about 729% year-over-year (Business Insider).

In Japan as well, starting in the second half of 2025, foreign AI companies, Japanese AI and SaaS companies, and major SIs have begun recruiting one after another. This article compiles the companies hiring as of October 2026, the three types of FDE jobs, five points to check in job postings, and even freelance/contract positions. The results of counting currently open postings are in "A tally of 97 FDE job postings in Japan".

The job itself is covered in "What is an FDE? The meaning and work of a Forward Deployed Engineer", and salaries in "How much does an FDE earn?".

Three types of FDE jobs

An individual survey analyzing 35 FDE job postings in Japan (26 Japanese companies, 9 foreign companies) (gaijineers, February 2026) divides them into three types based on the job descriptions in the postings.

TypeNature of the workShare (same survey)
Builder typeImplementing solutions yourself at customer sites and taking them to production81% of Japanese companies; all 9 foreign companies

Internal Tools Builder typeBuilding internal toolsA few postings among Japanese companies

Even among jobs titled "FDE", the daily work differs between the Builder type and the Sales Engineer-plus type. Deciding which one you want before applying will change how you read job postings.

Five points to check in a job posting

If you read a job posting through the eyes of an employer hiring an FDE, here are the five things to look at.

1. Will you write code yourself?

Even if a posting mentions "implementation" or "building", the job differs depending on whether you actually write the code yourself or a separate team does. Check whether the job description includes phrases like "from design to implementation" or "deployment to production environments", and whether the technical requirements list specific languages or frameworks.

2. Where does responsibility end?

Does the job end when the PoC (validation) is complete, at delivery, or only when production operations and business metrics actually move? You can tell by the presence or absence of words like "adoption", "operational improvement", and "KPI". The further out the endpoint, the more the job resembles a true FDE role.

3. Distance from the customer site

Is it full-time on-site, a few days a week, or mostly remote? OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting requires three days in the office per week. Since going into customer sites is the essence of FDE work, how much time you spend there shapes your working style.

4. Language and customers

Some foreign companies' Tokyo offices require both Japanese and English. The language used and business customs change depending on whether the customer is a domestic company or the Japanese subsidiary of a global company.

5. Form of Compensation

Whether fixed overtime pay is included, whether the figure is based on assumptions like a "theoretical annual salary," and whether there is stock compensation. I wrote about this in detail in "How Much Does an FDE Earn?"

Experience Evaluated in the Selection Process

What is evaluated in the FDE selection process is not FDE experience itself. We believe the following three things are what matter.

  • Experience moving business numbers. Whether you have changed some number through your own work, such as man-hours, sales, or retention rates. Can you express it in terms of amounts or percentages?
  • Experience structuring ambiguous problems on your own. Whether you have taken a question like "why are customers leaving," investigated it, broken it down, and decided on countermeasures.
  • Experience interacting directly with people in the field. Experience gathering requirements, showing what you built, and fixing it. It does not have to be external customers; another department within your company is fine.

On the technical side, experience running systems with LLMs integrated in production is a strong plus. However, even without it, if you have the three things above, you can make up for the AI part on the job.

Entry Points into the Role: Where Most People Come From

Since FDE is a new role, there are almost no people with direct experience. The entry points are adjacent roles.

Original RoleWhat You Can BringWhat You Make Up for on the Job
Systems Engineer / Software EngineerImplementation skills, production operations experienceSpeaking in terms of business numbers, negotiating with customers
IT ConsultantStructuring problems, conversations with managementImplementing things yourself
Product ManagerRequirements definition, prioritization, user researchImplementing things yourself
Data ScientistAnalysis, model buildingMaking solutions actually used and embedded in business operations

Whichever role you come from, what is missing is only one or two things. We believe that being able to make up for the rest on the job is one of the great things about being an FDE.

Contract, Side-Job, and Freelance Positions

Most openings are for full-time employees, but contract-based positions are starting to appear. SB OAI Japan is hiring through secondment from SoftBank. Freelance job sites have also created an FDE category.

If you take on FDE work as a contractor, you should make sure the contract includes the following two points.

  • The endpoint of responsibility. Whether it goes up to the PoC, to production operation, or until business numbers actually move.
  • How results are measured. Which numbers, at what point in time, and how they will be measured.

If these remain ambiguous, it ends up being on-site client work that merely carries the FDE name. I wrote about the difference between FDE and SES in "What Is the Difference Between FDE and SES?"
