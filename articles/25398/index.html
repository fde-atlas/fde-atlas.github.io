Summary

BizDev Tech, run by DeFactory, analyzed its open Azure freelance listings in Japan (October 2026): 110 listings after deduplication, 87 with listed rates, averaging ¥790,000/month (median ¥750,000) — ¥30,000 above the all-role average, or about ¥9.48 million annualized over 12 months. Rates vary by role and stack: operations/maintenance averages ¥717,000, migration ¥866,000, lead/PM ¥905,000. Listings mentioning AWS, GCP, or Terraform average ¥856,000 versus ¥734,000 without. Of 24 listings paying ¥900,000+, most involve migration, lead/PM, AI, or network design. AWS listings (368) outnumber Azure roughly 3.3 to 1; only 13 Azure listings are fully remote, with 54 hybrid. No listing required Azure certifications.

Key points

  • 110 open Azure listings average ¥790,000/month, ¥30,000 above the all-role average.
  • Migration roles average ¥866,000 and lead/PM ¥905,000; operations roles only ¥717,000.
  • Listings also mentioning AWS, GCP, or Terraform average ¥856,000, ¥122,000 more than those without.
  • AWS listings outnumber Azure about 3.3 to 1; only 13 Azure listings are fully remote.
  • No listing required Azure certifications; three listed them as preferred.

Many people who have worked for years with on-premises or Windows Server infrastructure wonder whether they can go independent with Azure projects. To conclude upfront, the average rate for open Azure projects is ¥790,000 per month, about ¥30,000 higher than the average across all job types. Annualized, that is approximately ¥9.48 million in revenue (assuming 12 months of continuous work, and not take-home pay). On the other hand, AWS projects number about 3.3 times those for Azure, and only 13 projects are fully remote. However, the experience requirement for migration projects is "2+ years of building and operating Azure environments," and whether you can apply with on-premises operations experience alone cannot be determined from the project listings alone. No projects requiring Azure-related certifications were found. For those mainly experienced in on-premises with limited Azure experience, the path is to gain experience by getting involved in Azure building or migration at your current job, then target migration and design projects (Chapter 9). In this article, we examine the details of Azure projects using data from open projects on BizDev Tech, operated by our company.

Only 3 of the 25 operations/maintenance/monitoring projects with listed rates reach 900,000 yen. Based on trends in project descriptions, the likely order in which someone with long operations experience can raise their rate is as follows.

  • Build experience in Azure build and migration (only 8 of 110 projects state a required number of years of Azure experience; most do not mention years)
  • Build a track record in design and build work, and take on migration design or network design
  • Take on leading multiple members or PMO roles

Only 8 of 110 projects state "N years or more" immediately after Azure or cloud, with 4 requiring 2 or more years and 4 requiring 3 or more years (one says "2–3 years or more"). Within this range, no projects requiring 5 or more years appeared.

Both entry-level projects require 2–3 years or more of hands-on Azure experience. Q. I have long experience, but my rate isn't going up. What am I missing?

A. Based on your current experience, we'll tell you via chat which types of projects would raise your rate, comparing against actual project listings.

You can chat without registering as a member. Our representative, Hirano, will reply by the next business day. About 80% of our projects are first-tier (direct) contracts.

The strengths of Azure projects are that their average rate is 30,000 yen higher than the overall average, and that high rates appear in migration, AI, and network work. The weaknesses are fewer projects than AWS and fewer fully remote options.

The average of the 87 projects with listed rates is 790,000 yen, 30,000 yen higher than the all-occupation average of 760,000 yen. There are 40 projects (46.0%) at 800,000 yen or more per month. Migration from VMware to Azure is an entry point where on-premises operations experience can be leveraged, and some projects pay 900,000 yen or more.

There are 368 AWS projects versus 110 Azure projects—less than a third of AWS. The 58 projects that do not mention AWS, GCP, or Terraform in their descriptions average 734,000 yen. Fully remote projects are few at 13. Also, there are 37 onsite projects, averaging 709,000 yen, lower than the overall average.

The 19 projects with Azure in the title (projects where Azure is the main focus) average 792,000 yen (17 with listed rates), about the same as the overall 790,000 yen. Even projects centered on Azure alone can sustain competitive rates.

However, with fewer projects, gaps between opportunities are more likely. Being able to also handle AWS or Terraform broadens the range of projects you can apply for, and projects listing both Azure and AWS average 864,000 yen. Centering on Azure and adding a second cloud tends to broaden your options and raise rates. That said, the number of such projects is small, so causality cannot be confirmed. For working as an advisor on cloud projects, the article on cloud consultants is a useful reference.

There are two ways to find Azure listings: filtering by technical tags in listing names, or using agents and referrals. Many job listing pages show only the listing name and maximum monthly rate; the role and accompanying technologies are only visible after opening the listing details.

Listing names on job sites carry tags for technologies and roles, such as 【Azure/RAG】, 【Intune/Azure/EntraID】, 【Azure/Terraform】, and 【Azure/PMO】. Reading roles from the tags: RAG and AI indicate generative AI infrastructure, EntraID and Intune indicate authentication and device management, Terraform indicates construction automation, and PMO indicates a facilitation role. Looking at the figures in Chapters 2-5 of this article, listings with AI, Terraform, or PMO tags tend to have higher rates.

About 80% of listings on BizDev Tech, which we operate, are first-tier (direct contracts with end-user companies, with no intermediary company). Start by comparing the required skills and settlement hours in the listing details against your own experience.

Before the interview, confirm the following 4 points through the listing details and the interview.

  • Work arrangement: On-site, hybrid, or fully remote; frequency of office attendance
  • Accompanying technologies: Technologies required beyond Azure, such as AWS, Terraform, and Entra ID
  • Role: Whether you will handle construction, migration, operations and maintenance, or leadership
  • Years of experience: Whether 2 or 3 years of required Azure experience

The rate can move by ¥100,000 or more depending on role and work arrangement. Before applying, check whether these 4 points match your conditions.

Q. Can you review my skill sheet? I want to apply for listings with higher rates than my current one.

A. Yes. A member of our team at Defactory (Hirano) will compare it against actual listing details and reply via chat with ways to write it that tend to raise your rate (scope of responsibility and decisions you made independently).

If you don't yet have a listing you want to apply for, you can also receive new matching listings by email (no phone number required, registration with a Google account accepted, for those with 5+ years of total IT work experience). About 80% of our listings are first-tier.

The listings to target change with your experience. Those with long on-premises operations experience should start with migration and construction, while those with long cloud experience should target listings involving leadership and design.

On-premises Windows Server and VMware operations experience can be leveraged in Azure migration listings. Listings involving Windows Server numbered 10 (average ¥736,000), and Entra ID/Active Directory numbered 20 (¥734,000), with rates lower than the overall average.

To raise your rate, move toward migration (¥866,000) or design (¥846,000) listings. For VMware-to-Azure migration listings, the 2 listings that stated years as a condition required "2+ years of Azure environment construction and operations". Whether you can apply with only on-premises operations experience cannot be determined from the listing details alone. Those with around 8 years of experience, primarily on-premises, should proceed in the following order.

  • Get involved in Azure migration or construction at your current job to build years of Azure practical experience
  • While your years are insufficient, search listing details for migration and construction listings that do not state a required number of years
  • After 2 years, apply for migration and design listings (averages of ¥866,000 and ¥846,000)

This order is an inference based on trends in listing details.
