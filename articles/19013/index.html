FDE Campus Hiring Requirements and Resume Writing Guide for New Graduates

Original title: 应届生 FDE 校招岗位要求与简历写法

FDE Times · 2026-10-28 · 4 min read

Summary

This guide examines new-grad FDE openings at Palantir, C3 AI, and Applied Intuition for December 2026 to 2027 graduates. It argues the coding bar is broad—CS fundamentals plus one solid language like Python or C++—while job descriptions emphasize stakeholder management, building solutions for specific customers, and owning decisions. FDE Academy advises that one deployed project with real users and a written case study beats eight polished demos, and warns applicants to check travel requirements (Palantir: 25–50%) and avoid listing too many languages.

Palantir, C3 AI, and Applied Intuition are all hiring for the 2027 class, and the job descriptions treat customers as being just as important as code.

Key Takeaways

  • Palantir, C3 AI, and Applied Intuition all have FDE roles specifically targeting graduates from December 2026 through 2027.
  • The coding requirements in the job descriptions are fairly broad: computer science fundamentals, plus solid proficiency in one language such as Python or C++. The rest is about working directly with customers and maintaining relationships with multiple stakeholders.
  • FDE Academy believes that one deployed project with real users and a case study write-up beats eight polished demos.

You are a senior in college and you see a job posting for "Forward Deployed Engineer, New Grad." Many people scroll right past it, assuming that working side by side with customers and making decisions yourself is something only people with five years of experience get to do.

That assumption is wrong. C3 AI has a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role specifically for graduates of December 2026 or summer/fall 2027. Palantir is hiring new grad Forward Deployed Software Engineers for its Commercial business, targeting students graduating in December 2026 or spring 2027; Applied Intuition has also posted a new grad FDE role for December 2026 graduates.

So the real question is no longer whether new grads can become FDEs, but whether your application materials reflect the work described in these job postings.

FDE Academy is a training site for this career path. It believes the gap for new grads lies mainly in preparation, not talent; and preparation starts with understanding the job description.

The Coding Bar Is Broad; the Hard Part Is Customers

Read the three job descriptions carefully and the coding requirements are actually quite broad. C3 AI requires hands-on experience with data structures, algorithms, cloud, and relational or NoSQL databases, plus an object-oriented language. Applied Intuition requires a bachelor's or master's degree (in progress or completed), strong programming skills, citing C++ or Python as examples.

What's worth noting are the remaining items. Palantir expects new hires to maintain relationships with all stakeholders, from users struggling with day-to-day work to executives who make the final call; and to build applications, LLM workflows, and production-grade solutions tailored to each customer's specific situation.

Applied Intuition, meanwhile, has FDEs work directly with customers, deploy and integrate products in real environments, and take ownership of decisions.

The recruiting platform Paraform believes FDEs need five core skills: a backend language, cloud infrastructure, data engineering, applied AI, and customer communication.

Mapping the three new grad roles against these five skills, degrees and code are just the baseline. Customer collaboration and ownership of decisions are where you should proactively build evidence, because coursework rarely asks you to do either.

How Should You Present a Small Project?

FDE Academy believes the biggest differentiator for new grads is a project that has been deployed and has real users using it.

In its view, one such project with a case study write-up is more persuasive than eight polished demos. This advice matches the job descriptions, because with real users, you are forced to do the very things the job descriptions describe.

For example. A volunteer club at your school has ten coordinators who every week manually copy data from five spreadsheets into a report for the club director.

You write a Python tool that reads these spreadsheets and generates a draft report. You sit next to each coordinator as they try it out, revise based on feedback, then demo it to the director and finalize the official report template.

Technically, this is a small project. Its value lies in the fact that you can map every part of the project to a specific line in the job description:

Job description itemWeaker resume phrasingStronger resume phrasing
Work directly with customers on deploymentDeveloped a report automation toolBefore writing the first line of code, sat with 10 coordinators to observe how they produced reports
Maintain relationships from users to executivesGood team playerGathered feedback from daily users, reported to the club director, and got the new report template approved
Design solutions around a specific customer's situationResponsible for the backendAfter asking users, decided to remove two unused data fields, then owned deployment and support
Proficient in one programming languagePython, Java, C++, Go, RustPython: read and cleaned data from 5 inconsistently formatted spreadsheets

The numbers in the table are hypothetical; replace them with your own. Writing the right-hand column requires learning nothing new—just retell the same project using the job description's wording.

Getting Started, in Order

Choose users first, technology second. Find a small group around you doing repetitive manual work, one that includes someone with the authority to say "yes, let's use this." In your project, this person is the "executive who makes the final call" from Palantir's job description.

Next, get a working version into real users' hands as soon as possible, even if it's rough. Record every conversation: what they wanted, what you decided to do, and why. These records later become your case study and interview answers.

Finally, measure one concrete thing, such as how many people are still using it after a month, or how much time each report saves. Write a one-page case study and link it on your resume. For interviews, prepare a story about a time when what users wanted was completely different from your plan, and how you handled it.

Mistakes That Weaken Your Application

First, a portfolio full of tutorial exercises and demos nobody uses. Second, listing six languages when the role only asks for proficiency in one; such a list buries what you're best at.

Another mistake is not reading the working conditions carefully. Palantir states its new grad roles require travel 25%–50% of the time, depending on team and location; C3 AI only says you must be willing to travel when needed; Applied Intuition's job description doesn't mention travel at all.

Wikipedia lists travel requirements and the pressure to solve customer problems on short timelines as downsides of the job. If you don't want to be away from home up to half the time, ask about it in the first round.

The last mistake is treating the FDE role as a SWE job with a few extra meetings. Software company Capicua describes FDEs as engineers who write production code directly on customers' infrastructure and data.

If you can't get into an FDE role directly, pick an engineering job that exposes you to real systems and real users. That way, a few years later, your resume will already contain content like the right-hand column of the table above.

The 2027 roles are already open. If only a few months remain before the deadline, spend the time on a group of real users rather than adding another language to your resume.
