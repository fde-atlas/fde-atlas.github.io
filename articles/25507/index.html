What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer? Role + Examples

Strategy Canon · 2026-09-15 · 1 min read

Summary

The article defines the forward deployed engineer (FDE): a software engineer embedded in a customer's operations, working with real users, data and workflows until the product actually works. Improvements made on site feed back into the core product, so each subsequent deployment needs less custom work. Palantir popularized the role: its engineers adjusted the product on site and demoed directly to intelligence analysts, bypassing IT. In 2016, soldiers in the 82nd Airborne did in hours with Palantir what took days with the Raytheon-integrated system. The article also warns the model is expensive and fails when every deployment remains a separate custom build, turning the company into a consulting firm.

Key points

  • FDEs work on site with real users, data and workflows.
  • On-site fixes flow back into the core product, reducing later custom work.
  • Palantir won analysts directly, skipping IT departments.
  • 82nd Airborne case: hours with Palantir vs. days with Raytheon.
  • The model fails if deployments stay one-off custom builds.

Full text

A forward deployed engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who works inside a customer's operations, with its real users, data and workflows. FDEs stay until the product does the job, and what they build on site goes back into the core product, so later customers need less custom work. Palantir made the role well known.

Also calledforward deployed engineersFDEembedded engineering

How Forward-Deployed Engineering works

An engineer on site sees the real problem and can fix it in days, without long chains of handoffs. When the fix goes into the core software, the next customer starts with it already in place, so each deployment needs less custom work. Palantir's engineers adjusted the product on site and demonstrated it to the intelligence analysts who would use it, skipping the IT department. In 2016, soldiers in the 82nd Airborne did work in hours with Palantir that took days with the Raytheon-integrated system.

Embedded engineering teams are expensive. The model fails when each deployment stays a separate custom build, which turns the company into a consulting firm.
