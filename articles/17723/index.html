FDE Salary: Forward Deployed Engineer Levels and Compensation

Original title: FDE 薪资多少：前线部署工程师职级与薪酬

FDE Times · 2026-10-08 · 6 min read

Summary

Using public job postings from Palantir and OpenAI plus salary data, the article maps the FDE career ladder. Palantir's Forward Deployed Software Engineer role requires just one year of post-graduation experience, with base pay of $135,000–$200,000. OpenAI's Platform Engineer role needs five years and pays $230,000–$385,000 plus equity, while its FDE Manager role lists $345,000 and requires eight years including two managing FDEs—showing a management track exists within the role itself. The author warns that three median figures ($190,000 from 135 postings, $216,250 from Levels.fyi self-reports, $385,000 from a vendor report) measure different things and shouldn't be conflated. Outside the US, pay runs roughly 50–70% of US levels; negotiate against the specific posting.

Key points

  • Palantir's FDSE needs only one year of experience; base pay is $135,000–$200,000.
  • OpenAI's Platform Engineer role pays $230,000–$385,000 plus equity, requiring five years.
  • OpenAI's FDE Manager role lists $345,000 and requires two years managing FDEs.
  • Three median salary figures measure different things and can't serve as negotiation benchmarks.
  • Non-US salaries run about 50–70% of US levels per the vendor report.

Key Takeaways

  • Palantir hires FDSEs with only 1+ years of post-graduation experience, with an estimated base salary of $135,000–$200,000: the entry bar is lower than many people think.
  • OpenAI already has a Manager, FDE level: listed compensation is $345,000, requiring 8+ years of experience, including at least 2 years managing FDEs—the management promotion path exists within this career itself.
  • The three medians measure three different things: $190,000 base salary from 135 job postings, $216,250 self-reported total compensation on Levels.fyi, and $385,000 mid-level total compensation from a vendor report that reuses Levels.fyi data—don't conflate them.

Palantir is hiring Forward Deployed Software Engineers with only one-plus year of post-graduation work experience, with an estimated base salary of $135,000–$200,000 per year. Meanwhile, OpenAI has posted a Manager, Forward Deployed Engineering role paying $345,000, requiring eight-plus years of experience, including at least two years managing Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs).

Between the two job postings lie seven years of career progression—and a ladder that anyone can see clearly on public job boards.

For such a new career, that's unusual: salary and promotion paths are usually hard to find references for. Putting Palantir's and OpenAI's job postings alongside public salary data, three things stand out: a low entry bar; a management layer that already exists; and, from the middle level upward, equity driving total compensation far away from base salary.

For developers considering a move into forward deployed engineering—whether in Vietnam, where this article was originally published, or elsewhere—this ladder answers two practical questions: which level to aim for, and which numbers in salary reports can be compared with each other.

The entry bar is lower than you'd think

The most striking thing about Palantir's job posting isn't the salary but the experience requirement: one-plus year of relevant work experience after graduation. Just one year.

The role has an estimated base salary of $135,000–$200,000, total compensation may include restricted stock units (RSUs), and the job requires up to 25% travel to customer sites.

In other words, being an FDE at Palantir doesn't require already being a senior engineer. If you have two or three years of experience, you've already cleared the tenure bar. The real threshold, if there is one, lies in the word "relevant"—and that depends on your resume, not your years of service.

The middle level: OpenAI's floor is above Palantir's ceiling

One level up, OpenAI is hiring a Platform Engineer in New York for Forward Deployed Engineering, requiring five-plus years of experience as a software engineer or machine learning engineer, with a salary range of $230,000–$385,000 per year plus equity. OpenAI's $230,000 floor is already above Palantir's $200,000 ceiling.

The $155,000 spread between the floor and ceiling of the same role says something in itself. The requirement is only five years minimum, so someone who just hit five years and someone far beyond it can both apply to the same role—and such a wide range leaves room to offer different salaries to the two types of candidates.

Add in unquantified equity, and this is exactly the level where total compensation starts to visibly diverge from base salary.

The manager level does exist

OpenAI's Manager, Forward Deployed Engineering job posting lists only one number: $345,000. The role requires eight-plus years of technical or technical delivery experience, including two-plus years managing FDEs, and the hire will lead a team of FDEs.

The significance of this posting lies not in the number but in the fact it reveals: the management promotion path exists within this career itself. What's required is experience managing FDEs, and the job is leading a team of FDEs; promotion doesn't mean switching to a different role. At least at one frontier lab, FDEs have their own management hierarchy.

The $345,000 figure also needs careful interpretation. The posting calls it "compensation"—a single number, without the base salary and equity breakdown given in the Platform Engineer posting. So don't compare it directly against the $230,000–$385,000 individual contributor range to judge who earns more; the two postings use different yardsticks.

Three medians, three different measurements

At this point, the three medians barely line up.

A recruiting firm analyzed 135 job postings and found a median FDE base salary of $190,000 for 2026 (covering all regions), with an interquartile range of $160,000–$220,000. Levels.fyi, where engineers self-report, gives a median total compensation of $216,250.

Perspective AI's salary report, aggregating data from Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, job postings, Blind, and self-reported data, gives a median mid-level FDE total compensation of $385,000, $610,000 at the staff level, and over $1.2 million at the principal level at frontier labs.

The large gaps between the numbers aren't contradictory, because each measures something different. $190,000 is base salary in job postings; $216,250 is total compensation self-reported by actual practitioners; $385,000 is mid-level total compensation in a vendor report—and that report itself says what pushes the top up is equity at frontier labs.

The three datasets aren't independent of each other either: Perspective AI's report uses Levels.fyi data as one of its inputs. And both the $190,000 and $385,000 figures come from content published by companies selling recruiting services or software.

So none of the three numbers should serve as a salary-negotiation benchmark. The benchmark is the job posting of the company you're applying to. The only value of the $190,000 figure is that it matches the postings above: it falls in the upper half of Palantir's range and below OpenAI's floor, so it can be used to cross-check base salary.

Perspective AI's report also gives Anthropic's Applied AI Engineer a range: from $300,000 at L3 mid-level to $1.2 million at L6 principal. L3–L6 is the report's own grading, not Anthropic's published leveling system.

But they reinforce the same conclusion: in this report, total compensation quadruples from mid-level to principal, and by the report's own account, the top end comes from equity, not base salary.

Which numbers apply outside the US?

All the numbers above are for the US market. Perspective AI's report explicitly states that its data only covers the US, and that salaries outside the US are only 50–70% of US levels.

This 50–70% belongs to this report alone and says nothing about Vietnam or any specific remote role. The only conclusion you can draw is: don't carry the US salary ranges unchanged into negotiations for roles outside the US—ask the company directly which salary band this role is set at.

So how should you use this ladder? The first piece of advice is to aim at the right rung.

If you have two to four years of experience, you already exceed Palantir's 1+ year requirement but haven't reached OpenAI's 5+ year threshold; with five or more years, you can apply to both types of roles. Whichever category you fall into, don't skip the "entry" role just because your years of experience exceed the requirement: its salary range still spans $65,000.

The second is to read job postings in three columns: years of experience, salary range, and compensation structure beyond salary. The three postings above show that the third column changes with level: Palantir offers RSUs that "may include," OpenAI writes equity into the salary range, and the manager level gives only a single "compensation" figure.

When writing your resume, target the requirements of the Palantir posting itself: "relevant" experience after graduation, and willingness to spend up to 25% of your time on-site with customers.

The most convincing way to prove "relevant" is to describe in one sentence an experience where you worked directly with customers or end users, understood their needs, and personally delivered results—this carries more weight than a list of technical terms.

The ladder is already written on job boards, with specific years and amounts. What remains is deciding which rung to step onto first.
