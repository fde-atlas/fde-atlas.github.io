How to Advance AI-Driven Business Improvement | Steps from a Practicing FDE

Original title: AI業務改善の進め方｜FDE実践者が教える手順

日本Fde協会 · 2026-07-05 · 8 min read

Summary

The head of the Japan FDE Association, drawing on experience building several AI SaaS products, argues that AI-driven business improvement succeeds or fails based on frontline involvement, not technical skill. Starting from the technology itself leads to unused tools and stalled PoCs. The article lays out a five-step FDE method—observe workflows, narrow to one AI-suitable problem, build a minimal prototype, run on-site tests, then embed and improve—citing results like a 40% cut in inquiry handling time within a month. AI suits only high-volume repetitive judgments and time-consuming pattern recognition; frontline staff should act as supervisors of AI output. Problem identification should stay in-house, while implementation can be outsourced, ideally to FDEs who stay engaged through adoption.

Key points

  • Success depends on frontline involvement, not technology.
  • Five steps: observe, narrow to one problem, prototype, on-site test, embed.
  • AI fits only repetitive judgments and slow pattern-recognition tasks.
  • Frontline staff shift from creating work to checking AI output.
  • Keep problem definition in-house; implementation can go to FDEs.

More and more companies are taking on AI business improvement, but I have heard the same complaints over and over: "We introduced a tool, but no one on the ground uses it," or "Our PoC never moved on to production." While serving as the representative director of the Japan FDE Association, I have also developed multiple AI SaaS products, including automated social media posting, lead generation, and content generation. What my experience has taught me is that the success or failure of AI business improvement depends not on technical skill, but on "how you get the front line involved."

In this article, I explain the approach to AI business improvement that FDEs practice, in five steps. If you want to learn about the FDE way of working itself first, please see "What is an FDE? A Practitioner Explains How It Differs from SEs and SIers".

The Biggest Reason AI Business Improvement Fails

Starting with "AI First" Always Leads to Failure

The most common failure pattern in AI business improvement is starting from "we want to use AI." Management issues an order like "we need to adopt AI too," and the latest AI tool is introduced just for the sake of it. But because it doesn't fit the front line's workflows, it goes unused and is forgotten a few months later. I have seen this pattern at many companies.

When I launched an e-commerce business, I made this very mistake myself. Believing that "if you build something technically great, it will sell," I implemented a sophisticated AI-powered recommendation engine. But what customers actually wanted was not recommendation accuracy—it was speed from order to delivery. After six months with almost zero sales, I learned a painful lesson: "problems come first, technology comes later."

What Sets FDEs Apart: Starting from the Problem

FDEs don't enter the front line in order to introduce AI. They enter to solve the front line's problems, and use AI as the solution only if it's the best fit. If AI isn't necessary, an Excel macro or a no-code tool may be enough. This "tool-agnostic" mindset is the fundamental prerequisite for successful AI business improvement.

Here is the concrete procedure FDEs follow when driving AI business improvement on the ground, organized into five steps.

Step 1: Work Observation—Seeing the Front Line with Your Own Eyes

The first thing to do is simply observe the front line's work. Interviews alone are not enough. Sit next to the person in charge and time for yourself how many minutes each task takes. When I did this with a manufacturing client, I discovered that a data entry task the person described as "no big deal" was actually taking over two hours every day. They had become so accustomed to it that they never noticed the inefficiency.

Step 2: Narrowing Down the Problem—Choosing One Thing for AI to Solve

From the problems found through observation, choose just one where AI is effective. There are three criteria here: Does it occur repeatedly? Is the data available? Is it work that doesn't really need to be done by humans? A problem that meets all three is the first target for AI business improvement. If you try to solve everything with AI, nothing gets done properly.

Step 3: Prototype—Building and Demonstrating a Minimal AI Feature

Once the problem is decided, build a minimal AI feature into a prototype. In AI SaaS development, I often start by writing a Python script of a few dozen lines, running real data through it, and showing the results. When developing the automated social media posting tool, I initially built only post generation for a single platform and showed the client, "posts like this can be created automatically." For details on concrete prototyping methods, see "FDE Prototyping Techniques | Show Something Working in One Day".

Step 4: Field Testing—Having the Team Use It in Real Work

Once the prototype works, have the team use it in the field right away. What matters here is that the FDE is present on-site. Explaining how to use it, handing over a manual, and leaving will not lead to adoption. Sit beside them, ask "Isn't this part hard to use?", and make adjustments on the spot. When introducing the content generation tool, I visited the site every day for a week, observed how the person in charge was editing the AI's output, and improved its accuracy.

Step 5: Adoption and Improvement—Creating a State Where "We Can't Go Back to Life Without AI"

The goal of AI business improvement is not "introduction" but "adoption." True success is when the front line uses AI naturally and feels "we can't go back to how things were before this." With the lead generation tool, we achieved a 40% reduction in inquiry handling time within one month of introduction. Confirming this figure together with the person in charge and sharing that "if we stop, we'll go back to how things were" led to an organizational decision to continue using AI.

What FDEs Are Careful About in AI Business Improvement

"Use AI Where It Works"—Staying Pragmatic

AI is not a panacea. What my experience developing multiple AI SaaS products has shown me is that AI truly delivers results only in two areas: "large volumes of routine judgments" and "pattern recognition that takes humans a long time." For other problems, a simpler solution is almost always faster and cheaper.

Make Front-Line Staff the "Supervisors" of AI

AI output is not 100% accurate. That is precisely why front-line staff are given the role of "checking and correcting AI output." This doesn't add work—instead of "creating from scratch," the job becomes "checking and correcting," which dramatically reduces the burden. At the same time, it dispels the anxiety of having one's job taken by AI. I learned this during my time in fund management at a financial institution: the principle that humans always make the final judgment holds true even in the age of AI.

Outsourcing AI Business Improvement vs. Doing It In-House

I also receive many questions like "Should AI-driven business improvement be done in-house or outsourced?" My conclusion is that "identifying the problem and designing the direction should be done by people who know the front line," while implementation can be done in partnership with external experts.

However, if you simply outsource everything to a typical SIer or AI vendor, you tend to end up with the traditional "requirements definition → development → delivery" process. An FDE embeds themselves in the customer's workplace and provides end-to-end support, from identifying issues through implementation and adoption. This difference greatly influences the success rate of AI-driven business improvement. If your company has no one capable of working like an FDE, consider partnering with an external FDE.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Roughly how much does AI business improvement cost?

A. It depends on the scale of the issue, but the phase where an FDE builds and validates a prototype typically costs from several hundred thousand to several million yen. A key advantage is that you can verify the impact with a prototype before committing to large-scale system development, making it easier to judge the return on investment.

Q. Can employees without AI expertise get involved in AI business improvement?

A. Yes, they can. In fact, the people who know the day-to-day operations best are the stars of the process. FDEs and engineers handle the technical aspects of AI, while the people on the ground make decisions such as "what to delegate to AI" and "whether the output is correct."

Q. Is AI business improvement effective for small companies too?

A. Yes, it is. In fact, smaller organizations make decisions faster, so the speed from implementation to adoption is dramatically quicker. One strength of AI business improvement is that even a small team can make a big impact.
