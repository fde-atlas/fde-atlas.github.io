FDE Career Paths | Three Options Five Years from Now

Original title: FDEのキャリアパス｜5年後の3つの選択肢

日本Fde協会 · 2026-09-05 · 6 min read

Summary

An article by the Japan FDE Association outlines three career directions for forward deployed engineers after a few years of experience: going independent to run small projects end-to-end, being promoted within a company to lead cross-departmental deployments, or specializing in a narrow field such as security, data integration, or AI agent design. The author argues that what separates senior careers is not coding speed but two factors: building governance, permissions, and audit trails into designs from the start, and preventing knowledge silos through documentation and handover. The association's private FDE certification is presented as a voluntary skill benchmark with no legal force and no promises about income or career outcomes.

Key points

  • Three paths: independence, internal promotion, or specialization—none ranked higher.
  • Governance and audit design determine the scale of projects entrusted to an FDE.
  • Documentation and handover skills mark the senior career fork.
  • The FDE certification is a private, voluntary benchmark, not a legal requirement.

How to chart your FDE career path is a question that inevitably confronts anyone who has started working hands-on in the field. As the Japan FDE Association, having practiced both the technical and business sides in the field ourselves, we have organized the overall picture of this career. Reading this article will give you an overview of the three directions—independence, internal promotion, and specialization—and help you understand what you should develop next from where you currently stand. Here, FDE refers to Forward Deployed Engineer (an engineer who embeds in the customer's field) and is not an abbreviation for disk encryption. We are not in a position to recommend any particular path, so we can neutrally present the conditions necessary for everyone. Let's think through this together.

Three Directions for an FDE Career Path

After a few years of FDE experience, the paths you can pursue broadly fall into three categories. None is superior or inferior to the others; suitability varies with personality and aspirations. First, let's grasp the overall picture.

Going Independent and Running Small Projects Yourself

The first is independence. You enter the customer's field on your own and handle everything end to end, from interviewing stakeholders about their operations to designing improvements and embedding them into daily operations. Our representative director, Maekawa, has drawn on his PM experience at a major engineering company to work in diverse settings, from finance to launching an e-commerce business, entering each field himself and solving problems. The path of independence centers on this "do it yourself" attitude. How to enter this field from scratch is covered in detail in How to Become an FDE.

Getting Promoted Internally and Leading Implementations

The second is the path of advancing within a company. Your role expands from one-off improvements to leading implementations that span multiple departments. If you are concerned about how to draw the line with in-house engineers, see Role Division Between FDEs and In-House Engineers.

Specializing in a Specific Domain

The third is specialization. By going deep into a specific area such as security, data integration, or AI agent design, you establish a position of "the go-to person in this field." A SaaS company executive once told us that during their growth phase they struggled because they had no specialist they could entrust with governance design. Narrow, deep expertise has solid demand.

The Real Axis That Separates Senior Careers

Common career advice tends to stop at discussing skill acquisition as "an engineer who embeds in the customer's field." However, from our firsthand experience in the field, whichever of the three directions you pursue, what separates senior roles is not how fast you write code.

Can You Handle Governance and Audits?

How you manage permissions, logs, and audit trails becomes a major source of rework if added later. One manufacturing client built an improvement tool first and then had to go back and sort out "who is allowed to perform which operations." Whether you can incorporate governance considerations into the design from the start changes the scale of projects you can be entrusted with. This is an area that market commentary rarely touches on.

Can You Prevent Knowledge Silos Through Proper Handoffs?

The other is documentation and handoff. A system only you can operate may be valued in the short term, but it is a risk to the organization. Customers sometimes tell us, "You know our business better than our own employees," but the real dividing line for senior roles is whether you can go beyond that and leave that knowledge behind for others. Whether you go independent or get promoted internally, the quality of your handoff design determines the total trust you earn.

Certification Grades Serve as a Benchmark for This Mastery

The FDE certification we are developing is designed to show, in stages, the milestones of such mastery. Grades serve as a benchmark for the abilities certified: for example, Grade 3 is the standard for being able to independently handle small projects, while Grade 2 or above is the level for involvement in multi-department or high-risk projects. The FDE certification is a private credential operated independently by the association, and holding or not holding it does not legally restrict your ability to perform this work. Not taking on projects beyond your grade on your own is a matter of self-discipline as a practitioner, not a legal restriction. You can review the grade system in What Is the FDE Certification?.

Where to Read Next Based on Where You Are Now

Once you understand the three directions and the axes that separate them, it's time to dig deeper according to your own situation. Here we guide you to the entry points for each path.

Confirming How This Differs from Other Work Styles

If you are considering independence, understanding how it differs from SES, a common point of comparison in terms of contract type, will make your decision easier. How SES and FDE Differ organizes the differences in working styles. Also, FDEs are often confused with consultants since both solve problems with technology, but we explain the distinction in Consultants vs. FDEs. Maekawa himself, when he first moved from finance to e-commerce, operated under the assumption that "if you build something good, it will sell," and had almost zero sales for about six months. The realization that neither technology nor business alone is enough lies at the core of the FDE way of working.

Grasping the Full Picture of Required Skills

Whichever direction you pursue, the foundational skill set is the same. From interviewing and business design to governance and audit readiness, the breadth of areas you cover is summarized in The Full Picture of Skills Required for FDEs. Start by checking on that map where you currently stand and which skills you have been developing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Is independence the ultimate goal of an FDE career? No. None of independence, internal promotion, or specialization is superior to the others. Those who want to run projects themselves suit independence, those who want to lead implementations within an organization suit internal promotion, and those who want to master a narrow domain suit specialization. It is a choice based on your aspirations.

Q. What should I hone to move into a senior role in five years? More than coding speed, mastery of governance and audit readiness, and of documentation and handoffs that prevent knowledge silos, will pay off. These raise the scale of projects you can be entrusted with and broaden the paths available to you.

Q. Will getting the FDE certification raise my salary or advance my career? The certification indicates a benchmark of certified abilities and does not promise or imply outcomes in compensation or career. Grades function as a standard for users to judge "how much can be safely delegated."
