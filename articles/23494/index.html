Summary

Written by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, this Japanese article explains typical costs for web system development. It walks through project phases (requirements definition, design, coding, testing, maintenance) and notes pricing is usually calculated in person-months: headcount × months × monthly rate. It lists rate benchmarks (programmers 400,000–1,000,000 yen/month; senior engineers up to 2,000,000) and reference prices for common systems like mail forms (from 30,000 yen), CRM, CMS, and EC sites (from 500,000 yen). It closes with four cost-optimization tips: define a concrete vision, document deliverables, don't equate high rates with quality, and weigh payback period; MVP development is suggested for budget-conscious projects.

Key points

  • Costs are quoted in person-months: engineers × months × monthly rate.
  • Programmer rates range 400k–1M yen/month; senior engineers up to 2M.
  • Mail forms start at 30k yen; CRM, CMS, EC sites from 200k–500k yen.
  • MVP development typically takes 2–3 months; testing 2 weeks to a month.
  • Four tips: clear vision, documentation, skepticism about high rates, payback planning.

What specific tasks incur costs in system development? System development involves a variety of tasks, but when ordering development, estimates are mainly provided for the following items.

In system development, requirements definition is the process where engineers and other technical staff clarify the specific challenges and objectives of building the system. In other words, it is specialized work that defines how the system will operate and its specifications.

On the other hand, demand definition is the process where people without specialized knowledge (executives, business owners, etc.) communicate to engineers what features they want developed in the system. It is the work of defining the specific content of requirements, such as "We want to achieve XX, so we need a feature like YY."

Since these are extremely important processes for identifying necessary features and determining the overall picture of the final system, if you have an ideal vision, it is best to voice your requests freely.

To avoid situations where "it turned out different from what I expected" after development has progressed, it is important to clearly communicate your opinions to the development team during the requirements definition stage and share as concrete an image as possible.

Generally, requirements definition and demand definition take several days.

Design is the process of concretely designing the system's structure and features according to what was decided in the requirements definition. You need to consider the design from various perspectives, not just incorporating sufficient features, but also checking for security issues, among other things.

Design is divided into basic design (design of feature lists, data input/output, etc.) and detailed design (design of servers, databases, etc.), and is further classified into external design and internal design depending on the content.

External design covers the system's design, i.e., what is known as the UI (user interface). Since the UI is the part where users directly perceive convenience and appeal, it is developed through careful coordination with the development team. For web systems in particular, the user experience is important, so it is advisable to secure a reasonable budget for UI design.

Internal design, on the other hand, is the design of the actual functionality, carried out mainly by members with specialized skills such as programmers and engineers.

As with requirements definition, design also requires several days.

This is the process of actually programming based on the design documents.

From requirements definition through the design process, it generally takes about 10 days from contract to the actual start of development, and depending on scale, it may take around 20 days. The development workload itself varies depending on the scale of development, but taking MVP development as an example, it takes an average of 2 to 3 months.

This is the process of testing whether the developed system operates according to the design documents and whether there is any suspicious behavior. Testing takes anywhere from 2 weeks to about 1 month depending on scale.

If defects are found, they are corrected each time, and once there are no more issues, the system is officially released (delivered).

Even after system development is complete, troubleshooting, updates, and other tasks need to be performed as needed. Maintenance and operation costs are incurred for this purpose.

Note that maintenance and operation costs are generally calculated separately from the costs of developing the system itself.

In addition, depending on the situation, costs may also be incurred for schedule management, equipment purchases, business trips/meetings, and so on.

For web system development, it is impossible to definitively state "XX yen is required."

This is because system development costs are generally calculated using "person-month" rates.

A person-month is a workload unit calculated by multiplying "the number of technical staff (engineers, programmers) involved in system development" by "the monthly labor cost required per person."

Simply put, costs are calculated as "number of people required for system development × number of months."

For example, if 3 engineers (monthly rate of 600,000 yen) develop a system over 3 months, that equals "9 person-months," calculated as follows:

3 people × 3 months × 600,000 yen = 5,400,000 yen

However, the required person-months vary considerably depending on the project, such as the content of the system you want to build and how far you want to systematize it. Rates also differ depending on the engineers' skill levels.

In other words, different systems involve different development scales, staffing levels, engineer skill levels, and development periods, so variation in costs is only natural. If the development period extends beyond the schedule, the additional costs will be added on top.

Most of the cost of web system development is labor costs, and those labor costs can easily fluctuate depending on various factors. To learn more about the development cost of the system you are considering, it is a good idea to request an estimate or take advantage of free consultations.

As a rough guideline, the monthly rates for programmers and engineers are as follows.

・Programmer (subcontractor/freelancer): 400,000–800,000 yen

・Programmer (major company): 600,000–1,000,000 yen

・Junior engineer: 600,000–1,000,000 yen

・Mid-level engineer: 1,000,000–1,200,000 yen

・Senior engineer: 1,200,000–2,000,000 yen

Note that there is no clear definition of the differences between junior, mid-level, and senior engineers, but you can generally think of them as follows.

Junior: Understands the basics of programming and can mainly handle downstream processes such as testing.

Mid-level: Has several years of professional experience and can lead multiple engineers and programmers.

Senior: Can handle negotiations with clients as well as upstream processes such as requirements definition and requirements specification.

Generally, development companies already have the foundations for system development, so they do not build every system from scratch. If a development company has something that is already fairly well developed, it may be able to build a system at costs like the following.

As explained above, it can be difficult to grasp the exact cost of system development, and many people may find it hard to set a budget. Finally, we will introduce some tips for proceeding with system development at optimal cost.

To proceed with development at optimal cost, it is first important to have as concrete a vision as possible of the system you want to develop.

The cost of developing a web system varies greatly depending on what features you want, what purpose the system will serve, and what level of quality you want to build. If the level of technical expertise required for the build changes, the monthly rates will differ significantly, and a longer development period will only drive up labor costs.

If changes to the design or programming come in after development has started, such as "we actually want to change this part" or "please fix this because it doesn't match our image," it creates extra work for the engineers and programmers.

To avoid discrepancies with the development side, it is important to have a clear image of the finished product

at the time of placing the order. It is a good idea to identify specific issues and the solutions you need in advance.

Of course, if you have any doubts or questions about specialized areas, please feel free to consult us during interviews for requirements definition.

In addition to sharing a concrete image of the finished product with the development side, it is also important to document the deliverables produced during the development process.

By keeping visible records of the deliverables for each process and task, such as requirements definition documents, design documents, programs, and reports, you can reduce the risk of mistakes, discrepancies, and delays.

Just because the monthly rate is high does not necessarily mean a system of the ideal quality will be completed. For example, many major development companies proceed with development while making use of subsidiaries and outsourcing, which raises their monthly rates accordingly.

In other words, a high monthly rate does not directly translate into higher quality engineers or programmers.

In that respect, small and medium-sized enterprises and individually run development companies can be more agile and communicate more frequently, so they may actually be able to develop more smoothly and at lower cost.

When communication is easy, discrepancies regarding development decrease, and the possibility of wasted development time

is also likely to be reduced.

It is important not to be swayed by brand image alone, but to consider whether the company will deliver development that is worth the cost.

Developing a web system presumably means you are planning to operate some kind of service.

It is also an important perspective to estimate how much profit the service can be expected to generate over the next several years before considering the system development cost.

If you only plan to operate it for two or three years, developing it without spending so much may offer better cost-effectiveness.

It is a good idea to consider not only development but also the costs of future maintenance and operation when deciding how much to allocate for development. For example, if you want to keep costs down and develop quickly, you should consider MVP development. In MVP development, development focuses only on the minimum necessary features, which helps reduce unnecessary costs.

The cost of system development varies depending on various factors, such as the scale of development, man-month rates, and the number of years of operation. If you are at risk of exceeding your budget or want to optimize costs as much as possible, keep the following four points in mind.

1. Place orders with a concrete vision in mind 2. Document the deliverables 3. Don't place too much trust in high man-month rates 4. Consider the period over which development costs can be recovered
