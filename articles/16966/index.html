Summary

This article traces OpenAI's forward deployed engineering (FDE) organization, launched in January 2025 under Colin Jarvis and modeled on Palantir's approach of embedding engineers who write production code inside customer systems. In May 2026, OpenAI spun the function into DeployCo, a majority-owned subsidiary backed by over $4B from 19 investors, and acquired London-based Tomoro with roughly 150 deployment specialists. It also details the SB OAI Japan joint venture with SoftBank and its Japan-exclusive product Crystal intelligence, case studies with John Deere, Morgan Stanley and BBVA, and the strategic rationale: closing the deployment gap that leaves most enterprise AI pilots without measurable value.

Key points

  • OpenAI's FDE team grew from 2 people in early 2025 to a planned 52 by year-end.
  • DeployCo was funded with over $4B; analysts expect 2,000-4,000 engineers within three years.
  • SB OAI Japan is a 50/50 SoftBank joint venture launching Crystal intelligence in 2026.
  • MIT NANDA found only 5% of integrated AI pilots generate million-dollar-scale value.
  • Gartner predicts 70% of enterprises will be forced off FDE-led contracts by 2028.

Insight 06

From DeployCo and the Tomoro Acquisition to SB OAI Japan

OpenAI, the company that changed the world with ChatGPT, is now attempting to spin out an "engineering unit embedded on-site at customer companies" into an independent company with $4B (roughly ¥600 billion). Why would a frontier model developer bet this heavily on a labor-intensive, consulting-like model? This article systematically covers OpenAI's FDE function launched in January 2025 under Colin Jarvis, the founding of OpenAI Deployment Company (DeployCo) in May 2026 and the Tomoro acquisition, the John Deere / Morgan Stanley / BBVA case studies, and the joint venture with SoftBank, "SB OAI Japan," through to Crystal intelligence.

The MIT NANDA report "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" published in July 2025 (150 executive interviews, 350 employee surveys, and analysis of 300 public AI deployments) concluded as follows:

"Of the AI pilots that were integrated, only 5% are generating millions of dollars in value, while the vast majority remain stalled without delivering meaningful results to the P&L"

The bottleneck lies not in model performance but in "connecting to enterprise data, business workflows, and regulations." OpenAI's FDE was designed precisely as a mechanism to pull companies in that 95% up into the 5%.

OpenAI was founded in December 2015 in Delaware as a nonprofit by Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Greg Brockman, Ilya Sutskever, and others. Its mission is "to ensure AGI benefits all of humanity." In 2019 it moved to a capped-profit structure, and in 2025 it became a Public Benefit Corporation (PBC), with the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation holding 26%.

  • ARR: $2B in 2023 → $6B in 2024 → over $20B in 2025 → $25B as of March 2026 (monthly run rate of $2B)
  • ChatGPT WAU: over 900 million (March 2026), over 9 million paid seats for ChatGPT Enterprise, over 1 million business customers
  • Enterprise revenue accounts for over 40% of the total, expected to rival consumer by the end of 2026 (CFO Sarah Friar)
  • On March 31, 2026, OpenAI announced $122B in funding commitments, at a post-money valuation of $852B (co-led by SoftBank, a16z, D.E. Shaw Ventures, MGX, and TPG)

At the core of this rapid growth emerged the FDE organization that fills the "last mile of deployment" for enterprise customers.

On January 15, 2025, Colin Jarvis (OpenAI Global Head of Forward Deployed Engineering) made the official announcement on X.

"I'm excited to announce that I'm leading a new Forward Deployed Engineering function at OpenAI. Our focus is getting customers to production—whether that's building novel applications from zero to one, or helping scale proven use cases like customer service or recommendations to fit customers' specific systems and customer infrastructure."

Jarvis lives in Glasgow and has an unusual background: a Master's in Celtic Studies and a Master's in Strategy and International Security from the University of Edinburgh. He joined in 2022 as a Solutions Architect, went on to become Head of Solutions Architecture, and then launched the FDE function. He writes code himself, including committing a Qdrant integration to the OpenAI cookbook.

The team is expanding rapidly. Numbers Jarvis himself stated on Altimeter Capital's podcast (November 2025):

  • Early 2025: 2 people
  • As of November 2025: 39 people
  • End-of-2025 plan: 52 people
  • 24 job postings across the US, Europe, and Japan, with base salaries up to $345,000 on the SF scale plus equity (Business Insider)

Jarvis's own explanation of the motivation for launching the FDE function is clear: "When ChatGPT came out in 2022, everyone was excited, but getting value from the models was quite difficult." "The only approach that consistently worked was to embed directly with customers, learn their workflows, and work alongside their staff. That's what led OpenAI toward the forward-deployed model."

OpenAI maintains the SA role, but it is clearly distinguished from FDE (per interviews by Pragmatic Engineer / Gergely Orosz):

  • Solutions Architect (SA): advisory role. Rarely writes production code on customer infrastructure. Builds MVPs/PoCs using anonymized or offline data.
  • FDE: goes inside the customer's systems and writes production-grade code. In the words of OpenAI's official job postings: "own everything from discovery, technical scoping, system design, building, and production rollout, working directly with the customer's engineering and domain teams" and "measure success through production adoption rates, workflow impact, and changing product and model roadmaps through eval-driven feedback."

Jarvis himself has published OpenAI's approach to running FDE projects:

  • Validation phase: The FDE lands on the customer's site and re-examines "is the scoped work truly the most valuable thing for the customer?" They build evals from user input/labeling and agree on pass criteria with the customer.
  • Delivery phase: build the features, continuously improve eval scores (hill-climb on evals), and finally report the results against the original goals.

In Jarvis's words: "FDEs work amid enormous ambiguity. What the customer described during scoping is often not the most valuable work."

The mechanism for feeding knowledge back internally is also institutionalized: biweekly sharing sessions with the Research team, biweekly readouts with the Head of Product and PMs, a Slack "FDE Field notes" channel, and quarterly all-FDE bootcamps. A circuit that feeds lessons learned in the field back into the product roadmap and model evals has been institutionalized.

OpenAI's FDE is not a new invention, but a port of the model Palantir spent roughly 20 years refining. Shyam Sankar (employee #13, now President and CTO) coined the term "Forward Deployed Engineer," and in the early 2010s established the paired structure of Echo (Deployment Strategist) and Delta (FDE).

Bob McGrew (former Palantir executive, later OpenAI's Chief Research Officer) defined the FDE role as "someone who sits at the customer site and bridges the gap between what the product can do and what the customer needs," emphasizing the importance of the feedback loop of "laying a gravel road that the platform side later paves into a highway."

The details of the Palantir model itself are covered in A Deep Dive into Palantir's FDE Model. This article is best understood as its "OpenAI port."

The inflow of Palantir talent into OpenAI is also striking. Bob McGrew served as OpenAI CRO, and VP of Public Policy Ryan Beiermeister also came from Palantir (per WSJ reporting). An alumni network known as the "Palantir Mafia" is quietly spreading through the operations layer of AI companies.

On May 11, 2026, OpenAI announced it would spin off its FDE function into an independent subsidiary: the "OpenAI Deployment Company" (DeployCo).

  • Committed capital: over $4B
  • Investors (19 companies total): TPG (lead), Advent, Bain Capital, Brookfield ($500M), Goldman Sachs, SoftBank, Warburg Pincus, B Capital, BBVA, Emergence Capital, Goanna, WCAS, and others
  • Consulting and SI partner firms: Bain & Company, Capgemini, McKinsey & Company
  • Ownership: majority-owned and controlled by OpenAI
  • Multiple secondary sources indicate that OpenAI COO Brad Lightcap will oversee it
  • Estimated pre-money valuation: approximately $10B

At the same time, OpenAI acquired London-based AI consultancy Tomoro, including its roughly 150 FDEs and deployment specialists. Tomoro's customers included Mattel, Red Bull, Tesco, Virgin Atlantic, and Supercell.

Analyst estimates project a deployment engineering organization of 2,000 to 4,000 people within three years (TNW). CRO Denise Dresser commented: "AI is increasingly able to do meaningful work. The challenge now is helping integrate these systems into companies' infrastructure and workflows. DeployCo is designed to fill that gap."

Mitch Ashley of Futurum Group points out sharply: "The $4B deployment division is OpenAI's own admission that frontier model access alone does not translate into enterprise revenue."

  • Closing the deployment gap: someone has to bridge frontier models with enterprises' legacy systems, data silos, and regulatory realities.
  • A product discovery engine: patterns FDEs find in the field flow back into Product and Research, becoming next-generation model and platform features (isomorphic to Palantir's "gravel road → paved highway").
  • Lock-in: when an FDE spends six months building an AI system deeply integrated into a customer's data, workflows, compliance, and architecture, it becomes "irreplaceable infrastructure" (MindStudio).
  • Adding a revenue axis through outcome-based billing: DeployCo's fees are "expected to be set at the top range of the consulting industry, as outcome-linked retainers rather than per-seat pricing" (GREY Journal). It will grow into a revenue line separate from API and ChatGPT subscriptions.
  • Reclaiming enterprise share: Menlo Ventures' December 2025 survey shows OpenAI 27% vs Anthropic 40% vs Google 21%. OpenAI partner Deedy Das said: "The era of OpenAI's automatic victory is over. Catching up with Anthropic may be difficult for anyone." It is a strategic pivot to move the contest to "depth of implementation."

OpenAI's FDEs literally went into farms in Iowa and co-developed John Deere's "See & Spray" AI feature. The Pragmatic Engineer article includes a photo of Colin Jarvis himself standing in a field—true to the etymology of "forward deployed" (the military term for front-line positioning).

OpenAI's official report: "After reviewing domain expertise and hundreds of real cases, building a custom eval system to measure accuracy, and iterating rapidly to improve model performance, John Deere was able to reduce pesticide usage by up to 70% and increase customer engagement."

Timing constraints were also extremely tight—the integration had to be ready "in time for the next planting season." The FDE team completed the integration within the limited timeframe. CTO Justin Rose's vision: an "AI-driven customer success function where a small team, supported by real-time telematics, delivers personalized support at a 1:1,000 ratio."

One of OpenAI's earliest enterprise customers. Built "AI @ Morgan Stanley Assistant" (a chatbot for internal wealth management) based on GPT-4.

  • David Wu (Head of Firmwide AI Product & Architecture Strategy): "We went from being able to answer 7,000 questions to being able to answer virtually any question from a corpus of 100,000 documents."
  • Kaitlin Elliott (Head of Firmwide Generative AI Solutions): "Feedback from advisors has been overwhelmingly positive. Follow-ups that used to take days now happen within hours."
  • Over 98% of advisor teams use the Assistant daily (OpenAI official)
  • Document search efficiency improved from 20% to 80%

Jarvis himself looks back on this project as "the decisive case in establishing the forward-deployed model."

Spanish banking giant BBVA started in May 2024 with 3,300 ChatGPT Enterprise licenses, and after reaching 11,000 users, rolled out to all 120,000 employees across 25 countries by December 2025. It is one of the largest generative AI enterprise deployments in the financial services industry.

  • At the 11,000-user stage, "employees save nearly 3 hours per week from routine tasks, and over 80% use it daily"
  • Employees themselves created over 4,800 custom GPTs
  • "BBVA has a dedicated team working directly with OpenAI's Product / Research / Technology Success teams"—this is the job of OpenAI-side FDEs

Chairman Carlos Torres Vila: "Our alliance with OpenAI will accelerate the pace at which we integrate AI natively to create a smarter, more proactive, and fully personalized banking experience." Sam Altman described BBVA as "a powerful example of how a large financial institution can adopt AI at serious speed."

On February 5, 2026, OpenAI announced a new enterprise AI platform called Frontier. It is a platform for building, deploying, and managing AI agents, featuring shared business context, agent identity / permissions / guardrails, and integrations with CRMs, data warehouses, and ticketing tools.

  • Initial customers: HP, Intuit, Oracle, State Farm, Thermo Fisher, Uber
  • Pilots: BBVA, Cisco, T-Mobile
  • Official: "OpenAI helps design, deploy, and operationalize agent workflows through Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs)" (InfoQ)

On February 23, 2026, OpenAI announced Frontier Alliances (multi-year partnerships with Accenture / BCG / Capgemini / McKinsey). "Forward-deployed engineering teams work alongside partners to support enterprise customers' AI adoption and adaptation" (CIO Dive).

  • Anthropic: has "Forward Deployed Engineer, Applied AI" roles within its Applied AI team (25–50% travel, 3+ years of experience). In its Series G in February 2026, it reached a post-money valuation of $380B (ARR $14B, $19B as of March). It also announced a $1.5B enterprise AI services JV led by Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs. For FIS's financial crime AI agent, Anthropic FDEs co-designed it and even handled knowledge transfer so FIS could build additional agents on its own.
  • Google Cloud: CEO Thomas Kurian at Google Cloud Next 2026: "The era of pilots is over. The era of agents has begun." Applied FDEs earn a base of $127K–$183K, and FDE IV $183K–$265K. 59 new FDE job postings were published (US, India, Brazil, Australia, Mexico, Singapore, South Korea, Canada).
  • Accenture: In March 2026, launched a "dedicated forward deployed engineering practice" through its partnership with Microsoft.
  • Others: Scale AI, Cohere, Mistral, Decagon, Sierra, Cresta, Glean, Hebbia, Harvey, Writer, Ramp (operating with about 15 people under 4 core operating principles), and many other YC-backed AI application companies are hiring FDEs.

According to an official Salesforce blog citing analyses by Indeed and the Financial Times, FDE-related job postings grew +800% from January to September 2025. a16z's Joe Schmidt IV called the FDE "the hottest job at startups."

As of May 2026, the following openings are listed on OpenAI's official careers page:

  • Forward Deployed Engineer - Tokyo (Model Deployment for Business division)
  • Forward Deployed Software Engineer - Tokyo (same as above, with heavier requirements)

The distinctive requirement: "Complete Japanese-English bilingualism (reading, writing, and speaking) is mandatory. Resumes must be submitted in English, and interviews are conducted in both languages." "Business trips are mainly domestic."

Even more notable is SB OAI Japan—a Japanese entity established on November 5, 2025 as a 50/50 joint venture between OpenAI and SoftBank Group (based at Coast 1-7-1, Minato-ku, Tokyo).

  • Product: "Crystal intelligence"—a Japan-exclusive enterprise AI solution, scheduled to roll out in 2026
  • The first customer is SoftBank itself (SoftBank Group already uses roughly 2.5 million custom GPTs internally)
  • Follow-up on February 6, 2026: leveraging OpenAI's "Frontier" platform, "SB OAI Japan will support each customer enterprise end-to-end through Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), from design and build through operations"
  • Masayoshi Son: "With the launch of SB OAI Japan, AI agents will understand our goals, coordinate with one another, and autonomously carry out the tasks we want done."
  • Colin Jarvis: "SoftBank has been a fantastic partner pushing the frontier of what agents can do in the enterprise. Through the Frontier platform and SB OAI Japan, we will build a replicable model for enterprise AI adoption across Japan."

Japanese IT outsourcing is still dominated by SI/SES—a specification-consumption, man-month model. In contrast, the FDE is a structurally different category of service: "defining the problem yourself, writing production code, measuring with evals, and feeding feedback back into the product." The job descriptions, hiring criteria, and contract structures are all fundamentally different from existing SIers.

A notable prediction from Alex Coqueiro, Senior Director Analyst at Gartner: "By 2028, 70% of enterprises will be forced to move away from high-cost, FDE-led contracts and will face a skills shortage in evolving these capabilities independently in-house" (CIO.com).

In other words, Japanese companies face a binary choice:

  • Rely on external FDEs (OpenAI DeployCo / SB OAI Japan / partner SIers): faster to land, but at the cost of accepting long-term vendor lock-in and high costs.
  • Build in-house FDEs: requires time and investment in talent. It is difficult to convert Japanese SIer alumni directly into FDEs (the requirements-definition culture is different). Consider securing "dual-wielding" talent—former consultants who are also full-stack engineers—or importing talent from overseas.

The structural reasons why PoCs get stuck at the implementation stage are organized in Why AI PoCs stall and go unused, and how to avoid it, and the skill requirements at the individual FDE level are organized in The 3 skills an FDE needs.

  • Freshness of the numbers: FDE job postings, OpenAI/Palantir ARR and market caps, and FDE headcounts are changing rapidly. Be sure to check the latest figures before making decisions.
  • The reality of DeployCo: as of its launch in May 2026, its actual operations, revenue contribution, and customer outcomes are still unproven.
  • Gartner's 2028 70% prediction: it is a prediction, not an established fact. It is best read as a counterweight to "the risk of over-reliance on external FDEs."
  • MIT NANDA's "95%": the original source states that "of integrated AI pilots, only 5% are generating millions of dollars in value." Some commentators have raised methodological objections, so cite it with care.

Large-scale FDE operations like OpenAI's DeployCo and SB OAI Japan are optimized first for Fortune 500-scale customers. However, the FDE philosophy itself—going deep into the field, owning design, implementation, and operations end-to-end, and embedding improvements into the business through short iteration cycles—can also be applied by much smaller Japanese companies.
