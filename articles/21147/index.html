What is a Customer Success Engineer (CSE)?

A Customer Success Engineer (CSE) is a role that supports customers who have adopted SaaS or AI products from a technical standpoint, so they can continuously derive value. Unlike a mere support engineer, a CSE sets "customer success" as a KPI, and even after onboarding is complete, prevents churn (cancellations) through expansion proposals and troubleshooting.

Why It Matters

In the SaaS business, it is widely known that the cost of acquiring new customers (CAC) is generally several times higher than the cost of retaining existing ones. For this reason, whether customers become established after product adoption and renew or expand their contracts greatly affects business continuity.

A CSE handles both churn prevention and expansion revenue. Specifically, they work with both technical expertise and customer understanding, covering everything from API connection setup and support for embedding the product into internal workflows, to health checks using usage logs and proposals for leveraging new features. Major SaaS and AI companies such as Salesforce, HubSpot, Zendesk, and Anthropic have established CSE positions because, in an era of increasing product complexity, technical customer success has become essential.

Difference from FDE: Pre-Adoption vs Post-Adoption

CSEs and Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) are easily confused because both appear to "solve customers' technical challenges," but the phases in which they operate are fundamentally different.

PerspectiveCSEFDE
Main activity phasePost-adoption (retention and expansion)Pre-adoption to early stage (problem discovery and PoC)
Main KPIsChurn rate, NRRClose rate, PoC success rate
Relationship with customersContinuous, long-termShort-term, intensive
Typical tasksOnboarding, expansion proposals, health checksBusiness analysis, prototype building, proposals
Similar roleA technical version of a CSM (Customer Success Manager)A field-oriented version of a Solutions Engineer

Compared with Solutions Engineering, where Solutions Engineers primarily focus on "pre-sale demos and proposals," the CSE's main battleground is "post-sale retention."

Scope of Work: The Four Roles of a CSE

The practical work of a CSE falls broadly into the following four areas.

  • Onboarding support: Technical support for initial setup, such as API connections, SSO configuration, and data migration, shortening the time to value realization (Time to Value)
  • Proactive health checks: Analyzing usage logs, login frequency, and feature utilization to detect at-risk customers early and intervene
  • Troubleshooting: Resolving technical issues and integration problems without waiting for escalation. Also serves as a bridge to the development team
  • Expansion proposals (Upsell/Cross-sell): Proposing higher-tier plans or additional modules based on customer maturity, contributing to revenue growth in collaboration with CSMs

How the Role Is Changing in the AI Era

Behind the active hiring of CSEs by AI providers such as Anthropic and OpenAI from 2025 to 2026 lies the unique difficulty of AI products. Since the output quality of LLMs varies greatly depending on configuration, prompt engineering and optimizing RAG architectures have become key CSE skills.

In addition, supporting workflow automation using AI agents and MCP (Model Context Protocol) is expanding as a new area of CSE work. The role is deepening, shifting from the traditional "teaching product operation" to "embedding AI into customers' business processes."

As with the difference between DevRel and FDE, the boundaries of the CSE role are fluid, so expectations vary by company. When reading job postings, checking three points—"Is it onboarding-focused?", "Does it carry churn-prevention KPIs?", and "Does it involve writing code?"—will clarify the role's outline.

Practical Use Cases

When small and mid-sized SaaS companies and AI startups adopt CSE-style practices, it leads to improved churn rates. For example, among the clients supported by Tufe Company, there are not a few cases where customers consider churning due to a feeling of "not being able to make full use" of the AI tools they adopted. In such cases, taking a CSE-like approach—visualizing usage, supporting workflow integration, and conducting regular usage reviews—can encourage customers to become self-sufficient, leading to continued use and upgrades.

Common Misconceptions and Points to Note

  • A CSE is not a help desk: Handling inquiries is not the main job; the essence of a CSE is to anticipate customer success metrics and act proactively. Staying in a passive support role prevents them from delivering their true value.
  • Confusion with CSM (Customer Success Manager): While CSMs focus primarily on business-side relationship building and renewal negotiations, CSEs are the technical implementation version of that role. Customer retention is achieved only when the two collaborate.
  • Not a junior role to FDE: FDE and CSE are parallel, distinct roles. If the FDE is the "standard-bearer" of the earlier stage, the CSE is the "companion" of the later stage, and both are indispensable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What skills are needed to become a CSE?

In addition to technical skills such as API integration, SQL, and basic scripting (e.g., Python), a business sense for understanding customers' operational challenges is required. The frequency of writing code varies by company, but the most important thing is an attitude of "using technology to solve customer problems."

Q. How does a CSE differ from a Solutions Engineer?

A Solutions Engineer (SE) is mainly responsible for pre-sale demos and technical proposals, working during the sales phase. A CSE, on the other hand, primarily operates in the post-sale retention phase. Also see the article on Solutions Engineering. Both serve as bridges between technology and business, but they get involved at different times.

Q. How does the work of a CSE on AI products differ from traditional work?

Since LLM-based products produce outputs that vary greatly depending on configuration, prompts, and data quality, CSEs are expected to have knowledge of prompt engineering and RAG. In addition, because customer use cases are highly diverse, the ability to propose individually optimized solutions—rather than relying on generic manuals—is essential.
