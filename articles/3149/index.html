The State of FDE as a Service, 2026

Plank · 2026-06-11 · 15 min read

Summary

Plank Research's 2026 report maps forward-deployed engineering's rise from a Palantir job title to a multi-billion-dollar category. OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and AWS committed over $9B to deployment ventures, while Palantir alumni founded 111+ startups raising about $11.6B. FDE job postings grew 729% year over year, yet only about 2,000 of roughly 17,000 US FDEs can reliably deliver enterprise AI returns. Analysis of 15 production engagements shows 60% ship agentic systems, 27% involve voice, and no two stacks are identical—supporting vendor-neutral specialists. Most AI pilots still fail to reach production, and Gartner expects 7 in 10 enterprises to abandon FDE-led agentic projects. The report predicts independents will serve startups the labs ignore, agent QA becomes the bottleneck, and time-to-embed becomes the key buying criterion.

Key points

  • OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and AWS committed $9B+ to FDE ventures in 2026
  • FDE postings grew 729%, but only ~2,000 of 17,000 US FDEs deliver reliably
  • Of 15 production engagements, 60% ship agents and zero share identical stacks
  • Gartner expects 7 in 10 enterprises to abandon FDE-led agentic AI projects
  • Independent vendor-neutral FDE firms are positioned to serve underserved startups

Forward-deployed engineering went from a Palantir job title to a multi-billion-dollar category in under a year: OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Amazon committed $9B+ to deployment ventures, FDE job postings grew 729% in the year to April 2026, and the failure rate of unassisted AI pilots remains the highest-stakes number in enterprise software. This report maps where the category stands in 2026, combining third-party market data with first-party analysis of 15 documented production engagements.

Key findings

Why is everyone hiring forward-deployed engineers?

Because the labs proved the economics. The OpenAI Deployment Company launched in May 2026 with a $4B+ raise led by TPG, and Anthropic stood up a roughly $1.5B services joint venture with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman and Goldman Sachs in the same month. Both pair embedded engineers with ownership of the outcome, modeled on Palantir, whose deployment-led business grew 93% year over year in Q2 2026, with US commercial revenue up 149% to $764M. Within weeks the hyperscalers followed: AWS committed $1B in June and Microsoft $2.5B in July, both embedding thousands of their own engineers inside customers. Across the 292 postings in our census that disclose a range, the median band runs $153K to $220K, and 18% top $300K.

Who is buying the deployment layer?

Capital is one signal; M&A is the other. In two years the labs, the hyperscalers, the consultancies, and the tooling vendors all went shopping for the same three things: forward-deployed engineering teams, agent and evaluation tooling, and the talent to run them. The pattern says the value is being fought over at the deployment layer, not the model.

Why does adoption lag what models can do?

Sequoia’s Pat Grady calls it the diffusion gap: the distance between what models can do and how much of that organisations have put to work. Each generation widens it. ChatGPT (November 2022), OpenAI’s o1 reasoning models (late 2024) and long-horizon agents with Claude Code and Opus 4.5 (autumn 2025) each opened new work before most companies had absorbed the last. Grady sees the gap as the opportunity for application-layer companies, and notes the labs competing for usage through price cuts and consultant-led deployments in the Fortune 500.

Where the gap shows up: pilots that never ship

Four independent research houses, one conclusion: most enterprise AI never ships, and the trend is worsening, not improving. S&P Global's 42% abandonment figure was 17% a year earlier, and Gartner expects more than 40% of agentic projects to be canceled by 2027. The gap is not model capability. It is integration, evaluation, and ownership of the tacit reasoning behind a company's decisions, which, as Foundation Capital argues in its context-graph thesis, was never captured as data in the first place.

Zero identical stacks in fifteen engagements

Across 15 engagements we counted 35+ distinct tools: OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s APIs, open models, three agent frameworks, two voice stacks, and classic ML alongside them. The median engagement combines four. LangGraph is the most-used tool in the census and it is still absent from 8 of the 15; the next two, OpenAI’s Agents SDK and the Vercel AI SDK, appear in 5 each. A specialist in any one vendor’s stack arrives covering roughly one of the four tools a median engagement runs on.

The clearest outside corroboration comes from Andrew Ng: vendor-neutral FDEs are hard to find, he notes, because lab FDEs exist to integrate their employer’s product, and letting them bind your processes “significantly reduces optionality.”

The category outgrows the labs

In twelve months the FDE title spread from Palantir to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Cloud, Microsoft, Accenture, Deloitte, and ServiceNow, all now selling or staffing forward-deployed engineering by name. But lab and SI FDEs serve large enterprises and deploy their employer's stack, which leaves the startups and scale-ups they won't staff, the bulk of the market by company count, underserved. If prior platform shifts are any guide, independent specialists grow up around exactly that gap.

Agent QA becomes the bottleneck, then the standard

57% of organizations now run agents in production, and quality is the #1 deployment barrier at 32%, ahead of latency and cost (LangChain, n=1,340). As the constraint moves from building to verifying, per-PR testing by humans plus AI becomes table stakes for production AI by 2027.

Requirements and workflow design become the edge

Agents, evals, and MCP are becoming table stakes. The durable edge sits upstream, in the work a model cannot do for you: reading what a business actually needs, extracting the tacit rules buried in its operations, and redesigning workflows around AI rather than bolting it on.

Time-to-embed becomes the buying criterion

Engineering hires average around seven weeks, and full-cycle AI hiring often runs two to four months. With pilots dying on those clocks, speed of embedding decides vendors more than headcount price. Services that put a productive engineer inside a team within weeks will price at a premium and still win on total cost.

Common questions

How big is the forward-deployed engineering market in 2026?

OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Amazon have committed more than $9 billion to forward-deployed engineering ventures since May 2026, and Palantir's own deployment business grew revenue 93% year over year in the same quarter.

How fast is demand for forward-deployed engineers growing?

FDE job postings grew 729% in the year to April 2026, and demand is forecast to rise another 2,100% against a qualified US talent pool of about 2,000 engineers.

How many forward-deployed engineer roles are open right now?

Plank's own census counted 1,276 open forward-deployed engineer roles at 547 companies as of October 30, 2026, tracked directly from public ATS job boards.

Why do most AI pilots fail to reach production?

S&P Global found 42% of companies abandoned most AI initiatives in 2025, up from 17% in 2024, and Gartner expects more than 40% of agentic AI projects canceled by 2027. Both trace to weak integration and evaluation, not model quality.

What are companies actually building with forward-deployed engineers?

Across 15 documented production engagements, agentic systems lead at 60% of workloads and voice AI is second at 27%, spanning eight sectors and more than 35 distinct tools with no two identical stacks.

Why doesn't one AI vendor's stack cover a typical deployment?

LangGraph, the most-used tool in Plank's engagement census, is still missing from 8 of 15 engagements. A typical engagement combines four different tools, so one vendor's stack covers about a quarter of the job.
