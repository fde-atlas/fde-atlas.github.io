How to Evaluate a Forward Deployed Engineering Team

FWD Deployment · 2026-10-30 · 9 min read

Summary

This guide explains how to vet a forward deployed engineering (FDE) team before granting credentials, focusing on three checks: independent evidence of security controls (Trust Center reports and, for public vendors, the cybersecurity section of Form 10-K), a written allocation of data responsibility, and contract terms covering access, incident notice, and liability. It clarifies that SOC 2 Type II is an auditor attestation, not a government certification, and no reviewed source makes any certification universally mandatory. PCI DSS obligations apply if engineers touch cardholder data, requiring written agreements, provider lists, and annual monitoring. The author recommends customer-controlled VPC or hybrid deployment as a risk-control practice, and notes that indemnification caps and SLA clocks have no market standard, so they must be negotiated deal by deal and tested via tabletop exercises.

Key points

  • Verify Trust Center reports and public vendors' 10-K cybersecurity disclosures.
  • SOC 2 Type II is an attestation, not a government certification; no universal mandate exists.
  • PCI DSS requires written responsibility agreements, provider lists, and annual monitoring.
  • Default to customer-controlled VPC or hybrid setups; data stays in your environment.
  • No standard indemnification caps or SLA times exist; negotiate and test them yourself.

Evaluate a forward deployed engineering (FDE) team by checking three things before any engineer gets credentials: independent evidence of its security controls (Trust Center reports and, for public companies, SEC filings), a written allocation of responsibility for your data, and contract terms for access, incident notice, and liability. Under PCI DSS, written agreements must assign responsibility for account data security to relevant third-party service providers, so the contract is part of the compliance check, not an afterthought.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Have counsel read any rule that applies to your charter, your data, and your regulators.

What evidence should you collect before you shortlist an FDE firm?

Collect evidence from the vendor in a fixed order so you can compare firms on equal terms. Our method for picking the companies on this site follows the same idea: judge on documents, not sales claims.

  • Trust Center or security portal. Request the current SOC 2 Type II report, not just a badge on a web page.
  • PCI DSS status. Ask whether the firm will touch the cardholder-data environment, and request its compliance documentation if it will.
  • Written risk-assessment process. Ask how often it assesses cybersecurity risk and what inputs it uses.
  • Subcontractor list. Ask who else will see your data or systems.
  • SEC filings. If the firm or its parent is public, read the cybersecurity section of the latest Form 10-K.

Which compliance frameworks should you verify, and where?

The table maps each framework to what you can actually verify and where to look. Applicability depends on your institution, the services, and the data involved.

Framework What to verify Where to look
SOC 2 Type II An attestation report from an independent auditor. It is not a government certification. Trust Center, under NDA
PCI DSS Applies to companies that accept or process payment-card information. Requires annual validation of required controls. Trust Center, attestation documents, your own scoping
GLBA Whether you are a covered financial institution, and which services and data are involved. Counsel should confirm the controlling text. Your compliance and legal teams
SEC cybersecurity disclosure Domestic registrants describe their processes for assessing, identifying, and managing material cybersecurity risks in Form 10-K. Vendor's 10-K, if public

The sources we reviewed do not make SOC 2 Type II legally mandatory for every FDE provider. Treat it as a strong buyer requirement you set in your own vendor policy, not as a rule you can cite to a vendor.

What should you look for in a vendor's SEC filings?

Public vendors must describe their cybersecurity risk processes in the Form 10-K. The SEC's compliance guide on cybersecurity disclosure says domestic registrants must describe their processes for assessing, identifying, and managing material cybersecurity risks.

Read those passages against a short list:

  • Does the filing describe a risk assessment performed at least annually? One provider's 2025 Form 10-K describes an annual assessment drawing on internal information, known vulnerabilities, external information, industry trends, and third-party evaluations. That is a useful benchmark for what a mature description looks like.
  • Does the filing mention PCI DSS? The Primerica 2025 Form 10-K states that PCI DSS applies to companies that accept payment cards or process payment-card information and requires annual validation of required controls.
  • Is the language specific to the vendor's own practices, or generic boilerplate?

Private FDE firms will not have a 10-K. For them, rely on the Trust Center evidence and a security questionnaire, and ask for the same annual-assessment detail in writing.

How do you check PCI DSS exposure for an FDE team?

Start by deciding whether the engineers will touch the cardholder-data environment at all. The PCI DSS Quick Reference Guide says third parties may store, process, or transmit cardholder data or manage components of that environment, but the parties must identify assessment scope and responsibility.

Then confirm four obligations from the PCI DSS v4.0 SAQ B:

  • Keep a list of third-party service providers that share account data or could affect its security, with a description of each service.
  • Hold a written agreement assigning responsibility for account data security to each relevant provider.
  • Assign access by job classification and function, limited to least privilege.
  • Keep an incident-response plan ready to activate for suspected or confirmed incidents.

You must also monitor each provider's PCI DSS compliance status at least annually, per the PCI DSS v4.0 SAQ P2PE. Put that date on your vendor calendar when you sign.

PCI scope for an FDE subcontractor depends on the engineers' access to payment-card systems. If the work uses tokenized or de-identified data only, say so in the statement of work.

Should FDEs work inside your VPC or in a hybrid model?

Default to a customer-controlled VPC or a hybrid model where the data stays in your environment. This is a risk-control recommendation inferred from access control, segregation, and monitoring requirements. The sources do not mandate VPC deployment.

NIST guidance in SP 800-53 Rev. 4 states that external-system access to organization-controlled information should be allowed only after required controls are verified or an approved connection or processing agreement is retained. In practice, that means no vendor laptop connects to production data until you have verified its controls or signed a connection agreement.

A practical setup has these parts:

  • Engineers work from your VPC or a vendor-managed environment that you have reviewed, never from personal devices.
  • Data stays in your environment, and engineers build against it in place.
  • Every session is logged and the logs belong to you.

Our piece on securely granting forward deployed engineers access covers the access mechanics in another regulated setting.

How do you structure role-based access control for external FDEs?

Define roles by function, grant the least privilege that function needs, and make every grant expire. NIST SP 800-53 describes role-based access control as enforcing access according to organization-defined roles and authorized users. PCI DSS likewise requires access assigned by job classification and function, limited to least privilege.

  • Name the roles. For example: data-pipeline developer, integration tester, and engagement lead. Each gets its own access profile.
  • Map each role to datasets. Give each role only the schemas or tables it needs.
  • Use your credentials. NIST's external-credential control requires accepting only credentials that meet applicable NIST requirements, per the SP 800-53 Rev. 5 draft control markup.
  • Review on a schedule. Set the review interval in the contract, and revoke access on the engineer's last day.

Which contract clauses matter most?

Write these six clauses into the master services agreement or statement of work. The government-clause references are structural benchmarks, not market standards. Federal contracts differ from commercial banking deals.

1. Data residency and location schedule

List the locations, systems, and environments where your data may sit, and prohibit moving it elsewhere without written approval. The sources reviewed set no specific residency rule, so this term comes from your own policy and your regulators' expectations.

2. Access and environment controls

State that engineers work only inside the approved VPC or hybrid environment, under the role map above, with named individuals and logged sessions. Add a prohibition on copying data to local machines.

3. Responsibility allocation

Include a table of who is responsible for which controls. This mirrors the PCI requirement for a written agreement that assigns responsibility for account data security, and for identifying scope and responsibility.

4. Incident notice

Require prompt notice and a ready incident plan. PCI DSS requires an immediate response to suspected and confirmed incidents that could affect the cardholder-data environment. A government clause in VA Acquisition Regulation Part 852 shows that contracts can require immediate notification of a security incident involving sensitive personal information. Define who calls whom, and have the vendor's incident-response support integrated into your own capability, as NIST advises.

5. Breach cost recovery

The same VA regulation shows two structures you can borrow: liquidated damages per affected individual to cover notification, breach analysis, and credit monitoring, and broad indemnification for claims, loss, damage, injury, and liability arising from contract performance. Whether to adopt either, and at what amount, is a negotiation.

6. Audit and annual review

Reserve the right to review the vendor's SOC 2 Type II report and PCI status every year, matching the annual PCI monitoring requirement.

What indemnification caps and SLA metrics should you expect?

No source we reviewed sets a standard indemnification cap, a percentage-of-fees cap, a required uncapped breach carve-out, or a mandatory insurance limit. Anyone quoting you a market-standard number should show you the document it came from.

Treat the cap as a three-part negotiation:

  • Ask whether data-breach costs sit inside the general cap or in a separate, higher one.
  • Ask whether notification and credit-monitoring costs are recoverable per affected person.
  • Ask for proof of cyber insurance, with the limit set by your risk team and counsel.

The same holds for response times. No source supplies mandatory acknowledgment, containment, or recovery windows. Set your own clock: when it starts, what counts as acknowledgment, and what the vendor owes you at each stage. Then test it in a tabletop exercise before go-live.

How do you choose the engagement model?

Pick the model that matches how much of the work your own team can direct. Dplyz, for example, places senior engineers inside a business to connect its data and run AI agents and workflows in production. It offers three ways to work: an embedded team, project delivery, or technical leadership. Dplyz does not publish a price list, so ask for a scoped quote.

Whichever firm you pick, compare its answers against the same checklist. Common questions

What compliance certifications are mandatory for a forward deployed engineering firm in banking?

The sources we reviewed do not establish a single mandatory certification for every FDE firm. SOC 2 is an attestation report, not a government certification, and PCI DSS applies where payment-card information is accepted or processed. Have counsel confirm what applies to your institution and the data involved.

Should forward deployed engineers operate inside our VPC or use a hybrid cloud model?

Default to your VPC or a hybrid model where data stays in your environment. This is a risk-control recommendation based on access, segregation, and monitoring requirements, not a rule in the cited sources. NIST says external-system access should follow verified controls or an approved connection agreement.

What are standard indemnification caps for financial data breaches in FDE contracts?

No source we reviewed states a standard cap, so negotiate it deal by deal. Decide whether breach costs get a separate cap, whether per-person costs are recoverable, and what cyber insurance the vendor must carry.

How do you structure role-based access control (RBAC) for external forward deployed teams?

Define roles by function, map each role to specific datasets, and grant least privilege. NIST describes RBAC as access enforced by organization-defined roles, and PCI DSS requires access by job classification and function. Review access on a set schedule and revoke it when the engagement ends.

What SLA metrics should be included for FDE incident response times?

Define when the clock starts, what counts as acknowledgment, containment, and recovery, and who notifies whom. No source we reviewed gives mandatory times, so set them with your security team. PCI DSS requires an immediate response and a ready incident-response plan.
