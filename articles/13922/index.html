The core of the FDE is not "being on-site" but "being accountable for production outcomes"

Many enterprise AI projects fail not because the models lack capability, but because problem definition, system integration, permission governance, and user adoption are handled by different teams, leaving no one accountable for real business outcomes.

The role of the Shenyun FDE is to organize these scattered efforts into a single chain of accountability. The FDE enters the business front line early, first understanding the processes, people, systems, data, and metrics, then coordinating with the engineering team to rapidly build verifiable applications, and continuing to drive production deployment and real user adoption.

Whether a role is an FDE is judged not by whether they are on the customer's premises, but by whether they can connect business problems, engineering implementation, production governance, and sustained adoption.

How the Shenyun FDE model works

Shenyun has developed the FDE into a service mechanism for enterprise AI productionization, rather than a single role. It typically consists of the following four parties:

  • Customer business owner: defines goals, priorities, and acceptance metrics;
  • Frontline users: provide real processes, feedback, and adoption status;
  • Customer technical team: provides systems, data, permissions, and security constraints;
  • Shenyun FDE and engineering team: complete scenario definition, application building, Tencent Cloud production integration, eval governance, and operational scaling.

This joint team works toward the same business outcome, rather than separately handling consulting, development, implementation, and after-sales support.

The four phases of the Shenyun FDE

  • DEFINE identify the right problem: confirm the business owner, value baseline, data conditions, and priority scenarios.
  • VALIDATE close the loop: build a minimal viable version and have real users complete a full task.
  • DEPLOY go to production: integrate knowledge, systems, permissions, evals, monitoring, and human review.
  • ADOPT sustain adoption: track usage and business metrics, and replicate the assets to more processes.

The six responsibilities of the FDE

  • Business discovery: find high-frequency, high-cost, measurable problems;
  • Scenario definition: clarify users, tasks, data, permissions, and acceptance criteria;
  • Application building: coordinate the development of agents, workflows, knowledge bases, and connectors;
  • Production integration: connect WorkBuddy, enterprise systems, and the Tencent Cloud production environment;
  • Eval governance: establish evals for accuracy, security, cost, and business impact;
  • Operational scaling: continuously optimize and replicate to more scenarios.

The key differences between the FDE and ordinary implementation or outsourcing

Delivery boundaryFeature delivery or product acceptanceValue validation, production launch, and sustained adoption
Team relationshipConsulting, development, and implementation are separateA single chain of accountability spans business and engineering

Why Shenyun adopts the FDE model

Shenyun's goal is not to help enterprises "own AI," but to bring AI into real business processes. Enterprise AI productionization requires simultaneously handling business judgment, application development, system integration, cloud deployment, security governance, and user operations, which calls for a forward deployed, engineering-driven, outcome-oriented service approach like the FDE.

If you are evaluating an AI scenario, you can first use the Shenyun FDE model to determine whether the scenario is worth validating.

Frequently asked questions

Is an FDE the same as an on-site programmer?

No. Being on-site is just a work location; the FDE emphasizes comprehensive accountability for problem definition, engineering build, production launch, and sustained adoption.

What kinds of enterprises is the FDE suited for?

It suits enterprise AI projects with clear business value but not yet fully defined requirements, that need cross-system integration, and that require ongoing operations after launch.
