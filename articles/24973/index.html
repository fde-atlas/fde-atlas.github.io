What is LLMOps? Explaining the Differences from MLOps, the Operations Cycle, and FDE Practices

Original title: LLMOpsとは？MLOpsとの違い・運用サイクルとFDEの実務を解説

FDE総研 · 2026-10-02 · 12 min read

Summary

This Japanese article explains LLMOps: the practices for running LLM applications reliably in production, covering model selection, prompt and RAG data versioning, evals, observability, guardrails, and cost control. It argues the discipline centers less on infrastructure and more on defining and measuring output quality. Unlike MLOps, where retraining is the main lever, LLMOps improves systems mainly through prompt and retrieval adjustments, using LLM-as-a-Judge plus human review. The author proposes a small iterative cycle—measure, evaluate, improve, verify, release—growing eval sets from real failure cases. For FDEs, LLMOps is becoming core work: translating business standards into evals, analyzing traces, and handing off self-sustaining operations to customers. Companies should treat LLM apps as ongoing operational infrastructure, not deliverables.

Key points

  • LLMOps spans model selection, prompt management, evals, monitoring, guardrails, and cost control.
  • Unlike MLOps, improvement relies mainly on prompt and RAG tuning rather than retraining.
  • A small measure-evaluate-improve-verify-release cycle grows eval sets from production failures.
  • FDEs turn business standards into evals, analyze traces, and enable customer self-operation.
  • Companies should treat LLM apps as continuously operated infrastructure, not one-time deliverables.

What Is LLMOps? Its Meaning and Why It Is Needed

LLMOps is an approach to operations for running applications built with LLMs stably in production while continuously improving quality, safety, and cost.

LLMOps stands for "Large Language Model Operations." It refers collectively to the methods, tools, and organizational structures needed to keep chatbots, RAG (retrieval-augmented generation), and AI agents built on LLMs such as GPT and Claude running continuously in business operations, rather than treating them as finished once built.

What Does the Definition of LLMOps Cover?

The scope of LLMOps covers the entire lifecycle, from model selection to prompt management, evaluation, monitoring, and improvement.

In general, the following areas are often collectively referred to as LLMOps.

  • Model selection and switching: choosing among commercial APIs, open-weight models, and fine-tuned models
  • Prompt and context management: version control of system prompts, few-shot examples, and documents passed through RAG
  • Evaluation (Evals): continuously measuring the accuracy, usefulness, and safety of outputs against a test set
  • Observability: recording inputs and outputs, latency, token consumption, and errors for each request
  • Guardrails: measures against personal data leakage, inappropriate outputs, and prompt injection

It is often perceived as "a topic about the infrastructure that runs LLMs," but in practice the emphasis is rather on "how to define and measure output quality." This is a defining characteristic of LLMOps.

Why Is LLMOps Needed Now?

LLM outputs vary even for the same input, and behavior changes with model updates. For this reason, quality cannot be guaranteed by traditional testing alone.

While a PoC (proof of concept) for an LLM application can be built in a few days, production operation brings different challenges. Common issues heard in the field include the following.

IssueWhat Actually HappensHow LLMOps Addresses It
Non-deterministic outputsThe wording or conclusions of answers change even for the same questionRegression testing with evaluation sets
Impact of model updatesBehavior changes due to provider-side model updates or deprecation of older modelsPinning model versions and comparative evaluation before migration
HallucinationsOutputting plausible but incorrect informationPresenting RAG evidence, factuality evaluation, and human review
Cost fluctuationsAPI costs balloon due to increased usage or bloated promptsToken measurement, caching, and using different models appropriately
SecurityPrompt injection or inclusion of sensitive informationInput/output filters, permission design, and audit logs

When introducing LLM applications at customer companies as an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), you often hit the wall of "it worked well in the demo, but accuracy drops with real field data." It may be closest to reality to think of LLMOps as the steady work of building mechanisms to overcome this wall.

How Do MLOps and LLMOps Differ?

MLOps centers on "training models and operating them." LLMOps centers on "how to make the best use of existing foundation models."

LLMOps is a concept that extends MLOps. However, the levers you pull for improvement and the way quality is measured differ significantly.

How Do MLOps and LLMOps Differ in a Comparison Table?

The biggest difference is the means of improvement. MLOps relies mainly on retraining, while LLMOps relies mainly on adjusting prompts and context.

PerspectiveMLOpsLLMOps
Main subjectPrediction and classification models trained in-houseApplications using external or open foundation models (LLMs)
Main levers for improvementFeature design, retraining, hyperparameter tuningPrompts, RAG retrieval accuracy, model selection, and fine-tuning as needed
Quality evaluationClear quantitative metrics such as accuracy and AUCNo single correct answer; combines LLM-as-a-Judge and human evaluation
Main costsComputational resources for trainingToken-based billing at inference time, or GPUs for inference
What is version-controlledData, models, and codePrompts, model names and versions, search indexes, and evaluation sets
Main risksData drift and accuracy degradationHallucinations, prompt injection, and information leakage
People involvedData scientists and ML engineersApplication engineers, domain experts, and FDEs

How Much of MLOps Knowledge Can Be Used in LLMOps?

MLOps principles such as ensuring reproducibility, automated testing, monitoring, and staged releases serve directly as the foundation for LLMOps as well.

The ideas of "version-controlling data, code, and models together" and "monitoring production behavior and detecting degradation" remain unchanged in LLMOps. MLOps-derived tools such as MLflow and Weights & Biases have also been strengthening their LLM trace recording and evaluation features.

On the other hand, LLMOps differs in that evaluation design must be created together with the people responsible for the business. This is because "whether this answer is correct for the business" often cannot be judged by engineers alone. This is where FDEs, who work on-site with customers, come in.

What Are the Components of LLMOps?

LLMOps becomes easier to organize when divided into three roles: "prompt and data management," "evaluation and monitoring," and "guardrails and cost management."

Starting from tool names makes it easy to lose sight of the big picture, so it's best to first break things down by role.

How do you manage prompts and data?

Prompts, RAG documents, and evaluation data should be version-controlled just like code, so you can track change history.

  • Prompt version control: Manage with Git, or use prompt management features like Langfuse or LangSmith. Make it possible to identify which version is running in production
  • RAG data pipeline: Make document ingestion, splitting (chunking), embedding generation, and index updates re-runnable
  • Evaluation datasets: Accumulate representative questions paired with expected answers and judgment criteria

In practice, it's not uncommon for prompts to keep growing while hardcoded in application code, with no one knowing who changed what or when. Sharing the premise early on with the team that "prompts are also deliverables" will make things easier later.

What do you measure in evaluation and monitoring?

Measure evaluation along four axes—quality, safety, latency, and cost—and design pre-release testing and production monitoring separately.

AxisRepresentative metricsExample measurement methods
QualityAccuracy, consistency with sources, helpfulness of answersEvaluation set + LLM-as-a-Judge, manual sampling evaluation
SafetyRate of inappropriate output, presence of sensitive informationRule-based filters, classification models
LatencyTime to response, time to first tokenAggregation of trace logs
CostTokens and cost per requestAPI usage logs, dashboards

For production monitoring, indirect metrics like user "helpful/not helpful" feedback and the rate of follow-up questions after an answer are also effective. LLM-as-a-Judge is convenient, but the judging LLM itself has biases. It's safest to periodically cross-check against manual evaluation to confirm the judgments haven't drifted.

What do guardrails and cost management do?

Guardrails are mechanisms that protect input/output safety, while cost management is a mechanism that keeps costs down through model selection and caching.

Representative examples of guardrails are as follows.

  • Input side: Masking personal information, detecting prompt injection
  • Output side: Filtering prohibited expressions, suppressing unsupported assertions, validating output format (e.g., JSON)
  • Permissions side: Narrowing the documents accessible via RAG according to user permissions

For cost management, "routing" is commonly used, assigning high-performance models to difficult tasks and lightweight models to routine classification or summarization. Leveraging provider-provided prompt caching is also effective. However, the extent of savings varies greatly by use case, so it's realistic to follow the order of measuring first, then taking action.

How do you run the LLMOps operations cycle?

LLMOps operations involve repeating "measure → evaluate → improve → verify → release" in small cycles, improving quality while growing the evaluation set.

Don't try to complete everything at once; design the cycle assuming you'll keep learning from production logs.

What are the basic steps of the operations cycle?

The basic flow is six steps. Deciding evaluation criteria upfront is key to making improvements independent of intuition.

  • Define use cases and evaluation criteria: Agree with business stakeholders on whose work, what tasks, and to what standard counts as success
  • Build a baseline: Create an initial version with a simple prompt and model, and measure scores with the evaluation set
  • Collect traces: Record inputs/outputs, retrieval results, latency, and cost in production or a pilot environment
  • Analyze and classify failures: Categorize incorrect answers into patterns (retrieval misses, misunderstood instructions, knowledge gaps, format errors, etc.)
  • Improve and regression test: Decide whether to change the prompt, retrieval, or model, make fixes, and check with the evaluation set for regressions
  • Gradual release: Roll out to all users after limited release to some users or A/B testing, then return to step 3

How do you grow the evaluation set?

Don't aim for perfection from the start; grow the evaluation set by continuously adding failure cases found in production.

At launch, it's realistic to ask business stakeholders for "frequently asked questions" and "questions where mistakes would be problematic," and start with a small set of a few dozen cases. Once operations begin, add answers that users rated poorly and incorrect answers found through log analysis to the evaluation set. This builds a mechanism that prevents repeating the same failures.

As the evaluation set grows, it also becomes easier to decide on model switches. When a new model comes out, you can immediately compare "how much better it is for your company's work."

What's the trick to running small cycles?

Rather than assembling a heavy set of tools from the start, starting with just trace recording and an evaluation set lets you run cycles faster.

From a hands-on perspective, setting things up in the following order seems to minimize rework.

StageSet up firstCan be deferred
PoCAgreement on evaluation criteria, small evaluation setFull-scale dashboards
PilotTrace recording, habit of classifying failuresAutomatic routing
ProductionAutomated regression testing, cost monitoring, guardrailsFine-tuning (once its necessity becomes clear)

Fine-tuning is a powerful tool, but many problems can be solved by improving prompts or retrieval. Verifying with evaluation data that "prompts have hit their limits" before considering it makes it easier to justify the cost-effectiveness.

What role does an FDE play in LLMOps, and what skills are needed?

An FDE stands between the customer's business and the LLM application, consistently handling everything from building evaluation criteria to production improvement on the ground.

FDE is a role that spread at Palantir—engineers who embed themselves in customer operations and make software stick in the business. As more companies adopt FDE-style teams for generative AI adoption support, LLMOps is becoming one of the FDE's core responsibilities.

What does an FDE concretely do in LLMOps?

The FDE's job is to translate "business correctness" into evaluation, improve based on logs, and leave behind operations the customer can run on their own.

PhaseFDE's main work
Requirements definitionObserve business workflows and separate what to delegate to the LLM from what humans decide
Evaluation designWork with business stakeholders to build evaluation sets and pass criteria
ImplementationImplement prompts, RAG, and tool integrations, and connect them to the customer's existing systems
Operations and improvementAnalyze traces and apply countermeasures for each failure pattern
InstitutionalizationHand over procedures and dashboards so the customer's team can run evaluation and improvement on their own

What sets this apart from typical SI or contract development is that the evaluation and improvement loop itself is run at the customer's site. This differs considerably from an approach of building to spec and delivering, and it tends to involve spending more time digging into "why accuracy isn't improving" together with the customer.

What skills does an FDE strong in LLMOps need?

Three skills are required: the ability to implement LLM applications, to design evaluations, and to understand business operations. Of these, evaluation design is the area most likely to differentiate this role from other positions.

  • Implementation skills: Application development in Python and similar languages, API integration, RAG construction, and deployment on the cloud
  • Evaluation design skills: The ability to articulate criteria that can measure ambiguous business quality and implement them as evaluation sets
  • Observability knowledge: The ability to design traces, logs, and metrics and isolate the causes of failures
  • Security and governance: Permission design, handling of personal information, and compliance with the customer's internal policies
  • Business understanding and communication: The ability to draw tacit knowledge out of frontline staff and explain return on investment to management

Engineers with MLOps or SRE experience can apply their monitoring and automation expertise directly. Web application developers will find it easier to grasp the essentials of LLMOps if they first gain experience building evaluation sets and running regression tests.

How should companies considering FDE adoption view LLMOps?

Companies need to treat LLM applications not as "deliverables" but as "business infrastructure that will be operated continuously," and invest in a system for evaluation and improvement.

Commonly cited failure cases of generative AI adoption include projects stalling at the PoC stage, or cases where no one monitors accuracy after deployment. Whether bringing in an FDE externally or developing one internally, deciding the following points at the contracting or organizational stage makes it less likely that operations will become a mere formality.

  • Who decides the evaluation criteria and pass thresholds, and who approves them
  • The extent to which production logs may be analyzed for improvement purposes (handling of personal and confidential information)
  • The internal staff who will run evaluation and improvement after the FDE's support ends

LLM performance will continue to be updated going forward. That is precisely why, over the long term, the differentiator will be not the model's performance itself but whether a company has a mechanism for measuring what works and what doesn't in its own business. LLMOps is the practice of rooting that mechanism in the field, and it is an area where FDEs can readily demonstrate their expertise.
