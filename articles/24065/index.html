How to Choose an FDE Recruiting Agent | When to Use Specialized, Generalist, or Direct Application

Original title: FDE転職エージェントの選び方｜特化型・総合型・直接応募の使い分け

FDE総研 · 2026-10-20 · 13 min read

Summary

This Japanese guide from FDE総研 explains how to choose recruiters for Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) job searches in Japan. Because few Japanese companies post jobs under the FDE title—similar roles appear as Solutions Engineer, Implementation Engineer, or AI implementation consultant—keyword searches alone miss many openings. The author recommends matching the channel to your situation: FDE-specialized, generalist, IT-specialized, foreign-firm/high-class, or scout-type services, usually combining two or three, while direct application works best when your target company is fixed. The article lists five questions to test whether a recruiter understands FDE, a table for distinguishing genuine FDE roles from SES-style client-site staffing (contract type and who directs daily work), and advice to rewrite your resume as “technology × customer outcomes” and hand recruiters a keyword table of alternative job titles. It also covers direct applications via Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic career pages, LinkedIn alerts, and referrals.

Key points

  • Few Japanese firms use the FDE title; recruiters help find equivalent roles posted under other names.
  • Direct application suffices if your target company is fixed; recruiters suit first-timers and salary negotiation.
  • Five checks reveal whether a recruiter understands FDE work.
  • Contract type and command structure distinguish real FDE roles from SES client-site jobs.
  • Run direct applications, LinkedIn, and referrals in parallel rather than picking one channel.

Do you need an agent for an FDE job change?

An agent is not required for an FDE job change, but since FDE is a role whose job titles vary, using one alongside other methods improves search efficiency.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a job title popularized by Palantir Technologies for engineers who work deeply on-site with customers (Palantir's official careers page). In Japan, few companies recruit under the name "FDE," and in reality, similar roles are often posted under other names such as "Solutions Engineer," "Customer Engineer," "Implementation Engineer," or "AI Implementation Consultant."

For this reason, simply searching for "FDE" on job boards will only surface a portion of the relevant openings. The value of an agent lies in their ability to find jobs whose titles don't match by looking at the actual work content.

Who should use an agent, and who doesn't need one

If you're unsure about translating job titles or negotiating salary, an agent is a good fit; if you already know where you want to apply, direct application is sufficient.

The earlier the stage—where the desire to "become an FDE" comes first and you haven't yet sorted out which of your experiences will be valued—the more a third-party perspective helps.

How do you choose an agent strong in FDE job changes?

Choose an FDE job change agent not based on claims of being "FDE-strong," but on the agent's technical understanding and track record supporting client-facing roles.

FDE work combines software development, data integration, understanding the customer's business, and stakeholder coordination. Its evaluation criteria differ from both pure development roles and consulting roles. For this reason, judging by "Can this agent explain FDE work?" rather than the agency's brand helps prevent mismatches.

5 checkpoints to assess in the meeting

In the first meeting for an FDE job change, checking the following 5 points will let you roughly tell whether the agent understands FDE.

  • Can they explain FDE work in their own words?: See whether they give a concrete explanation like "a role that advances development and business improvement together on the customer's site."
  • Do they proactively suggest jobs under other names?: Check whether they bring up openings with different names, such as Solutions Engineer or Implementation Engineer.
  • Can they hold a conversation about tech stacks?: Check whether discussions about Python, SQL, data pipelines, cloud, and LLM usage connect well.
  • Do they push you to apply to many jobs?: Agents who focus only on application counts may be taking the fit of the role lightly.
  • Whether they have information on what happens to people after they join: Recruiters who can talk about "what kinds of projects new hires work on after joining" tend to have deep relationships with companies.

How to identify client-site placement jobs that call themselves "FDE"

Some FDE job postings are in reality close to SES-style client-site placement, and you can tell them apart by asking about the contract type and who gives work instructions.

How can you use an FDE recruiting agent effectively?

To make the most of an FDE recruiting agent, organize your career history around "technology × client outcomes" before registering, and give the recruiter the keywords to search for.

Agents search for jobs based on the information you provide. If you only say "I'm interested in FDE," you tend to get referred to generic development roles. The more material you prepare and hand over yourself, the more accurate the referrals become.

Write your resume around "technology × client outcomes"

In FDE selection processes, what matters is not what you built but "whose problems you changed, and how, using technology," so write in that order.

ItemExample of how to write it (structure only)
Client / usersWho the work was for, such as an internal sales department or a manufacturing site of a business partner
Probleme.g., totals were compiled manually, data was scattered across departments
Technical approachLanguages used, data platforms, systems integrated
Coordination with stakeholdersWho you gathered requirements from, how you reached agreement
ResultsInclude actual measured figures if available (do not write estimated numbers)

Experience in contract development, in-house engineering, customer success, data analysis, and similar roles becomes easier to convey as "FDE-like experience" when rewritten in this format.

Give the recruiter keywords for reinterpreting job postings

Because FDE-equivalent jobs are scattered across different titles, give the recruiter multiple job titles and have them search based on the job description conditions.

Even with the same job title, whether the actual work leans toward pre-sales or development varies by company. In interviews, verify by asking about "weekly time allocation" and "proportion of coding."

How to proceed from selection to offer

Working with an agent through the FDE selection process in five steps, from self-analysis to negotiating conditions, prevents information mismatches.

  • Self-analysis: Summarize your career into 3-5 "technology × customer outcomes" stories
  • First meeting: Assess the agent using 5 checkpoints and hand them a keyword sheet
  • Screening job postings: For referred jobs, confirm through the recruiter "the proportion of time spent at client sites" and "development discretion."
  • Interview preparation: Ask in advance whether there are case interviews (interviews where you solve client problems together) or coding tests.
  • Negotiating conditions: Confirm the frequency of business trips and on-site placement, and how performance is evaluated, just as you would salary.

How is an FDE career change different from moving to SWE, consulting, or SIer roles?

An FDE career change differs from moving to SWE, IT consulting, PdM, or SIer roles in that it tests both your development skills and your understanding of the customer's business.

Comparison by role and trade-offs

FDEs are closer to customers than SWEs, write code themselves unlike IT consultants, and are closer to their own company's accountability than SIer roles.

Comparison targetMain difference from FDEExperience valued in career changesDetailed comparison
SWE (Software Engineer)FDEs get involved from problem definition at the customer's siteDevelopment experience interacting directly with usersDifference between FDE and engineers
IT consultantFDEs not only propose but also implement themselvesExperience handling everything from proposal to implementationDifference between FDE and consultants
PdMFDEs focus on deployment for individual customers, while PdMs focus on overall product directionExperience feeding individual customer issues back into the productDifference between FDE and product managers
SIerDifferent contract and accountability structuresExperience consistently handling everything from requirements definition to deploymentDifference between FDE and SIer

Who an FDE career change is not suited for

FDEs spend a high proportion of their time at customer sites, so it may not suit people who want to focus solely on development or who want to avoid business trips and on-site assignments.

  • People who want to maximize their coding time rather than coordinate with customers
  • People who want to avoid business trips and work at customer sites (always check the frequency for each job posting)
  • People who want to start building only after specifications are finalized
  • People who want to focus on proposals and planning without doing development

Even if you feel it's not a good fit, after confirming the role in What is an FDE engineer, you have the option of gradually transitioning from a similar role.

Searching directly on company career pages and LinkedIn

For job openings close to the original FDE model, it's most reliable to search for English job titles on official career pages of companies like Palantir and on LinkedIn.

On the official Palantir Technologies career page, positions such as "Forward Deployed Software Engineer" are posted, and you can filter by location including Japan. Overseas, AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic have also posted jobs under the title Forward Deployed Engineer (OpenAI's FDE, Anthropic's FDE). Since applications can only be completed on each company's official page, keep a record of which routes you've applied through to avoid duplicate applications alongside agent-based ones. On LinkedIn, search for "Forward Deployed," set the location to "Japan," and register an alert so you don't miss new openings.

Getting in through referrals, communities, or side jobs

Connecting with current FDEs lets you learn about the actual work that doesn't appear in job postings, and can also lead to applying through referrals (employee introductions).

Attending study groups on data engineering and LLM applications, participating in technical conferences, and publishing your own content are methods that take time but reliably pay off. If you gain experience in projects close to customer sites through side jobs or contract work, you can also build up achievements in "technology × customer outcomes" that you can write on your resume (Can freelance FDEs work).

Rather than limiting yourself to just one of agents, direct applications, or networking, pursuing all three in parallel increases your chances of finding an FDE position that suits you. When comparing potential employers, it's easier to decide by using FDE Soken's comparison criteria: "which customers, what kinds of problems, and at what distance you can engage with them" and "the proportion of time spent writing code."
