The FDE Seat: Forward Deployed Engineering

Falkster.AI · 2026-11-15 · 8 min read

Summary

This essay frames the forward deployed engineer as a product builder working from the services side, arguing that value lies in closing the loop from customer deployment to adoption and back into the roadmap. It proposes that FDE organizations report to the CPO, with dotted lines to sales and engineering, and covers practices like pre-build evals, written handback packets, and scaling by reducing engineers per deployment. It cites OpenAI, Anthropic, and Palantir examples, noting only about 2,000 of 17,000 US FDEs deliver real ROI while demand for the role grew 2,100%.

Key points

  • The FDE is a product builder, not a sales or services role.
  • The deployment-to-product loop needs one owner under the CPO.
  • Evals are written before building, with customer experts labeling cases.
  • Reporting to sales turns FDEs into sales engineers within two quarters.
  • Scaling means fewer engineers per deployment, not more hires.

Part 14

Palantir Can't Satiate Demand: What Breaks in the FDE Org at 100

Palantir says it 'cannot satiate demand' and must re-resource. Five things break between the tenth FDE and the hundredth, and what the inventor did instead.

  • Who owns the deployment agent when agents do the forward deployed build?Product, under the CPO. On November 10, 2026, IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise with a fleet of more than 6,000 AI agents and assets and forward-deployed engineers attached for qualifying engagements. Microsoft's Kevin Shatzkamer describes FDE teams augmented with coding assistants and deployment agents, and ServiceNow's chief product officer, Amit Zavery, said on October 28, 2026 that the company is building AI implementation agents that do a lot of the heavy lifting at go-live. Software that every deployment runs is a product, so it needs a product manager, a backlog, and a quality bar. The human forward deployed engineer keeps four decisions: the choice of workflow, the judgment of the customer's senior people, the bar for go-live, and the handback to the roadmap. Falk's number for the field is human hours per go-live, sorted into decision hours and gap hours, where every gap hour is a bug report against the deployment agent.
  • Did the 2026 midterms reject AI?No. The 2026 midterms rejected the bill for AI infrastructure, not AI. Voters in ten states decided 41 data center measures and went against development in 40 of them; only Harwood, North Dakota, said yes. NBC's exit poll put AI and data centers as a crucial or major factor for 56% of voters, but the AP Voter Poll found only about a quarter called it crucial, behind cost of living and corruption. The verdict was on who pays for power and what a town gets for hosting the building. Falk's read: that priced the compute moat and moved the scarce resource to adoption, which is a product problem.
  • Should forward deployed engineers report to the CPO?Yes. The reporting line is the scorecard, and the scorecard decides what the field produces: bookings under sales, utilization under services, a clean harness under engineering. None of those is the reason to put an engineer inside a customer's building. The reason is that a deployment is the product's fastest signal about what to build next, and the FDE's real output at the end of one is a decision about the product, a workflow that should be a feature, an eval the roadmap should be held to, a customer's process that is the design spec. Only the function that owns the roadmap can act on that in hours instead of quarters. On the Q3 2026 earnings calls of NICE (November 4), ServiceNow (October 28), and Palantir (November 2), the FDE team appeared with engagements, time to value, and demand, and no number about what the field contributed to the product, which is the tell. Falk's rule: the FDE org reports to the CPO, with dotted lines to sales for the promise and to engineering for the harness, and a scorecard of generalization rate, roadmap items that started in a deployment, and days from field finding to roadmap decision.
  • What breaks when a forward deployed engineering team scales?Five things, in the order they usually show up. The handback stops being read, because one product manager can read ten packets a year and nobody reads two hundred. The engineers end up on islands, because the manager and tech lead layer arrives late. The maintenance tail eats the team, because shipped workflows stay attached to whoever shipped them, and in Milos Mandic's State of FDE 2026 survey twelve of 24 FDEs already spend more than a tenth of their week on delivered systems. The reporting line fragments, because a new region gets stood up under sales. And the bench appears, because the plan was written in headcount and demand arrives in deployments. Palantir's Q3 2026 call, where Alex Karp said the company cannot satiate demand and has to re-resource because it lacks the bandwidth, shows the alternative: 686 US commercial customers, revenue per customer up 88 percent, pilots converting at 5x to 15x, contracts starting at $25 million, and a platform that keeps what the engineers built. An FDE org scales by needing fewer engineers per deployment, not by hiring the hundredth.
  • What did agent harnesses change for forward deployed engineers?They moved the forward deployed engineer's deliverable up a layer. Anthropic's S-1, filed October 26, 2026, defines Applied AI teams as the ones that include forward-deployed engineers and says a model alone is not an agent: the harness is the secure environment, credentials, memory, and orchestration loop, and building it was, until recently, work each customer took on itself. The filing now sells that harness as the Agent SDK, the same one that powers Claude Code, and as Claude Managed Agents, which Anthropic operates. So the engineer on site no longer writes the loop, the retries, or the credential handling. What is left is what the filing says is still missing: connectors that do not yet exist, governance frameworks that are not yet written, orchestration logic specific to the customer, and the skills that turn institutional knowledge into agent capabilities. The FDE's output became a skill, a connector, and a row set rather than a repository, which is cheaper to hand back and easier to generalize. The tool also arrives first: the filing says Claude is often already in use by the time its direct teams engage a customer, so week one is an audit of what is running rather than an install. And one thing the tooling did not change. A prompt is code with nothing checking it, as Lesson 08 of Becoming Forward Deployed Engineer put it the same day. Changes got cheap. Verification stayed the FDE's job, and the eval on every change is how it gets done.
  • Who writes the eval in a forward deployed engineering deployment?The forward deployed engineer writes it, before building, and the customer's domain experts supply the labels. Colin Jarvis's October 22, 2026 account of OpenAI's FDE team has the engineers building evals with the customer's engineers before building the system, and a skill for writing fresh evals by the time a rebuilt deployment reached the next team. Saurabh Singh's method in the Vibe Engines handbook is the first-week version: two or three of the customer's experts label about 40 real cases independently, the disagreements become a written rubric, and the rubric becomes the labeling guide, the LLM-judge prompt, and the acceptance language in the statement of work. The eval is the deployment artifact. It is a frozen, versioned set of the customer's real cases with a target set against the human baseline, an error budget for the mistakes that are forbidden at any rate, the judge's agreement rate with human graders, and cost and latency limits. It travels into the handback to product, rides on the harness pull request, becomes the customer's independence test at month two, and is the customer's property in the contract. The product manager writes a second eval later, the one the generalized version has to pass, from the same rows. Aneel Bhusri's standard for AI running payroll, that almost right isn't good enough, only means something if someone wrote down the number for almost.
