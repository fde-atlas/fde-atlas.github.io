Palantir Consulting & Implementation Services

Full text

Independent Palantir implementation consultancy. Foundry, AIP, and ontology work delivered by forward deployed engineers, with security configured before go-live.

Definition

Palantir consulting helps enterprises plan, deploy, and scale Palantir's platforms: Foundry for data and operations, AIP for AI-driven workflows, and the ontology that connects both to how the business actually runs. BD Emerson fields forward deployed engineers who build alongside your teams, plus the security and governance practitioners who make the deployment defensible to CISOs, auditors, and regulators. Engagements are shaped by your data landscape, use cases, security requirements, and operating model.

Independence

BD Emerson builds on Palantir Foundry and AIP as an implementation team and is part of Andersen Consulting. Your production license stays a direct contract between you and Palantir. Before you sign one, we can build a proof of value on Andersen Consulting's Foundry environment, so the first use case runs on your data ahead of the license decision.

We describe our Palantir work by what our engineers ship: ontology models, pipelines, Workshop applications, and AIP agents running in your environment. We claim no partner tier, badge, or certification from Palantir. Ask for the specific builds and the people who did them, and judge the capability on that.

Palantir licensing stays between you and Palantir. We never resell seats or platform capacity, and we take no margin on what you pay them. You pay us for engineering time and deliverables, so our incentive runs toward the smallest platform footprint that does the job.

No license quota shapes our advice. When Foundry is the wrong tool for a workload, we say so and point you at the cheaper path, including tools you already own. That holds inside a Foundry program too, where we will argue against building a use case that an existing report or warehouse already handles.

Services

Engagement models

Five entry points, each with a defined output and a decision at the end. Scope, source system count, and ontology complexity drive what any of them costs.

Working sessions with your operators and data owners produce a first ontology: object types, link types, properties, and the source system behind each one. You leave with the model documented, a mapped inventory of source systems, and a ranked list of use cases the model can support. Cost tracks the number of source systems and how contested the definitions are across teams. The model is yours whether or not we do the build.

One use case taken from source data to an application people use in their daily job. The arc covers pipeline builds, ontology objects, a Workshop app, and the access controls that decide who sees which rows. Duration depends on how many source systems feed the use case and how much cleanup the data needs before it lands. Your team pairs with our engineers through the build, so your staff can maintain the pipelines and the app at the end.

An AIP agent scoped to a task your team already does by hand, wired to the ontology so it reads real objects instead of loose documents. We build the retrieval, the tool calls, the evaluation set, and the guardrails that keep the agent inside approved data. Cost follows the number of tools the agent needs and how strict your review and logging requirements are. You own the agent configuration, the evaluation set, and the test results that show how it behaves.

A standing block of engineering hours each month, staffed by engineers who sit with your operators and build against their workflow. Use it for a backlog of small builds, ontology changes as the business shifts, or holding a platform steady after a first arc. Cost is a function of how many engineers you reserve and how many environments they support. Work is tracked per build, and every pipeline, object, and application stays in your instance.

A review of a Foundry or AIP deployment against how it is actually configured: markings, access policies, group membership, external connections, and audit logging. You get findings ranked by exposure, the configuration changes that close them, and an evidence pack mapping controls to the framework you report against. Environment count and whether an air-gapped deployment is in scope drive the effort. The pack is yours to hand an auditor, a customer, or your board.

Our approach

01 We pick the first use case by business value and data readiness, not platform ambition. A deployed workflow in production beats a reference architecture on a slide.

02 Forward deployed engineers pair with your operators and engineers from the first sprint. Every build transfers skills, so the platform grows internal owners instead of external dependencies.

03 Access controls, markings, lineage, and audit are configured before the first user logs in. Security and governance are part of the architecture, not a hardening phase.

04 Once the first use case carries weight in production, we industrialize: more pipelines, more applications, AIP agents on the ontology, and an operating model that holds it together.

05 Control narratives, access decisions, and lineage are captured while the build happens. When the first evidence request arrives, the answer already exists.

06 The first use case pays for the ontology. Every use case after it reuses the same objects, links, and permissions, so the second build costs less than the first.

07 Engagements are designed to end. Your engineers own the pipelines, the ontology, and the applications, with runbooks and training to match.

Our Advantage

Our practitioners build in Foundry and AIP alongside your team, in your data, against your operating problems. Delivery is measured in working software, not workshops.

BD Emerson runs security, privacy, and compliance programs for a living. Your Palantir deployment inherits that: hardened configuration, governed access, and evidence that satisfies auditors and enterprise customers.

We carry no reseller quota, so our advice follows the workload. Where Palantir fits, we deploy it well. Where it does not, we say so and design the alternative.

BD Emerson does not resell Palantir licenses and takes no margin on your platform spend. You buy the software from Palantir and delivery from us. That separation is why our advice on scope and sequencing carries no commercial bias.

Public sector

Palantir programs in federal and defense work run on two clocks: the platform build and the authorization package. We handle implementation and readiness on both, and we do not perform assessments ourselves.

We prepare a system for FedRAMP authorization: boundary definition, control implementation, the SSP and supporting documents, and the remediation work that comes out of a gap review. BD Emerson does implementation and readiness only and is not a 3PAO, so an independent assessor performs the audit. Effort tracks the impact level you target, the size of the boundary, and how much of the control set already operates. You keep the documentation package and the control evidence.

We scope where CUI lives, assess the NIST 800-171 controls against how your systems are configured today, and produce a gap assessment with an SPRS score and a POA&M. BD Emerson is not a C3PAO, so a certified assessor conducts the certification assessment. Effort depends on how many systems and enclaves fall in scope and whether CUI can be narrowed to a smaller boundary. You end up with the SSP, the POA&M, and the evidence a C3PAO will ask to see.
