Can You Become an FDE as a New Grad? The Reality of New Graduate Hiring and What to Prepare Before Joining

Is FDE a role that new graduates can get into?

It is possible to become an FDE as a new graduate. However, openings are limited, and many companies prioritize hiring experienced mid-career professionals.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role that embeds at the customer's site and single-handedly handles everything from problem discovery to implementation and operational adoption. It became widely known when Palantir popularized the title "Forward Deployed Software Engineer." In recent years, similar positions have been spreading at generative AI companies and data infrastructure companies.

New-grad hiring is "narrow, but not closed"

Only a limited number of companies offer new-grad positions, but Palantir, the origin of the FDE role, has a track record of recruiting for bachelor's and master's degree holders.

Palantir positions FDE as a role to "develop," and is known for its culture of hiring new graduates as FDEs and training them in the field. On the other hand, most companies that newly established FDE roles seek engineers with customer-facing experience who can contribute immediately. As a result, new-grad openings are still a minority. Since hiring conditions change year by year, check each company's recruiting page for the latest information.

The difference from mid-career hiring lies in "expectations" and "development period"

New-grad FDEs are not expected to be immediately productive; rather, they are valued for learning speed and adaptability in the field. For this reason, the first one to two years are generally treated as a development period.

PerspectiveNew-grad FDEMid-career FDE
Main expectationsLearning speed, intelligence, opennessImmediate productivity, track record in customer negotiations
Initial scope of workAccompanying senior FDEs on projects, handling parts of themOwning projects independently or as lead
Evaluation criteriaGrowth curve, ability to absorb technologyCustomer outcomes, ability to complete projects
Hiring focusCoding tests, case interviewsWork history, past implementation cases

From my perspective as a practitioner, FDE work starts from a state of "having technical skills but not knowing the customer's business." Business understanding can only be gained within actual projects. My honest assessment is that being a new graduate is not a decisive disadvantage.

Which companies hire new-grad FDEs?

The most prominent example of new-grad FDE hiring is Palantir. Other AI and data companies are mostly mid-career-focused, with new-grad openings tending to be limited.

Companies recruiting new graduates under the job title "FDE" are still rare both in Japan and abroad. However, new-grad positions that effectively do FDE-like work exist under different job titles. Rather than judging by the title alone, it's important to carefully read the job description.

Trends among companies hiring new grads under the FDE job title

New-grad FDE hiring is concentrated in companies like Palantir that place FDEs at the core of their organization. In Japan, some years have new-grad openings and others do not.

Companies conducting FDE-style hiring can be broadly divided into the following three types.

Company typeNew-grad hiring trendsRepresentative examples and characteristics
FDE-origin typeTend to offer new-grad openingsPalantir (has a track record of recruiting under the FDSE title)
Generative AI / LLM companiesMid-career-focused, limited new-grad openingsA growing movement to establish FDE roles responsible for supporting customer implementation
Domestic SaaS / DX companiesDifferent titles but similar roles exist"Implementation Engineer," "Solutions Engineer," etc.

The presence and number of new-grad openings at specific companies change every year. Rather than making definitive claims here, I recommend checking primary sources such as the recruiting pages of your target companies and internship postings.

Similar positions that don't use the "FDE" title are also worth targeting

If you look for roles that handle implementation on-site with customers without fixating on the "FDE" title, your options as a new graduate expand significantly.

In Japan, the following job titles often involve roles close to FDE.

  • Implementation Engineer / Onboarding Engineer (providing technical support for SaaS customer onboarding)
  • Solutions Engineer / Sales Engineer (handling technical validation from the proposal stage)
  • Customer Success Engineer (supporting post-implementation usage and adoption from a technical standpoint)
  • In-house DX Promotion Engineer (solving operational challenges at companies through systems)

In businesses like VACAN, which implement and operate their own products tailored to customer sites, engineers naturally have opportunities to work in the field. In job postings, checking two points—whether you "interact directly with customers" and whether you "do the implementation yourself"—rather than the job title, makes it easier to judge how close the role is to FDE.

What skills are required of new-grad FDEs?

New-grad FDEs need, in addition to foundational software development skills, the ability to structure ambiguous problems and the ability to communicate with customers.

FDE selection processes assess not only coding ability but also the thinking skills to discern "what the customer is truly struggling with." Palantir's selection process is known to include case-style interviews in addition to coding interviews.

For technical skills, "broad and shallow, plus one deep area" is the basic approach

At the new-grad stage, a profile that broadly covers the fundamentals of data processing, web development, and cloud, while having one area of expertise, tends to be well evaluated.

Skill areaGuideline at new-grad stageReason
Programming (Python / TypeScript, etc.)Able to build a small app independentlyThere are many situations requiring quick prototyping in the field
SQL and data processingCan write joins and aggregations on your ownUnderstanding customer data is the starting point for everything
Cloud and infrastructure basicsUnderstands the deployment processDeployment work in customer environments will be required
Algorithms and data structuresCan handle coding testsFrequently assessed in selection processes
Using LLMs and AI toolsSimple implementation experience using APIsDemand is growing in recent FDE projects

Non-technical skills often make the difference between pass and fail

In FDE selection, what distinguishes candidates with similar technical skills is the ability to articulate problems and the attitude of thinking from the other party's perspective.

What I often see in the field is that the "features customers say they want" and their "real underlying problem" frequently don't align. New graduates aren't expected to have perfect business knowledge. However, the attitude of honestly asking "why is this necessary?" tends to be highly valued both in interviews and on the job.

  • Problem structuring: Able to break vague requests down into small, verifiable questions
  • Communication skills: Able to explain technical decisions in terms non-engineers can understand
  • Ownership: Doesn't limit their scope of responsibility; proactively takes on whatever is needed to achieve results
  • Stress tolerance: Can operate assuming spec changes and on-site confusion

What should students aiming to become FDEs as new graduates prepare?

What you should do while in school is gain experience "building something and having people use it," and prepare for case interviews. Track record matters more than certifications.

An FDE is a role that "delivers things that work in the customer's environment." Preparing by accumulating experiences close to this is the most effective approach. Beyond class assignments, consciously seek out development experience with real users.

A roadmap for experiences to build while in school

Preparing in stages by school year keeps things manageable. The general guideline is to solidify fundamentals first, then develop for real users, and finally move on to selection preparation.

  • Building fundamentals (roughly 1st-2nd year of undergrad): Learn Python and SQL, and complete one small web application from start to finish
  • Development for real users (roughly 2nd-3rd year of undergrad): Solve familiar business problems at a lab, club, or part-time job with a tool, and have people actually use it
  • Internships (roughly 3rd year of undergrad / 1st year of grad school): Join a long-term internship that involves customer interaction or deployment support
  • Selection preparation (job-hunting period): Practice for coding tests and case interviews in parallel

The second experience in particular, "solving familiar business problems," is a microcosm of FDE work itself. Being able to concretely describe "whose problem you solved, how, and what the results were" in interviews becomes a strength.

How to prepare for case interviews and your portfolio

In case interviews, the thought process matters more than the correct answer. Organizing your portfolio around three points—problem, solution, and results—makes it easy to convey.

Case interviews may present questions with no single correct answer, such as "what data would you collect to reduce wait times at a hospital?" Rather than jumping straight to a solution, be mindful of the flow: confirm assumptions through questions, form a hypothesis, and show how you would verify it.

If you organize your portfolio as follows, interviewers can evaluate it more easily.

ItemWhat to write
ProblemWho was struggling, and in what situation
SolutionReasons for technology choices, and alternatives considered and discarded
ResultsUser reactions, work reduced, areas for improvement
LearningsWhat you would change if you did it again

What are the benefits and risks of becoming an FDE as a new graduate?

The biggest benefit of being a new-graduate FDE is the speed of growth. On the other hand, there are risks: your specialization can become vague, and the workload can be heavy.

An FDE is a rare position that touches both technology and business. However, choosing FDE early in your career involves both gains and losses. It's important to understand both before joining.

The benefits: early acquisition of "business understanding" and "market value"

Working as an FDE from the start of your career lets you understand both customer operations and technology while young, broadening your career options.

  • Early business understanding: Learn customer workflows and decision-making structures directly in the field
  • Visible results: See up close how your implementations change customers' operations
  • Many career paths: Product manager, solution architect, entrepreneurship, and more—the next steps are wide open
  • Market scarcity: People with experience in both technology and customer relations are increasingly valued as demand for AI adoption grows

The risks: "shallow specialization" and "heavy workload"

Because FDEs cover a wide range of responsibilities, it can be hard to find time to deepen any specific technology. The mental strain of customer interactions is also not insignificant.

RiskDescriptionExample countermeasures
Vague specializationHandling everything broadly tends to make your technical skills shallowPick one "technology to deepen" per project and keep a record
Heavy workloadEasily at the mercy of customer deadlines and changing requirementsCheck travel frequency and actual working conditions before joining
Hard-to-see evaluationYour results tend to get buried on the customer sideBuild the habit of documenting your contributions with numbers and examples
Differences in training systemsSome companies leave you to OJT aloneAsk in interviews about mentor systems and shadowing periods

To add one point from a practitioner's perspective: I feel the success of a new-graduate FDE depends less on the individual's ability than on the project they're first assigned to and the quality of senior FDEs. In the selection process, try asking specifically "what will I do in my first six months after joining?" It's a good clue for judging whether the company has a solid training system.
