How to Verify the Authenticity of an FDE Certificate? Query Portals, Verification Methods, and Reasons Why Results May Not Be Found Across the Four Systems

Original title: FDE 证书怎么查真伪？四套体系的查询入口、验证方法、查不到的原因

FDE 指南 · 2026-10-26 · 4 min read

Summary

This guide explains that there is no unified verification platform for FDE certificates, since four issuing bodies—the MIIT Education and Examination Center, the MIIT Talent Exchange Center, Tencent Cloud, and TIIDA × DAMA Greater China—each maintain separate databases. It lists each system's lookup method, notes that the Education and Examination Center requires certificate number, name, and ID number together, and offers a three-step verification process: check the issuer's full name on the certificate itself, distinguish certificate types (none of the four are state-listed vocational qualifications), and verify numbers only on the issuer's official site rather than via links from salespeople. It also covers six reasons a lookup may fail and warns against guaranteed-pass and job-salary claims.

Key points

  • No unified FDE certificate lookup exists; four issuers run separate databases.
  • MIIT Education and Examination Center requires number, name, and ID together.
  • Verify by checking issuer name, certificate type, and official-site lookup.
  • None of the four FDE certificates are state-listed vocational qualifications.
  • Six possible reasons for failed lookups; forgery is the last consideration.

First, remember one thing

There is no unified verification platform for FDE certificates.

That's because the issuing bodies are not one entity. CFDE is an international professional organization, the two certificates from units under the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology belong to two different centers, and Tencent Cloud is a vendor certification. The four systems each maintain their own databases and their own verification channels—there is no such thing as "one entry point to verify all FDE certificates."

Any page claiming "we can verify all FDE certificates in one place" deserves suspicion first.

The Education and Examination Center's verification page requires all three items to be entered simultaneously; missing any one will return no results. This design itself is also a verification point: if a "verification page" lets you retrieve a certificate by entering only a name, it is most likely not official.

The verification entry points for the other three organizations change as pages are redesigned, so this article does not provide specific URLs to avoid misleading readers with broken links. The judgment method is below.

A three-step verification method

For any certificate you receive, follow these three steps; in two minutes you can rule out the vast majority of problems.

Step 1: Check the full name of the issuing body printed on the certificate

Don't look at what the promotional page says—look at the signature printed on the certificate itself.

Focus on distinguishing the two most easily confused pairs:

  • Talent Exchange Center of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology vs. Education and Examination Center of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology—these are two different organizations; their certificates are not interchangeable, and their verification entry points differ
  • "Ministry of Industry and Information Technology" vs. "a certain center of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology"—the former is the ministry itself, the latter is a subordinate public institution. If the certificate's signature only says "MIIT" without a specific organization, it is not standard practice

Step 2: Check how the certificate's nature is described

Legitimate certificates clearly state their nature. There are three common types of descriptions:

  • Position competency evaluation—evaluates whether you possess the competencies for a certain position; this is a proficiency evaluation
  • Training certificate / completion certificate—certifies that you completed a certain training program
  • Vocational qualification certificate—this term is strictly restricted; only qualifications listed in the National Vocational Qualification Catalog may use it

The first two types are not vocational qualifications. Of the four publicly verifiable FDE certificate systems, none is an access-type vocational qualification. If a salesperson describes an "FDE certificate" as a "national vocational qualification," that is a clearly incorrect statement.

Step 3: Verify the number on the issuing body's official website

After obtaining the certificate number, verify it on the issuing body's own official website—do not click verification links sent by salespeople.

A link sent by a salesperson may be a counterfeit verification page that displays "verification passed" for any number. The judgment method is simple: go to the official website yourself via a search engine or by typing the domain directly, find "certificate verification," and enter the number again. Only matching results from both sides count.

If verification fails, it's usually one of these six reasons

After ruling out input errors, failure to verify is generally due to one of the following situations:

1. It's a completion certificate issued by a training institution, not a certificate from the issuing body. Training institutions and issuing bodies are two different things. A certificate printed by an institution itself can only be verified in that institution's own system.

2. The number is an internal institution number. Some certificates have numbers, but those are training class student IDs or order numbers, not the issuing body's certificate numbers.

3. The issuing body's name is similar but different. Many organizations have names containing "MIIT," "talent," or "education"—a one-character difference means a different organization.

4. The certificate has not yet been entered into the database. Some systems take time to sync to the verification system after an exam is passed; this delay ranges from a few days to a few weeks.

5. The verification entry point itself has been redesigned. Websites of subordinate units under ministries are redesigned fairly often; broken old links are the norm and do not indicate a problem with the certificate.

6. The certificate is fake. This should be considered last, but if the first five possibilities have been ruled out, it must be taken seriously.

Three tips to avoid pitfalls

Beware of "guaranteed pass." Anyone promising a "guaranteed pass" or "certificate without taking the exam" should be avoided, no matter who the issuing body claims to be. Legitimate exams always carry the possibility of failure.

Beware of "certificate means high salary." A certificate proves you studied and passed an exam; it does not prove you can do the work. Those who bundle certificates with employment promises are selling anxiety, not capability.

Beware of "taking more exams is safer." The content of the four systems overlaps, and the marginal benefit of holding multiple certificates is very low. Choosing the one that best matches your actual needs is more useful than collecting them like stamps.

Next steps

If you haven't decided which system to pursue yet, first read "How Many Types of FDE Certification Are There", which lays out the differences among the four systems point by point; if you're already weighing whether it's worth it, read "The Value of FDE Certification".
