Forward Pricing Model for Healthcare FDE Contracts

FWD Deployment · 2026-11-09 · 8 min read

Summary

This guide explains how to structure pricing for healthcare forward deployed engineering contracts, recommending a hybrid model: capped time-and-materials (T&M) for work that cannot yet be estimated, fixed prices for definable work, and payments tied to acceptance tests. Drawing on FAR Part 16 and DFARS 216.601, it shows that every T&M rate consists of wages, overhead, G&A expenses, and profit, and provides a template for rate proposals. It includes sample milestone language for HL7/FHIR integrations and HIPAA gates based on NIST SP 800-66 Rev. 2, covering ePHI that is created, received, maintained, or transmitted. Story points should be converted into hours or acceptance milestones before entering contracts, and no universal price exists for HIPAA compliance work. The article is general information, not legal advice.

Key points

  • Use a hybrid model: capped T&M for unknown work, fixed prices for definable work.
  • T&M rates are built from wages, overhead, G&A expenses, and profit.
  • FHIR milestones should specify interface, environment, data volume, and acceptance signer.
  • HIPAA gates should require evidence of controls for ePHI in all four handling verbs.
  • Convert story points into fixed-rate hours or acceptance milestones before contracting.

The safest way to structure a forward pricing model for a healthcare forward deployed engineering (FDE) contract is a hybrid. Use time and materials (T&M) only for work you can't yet estimate, set fixed prices for the pieces you can define, and pay against acceptance tests. Under Federal Acquisition Regulation Part 16, T&M pays direct labor hours at specified fixed hourly rates plus the actual cost of materials, and it is not a fixed-price contract.

This guide shows what to ask for in a rate proposal, how to compare the three models, and where to put HL7/FHIR and HIPAA gates in the payment schedule. It is general information, not legal advice, so have counsel read the final clauses.

What does a T&M contract actually pay for?

A T&M contract pays for hours worked at fixed hourly rates, plus the actual cost of materials. That comes from FAR Part 16. Your risk is that hours can keep running, because the price is not fixed.

The same regulation says T&M may be used when the customer cannot accurately estimate the work's extent or duration, or anticipate costs with reasonable confidence. For a health system, that describes discovery on a legacy interface engine or an undocumented EHR customization.

If you can write down the interface list, the data volumes and the test plan, you are no longer in that situation. Price that work differently.

How do you keep T&M from taking over the contract?

Cap it and shrink it. DFARS 216.601 calls for structuring T&M requirements to minimize their use, including by limiting the value or duration of the T&M portion and establishing fixed prices for portions of the requirement.

The same section says indefinite-delivery contracts authorizing T&M orders should also authorize cost-reimbursement, incentive, or fixed-price orders to the maximum extent practicable. Apply that to a master services agreement: let it allow T&M statements of work for discovery and fixed-price statements of work for defined builds.

A practical structure:

  • Phase 1, discovery: T&M with a stated dollar ceiling and an end date.
  • Phase 2, defined interfaces: fixed price per interface or per group of interfaces.
  • Phase 3, security and compliance work: milestone payments against evidence you can inspect.

What goes into a forward pricing rate proposal?

Ask the firm to show how each hourly rate is built. The federal sources agree on the components, and that gives you a clean template for any rate sheet.

Source What the labor rate includes Where it applies
FAR 16.601 Wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit T&M pricing
GSA Alliant 2 Wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit T&M and labor-hour orders only
GSA VETS 2 Direct labor cost, overhead, general and administrative expenses, fringe benefits, and profit T&M and labor-hour orders only; excludes the 0.75 percent contract access fee

Alliant 2 also supports fixed-price, cost-reimbursement, labor-hour, T&M and hybrid contract types, which shows that hybrid structures are a normal procurement choice.

The sources reviewed do not give market rates for FDE teams, so the dollar figures come from your negotiation. Ask for a proposal with these columns for each role:

  • Role (for example, integration engineer, security engineer, engagement lead)
  • Fixed hourly rate for the full term
  • Build-up: wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, profit
  • Estimated hours and a not-to-exceed amount
  • Fees or pass-through costs listed separately, like the VETS 2 access fee

To calculate a proposal for an integration project, multiply estimated hours by the fixed rate for each role, add the cost of materials, and set the total as the ceiling. The hours depend on your EHR, your interface count, your data volumes and your environments.

How do T&M, fixed scope and outcome-based pricing compare?

Model What you pay for Best used when Main risk
T&M Hours at fixed rates plus actual materials You can't accurately estimate extent or duration Cost grows with hours unless you cap value or duration
Fixed scope A set price for a defined portion of the work You can define the deliverable and its tests Disputes over what the defined scope includes
Outcome-based A result you define and measure You can name a metric both sides can verify A badly defined metric pays for the wrong thing

Outcome-based terms, including HL7/FHIR milestone definitions, acceptance tests, payment percentages and service levels, must be negotiated for your specific EHR, interfaces, data volumes, environments and clinical workflows. For a side-by-side view of a neighboring industry, see FDE pricing models for insurers.

How do you tie payments to HL7/FHIR integration milestones?

Break the integration into units that can pass or fail, then attach a payment to each. Define each unit by interface, environment, data volume and clinical workflow, because those are the variables that change the work.

A milestone definition should name:

  • The interface or resource set covered.
  • The environment where the test runs (test, staging or production).
  • The test data volume and the pass criteria.
  • The person on your side who signs acceptance.
  • The percentage of the milestone fee released on acceptance.

Sample drafting language for your counsel to adapt:

Supplier will deliver the [interface name] in the [environment] in accordance with the Acceptance Test Plan attached as Exhibit [X]. Customer will pay [__] percent of the Milestone Fee within [__] days after written acceptance. Acceptance occurs when all tests in Exhibit [X] pass at the stated volume, or when Customer fails to give written notice of a defect within [__] business days of delivery.

The percentages and day counts are yours to set. None of the sources reviewed fix them.

Can you use story points to price EHR migration work?

Story points are an internal estimation method, not a source-established contractual pricing standard. Let the firm use them to plan sprints.

In the contract, convert the estimate into one of two things: hours at fixed rates under a not-to-exceed cap, or a fixed price per accepted milestone. Either one gives you a unit you can audit.

How do you tie payments to HIPAA compliance benchmarks?

Anchor the gates to the protection objective. NIST SP 800-66 Rev. 2 says the HIPAA Security Rule focuses on safeguarding electronic protected health information (ePHI) held or maintained by regulated entities. It also says covered ePHI includes information created, received, maintained, or transmitted by a regulated entity, and that it must be protected against reasonably anticipated threats, hazards, and impermissible uses or disclosures.

That wording gives you four verbs to test against: created, received, maintained, transmitted. For each, ask the firm to show evidence of the controls that protect the data in your environment. A gate might read:

No Milestone Fee is payable for any deliverable that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits ePHI until Supplier delivers written evidence, reviewed by Customer's security officer, that the controls listed in Exhibit [Y] are in place for that deliverable.

HIPAA compliance is risk- and control-dependent. The NIST material states the objective but does not set a universal price, so price security work by the controls your risk analysis requires. For access setup, see how to securely grant forward deployed engineers access.

Which contract terms protect you from scope creep?

  • Fixed hourly rates for the full term. This matches the T&M definition and stops rate drift.
  • A ceiling on T&M value or duration. This applies the DFARS approach of limiting the T&M portion.
  • Fixed prices for defined portions. Move work out of T&M once you can define it.
  • Written change orders. No hours above the ceiling without your signed approval.
  • Named acceptance tests. Scope is whatever the test plan says, and nothing else.
  • Separate lines for fees. Pass-through charges stay visible.

How do you choose between an embedded team, project delivery and technical leadership?

Match the engagement shape to how well you can define the work, then price each statement of work accordingly. Our comparison of an embedded FDE team and a project delivery team covers the operating differences.

Dplyz is one firm that offers all three ways to work: an embedded team, project delivery or technical leadership. It places senior engineers inside a business to connect data, build products and run AI agents and workflows in production, and it doesn't publish a price list. So request a written rate proposal in the format above. You can compare other firms on the forward deployed engineering firms page.

What should you do before you sign?

  • List the interfaces and mark each as definable or unknown.
  • Put unknown work in a capped T&M phase and definable work in fixed-price milestones.
  • Get a rate build-up with the four components for every role.
  • Write acceptance tests for each FHIR deliverable.
  • Add the ePHI evidence gate to every milestone that touches patient data.
  • Have counsel review the clauses.

Common questions

What is the difference between time and materials vs outcome-based pricing in a forward deployed model?

T&M pays for direct labor hours at fixed hourly rates plus actual materials, so cost follows effort. Outcome-based pricing pays for a result you define, and its metrics and payment percentages must be negotiated for your specific systems.

How do you calculate a forward pricing rate proposal for healthcare data integrations?

Ask for a fixed hourly rate per role built from wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit. Multiply estimated hours by each rate, add materials, and set a not-to-exceed ceiling. The hours depend on your EHR, interfaces, data volumes and environments.

Can you use story points to price forward deployment engineering for EHR migrations?

Story points are an internal estimation method, not a source-established contractual pricing standard. Use them for planning, then convert the estimate into hours at fixed rates or into fixed-price acceptance milestones.

What contract terms protect healthcare systems from scope creep in a forward deployed model?

Fixed hourly rates for the term, a ceiling on T&M value or duration, fixed prices for defined portions, written change orders and named acceptance tests. These follow the federal approach of limiting the T&M portion and fixing prices where possible.

How do forward deployed engineering firms price HIPAA compliance and security workflows?

The NIST material states the protection objective for ePHI but does not set a universal price. Ask each firm to price security work against the controls your risk analysis requires, and tie payment to evidence that those controls are in place.
