LLM-as-judge

FDE Interview Prep · 2026 · 1 min read

Summary

This piece explains LLM-as-judge: using a language model with a rubric, and sometimes a reference answer, to score outputs or choose between two responses. It stresses that judges are models with measurable flaws—Zheng et al. documented position bias, verbosity bias and self-enhancement bias—so a judge's scores are meaningless until validated against human labels. Framed for FDE interviews, it cites Scale AI's Frontier Agents postings requiring evaluation harnesses with LLM judges, and a Hippocratic AI take-home asking candidates to add a judge checking age-appropriateness of children's stories. Strong candidates test judges by swapping answer order and padding answers, report catch rates and kappa with expert labels, use a judge from a different model family, and keep human review after launch.

Key points

  • LLM-as-judge scores outputs or picks winners using a rubric.
  • Zheng et al. found position, verbosity and self-enhancement bias in judges.
  • Scale AI Frontier Agents roles require deploying LLM-judge evaluation harnesses.
  • Hippocratic AI take-home adds a judge checking story age-appropriateness.
  • Validate judges via order swaps, catch rates, kappa, and cross-family models.

Full text

LLM-as-judge means using a language model, given a rubric and often a reference answer, to score outputs or pick the better of two. The judge is a model with its own measurable errors: Zheng et al. found position bias, verbosity bias and self-enhancement bias in model judges, so a judge’s scores mean little until its agreement with human labels has been measured.

In FDE interviews

Scale AI’s Frontier Agents postings ask the engineer to deploy evaluation harnesses that use LLM-as-a-Judge alongside offline benchmarks, online experiments, golden datasets and regression suites. Source 1Frontier Agents Engineer (Forward Deployed Engineering)PublisherScale AI (Greenhouse)Source typecompany job posting One candidate reported, in a repo created in August 2026, that the Hippocratic AI take-home for an AI Deployment Engineer role asked for a program that turns a bedtime-story request into a story suitable for ages 5 to 10 and adds an LLM judge. Source 2Hippocratic AI Coding AssignmentPublisherakshay-menta (GitHub)Source typecandidate’s take-home repository A judge there has to check fit for the audience, not only whether the story is good.

A strong candidate writes a rubric with a pass or fail criterion per dimension and tests the judge by swapping answer order and padding answers. Before anyone relies on its scores, they report how often it catches known failures and how often it passes known-good outputs, or its kappa with expert labels; raw agreement flatters a judge that mostly sees passes. They use a judge from a different model family from the one it grades, and keep people reviewing a sample after launch.
