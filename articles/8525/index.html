Palantir Forward Deployed Software Engineer (4/5): Work that I look forward to every day

Taro · 2014-04-07 · 2 min read

Summary

A 4/5 Glassdoor-style review from a current Palantir Forward Deployed Software Engineer with two years at the company, based in Washington, D.C. The reviewer recommends Palantir and approves of the CEO, citing smart, friendly colleagues, work with real impact, extensive travel opportunities, and flexible hours. Main complaints: plenty of tedious technical grunt work and a risk of landing on boring projects, difficulty standing out among talented peers, no clear internal advancement path, and uneven work/life balance across teams. The advice to management is to fix the advancement problem.

Key points

  • Current FDE with 2 years at Palantir rates it 4/5 and recommends it.
  • Praises intelligent colleagues, impactful work, and flexible scheduling.
  • Travel to cities like San Francisco, London, Sydney, and the UAE is a perk.
  • Downsides include grunt work, unclear advancement, and uneven work/life balance.
  • Reviewer urges management to fix the internal promotion path.

Current Employee

Washington, District of Columbia

Forward Deployed Software Engineer•Current Employee•Worked at Palantir for 2 years

4.0

RecommendsPositive OutlookApproves of CEO

Pros

The people are great, friendly, and very intelligent.

Interesting work that can have a real impact.

Awesome travel opportunities, including San Francisco, Los Angeles, New York City, Washington D.C., London, Sydney, and the UAE, plus more.

The schedule is up to you. Come in and leave whenever, as long as the work is getting done.

Cons

There is a fair amount of technical grunt work to be done, and if you're not careful, you'll end up on boring projects. You can avoid this by proving yourself valuable in other areas.

It's hard to stand out amongst peers when everyone is so talented.

There's no clear path to advancement internally. They're working on this.

Work/life balance varies across teams. Some people are overworked (not me...).

Advice to Management

Figure out the advancement piece. Only fools work harder for no reason.
