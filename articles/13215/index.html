Summary

Key takeaways

  • Forward deployed engineering is a delivery model in which engineers work directly inside the customer’s repositories, data infrastructure, production systems, and delivery processes.
  • Forward deployed engineers are senior software engineers who write and ship production code rather than consultants or solutions engineers who only advise or support sales.
  • There are two main FDE models: product-company FDEs that deploy their employer’s platform and services FDEs that implement custom systems on the client’s own stack.
  • Product-company FDEs are the better fit when a company has already committed to a specific platform and needs vendor-side implementation support.
  • Services FDEs are better suited to organizations that want AI or software implemented in their own environment without tying the whole delivery model to one platform vendor.
  • FDEs typically begin with discovery inside the real system, including reading the codebase, profiling data, mapping integrations, and understanding operational constraints.
  • The model is especially relevant for AI projects because real deployment problems often appear in data quality, authentication, permissions, integrations, evaluation, observability, and production operations.
  • A strong forward deployed engagement continues through productionization rather than ending with a prototype or proof of concept.
  • Production continuity matters, so the same engineers should ideally remain involved through deployment, incident handling, technical debt, and ongoing support.
  • The defining test of a real FDE model is whether engineers actually work inside the customer environment and take hands-on responsibility for production engineering.

When this applies

This applies when a company needs senior engineers to work directly inside its existing technology environment rather than build from a detached specification. It is especially useful for enterprise AI implementation, RAG systems, agentic workflows, MCP servers, complex integrations, platform deployment, data readiness, and production hardening. Forward deployed engineering is also a strong fit when requirements are expected to evolve as engineers discover real constraints in the codebase, infrastructure, data, security model, and business workflows.

When this does not apply

This does not apply as directly when the requirement is limited to high-level strategy, advisory consulting, product demonstrations, or a clearly isolated project that can be delivered independently from the customer’s systems. It may also be unnecessary when an organization only needs standard staff placement without embedded delivery responsibility or when a platform can be deployed through straightforward configuration with little custom integration. Companies should also avoid the model if security policies make it impossible to give engineers enough access to understand and improve the systems they are responsible for.

Checklist

  • Define whether you need a product-company FDE or a services FDE.
  • Clarify whether the engineer will work directly in your repositories, cloud accounts, data systems, and delivery tools.
  • Confirm that the assigned engineers write and ship production code.
  • Define the business outcome rather than handing the team only a fixed technical specification.
  • Give engineers enough access to inspect the real systems and constraints they need to improve.
  • Include codebase discovery, data profiling, and integration mapping at the beginning of the engagement.
  • Identify authentication, permissions, API, and data dependencies early.
  • Define who owns architecture and implementation decisions inside the engagement.
  • Require evaluation, observability, security, and deployment planning as part of productionization.
  • Set clear responsibility for production incidents and regressions after launch.
  • Confirm whether the same engineers will remain involved after the initial deployment.
  • Define documentation, runbooks, and knowledge-transfer expectations before production handover.
  • Check the seniority and production experience of the actual engineers assigned to the work.
  • Clarify ownership of technical debt, maintenance, and long-term support.
  • Evaluate the provider based on engineering depth and production continuity rather than strategic positioning or branding alone.

Common pitfalls

  • Calling an engagement forward deployed when engineers do not actually work inside the customer’s environment.
  • Confusing FDEs with solutions engineers, consultants, or sales-support roles that rarely write production code.
  • Restricting access so heavily that engineers cannot investigate the systems they are expected to improve.
  • Ending the engagement at prototype delivery without evaluation, observability, deployment, and production ownership.
  • Moving post-launch support to a generic ticket queue that has little knowledge of the AI architecture or codebase.
  • Choosing a provider based on strategic positioning rather than engineering seniority and production experience.
  • Assuming talent matching or staff placement alone constitutes a forward deployed delivery model.
  • Failing to define who owns incidents, regressions, technical debt, and maintenance after launch.
  • Using a platform vendor’s FDE team when the real requirement is vendor-neutral implementation across multiple systems.
  • Treating the FDE as an external advisor instead of integrating the engineer into real development, review, and operational workflows.

What does a forward deployed engineer do?

An FDE does the work between the product and the customer’s real systems. A typical week has five parts:

  • Meet users and map the real workflow and the real data.
  • Write production code: integrations, data pipelines, agents and custom features.
  • Deploy to the customer’s cloud or on-premises systems and fix what breaks.
  • Tell the product team which gaps block adoption.
  • Train the customer’s team so the solution keeps working after the FDE leaves.

What is forward deployed engineering?

Forward deployed engineering places engineers at the point of use. Instead of building to a written specification and handing the result over, a forward deployed engineer works in the customer’s environment: their repositories, their data, their production systems, their meetings. Implementation decisions get made against the real system, with real constraints visible, which is why the model has become the default way serious AI gets deployed into enterprises.

To see how companies define and hire for this role, explore our Forward Deployed Engineer Index 2026. The research analyzes 27 public job postings from 19 employers across eight countries, covering engineering responsibilities, required skills, work arrangements, and salary transparency.

AI systems are unusually sensitive to the specifics of the data and infrastructure they land in, and a specification written before contact with either is mostly fiction.

What do forward deployed engineers actually do?

  • Discovery inside the system. Reading the codebase, profiling the data, and mapping the integration surface before proposing an approach.
  • Implementation against reality. Building and adapting systems, for AI work that means RAG pipelines, agents, MCP servers, and integrations, directly in the client stack.
  • Tight-loop collaboration. Sitting in the client’s standups and reviews, so scope adjusts to what the system reveals rather than to change requests.
  • Productionization. Evaluation, observability, security posture, and deployment, not a prototype handoff.
  • Staying power. The strongest versions of the model keep the same engineers through production and into ongoing support.

Yes, forward deployed engineers code. The role is a senior software engineering role with unusual customer proximity, not a solutions-consulting role with occasional scripting.

Where the term comes from, and the two FDE models

The title was popularized by Palantir, which built its delivery motion around engineers embedded with customers, and it has since spread across the AI industry: major AI companies, including OpenAI and Anthropic, now run forward deployed engineering teams that help enterprises implement their platforms.

That history matters to buyers for one practical reason: it defines the first of two distinct models, and choosing between them is the actual decision.

Model Who employs the FDE What they deploy When it fits you

Services FDE An engineering services firm AI systems built for you, inside your environment, on your stack You want the outcome in your systems without hiring FDEs onto your payroll or committing to one vendor’s motion

One boundary worth stating plainly: forward deployed engineering is generic industry vocabulary for a delivery model. A services firm using the term is describing how its engineers work, not claiming lineage from or affiliation with the companies that popularized it.

How to become a forward deployed engineer

  • Become a strong software engineer first: 3 to 5 years of production code in Python, TypeScript or Java.
  • Learn data work: SQL, data pipelines and one cloud platform (AWS, Azure or Google Cloud).
  • Learn to build with LLMs: APIs, RAG, agents, evaluations and guardrails.
  • Practise customer work: requirement interviews, demos and short written updates.
  • Build 2 or 3 end-to-end projects on messy, real data and deploy them.
  • Apply for FDE, deployment engineer and solutions engineer roles at AI and data companies, or join a services firm that embeds engineers with clients.

Many FDEs come from startups or consulting firms, where they already shipped code for customers.

Six checks before you hire an FDE

  • Confirm the engineering bar first. This person writes production code. Test that the way you would test any senior engineer. Communication skill does not substitute for it.
  • Test for ambiguity. Ask about a project where the requirements were wrong and the candidate found out on site. What did they change, and who did they tell.
  • Test for production ownership. Ask who was awake at 3am after their deployment went live, and what happened. Candidates who have only built prototypes will not have an answer.
  • Check integration depth. Most of this work is data, authentication and permissions. Ask about the ugliest integration they have shipped.
  • Check customer-facing range. The engineer will speak to an operations lead in the morning and a chief technology officer in the afternoon. Both conversations have to land.
  • Decide who owns the feedback loop. Someone senior on your side has to own what the engineer learns in the field. Without that owner, the work stays invisible and the value leaks.
