What Is an Applied AI Engineer? Role, Pay and Fit

FWD Deployment · 2026-10-14 · 8 min read

Summary

This guide argues that "applied AI engineer" has no universal definition, so job postings are the best source of truth. Using four OpenAI postings as examples, it shows the two applied AI engineer roles (Enterprise and API Platform, Digital Natives) list $251,000–$278,000 base plus equity, and explicitly name forward-deployed engineering as a relevant background, confirming overlap with FDE roles. OpenAI's FDE postings ($185,000–$300,000 and $185,000–$325,000 base) focus on end-to-end production deployments and custom API-based software. The author stresses these are one company's posted ranges, not market averages or total compensation, and advises matching the role to project shape, plus settling scope, acceptance and exit terms in writing before hiring or contracting.

An applied AI engineer is an engineer who puts AI models to work on real problems, and the title has no single industry definition. OpenAI's Applied AI Engineer, Enterprise posting lists a base-pay range of $251,000 to $278,000 plus equity, and it names applied AI engineering and forward-deployed engineering as relevant backgrounds.

That second point is the useful one for you. The role sits right next to forward deployed engineering, and the right choice depends on the shape of your project.

What is an applied AI engineer, exactly?

No standard definition exists. Companies use the title differently, and responsibilities change with the company and the domain.

The most reliable way to define the role is to read what a company actually posts. This article uses OpenAI's postings because they are specific about pay and about how the role relates to forward deployed work.

Our working reading, which is an interpretation and not a rule: an applied AI engineer takes existing AI models and gets them working for a defined customer or business need. Treat that as a starting point and test it against any posting you read.

What do OpenAI's job postings say?

Four OpenAI postings give a useful picture of the neighboring roles:

  • Applied AI Engineer, Enterprise: lists applied AI engineering and forward-deployed engineering as relevant backgrounds, with a base range of $251,000 to $278,000 plus equity.
  • Applied AI Engineer, API Platform, Digital Natives: the API platform posting lists the same $251,000 to $278,000 plus equity.
  • Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE): the FDE posting describes leading complex, end-to-end deployments of frontier models in production with strategic customers.
  • Forward Deployed Software Engineer: the software engineer posting describes building custom software with OpenAI's APIs to solve customer problems.

Notice that the two applied AI titles name a customer segment, Enterprise and Digital Natives. The two forward deployed descriptions name the work itself: production deployments and custom software.

How does applied AI engineering overlap with forward deployed engineering?

The overlap is written into the posting. OpenAI lists both applied AI engineering and forward-deployed engineering as relevant backgrounds for its applied AI engineer role, so a person can move between the two kinds of work.

The difference shows up in how OpenAI describes the forward deployed jobs. One is about leading complex, end-to-end deployments of frontier models in production with strategic customers. The other is about building custom software with APIs to solve customer problems.

For a fuller view of the forward deployed side, see our page on what the forward deployed engineer job is and who hires for it, and our comparison of forward deployed engineer vs software engineer.

Which role does your project need?

The postings do not tell you which to hire, so this is our interpretation of the wording. Match the role to the shape of the work:

  • You need a model running in production, start to finish, for a high-stakes customer or business unit. This matches the language OpenAI uses for forward deployed engineers: complex, end-to-end deployments in production.
  • You need custom software built on top of a model provider's APIs to fix a specific problem. This matches OpenAI's description of the forward deployed software engineer.
  • You need someone to help a defined segment of customers or users adopt AI capabilities. The Enterprise and Digital Natives titles suggest this is where applied AI engineer roles point, though the title alone does not prove it.

If your project spans all three, you are probably buying a team, not a single role. That is where the build-or-bring-in question gets real.

What does an applied AI engineer earn?

Here are the base-pay ranges OpenAI posted for the four roles above. Each is plus equity.

OpenAI posting Posted base pay Width of range
Applied AI Engineer, Enterprise $251,000 to $278,000 $27,000
Applied AI Engineer, API Platform, Digital Natives $251,000 to $278,000 $27,000
Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) $185,000 to $300,000 $115,000
Forward Deployed Software Engineer $185,000 to $325,000 $140,000

Three cautions apply. First, these are posted base ranges from one company. They are not a market average for applied AI engineers, and they are not total compensation.

Second, OpenAI states that compensation varies by factors including location, job-related knowledge, skills and experience. Third, OpenAI states that total compensation can include equity and performance-related bonuses on top of base pay.

Use the figures as a reference point for what one frontier AI company advertises. Then price your own role against the market where you hire. Our page on forward deployed engineer salary and posted pay ranges collects more postings.

What skills and experience should you require?

OpenAI's healthcare forward deployed engineer posting gives one concrete threshold. It requires at least six years of relevant experience, which can be software, machine-learning, solutions, consulting or comparable experience.

That mix tells you something. The company accepts several routes into customer-facing AI work, not one degree or one title.

Build your own requirements list from the work, using this checklist:

  • Has shipped software that runs in production, not only prototypes.
  • Has built against model APIs to solve a specific problem.
  • Can work directly with the people who own the problem, from executives to end users.
  • Has a stated minimum of years in software, machine-learning, solutions or consulting work, such as the six-year bar in the healthcare posting.

These are our suggestions drawn from the wording of the postings, so adjust them to your project and level.

How is an applied AI engineer different from a machine learning engineer?

The postings we reviewed do not set out a formal comparison, so treat any clean split with caution. What the postings do show is that machine-learning experience is one accepted background for customer-facing deployment work, alongside software, solutions and consulting.

Our interpretation: an applied AI engineer is defined by the use case and the customer, while a machine learning engineer title tells you about the discipline. In your hiring, define the work you need done and screen on that, whatever the title says.

Should you build an internal team or bring in a firm?

The available postings are not enough to say which is cheaper or faster, and the answer depends on your situation. Work through these steps instead:

  • Write the project in one paragraph: the system, the users and what running in production means for you.
  • Decide whether the need is ongoing or has an end date.
  • List the skills you lack today, using the checklist above.
  • Price an in-house hire using a posted range as a reference, then add equity, bonus and benefits at your own rates.
  • Ask firms for a written proposal against the same one-paragraph project description.

If you go the firm route, structure matters. Dplyz places senior engineers inside a business to connect its data, build products and run AI agents and workflows in production. It offers three ways to work: an embedded team, project delivery or technical leadership. It does not publish a price list, so you would request a quote.

You can compare other firms in our profiles of forward deployed engineering firms.

What contract and employment points should you settle?

OpenAI's healthcare forward deployed engineer posting states that the roles are at-will. It also states that compensation components may change based on performance, company results or market conditions.

If you hire, that is a useful reminder to read your own offer terms closely: what is base, what is equity or bonus, and what can change.

If you contract a firm, the terms depend on the deal, and we do not have standard terms to cite. Put these in writing before work starts:

  • Scope and deliverables: what gets built and what counts as done.
  • Acceptance: who tests it and by what measure.
  • Ownership: who owns the code and the work product.
  • Data use: what the firm may do with your data.
  • Security: the requirements the team must meet in your systems.
  • Termination: how either side ends the work, and what handover you receive.

This is general information, not legal advice, so have a lawyer read any contract or worker-classification question before you sign.

Common questions

What is the difference between an applied AI engineer vs forward deployed engineer?

OpenAI describes forward deployed engineers as leading complex, end-to-end deployments of frontier models in production with strategic customers. Its applied AI engineer postings name both applied AI engineering and forward-deployed engineering as relevant backgrounds, so the roles overlap, and the exact split depends on the employer.

What are the typical applied AI engineer roles and responsibilities?

There is no universal list, because titles and duties vary by company. OpenAI's postings name customer segments such as Enterprise and API Platform, Digital Natives, and its related forward deployed roles focus on production deployments and custom software built with APIs.

What is the average applied AI engineer salary at companies like OpenAI and Anthropic?

We cannot give an average. OpenAI's two cited applied AI engineer postings each list $251,000 to $278,000 base plus equity, and OpenAI says total compensation can also include performance-related bonuses. We have no Anthropic figure to report.

What are the key differences between an applied AI engineer and a machine learning engineer?

No posting we reviewed sets out a formal comparison. OpenAI's healthcare forward deployed posting accepts machine-learning experience as one of several relevant backgrounds, so define the work you need and screen on that rather than on the title.

What are common applied AI engineer interview questions?

We do not have posted interview questions to cite. A practical approach is to build your interview from the posting itself, for example by asking a candidate to walk through a deployment they took to production and a piece of software they built against a model API.
