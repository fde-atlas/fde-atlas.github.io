Tool Profile: vLLM

Original title: 工具档案：vLLM——私有化交付里的高吞吐推理引擎

FDE中国 · 2026-08-18 · 1 min read

Summary

This tool profile from FDE中国 introduces vLLM, a high-throughput LLM inference engine that has become a de facto standard for self-hosted model deployment. In forward deployed engineering, it typically appears during deployment and load-testing phases of private, on-premise projects, where issues like low concurrency, long-context timeouts, and exhausted GPU memory originate in the inference layer. The editor warns against treating vLLM as an install-and-run launcher: throughput baselines, load-test reports, and scaling plans are the real deliverables. FDEs need not be inference experts but must read throughput and latency metrics and speak the customer infrastructure team's language. In China, domestic chips and models add compatibility variables to verify before selection. The recommendation: keep a private deployment checklist with inference load testing as a mandatory pre-launch gate.

Key points

  • vLLM is a high-throughput inference engine and a de facto standard for self-hosted model deployment.
  • In FDE work it matters most during deployment and load testing in customer on-premise environments.
  • Common failure modes—low concurrency, long-context timeouts, full GPU memory—originate in the inference layer.
  • Deliverables include throughput baselines, load-test reports, and scaling plans, not just installation.
  • Domestic chips and models in China require compatibility validation before selection.

Full text

vLLM is a high-throughput LLM inference serving engine and one of the de facto standards for self-hosted model deployment. In Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) delivery, it mainly appears during the deployment and load-testing phases of on-premise projects: running open-source or domestic models as stable services inside the customer's intranet…

Editor's note FDEChina Editorial Team

This card targets the most common—and most easily underestimated—scenario for FDEs in China: on-premise deployment. The moment the model service goes live on the customer's intranet, problems like low concurrency, long-text timeouts, and GPU memory exhaustion all originate at the inference layer. The common misuse is treating it as a "just install and run" launcher: throughput baselines, load-test reports, and scaling plans are the actual deliverables—installing without load testing means you haven't delivered.

Compared with the site's content on on-premise and delivery models, this card's added value is writing the inference layer into the FDE capability checklist: you don't need to become an inference optimization expert, but you should be able to read throughput and latency metrics and speak the same language as the customer's infrastructure team. In the Chinese context, the compatibility of domestic chips with domestic models is an extra variable—validate before selecting. Action item: maintain your own on-premise deployment checklist and make inference load testing a mandatory pre-launch gate.
