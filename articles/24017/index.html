Can Freelance FDEs Succeed? A Practical Guide to Winning Projects, Contracts, and Setting Rates

Original title: フリーランスFDEは成立する？案件獲得・契約・単価の決め方を実務で整理

FDE総研 · 2026-08-17 · 22 min read

Summary

This FDE総研 article examines whether freelance FDE work is viable in Japan for sole proprietors. It argues the role works if one person can handle requirements gathering, implementation, and adoption support, but only tasks with definable deliverables and acceptance criteria can be outsourced; resident work under client direction risks being deemed disguised employment. It recommends quasi-delegated (jun-i-nin) contracts with fixed-spec work carved out as contracts for work, covers the November 2024 freelance law (60-day payment rule), the invoice tax regime's phase-down of input tax credits, and a method to derive a monthly rate floor from desired take-home income. It closes by comparing freelance versus employed FDE careers on income, insurance, and accumulated expertise.

Key points

  • Freelance FDE is viable only when deliverables and acceptance criteria can be written down; vague accompaniment cannot be contracted.
  • Use quasi-delegated contracts as the base, carve out fixed-spec work as contracts for work, and rewrite scope at each renewal.
  • The 2024 freelance law mandates written terms and payment within 60 days, easing payment-term negotiations.
  • Calculate a rate floor from desired take-home plus taxes, insurance, and expenses, divided by billable months (not 12).
  • Before going independent: multiple solo requirements negotiations, three reachable contacts, and 6–12 months of living expenses.

Is FDE a profession that can work as a freelancer?

The FDE role, defined and popularized by Palantir, can work on a freelance basis if you can complete the three stages: requirements definition, implementation, and adoption support.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role that Palantir defined within its own deployment process and that became widely known (see What is an FDE for details). The scope of responsibility extends to connecting the product to the customer's business processes and getting it into a working state. This is what distinguishes FDEs from engineers doing contracted development or customer success. And how broadly "connecting to business processes" is interpreted is the deciding factor in whether an FDE can succeed as a freelancer.

How much of FDE work can be carved out as contracted services?

Only work whose scope of results and acceptance criteria can be defined in writing can be carved out as contracted services. Vague "accompaniment" is not suited to contracting.

When FDE work is translated into service contracts, the units that are easy to carve out and those that are not divide clearly.

Work unitEase of carving out for contractingReason
Initial business interviews and requirements organizationHighCan be delimited by deliverables (business flow diagrams, requirements lists)
Data integration design and implementation (ETL, API connections)HighOperating conditions and acceptance criteria can be written down
Per-customer customization implementation on an existing productModerateHeavily dependent on the ordering party's codebase conventions
Post-deployment adoption support and field trainingModerateTends to break down unless structured as hourly quasi-delegation
Standing attendance at customer decision-makingLowApproaches direction and control, making the contract's substance resemble employment

A common stumbling block heard in the field is: "I was able to carve out work for the first few months, but after that I could no longer explain what I was doing." Because an FDE's value arises from getting inside the customer's business, the scope of work tends to expand if left alone. If continuing under service contracts, it is realistic to build in, from the start, a process of rewriting the scope of responsibility at each contract renewal.

What combination of skills does a freelance FDE need?

A freelance FDE needs the combination of abilities to move fluidly among four things alone: business understanding, data design, implementation, and consensus building.

The reason freelance FDEs are valued lies not so much in technical skill itself as in the fact that "no intermediary is needed." Talk with the customer's business staff, sketch a data model on the spot, and show something working the following week. This speed of iteration becomes the reason to hire for ordering parties who do not have an in-house deployment team.

The following combination tends to be the practical minimum.

  • Business interviews and documentation: Translate verbal explanations from the field into business flows and glossaries
  • Data design: Read existing system schemas and design intermediate tables for integration
  • Implementation: Handle SQL, Python scripts, API integration, and simple frontends end to end
  • Consensus building: Communicate "what cannot be done" early, paired with alternatives

In domains that handle real-time field conditions as data, like the product of VACAN Technologies (VACAN, Inc.), physical conditions such as installation, operation, and field operations connect directly to requirements. Requirements cannot be pinned down by desk-based data design alone. There are cases where the very attitude of going to the field to verify things is evaluated as an FDE's real capability.

What working arrangements are difficult to sustain under service contracts?

Working on-site under direction and control, even with a service contract in place, risks being judged as employment based on the actual conditions.

In assessing worker status, substance is examined rather than the contract's title. The main assessment factors are as follows, and judgments are made by considering them together (for differences in contract structure, see also Differences between FDE and SES).

Assessment perspectiveConditions likely to be viewed as employment
Freedom to decline requestsUnable to decline work requests
Direction on task executionGiven specific instructions on how to perform the work
Constraints on time and placeWorking hours and location are constrained
Nature of compensationPaid for hours of labor provided

If you attend the ordering party's morning meetings daily and are assigned that day's tasks on the spot, there is a risk of being flagged as disguised contracting. Because FDEs embed themselves at customer sites, this line tends to blur. Submit activity reports based on results. Decide your own work procedures. Hold multiple clients. Keeping a record of these practices protects both the ordering party and the freelance FDE.

How can you distinguish a "self-proclaimed FDE" from a genuine freelance FDE?

The criterion is not the title. It is whether you can describe cases where you consistently handled everything from grasping business issues through implementation to operational adoption.

As the FDE name has spread, anyone can claim it. What ordering parties want to verify is whether you have actually taken responsibility for experience reaching the point of "connecting to business processes and getting things working," which is the FDE's scope of responsibility. The FDE Research Institute evaluates track records from the following four perspectives.

Verification perspectiveExamples that convey FDE track recordExamples that fall short
Grasping the businessTranslated verbal explanations from the field into business flows and glossariesOnly worked within requirements handed to them
Data and implementationRead existing system schemas and designed and implemented integrationsOnly made configuration changes to existing tools
AdoptionVerified whether the deployed system entered actual operationInvolvement ended at delivery
The judgment of what not to buildBeing able to explain what you didn't build and whyOnly being able to talk about the number of features you built

How to concretely structure your track record is covered in the next H2, "How to Visualize Track Record and Trust?"

Where can freelance FDEs find projects?

According to FDE Soken's classification, there are five channels for freelance FDEs to acquire work: use agents right after going independent, then shift to direct transactions as the core once you have a few projects under your belt (How to Find FDE Job Openings).

Under what job titles are freelance FDE projects posted?

FDE projects are almost never found by searching the string "FDE wanted." Searching related job titles is the shortcut.

In Japan, job postings under the FDE title are still not common. However, an increasing number of operating companies offering SaaS or data products are effectively commissioning the same work as FDEs on a contracting basis. Use the following keywords when searching.

Search keywordFDE work it closely resembles in practice
Implementation support engineerConnecting the product to the customer's operations and getting it working
Solutions engineer / Solutions architectDesigning and implementing configurations tailored to customer challenges
Implementation consultant + developmentHandling everything from requirements definition to implementation solo
PoC developmentBuilding something functional in a minimal scope and validating it
Data infrastructure construction supportDesigning and implementing data integration
Customer engineeringPost-implementation support for individual clients and adoption assistance

Demand is most likely to arise when SaaS companies at the Series A–B stage start taking on enterprise customers and hit a wall with implementation, prompting them to seek outside help. Rather than waiting to be approached while calling yourself an FDE, it's easier to find projects by actively pursuing this kind of latent demand.

How should you balance direct transactions and going through agents?

Direct transactions offer advantages in rates and autonomy, while agents offer speed of getting started and credit protection, so switching between them depending on the period is the realistic approach.

ChannelSpeed of getting startedFeesFreedom in rate negotiationBest suited period

Direct transactions (former colleagues, former clients)ModerateNoneHighAfter accumulating a few projects
Development from advisor/consultant contractsSlowNoneHighWhen you have clear expertise in a specific industry

Going through agents has the advantage of handling administrative matters for you, such as contract templates and payment guarantees. However, work like FDE, where the scope of duties shifts, tends to be forced into generic frameworks like "a fixed number of hours per month," making it hard to get credit for effort spent on requirements definition. Before signing, confirm the granularity of work reporting and how scope changes will be handled.

How to Visualize Track Record and Trust?

What wins trust is not certifications or titles. It's whether you can describe how the customer's operations changed, in a reproducible form.

FDE results don't come across through lines of code or elegant architecture. What the client wants to know comes down to one question: "Can the same thing be done for us?" Track records are easiest to convey when structured in the following order.

  • State of operations before implementation: Who was doing what, how often, and how much of it was manual
  • Constraints: Existing systems, budget, on-site receptiveness, timeline
  • Actions taken: What was built and what was not (the judgment of what not to build is valued most)
  • Changes: Which operational steps were eliminated, whose decision-making became faster
  • Remaining issues: What was still unresolved at the point of handover

If confidentiality obligations prevent naming specific companies, abstracting to the level of industry and scale (e.g., a retailer with several hundred employees) is sufficient. In fact, some say that people who can describe how they wound down a project that didn't go well find it easier to put clients at ease.

How should you design the first project to lead to continued work?

For the first engagement, keep it small and limit the scope to what can be validated in about two weeks. This makes it easy for both parties to gather the information needed to decide whether to continue.

If the first contract is set up as long-term and full-commitment from the start, the loss is large for both parties if it turns out to be a poor fit. What often works well on the ground is the following three-stage approach.

PhaseDurationContract typeContent
Diagnosis1–2 weeksFixed feeTake stock of current operations and data, and produce a feasibility assessment and effort estimate
PoCA few weeks to a few monthsQuasi-delegate contractBuild something functional in a minimal scope and let the team experience it
Production implementation3–6 monthsQuasi-delegate + partial fixed-price contractBuild it into a form that can withstand production use and leave handover documentation

The deliverables of the diagnosis phase (current-state analysis and estimate) become the proposal for the next phase as-is. Whether you can charge for this, rather than doing it as unpaid sales activity, is often the deciding factor in whether you can sustain yourself as a freelance FDE.

What legal frameworks should freelance FDEs know regarding contracts and compensation?

Three things shape freelance FDE contracts and compensation: whether the contract is quasi-delegate or fixed-price, the new Freelancer Act effective November 1, 2024, and the Invoice System that started in October 2023.

The following assumes you take on work as a sole proprietor in Japan. Since regulations are subject to revision, check the latest materials published by the Japan Fair Trade Commission, the Small and Medium Enterprise Agency, and the National Tax Agency before signing contracts.

How to divide contract type and deliverable responsibility?

FDE work is fundamentally based on a quasi-delegate contract. It's safest to carve out only the parts that carry deliverable responsibility as a fixed-price contract.

ItemQuasi-delegate contractFixed-price (contract for work) contract
Nature of obligationPerform the work with the duty of due professional careComplete the work
Basis of compensationThe work and its performance itself (percentage-of-performance type is common)The completed deliverable
Liability for non-conformityGenerally not borneBorne (organized as liability for non-conformity under the April 2020 Civil Code revision)
Suitability for FDE workRequirements definition, implementation support, operational adoptionClearly defined feature development, data migration

For FDE work, requirements are usually not fixed when you start. If you take it on as a fixed-price contract, every requirement change becomes unpaid extra work. Make quasi-delegation (junyōinin) the default, and carve out only the parts where specifications can be fixed, such as "a feature that outputs this report," as fixed-price work. In practice, this structure is the easiest to handle.

The five items you should especially check in the contract are as follows.

  • Ownership of intellectual property rights in deliverables (whether you can stipulate that generic libraries and know-how remain yours)
  • Cap on damages (whether there is a clause limiting liability to the amount of compensation already received)
  • Notice period for mid-term termination
  • Scope and duration of confidentiality (including whether you may publicize your track record)
  • Procedure for scope changes (whose approval is required and how it is reflected in compensation)

What protections do the Freelance Act and the Fair Transactions Act (formerly the Subcontract Act) provide?

The Freelance Act took effect on November 1, 2024, obligating clients to clearly state transaction terms and to pay compensation within 60 days.

Its official name is the "Act on Ensuring Proper Transactions with Specified Entrusted Service Providers." It applies to transactions in which individuals without employees (specified entrusted service providers) receive commissioned work. Its main provisions are as follows.

ObligationContent
Disclosure of transaction termsState the work content, compensation amount, payment due date, etc., in writing or by electronic means
Compensation payment due dateSet the payment date as short a period as possible, within 60 days from the date of receipt of the deliverables
禁止行為 受領拒否、報酬の減額、返品、買いたたき、不当な経済上の利益の提供要請など
Accurate Representation of Recruitment InformationProhibition of False or Misleading Representations
Consideration for childcare and nursing care / harassment measuresClients with ongoing commissioned work are obligated to establish appropriate systems
Notice of mid-term terminationTermination of ongoing commissioned work generally requires 30 days' advance notice

Some obligations apply only when the client is a "commissioning business that uses employees" or when the transaction is "ongoing commissioned work." You need to confirm applicability for each transaction. Also, depending on the client's size, laws descended from the Act against Delay in Payment of Subcontract Proceeds (Subcontract Act) may apply. Because the Subcontract Act was renamed the "Act on Ensuring Proper Transactions with Small and Medium-Sized Entrusted Businesses (Fair Transactions Act)" in the revision effective January 1, 2026, check the latest published materials for applicability requirements. The fact that the Freelance Act applies regardless of the client's size is its major practical feature.

To add a practitioner's perspective, the effect of "making it easier to negotiate for payment due dates to be written into the contract" is greater than the law's existence itself. Rather than using the statute as a shield for disputes, it is realistic to use it as a tool to shorten the initial alignment of terms.

How does the consumption tax and invoice system affect rates?

The invoice system started on October 1, 2023, and the deductible percentage for purchases from tax-exempt businesses dropped from 80% to 70% in October 2026.

There is a transitional measure for the input tax credit on purchases from tax-exempt businesses. The deductible percentage changes by period.

PeriodDeductible percentage for purchases from tax-exempt businesses
October 1, 2023 – September 30, 202680% of the equivalent input tax amount
October 1, 2026 – September 30, 202870% of the equivalent input tax amount
October 1, 2028 – September 30, 203050% of the equivalent input tax amount
October 1, 2030 – September 30, 203130% of the equivalent input tax amount
On or after October 1, 2031Not deductible

The FY2026 tax reform extended the transitional measure by two years, and as of October 1, 2026, the deductible percentage dropped from 80% to 70%. If you continue doing business as a tax-exempt business, the client's effective burden increases, so they may ask you to renegotiate your rate. Also, the so-called 20% special provision (a special measure setting the tax payable at 20% of the consumption tax on the taxable base) applies to taxable periods that include days up to September 30, 2026. For sole proprietors whose taxable periods follow the calendar year, 2026 will be the last applicable year. After that, a "30% special provision" setting the tax payable at 30% of the output tax is available for sole proprietors only, for fiscal years 2027 and 2028.

Note that compensation for system development or consulting is generally not subject to withholding, unless it falls under categories such as manuscript fees or design fees listed as withholding targets in the Income Tax Act. However, how invoices are prepared varies by project, so align on the treatment with the client's accounting staff before your first invoice.

How do you calculate a freelance FDE's monthly rate?

The lower bound of the monthly rate is calculated by adding taxes, social insurance, and expenses to your desired take-home amount, then dividing by the number of available working months, estimated at less than 12.

Don't set your rate from market averages; work backward from your desired take-home amount. FDE Soken organizes this into the following three formulas.

FormulaWhen to use it
Lower bound of monthly rate = (desired annual take-home + income tax, resident tax, National Health Insurance, National Pension + expenses) ÷ available working monthsSet the non-negotiable floor for negotiations. Use not 12 but the number of months after subtracting non-working periods for sales, learning, and admin work (if you expect 1–2 non-working months per year, use 10–11 months as a guideline)
Hourly rate = monthly rate ÷ contractual monthly working hoursUse as the basis for settlement when working hours increase due to scope changes
Actual amount received = quoted rate − agent's feeCompare agent-mediated projects on the same scale as direct transactions

The steps for building this up are as follows.

  • Decide your desired annual take-home amount
  • Add income tax, resident tax, National Health Insurance, and National Pension burdens (varies by municipality and income)
  • Add expenses (PC, communications, books, travel, accounting software, etc.)
  • Allow for non-working periods (secure time for sales, learning, and admin as non-billable)
  • Divide the total by available working months to get the monthly floor
  • Adjust up or down from that floor based on project difficulty, risk, and degree of commitment

If you simply divide your salaried annual income by 12 to get your monthly rate, you completely omit the social insurance premiums the company paid, bonuses, and non-working periods. This is the most common mistake right after going independent. In negotiations, agree in advance—before the rate number itself—on these three things: making the diagnosis phase paid fixed-price work, contracting in stages, and writing the scope-change procedure into the contract. Agreeing to these three first makes it easier to defend your rate later. For an overview of market rates, see FDE fees and rates.

How do years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size change the calculus for a freelance FDE?

FDE Soken recommends confirming five conditions before going independent: build a network of three contacts and experience revising contracts while still a full-time employee (FDE careers).

How many years of experience are needed to go independent?

Experience is measured by substance, not years. Use as a benchmark whether you have multiple experiences completing requirements alignment without a supervisor present.

In practice, going independent after meeting the following conditions makes it easier to avoid stalling in the first year.

No.Condition to meet before going independentNeed to deliberately build while a full-time employee
1Multiple experiences completing requirements alignment with clients without a supervisor presentMedium
2At least three former colleagues or former clients you can reach out to yourselfHigh
3Cash savings covering 6 to 12 months of living expensesMedium
4Experience reading contracts and requesting revisionsHigh
5Ability to record and explain your own working hours and results for the past yearMedium

Of these, 2 and 4 cannot be acquired unless you deliberately build them while a full-time employee. Going independent before you have them makes it easy to stumble in negotiations and sales before even reaching discussions about rates.

How do English ability and client company size affect projects?

English ability is outside the scope of this article; for domestic projects, the client company's size and implementation stage affect contract terms and applicable law.

This article assumes working as a sole proprietor in Japan. FDE Soken has no primary data on whether English ability is necessary. For the recruitment requirements of overseas-origin FDE organizations such as Palantir, please check each company's official recruiting pages.

Differences by client company size appear in the following two points.

  • Implementation stage: Demand tends to arise from SaaS companies at Series A to B that are starting to acquire enterprise customers and are stuck on implementation
  • Applicable law: The new Freelance Act applies regardless of the client's size. On the other hand, whether the Act on Subcontracting (formerly the Subcontract Act) applies depends on the client's size

Should you choose full-time FDE or freelance FDE?

Choose between full-time and freelance FDE based not on annual income but on the density of learning opportunities and risk tolerance, and assume contracts end in 3 to 6 months (What is an FDE engineer).

How do income and stability differ?

Freelance FDEs can more easily increase gross pay. On the other hand, fully self-paid social insurance premiums and non-working periods push down actual take-home pay.

Comparison axisFull-time FDEFreelance FDE
Pay variabilitySmall (monthly salary and bonuses)Large (directly tied to availability of projects)
Social insuranceSplit between employer and employee, enrolled in employees' pensionNational health insurance and national pension in principle, fully self-paid
Income during non-working periodsMaintainedZero
Expense deductionsGenerally not possibleBusiness-related expenses possible (blue tax return special deduction may also apply)
Credit (e.g., mortgage)Easier to get approvedTends to be unfavorable for the first few years after starting the business

If you choose the blue tax return and meet requirements such as bookkeeping with double-entry accounting and electronic filing via e-Tax, you can receive a blue tax return special deduction of up to 650,000 yen through fiscal year 2026. Under the FY2026 tax reform, from fiscal year 2027 onward, the deduction is said to be revised to up to 750,000 yen if you meet requirements such as preserving proper electronic ledgers. When comparing the actual take-home pay of a freelance FDE, calculate including this deduction and expense accounting. Judging by gross pay alone leads to mistakes.

How does the accumulation of career assets differ?

Full-time FDEs can more easily accumulate long-term accompaniment experience including failures, while freelance FDEs can more easily accumulate short-term launch experience.

FDE capability grows significantly from experience seeing through whether "what you implemented is still being used a year later," rather than from the number of implementations. Freelance FDEs, whose contracts end in 3 to 6 months, often cannot do this long-term observation. Conversely, full-time FDEs can only work on their own company's product, so the range of industries and issues they handle tends not to broaden.

At companies like Palantir that have their own in-house FDE organizations, the implementation process itself is accumulated as methodology and passed on to successors. Being able to participate in this "organizational learning" is an advantage of being a full-time FDE. On the other hand, freelance FDEs can move across multiple industries and products in short periods, so their cross-industry pattern recognition develops quickly. It is not a matter of which is superior; the types of assets that accumulate differ. As the FDE role becomes established in Japan, a possible mainstream path is for people who learned organizational methodology as full-time employees to later go freelance and work across industries.

Who is not suited to being a freelance FDE?

Those who want on-site work under direct supervision, those who don't read contracts, and those who want to see things through until one year after implementation are not suited to being freelance FDEs.

Based on the contract and career characteristics outlined in this article, if any of the following apply to you, consider a full-time FDE role or a different profession first.

  • You want the client to assign you tasks every day (in practice this resembles employment and risks disguised subcontracting)
  • You find it difficult to read the IP, damages, and scope-change clauses of a contract yourself and request revisions
  • You find fulfillment in seeing whether what was implemented is still in use a year later
  • You have no savings of at least six months of living expenses and would struggle to endure periods without income
  • You find it a hassle to rewrite your scope of responsibilities every time a contract is renewed
