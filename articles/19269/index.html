AWS AIF-C01 Exam Prep Guide: How to Study as an FDE

Original title: AWS AIF-C01 备考指南：FDE 怎么学

FDE Times · 2026-11-09 · 4 min read

Summary

This FDE Times guide evaluates whether the AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01) exam is worth taking for forward deployed engineers. The exam tests conceptual understanding of AI and AWS tools, explicitly excluding model coding, pipeline building, and hyperparameter tuning—the core of FDE fieldwork. The author breaks down the scoring structure, recommends a study order using free Skill Builder materials and the Official Pretest, and argues the biggest gap is evals: the exam teaches metric names but not how to build evaluation sets. The suggested path is to pair the certificate with a hands-on RAG project built on Bedrock Knowledge Bases, with recorded evaluation numbers, to demonstrate the building skills AWS does not test.

Key points

  • The exam has 65 questions, 50 scored, passing at 700 of 1000–1000, costing $100, valid three years.
  • Generative AI foundations plus foundation model applications account for roughly 26 of the 50 scored questions.
  • RAG questions use business scenarios, training FDEs to justify architecture choices to customers.
  • Model customization options differ in cost: prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, pretraining, in rising order of investment.
  • Recommended sequence: free Skill Builder content, Official Pretest, then a self-built RAG project with evals.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • AIF-C01 tests understanding, not building ability: model programming and building AI/ML pipelines are not on the exam
  • The "Applications of Foundation Models" domain accounts for 28%, roughly 14 of the 50 scored questions, and is the part FDEs should read most carefully
  • Start with the free Skill Builder content, take the Official Pretest before the exam, then build your own RAG project with evals

The exam guide for AWS Certified AI Practitioner contains a sentence worth reading slowly: the target candidate is someone who uses AI/ML solutions on AWS, not necessarily someone who builds them. For a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), this sentence determines how the entire course should be studied.

AIF-C01 is a foundational-level certification issued by AWS. The exam tests understanding of AI concepts and AWS AI tools, with an emphasis on business applications. The exam guide also explicitly lists what is not covered: programming models, building and deploying AI/ML pipelines or infrastructure, and hyperparameter tuning.

Yet these are exactly the things an FDE does at customer sites. So is it still worth studying? If you are about to join a deployment project on AWS and need to speak the same language as the customer's architects on Bedrock, RAG, or model evals, yes. If you expect this certificate to prove that you have pushed an AI agent to production, no.

Where exactly do the points fall?

The exam has 65 questions with a 90-minute time limit, of which 15 are unscored; what really determines the result is the 50 scored ones. Multiplying the 50 questions by each domain's weight gives the following estimates:

DomainWeightEstimated scored questions
Fundamentals of AI and ML20%about 10
Fundamentals of Generative AI24%about 12
Applications of Foundation Models28%about 14
Responsible AI14%about 7
Security, Compliance, and Governance14%about 7

The score range is 100–1,000, with 700 required to pass. Scoring is compensatory; you don't need to pass each domain individually. Just the two domains of generative AI and foundation models account for about 26 of the 50 questions. The exam fee is 100 USD, and the certification is valid for three years.

Fortunately for FDEs, the highest-scoring domain is also the closest to actual work: Domain 3. Three knowledge points in it are worth keeping after the exam.

RAG is tested in the form of business scenarios

The exam requires you to define retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and describe its business applications, using Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases as an example. This style of question trains exactly the ability many FDEs lack: explaining RAG through the customer's problem, rather than drawing a vector diagram.

Imagine an insurance company that wants a chatbot to answer questions based on internal policy terms, which change every quarter.

Write a question for yourself in exam style: the company wants answers to always match the latest terms at low cost. Should it pretrain its own model, fine-tune it quarterly, paste all the terms into the prompt, or use RAG based on Bedrock Knowledge Bases?

The approach is elimination. Pretraining is too costly for a deployment project. Quarterly fine-tuning means retraining every time the terms change. Pasting all documents into the prompt means paying tokens for every term with every question.

What remains is RAG: documents live outside the model, answers change when documents are updated, and no retraining is needed at all. This is also what you would say to the customer's business side. When reviewing Domain 3, practice articulating reasons to eliminate wrong options, because in real meetings, that is exactly how you defend an architecture proposal.

Each model customization method puts the cost in a different place

The exam asks about cost trade-offs among pretraining, fine-tuning, in-context learning, RAG, and model distillation. At customer sites, this question usually appears as "why not just fine-tune it?"

A reasonable answer ranks by cost: in-context learning only takes prompt-writing effort and tokens; RAG requires additional investment in building and maintaining data sources; fine-tuning requires labeled data and retraining with every change; pretraining is almost never an option for deployment projects.

The exam only tests at the conceptual level, but if you can write this comparison table yourself, you already have the material for your first architecture consultation.

Model evals: the exam teaches terminology, customers want numbers

Domain 3 covers Bedrock Model Evaluation, benchmark datasets, human-in-the-loop evals, and metrics such as ROUGE, BLEU, BERTScore, and LLM-as-a-judge. All of this stays at the conceptual level and won't teach you to build your own eval sets.

This is the biggest gap in the entire course. Knowing the definition of ROUGE is enough to pass the exam. But to prove to a customer that the chatbot answers correctly, you need a set of questions distilled from the customer's own documents, paired with reference answers, rerun after every prompt change.

What order of study is most efficient?

If you're starting from scratch, AWS Educate has a learning path leading to AIF-C01 covering Foundations of ML and Generative AI, including Amazon Bedrock. Then move on to the Exam Prep Plan on AWS Skill Builder, which is AWS's designated exam prep path.

The free version includes a two-hour Exam Prep course and 20 official practice questions, enough to get familiar with the question types before paying. The Enhanced version requires a paid subscription and adds more practice questions and labs.

The Official Pretest comes with a Skill Builder subscription, so you'll most likely need a paid plan to take it. If you've already decided to pay, use this pretest to assess your readiness before scheduling the exam; if not, the free 20 questions are the minimum self-assessment.

When reviewing, use the list of in-scope services as a framework: Amazon Bedrock, Bedrock AgentCore, Amazon Nova, SageMaker AI, SageMaker JumpStart, plus Kiro and Strands Agents among the developer tools. AWS notes that this list is not exhaustive and may change, so there's no need to memorize it.

What should you fill in yourself after the exam?

Exactly the content the exam guide excludes. Build a Bedrock Knowledge Base on a set of public documents, write your own eval set, and record the scores before and after each change. This project proves you can do the work the exam doesn't test.

On your resume, don't let the certificate stand alone. List it together with this project, with measured numbers attached. If the FDE job description you're targeting includes terms like "Bedrock", "RAG", or "evaluation", AIF-C01 shows you understand these terms; but also be prepared to prove your building ability, because AWS has made clear that this part is not on the exam.
