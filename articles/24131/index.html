Is an FDE Outsourcing Contract a Quasi-Mandate (Juninin) or a Contract for Work (Ukeoi)? Key Points to Check Regarding Disguised Contracting and the New Freelance Law

Original title: FDEの業務委託契約は準委任か請負か？偽装請負とフリーランス新法の確認点

FDE総研 · 2026-11-10 · 14 min read

Summary

This FDE Soken (operated by VACAN) article explains how to structure outsourcing contracts for Forward Deployed Engineers in Japan. It argues the contract type should follow the project phase: quasi-mandate (jun'inin) during exploratory work when specifications are undefined, and ukeoi (contract for work) or deliverable-based quasi-mandate once specs are fixed. Using MHLW Notice 37 and 2021 guidance, it shows that on-site presence or Slack use alone does not constitute disguised dispatch—what matters is who directs the work directly. It also covers freelancer-act obligations (written terms, payment within 60 days), invoice registration's effect on pricing, contract clauses for scope changes, deliverable acceptance, data deletion and generative AI use, and a seven-point pre-signature checklist.

Key points

  • Match contract type to phase: quasi-mandate for exploration, ukeoi after specs are fixed.
  • Disguised dispatch hinges on direct instruction by the client, not on-site presence.
  • Routing requests through the vendor's single point of contact lowers most risk.
  • Japan's freelancer act mandates written terms and payment within 60 days.
  • Seven pre-signature checks include liability caps and copyright assignment scope.

For FDE contracts for services, you need to choose the contract form according to the phase: delegation (jun'inin) while the approach to the work is still being explored, and contract for work (ukeoi) once the specifications of what to build are fixed. An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer role who works on-site with customers, handling everything from problem discovery through implementation to operational embedding. Palantir is said to have popularized the name. What FDE Soken (operated by VACAN, Inc.) covers in this article is how to choose contracts when an FDE works as an independent contractor and when a company commissions an FDE. Based on primary sources—the Civil Code, MHLW public notices, and the new freelance law—we organize the points to verify before signing. Legal and regulatory information is current as of November 2026.

Should an FDE Contract for Services Be Delegation (Jun'inin), Contract for Work (Ukeoi), or Dispatch?

The basic approach for FDE contracts for services is to use delegation under Article 656 of the Civil Code during the exploration period when deliverables are not yet defined, and a contract for work under Article 632 of the Civil Code once specifications are finalized.

FDE work starts with deciding "what to build" together with the customer. If you promise "completion" under a contract for work from the start, disputes over the definition of completion are likely. In practice, it is sensible not to fix a single contract form but to assume switching between forms for each stage of the work.

The difference between the three types comes down to "what you pay for" and "who directs the work"

Under delegation, compensation is for performing the work; under a contract for work, it is for completing the job. Only dispatch allows the client to give direct instructions to the worker.

Does the "deliverable-completion delegation" from the 2020 Civil Code revision suit FDEs?

Deliverable-completion delegation (Civil Code Article 648-2) becomes an option toward the end of a PoC where deliverables can be specified.

The revised Civil Code, effective April 2020, explicitly provided for delegation under which compensation is paid when a deliverable is handed over. Unlike a contract for work, there is no "obligation to complete." It fits situations such as "delivery of a PoC report and an evaluation dashboard," where deliverables can be specified but achieving target values cannot be promised. However, if the acceptance conditions for deliverables are vague, the same disputes as with a contract for work will arise. Writing acceptance criteria in detail is a prerequisite.

FDE Soken's decision criteria: "degree of specification finality" and "need for direct supervision"

FDE Soken decides the contract form along two axes: whether specifications are fixed, and whether the client needs to give direct instructions on the work.

From the perspective of VACAN practitioners, we often feel that the cause of contract disputes in FDE projects lies less in the legal classification itself than in "entering the implementation period while still under an exploration-period contract." Setting a date in advance to review the contract when the phase switches makes scope-change negotiations easier.

Does FDE On-Site Presence, Slack Requests, or Scrum Participation Constitute Disguised Contracting?

FDE on-site presence itself is not illegal. What MHLW Public Notice No. 37 examines is whether the client directly supervises the worker.

Disguised contracting refers to a situation where the contract is nominally a contract for work or delegation, but the reality is worker dispatch (supervisory orders by the client). The standard is the "Criteria for Distinguishing Worker Dispatch Businesses from Businesses Conducted under Contracts for Work" (MHLW Public Notice No. 37 of 1986). It is not determined solely by where the work is done, such as being on-site or remote.

Four situations that become the dividing line for judgment

Even with the same on-site presence or Slack, the evaluation differs depending on whether requests go through the contractor's responsible manager or the client gives instructions directly to the individual.

SituationOperation unlikely to be problematicOperation likely to raise disguised-contracting suspicion
On-site presence at clientThe contractor decides how and in what order the work proceedsThe client individually directs seating, work order, and overtime
Slack requestsRequests go through the contractor's point of contact, and the contractor decides prioritiesThe client's employees assign tasks directly to the individual FDE
Scrum participationThe team plans autonomously as equalsThe client's PO assigns and manages work for the individual FDE
AttendanceWorking-hour reports kept only as records for billingThe client manages start times, end times, and breaks

The Agile Development Approach Shown in the 2021 Q&A Collection

In its Q&A Collection (3rd Edition) of September 2021, the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare indicated that agile development does not automatically constitute disguised contracting.

The Q&A Collection states that, where the client and the vendor's developers collaborate autonomously as a team on an equal footing, that alone does not mean the arrangement is judged to be disguised contracting. On the other hand, it states that if, in practice, the client directly gives work instructions to and manages the vendor's workers, the arrangement may be judged as worker dispatch regardless of the contract's name. What is questioned is not the fact that an FDE participates in Scrum itself, but who is giving instructions to whom within it.

Consolidating the Point of Contact Alone Reduces Many Risks

Consolidating the point of contact for requests to the vendor's manager and separating Slack channels is the most realistic countermeasure.

Since an FDE's value lies in being close to the customer, consolidating the point of contact often draws complaints that "speed will drop." In practice, I believe that in many cases both can be achieved through an operation where all requests are written in a shared channel and priorities are decided by the vendor's manager. If the FDE is contracting independently as an individual, stating in the contract that the FDE also serves as the manager makes it less likely that the party issuing directives becomes ambiguous.

When an FDE Works Under a Business Outsourcing Contract, What Should Be Checked Regarding the New Freelance Law and the Invoice System?

Under the New Freelance Law, which took effect on November 1, 2024, it became the client's obligation to state transaction terms in writing and to pay remuneration within 60 days.

Its official name is the "Act on the Rationalization of Transactions Related to Specified Subcontracted Business Operators." An FDE who contracts independently without employees is protected as a "specified subcontracted business operator." Even if a corporation has been formed, it may still be covered if it has only one representative and no employees.

The Client's Obligations Increase Depending on the Contract Period

The obligations under the New Freelance Law increase in three stages: all transactions, one month or more, and six months or more. The contract period is the first item to check.

The points of contact are divided between the Fair Trade Commission and the Small and Medium Enterprise Agency (transaction fairness) and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (working environment). Note that if the client is an individual without employees, only the disclosure obligation under Article 3 applies.

Consider Invoice Registration Together with Rate Negotiations

For parties paying an FDE who is not registered for the invoice system, the input tax credit shrinks according to the transitional measure's percentage. Whether to register directly affects rate negotiations.

The invoice system started on October 1, 2023. For purchases from tax-exempt businesses, there was a transitional measure allowing an 80% deduction until September 30, 2026. Under the FY2026 (Reiwa 8) tax reform, it is stipulated that the deduction will be reduced in stages to 70% from October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2028, 50% from October 2028 to September 2030, and 30% from October 2030 to September 2031. The "20% special measure" for those who moved from tax-exempt to taxable status is a measure through Reiwa 8 (2026), and for sole proprietors only, a "30% special measure" is stipulated for Reiwa 9 to Reiwa 10 (2027-2028). Please check the details of application on the National Tax Agency's special website.

  • Ask whether the client is a taxable business or uses the simplified tax system (this changes how significant non-registration's impact is)
  • Discuss before the contract whether the consumption tax burden upon registration is reflected in the rate
  • Check with a tax accountant whether the remuneration includes work subject to withholding tax

How Does an FDE's Contract Type Change by Years of Experience, Current Position, English Ability, and Company Size? Who Is It Not Suited For?

An FDE's contract type is determined less by years of experience than by "whether they can write specifications themselves." FDE Soken organizes this by applying four prerequisite conditions to quasi-mandate (jun-iinin) and contract for work (ukeoi) under the Civil Code.

Differences by Years of Experience and Current Position

For those with little experience or working a side job, quasi-mandate is the basic choice. A contract for work is best chosen only after gaining experience handling everything alone from requirements definition to delivery.

Prerequisite ConditionSuitable Contract TypePoints to Check
Less than 3 years of practical experienceQuasi-mandate (with a cap on working hours)Structure it so that no responsibility for completion is borne. Whether the client has a review system in place
3 or more years of experience, with requirements definition experienceCombine quasi-mandate and contract for work by phaseDecide in advance a date to review the contract when switching phases
Currently a full-time employee contracting as a side jobQuasi-mandate (clearly stating a weekly cap on working hours)The side-job provisions of the employment rules, non-compete clauses, and whether deliverable rights belong to the current employer
Already independent, contracting with multiple companiesUse quasi-mandate, contract for work, and deliverable-completion quasi-mandate appropriatelyApplicability of the New Freelance Law, invoice registration, cap on damages

Differences by English Ability and Company Size

When working with overseas customers or foreign-affiliated companies, decide the governing law and jurisdiction for disputes first. If the counterparty is a large company, vendor registration and checking internal regulations come first.

  • When contracting and working in English: The definitions of "Services" and "Deliverables" in English contracts may diverge from quasi-mandate and contract for work under Japanese law. Clearly state the governing law, the court of jurisdiction, and which language version is the authoritative text.
  • The client is a large company: Vendor registration, information security reviews, and the scope of permitted modifications to template contracts are often fixed. Check whether there is room to supplement non-negotiable clauses with a memorandum in an appendix. The revised Subcontract Act (Act on the Rationalization of Small and Medium-Sized Subcontract Transactions) taking effect in January 2026 applies depending on whether the parties meet the capital and employee count criteria.
  • The client is a startup: There is a tendency for work to begin without a contract. Use the disclosure obligation under Article 3 of the New Freelance Law as leverage to have at least the minimum terms put in writing.

How Does FDE Business Outsourcing Differ from Full-Time SWEs, IT Consultants, PdMs, and SIers? Who Is It Not Suited For?

An FDE working under business outsourcing, unlike a full-time SWE, works on the premise of not being a "worker" under Article 9 of the Labor Standards Act. It is a way of working outside the protections of working hours and dismissal.

As this table shows, unless the contract specifies who absorbs scope changes, a contract-based FDE tends to absorb them personally and without compensation. As a practitioner at VACAN, I believe this is the point that deserves the most time in contract negotiations.

Who is not suited to being a contract-based FDE

For those who find it difficult to proactively negotiate terms in ambiguous situations, being a contract-based FDE will be a heavy burden.

  • Those who accept specification changes as requested and struggle to issue additional estimates
  • Those who value the protections of employment, such as stable working hours and social insurance
  • Those who want to focus on the technology itself rather than the customer's business (a full-time SWE is likely a better fit)
  • Those who do not want to manage contracting, invoicing, and tax paperwork themselves

What clauses should be included in an FDE contract for work? Sample wording for scope changes, deliverable definitions, data deletion, and generative AI?

An FDE contract for work should include, at minimum, four clauses: scope changes, deliverable definitions, data deletion, and generative AI use. Compliance with Article 61, Paragraph 2 of the Copyright Act is also required.

The sample clauses below illustrate general principles. When actually using them, have them reviewed by a lawyer in light of the circumstances of the project.

Sample clauses for scope changes and deliverable definitions

For scope changes, the key is to state "no work begins until agreed in writing"; for deliverable definitions, to state "acceptance criteria and deadlines."

(Changes to Scope of Work) When Party A or Party B requests a change to the scope or content of the work, the parties shall consult through a document (including electronic means) describing the change, additional compensation, and impact on the schedule, and shall not begin the modified work until both parties have agreed.
(Acceptance of Deliverables) Party A shall inspect the deliverables in accordance with the acceptance criteria in the appendix within 10 business days from the date of delivery by Party B and notify Party B of the results. If no notice is given within this period, the deliverables shall be deemed to have passed inspection.

The "10 business days" inspection period is an example. Set a length that does not conflict with the payment deadline of 60 days or less under the new Freelance Act.

Sample clauses for data deletion and generative AI use

For customer data, specify "deletion after contract termination and reporting of the deletion"; for generative AI, specify "the permitted scope of use and information that must not be entered."

(Return and Deletion of Data) Within 30 days from the termination date of this agreement, Party B shall return or delete the data provided by Party A and any copies thereof, and shall report in writing to Party A that deletion has been completed.
(Use of Generative AI) Party B may use generative AI in the work only with services separately approved by Party A. Party B shall not enter Party A's confidential information or personal information into generative AI services configured to use input content for model training.

When handling personal data, the client has a duty under Article 25 of the Act on the Protection of Personal Information to supervise the contractor. On the FDE side, being prepared for questions about access permissions and the work environment will help discussions go smoothly.

7 items to verify before signing

Before signing, verify the following 7 items in order, including rights attribution and liability caps.

  • Whether the contract type (quasi-delegation, contract for work, or deliverable-completion quasi-delegation) matches the workflow
  • Whether the point of contact for direction (the responsible person on the contractor's side) is clearly stated
  • The procedure for scope changes and how additional compensation is determined
  • Acceptance criteria for deliverables, inspection period, and payment deadline for compensation (within 60 days)
  • The scope of copyright transfer (especially whether rights under Articles 27 and 28 are explicitly stated), the clause waiving exercise of moral rights of authorship, and the handling of code the individual already possessed
  • Rules for data return/deletion and generative AI use
  • The cap on damages (e.g., the total amount of compensation received) and the notice period for mid-term termination
