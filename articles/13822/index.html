Learn Forward Deployed Engineering

Fieldwork · 2026-11-13 · 4 min read

Summary

This article presents a self-study roadmap for becoming a Forward Deployed Engineer, organized into 6 phases and 11 skills over roughly 24–36 weeks. Each phase has concrete readiness criteria and a proof-of-skill project, such as deploying a CRUD app with a failure plan, building a permission-aware RAG assistant with evals, and running an end-to-end mock client engagement. It covers engineering basics, applied GenAI, consulting-style discovery, production reliability, stakeholder handling, and interview preparation, emphasizing ownership of outcomes in messy real-world conditions.

Key points

  • Six-phase roadmap with 11 skills, roughly 24–36 weeks total.
  • Every phase ends with a proof-of-skill project, not just study.
  • Covers discovery, permission-aware RAG, evals, and incident response.
  • Phase 5 simulates clients: pushback, incidents, and rollout gates.
  • Final phase drills interview frameworks and senior-level judgment.

The roadmap

  • 1

    Engineering baseline

    2–4 weeks · skip if you ship production code todayBe able to build, deploy and run a small app on your own, and explain what happens when it breaks.You're ready to move on when
    • You write Python and SQL with tests, logging and config instead of hardcoded values.
    • You can deploy to a live URL with environment variables and no secrets in the code.
    • You're comfortable with Git, the command line and calling REST APIs.
    Proof-of-skill projectA deployed app with a failure planShip a small CRUD app with login to a public URL. Its README explains how to run it, what happens when the database or an API is down, and how you'd roll back a bad deploy.
  • 2

    AI foundations & first builds

    4–6 weeksUnderstand how LLMs behave and build something that makes useful decisions from messy input.You're ready to move on when
    • You can explain tokens, embeddings and context windows without jargon.
    • You use structured outputs and write prompts as precise specs.
    • You measure quality on a small real dataset instead of eyeballing it.
    Proof-of-skill projectTriage app from a three-sentence briefTake a vague brief ("help our team sort incoming requests"). Write category definitions, measure how often two people agree on 30 samples, build a classifier with structured output, keep keys server-side, and ship it to a live URL with its accuracy on your test set.
  • 3

    Discovery & consulting core

    4–6 weeks · run alongside the next phaseDiagnose before you prescribe. Learn to find the real problem and get people aligned around it.You're ready to move on when
    • You turn vague requests into sharp problem statements with a baseline and a trust threshold.
    • You map real workflows, including exceptions, and spot the decision points.
    • You can write and present an answer-first recommendation in two minutes.
    Proof-of-skill projectOne-page discovery memo + two-minute pitchPick a realistic business with several problems (a clinic, a logistics firm, a support team). Rank the problems with evidence, recommend one pilot, name its owner, list what you won't build, then record a two-minute executive pitch.
  • 4

    Production GenAI & integration

    8–10 weeksBuild systems that survive real data, real permissions and real failures.You're ready to move on when
    • Your RAG system filters by permission before retrieval and cites its sources.
    • Every change runs against an eval suite with written release gates.
    • Your ingestion job is retry-safe and you can see every request in traces.
    Proof-of-skill projectPermission-aware document assistantBuild an assistant over a document set with per-user permissions. Include citations, an eval suite with gates, request tracing, a retry-safe incremental sync, a written failure register, and a short security data-flow diagram.
  • 5

    Client simulation & ownership

    4–6 weeksPerform under pressure with real people in the loop: pushback, incidents and adoption problems.Stakeholder communicationReliability & incident responseRollout & adoptionOwnership & business valueYou're ready to move on when
    • You handle a security block, an impatient exec and an angry user in role-plays without caving or arguing.
    • You can run the first 90 minutes of an incident and write the customer update.
    • Your rollout plans have gates, stop conditions and named owners.
    Proof-of-skill projectEnd-to-end mock engagementWith a partner playing the customer: run discovery, build a small pilot, deploy it, present it, then handle a simulated incident and write the postmortem. Record it and review it against the interview rubric.
  • 6

    Interview readiness & senior judgment

    2–4 weeks · repeat as neededTurn skill into offers, and show the judgment that separates senior FDEs.You're ready to move on when
    • You run a scenario answer framework by reflex: context, current system, success, integration, failure, security, rollout, trade-offs.
    • You have 6–8 first-person stories with numbers, covering ambiguity, incidents, conflict and delivery.
    • You score 4/5 or better on every rubric dimension in three unaided mocks in a row.
    Proof-of-skill project45-minute capstone, no notesTake a struggling AI pilot that users have stopped trusting. In 45 minutes, diagnose it, propose a fix, define evals and gates, plan the rollout and write the executive update, then score yourself honestly.

All skills

Problem discovery & reframing

Turn a vague request into a sharp problem worth solving, before you write any code.

Workflow & process mapping

See how work really flows, including the spreadsheet nobody admits to.

Stakeholder communication

Explain the same truth to an exec, an engineer and a frontline user, and get a decision.

Practical coding & data

Clean, tested code for messy real-world data, written while you explain your thinking.

Integration & enterprise systems

Get your software working inside the customer's CRM, warehouse and legacy APIs.

Applied GenAI: RAG, agents & tools

Build LLM systems that are grounded, bounded and debuggable, not just impressive in a demo.

Evaluation & quality

Prove it works before launch, and know the moment it stops working after.

Security, identity & data governance

Earn the CISO's yes: auth, permissions, tenancy and data handling done properly.

Reliability & incident response

Build systems that fail safely, and stay calm when they break at 2 AM.

Rollout & adoption

Take it from demo to pilot to production, and make sure people actually use it.

Ownership & business value

Own the outcome, not the ticket. Then turn what you learn in the field into product.
