Where FDE Support Comes In Handy: Challenges It's Suited For and Not Suited For

Original title: FDE支援の使いどころ｜向いている課題・向かない課題

株式会社Sai · 2026-07-25 · 5 min read

Summary

This article by Sai Inc. examines when FDE-style support works and when it does not, from the client's perspective. It argues FDEs excel when a problem exists but requirements cannot yet be defined, since discovering requirements is part of the job. It is unsuitable for large projects with fixed specs, problems solved by off-the-shelf SaaS, or clients expecting to hand everything over. Client preparation needs only three things: willingness to show real operations, one key on-site contact, and agreement to start small. ROI should be judged via cost reduction, growth value, and asset value (retained in-house capability). When choosing a partner, verify who actually comes on-site, what the first two weeks involve, and whether an exit plan exists.

Key points

  • FDE support fits best when problems exist but requirements are undefined.
  • It is wrong for fixed-spec large builds, SaaS-sufficient needs, or full delegation.
  • Clients need three preparations: show real operations, one key contact, start small.
  • Judge ROI by cost reduction, growth value, and retained in-house capability.
  • Vet partners on who shows up, first-two-weeks plan, and exit design.

The term FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is appearing more and more often, and a growing number of executives and DX managers are wondering, "Could we use this too?"

The short answer: FDE-style support is not a panacea. The problems it helps and the problems it doesn't are clearly distinct. This article explains how to tell the difference from the perspective of the commissioning company.

Problems FDE-style support is "suited to"

① A problem exists, but requirements can't be pinned down

"We know our operations are inefficient, but we can't put into words what should be systematized or how"—this vague, murky stage is exactly where FDEs excel. Traditional contracted development can't be commissioned without settled requirements, but for an FDE, finding the requirements is part of the job.

② Wanting to run things in-house after the build

For companies that say, "We don't want to stay dependent on a vendor; eventually we want to improve things ourselves," the FDE model—transferring skills while building together on-site—is ideal. See the in-housing roadmap for details.

③ Wanting generative AI and automation to "take hold in operations," not just to "try it out"

For cases where you want to go beyond tool rollouts and training and actually change day-to-day operations. Generative AI business automation only delivers results when paired with on-site friction removal and adoption support.

④ The launch phase of a new business or service

In the launch phase, where specs change weekly, traditional development that assumes settled requirements is a terrible fit. The FDE approach of rapidly cycling between observation and implementation (the six phases) has a structure that handles this uncertainty well.

Problems FDE-style support is "not suited to"

In the interest of fairness, let's also be clear about the cases where it doesn't fit.

  • Large-scale development with fully settled requirements: For large builds with a spec sheet and no changes, handing it to a well-staffed contracted developer or SIer is cheaper and more reliable
  • Problems an off-the-shelf tool already solves: For areas like attendance management or expense reimbursement where SaaS is sufficient, custom work is unnecessary. It's worth consulting an FDE just to make this judgment, but you shouldn't build it
  • The expectation of "just handing it all over": FDE is a model that delivers results through collaboration with the front lines. If the field can't spare any time at all, its strengths won't show

For a detailed comparison of the different types of consultants, see Who should you consult about AI adoption and DX?.

The 3 preparations needed on the receiving side

The success or failure of FDE-style support also depends heavily on the receiving side's preparation. That said, nothing elaborate is required.

  • Willingness to show the front lines: Showing the reality of operations, actual data, and the parts that aren't working is the starting point. There is no need to "tidy things up before showing them"
  • Naming one key person from the field: Having a field-side contact who can spend a few hours a week answering questions and giving feedback changes the speed by several times
  • Agreement to start small: Not aiming for company-wide reform from day one, but making success in one workflow and one team the first goal—this agreement from management protects the project

How should cost-effectiveness be evaluated?

FDE-style support should be viewed not as "outsourced development billed per person-month" but as an investment in problem-solving and organizational capability. As a rule of thumb, we recommend the following three questions.

  • How many hours per month, and how much cost, does the target workflow consume? (reduction value)
  • If the problem were solved, what would happen to revenue and customer experience? (growth value)
  • How do you value leaving behind an in-house ability to "build and improve"? (asset value)

The third is especially easy to overlook, but whether the organization keeps improving after the engagement ends completely changes the payback period. For a detailed guide to using these three yardsticks, see How to think about the cost-effectiveness of AI investment.

Checkpoints for selecting a partner

More and more companies call themselves FDE or accompaniment-style, but the reality varies widely. In sales meetings, confirm the following three points.

  • Who will actually come to the front lines—Is the proposer the same as the executor? Verify the skills and experience of the people who will actually do the hands-on work
  • What they will do in the first two weeks—Can they answer concretely? If the answer is "we'll start with requirements definition," that's traditional contracted development
  • Is there a graduation plan—How do they define adoption and handover? Be wary of proposals that assume they will stay indefinitely

As for the earlier decision of whether to use an external partner or hire FDE talent in-house, see Choosing between hiring FDEs and using external partners for a detailed breakdown.
