Is a Career Change to FDE Possible in Your 40s? Valued Experience and How to Overcome the Age Barrier

Original title: 40代のFDE転職は可能？評価される経験と年齢の壁の越え方

FDE総研 · 2026-11-17 · 10 min read

Summary

This article from FDE Soken (run by VACAN) argues that changing careers into a Forward Deployed Engineer role in one's 40s is feasible, with experience mattering more than age. It frames evaluation around three stages: defining customer problems, implementing solutions, and getting them adopted in the field. Tables compare how current role (SIer PM, in-house SWE, IT consultant, internal SE) and years of experience affect transition difficulty, recommending filling gaps in order: customer contact, implementation ability, domain knowledge, then English. It notes Japanese law generally prohibits age limits in hiring, and offers concrete resume, portfolio, and interview preparation advice, plus a comparison of FDE against SWE, IT consulting, PdM, and SIer roles.

Key points

  • Hiring should weigh end-to-end problem definition, implementation, and adoption over age.
  • Main disadvantages for 40s candidates: distance from hands-on coding and fixed-role preferences.
  • Recommended gap-filling order: customer contact, implementation, domain knowledge, English.
  • Japanese law (Article 9) generally bans age limits in recruiting, with narrow exceptions.
  • Interview prep: 3-minute case narratives covering situation, judgment, result, and limits.

Changing careers to FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) in your 40s is possible. FDE Soken believes the evaluation criteria should weigh "experience of defining customer business problems yourself, implementing solutions, and carrying them through to actual on-site use" more heavily than age.

Can you switch to FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) in your 40s? What experience gets evaluated in hiring?

An FDE career change in your 40s is possible, and the evaluation criterion FDE Soken values most is experience of carrying through all three stages—problem definition, implementation, and adoption—as one continuous effort (for the origin of the role, see Palantir's hiring page).

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer role that embeds at customer sites and handles everything from problem definition to software implementation and on-site operation and adoption, alone or as a team. Widely known as a job title used by Palantir, in Japan job postings with the same name or a similar role are appearing, mainly at SaaS and AI-related companies. FDE Soken is a media outlet operated by VACAN covering FDE careers and practice.

What experience makes FDE candidates in their 40s more likely to be evaluated favorably?

For FDE candidates in their 40s, what gets evaluated is experience of understanding customer operations, making technical decisions yourself, and getting results adopted in the field. Rather than lines of code written or the number of technologies handled, the tendency is to value being able to explain "what the problem was, why you chose that approach, and how it came to be used."

FDE Soken recommends that people in their 40s inventory their experience across the following three stages.

What are the disadvantages in an FDE career change in your 40s?

The two main disadvantages in an FDE career change in your 40s are being long removed from hands-on implementation and showing a preference for fixed roles. FDE is a role that is hard to perform with "management only" or "implementation only"—the day after meeting with a customer, you may find yourself processing data or fixing the UI yourself.

From VACAN's practical perspective, the longer someone has been in a management role, the more realistic it is to prepare for being asked in interviews "when was the last time you built something yourself and showed it to a customer." If you have something you built and ran yourself within the last one to two years, even outside of work, this concern eases considerably.

How do years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size change an FDE career change in your 40s?

The difficulty of an FDE career change in your 40s varies with four conditions: years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size. FDE Soken treats "whether your current role puts you in contact with customer sites" as the top-priority condition (you can check the role's requirements on Palantir's hiring page).

How do combinations of years of experience and current role change an FDE career change in your 40s?

In terms of combinations of experience and current role, "engineers who have worked at customer sites" transition most easily, while "internal system maintenance only" takes the most preparation time. Rather than the length of your career itself, what matters is how much time you have spent interacting directly with customers.

How do English ability and company size change how you choose an FDE role in your 40s?

Regarding English ability and company size, at large foreign companies English proficiency tends to be a prerequisite, while domestic startups tend to weigh industry knowledge heavily. If your English is strong but your years of experience are short, it is reasonable to prioritize building an implementation track record first and treat English ability as a condition for broadening where you apply.

When conditions conflict, FDE Soken recommends filling gaps in the priority order of "customer touchpoints → implementation skills → industry knowledge → English ability." English skills can broaden your application options later, but lacking customer touchpoints and implementation skills makes it likely you'll be judged as unsuited to the role itself.

How do you get past age barriers in job listings, such as "up to 40 years old"?

In Japan, Article 9 of the Act on Comprehensively Advancing Labor Measures in principle prohibits age limits in recruitment and hiring. FDE Soken believes that age barriers are overcome not in the job listing itself but in the selection process.

If a job listing says "up to 40 years old," can you not apply?

Even if a job listing says "up to 40 years old," an age cap is permitted in mid-career hiring for FDE roles only when it falls under exception grounds specified by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. A typical exception is recruiting young people for indefinite-term employment for the purpose of long-term career development, in which case conditions such as not requiring prior work experience apply.

If an FDE job listing that requires practical experience states only an age limit, check whether the exception grounds are stated. After that, it is realistic to either apply or, via a career-change agent, verify whether experience is weighted more heavily than age. For interpretations of the law and the appropriateness of individual job listings, check the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's guidance as the primary source.

What should people in their 40s do to remove age concerns during selection?

For people in their 40s, the most effective way to dispel age concerns is to demonstrate "speed of relearning" and "flexibility in working with younger bosses and client contacts" with concrete examples. This is because recruiters' concerns about people in their 40s often lie less in age itself than in adaptability to new technologies and ease of integration within the organization.

  • Show one technology you recently learned in the past year and something you built with it
  • Talk about an experience delivering results under a younger leader
  • Explain, in terms of how you work, why you don't insist on titles or positions
  • State upfront whether you can accommodate on-site assignments and business trips to client locations

Should people in their 40s choose FDE, SWE, IT consulting, PdM, or SIer? Who is not suited to it?

For people in their 40s choosing a role: if you want to handle implementation on-site with customers, FDE fits; if you want to deepen your technical skills, SWE fits. FDE Soken compares the five roles along two axes: "distance from the customer" and "weight of implementation" (see also Palantir's careers page).

What kind of person in their 40s is not suited to FDE?

People in their 40s not suited to FDE are those who want to work within a clearly defined scope of responsibilities, and those who find uncertain situations at client sites burdensome. In FDE work, tasks shift day by day among requirements gathering, data processing, implementation, and explaining to clients. Whether you can enjoy this variety determines suitability more than age does.

  • For those who want to focus on developing subordinates as a manager, PdM or engineering manager tends to be a better fit
  • For those who want to master a single technical domain deeply, SWE tends to be a better fit
  • Those with family circumstances that make business trips or on-site client work difficult need to confirm working conditions before applying

From VACAN's practical perspective, we believe aptitude for FDE-like roles lies not in "someone who builds what the customer asks for" but in "someone who, while building, helps uncover problems the customer hasn't yet been able to articulate."

What resume, portfolio, and interview preparation should people in their 40s prepare for an FDE career change?

In an FDE career change for people in their 40s, the key is to make the three elements—resume, portfolio, and interview—consistent with the flow of "problem definition, implementation, and adoption." This is the preparation framework FDE Soken recommends (role requirements can be checked on Palantir's careers page).

What should people in their 40s write in their resume and portfolio?

For a resume of someone in their 40s, it is more effective to write deeply about two or three representative projects following FDE's three stages, rather than listing your career chronologically. For the portfolio, prioritize making the customer's problem and your own judgment readable, rather than scale.

ResumeFor each project: "the problem, your judgment, the result, and the remaining limitations"A mere list of technologies used and project names

What should people in their 40s prepare for an FDE interview?

For an FDE interview for someone in their 40s, it is effective to prepare on the assumption that you will be asked in case format "how you would translate a customer's vague request into a defined problem." Being able to talk not only about past successes but also about mistakes in judgment and what you changed as a result conveys your speed of relearning.

  • Organize a representative project so you can present it in three minutes in the order "situation → judgment → result → limitations"
  • Practice explaining aloud what you would do in the first week for a hypothetical case with a vague customer request
  • Be ready to explain, for code you wrote recently, the design rationale and what you would change if you rebuilt it
  • Decide in advance your own position on on-site assignments, business trips, and working hours

At FDE Soken, we see a career change in one's 40s not as "competing on the same playing field as younger candidates" but as an opportunity to reposition yourself as "someone who can turn their understanding of business and experience in judgment into customer outcomes through implementation."
