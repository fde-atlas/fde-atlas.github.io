Forward deployed engineer vs solutions engineer

Kernavique · 2026-11-20 · 15 min read

Summary

Kernavique compares job ads from Palantir, Anthropic, Databricks, Scale AI, Ramp and OpenAI to distinguish forward deployed engineers from solutions architects (the closest readable title to solutions engineer). Every forward deployed ad requires shipping production code in customer environments, while solutions architect ads describe pre-sales advisory work—Databricks asks for pre-sales experience and Scale AI places the role in Enterprise Sales. The article offers five checks for decoding what a posting really is, and notes Bloomberry found 60% of forward deployed postings are coding-heavy and 0% carry quotas.

Key points

  • All forward deployed ads require production code; no solutions architect ad lists it as a duty.
  • Databricks asks for pre-sales experience; Scale AI files architects under Enterprise Sales.
  • Palantir's Delta works one customer; Dev builds the platform, with embedding optional and short.
  • Five checks: department, code line, sale timing, travel figure, and success metrics.
  • Bloomberry: 60% of postings are builders coding 70-90%; exactly 0% are quota-carrying.

A solutions architect, the nearest title to solutions engineer in the ads we read, is the technical voice in a deal: Databricks asks for pre-sales experience and Scale AI places the role in Enterprise Sales. A product software engineer builds the platform itself. We read no employer ad titled sales engineer or solutions engineer, so those comparisons rest on neighboring titles and third-party comment.

The short version

Four things separate these roles in employers’ own ads: whether the person writes production code, whether the work comes before or after the sale, how much of it happens at the customer’s site, and which department the role sits in.

  • Forward deployed engineer. Builds and ships production software for one customer at a time. Every employer source we read says so.
  • Solutions architect. Advises, prototypes and carries the technical side of a deal. It is the nearest title to “solutions engineer” that we could read in an employer’s ad.
  • Product software engineer. Builds the platform that every customer uses. Palantir’s ad treats time embedded with a customer as an optional short assignment.
  • Sales engineer. We read no employer ad with this title. What follows on it is third-party comment, labeled as such.

Unless a line says otherwise, every ad and page below was read on November 20, 2026. For what the role involves day to day, see our forward deployed engineer role page.

Two things stand out. Every forward deployed row says the person builds production software, and no solutions architect row lists that as a duty. And two solutions architect ads name the sale directly: Databricks’ asks for pre-sales experience, and Scale AI’s places the role in Enterprise Sales.

How named employers describe each role

Forward deployed engineer. Palantir’s ads say these engineers “work directly with customers to quickly understand their greatest problems” and “lead the way in developing a solution, from high-level system design and prototyping to application development”. Its Tactical Edge ad adds: “your responsibilities look similar to those of a startup CTO”.

Databricks says its forward deployed engineers “will work with customers to build and productionize solutions to their data & AI challenges” (ad first published August 21, 2026). Anthropic’s manager ad says they “embed directly with strategic customers” (first published February 5, 2026).

Ramp’s engineering blog goes furthest on product work. The team “builds generalized features, platforms, and AI that increase our rate of reaching product-market fit in Enterprise”, and is “probably the most cross-functional and comms-heavy engineering team in the org.” The page we read showed no author or date. Ramp also says the role varies by company with “the boundaries of ownership with other solutions functions.”

Solutions architect. Scale AI’s ad (first published April 22, 2025) puts the role beside an Account Executive, a Project Manager and a Tech Lead, and asks for “Hands-on proficiency in Python or equivalent language”. The work it lists is prototypes and demos. Anthropic’s public sector architect works with “Sales, Product, and Engineering teams” and prefers “5+ years in Solutions Architect, Sales Engineer, Technical Account Manager or equivalent roles” (first published September 23, 2026).

Databricks called the role “the main technical voice for Databricks”. We read that ad only as a copy on an investor’s job board, where it was marked closed, so treat it as a past ad.

Product software engineer. Palantir’s backend engineer ad opens: “Backend Software Engineers at Palantir build software at scale to transform how organisations use data.” The same ad offers an optional “Frontline” program: “This unique, short-term assignment involves being embedded with customers”. In Palantir’s own ad, then, customer embedding is a temporary assignment for a product engineer and the whole job for a forward deployed one.

The Pragmatic Engineer (Gergely Orosz, August 12, 2025) reports Palantir’s shorthand for the split: a Delta is “one customer, many capabilities” and a Dev is “one capability, many customers.” The article is quoting a Palantir blog post that we could not open, so we give it as a third party’s report.

Sales engineer. No employer ad with this title was read, so we have no employer definition. The only employer text that names the title is Anthropic’s architect ad, which lists it as a qualifying background. Two third parties comment:

  • The Pragmatic Engineer says titles such as Solutions Architect, Sales Engineer and Technical Delivery Engineer come close, and that forward deployed engineers “also contribute to the product their company sells.” Of OpenAI’s solutions architects it says “they rarely write code on customers’ infrastructure.”
  • First Round Review (published February 24, 2026) quotes Tiffany Siu of First Round, who previously hired for Palantir. Her view is that a founder who wants the hire to carry deal or demo quotas wants someone closer to sales. For sales-type roles she suggests metrics such as “quota, user adoption and usage.”

The title variants and what the ads show

Forward deployed software engineer. Palantir’s title. Its New York commercial ad asks for at least one year of post-college work experience and “Ability and interest to travel up to 25%, as needed to client sites”. Palantir’s board lists these ads under the team label “Delta”. The ads do not explain the label.

Forward deployed AI engineer. Also Palantir’s. Its New York ad likens the job to “a hands-on AI startup CTO” (read October 11, 2026).

Deployment strategist. Palantir’s second customer-facing role, under the team label “Echo”, again unexplained in the ads. The New York ad says: “At its core, the Deployment Strategist role centers around using data in operational and real-world action.” The US Government ad lists duties such as “Go onsite and meet with customer analysts to understand the critical questions they need to answer” and “Lead training sessions to ensure that the product is meeting user needs”.

That ad calls programming experience “a plus”. The New York ad, as our read captured it, lists the same experience under requirements, so check the ad in front of you. Palantir publishes $110,000 to $170,000 a year for the deployment strategist against $135,000 to $200,000 a year for the forward deployed software engineer.

Applied AI engineer. Anthropic’s title, on the same Applied AI team as its forward deployed engineers and its architects. The public sector ad (first published October 6, 2026) asks for “Strong programming skills with proficiency in Python or TypeScript and experience building production applications”. Its duties lean toward the customer’s own engineers: “Lead hands-on technical workshops and code reviews with customer engineering teams”.

Other names for nearby work. Scale AI titles one forward deployed ad “Frontier Agents Engineer (Forward Deployed Engineering)” and runs a separate “Field Engineer, Public Sector” role built around data integrations.

How to tell which job an ad really is

The title will not tell you. Bloomberry, which analyzed 1,000 postings, says companies use the forward deployed title “to describe three different jobs” (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026). By its own classification, which has no stated method, 60% are builders doing “70-90% coding, 30-50% travel” and 30% are a “Sales Engineer+” variant doing “30-40% coding, <20% travel”.

Five checks, each taken from the ads above:

  • Find the department. Scale AI files its forward deployed ad under “Enterprise Engineering” and its solutions architect ad under “Enterprise Sales”. Databricks files its forward deployed ad under “Professional Services Operations”.
  • Read the code line. “Build and productionize” is one job. Prototypes and demos are another. Code as “a plus” is a third.
  • Look for the sale. Words such as pre-sales, deal and close place the role before the contract. “Billable” and post-sales point to work after it.
  • Check the travel figure. Most of the forward deployed ads in the table give one, from 20% to 75%. The solutions architect ads we read give none.
  • Ask how success is judged. Most ads do not say. Ramp ties team metrics to customer and business outcomes. If the answer in an interview is a quota, note that Bloomberry found exactly 0% of the postings it analyzed were quota-carrying.

If the ad passes these checks as a builder role, expect an engineering loop. Siu told First Round Review that Palantir’s forward deployed applicants “had to pass the same interview loops and facets as software engineers.” Our forward deployed engineer interview guide covers what is public about those loops.

Which one to hire

Start from the work you need done in the next year.

  • Production code inside a customer’s systems, with lessons flowing back into your product. That is the forward deployed engineer in every employer source above. Jake Stauch, CEO of Serval, told First Round Review to treat the hire as “an actual member of the software engineering team”.
  • Technical help winning deals. Scale AI’s solutions architect ad describes that job: discovery through close, with prototypes and demos.
  • On-site work with users, training and presenting to executives. Palantir’s deployment strategist ads describe that, with code optional in one of the two we read.
  • A capability every customer is asking for. That is product engineering. Palantir’s Dev ad keeps customer embedding optional and short.

Be clear about where the role reports. Ramp keeps the team inside engineering. Databricks puts it in professional services and calls the engineers billable. Both are employers’ real choices, and they produce different jobs.

Check that you have the customers for it. James Honsa, co-founder of Genera and formerly head of legal engineering at Ironclad, told First Round Review that the work is “definitionally an upmarket motion.”

The two roles also draw on overlapping people. In Bloomberry’s sample of 100 LinkedIn profiles, 22% of forward deployed engineers were previously solutions engineers or architects (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026). Bloomberry does not say how the profiles were chosen. Our guide on how to hire a forward deployed engineer covers screening and where these engineers come from.

In practice

If you are an engineer, run the five checks before you apply, and ask in the first conversation where the role reports and how much of the week is code. If the builder version is the one you want, send us your details and résumé.

If you are hiring, write down the code you expect this person to ship, whether the work starts before or after the contract, the travel, and the department. Then choose the title that matches, not the one that is fashionable. If the answer is a forward deployed engineer, ask Kernavique for help.

What we left out

  • An employer definition of the sales engineer. We read no ad with that title, and we did not fill the gap from general knowledge.
  • Any ad titled solutions engineer. The neighboring ads we could read are titled solutions architect. OpenAI’s solutions ads were not read.
  • What “Delta” and “Echo” mean in Palantir’s words. We saw only the team labels on its job board. Palantir’s blog post on the split could not be opened.
  • Quoted lines from OpenAI’s ad. Its ad pages returned only a title on November 20, 2026, so the OpenAI row rests on our October 11, 2026 read and a third-party article.
  • Ramp’s own job ad. We read its engineering blog, not an ad.
  • Databricks’ solutions architect on its own site. We read a copy of a closed ad, so we do not describe the role as open.
  • Split of coding time for the neighboring roles. No ad we read gives one.

Questions

  • Is a forward deployed engineer the same as a solutions engineer?
  • Not in the ads we read on November 20, 2026. Forward deployed engineer ads from Databricks, Scale AI and Anthropic all describe building production systems for customers. The nearest neighboring title we could read, solutions architect, is described as an advisory or pre-sales role: Scale AI’s ad sits in its Enterprise Sales department and asks for prototypes and demos. We read no ad titled solutions engineer.
  • Is a forward deployed engineer a sales engineer?
  • We read no employer ad titled sales engineer, so there is no employer definition to compare. Bloomberry classes 30% of postings with the forward deployed title as a “Sales Engineer+” variant doing “30-40% coding”, and found that none of the postings it analyzed were quota-carrying (Bloomberry, updated January 25, 2026). Its classification has no stated method.
  • Does a forward deployed engineer write production code?
  • Yes, in every employer source we read. Scale AI’s ad says its engineers “architect, integrate, deploy, and operate production AI systems”, Databricks says they “build and productionize solutions”, and Ramp says “We spend most of our time building features, many of which are shipped in the core product.” All three were read on November 20, 2026.
  • What is a deployment strategist at Palantir?
  • A customer-facing role that Palantir lists under the team label “Echo”, beside forward deployed software engineers under “Delta”. Its US Government ad calls programming experience “a plus” and asks for travel of 25 to 75%. Duties include meeting customer analysts on site, leading training sessions and presenting to executives. The ads do not explain the two labels (read November 20, 2026).
  • How much does each role travel?
  • The forward deployed ads give figures: 20% at Databricks, “up to 25%” for Palantir’s New York role and “as needed (25-50%)” in Anthropic’s manager ad (all read November 20, 2026). The solutions architect ads we read give no travel figure. Anthropic’s public sector architect and applied AI engineer ads say the work is primarily on-site in secure government facilities.
  • Which role should a company hire first?
  • Start from the work, not the title. If the person will own production code inside a customer’s systems, hire a forward deployed engineer and hold a software engineer’s bar. If the need is technical help winning deals, the solutions architect ads describe that job. If you plan to set deal or demo quotas, Tiffany Siu of First Round, who previously hired for Palantir, told First Round Review you want someone closer to sales.
