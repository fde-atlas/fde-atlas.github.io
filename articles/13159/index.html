AI Discovery Day: 2-Hour Use-Case Scoping Session ($500)

Nasser Ghanemzadeh · 2026-10-31 · 6 min read

Summary

Nasser Ghanemzadeh offers a paid "AI Discovery Day": a two-hour, $500 use-case scoping session for B2B SaaS teams. He maps the client's target workflow step by step, labeling each step as rule-following or judgement, then assesses whether it can ship as an autopilot within a 10-business-day Sprint. The session ends with a one-page memo containing a one-sentence Sprint Brief, a first scoring rubric, cost-of-replacement figures, and an explicit go or no-go recommendation. The $500 is credited toward a Sprint if one follows; if not, the client keeps the memo with no follow-up sales. The service targets founder-led B2B SaaS companies of 5–50 people post-product-market-fit, and excludes enterprise procurement processes, indie hackers, and generic AI strategy consulting.

02 · The document

What you walk away with

By the end of the 2 hours you have a one-page memo, written on the call and ready to forward to your team. It holds:

  • The workflow written out step by step, with each step marked as rule-following or judgement.
  • A one-sentence Sprint Brief, written with the Brief template.
  • A first rubric for scoring the workflow's output.
  • What the workflow replaces and what that costs you now.
  • Whether your data and APIs can carry it in a Sprint.
  • A go or no-go on whether a Sprint is the right next step, and if it isn't, what to do first.
  • Honest answers about what fits in 10 business days and what doesn't.

03 · Fit

Who this is for

  • B2B SaaS founders or product leaders with one workflow in mind for their first autopilot.
  • Teams with an AI engineer or forward deployed engineer role that has been open for more than a month.
  • Teams that tried building an AI workflow, in house or through a contractor or agency, and got stuck between prototype and production.
  • Teams that know AI should be part of their product but want a second opinion before committing engineering time.
  • Teams comparing Sprint vendors and wanting to validate fit before signing.

Who this is not for

  • Teams looking for a generic AI strategy consultation.
  • Solo indie hackers without a B2B SaaS product.
  • Enterprise teams with formal procurement or RFP processes.
  • Anyone who wants a quick first conversation. Start with the free 15-minute Scoping Call.

04 · The two hours

How a Discovery Day works

The 2 hours are structured but not rigid. Rough shape:

Minutes 0–30: Your context. Current product, team, AI experiments to date, what's worked and what hasn't, what's pulling the AI conversation forward inside your company.

Minutes 30–75: The workflow, step by step. With the person who does it today, I write the workflow out step by step and mark each step as rule-following or judgement: inputs, outputs, data sources, what good output looks like, who reviews it before it ships.

Minutes 75–105: Feasibility. What fits in a 10-day Sprint and what doesn't. Risk classification (low / medium / high). What the workflow replaces and what that costs you now. Where the Day-5 prototype likely lands.

Minutes 105–120: The memo. Go or no-go, the one-sentence Brief, a first rubric, and the next step. One page, shared as a Google Doc you keep.

A Discovery Day can end with a Sprint recommendation or with "here's what to do first, come back when X is in place." Both are valid outcomes. The point of a Discovery Day is to find out which one you are, not to convince you to buy a Sprint.

05 · The credit

The credit toward Sprint

If you continue to a full Sprint, the $500 is credited to the Sprint.

So a Discovery Day followed by a Sprint costs the Sprint price, not the Sprint price plus $500.

If we agree the Sprint isn't the right next step, the $500 covers the memo and the 2 hours. No upsell, no follow-up sequence, no retainer pitch.

06 · Before the call

What I need from you before the call

A short pre-call form, sent after booking. Three things:

  • The workflow you're considering. One paragraph is enough. Examples of the workflows a Discovery Day scopes: investor update assistant, runway scenario explainer, customer summary generator, support ticket triage, onboarding assistant.
  • Your stack and data access. What's your product built in, where does the relevant data live (Stripe, your DB, HubSpot, Linear, Notion, your CRM, your admin), and what AI provider you already use (Anthropic, OpenAI, Bedrock, Vertex) if any.
  • The decision-maker question. Who needs to approve a Sprint, and are they on the call. If they're not, a Discovery Day is fine, but the Sprint recommendation will be harder to act on.

That's it. No data dumps, no NDAs to sign before the call. We sign a mutual NDA on the call itself if either side wants one.

FAQ

What if we decide not to do a Sprint after the Discovery Day?

Then you walk away with the one-page memo and its go or no-go, plus 2 hours of focused outside perspective on your AI roadmap. No follow-up sales sequence. You're free to take the memo to another vendor, to your internal team, or to nobody at all.

Do you sign an NDA?

If either side wants a mutual NDA, we sign one on the call itself before getting into specifics. I don't ask for NDAs to be signed before booking, and I don't expect you to.

What if our data isn't ready?

Then the memo says so plainly: "the workflow makes sense, but the data foundation isn't there yet." The memo will say exactly what to do first, usually a 2 to 4 week data-readiness project that you can run internally, and we revisit Sprint readiness when it's done.

How is this different from the free Scoping Call?

The Scoping Call is free and takes 15 minutes: we pick the workflow and check whether your data can carry it in ten days. The Discovery Day is a paid two-hour working session that ends with a one-page memo you keep: the workflow written out step by step, the one-sentence Brief, a first rubric, and a go or no-go. Most founders start with the call.

What if I'm not sure I want a Sprint yet?

Then a Discovery Day is exactly the right starting point. A no-go is a useful result too: the memo says why, and what to do first.

Can a co-founder or technical lead join?

Yes. Up to three people from your side on the call. Beyond three the conversation slows down. If you want broader team alignment, the right pattern is: founder + tech lead on the Discovery Day, then I record a Loom walkthrough of the memo for everyone else.

What if our use case is in a high-risk category (legal, medical, compliance, payments)?

The Discovery Day still works, but the Sprint recommendation will likely be "not yet" or "redefine scope to a lower-risk adjacent workflow." High-risk first Sprints don't make sense for teams new to AI deployment. The memo will explain what does.

Do you work with companies outside B2B SaaS?

Mostly no. The methodology, examples, and Sprint structure are built around founder-led B2B SaaS at 5 to 50 people, post-product-market-fit. If you're outside that, the Discovery Day may still be useful but the Sprint may not be the right vehicle.
