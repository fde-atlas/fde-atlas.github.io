What is a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)? Why the role born at Palantir is growing in Japan | FDE board

Original title: Forward Deployed Engineer(FDE)とは｜Palantir発祥の職種が日本で増えている理由

Summary

This article explains the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role: engineers embedded at customer sites who write code themselves, taking products from vague problem definition to production, and feeding field learnings back into their company's product. The role originated at Palantir in the early 2010s (internal team name Delta), where FDEs outnumbered regular engineers until around 2016. Since 2025, LLM vendors like OpenAI and Anthropic have adopted the same model because enterprise AI deployment recreates the structural problem Palantir faced. As of August 2026, OpenAI and Palantir are hiring in Tokyo (bilingual Japanese/English required; Palantir lists 25–75% travel), and Japanese companies including LayerX, Sakana AI, JDSC, Loglass, and Stockmark post similar roles. The article distinguishes FDEs from solutions architects, sales engineers, and consultants, and notes job titles vary widely, so candidates should check both foreign companies' career sites and Japanese job boards.

Key points

  • FDEs work at customer sites, coding from problem definition through production.
  • Palantir created the role (team Delta); FDEs outnumbered regular engineers until ~2016.
  • LLM enterprise deployment since 2025 revived the same structural need.
  • OpenAI and Palantir hire FDEs in Tokyo; bilingual skills required.
  • Japanese startups like LayerX and Sakana AI also recruit for equivalent roles.

This article is for those who have seen the job title "Forward Deployed Engineer" on job sites and wondered whether it is an SE, a consultant, or contracted development work. This role was created by Palantir in the early 2010s, and since 2025, companies that deploy LLMs to enterprises have increasingly adopted it. Here, we explain what the role involves, how it came about, how it differs from similar roles, and the current hiring situation in Japan, based on what can be verified from job postings actually published as of the time of writing (2026-08).

The Short Answer

  • An FDE is an engineer who embeds themselves within a customer company, writes code themselves, and brings the product all the way to production. They are not an architect who just hands over design documents, nor a pre-sales person who only makes proposals.
  • The role was created by Palantir under the internal name "Delta," and until around 2016, the company reportedly had more FDEs than regular software engineers. Since 2025, companies on the deployment side of LLMs, such as OpenAI, have begun establishing the same type of role.
  • In Japan as well, as of the time of writing (2026-08), OpenAI and Palantir are hiring in Tokyo, and domestic AI companies are also recruiting under the same title or for essentially the same duties. A list of job postings can be found on FDE board.

What Does an FDE Do?

In a nutshell, a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is "an engineer who embeds themselves in the customer's workplace and implements things themselves until their company's product is running in the customer's business operations." As the name suggests—Deployed to the Forward (front) line—they are often seated not at their own company's office but on the customer's side, attending the customer's meetings, looking at the customer's data, and placing code in the customer's environment.

The biggest difference from a regular software engineer is how the work begins. Instead of receiving a specification document, the work starts from questions that have not yet taken shape, such as "Why can't we predict our order lead times?" or "How can we detect fraudulent transactions faster?" The same person is responsible for everything from deciding what to build together with the customer, to building it, running it in production, and fixing it when it breaks.

Another characteristic is that FDEs play a role in feeding what they observe in the field back into their company's product. Rather than leaving customer-specific customizations as one-off work, they distill recurring patterns into product features or internal templates. Both Palantir's and OpenAI's job postings contain language to the effect of "feeding field feedback back into product and model development." This means FDEs are positioned as part of product development, not merely an implementation support unit.

A Role Born at Palantir

This role was created by Palantir Technologies in the early 2010s. Internally, the company's FDSE (Forward Deployed Software Engineer) team is called "Delta," and the non-engineer team that untangles customers' business problems, called "Deployment Strategist," is called "Echo." This can still be confirmed from the team labels in the company's job postings as of the time of writing (2026-08).

Why was this model necessary? Because Palantir's early customers were intelligence agencies, which by their nature were in a position where they could not explain to outsiders "what problems they were facing." The usual contracted development approach of gathering requirements and taking them back to the office simply could not work. The starting point of this role was, in effect, "then just station engineers at the client site." Until around 2016, Palantir reportedly had more FDEs than regular software engineers.

What is interesting is that Palantir later consolidated the customer-specific implementations built under this model into a product called Foundry. The cycle in which field implementations become raw material for the product was designed in from the very beginning. Whether a company can treat FDEs not as an "unprofitable contracting division" but as an "entry point for product development" is what separates companies that adopt this role successfully from those that do not.

Why AI Companies Started Hiring FDEs After 2025

Even with high model performance, LLMs do not simply plug into a customer's business operations as-is. Which decisions in which processes should be delegated to AI, which internal data should be provided, and who notices and how when outputs are wrong—deciding and implementing all of this requires a full workstream for each customer. Moreover, this work becomes extremely slow unless the person who understands the customer's business and the person who can write code are the same person.

In other words, the same structure Palantir faced with intelligence agencies reappeared in enterprise LLM adoption. That is why the same solution was adopted—this is the reason this role has suddenly proliferated. As of the time of writing (2026-08), OpenAI is hiring FDEs in multiple cities including San Francisco, New York, Seattle, London, Paris, Tokyo, Seoul, and Singapore, and Anthropic is also advertising Forward Deployed Engineer positions in San Francisco, New York, Seattle, London, Paris, and Munich.

In Japan as well, LayerX announced the start of FDE hiring on its engineering blog in July 2025, and companies such as Sakana AI, JDSC, Loglass, and Stockmark have posted jobs with the same title or essentially the same duties. From late 2025 into 2026, the title can be considered to have become fairly established as a job category.

How It Differs from Similar Roles

The FDE role resembles each of several existing roles. However, since the "similar parts" differ in each case, a comparison table makes the distinctions clear.

The key is the two columns on the right. An FDE both places code in the customer's environment themselves and also makes changes to the internals of their own company's product. Having both of these simultaneously is what makes an FDE; if a role has only one, it should carry a different job title. When reading job postings, look for two things—"who does the implementation?" and "is there a path for feedback into the product?"—to distinguish postings that use the FDE title but differ in substance.

Hiring situation in Japan (as of August 2026)

Based on what can be confirmed from job postings published as of this writing (August 2026), FDE positions in Japan fall roughly into three types.

Foreign AI companies: OpenAI is hiring a Forward Deployed Engineer and a manager for that role in Tokyo, and Palantir is hiring a Forward Deployed Software Engineer (government) and a Deployment Strategist (commercial and government) in Tokyo. Both require fluency in Japanese and English, and Palantir's Tokyo job posting states 25-75% travel. Note that Anthropic has no Forward Deployed Engineer openings in Tokyo as of this writing, but is hiring for similar roles under the titles Applied AI Architect / Applied AI Engineer. This is one example of how searching by job title alone will cause you to miss openings.

Domestic AI startups place FDEs in roles that put their own AI products into customers' business operations. Salary ranges are lower than at foreign companies, but many positions do not require English, making them environments where people familiar with Japanese business practices can thrive. The third type is SIers and consulting firms, which mainly consist of traditional system implementation teams retooled for LLM work. Even under the same FDE title, the expected depth of implementation varies considerably from company to company.

  • Foreign AI companies (OpenAI, Palantir, etc.) — Japanese-English bilingual required, high compensation, travel and on-site work at clients assumed
  • Domestic AI startups (LayerX, Sakana AI, JDSC, etc.) — implementing their own products is the main battleground; many positions do not require English
  • SIers and consulting firms — a role of layering LLMs onto existing customer bases. Depth of implementation varies greatly by company

Who is a good fit — and who is not

Judging from job posting requirements and the structure of this role, fit or lack thereof is quite clear. First, a good fit is someone who is not uncomfortable with undefined specifications. An FDE's work starts with identifying problems that customers cannot articulate themselves. It is not a good fit for people who want to say "please give me the requirements."

Next, someone who dislikes neither writing code nor talking to people. If you are good at one but find the other painful, you will burn out. In fact, compensation for this role is high precisely because few people in the market possess both skills simultaneously.

Conversely, poor fits are people who want to keep digging deep into a single technology, and people who want to settle in for long-term product development. FDEs change technologies and industries from client to client, and travel frequently. The fact that job postings explicitly state travel ratios of 25-50% or 25-75% shows this is a role that changes your lifestyle. There are many opportunities to test your skills, but it does not suit people seeking a stable environment.

How to find FDE jobs

The tricky part about FDE job postings is that job titles vary from company to company: Forward Deployed Engineer, Forward Deployed Software Engineer, Deployment Strategist, Applied AI Engineer, AI Solutions Engineer, Implementation Engineer. Although the content of each is similar, searching with a single term will miss more than half of them.

On top of that, foreign companies' Japan-focused jobs are posted directly on their own recruitment management systems and often never appear on domestic job boards. Conversely, domestic startups' jobs are on the job boards. You cannot grasp the full picture without looking across both.

Frequently asked questions

  • Is an FDE an engineer or a consultant?
  • An engineer. When defining customer problems, they work in a way close to consultants, but the deliverable is working software, and the role includes writing code yourself and carrying it through to production. Roles that end with design or proposals are not called FDE. Note that Palantir separately has a non-engineer-leaning role called Deployment Strategist on the same team, responsible for understanding the customer's business side.
  • Can you become an FDE with Japanese only?
  • It depends on the company. As far as can be confirmed as of this writing (August 2026), OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting requires fluency in both Japanese and English, explicitly stating that resumes must be in English and interviews conducted in both languages. Palantir's Tokyo posting also lists both English and Japanese as requirements. On the other hand, a considerable number of domestic AI startups' FDE postings do not require English.
  • Can you move to FDE from an SIer or SES?
  • Experience implementing while interacting directly with customers is valued directly in this role. What tends to be lacking is experience implementing with LLMs (prompt design, agent architecture, how to build evaluations) and the working style of deciding things yourself when requirements are undefined. Put the other way, if you fill those two gaps, you already have the foundation. For details, see the article "How to become an FDE."
  • How is an FDE different from contracted development?
  • In contracted development, you take the customer's requirements, build a system for them, deliver it, and you are done. An FDE works for a company that has its own product, and the job is to put that product into customers' operations, with the role extending to feeding the insights gained back into the company's product. Structurally, the difference is that what you build becomes an asset for the next customer.
