What does "FDE-ization" of VCs mean? The substance of support that embeds investors into portfolio companies, and how recipients can evaluate it

Original title: VCのFDE化とは。投資先に入り込む支援の中身と、受ける側の見極め方

TIMEWELL · 2026-10-21 · 7 min read

Summary

TIMEWELL's Hamamoto analyzes Theory Ventures' "Forward Deployment" initiative announced October 16, 2026: through its Intelligence Team's GTMINT and TALINT functions, the firm embeds people in portfolio companies to help build sales and hiring operations. The Ollama example cites one day per week and hiring plus GTM plans completed within 90 days, but these are the firm's own claims, with no disclosed costs or third-party verification, and the approach rests on concentrated investing in few companies. The author lists five points founders should clarify before accepting such support.

Key points

  • Theory Ventures embeds staff in portfolio companies to launch sales and hiring.
  • Ollama case: one day weekly, hiring and GTM plans done within 90 days.
  • Results are self-reported; costs and independent verification are undisclosed.
  • The model depends on concentrated investing, limiting broader replication.
  • Founders should pre-agree scope, confidentiality, decision rights, exit, and fees.

Hello, this is Hamamoto from TIMEWELL Inc.

Recently, I often see discussions about "VCs becoming FDEs." One trigger was that the US venture capital firm Theory Ventures announced an initiative on October 16, 2026, to send people into portfolio companies to help launch sales and hiring efforts together. 1 From a founder's perspective, an investor who not only provides money and advice but also works alongside you is a reassuring presence.

In this article, I separate what was announced from what remains only the firm's own description. On that basis, I organize the meaning of VCs moving from advice to execution, and the points that receiving-side founders should confirm in advance. Since the basis is a single firm's announcement, I will not go so far as to call it "a trend across all VCs." What I cover here is one announced initiative and the perspective for thinking about how it might apply to your own company.

What has been announced

On October 16, 2026, Theory Ventures published an article titled "Forward Deployment." The author is Lauren DeMeuse of the firm. The firm writes that it created an "Intelligence Team" to make its partners' investment judgment methods reproducible as an organization. It has five functions: SIGINT, which builds data systems; HUMINT, which cultivates networks of experts; GTMINT, which accelerates portfolio companies' go-to-market through direct collaboration; MINT, which develops marketing methods; and TALINT, which connects talent and companies. 2

Of these, the ones that embed into portfolio companies are GTMINT and TALINT. The Forward Deployment article portrays the FDE as someone who works side by side at the customer's site, understands the business, and fills in the last portion through customization. It then applies the same idea to VCs: capital and advice take a company most of the way. But the remaining parts—hiring the first salespeople, searching for the first executives, discussions at the board—are done in-house. That is why Theory enters the field, the article explains. 1

As a concrete example, the portfolio company Ollama (a platform for running open-weight models) is cited. The article's description is as follows. 1

  • The engagement began in the summer of 2026 in the form of one day per week
  • The person in charge of sales go-to-market (GTM) participated in dozens of interviews
  • Within 90 days, the hiring and GTM plans were put in place
  • A functional, extensible GTM tech stack was built
  • A customer pipeline was created for the new product, Ollama Enterprise
  • The founding team's sales organization was launched

In a comment from Ollama's CEO, it is stated that the GTM stack will serve Ollama's millions of developers.

What has not been announced

There is also much that this article does not cover.

  • Cost. There is no mention of what the portfolio company bears for the engagement.
  • Team size and standard duration and frequency. The example's "one day per week" and "within 90 days" are specific to Ollama. They are not presented as a standard model.
  • Verification of results. The results are based on the firm's own description and a testimonial from Ollama's CEO. No third-party verification is mentioned.
  • Scope. It is written that the practices currently offered are the two of GTMINT and TALINT. There is no mention of expansion into other areas.

The article also states the premise that makes this engagement possible: because the firm pursues a strategy of concentrated investment in a small number of companies, it can embed into portfolio companies as needed. 1 This presumably means it would be difficult for a VC with many portfolio companies to do the same.

How to read the facts

I read this announcement as follows.

The move from advice to execution overlaps with the spread of FDEs. Sending people into customer sites to fill in the last portion. This model spread across AI implementation work from 2025 to 2026. I organize those numbers in Why did the FDE market expand so rapidly?. Theory's initiative can be read as that idea arriving in the world of investing.

Founders' bottlenecks really do lie in "the first sales" and "the first hires." What money and advice cannot reach is the part that requires actually doing the work. How to choose the first salesperson, what to ask in interviews, how to build a sales playbook. If someone experienced joins even one day a week, I believe the speed of getting things off the ground could change.

However, the magnitude of the effect is still unknown. Since the results are the firm's own description, it is too early to take "up and running in 90 days" as a general expectation. Ollama is a company already used by many developers, so conditions may have been favorable. This point cannot be determined from the article.

Five points receiving-side founders should confirm

From here on, this is not announced fact but my own organization. When a VC offers execution-side support, confirming the following points in advance reduces later discrepancies.

1. What will be done, by whom, and how much.

"Entering the field" means different things to different people. Will they advise on sales, join sales meetings, attend hiring interviews, create materials? Write down in advance the person's name, how many days per week, and for how many months. The "one day per week" and "within 90 days" in Theory's example can serve as reference points for confirmation.

2. How will portfolio company information be handled?

Getting involved in sales operations means touching customer names, deal statuses, and hiring candidate information. Confirm the scope of confidentiality and the line on what information the VC shares with other portfolio companies. This is especially important when the same VC has invested in a competitor in the same market.

3. Who holds decision-making authority?

Final hiring decisions, sales policy, pricing. These are decided by the founder. Agree on the line that the VC's staff are in a supporting role and hold no decision-making authority. Between "advice" and "execution" lies the issue of who leads decision-making.

4. Decide in advance how it will end.

How long should the support continue, and how do you transition to self-sufficiency? Even in the Anthropic and FIS partnership covered in the earlier article on the FDE market, it was explicitly stated that the aim of embedding FDEs is not to have them stay permanently, but to hand over knowledge so the other side can operate on its own. I believe the same thinking applies to VC support. Hand down the sales playbook to people inside the company. Document the hiring procedures and leave them behind. Decide what will remain after the engagement ends.

5. Agreeing on costs and compensation.

The Theory article contains no mention of costs. If your company receives this support, confirm in writing whether this assistance is included as a condition of the investment, whether separate fees apply, and how it affects equity or contracts.

How this differs from placing an FDE in your own company

VC support rests on the relationship whereby the growth of the portfolio company benefits the VC itself. On the other hand, when a operating company places an FDE in-house or uses an external FDE, costs and outcomes are made explicit through a contract. Both share a common point: deciding "what will be done, by whom, and to what extent" and "how it will end."

If you want to adopt the model of FDEs working on the front lines into your own team, see WARP's program.

Summary

Theory Ventures announced a Forward Deployment initiative that sends people into portfolio companies and launches sales and hiring together. In the Ollama case, it says that with one day of involvement per week, hiring and go-to-market plans were put in place within 90 days. However, this is the company's own account; costs and third-party verification are not mentioned. It is also stated that the approach assumes concentrated investment in a small number of companies.

From a CEO's perspective, I believe investors who go beyond money and advice and work alongside you hands-on are valuable. That is precisely why the most important thing is to clarify in advance what will be done, by whom, how much, to what extent, and how it will end.

If you would like to discuss designing involvement in your company or how to receive external support, please reach out via individual consultation. If further updates emerge, I will update this article.
