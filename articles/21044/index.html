OpenAI operates the most systematic FDE model in the generative AI era. The company's FDE division grew rapidly from just 2 members in early 2025, and by May 2026 had developed into a large-scale dedicated organization known as the "OpenAI Deployment Company."

As the second installment of the FDE Case Study series, this article examines representative cases with published performance figures, including Morgan Stanley, John Deere, and Klarna, and analyzes from primary sources what OpenAI's FDEs actually did and why they achieved results. Please also see the first installment on Palantir and the third installment on SaaS startups.

Key Takeaways of This Article

  • OpenAI's FDE team started with 2 members in early 2025 and had expanded to over 10 members across 8 cities on 3 continents, including Tokyo, as of August of the same year.
  • In the Morgan Stanley case, OpenAI's FDE completed the RAG technical pipeline in 6-8 weeks, then spent about 4 months on building trust, resulting in a system used by 98% of wealth advisors.
  • In the John Deere case, accuracy was quantified with a custom evaluation system before scaling, and chemical pesticide use was reportedly reduced by up to 70%.
  • The multi-agent design insights gained from the Klarna project went through the open-source Swarm and became Agents SDK, an official OpenAI product.
  • Common to OpenAI's FDE cases is evaluation-driven development: building evaluation sets (Evals) before full-scale development.

Background: How OpenAI's FDE Organization Was Born and Grew

OpenAI's FDE function took shape starting with Colin Jarvis, who joined in November 2022, the same month ChatGPT was released. The team started with 2 members in early 2025 and had expanded to over 10 members across 8 cities on 3 continents, including Tokyo, as of August of the same year (according to reporting by The Pragmatic Engineer). In a subsequent interview, it was mentioned that the team had grown to 39 members, with plans to reach a scale of 50 by the end of the year. Furthermore, in May 2026, the establishment of the "OpenAI Deployment Company," centered on the FDE model, was reported, marking the entry into a business scaling phase involving the acquisition of over 150 engineers through acquisitions and major external investment.

Notably, the company explains that it deliberately limits FDE deployment to "problems worth tens of millions to billions of dollars." Since FDE is a high-cost model, profitability is achieved through the size of the target problem — the same discipline as Palantir's high-ACV strategy.

Case 1: Morgan Stanley — The "4 Months of Trust-Building" That Achieved a 98% Adoption Rate

An early representative case for OpenAI is the AI research system for the wealth management division of financial giant Morgan Stanley. A system was built that allows interactive search and summarization of internal research reports, and it is reported that ultimately 98% of wealth advisors used it, with research report usage increasing 3-fold.

What is important in this case is how time was used. The RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) technical pipeline itself was completed in 6-8 weeks, but an additional 4 months were then invested in "trust-building" activities such as pilot operation, user feedback, and the development of answer verification tools. In the heavily regulated financial domain, there is a large gap between something working technically and frontline staff trusting and using it, and the second half of the FDE's work was spent bridging this gap.

Case 2: John Deere — Reducing Pesticide Use by Up to 70%

The collaboration with agricultural machinery giant John Deere began with one of the earliest engagements, involving 1-2 FDEs in Europe. It involved automating AI-driven, personalized spraying recommendations for each farm. Under the tight deadline of meeting the planting season, the team reviewed hundreds of real examples with domain experts, built a custom evaluation system to measure accuracy, and then scaled. As a result, chemical pesticide use was reportedly reduced by up to 70%, and customer engagement increased 6-fold.

  • Start with real-example reviews with domain experts (hundreds of real cases manually verified)
  • Quantify accuracy with a custom evaluation system (Evals) before scaling
  • Develop backward from the business deadline of "the next planting season"

Case 3: Klarna and T-Mobile — Agents SDK Was Born from Customer Projects

In the customer service automation project for payments company Klarna (2023), manual prompt operations broke down when dealing with over 400 policies, and the FDE team built a parameterized instruction system with evaluation sets for each intent. The multi-agent design insights gained here were released as the open-source framework "Swarm" and later evolved into Agents SDK, an official OpenAI product. Furthermore, in the T-Mobile project (said to be 10 times more complex than Klarna's), this design was proven to withstand scaling.

This is a clear example of the core value loop of the FDE model: "discoveries made at customer sites by FDEs become the company's own products." Deeply solving one company's problem ultimately produced an SDK for all customers. OpenAI itself summarized: "The biggest mistake is generalizing too early. Deeply solving a specific customer's problem almost always yields generalizable insights."

Case 4: Semiconductor and Manufacturing — Transforming Engineering Work Itself

The collaboration with a European semiconductor company (name undisclosed in public information) is one of the company's largest projects, covering 10 use cases spanning chip design, verification, and performance measurement, achieving 20-30% efficiency gains in the initial division with a 50% target overall. Bug investigation and triage agents built on Codex have evolved step by step, from investigation to automated fixes and pull request creation. Also, in the case of an Asian automaker, an architecture of "combining LLMs with deterministic logic" was adopted, where LLMs supported cross-departmental coordination during supply chain disruptions while business rules such as minimum order quantities and lead times were enforced with deterministic code.

Success Patterns Common to OpenAI's Cases

  • 01 Evaluation-driven development: Build an evaluation set before full-scale development. The principle: "No LLM application is complete without evaluation."
  • 02 Plan trust-building as a project phase: Devote the months after technical completion to pilots, verification tools, and dialogue with field teams (Morgan Stanley's 4 months)
  • 03 Select projects based on the monetary scale of the problem: Focus only on problems worth tens of millions of dollars or more to make the FDE model economically viable
  • 04 Go deep, then generalize: As a byproduct of deeply solving one company's problem, products (Swarm → Agents SDK) and reproducible methods emerge
  • 05 Use LLMs and deterministic systems appropriately: Protect business-critical rules with deterministic code, and use LLMs only where probabilistic reasoning creates value

Amid MIT research reporting that "95% of enterprise AI deployments fail," OpenAI's FDE functions precisely as a methodology for creating the "successful 5%." FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How large is OpenAI's FDE team?

According to public information, it started with 2 members in early 2025, expanded to over 10 members across 8 cities on 3 continents including Tokyo by August of that year, and subsequent interviews mentioned 39 members with plans to reach 50 by year-end. In May 2026, the establishment of the "OpenAI Deployment Company" was reported, expanding into a dedicated organization with over 150 engineers added through acquisitions.

Q: Which OpenAI FDE case achieved the greatest results?

Representative publicly reported figures include Morgan Stanley's 98% wealth advisor adoption rate and 3x research utilization, John Deere's up to 70% reduction in pesticide use and 6x customer engagement, and 20-30% efficiency gains in a European semiconductor company's initial department.

Q: Can Japanese companies also receive OpenAI FDE support?

OpenAI has Tokyo as one of its FDE bases, and Japanese enterprises are part of its target market. However, since the company focuses on "problems worth tens of millions of dollars or more," for many Japanese companies, hiring FDE talent and building in-house is the more realistic option.

Q: What is the most important takeaway from OpenAI's cases for a company's own AI adoption?

"Build an evaluation set (Evals) before full-scale development." Review real examples with domain experts and create a state where accuracy can be measured quantitatively before scaling—this sequence was common to both Morgan Stanley and John Deere, and is considered the dividing line between the failing 95% and the successful 5%.
