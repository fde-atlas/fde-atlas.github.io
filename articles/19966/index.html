Why Do You Want to Work at Palantir? Sample Answer

Design Gurus · 2024-11-30 · 4 min read

Summary

This article explains how to answer the "why Palantir" interview question. It argues interviewers want one concrete reason: name a specific product (Gotham, Foundry, Apollo, or AIP), describe what it does, and connect it to work you have already done, plus a first-year goal. It distinguishes software engineer, forward deployed engineer, and deployment strategist tracks, warning that motivation must match the role applied for. A roughly 160-word sample answer is provided, along with weak generic answers to avoid and preparation steps.

Key points

  • Name a specific Palantir product and its function.
  • Link the product to one project you built.
  • Match your motivation to the role track applied for.
  • Keep the answer around 160 words, under 90 seconds.
  • Generic praise answers fail; specificity is the test.

Palantir wants one concrete reason, not praise for the company. The answer that works names a Palantir product, says what that product does, and links it to work you have already done.

A strong answer has three parts. You say which part of Palantir's work you care about. You point to something you built that is close to it. You say what you want to do there in your first year.

Palantir builds data platforms. Its customers are governments, defense agencies, hospitals, banks and factories. Most engineers there work on a named customer problem, not on an internal tool.

That last fact is what your answer should turn on.

What Palantir Builds

Pick one product and mention it by name. Interviewers notice when you cannot.

Product What it does Who uses it
Gotham Links scattered records into one view for investigations Defense and government teams
Foundry Joins data across a whole company and runs apps on top Energy, health, manufacturing, banking
Apollo Ships and updates software into locked-down networks Palantir and its customers
AIP Puts AI models on top of Foundry and Gotham data Government and industry

The Role Changes the Answer

Palantir hires on a few clear tracks. Your reason should match the one you applied to.

Software engineers build the platform itself. A forward deployed engineer, meaning an engineer who works on site with the customer, writes code inside the customer's data. A deployment strategist solves the customer's business problem and is not a coding role.

If you say you want deep platform work in a forward deployed interview, the answer lands wrong.

A Sample Answer You Can Adapt

"I spent the last two years building data pipelines for a hospital network. The modeling was never the hard part. The hard part was that the patient records, the billing records and the staffing records never agreed with each other.

I read how Foundry handles this with an ontology, meaning one agreed set of names for people, places and things across every source. That is the exact problem I kept hitting, and I would rather work on it as a product than rebuild it for one hospital.

I also want to sit closer to the people who use what I build. Today I hand work to a product team and hear nothing for a quarter. The forward deployed model is the opposite. I want to be in the room, see the real data, and ship something they use that week.

In my first year I want to work on ingestion, where my pipeline experience is worth most."

That answer is about 160 words. Spoken, it runs under a minute and a half. That is the right length.

What Makes an Answer Specific

  • A named product. Foundry, Gotham, Apollo or AIP, with one line on what it does.
  • A named problem you have had. Messy joins, slow access reviews, data that cannot leave a network.
  • Your own work as the link. One project, one sentence, no resume recital.
  • A view on the model. Say why deployment near the customer suits you, or why it does not and you want platform work instead.
  • One honest limit. Palantir works with defense and intelligence customers. If you have thought about that and are comfortable, say so plainly. Interviewers ask.

Answers That Do Not Work

Generic answers repeat the careers page back. Every one of these is weak on its own:

  • "Palantir solves hard problems." So does every employer you could name.
  • "I want to work with talented people." This says nothing about Palantir.
  • "I love the mission." Name the mission and one customer type, or leave it out.
  • "The technology is cutting edge." Which technology, and compared to what?

The test is simple. If you can swap in another company name and the sentence still reads fine, cut it.

How to Prepare

  • Read one product page and one customer story. Thirty minutes is enough to name a product and a use case correctly.
  • Write the answer out, then cut it in half. Keep the project, the product and the first-year goal.
  • Check the neighbouring questions. The same reasoning is needed for why do you want to work at Palantir and why do you want to join Palantir.
  • Know the rest of the loop. Read what the Palantir interview process looks like round by round so your motivation answer fits the stage you are in.
  • Prepare the stories behind it. Grokking the Behavioral Interview covers the structure interviewers expect for follow-up questions.
  • Do not skip the technical rounds. Motivation gets you past the recruiter. Grokking the Coding Interview and what to expect in the Palantir system design interview cover the rest.
  • Ask a question back. A short question about the team's customer shows the interest is real.
