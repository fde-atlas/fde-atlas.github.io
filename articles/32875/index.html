Solutions Engineer to Forward Deployed Engineer: The Coding Gap

FDE India · 2026-11-17 · 8 min read

Summary

Comparing seven Indian job listings, this guide argues the main gap between solutions engineers and FDEs is ownership of production code, not experience or pre-sales skills. DevRev's FDE roles are 70–80% hands-on implementation, while Scaler estimates solutions engineers code only about 20% of the week. Experience bars favor solutions roles (Freshworks asks 8+ years). The line is blurring: Prodapt requires a year of AI presales for an FDE title, while Resilinc excludes general pre-sales. The author recommends proving the gap is closed by taking a proof of concept to production with tests, monitoring, an eval set and an incident write-up, plus a 10-week plan.

A solutions or sales engineer can move into a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role in India, and already has half the job: discovery, demos and handling customer stakeholders. What’s missing is almost always the same thing: owning code that runs in production after the deal closes. This guide reads seven current India listings side by side, names the exact gap, and shows how to prove you have closed it.

If your question is whether the move pays, we covered that in solutions engineer vs FDE salary in India. This page is about the skills and the pitch.

Where pre-sales ends and FDE work begins

In a typical pre-sales role, the solutions engineer runs discovery, demos and proofs of concept that show the product fits, then hands over once the deal is signed. An FDE picks up from there and builds what the customer actually uses. DevRev’s India FDE listings describe that job as designing, developing and launching AI agents, integrations and automations for customers.

DevRev’s Mumbai FDE listing, mirrored on jobfound.org on 1 November 2026, spells out the split: 80% of the role is customer-facing, travel can reach 80%, and coding and integration take up “70-80% hands-on technical implementation”. It asks for 5+ years, TypeScript/JavaScript and Python, and posts pay of ₹28–36 LPA.

Recruiting marketplace Paraform puts the same idea in one line: “The SE sells the vision. The FDE builds it.” Its April 2026 comparison says the solutions engineer is judged on revenue influenced, the FDE on what ships. Scaler frames it as ownership: the FDE is measured on go-live and system health after launch, not the technical win.

DevRev’s figure is from its own listing (Nov 2026). Scaler’s 70% and 20% are its estimates, with no source given (Aug 2026).

Three things stand out. First, experience is not what separates the roles: Freshworks wants 8+ years for a lead solutions role, more than any FDE listing above. Second, the line is blurring. Neuron7 calls its role a solutions engineer but makes it accountable for customer outcomes through to a live deployment, which is FDE work under another name. Third, Prodapt, an IT services firm, has made pre-sales a requirement for an FDE title: it says delivery-only AI experience does not count.

The coding gap, skill by skill

Pre-sales work builds real skills. The question is what the FDE listings add on top.

Proving production coding when your title says “solutions”

Hiring managers read a solutions engineer’s CV with one doubt: have you ever owned something after the demo? Answer it with evidence, not adjectives.

  • A proof of concept that went live. If one of your POCs became a production integration, say who ran it, how many users or requests it handled, and what you changed after launch.
  • Merged code in a real repository. Perspective AI, which sells customer research tools, claims in its 2026 practitioner survey that 71% of FDEs ship merged pull requests into customer repositories every month, against under 5% of traditional SEs. It is a vendor survey, but the signal it points to is right: pull requests beat slide decks.
  • An incident you handled. One paragraph on a failure in a running system, how you found it and what you fixed is worth more than three certifications.
  • An eval set. For AI roles, a small labelled test set and the accuracy numbers before and after your changes shows the habit Freshworks and Prodapt ask for.

Two routes in: internal move or new employer

Moving inside your company is often the shortest path, because you already know the product and its customers. If your employer has a delivery, professional services or FDE team, ask to shadow the delivery team on one account you sold, and offer to build the first integration yourself.

Moving to a new employer, pick roles that value your background. Prodapt’s presales FDE role treats a year of AI presales as a must-have. Post-sale roles like Resilinc’s say plainly they don’t own general pre-sales demonstrations, so for those, lead with delivery and keep the sales story short. Check our FDE interview process guide for how the rounds differ.

A 10-week plan to close the coding gap

  • Weeks 1–2: pick one past POC and rebuild it properly. Same use case, but as a Python service with FastAPI, typed models, tests and structured logging. Put it in a public repository.
  • Weeks 3–4: add a real integration. Connect it to a second system through its API and a webhook, with retries, authentication and failure handling. This mirrors the integration work in DevRev’s and Resilinc’s listings.
  • Weeks 5–6: ship it. Containerise with Docker, add a CI pipeline that runs your tests, deploy it on a cloud free tier, and add basic monitoring and alerts.
  • Week 7: add the AI layer with evals. Add a retrieval or agent step, build a 30–50 case test set, and record accuracy, latency and cost before and after one improvement.
  • Week 8: break it on purpose. Simulate a failed upstream API or bad data, debug it from logs alone, and write a short incident note.
  • Week 9: practise the coding round. Timed problems in Python on data handling and APIs, as Scaler’s plan suggests, plus one integration-design mock.
  • Week 10: reposition and apply. Rewrite your CV around what went live, then apply to roles that value pre-sales, such as presales FDE roles at services firms, or move internally.

Frequently asked questions

Can a solutions engineer become a forward deployed engineer in India?

Yes, and it is one of the more natural moves, because discovery, demos and stakeholder handling are half the FDE job. The gap is production engineering: India FDE listings from DevRev, Resilinc and Prodapt ask for strong Python, integration work and owning deployments, which most pre-sales roles don't require.

How much coding does an FDE do compared with a solutions engineer?

DevRev's India FDE listings put hands-on coding and integration at 70–80% of the role. Scaler estimates FDEs spend about 70% of the week coding against about 20% for solutions engineers, though it gives no source for that split.

What should a solutions engineer learn first to move into an FDE role?

Production Python first: an API service with tests, error handling and logging, deployed with Docker and CI. Then integrations through APIs and webhooks, and then LLM agent work, which India listings from Neuron7, Freshworks and Prodapt all name.

Is pre-sales experience useful for FDE jobs in India?

Increasingly. Prodapt's FDE – Data & AI Presales role requires at least a year of dedicated AI presales, But post-sale FDE roles such as Resilinc's want proof you can run what you build.
