Many companies have already tested generative AI. They ran proofs of concept, built internal chatbots, and gave demos to management. But few have managed to move these initiatives into daily operations, integrate them into their own systems, and obtain reliable data and measurable returns.

This gap between pilot development and production has gained a new name in the tech market: the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) operates precisely in this space. Companies like Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic use this model to bring their AI products into large organizations. In this article, we explain what these professionals do, why they play a central role in AI projects, and how your company can adopt this working model without building a team from scratch.

What is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

A Forward Deployed Engineer is a software engineer who works directly with customers, understands the problem, builds the solution, integrates it with existing systems, and puts everything into production. The term comes from military vocabulary: forward deployed refers to personnel stationed on the front lines.

In practice, the FDE combines skills that are usually spread across different areas:

  • Software engineering: writing code, creating APIs, integrations, and tests.
  • Solutions engineering: understanding the customer's architecture and choosing the right technologies.
  • Consulting: mapping business processes, constraints, and objectives.
  • Product: identifying recurring patterns across customers and turning them into standard features.
  • Customer success: the work is only done when the customer uses the solution and sees results.

What makes the FDE unique is accountability for outcomes. Instead of delivering reports or polished presentations, the FDE delivers an application that actually works.

Where does the FDE model come from?

This role became well known at Palantir, a company that serves government agencies, the military, and large enterprises with solutions for highly specific problems. Data is scattered, every organization has its own processes, and security requirements are strict. Simply delivering software and a user manual is not enough.

The response was to send engineers to work where the problem happens. They observe operations, talk to users, analyze data, and build the solution together with the customer.

With the arrival of language models, model capability came back with force. OpenAI and Anthropic both created dedicated teams for this work, because they realized something simple: offering powerful models via API is only the beginning. The real challenge is connecting the model to the company's own data, systems, internal rules, and security requirements.

Why do so many AI projects stall at the pilot stage?

The typical customer need rarely arrives as a technical specification. It usually sounds like this: "We need to cut the time to analyze these documents from three days to a few hours." From that sentence, someone has to figure out which documents are involved, where the data lives, which regulatory rules apply, which model to use, how to measure response quality, and how to deploy everything to production safely.

If that person doesn't exist, the project usually stalls at one of these points:

  • The wrong problem is solved. The area asks for a chatbot, but the real problem is that no one can find the right information. Or it asks for an autonomous agent to handle a process that still lacks clear rules.
  • The proof of concept can't talk to the systems. It works with a test spreadsheet, but it doesn't connect to the ERP, CRM, or data lake.
  • There is no governance. Each area creates its own automations with no data usage policy or access control. This is what we call... AI without governance.
  • No one measures the return. Without clear metrics defined before the project starts, it's impossible to prove to the CFO that the investment was worth it.

The FDE exists precisely to overcome these four obstacles. It asks the right questions, separates real needs from premature solutions, and builds, integrates, and monitors usage.

What does the FDE's day-to-day work look like?

The routine varies by customer, but a week might include meetings with executives, data architecture analysis, Python APIs, React interfaces, cloud infrastructure provisioning, RAG pipeline testing, and troubleshooting AI agents.

There is also a less visible but very important task: deciding what should be customized and what should be standardized. Four questions guide this decision:

  • Which features need to be developed specifically for this customer?
  • What should go onto the main platform, for everyone to use?
  • Which problems can be solved with configuration alone?
  • What would create technical debt that can never be repaid?

This judgment is as important as programming skill. It prevents the company from accumulating dozens of isolated solutions that no one can maintain.

How to adopt the FDE model without building a team from scratch.

For most Brazilian companies, the challenge is that this talent is scarce and expensive. Finding someone who masters software engineering, cloud, data, generative AI, and communicates well with management takes months. Maintaining a team made up entirely of this profile costs even more.

To solve this, Flexa Cloud created the Hyperautomation and Governed AI Center of Excellence (CoE). In practice, it operates as a forward deployed service model: a team of AWS specialists works as an extension of your company, dives deep into the problems and is accountable for the final result, all under annual contracts with predictable costs.

The Center of Excellence follows a six-step methodology, the Flexa Pipeline, which organizes all AI and automation requests into a single queue:

  • Intake: capturing pain points and ideas from all areas.
  • Triage: initial technical and strategic screening.
  • Analysis: feasibility study and solution design.
  • Prioritization: ranking by expected benefit and technical effort.
  • Execution: agile development and native deployment on AWS.
  • ROI measurement: validating actual impact using the Business Value Realization (BVR) methodology.

Beyond the engineering team, the Center of Excellence includes governance and AI usage policies for the whole organization, centralized demand management, model fine-tuning for specific cases, and training workshops for internal teams. Does your company need the forward deployed engineering model?

If you identify with at least two of the situations below, this model is worth considering:

  • You have already run AI proofs of concept, but nothing has gone to production yet.
  • Different departments are creating automation systems on their own, without coordination from the IT department.
  • The backlog of automation requests keeps growing.
  • The board is asking what the ROI on AI is, but no one can give a concrete number.
  • Your internal team knows the business, but lacks experience building AI architecture on AWS.

Frequently asked questions about Forward Deployed Engineers

What does a Forward Deployed Engineer do?

He works closely with the customer to understand business problems, builds solutions, integrates them with existing systems, and puts them into production, taking responsibility for the final result.

What is the difference between an FDE and a consultant?

A consultant typically provides diagnoses and recommendations. An FDE writes code, integrates systems, and only considers the work done once the solution is in use and delivering impact.

Why is the FDE model so important in AI projects?

Because an AI model alone does not solve problems. Its value only emerges when it is combined with the company's data, systems, rules, and security requirements, which is exactly what an FDE does.

How can you adopt the forward deployed engineering model without building an internal team?
