Corporate Generative AI Training (Customized to Job Roles) | Pricing, Implementation Examples, and Subsidies

Original title: 法人向け生成AI研修（職務に合わせたカスタム型）｜料金・実施例・助成金

株式会社Aiworker · 2025-11-30 · 17 min read

Summary

This is a service page from Japanese company AIworker promoting custom generative AI training for corporations. Training uses trainees' actual work as exercises rather than generic examples, covering tools like ChatGPT, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and Gemini. It offers four formats: a 12-hour role-specific custom course (400,000 yen per trainee), group sessions (400,000–500,000 yen per session), and executive workshops (300,000–600,000 yen). Case studies include an imported-car dealer whose 18 executives rated a 3-day workshop 9.3/10, and a payment company where 11 non-IT departments built working AI outputs. The page details Japan's human resource development subsidy: after an August 2026 revision, generic prompt training and executives no longer qualify, role-linked training does, and the program runs until March 31, 2027.

Key points

  • Training exercises are drawn from each department's real work, identified through pre-training interviews.
  • Four formats offered: 12-hour role-specific course, two group sessions, and executive workshops.
  • Executive workshop at a car dealer scored 9.3/10 satisfaction; 11 non-IT departments completed working AI projects.
  • Japan's reskilling subsidy can apply, but executives and generic prompt training were excluded in the August 2026 revision.
  • The subsidy course is a temporary measure valid until March 31, 2027, with a plan notification due 1–6 months before training starts.

Inquiries from those considering training

What's happening on the ground

From those considering generative AI training, we often receive inquiries like the following. In every case, people are stuck not with how to use the tools, but with not yet deciding where in their own work to use them.

The tools were distributed, but only some employees are using them

Even when accounts are distributed company-wide, the gap between those who use them daily and those who don't keeps widening.

Even after training, people don't know how to apply it to their own work

When learning with generic examples, people get stuck when trying to translate it to their own tasks.

Executives and frontline staff aren't aligned on what AI should achieve

Even when directives are issued, understanding of what is allowed and what is not varies from person to person.

We planned to use a government subsidy, but after the revision we don't know if we still qualify

The August 2026 revision excluded training not tied to job duties from eligibility.

Training ends and nothing remains six months later

Unless the situations where the training will be applied and the responsible people are decided afterward, usage won't spread within the company.

To change how employees work, you need to choose materials from participants' actual duties and decide in advance where the training will be applied.

Even before the target departments and headcount are decided, we'll spend 30 minutes thinking through the training design together.

Types of work covered in training

Most internal work falls into one of the following five categories. In the exercises, participants' actual work is sorted into these five categories and used as material.

1. Creating documents

Work where the previous document is copied and only the contents are rewritten

  • Reports, meeting minutes, proposals
  • Drafts of internal approval requests

2. Answering questions

Work where questions pile up with specific people

  • Checking internal rules and past cases
  • Routine inquiries to accounting or general affairs

3. Reading large volumes of material

Work where reading alone takes hours and omissions are a concern

  • Extracting key points from contracts and specifications
  • Trend analysis of survey results

4. Automating manual tasks

Work where the same operations are repeated by hand daily or monthly

  • Transferring data from PDF to Excel
  • Regular aggregations and reports

5. Finding errors

Work where manual checking still produces oversights

  • Cross-checking purchase orders against quotes
  • Missing entries in applications and contracts

Supported tools ChatGPT / Microsoft 365 Copilot / Copilot Studio / Gemini & NotebookLM / Claude

Materials and tools are decided after learning about participants' actual work.

Structure of the role-based training

The 12-hour role-based training is structured, for example, as follows. The exercises in sessions 2 through 4 are tailored to the participating department's work.

The standard format is one session per week for 5 weeks, held either in person or online. For training with more than 30 participants, we narrow the material down to a single topic, with everyone performing the same operations as the instructor and completing the work on their own machines.

Training case studies

These are cases where we provided training and subsequent support for building usage practices. Company names are withheld; industry and size are shown. Figures are only those verified through surveys or performance records; unmeasured items are noted as such.

Building an AI promotion organization through a 3-day program for management

ChallengeAlthough management had issued the directive "reform operations with AI," understanding of what could and could not be done was not aligned among the leadership.
What we didWe structured the 3-day workshop in the order of fundamentals, articulating each department's challenges, and implementation via homework plus roadmap creation. On day 3, the president presented an AI he had built himself. The workshop was conducted in both English and Japanese.
ResultsIn the post-training survey (14 respondents), overall satisfaction was 9.3, instructor clarity 9.6, and "would recommend to other employees" 9.5 (all out of 10). The training led to the creation of a four-tier AI promotion organization, with members already appointed.

Non-IT department employees building their own working systems

ChallengeThe participants were non-IT department employees who had only learned the basics in internal study sessions. Most had never used business automation tools.
What we didWe ran two rounds of coaching: 1 hour per week for 6 sessions. Our staff did not build anything; the employees built everything themselves. The first session was devoted to inventorying work, separating AI-handled steps from human-judgment steps upfront. We also designed a separate intermediate training (120 minutes) for 120 participants.
ResultsAcross 11 departments in total, every department we worked with completed a working deliverable within the period. One of these was a Copilot Studio agent that answers inquiries.

Department members building their own AI and teaching each other

ChallengeTime was consumed by writing sales emails, pre-call research, data entry into the sales management system, and training new hires, and teaching methods varied from person to person.
What we didWe handled the company-wide launch of AI adoption and support for how to use and build tools. Each department member built one AI for one task, and the builder taught its use in study sessions.
ResultsThe 7 participants reduced their work by 282 hours per month combined, and all 7 use it daily (February to July 2025).

Creating text for the explanation of important matters from management agreements

ChallengeThe task of finding usage restriction clauses in management agreements and transcribing them into the explanation of important matters document was concentrated among a few staff with real estate knowledge.
What we didWe used this task as the material for training held once a week for 5 sessions. We built a workflow where entering the management agreement PDF produces text that can be pasted directly into the relevant fields of the explanation of important matters document.
StatusEven after the training ended, the staff continue to use AI in their daily work. Time saved has not been measured.

In addition, in the training for executives and division heads at a food manufacturer, we heard that tailoring the content to the company aligned executives' understanding of AI goals. At an investment fund management company, after creating usage rules and adoption coaching, 4 out of 5 respondents use it almost daily. Other cases can be viewed in the case study list.

We will propose a training format and participant structure based on cases similar to your company.

Handling of subsidies

Job-specific custom training can be structured to qualify for the Human Resources Development Subsidy (Business Expansion Reskilling Support Course). Eligibility and subsidy amounts are determined by the Labor Bureau, and applications are handled in cooperation with a certified social insurance labor consultant.

Generic training is not covered

With the August 2026 revision, generic prompt training not tied to job duties was removed from eligibility.

Design training names and content tied to job duties

Executives are not covered

Eligible participants are those covered by employment insurance. Sessions taken by executives are not counted in the subsidy calculation.

Workshops for management are not eligible for subsidies

Deadline for the training plan notification

The plan notification must be submitted to the Labor Bureau between 6 months and 1 month before the training start date.

Build the schedule backward from the start date

Until March 31, 2027

This course is a measure available until March 31, 2027; its treatment in subsequent fiscal years has not been decided.

We recommend deciding the start timing early

Detailed conditions are summarized in the documentation for HR and human resource development departments and the training design example for sales departments.

Our strengths

We handle everything from organizing management issues to development, on-site support, and employee development under one company. As a result, tasks identified during training can be carried directly into the next initiative.

Curricula built after hearing about your operations

Rather than teaching fixed materials in sequence, we determine units based on participants' job duties

Before the training, we ask about the target department's operations and time-consuming tasks. Exercise materials are chosen from among them, so participants can apply what they learn to their own work the very next day.

The same sequence from management to the front line

We handle management workshops, manager-level inventory sessions, and job-specific training for the front line within a single company

If initiatives are rolled out to the front line without leadership sharing the same picture, each department proceeds differently and efforts tend to stall midway. We connect everything, from management policy to front-line usage, under a consistent approach.

Connecting post-training support and development

Tasks identified during training can be realized through ongoing support, in-house FDE development, and AI agent development

We organize and separate tasks that can be handled within the training from those that should be built as dedicated systems. If needed, we hand them over to ongoing generative AI support or AI agent development.

What remains after the training

The goal of the training is a state where usage continues in-house even six months later. To that end, we leave your company with the following three things.

Usage examples for each operation

  • Prompts created during the training
  • GPTs, Gems, and Copilot agents formatted for sharing within departments

Internal usage rules and promotion leads

  • Rules on what information may and may not be entered into AI
  • Employees who can handle questions in each department

A list of next operations to tackle and how to measure impact

  • A list of candidates raised during the training, ranked by expected impact
  • How to measure the number of users and time saved
  • The division between tasks handled by training and tasks handed to development
