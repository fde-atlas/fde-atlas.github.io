Forward Deployed Engineer News: 12 Stories From 2026, Newest First, and What Each Means for a Buyer

Hayat Amin · Operator · 2026-10-06 · 13 min read

Summary

Hayat Amin, a former technology CFO, reviews 12 forward deployed engineer stories from May to October 2026 from a buyer's perspective. Anthropic committed $100 million to train 10,000 engineers, Gartner predicts 70 percent of enterprises will abandon vendor-built agentic AI by 2028, and OpenAI, AWS, Microsoft and Accenture have all built dedicated units. The author argues FDE work has become an industry priced per use case per quarter, is mostly locked to one model or cloud, and that companies of 20 to 500 people are poorly served. He advises buyers to demand exit plans and internal owners before signing.

Key points

  • Gartner forecasts 70 percent of enterprises will abandon vendor FDE-built agentic AI by 2028.
  • Anthropic's Claude Frontier Academy will train 10,000 engineers for $100 million by end of 2027.
  • Of 231 open FDE titles counted, Palantir and Databricks hold 78 percent.
  • Microsoft is spending $2.5 billion with 6,000 engineers; AWS put $1 billion behind its FDE unit.
  • Nearly every programme ties to one model or cloud, leaving mid-sized companies underserved.

The biggest forward deployed engineer news of 2026 is that the job turned into an industry. Anthropic is spending $100 million to train 10,000 of them, Gartner says 70 percent of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor FDEs by 2028, and OpenAI, AWS, Microsoft and Accenture have each built a unit around them.

I'm Hayat Amin. I spent twenty years as a technology chief financial officer, sold three companies in that seat, and now do forward deployed work inside other people's companies myself. So I read this news as the person who gets the invoice. Below are 12 stories from May to this morning, newest first, each with what it means if you run a company and who should ignore it.

How I picked and ordered the 12

Google already shows my pages for forward deployed engineer news, at a best position of 2, and between 20 August and 5 October 2026 nobody clicked. The pages it showed were about other questions. This one answers the search.

Every story is ordered by date, newest first. I read each one on the company's own release where the page would load: Anthropic, Accenture, Devoteam and Ode. Gartner's and OpenAI's pages refused my reader, so for those two I used the trade reports that quote them and I say so in the item. Every figure below is from those pages or from my own count of the job feeds this morning. Where a number is my arithmetic, I say that too.

I left out a few things I couldn't read at source. Salesforce's 1,000 AI deployment specialists, announced in March, only reached me second hand in Channel Dive's AWS piece, so it isn't an item. I also left out third-party job censuses and counted the feeds myself.

The 12 stories, newest first

1. 6 October 2026: the live job feeds this morning

I read six employers' own job feeds on Greenhouse, Ashby and Lever this morning and counted every title with forward deployed in it. Palantir had 81 of 318 live postings. Databricks had 99 of 887. OpenAI had 28 of 823, up from 21 when I counted on 20 September. Scale AI had 15 of 188, Anthropic 6 of 644 and Datadog 2 of 440.

That's 231 titles, and Palantir and Databricks hold 180 of them, which is 78 percent by my division. Anthropic is the odd one. It has 6 open roles and a plan to train 10,000 people, which tells you it means to grow the job inside its partners and customers rather than on its own payroll.

Useful if you want to know who is hiring and where the experience sits. Wrong if you read it as the size of the profession, because these are open titles, not people in seats. Gartner puts the people at about 2,000, in story 4 below.

2. 5 October 2026: AWS publishes two FDE credentials

AWS now offers an FDE validated certification and an FDE advanced credential, with three free training pathways on Skill Builder called ground, orchestrate and prove. One pathway gets you the validated badge and all three get you the advanced one, Channel Dive reported on 5 October.

The line that matters to a buyer is the delivery menu. AWS says customers can choose a first-party AWS FDE, an AWS-led engagement with partner specialists, or a partner-led FDE. You can now ask a partner for the credential before you sign. A badge earned from three online pathways isn't delivery, though, so ask for the last system they put live as well.

3. 2 October 2026: Anthropic will spend $100 million training 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers

Anthropic launched Claude Frontier Academy with a $100 million commitment and a target of 10,000 engineers by the end of 2027. Note the F. In Anthropic's version FDE stands for Frontier Deployed Engineer. The first cohorts come from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk, and run in San Francisco, New York and London.

The format is borrowed from medicine. A multi-day in-person programme ends in a graded practical, a pass earns the Claude Resident Engineer badge, then a 12-week residency leading a real Claude project at the engineer's own employer, then a second assessment for the Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer badge. The first of those is expected in early 2027. Entry is by nomination through an Anthropic account team.

Good news if you're an enterprise with an account team and a strong engineer to nominate. If you run a company of 200 people you won't be sending anyone, and you'll meet these engineers on a consultancy's invoice. 5 of the 8 named first-cohort employers are consulting firms.

4. 29 September 2026: Gartner says 70 percent of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor FDEs by 2028

This is the one I'd read twice. Gartner predicts that by 2028, 70 percent of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor forward deployed engineering, trapped by cost and unable to keep developing it themselves. Its press page blocked my reader, so I took the detail from Channel Dive's report and CIO Dive. Mukul Saha, a Gartner senior director analyst, said many providers now use forward deployed as a label for implementation, professional services, solution engineering or AI consulting, some thoughtfully, others because it sounds more strategic.

CIO Dive gives Gartner's fee estimate as $200,000 to $400,000 a quarter per use case, before platform and integration costs. Four quarters of the low end is $800,000 a year for one use case, which is my multiplication. Saha also told Channel Dive there are about 2,000 active FDEs and demand is probably four times that.

As a CFO, the cost isn't what worries me. The failure Gartner names is that nobody inside the client can keep the system going once the vendor leaves. Ask for the exit plan and the named internal owner before you sign. Don't read this as FDEs don't work, though. Saha's own words were that the right FDE model brings good outcomes.

5. 28 September 2026: Devoteam certifies 20 engineers through Google Cloud's FDE programme

Devoteam announced that 20 of its engineers finished Google Cloud's Forward Deployed Engineer enablement programme, four days at its Madrid office from 1 to 4 September. To get in, each needed two Gemini Enterprise certifications, one Google Cloud Professional certification and a live Gemini Enterprise customer project. Devoteam wants 200 Gemini Enterprise specialists within 12 months, on top of the 650 or so Google Cloud specialists it already has.

This shows you what an FDE badge means at a systems integrator. It's four days on one vendor's agent stack, for engineers already billing on that vendor. Useful if you're a European buyer on Gemini Enterprise. Wrong if you aren't on Gemini, and it isn't a US story.

6. 8 September 2026: Accenture and Google Cloud plan a 1,000-person FDE workforce

Accenture and Google Cloud launched the Accenture Gemini Enterprise Business Group from New York and Sunnyvale, California, with a plan for a 1,000-person FDE workforce built on Accenture's nearly 50,000 Google Cloud-skilled people. The named proof is YouTube, where a Gemini Enterprise agent during NFL Sunday Ticket surge demand lifted customer sentiment 11 percent and cut average handle time 37 percent.

1,000 out of 50,000 is 2 percent, by my division. That's a specialist bench, not a change in how Accenture staffs. Good for a Fortune 500 company already on Google. Wrong for a mid-sized company, which won't be the first call for a team of that size.

7. 31 July 2026: DXC has 86 Claude-certified FDEs against a promise of tens of thousands

In June DXC Technology said it would work with Anthropic to train tens of thousands of Claude-certified forward deployed engineers. On its earnings call, chief executive Raul Fernandez said 86 were certified, and its Claude-based platform, DXC Oasis, was running in 57 customer environments. DXC modelled close to zero revenue from the Anthropic work for the year.

I don't read 86 as a failure. It took a month, and it's honest. I read it as the size of the gap between an announcement and a bench. Whoever you buy from, ask how many certified engineers are free this quarter, not how many are planned.

8. 15 July 2026: Ode with Anthropic launches for mid-sized companies

Anthropic, Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman launched Ode with Anthropic under its own name. It's built on Fractional AI, the applied AI services firm bought in May 2026, and run by Fractional AI's co-founders, Chris Taylor as chief executive and Eddie Siegel as chief technology officer. Goldman Sachs, General Atlantic, Leonard Green & Partners, Apollo Global Management, GIC and Sequoia Capital are in the consortium. TechCrunch reported 100 engineers at launch. PYMNTS, citing Bloomberg, reported that the deal ended Fractional AI's 11-month partnership with OpenAI.

Garvan Doyle, Anthropic's Head of Forward Deployed Engineering, Americas, said mid-size companies need partners with real implementation depth. Of the firms on this list, Ode is the one aimed at the middle market. Wrong for you if you want a partner that stays neutral between models, because Ode is built on Claude.

9. 2 July 2026: Microsoft puts $2.5 billion and 6,000 engineers into the Microsoft Frontier Company

Microsoft is spending $2.5 billion on a new operating business, the Microsoft Frontier Company, to put 6,000 engineers inside customer operations, CIO Dive reported. Land O'Lakes and Unilever were named as customers, and Microsoft said it has FDE partnerships with Accenture, Capgemini, EY, KPMG and PwC. In the same piece Alex Coqueiro, a Gartner senior director analyst, said 85 percent of tech providers will have FDE programmes as a core AI delivery model by the end of this year.

6,000 is three times Gartner's estimate of the whole active FDE workforce, my comparison of two published numbers. Good for a Microsoft shop with a large account. If you're on a different stack, it isn't for you.

10. 30 June 2026: AWS puts $1 billion behind its own FDE organisation

AWS stood up a forward deployed engineering organisation backed by $1 billion, as a separate business unit inside AWS rather than a standalone company, said Francessca Vasquez, its vice president of frontier AI engineering and services. The work runs in pods of five to six engineers on 45-day sprints, scoped around a business outcome instead of billable hours, and the stated aim is that customers can run the systems themselves afterwards.

Of the big announcements, this is the one with the clearest shape for a buyer: a pod size, a sprint length and a handover. Wrong for you if your data and systems don't live on AWS.

11. 13 May 2026: Google Cloud opens 59 FDE roles

Google Cloud chief executive Thomas Kurian asked for builders on LinkedIn, and Channel Dive counted 59 distinct roles in the US and abroad, including London, Paris and Hong Kong. A dozen applied AI forward deployed engineering roles in the New York and Atlanta areas paid a base of $127,000 to $183,000. Google also pointed to a $750 million commitment to its partners.

One line in that piece has stayed with me. Peter Bryant of Omdia said FDEs are going to be on site for customers at most a month. If that's true, the partner who takes over after month one matters more than the engineer who starts. Wrong for you if you were hoping a vendor engineer would stay.

12. 11 May 2026: OpenAI launches the OpenAI Deployment Company and buys Tomoro

OpenAI launched the OpenAI Deployment Company with more than $4 billion of initial investment, majority owned and controlled by OpenAI, and agreed to buy Tomoro, which brings about 150 forward deployed engineers and deployment specialists who have worked for Tesco, Virgin Atlantic and Supercell. TPG led, with Advent, Bain Capital and Brookfield as co-lead founding partners, alongside Goldman Sachs, SoftBank Corp. and Warburg Pincus, and Bain & Company, Capgemini and McKinsey & Company on the consulting side. 19 firms in all. OpenAI's own page blocked my reader, so these figures are from AI and Data Insider's report of the announcement.

OpenAI says a typical engagement starts with a diagnostic of where AI can create the most value. That's the one thing every serious firm on this list agrees on. Good for an enterprise that wants OpenAI's engineers close to the model. Wrong for you if you want to stay free to switch models.

What the 12 add up to if you run a company

The money is priced per use case per quarter, so a vendor FDE is a running cost, not a project fee. The engineers sit at the largest firms: Palantir and Databricks alone have 180 of the 231 open titles I counted, and 5 of the 8 employers in Anthropic's first cohorts are consultancies. And every programme here except DXC's is tied to one model or one cloud, whether that's OpenAI, Claude, AWS, Azure or Gemini.

The gap is the company of 20 to 500 people. Of the 12, only Ode says it was built for mid-size companies. Craig Donovan, chief operating officer of Pax8, told Channel Dive that the MSP channel will be the forward deployed engineers for small and midsize businesses. Maybe. Questions people actually ask

What is the latest forward deployed engineer news?

On 6 October 2026 the newest stories were AWS's two FDE credentials, reported on 5 October, and Anthropic's Claude Frontier Academy on 2 October, a $100 million plan to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027. The week before, Gartner predicted that 70 percent of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor forward deployed engineering by 2028.

What is a frontier deployed engineer?

It's Anthropic's name for the same job. Anthropic's Claude Frontier Academy trains Frontier Deployed Engineers, who earn a Claude Resident Engineer badge after a graded practical and a Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer badge after a 12-week residency leading a real Claude project at their own employer. Microsoft's equivalent certification is called Frontier Transformation Engineer, according to Channel Dive.

What does forward deployed engineer hacker news say?

The loudest take in Hacker News threads on the job, including Rise of the Forward Deployed Engineer, is that the job is the greatest rebrand in enterprise software history, a consultant with better margins. I answered that head on in is a forward deployed engineer just a consultant. Gartner's Mukul Saha made a similar point in September, that many providers now use forward deployed as a label for implementation or consulting.

How many forward deployed engineers are there?

Gartner's Mukul Saha told Channel Dive in September 2026 that there are about 2,000 active FDEs and demand is probably four times that. Open roles are a separate number. On 6 October 2026 I counted 231 open titles with forward deployed in them at six employers alone, 81 at Palantir and 99 at Databricks.

How much do forward deployed engineers cost a company?

Gartner estimates FDE consulting fees at $200,000 to $400,000 a quarter per use case before platform and integration costs, as reported by CIO Dive. That's what the buyer pays the vendor, not the engineer's salary. Google Cloud's New York and Atlanta postings in May paid a base of $127,000 to $183,000.

What is a forward deployed engineer?

A software engineer who works inside a customer's company until the product runs in production there. I wrote the full answer, with the job postings behind it, in what is a forward deployed engineer.
