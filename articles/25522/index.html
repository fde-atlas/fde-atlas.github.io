FDE vs Software Engineer vs Solutions Engineer

Infomaze | AI-Driven Business Solutions Company · 2026-10-29 · 4 min read

Summary

This article distinguishes three customer-adjacent engineering roles that are often confused. Software engineers build and maintain product codebases against internal roadmaps; solutions engineers support the sales cycle through demos, proofs of concept, and technical evaluation; forward deployed engineers work embedded with clients, discovering requirements through engagement and staying through implementation, production rollout, and early adoption. The piece compares the roles across codebase, client contact, skills, success metrics, and promotion paths, then advises job seekers and hiring companies on choosing among them, noting an SWE background is one route into FDE work but not the only one.

Key points

  • SWEs build product software; success means reliable, requirement-meeting code.
  • Solutions engineers focus on demos, PoCs, and deal support during sales.
  • FDEs work in client environments through deployment and adoption, with no clean handoff.
  • Key skills differ: technical craft, technical communication, and ambiguity management respectively.
  • Hiring choice depends on whether the problem is product-building or customer-specific delivery.

A software engineer (SWE) typically builds and maintains software as part of a product or engineering team, often for a broad user base. A solutions engineer typically supports customers during the technical sales process through demos, proofs of concept, solution design, and technical evaluation. A forward deployed engineer (FDE) combines hands-on software engineering with direct customer engagement, working on specific customer problems through implementation and, in many cases, production deployment and adoption.

The confusion between these three titles is understandable — all three sit close to the customer-facing side of engineering, all three can appear on the same team, and job descriptions often borrow language from each other. But the day-to-day reality, the skills the role rewards, and how each person's success actually gets measured are genuinely different jobs wearing similar-sounding names.

The three roles, side by side

Software Engineer

  • Builds and maintains software for a product or organization
  • Works from internal specs/roadmaps
  • Usually has limited direct end-client interaction
  • Success = reliable software that meets product requirements

Solutions Engineer

  • Supports the technical side of the sales cycle
  • Builds demos and PoCs
  • Deep client contact, often concentrated around evaluation and sales
  • Success = effective technical evaluation and deal support

Forward Deployed Engineer

  • Builds for specific customer problems, often embedded with the client team
  • Requirements discovered through direct customer engagement
  • Client contact through implementation and, where needed, adoption
  • Success = the solution works in the client's real environment

Comparing the roles on the things that actually matter

Codebase Central product codebase Demo / sandbox environments Client-specific systems and deployment environment
Client contact Usually limited Often high during evaluation and sales Often high through implementation and adoption
Requirements Usually shaped by product and engineering roadmaps Shaped by the customer opportunity and technical requirements Discovered and refined through customer engagement

Typical career origin Software engineering or other technical development roles Pre-sales, technical support, SWE Software engineering, technical consulting, or customer-facing engineering

Where the work happens

A SWE typically works within the product's codebase and engineering environment. A solutions engineer often works across demos, proofs of concept, and evaluation environments designed to demonstrate how a solution could work for a customer. An FDE works much closer to the customer's actual environment, dealing with real data structures, integrations, workflows, and deployment constraints.

How the job ends (or doesn't)

A solutions engineer's involvement often changes after the sales process, with implementation, delivery, or customer success teams taking on more of the ongoing work. A forward deployed engineer's involvement can extend through implementation, production rollout, and early adoption, depending on the engagement. A forward deployed engineer's involvement often doesn't have that clean handoff — they may stay through go-live, early adoption, and the first rounds of "this doesn't quite work the way we expected."

What gets you promoted

A SWE typically advances through technical impact, ownership, and contributions to the product or engineering organization. A solutions engineer may advance through strong customer outcomes, technical influence, and contribution to revenue-generating opportunities. An FDE may advance through successful customer deployments, technical ownership, problem-solving in ambiguous environments, and the ability to turn lessons from individual engagements into repeatable practices.

A solutions engineer helps demonstrate how a solution can work. A software engineer builds and evolves the product. A forward deployed engineer adapts engineering to make a solution work for a specific customer and their environment.

Which one should you aim for, or hire for?

If you're mapping out a career, the right path depends on the type of work you enjoy. Software engineering emphasizes building and evolving software at depth. Solutions engineering emphasizes technical communication, customer needs, and the sales process. Forward deployed engineering combines hands-on engineering with direct customer problem-solving and deployment. A strong software engineering foundation can be useful for an FDE career, but it isn't the only possible route.

If you're a company deciding what to hire, the distinction comes down to the problem you're trying to solve. A SWE is generally focused on building and evolving a product or internal system. A solutions engineer is generally focused on technical evaluation, solution design, and supporting the sales process. A forward deployed engineer is useful when the challenge requires hands-on engineering alongside direct work with a specific customer's systems, workflows, and requirements.
