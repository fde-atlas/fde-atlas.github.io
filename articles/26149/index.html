Two weeks ago I joined Sycamore as a Forward Deployed Engineer.

I have always been an FDE first, and the founder and editor of FDE Hub second. This newsletter works because it is written from inside the job, and I need to keep doing the job to write about it from experience.

Why Sycamore

Sycamore is building an agent operating system for the enterprise: software for building AI agents, and for governing them once they are running.

What drew me in is that the company is organised around problems I have been writing about here for a year. I have argued that agents earn autonomy in stages, and that trust decides how fast a deployment moves far more than capability does. I have written about systems that go stale when the business changes its rules and the software never hears about it.

At Sycamore those are product questions that a team of engineers and researchers works on every day. It is one thing to describe those problems in an essay. I wanted to work on them directly.

The job runs in both directions

I sit in the engineering team, and the organisation is very flat.

A large part of my work starts with research. There is a lot of it around me, and my job is to digest it, work out how it applies, and turn it into something repeatable, scalable and useful to customers.

The other part runs the opposite way. I sit with customers, work out what they really need, and bring that back into the product.

I have argued before that this loop is the reason FDE teams exist, and it is good to be standing in the middle of it. In two weeks I have jumped into building, started working with a customer and met prospects.

The pace

Much of my day goes into directing a variety of agents, and the amount that gets delivered is hard to compare with how I worked a year ago.

The pace of the engineering has astonished me, and I say that as someone who has already written about how fast building has become. Speed like this changes which part of the job is scarce, and I am still working out what that means in practice.

What this means for FDE Hub

FDE Hub stays vendor-neutral. I will keep writing about the craft, and the interviews will keep featuring practitioners from across the industry.

What changes is the material. I am building and sitting with customers every week, and that will find its way into the essays.

I have also recorded five new conversations for the video series, and they will come out over the coming weeks.

It has been a good two weeks.
