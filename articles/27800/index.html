Today's Judgment

Today's three signals showcase three different paths to enterprise AI scale-up—but the underlying logic is the same: prove it works in real scenarios first, then solidify it into replicable assets.

China Telecom treats its 300,000 internal employees as "Customer Zero," using real usage to accumulate more than 350 industry-specific agents, which it then offers to 37,000 industry customers; OpenAI turned its IT help desk into its first agent at scale, resolving about 45% of ticket volume; and Wuhan East Lake High-Tech Zone has begun formally recognizing "AI + manufacturing service providers" as a qualification.

For FDEs, the path to scale can be summarized in five steps: prove it on-site → confirm boundaries → package assets → drive organizational adoption → verify results. Skipping any step and declaring "it's live" is planting a landmine for yourself.

Key Signals

China Telecom: 300,000 internal users as "Customer Zero," then distilled into industry services

  • Source: China Telecom official ｜ Original link
  • On August 25, China Telecom disclosed: TeleAgent has more than 300,000 registered internal users and over 200 billion daily token calls; the group has developed more than 110 industry large models and over 350 industry-specific agents, serving more than 37,000 industry customers. A manufacturing case in the field: a single unit at Harbin Boiler Works involves nearly 1,500 components, previously tracked with paper ledgers and Excel; after spending years integrating sales, production, subcontracting, and procurement data, the company partnered with China Telecom to build an AI intelligent cloud platform.
  • Why it matters: The replication sequence is worth learning from for every FDE team—first let internal employees become Customer Zero, forming real tools and usage feedback, then distill standard solutions into customers' core workflows. This carries far less risk than the traditional path of "build a demo first, then find customers, then come back and revise." The power users in an organization who are already spontaneously using AI to solve problems should be treated as "internal benchmarks and co-creators," not just training targets. Note the boundaries: figures like 300,000 and 200 billion are official claims; the Harbin Boiler case did not disclose delay rates or cost before/after.

OpenAI's internal IT agent resolves about 45% of tickets, clearing backlogs as ticket volume doubles

  • Source: OpenAI official ｜ Original link
  • On August 25, OpenAI released the ChatGPT Work/Codex Admin plugin and disclosed: its internal IT team uses an agent to handle employee requests, triage, and execute support tickets, already resolving about 45% of ticket volume. While ticket volume roughly doubled, an operations dashboard helped eliminate the backlog. Key mechanism: the Admin plugin inherits existing roles, groups, and permission controls, without expanding the operator's privileges.
  • Why it matters: This is a complete production structure—high-frequency tickets, approved policies, executable actions, permission inheritance, exception escalation, and a real-time operations dashboard. Enterprise internal help desks are naturally suited to be the first agent at scale: high demand, clear rules, controllable risk, and failures can be escalated. But acceptance should be measured by resolution rate, escalation rate, backlog changes, handling time, and erroneous actions—not conversation counts. Note: the 45% figure is self-reported by OpenAI for a single organization; error rate, satisfaction, and per-ticket cost were not disclosed.

Wuhan East Lake High-Tech Zone: Local policy formally recognizes "AI + manufacturing service providers"

  • Source: Wuhan East Lake High-Tech Zone Administrative Committee
  • Wuhan East Lake High-Tech Zone has forwarded the 2026 application notice for "AI + manufacturing" service providers, clarifying that third-party services can cover five major stages: R&D design, pilot-scale validation, production manufacturing, marketing services, and operations management.
  • Why it matters: Local policy has begun formally recognizing the role of "AI + manufacturing service provider"—this is no small change. It means FDE teams can participate in enterprise AI deployment not just as "AI consultants" inside client organizations, but as policy-recognized third-party service providers, covering the full chain from R&D to operations. This further confirms the trend toward FDE professionalization (previous signals include the Shanghai Hongkou FDE bootcamp and Tencent Cloud renaming its certification to "Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) Certification"). It is still at the application stage; the selected list and project results remain to be tracked.

Additional context: Notably, on August 18 Tencent Cloud officially renamed its "Agent Development Platform AI Application Engineer Certification" to the "Tencent Cloud ADP Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) Certification", adding data permissions, hallucination, privilege escalation, and compliance boundaries to the exam scope—making it the first official cloud vendor certification in China named after "FDE."

Takeaways for FDE Practitioners

  • Find the "Customer Zero" in your organization: Every enterprise has power users who are already spontaneously using AI to solve real problems. An FDE's job is not just to teach people how to use AI, but to find these people, measure their tacit methods (time, quality, and failure counts on the same tasks), package them into reusable Skills, verify the results, and then replicate them to a second person and a second department.
  • Internal help desks are the best starting point for agents at scale: High demand, existing rules, controllable risk, inheritable permissions, and escalatable failures—filter candidates by this checklist; IT support, HR FAQs, and finance expense approvals are all high-priority candidates.
  • The most valuable judgment in AI automation is often "don't do it": System export limits of 100 rows, unstable page skins, closed APIs, data volumes exceeding processing capacity—these "hard boundaries" should be written into veto cards before development. FDEs should prove a task is worth automating before building it, and also formally document the reasons for not doing it.
  • FDE professionalization is accelerating: From the Shanghai Hongkou bootcamp to the Tencent Cloud FDE certification to the Wuhan service provider applications—government, cloud vendors, and industry are all establishing formal entry points for this role.
