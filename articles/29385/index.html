Forward Deployed Engineer vs. Solutions Architect vs. Software Engineer: What Is the Difference?

Design Gurus · 2026-11-14 · 3 min read

Summary

This article compares three engineering roles: software engineers build one product for many users, solutions architects advise a single customer with mostly diagrams and little code, and forward deployed engineers write production code inside one customer's environment. It contrasts who each role serves, how much code they write, and how success is measured, then details how their interview loops differ. Palantir created the FDE role, and AI companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Cognition now hire for it. The piece ends with advice on choosing a role and preparing for each interview format.

Key points

  • SWE loops test algorithms and system design; SA loops often skip live coding.
  • FDE interviews weight decomposition: turning vague problems into plans.
  • AWS solutions architect loops include three behavioral rounds out of five.
  • Cognition runs an executive pitch and live customer call simulation.
  • Many engineers move from SWE to FDE since coding skills transfer.

A software engineer builds a product for many customers, a solutions architect designs how one customer should use a product, and a forward deployed engineer builds and ships the working system for one customer. All three roles need engineering skill, but they differ in who they serve, how much code they write, and how they are measured. The interviews differ in the same way. A software engineer loop tests coding and system design. A solutions architect loop tests design and customer communication. A forward deployed engineer loop tests all of these, with the most weight on turning a vague customer problem into a plan.

Quick Comparison

Serves All users of the product One customer at a time, often before the sale One customer at a time, after the sale
Main output Product code Architecture designs and recommendations Working code in the customer's systems
Code written Most Least A lot, but customer-specific

Software Engineer

A software engineer (SWE) works on one product inside one company. The requirements arrive through product managers, and the engineer rarely meets a customer directly. Success means the product works well for thousands or millions of users. The interview loop is the most standard: coding rounds, a system design round, and behavioral questions.

Solutions Architect

A solutions architect (SA) helps customers decide how to use a product, often during the sales process. Cloud companies like AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft hire many SAs. The daily work is discovery calls, architecture diagrams, cost estimates, and presentations, with only small amounts of code. SA interviews often include a customer design round, a presentation to a hiring manager, and many behavioral rounds; at AWS, three of the five loop interviews are behavioral. What is the solutions architect interview process like? explains each round.

Forward Deployed Engineer

A forward deployed engineer (FDE) works directly with one customer, often on site or inside the customer's systems, and writes the code that makes the product work for them. Palantir created the role, and AI companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Cognition now hire FDEs to put their models into production at large customers. The FDE also reports what customers need back to the product team. Success means the customer's system runs in production and delivers a measurable result.

How the Interviews Differ

  • Coding. SWE loops use the hardest algorithm questions. FDE loops use practical, staged coding tied to a user. SA loops often skip live coding.
  • Design. SWE system design asks for scale. SA design asks for the right services and costs for a customer. FDE decomposition asks for the smallest system that solves a vague problem, as described in What is the Palantir decomposition interview?
  • Customer rounds. SA and FDE loops both include customer simulations. Cognition, for example, runs an executive pitch and a live customer call.
  • Behavioral. All three test ownership. SA and FDE loops also test how you handle difficult stakeholders.

How to Choose

Choose software engineering if you want deep work on one product and little customer contact. Choose solutions architecture if you enjoy design, presenting, and sales conversations more than building. Choose forward deployed engineering if you want to write production code and also work with customers every week. Many engineers move from SWE to FDE, because the coding skills transfer and the customer skills can be practiced.
