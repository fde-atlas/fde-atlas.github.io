The author argues many enterprises buy tools like Trae, MiniMax Code, and large models but lack the skills to configure agents, MCP, and workflows, leaving them idle. [AI] Introduction to FDE Forward Deployed Engineer_fde engineer-CSDN Blog

FDE, Forward Deployed Engineer. Mapped to the AI industry: instead of sitting in the company's back office writing generic products, the engineer is stationed long-term on-site in the customer's intranet/R&D environment, facing the customer's real, messy, restricted, and confidential business environment firsthand, closing the last mile between AI tools and enterprise business.

Companies today commonly purchase AI tools such as Trae, MiniMax Code, and large models, but their internal employees don't know how to configure agents, MCP, workflows, or tools, so the tools sit idle after purchase. The FDE is the role that solves this industry pain point of "bought it but can't use it, no value in deployment."

Understand the business, understand the product, understand AI, and be able to take a project to its ceiling. We can think of them as AI deployment engineers. The core is whether you can drive the customer's core results to land. The biggest problem with AI today is no longer that large models aren't smart enough, but that many companies don't know how to use AI. Previously, in a company, the product manager handled requirements raised by customers, programmers developed based on those requirements, and solution consultants communicated with customers; requirements could be analyzed when they came in, solutions could be designed, systems could be developed, engineering could be carried through to deployment, and customer problems could be solved. So the people best suited to turning AI into real productivity are precisely those closest to the FDE. The AI era needs: results-oriented talent. An engineer in a company who gets pulled into meetings simultaneously by the product, sales, and R&D departments is pretty close to the FDE role.

You need to fill in gaps in large model application development, AI product thinking, agent development, AI workflows, AI products, and calling large models.

When the customer hasn't yet figured out what they want, you help them figure it out, while also being able to build the prototype yourself. Every FDE communication has a deliverable: you must output a requirements brief that the customer can sign off on; what you say verbally must match what the system actually does. Most people like to passively wait for requirements, but the FDE is different—the FDE must proactively dig for requirements. You must be able to deploy independently, demo independently, and debug independently on the customer's site, and when problems arise you must solve them on the spot, which means the FDE's tech stack must be horizontal.

FDE is not a technical role but a results role—whether the customer's business goals are achieved is your evaluation criterion. Technology is merely a tool you use to achieve results, not your identity. The FDE's KPI doesn't depend on technology, but on the ability to make a pile of complex business requirements actually run—something current AI still can't do, because customer chaos is not a data problem but a people problem.
