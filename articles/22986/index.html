Summary

Written by Shohei Tokumitsu, CEO of DeFactory, this Japanese article gives a balanced overview of going freelance as an engineer, aimed at readers considering independence. Citing 2025 market data, it notes freelance IT engineers average about 750,000 yen per month (roughly 9 million yen annually), versus about 5.5 million yen for salaried engineers, with top contracts reaching 2.85 million yen monthly. It lists six benefits (high income, flexibility, choosing projects, tax deductions, no retirement age, fewer office politics) and six drawbacks (income instability, self-marketing, weak social credit for loans, admin/tax burden, self-funded training, no pension or severance), each with countermeasures. It ends with a 10-question aptitude checklist and advises having 3+ years of experience and six months of savings before going independent.

Key points

  • Freelance IT engineers average ~750,000 yen/month, roughly 9 million yen a year.
  • Salaried engineers average ~5.5 million yen; high rates require 3+ years' experience.
  • Six benefits include flexibility, project choice, tax deductions, and no retirement age.
  • Six drawbacks include unstable income, self-marketing, and no employer pension.
  • A 10-question checklist helps readers judge readiness before going independent.

1. What Is a Freelance Engineer? The Fundamental Differences from Being a Company Employee

\n\n\n\n

Freelance engineer or company employee engineer—which should you choose? There is no single right answer to this question.

\n\n\n\n

What matters is correctly understanding the differences between the two and making the choice that fits your own values and life stage.

\n\n\n\n

In this chapter, we clarify the definition of a freelance engineer and organize the differences from company employees from multiple perspectives, including "income structure," "employee benefits," and "social security." A comparison table is provided at the end of the chapter, so please use it as a reference for your decision.

\n\n\n\n Limited to engineers with 5+ years of experience: High-paying, direct-contract project information

1-1. Definition of Freelance Engineers and Types of Work Styles

\n\n\n\n

A freelance engineer is an engineer who is not employed by a company and works on IT development as a sole proprietor.

\n\n\n\n

Work styles can be broadly divided into two types.

\n\n\n\n

Remote Work Type

\n\n\n\n

This style involves working from home or a coworking space. It offers a high degree of freedom in terms of location and time, and even those living in rural areas can take on projects from urban areas. However, rates tend to be somewhat lower than the on-site type.

\n\n\n\n

On-Site Type

\n\n\n\n

This style involves visiting the client company and participating in development as a member of the team. There are many high-paying projects, and abundant opportunities to get involved from the upstream stages of projects. On the other hand, freedom in where and when you work is limited.

\n\n\n\n

It is not a matter of which is better or worse; what is important is choosing the style that matches the way you want to work.

\n\n\n\n

Also, in recent years, the FDE role has emerged as a new job type, beyond SES.

\n\n\n\n

[Reference] What Is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)? | Roles and Career Strategies Required in the AI Era

\n\n\n\n

1-2. Differences from Company Employees in Employment Status and Income Structure

\n\n\n\n

Company employee engineers receive a fixed salary every month and can also expect bonuses once or twice a year. Income stability is excellent, but there are limits to how much salaries can increase.

\n\n\n\n

Freelance engineers, on the other hand, are paid per project. While income fluctuates, it is possible to significantly increase your annual income in a short period depending on your abilities.

\n\n\n\n

However, if projects dry up, your income drops to zero. "The potential for high income" and "stability" are in a trade-off relationship.

\n\n\n\n

Which you prioritize depends on your family situation and life stage. Whether you are in an "offensive phase now" or a "phase of consolidating your defenses," calmly assess your own situation.

\n\n\n\n

2. Six Benefits of Being a Freelance Engineer

\n\n\n\n

Freelance engineers offer many attractions that company employees cannot enjoy.

\n\n\n\n

We will explain the six benefits—"high income," "freedom in how you work," "control over your career," "a reset of workplace relationships," "tax savings," and "no retirement age"—with market data and concrete examples.

\n\n\n\n

2-1. Achieve High Income Depending on Your Ability

\n\n\n\n

The greatest benefit of being a freelance engineer is the ability to achieve a high income depending on your skills.

\n\n\n\n

According to data as of November 2025, the average monthly rate for freelance IT engineer projects is about 750,000 yen, which translates to an annual income of 9 million yen. There are even projects with rates as high as 2.85 million yen, so an annual income exceeding 10 million yen is by no means a dream.

\n\n\n\n

Source: Levtech Freelance "Freelance Engineer Project Rate Market"

\n\n\n\n

Considering that the average annual income of company-employed engineers is about 5.5 million yen, the difference is clear.

\n\n\n\n

However, there is a caveat. Securing high-rate projects requires a certain level of skill and experience. As a guideline, it is said that with 3 or more years of practical experience, you can aim for monthly rates of 600,000 to 800,000 yen.

\n\n\n\n

2-2. Flexible Working Free from Constraints of Time and Place

\n\n\n\n

Freelance engineers can decide for themselves when and where they work.

\n\n\n\n

As long as deadlines are met, you can work intensively late at night or work remotely from rural areas or overseas. It accommodates diverse lifestyles, such as balancing childcare or caregiving, or working alongside a side job.

\n\n\n\n

According to the Freelancers Association's "Freelance White Paper 2024," the number one benefit freelancers feel is "being able to freely decide when and where to work."

\n\n\n\n

Source: Freelancers Association "Freelance White Paper 2024"

\n\n\n\n

This high degree of freedom will be a major attraction for those who feel stressed by rush-hour commutes and fixed working hours.

\n\n\n\n

2-3. Freedom to Choose Projects and Control Over Career Design

\n\n\n\n

For company employees, which project they are assigned to depends on the company's decisions.

\n\n\n\n

"I really want to try AI development, but I'm only given maintenance and operations work"—many people struggle with such frustrations.

\n\n\n\n

As a freelancer, you can choose the projects you take on. You can select projects aligned with your career direction, such as "I want to try AI development," "I want to specialize in Python," or "I want to gain experience in upstream processes."

\n\n\n\n

Because you can compete in your areas of strength, it is easier to perform well, which in turn tends to lead to high results and rewards.

\n\n\n\n

2-4. Reduced Stress from Workplace Relationships

\n\n\n\n

Many people struggle with workplace relationships. Compatibility with superiors, friction with colleagues, office politics—quite a few people want to be free from such stress.

\n\n\n\n

Freelance engineers do not belong to a fixed organization. Even if there is a client you don't get along with, you can reset the relationship once the contract ends.

\n\n\n\n

Of course, building good relationships with clients is necessary. However, you are freed from situations like "all I can do is wait for a transfer" or "I have no choice but to endure and keep working," which company employees face.

\n\n\n\n

For those who have repeatedly changed jobs due to stress from workplace relationships, freelancing often leads to mental stability.

\n\n\n\n

2-5. Tax Savings Through Business Expenses

\n\n\n\n

Freelancers, as sole proprietors, can deduct expenses necessary for their business. This makes it possible to reduce taxable income and save on taxes.

\n\n\n\n

Examples of deductible expenses:

\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • PCs, software, books
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Communication costs (smartphone and internet fees)
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Seminar participation fees and certification costs
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Travel expenses and costs of meals for meetings
    • \n\n\n\n
  • A proportional share of rent if part of your home is used as a workspace
    • \n

\n\n\n\n

Furthermore, by using blue tax return filing, you can receive a deduction of up to 650,000 yen.

\n\n\n\n

The option of "writing off expenses," which is unavailable to company employees, is a financial benefit unique to freelancing.

\n\n\n\n

2-6. No Retirement Age—Work for as Long as You Want

\n\n\n\n

Company employees have a mandatory retirement age of 60 to 65, but freelancers have no retirement age.

\n\n\n\n

As long as you have skills and stamina, you can keep working at any age. In fact, the number of people active as freelance engineers in their 60s and 70s is growing.

\n\n\n\n

In this era of 100-year lifespans, "being able to keep earning for a long time" is a major benefit.

\n\n\n\n

There is no need to worry about age-related barriers to changing jobs, and you can decide when to retire at your own pace—this, too, is an attraction unique to freelancing.

\n\n\n\n

\n【From a DeFactory Career Advisor's Perspective】\n\n\n\n"Among the advantages, the most underrated is 'the freedom to choose your projects.' I've seen many cases where people who were worn down by 'work they didn't want to do' as company employees saw their rates jump 1.5x the moment they went freelance and focused on their areas of expertise."\n

3. Disadvantages of Being a Freelance Engineer and How to Address Them

\n\n\n\n

While freelance engineering offers attractive advantages, it also comes with drawbacks that cannot be overlooked.

\n\n\n\n

However, if you know the disadvantages in advance, you can prepare countermeasures. In this chapter, we present six disadvantages along with their solutions.

\n\n\n\n

3-1. Income Tends to Be Unstable

\n\n\n\n

The biggest disadvantage of freelancing is unstable income.

\n\n\n\n

Months without projects mean zero income. The gap between busy and slow periods is large, and unlike a salaried employee, a fixed salary is not deposited every month.

\n\n\n\n

Especially right after going independent, landing projects can be difficult, and you may go through a period of drawing down your savings.

\n\n\n\n

【Countermeasures】

\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Sign quasi-delegation contracts for long-term engagements of six months to a year
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Diversify income sources by handling multiple projects in parallel
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Go independent only after securing at least six months' worth of living expenses in savings
    • \n

\n\n\n\n

3-2. Sales Efforts Are Required to Win Projects

\n\n\n\n

Unlike company employees, freelancers must continually secure projects on their own.

\n\n\n\n

Beyond technical skills, sales ability, communication skills, and self-promotion skills are also required. Many say "winning projects through sales efforts is the hardest part," and it is true that this is a high hurdle for introverted people who are not good at selling themselves.

\n\n\n\n

【Countermeasures】

\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Build a strong skill sheet and wait for scout offers
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Make use of agents and matching services
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Increase visibility by sharing content on social media and technical blogs
    • \n

\n\n\n\n

If you are not good at sales, it is important to build "a system where projects come to you without active selling."

\n\n\n\n

3-3. Lower Social Credibility Makes Approvals Harder to Obtain

\n\n\n\n

In Japan, the reality is that freelancers have lower social credibility.

\n\n\n\n

There is the disadvantage that it is harder to pass screening for mortgages, credit cards, and rental housing. Even with a high income right after going independent, cases of being rejected are not uncommon, with judgments like "there is no guarantee of stable earnings going forward."

\n\n\n\n

【Countermeasures】

\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Complete your mortgage, credit card, and rental contracts before going independent
    • \n\n\n\n
  • After going independent, prove your track record with three years of tax returns
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Use financial institutions that offer loans for freelancers
    • \n

\n\n\n\n

3-4. You Must Handle Administrative and Tax Tasks Yourself

\n\n\n\n

Freelancers must handle all administrative tasks themselves, including drafting contracts, issuing invoices, managing payments, and filing tax returns.

\n\n\n\n

The complexity of handling tasks that accounting and general affairs departments used to do for you as an employee can be a major burden for those who are not good at administrative work.

\n\n\n\n

【Countermeasures】

\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Automate with cloud accounting software such as freee or Money Forward
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Outsource your tax return to a tax accountant (around 100,000-150,000 yen per year)
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Streamline work by preparing templates for contracts and invoices
    • \n

\n\n\n\n

3-5. You Must Continue Upskilling at Your Own Expense

\n\n\n\n

The IT industry changes rapidly, and if your skills become outdated, winning projects becomes difficult.

\n\n\n\n

\n\n\n\n

\n\n\n\n

【Countermeasures】

\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Learning expenses can be recorded as deductible expenses, which also helps with tax savings
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Learn efficiently using Udemy, books, and technical conferences
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Deliberately choose projects that give you opportunities to work with new technologies
    • \n

\n\n\n\n

3-6. No Retirement Allowance or Employees' Pension, So You Must Prepare Retirement Funds

\n\n\n\n

Freelancers have no retirement allowance system and cannot enroll in the Employees' Pension Insurance. Your future pension amount will be smaller than that of a company employee.

\n\n\n\n

Even after working for many years, you will not receive a lump sum upon retirement, so you need to plan and prepare your retirement funds yourself.

\n\n\n\n

【Countermeasures】

\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Enroll in the Small Business Mutual Aid (full contributions are tax-deductible, up to 70,000 yen per month)
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Build retirement funds with iDeCo (individual-type defined contribution pension plan)
    • \n\n\n\n
  • Supplement your pension with the National Pension Fund
    • \n

\n\n\n\n

It is important to "build your system while you are earning well."

\n\n\n\n

\n【From a DeFactory Career Advisor's Perspective】\n\n\n\n"Many engineers say they are anxious about sales, but in fact, just by changing how you write your skill sheet, scout offers can come in without any active selling. It is not uncommon for response rates to more than double simply by writing about 'what problems you solved' rather than 'what you can do.'"\n

4. Who Is Suited (and Not Suited) to Being a Freelance Engineer

\n\n\n\n

With the advantages and disadvantages in mind, the next thing to consider is "whether it suits you."

\n\n\n\n

Freelancing isn't right for everyone. Assessing your aptitude is the key to success.

\n\n\n\n

In this chapter, we'll outline the characteristics of people who are and aren't suited to freelancing, and finish with a scoring-based aptitude assessment.

\n\n\n\n

For a detailed explanation of the skills needed to go independent and aim for an annual income of 10 million yen, see here.

\n\n\n\n

4-1. Characteristics of People Suited to Going Independent

\n\n\n\n

Here is a table summarizing the characteristics of people suited to freelancing.

\n\n\n\n

CharacteristicDetails
High specialized skills and extensive practical experienceA guideline is 3+ years of practical experience. You need to be at a level where you can say "leave it to me" in a specific technical area
Wants to work proactively / strong desire for self-determinationPeople with a strong desire to "decide their own career" and "choose the work they want to do" can make full use of the freedom freelancing offers

If three or more of these apply to you, you likely have the qualities to thrive as a freelancer.

\n\n\n\n

4-2. Tendencies of People Who Should Stay Salaried Employees

\n\n\n\n

On the other hand, people with the following tendencies may be happier staying a salaried employee rather than forcing themselves to go independent.

\n\n\n\n

TendencyDetails
Poor at self-managementMissing deadlines, unable to make plans, unable to manage money. People with these tendencies will struggle
Weak against lonelinessFreelancers basically work alone. It's not suited to people who find it hard without team spirit or casual chats with colleagues

Lacking experience or skillsEspecially for beginners or those unsure of their skills, it's recommended to first build experience as a salaried employee

\n\n\n\n

If many of these apply to you, there's no need to rush into going independent. Being a salaried employee also has advantages like "stability," "social credibility," and "employee benefits." Neither option is absolutely better than the other.
