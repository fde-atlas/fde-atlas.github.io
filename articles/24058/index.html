Is a Career Change to FDE in Your 30s Possible? Transition Routes by Previous Job, Job Comparison, and a 90-Day Plan

Original title: FDEへの30代転職は可能？前職別の移行ルートと求人比較・90日計画

FDE総研 · 2026-11-09 · 14 min read

Summary

A Japanese career guide from FDE総研 arguing that engineers in their 30s can still move into Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roles, a working style popularized by Palantir. It evaluates candidates on three axes—implementation, customer communication, and business understanding—showing typical gaps by prior role: SWEs lack client-facing skills, IT consultants and PdMs lack hands-on coding, and SIer alumni lack rapid prototyping. Citing November 2026 job-listing data from JAPAN AIラボ and SB Bit, it notes many domestic FDE roles list salaries above ¥10 million. It proposes comparing postings on five axes and a 90-day preparation plan for management-track candidates centered on building one working prototype.

Key points

  • 30s transitions are feasible; assess prior experience on three axes: implementation, customer communication, business understanding.
  • Typical gaps: SWEs lack client skills, consultants/PdMs lack coding, SIer alumni lack fast prototyping.
  • Japanese FDE listings (LayerX, ログラス, Sansan, etc.) often exceed ¥10M, but ranges vary widely.
  • Compare job postings on five axes: customer contact, implementation scope, product feedback loop, client count, evaluation metrics.
  • Management-track candidates should follow a 90-day plan producing one working prototype with a listen-build-revise cycle.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role that embeds at the customer's site and handles everything end-to-end, from discovering problems through implementation and adoption. The working style popularized by Palantir serves as its model. FDE Soken is a media site specializing in FDE, operated by VACAN Technologies, Inc. In this article, we organize the following five points in order for engineers in their 30s aiming to become FDEs.

  • Transition routes by previous role
  • How to compare job postings
  • How the answer differs by years of experience, current role, English ability, and company size
  • A 90-day plan for people leaning toward management
  • Differences from adjacent roles

Can you switch to FDE in your 30s? What experience is useful and what skills are missing, by previous role?

Yes, switching to FDE in your 30s is possible. FDE Soken measures previous roles along three axes: implementation, customer engagement, and business understanding.

FDE requires three abilities together: the ability to build software yourself, the ability to articulate problems with customers, and the ability to understand the customer's business. Candidates in their 30s often already have one or two of these from practical work. In career-change preparation, the deciding factor is whether you can fill the missing axis with concrete deliverables. If you want to confirm the definition of the role, reading What is an FDE? first will make things easier to follow.

How do useful experience and missing skills differ by previous role?

SWEs tend to lack customer engagement, IT consultants and PdMs tend to lack implementation, and SIer engineers tend to lack speed in hypothesis testing.

Organizing "directly transferable experience" and "commonly missing skills" for each previous role using FDE Soken's three axes gives the following table.

In FDE Soken's analysis, what tends to block career changes in your 30s is not the volume of technical knowledge but the gap in "time spent working with your own hands." In particular, people from SIers or IT consulting often have deep experience in design and coordination but little experience completing prototypes by writing the code themselves. In that case, what they can talk about in interviews becomes "projects they were involved in," and "things they built themselves" never comes up.

How does evaluation in your 30s differ from your 20s in a career change?

In your 30s, you are increasingly asked about "experience delivering results for customers through your own judgment" rather than potential.

In selection processes for people in their 20s, potential such as learning speed and receptiveness tends to carry more weight. In your 30s, you are more likely to be asked whether you can explain, in your own words, how you defined the customer's problem, what technical decisions you made, and what the results were. If you prepare one episode each, on both the technical and customer-facing sides, that you can tell in the order of "situation → decision → result," your core narrative will stay consistent in any company's selection process. Likely questions are summarized in Questions asked in FDE interviews.

What changes about the way you work when you become an FDE?

Meetings at client sites, business trips, and responses to production incidents increase, and how you spend your time shifts toward the client's schedule.

Since FDEs work in line with the client's business, there are situations where you cannot decide meeting times or visit frequency on your own. Because your 30s often overlap with childcare or caregiving, checking the following three points at the interview stage helps prevent mismatches after joining.

  • The frequency of on-site assignments and business trips, and the extent to which remote work is accepted
  • The first-response system for production incidents (whether there is an on-call rotation or the person in charge handles each case)
  • The number of clients handled simultaneously and the unevenness during busy periods

A day in the field is described in A Day in the Life of an FDE, and how the workload builds up in Is Being an FDE Tough?.

Which items in a job posting should you compare?

FDE Soken compares job postings across five axes: customer contact, implementation scope, feedback into the product, number of accounts handled, and evaluation metrics.

The term "FDE" in job postings can mean quite different things depending on the company. As of November 9, 2026, the FDE Soken editorial team uses the following five axes as its comparison criteria.

The axis where differences are most likely to appear is "feedback into the product." In businesses that handle field data, like VACAN, whether there is a process for reflecting insights gained at client sites into product specifications changes what experience remains after a few years, even with the same job content. How do the answers change depending on years of experience (implementation experience)?

The longer your implementation experience, the more realistic a direct application becomes; if it is short, a path through an internal transfer or an adjacent role is more viable.

Years of experience here refers not to years in the workforce, but to the period during which you wrote code yourself and ran it in production. For those with limited implementation experience, one path is to first get involved in client projects as a solutions engineer or AI engineer, then gradually shift toward FDE. The differences from adjacent roles are organized in FDE vs. Solutions Engineer. For a path from no experience, see How to Become an FDE Without Experience?.

What changes depending on your current position (player or manager)?

Active players who work hands-on can easily prove their implementation skills, while managers need to prepare a fresh, recent deliverable.

Managers have strengths such as experience running organizations and making budget decisions. On the other hand, without recent technical deliverables from the past few years, there is often insufficient material to verify implementation ability during selection. How to bridge this gap is covered concretely in the 90-day plan in the second half.

Does English ability change which jobs you can apply for?

FDE jobs at domestic companies are generally accessible even with Japanese as the main language, while for foreign companies like Palantir it is safest to be prepared to work in English.

FDE jobs at domestic SaaS and AI companies are likely to involve Japanese as the primary language for both clients and internal communication. At foreign companies, it is prudent to anticipate situations where you communicate in English with engineers at headquarters or teams in other countries. Applications to Palantir are made through Palantir's official careers page. Please verify application requirements and programming languages in the official job description for each position.

How do the answers for people in their 30s change depending on the size of the company they move to?

Startups tend to offer greater discretion and a broader scope of responsibility, while large and foreign companies tend to have more divided roles and well-established evaluation systems.

At startups, FDEs often handle a wide range of responsibilities, from joining sales visits to data integration and operational design. This suits people in their 30s who want to gain the experience of "running everything on their own." At large companies and foreign firms, role boundaries tend to be clearly defined, and while evaluation systems and training are well established, the scope of responsibility is often limited. If, due to family circumstances, this is a period when you prioritize stability in how you work, choosing the latter is a reasonable decision.

For people in their 30s leaning toward management, what should they prepare over 90 days for an FDE career change?

The FDE Research Institute recommends that people in their 30s leaning toward management divide the 90 days into three 30-day phases, with top priority on completing one working deliverable.

The purpose of each phase and the state you should reach by its end are summarized in the diagram directly below this heading. If you want to systematically understand the order of learning, the FDE learning roadmap is a useful reference.

Days 1–30: Where should you start?

Focus the first 30 days on regaining your implementation instincts and choosing one business problem to work on as your subject matter.

  • Write a small script in Python and SQL that retrieves and processes data from a local CSV or API
  • Call a generative AI API and try applying summarization or classification to business data
  • From your own department or nearby work, pick one "task that is done manually every week"
  • Map out the flow of that task while interviewing the people involved

People in management positions have an advantage at the stage of choosing the subject matter, because they already know who is struggling with what. How to conduct business interviews is covered in detail in How to conduct business interviews.

Days 31–60: What should you build?

In the next 30 days, build a prototype that runs on data close to real data and have your intended users try it.

  • For the business problem you chose, build a prototype with only the minimum necessary features
  • Run it without using confidential information, using anonymized or dummy data
  • Have 2–3 intended users actually try it, and record their reactions and points for improvement
  • Rebuild it at least once based on the feedback

What matters here is not polish, but completing at least one cycle of the "listen → build → revise" loop. This is because an FDE's work closely resembles repeating this loop at the customer's site.

Days 61–90: How should you prepare your applications?

In the final 30 days, translate the story of your prototype into your resume, narrow down FDE job openings using the five criteria, and apply.

  • Summarize your prototype on one page: the problem, your decisions, the results, and the remaining limitations
  • Compare job openings using the five-criteria table introduced earlier, and narrow down to 3–5 high-priority companies
  • Prepare anticipated interview Q&A on both technical and customer-facing aspects
  • If needed, also consult a recruiting agency specializing in FDE placements

How should you present your management experience in FDE selection processes?

Rather than the number of subordinates, it is more effective to describe specific situations where you defined a customer's problem and made technical decisions.

Simply writing "managed a team of X people" makes it hard to convey your suitability as an FDE. For example, write out situations where you compared vendor proposals and made a technology selection, where you reset priorities when requirements were unstable, or where you explained an incident to a customer. Then, presenting these alongside the prototype you built over the 90 days demonstrates both judgment and implementation skills.

What is the biggest difference between SWEs, IT consultants, PdMs, SIers, and FDEs?

The biggest difference lies in whether the same person handles everything from problem definition through implementation and adoption.

IT consultants define problems, SIers build to fixed specifications, SWEs build out products, and PdMs set direction. The FDE is the role that connects all of these at the customer's site, either alone or in a small team. For this reason, no matter which role you come from, you need to take on yourself the parts that others would normally handle. The differences from IT consultants are compared in detail in Differences between FDEs and consultants, and those from SIers in Differences between FDEs and SIers.

Who in their 30s is not suited to being an FDE?

It is not a good fit for people who want to master a specific technology deeply, who find customer-side coordination burdensome, or who cannot increase their travel.

  • People who want to spend a long time digging deep into one technical domain (FDEs tend to change technologies from customer to customer)
  • People who want to build only after requirements are fixed (FDEs often get involved while things are still ambiguous)
  • People who find coordination and explanations at customer sites highly burdensome
  • People who currently have circumstances preventing them from increasing business trips or on-site assignments
  • People who want to be evaluated on their individual technical achievements (FDE evaluations tend to center on customer outcomes)

If you want to check your suitability in more detail, see People suited and not suited to being an FDE.

When is it better to choose something other than FDE?

During periods when travel is difficult due to family circumstances, or when you want to deepen your technical expertise, choosing SWE or PdM is also a reasonable decision.

A career in your 30s is not decided by a single job change. It is possible to first deepen your implementation skills as an SWE and move to an FDE role a few years later. Conversely, you could experience FDE work first and then move on to PdM or going independent, armed with the business knowledge gained in the field. Realistically, which to prioritize should be decided by weighing your current life constraints against the axis you most want to develop. Career paths beyond FDE are introduced in FDE career paths.
