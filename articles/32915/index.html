How companies build a forward deployed engineering team

Kernavique · 2026-11-20 · 15 min read

Summary

Kernavique surveys how companies structure forward deployed engineering teams. Employers that describe their teams use pods or pairings rather than lone engineers: Salesforce uses pods of one deployment strategist and two engineers per client for about three months, while Databricks pairs an engineer with a strategist. Reporting lines vary widely across engineering, sales, customer success, professional services and standalone units. Most teams are recent: Ramp grew from 2 to 16 engineers starting fall 2023, and Salesforce launched its team in April 2025 with a 1,000-person target. The author notes no employer publishes engineer-to-customer ratios, attrition or time-to-fill data, and recommends a hiring sequence: a self-sufficient engineer first, then a customer-side partner, then a second engineer.

Key points

  • Salesforce pods pair one deployment strategist with two engineers, one client at a time.
  • Team placement varies: engineering, sales, customer success, professional services, or standalone units.
  • AWS announced a $1 billion forward deployed engineering investment in June 2026.
  • No employer publishes engineer-to-customer ratios, attrition, or time-to-fill figures.
  • Recommended hiring order: solo-capable engineer, customer-side partner, second engineer, mentoring lead.

What employers say about structure

Few employers describe how their forward deployed team is built. Those that do describe pods and pairings, not engineers working alone. Everything below was read on November 20, 2026.

We read these pages as text, through a tool that shortens long sentences. So we paraphrase, and we quote only a few words at a time.

Employer Structure it describes Source and date
Salesforce Some engineers work in pods of one deployment strategist and two forward deployed engineers. The engineers are the main coders. A pod focuses on one client for about three months. Salesforce 360 Blog, March 27, 2026
Palantir The Deployment Strategist ad describes a team of engineers, product designers and other strategists, and says strategists work with forward deployed engineers on the data pipeline. The engineer ad speaks of “small, agile teams”. Palantir job ads, no date shown
ServiceNow Miku Jha, who leads the function, says “It’s always a pod composition”, never a single engineer. Insight Partners, July 21, 2026
Databricks Marcello Pedersen describes a “two-in-a-box” model: an engineer paired with a deployment strategist. Insight Partners, July 21, 2026
PagerDuty A lead forward deployed engineer is embedded in a product feature team as “a virtual team member”. PagerDuty engineering blog, March 24, 2026
Deloitte Its Palantir ad describes pod-based delivery and expects the engineer to work independently inside a pod while mentoring newer team members. Deloitte job ad, no date shown
Tennr The role sits in an “implementation pod”. Tennr job ad, no date shown

Two cautions. Palantir’s engineer ad does not mention deployment strategists; the pairing appears only in the strategist ad. And the ServiceNow and Databricks statements were reported by Insight Partners, an investor in Databricks, so treat them as named statements passed on by an interested party.

The common shape is a pair or a trio: one or two engineers who write the code, and one person who owns the customer problem. Palantir and Databricks call that second person a deployment strategist. In the job feeds we read on November 20, 2026, 13 employers posted 83 roles with that title, 36 of them in the US.

When companies created the function

Most of these teams are new. These are the dated statements we could read.

Company What it says Source
Ramp The function began to take shape in “fall 2023” and grew from 2 to 16 engineers. Ramp engineering blog, no date shown
Salesforce It “launched its team in April 2025”. Salesforce 360 Blog, March 27, 2026
PagerDuty The function launched officially the week of the post. A first engagement of this kind dates to 2019. PagerDuty engineering blog, March 24, 2026
PostHog It had hired its first forward deployed engineer, to help with large migrations. PostHog blog, February 11, 2026
AWS A new forward deployed engineering organization, announced with “a $1 billion investment”. About Amazon, June 30, 2026
Turing Its ad describes joining “the initial team”. Turing job ad, first published September 17, 2026
Monte Carlo Its ad describes “an early hire into a new function”. Monte Carlo job ad, published November 6, 2026

Growth figures are rare. Salesforce says it tripled its team in six months and has committed to building a team of 1,000, but gives no current headcount (Salesforce newsroom, March 18, 2026; Salesforce 360 Blog, March 27, 2026). The AWS post gives no hiring target and does not say how the $1 billion is split.

PagerDuty’s account is the most useful for a company starting out, because it describes what came before. Its earlier services group reached nearly 40 engineers and consultants at its peak and served hundreds of customers, while the company had thousands. That gap is the background the post gives for the new team.

Where the team sits and how it feeds the product

There is no standard reporting line. Three employers have said where theirs sits:

  • PagerDuty proposed to “move the team under Product Development” (engineering blog, March 24, 2026).
  • ServiceNow’s Miku Jha places the team “in product and R&D” (Insight Partners, July 21, 2026).
  • Workato’s Bhaskar Roy calls it part of the product organization (Insight Partners, July 21, 2026).

Ramp’s post does not state a reporting line.

Job ads show more variety. We read the department field or ad text of 14 employers’ ads on November 20, 2026. Ten of them can be sorted into five places.

Where the ad places the team Employers and the label each uses
Engineering or R&D Intercom (“R&D”), Scale AI (“Enterprise Engineering”), C3 AI (“Forward Deployed Engineering”)
Sales or go-to-market Monte Carlo (team “Field Engineering”, department “Sales”), Anthropic (department “Applied AI”; the ad places the role in its GTM team)
Customer success or post-sales OpenRouter (“Customer Success”), AssemblyAI (“Customer Experience”), Domino Data Lab (“Customer Success Engineering”)
Professional services Snorkel AI’s Applied AI Engineer role (“220 - Solutions PS”)
A separate business unit Turing (“Enterprise AI”)

The other four, Tennr, Giga, Invisible Technologies and Deloitte, name a pod, a field engineering team or a practice without saying where it reports. A department field is an administrative label, not an organization chart, so read the table as a rough guide.

Wherever the team sits, the employers that write about it say the work must change the product:

  • Ramp says its engineers build features, many of which are “shipped in the core product”.
  • Salesforce says what its engineers learn on the front line goes back to the product team and engineers.
  • Decagon treats customer requirements as a chance to improve its core product (Decagon blog, May 11, 2026).
  • Anthropic’s ad asks the engineer to bring insights back to its Product and Engineering teams.
  • ServiceNow’s Jha says delivery without roadmap feedback is “only half the job”.

Two leaders warn about the opposite outcome. Jha warns against engineers becoming “the permanent glue” between the customer and the product. Rajkumar Irudayaraj of Alteryx says the function should be “a permanent learning loop” for the company, not a crutch for customers (Insight Partners, July 14, 2026).

What nobody publishes

Three things a founder would want to know are not published by any employer we read.

A ratio of engineers to customers. No employer we read on November 20, 2026, publishes how many customers one forward deployed engineer supports, or how many forward deployed engineers it has for each product engineer. The only figure we found comes from one practitioner. Kabir Sial, a former Palantir forward deployed engineer who is now an investor, wrote on CIO.com on August 7, 2026, that a platform-operator model allows 5-10 accounts for each engineer. He separates that model from the engineer who builds new product for one customer. It is his statement, not an employer’s.

Attrition and burnout. No employer has published attrition or burnout figures for forward deployed engineers. One data company publishes how long engineers have been with their current employer. We do not use it: in a role where hiring multiplied in 2025 and 2026, short tenure mostly reflects recent hiring, not people leaving.

Time to fill. No employer and no job-data provider we read publishes how long these roles take to fill or how long the ads stay open. Lightcast’s post of September 15, 2026, gives posting counts only. Some ads have been up a long time: Scale AI’s public-sector ad was first published on October 9, 2024, and updated on November 18, 2026. That may be one evergreen posting filled many times, so it is not evidence of a vacancy that stayed open.

What employers do publish is the load. PagerDuty’s Doug McClure writes of the work: “Not everyone can. Most people don’t want to.” Travel is stated in many ads, and it is the firmest fact you can plan around. Our pay by employer briefing lists the travel each ad states next to its pay.

The comments on scarcity that circulate come mostly from firms that sell hiring services, quoted in the trade press. We could not check them against employer data, so we leave them out. For counts of who is advertising, see who is hiring forward deployed engineers.

The first hire and the order of the next ones

This section is our advice. It is drawn from the structures above, and no employer publishes a hiring sequence.

First, decide which job you mean. One title covers a builder who writes production code and a pre-sales role that writes little. Our comparison of the forward deployed engineer and the solutions engineer sets out the difference. Sial’s view is that most companies should not hire the product-builder kind at all. Test your plan against that before you write an ad.

Then hire in this order.

  • An engineer who can run a deployment alone. The first person has no pod. Monte Carlo’s ad says its early hire will help write the playbook, “not inherit one”. PostHog’s first hire was pointed at building automation for large migrations. Hire someone who has shipped production code and has stood in front of a customer.
  • The customer-side partner. Salesforce, Palantir and Databricks each pair engineers with a deployment strategist. Once one engineer is spending more time scoping than building, add this person.
  • A second engineer. This gives you the pod Salesforce describes: one strategist and two engineers on one customer at a time.
  • A lead who mentors. Giga’s Senior / Staff ad asks the engineer to mentor others and for “4+ years of total managerial experience”. Deloitte’s ad expects mentoring from inside the pod. Promote or hire a lead before you start a second pod.
  • Onboarding and a route back to product. Salesforce built a six-week onboarding program it calls “Ready in Six”. PagerDuty onboards its engineers into each product area by having them learn a map of it first. Settle the reporting line and the way customer work reaches the roadmap before the team grows past one pod.

Salesforce also reports that around 40% to 50% of movement into its team is internal (Salesforce newsroom, March 18, 2026). Look at your own engineers and architects before you look outside.

If your customers are federal agencies, clearance narrows who you can hire and how fast. Our briefing on cleared forward deployed engineer roles sets out what employers ask for.

For the ad and the interview, use the job description and scorecard in how to hire a forward deployed engineer. Our forward deployed engineer role page describes the role itself.

Permanent, contract or advice

Permanent. This is how employers hire the role. On November 20, 2026, we read the job feeds of 177 employers. No posting titled forward deployed on an Ashby or Lever feed carried a contract, temporary or part-time type. Greenhouse feeds have no such field, so Greenhouse employers were not tested.

Contract. Contract ads with this title exist, but not from the employers above. A search of Dice with its contract filter returned 84 results on November 20, 2026. We opened four. All were posted by third-party firms, on terms of 6 to 12 months, and none named the end client. No AI lab, software vendor or consulting firm we read advertises a contract forward deployed role directly. If a deployment cannot wait for a permanent search, engineers on contract through their own companies can carry it.

A partner’s team. Some vendors point customers to consulting firms. Salesforce launched a Forward Deployed Engineering Partner Network on April 15, 2026, with Accenture and Deloitte as launch partners and over 30 other firms. Our briefing on Palantir Foundry and AIP forward deployed roles shows how two of those firms build such teams.

Advice. If you have not decided the shape of the team, decide that first. A Kernavique Capability Blueprint is fixed-scope advisory that sets out the roles, the hiring sequence and the mix of contract and permanent people.

In practice

Write down the code you expect this team to ship and the customers it will serve. Pick the reporting line that matches: product or engineering if the work should change the product, a customer-facing department if it should not. Hire one engineer who can run a deployment alone, then the customer-side partner, then a second engineer. Give the team a named route back to the roadmap, and state the travel in the ad.

Do not plan around a published ratio, attrition figure or time to fill. None exists.

What we left out

  • An investor report with survey figures on variable pay and on how companies charge for this work. It states no sample, so its figures cannot be read as market facts.
  • Team sizes reported by third parties. Figures for ElevenLabs and for a firm OpenAI acquired appear in an investor report and a newsletter. Neither is confirmed on a company page.
  • A data company’s tenure figures. They do not measure attrition, and the company sells hiring data.
  • Scarcity quotes and headcount estimates from firms that sell hiring services. We could not check them against employer data.
  • Statements by Palantir executives about other companies’ teams. We read them only as reported by a trade publication. The earnings call and shareholder letter were not opened.
  • OpenAI’s own account of its team. Its pages could not be read.
  • Hourly figures in contract ads. They are set by third-party firms, not by the employers who use the engineers.
  • Onboarding detail beyond Salesforce and PagerDuty. No other employer we read describes it.

Questions

  • How big is a forward deployed engineering pod?
  • Salesforce is the only employer we read that gives a number. Its blog of March 27, 2026, describes pods of one deployment strategist and two forward deployed engineers, each focused on one client for about three months. Palantir, ServiceNow, Databricks, Deloitte and Tennr also describe pods or pairings, without giving a size.
  • Who should a forward deployed engineering team report to?
  • Employers do not agree. PagerDuty proposed moving its team under Product Development, and leaders at ServiceNow and Workato place theirs in product or R&D (Insight Partners, July 21, 2026). Ads we read on November 20, 2026, also put the team in sales, customer success, professional services and a separate business unit. Choose the line that matches the code you expect the team to ship.
  • How many customers can one forward deployed engineer support?
  • No employer we read publishes a figure. The only number we found is from one former Palantir engineer, Kabir Sial, who wrote on CIO.com on August 7, 2026, that a platform-operator model allows 5-10 accounts for each engineer. That is one practitioner’s statement, not an employer’s, and it does not apply to engineers who build new product for a single customer.
  • Who should be the first hire on a forward deployed team?
  • Our advice is an engineer who can own a deployment from discovery to production alone, because the first person writes the playbook. Monte Carlo’s ad, published November 6, 2026, describes its role as an early hire into a new function. Add the customer-side partner second and a second engineer third, which gives you the pod Salesforce describes.
  • Is there data on attrition or time to fill for these roles?
  • No. We found no employer that publishes attrition, burnout or time-to-fill figures for forward deployed engineers, and no job-data provider we read publishes how long these ads stay open. Some ads have been up for more than a year, but they may be evergreen postings that are filled again and again.
  • Should the first engineers be permanent or on contract?
  • Employers hire this role as permanent staff. On November 20, 2026, across the job feeds of 177 employers, no posting titled forward deployed on an Ashby or Lever feed carried a contract, temporary or part-time type; Greenhouse feeds have no such field and were not tested. Engineers on contract through their own companies can carry a first deployment while a permanent search runs.
