WorkBuddy's real business opportunity is not selling software, but doing FDE.

Original title: WorkBuddy真正的商业机会，不是卖软件，而是做FDE

53AI · 2026-07-29 · 4 min read

Summary

The article argues that WorkBuddy's real business opportunity lies not in selling software, training, or prompt courses, but in FDE services. Buying AI does not mean using it: companies lack someone who enters the front lines, identifies the most costly or error-prone processes, and turns the tool into measurable results. The author distinguishes FDEs from sales, implementation, and training roles, lists four core competencies (business diagnosis, workflow design, value translation, organizational enablement), and proposes scaling from individual FDEs to an organizational service chain of diagnosis, co-creation, deployment support, and ongoing operations.

Key points

  • Buying AI is not the same as using AI; the missing role is the FDE.
  • FDEs deliver a continuously running business result, not courses or prompts.
  • Qualified FDEs need diagnosis, workflow design, value translation, and enablement skills.
  • Land one high-frequency, low-risk workflow first, then replicate it.
  • Advanced FDEs discuss growth, cost, cash flow, and risk with the boss.

But I'm increasingly convinced that WorkBuddy's real, bigger business opportunity lies not in the tool itself, but in the FDE.

Because enterprises never lack software with richer features. What bosses truly lack is someone who can step into the front lines of the business, identify the processes most worth improving, and turn WorkBuddy into visible efficiency, cost savings, and results.

That is the value of the FDE.

Buying AI doesn't mean the enterprise is actually using AI

Imagine this scenario.

A company with dozens of employees introduces WorkBuddy. Sales finishes the feature demo, implementation completes the configuration, and employees attend training. A month later, only a handful of people are actually using it consistently.

Sales is still digging through old proposals, contracts are still circulating in WeChat groups, and operations is still manually consolidating spreadsheets. The tool has entered the company, but the business processes haven't changed.

Software vendors can provide product capabilities, but it's hard for them to deeply understand each customer's clients, organization, and processes over the long term. People inside the company understand the business, but may not know how to translate problems into AI workflows.

The missing person in the middle is the FDE.

An FDE is not a senior salesperson, nor a software instructor

FDE usually stands for Forward Deployed Engineer. In this article, I interpret it as "frontline deployment consultant," emphasizing not just writing code, but working close to the customer's site to complete AI deployment and business implementation.

This is not a new term invented to package services. In May 2026, OpenAI announced the creation of a Deployment Company, explicitly stating that FDEs would enter organizations to work with business owners and frontline teams to identify high-value scenarios, restructure key workflows, and turn short-term gains into long-term systems. OpenAI's latest positioning of the FDE

In the WorkBuddy context, an FDE differs from at least three types of roles.

Sales answers "what features are there," while the FDE answers "which scenario to change first and what metrics it affects"; implementation staff handle accounts, permissions, and connections, while the FDE also asks where the process is slow, who is doing repetitive work, and which steps are error-prone; ordinary training aims for employees to understand, while the FDE aims for employees to actually use it and continue using it independently.

So what an FDE delivers is not a course or a set of prompts, but a business result that keeps running.

Why WorkBuddy is well suited as the FDE's implementation vehicle

WorkBuddy can plan and execute multi-step tasks, handle documents, spreadsheets, PPTs, and local files, and can also enter the enterprise's existing work environment through Skills, connectors, automation, and team collaboration.

Therefore, the FDE doesn't need to develop heavy systems from scratch. You can first organize the materials, historical proposals, and approval rules needed for sales quotes, and have WorkBuddy generate quote drafts; once manual verification confirms stability, connect documents, meetings, email, or project data, and gradually add automation.

First get one high-frequency, low-risk, easy-to-verify process running, then replicate it across more departments.

The truly effective implementation sequence is not to buy a pile of features first, but to get one result first.

A qualified FDE needs four core capabilities

First, business diagnosis. Don't start by asking "what do you want to use AI for," but rather "what are the company's most expensive, slowest, and most error-prone processes."

Second, workflow design. Break vague requirements into inputs, rules, execution, review, and outputs, clarifying which steps go to WorkBuddy and which must be confirmed by humans.

Third, translating business value. Customers don't pay for the number of prompts. The FDE must translate tool capabilities into quote turnaround time, material reuse, risk control, and management efficiency.

Fourth, organizational enablement. Through training, coaching, templates, and retrospectives, enable the customer's team to use it independently and keep improving.

This is also why people suited to be FDEs are often not those who only understand technology. Ideally, they have ToB enterprise services experience, understand the business models of small and medium-sized enterprises, are familiar with WorkBuddy, and can also do consulting, teaching, and on-site coaching.

The FDE's advanced skill is being able to talk business with the boss

Being able to configure WorkBuddy is only the entry-level skill of an FDE.

The higher-level requirement is being able to communicate with the boss and understand the needs the boss doesn't say outright.

When the boss says "employees don't know how to use AI," the underlying issue may be workforce efficiency; saying "we want to build a knowledge base" may reflect worry about losing institutional knowledge; saying "sales materials are a mess" may mean long deal cycles and slow onboarding of new hires.

The FDE cannot stop at answering questions at the feature level, but must return to the fundamental business needs of the enterprise: growth, cost, cash flow, risk, and organizational efficiency.

When communicating with the boss, don't just show what WorkBuddy can generate; instead, nail down three questions: which metric is most worth changing, who is accountable for the result, and how long until the first acceptance milestone.

Only when an FDE can translate AI language into the boss's business language have they truly reached the decision-making table.

From individual FDE to organizational FDE

To build a bigger business, the key is not for one person to serve more customers, but to turn individual capability into organizational capability.

Distill industry experience into scenario checklists, diagnostic questionnaires, and delivery SOPs; distill validated WorkBuddy workflows into templates, Skills, and acceptance criteria; then train a group of FDEs who can go on-site, talk business with bosses, and lead teams through implementation.

At the same time, establish a frontline feedback mechanism so that one customer's experience becomes a solution for the entire organization.

This way, the business model is no longer just selling software once or delivering a course, but can form a service chain of "business diagnosis, scenario co-creation, deployment coaching, and ongoing operations."

A few words from the heart

WorkBuddy lowers the technical barrier for enterprises to use AI, but it won't automatically remove the business and organizational barriers.

This is exactly the space where the FDE exists.

Selling software solves whether the customer has a tool.

Being an FDE solves whether the customer can get results.

An individual FDE earns money from project delivery; an organizational FDE builds replicable industry capability.

The stronger AI tools become, the more valuable the people who understand the business, understand the boss, and understand implementation.
