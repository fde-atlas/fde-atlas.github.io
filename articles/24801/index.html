How to Build an FDE Portfolio and Track Record | Presentation Styles That Impress in Hiring

Original title: FDEのポートフォリオ・実績の作り方｜採用で評価される見せ方

FDE Jobs · 2026-06-21 · 3 min read

Summary

This Japanese-language article from FDE Jobs explains how to build a portfolio and track record for Forward Deployed Engineer hiring. It argues that demonstrated experience solving customer problems hands-on and getting solutions adopted in the field matters more than skill lists, and recommends structuring each achievement around four elements: the problem, your role, your approach, and quantified results. Candidates without direct FDE experience can present adjacent experience—production deployments at work, personal LLM/RAG projects, or customer-facing episodes. For confidential work, hide proper nouns and abstract numbers (e.g., 'cut workload ~30% at a manufacturer'). The core advice: prepare one or two achievements you can discuss in depth, and honestly delineate your own contribution—exaggeration undermines trust.

Key points

  • Structure each achievement as problem, role, approach, quantified result.
  • Quantified outcomes and honest scoping of your contribution build trust.
  • Adjacent experience—deployments, LLM/RAG side projects, customer work—counts.
  • Handle confidential info by hiding names and abstracting numbers.
  • Prioritize one or two achievements you can discuss deeply in interviews.

Why Portfolios Work in FDE Hiring

In FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) hiring, achievements showing "you solved a customer's problem hands-on, it was actually used in the field, and it produced results" are evaluated far more decisively than certifications or a list of skills. That's because FDEs don't just build things—they see them through to full operation. A portfolio (collection of achievements) is your most effective weapon for conveying this "saw-it-through" experience to recruiters and interviewers in the shortest time.

Structure of Achievements That Get Evaluated: Write with the Four Elements

Structure each achievement around the following four elements to make it land.

ElementWhat to Write
ProblemThe business challenge the customer/field was facing (why it mattered)
Your RoleWhich parts of requirements definition/implementation/integration/adoption you handled, and to what extent
ApproachTechnology choices and design decisions, and the reasoning behind them (LLM/RAG, integration methods, etc.)
ResultsExpressed quantitatively (workload reduction, revenue, usage rate, retention)

In particular, "whether you can express results quantitatively" and "whether you can honestly delineate your scope of responsibility" determine your credibility.

What Achievements to Include

Even without direct FDE experience, adjacent experience framed in an "FDE context" will be fully evaluated.

Deployment and Production Operation Experience at Work

Even internal tools count as strong achievements if they were used in the field and produced results. Be mindful of confidentiality and express numbers as percentages or orders of magnitude.

Personal Projects and OSS (Especially LLM/RAG)

If you lack professional generative AI experience, demonstrating LLM API usage, RAG construction, and evaluation design through personal projects can compensate and show you can contribute immediately. A working product plus a record of your design decisions is an effective combination.

Episodes of Customer Engagement and Problem Solving

Experience from consulting, SE, SIer, or CS roles where you "translated vague requests into requirements and mobilized stakeholders" serves as proof of the customer-facing strength FDEs need.

Presentation Tips and Cautions

Prepare to speak in depth about 1–2 representative achievements, and be ready in interviews to discuss conversationally "why you chose that design" and "what happened as a result." For confidential information, the golden rule is to omit proper nouns and abstract the numbers. Not exaggerating and honestly showing your scope of responsibility actually builds trust. For the full picture of preparation, also see How to Become an FDE with No Experience and FDE Interview Questions.
