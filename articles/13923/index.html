Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)

Original title: 前置部署工程师：Forward Deployed Engineer（FDE）

FDE中国 · 2026-08-14 · 2 min read

Summary

This card from FDE China clarifies what people actually mean when they say FDE. It defines the role by three criteria: being deployed to the customer's site, working directly in real production environments, and having one person own both technical implementation and delivery end to end. The most common misuse, the author argues, is equating FDEs with on-site outsourced programmers: contractors answer for hours and feature lists, while FDEs answer for business outcomes and hold judgment over what to build—a judgment backed by contract structure, acceptance criteria, and product feedback loops. The card also distinguishes FDEs from pre-sales engineers (who stop at signing) and implementation consultants (who configure standard products rather than code). For the Chinese market, privatized deployment and data-sovereignty requirements make on-site roles look similar to FDEs, but closed-loop accountability and feeding field needs back into the product are the reliable tests. The author suggests applying these criteria to job descriptions and interviews.

Key points

  • FDE definition rests on three criteria: customer-site deployment, real production environments, and one-person delivery closure.
  • FDEs differ from outsourced contractors by owning business outcomes and holding scenario judgment, not just hours and feature lists.
  • Unlike pre-sales engineers, FDEs stay after the contract; unlike implementation consultants, they code beyond standard product configuration.
  • In China, privatized deployment makes on-site roles resemble FDEs, but accountability closure and product feedback are the real dividing lines.
  • Practical advice: use the accountability-closure test when writing or evaluating FDE job descriptions and interview questions.

Full text

This card addresses a high-frequency question: when people say FDE, what exactly are they talking about. Core breakdown: the definition hinges on three points—being dispatched into customer scenarios, working directly against real production environments, and having the same person own both technical implementation and delivery end-to-end. The most common misuse is equating FDE with on-site outsourced programmers: outsourced developers are accountable for hours and feature lists, while an FDE is accountable for business outcomes and holds the judgment to decide what to do and what not to do in a given scenario. That judgment isn't granted by a job title—it's supported jointly by contract structure, acceptance criteria, and the product feedback loop. Incremental insight: most articles introducing FDE stay at the level of Silicon Valley stories; this card adds the domestic context—on-premises deployment, data that can't leave the domain, and strong customization demands—making FDE highly similar in appearance to existing on-site roles in China. But the closed responsibility loop and product feedback are the real dividing line, and these two criteria are more reliable than any title. Action advice: use "closed responsibility loop" as your yardstick to check whether the FDE in your company's JD is a real FDE, and use the same criteria to probe the other side in interviews. If you're hiring, write these two points directly into the first paragraph of the JD. — FDEChina Editorial Team · Practitioner's Perspective

Definition

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a frontline engineering role dispatched to the customer's site, or with the customer's business as its main battlefield, writing code and delivering systems directly against real production environments. Its hallmark is that the closed loop of technical implementation and business delivery is completed by the same person.

Details

This role entered public view through Palantir's organizational practice, and was later made a core position by AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic: engineers are deployed directly at the customer's business site, responsible for the entire process from research, prototyping, and pilot to production launch and handover. The dividing line between an FDE and a Sales Engineer lies in where they go after the contract is signed—the Sales Engineer stops at winning the contract, while the FDE stays on site to build the system until it works. The dividing line from an implementation consultant lies in the means—implementation consultants mainly do configuration within a standard product, while FDEs use code and evals to solve long-tail needs the product doesn't cover.

A common misuse is to understand FDE as "an outsourced programmer stationed on site." The difference lies not in physical location but in the structure of responsibility: outsourced developers are accountable for hours and feature lists, while an FDE is accountable for business outcomes and holds the judgment to make trade-offs in the scenario. In the domestic context, on-premises deployment and customization demands make on-site delivery highly similar in appearance to FDE. To judge whether a position is truly an FDE, check whether it closes the loop on responsibility and whether it feeds field requirements back into the product.
