A survey of the official websites of 40 domestic companies calling themselves FDEs: in reality, their names alone don't tell you what you can ask them to do

Original title: FDEを名乗る国内40社の公式サイトを調査、名前だけでは何を頼めるか分からない実態

ゴムマリ株式会社 · 2026-11-02 · 18 min read

Summary

Gomumari, a Japanese company, examined the websites of 40 domestic firms marketing services under the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) label. Of the 33 firms with readable service pages, 13 either avoid calling their staff engineers or do not state that the provider does the development in their main plans. Only 5 firms disclose ownership or handover of deliverables, and only 14 mention minimum contract terms or phase-based breakpoints. Drawing on IPA and METI contracting guidelines plus Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic descriptions, the author defines 10 items buyers should verify before requesting quotes, and recommends confirming them in writing—especially completion criteria, phased exit options, and deliverable rights paired with post-handover maintainability. Monthly prices, where listed, ranged from 200,000 to 600,000 yen.

Key points

  • Survey of 40 Japanese firms using the FDE label; 33 had readable service pages.
  • 13 of 33 don't call staff engineers or don't state the provider builds anything.
  • Only 5 firms disclose deliverable rights; 14 state contract terms or breakpoints.
  • 10 pre-contract checklist items derived from IPA/METI guidelines and Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic.
  • Author urges written confirmation of completion criteria, exit options, and IP ownership.

We surveyed 40 domestic companies that sell AI adoption support under the name FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) and whose official website descriptions we were able to read.

Of the 33 companies whose FDE service pages we could read, 13 either do not call their practitioners engineers or do not state that the provider does the development in their main plans. Because services calling themselves FDE don't make clear from the name alone what you can request, we organized 10 items to verify before engaging, based on the IPA and METI contracting guidelines and the descriptions of Palantir, the origin of FDE, and OpenAI and Anthropic, which post FDE job listings, and counted the companies stating each item on their official websites. Only 5 companies state the rights to or handover of the deliverables, and only 14 state contract-period restrictions or breakpoints.

Below, we present the content of the descriptions of the 40 domestic companies, the number of companies for each of the 10 items, the criteria for judgment, and how we investigated.

Even among the 40 domestic companies calling themselves FDE, the name alone doesn't reveal the substance

FDE is the name of a way of working in which the provider's engineers embed within the customer, build, and deliver all the way to production operations (the embedding within the customer is based on the description of Palantir, the origin of FDE; the building and delivering to production is based on the FDE job listings of OpenAI and Anthropic. A detailed explanation of this way of working is summarized in What is an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer)?).

Of the 40 domestic companies calling themselves FDE, 33 had pages describing their FDE services that we could read, while for the remaining 7 we could only confirm the term FDE in announcements or press releases. Of the 33, 8 do not call their practitioners engineers, instead calling them consultants or specialists. 5 do not state that the provider does the development in their main plans; the breakdown is 2 companies offering accompaniment or advice supporting the customer's employees in building, 1 company configuring off-the-shelf tools, and 2 companies implementing their own products. 13 companies fell into one of these two categories.

Configuring their own products for each customer is also the model of Palantir, the origin of FDE. The 2 accompaniment companies describe building as a separate plan or separate estimate.

Across all 40 companies, of the 28 that describe the relationship between the person who hears about the work and the person who builds, 20 state that the same person both hears and builds, and 8 state that the listening role and the building role are split between different people or different teams.

Of the 37 companies that state they embed on-site, 10 state full-time presence or at least one day per week (regardless of whether it is a physical visit), while 27 state only a few visits per month or do not state a frequency. 12 of the 40 companies list pricing on their official websites (1 company could not be verified). For the 8 companies whose monthly pricing we could read, the monthly fee of each company's lowest plan ranged from 200,000 to 600,000 yen (these are the lower bounds each company presents, not market rates or actual transaction amounts).

Only 3 companies state that the rights to the deliverables belong to the client or remain as the client's assets, and even including companies that state they hand over the deliverables, there were 5. Criteria for judgment

If a company's description contained the following, we counted that item as "described." When in doubt, we counted it as the side that did not describe it, or the side further from the original form of the FDE (the provider's engineers embed themselves in the customer's organization and build).

  • 1. What is built The main plan states that the provider develops and implements software, AI agents, business systems, API integrations, or data infrastructure. We did not count cases limited to configuring off-the-shelf tools, assembling workflows, light automation, or prompt maintenance; implementing the provider's own products (including customer-specific agents built on top of the provider's own products); or advisory, training, or accompaniment only (where the customer's own employees do the building).
  • 2. Who hears about the work and who builds it The description uses a person-oriented term such as "FDE," "engineer," or "consultant" as the subject and states that the same person both hears about the work and builds it, or states that the listening role and the building role are split between different people or teams (if both appear in the same description, we counted it as split). We did not count descriptions that only say, with the company as the subject, "end-to-end from requirements definition to development." Whether the doer is called an engineer or a consultant/expert was read separately as a subcategory of this item.
  • 3. How many days per week and where they embed The description states that they are resident, at least one day per week, or work as part of the customer's team (physical on-site presence is not required). We did not count a few visits per month or vague "embedding" with no frequency stated.
  • 4. What the client provides The description states what the client must provide, such as the time of the people in charge of the work, decision-makers, or those who hold the correct answers for the work; preparation of data, permissions, and environments; or internal points of contact. We did not count phrases like "working hand in hand" alone.
  • 5. Team structure The description states the number of people assigned to this support or the combination of roles. We did not count only the company's total headcount.
  • 6. How results are measured The description states that before or during the support, indicators of impact such as time saved, KPIs, or cost-effectiveness are set, measured, or reported. We did not count only result figures from case studies.
  • 7. Handling of data The description addresses handling of customer data or confidential information, such as confidentiality, personal information, access permissions, building within the customer's environment, not using data for training, or information security certifications. We did not count only a link to the company's general privacy policy.
  • 8. Minimum period or checkpoints The description states a minimum contract period or a floor on the contract term, or that continuation can be decided in stages, such as deciding whether to proceed based on PoC results, contracting per phase, reviewing monthly, or being able to cancel at any time. We did not count only rough guidance on the duration of the process.
  • 9. Rights to and handover of what is built The description states that rights or copyright to what is built (source code, AI created, configurations, etc.) belong to the client, that it remains as a client asset, or that what is built is delivered or handed over to the client. We did not count descriptions that use the word "deliverables" without touching on rights or handover. Descriptions stating that only procedure manuals are handed over were counted under item 10, handover.
  • 10. Handover and operation/maintenance Handover: the description states, as the provider's job, enabling the client's employees to run things themselves, such as internalization, self-sufficiency, transfer, or providing procedure or design documents (we did not count general training on how to use AI or the word "accompaniment" alone). Operation/maintenance: the description states that the provider handles operation, maintenance, improvement, or incident response even after the build (we did not count only internal operation rules or organizational setup).

Verify substance, not names, in writing before the quote (our view)

Because the name FDE alone does not tell you what you can request, we recommend verifying the 10 items above in writing before receiving a quote. Not stating something on the official website does not mean the company has not decided it. Items not described must be asked about and confirmed by the client at the quoting stage. Among them, the following three, in AI development where what to build is hard to pin down, later affect cost and effort.

First: decide numerically what counts as done, down to the content and level of the results

  • Basis: METI's "Contract Checklist for AI Use and Development" states that rather than abstractly debating whether a development contract is a quasi-delegation or a contract for work, in many cases the key issue is how much the client demands regarding the content and level of the results.
  • Our case: In a project classifying about 700 invoice line items by expense category, we set completion criteria numerically before starting (2% or less overall, 1% or less amount-weighted), verified by actual measurement that they were met, and then delivered. In validation of AI features for a legal-domain SaaS company, the ground truth used for evaluation was revised under expert supervision, and the accuracy figures also moved. The sets of questions and ground truth used for evaluation (eval sets) are managed by version, and every item is re-measured whenever the ground truth changes.
  • What to ask at the quote: the completion criteria, and who holds the ground truth for those criteria.

Second: structure it so that whether to continue or stop can be decided at each stage

  • Basis: METI's AI volume divides AI development involving building trained models into stages—feasibility assessment, PoC, development, and additional training—and presents an approach that allows choosing not to proceed to the next stage if building proves infeasible.
  • Our case: In a project automating monthly comparison materials, the original aim was to automatically identify the causes of increases and decreases, but actual measurement showed that 17 of the 39 issues raised by humans had no corresponding column in the data, and we changed where the value of what we built would land midway. What to build is sometimes not decided until you try building it.
  • What to ask at the quote: 8 companies state a minimum contract period and 7 state stage-based or monthly checkpoints, so ask which form applies and what remains in your hands if you stop.

Third: decide as a set the rights to what is built and whether the client can fix it after you leave

  • Basis: The IPA/METI model contract templates let you choose from three options for who holds copyright to what is built, decided per contract. The commentary on the same model contracts requires the owner of the system—the client—to exercise appropriate management, such as smooth handover of business and design know-how, during the maintenance process.
  • Our case: In a project delivering a multilingual manufacturing-manual tool, we left a structure that let the field operate on its own even as machine models increased, so that we did not need to step in each time. In the monthly comparison materials project as well, the completion condition included not just that the values matched, but that the customer's side could reproduce the same output in their own environment.
  • A cautionary example: In a project where ownership of where the built system resides was transferred to the client midway, a review of the handover documents revealed settings that only the owner could change, such as the upper limit of the usage quota, leaving the provider unable to change them even if asked.
  • Questions to ask in a quote: who owns what is built, and who can fix what after the work ends. These are two separate questions, so both need to be put in writing.

Methodology and limitations

ItemContent
Survey periodOctober 31, 2026 (the date on which companies were gathered via lists, articles, announcements, and search, each company's official website was read, and judgments and tabulation were performed)
Surveying organizationIn-house survey (Gomumari Co., Ltd.)
Survey subjectsArticles comparing and listing FDEs, announcements on PR TIMES, and 79 domestic companies that came up in web searches as companies calling themselves FDEs, plus companies found under terms such as AI accompaniment support; from these, 43 companies (excluding ourselves) whose official websites we confirmed use the term FDE to describe their services. Of these, 40 companies whose descriptions we could read were counted
Number of valid responsesNot applicable, since the survey subjects are not people (companies read: 40; pages selected: 77)
Survey methodItems that the commissioning party should verify before signing a contract were consolidated into 14 items by reference to IPA and METI's "Information System Model Transactions and Contracts (Custom Development (Including Some Planning), Maintenance and Operation) <Second Edition>", METI's "Contract Guidelines on AI and Data Utilization, AI Edition" (June 2018) and "Contract Checklist for AI Utilization and Development" (February 2025), and the FDE descriptions of OpenAI, Anthropic, and Palantir (embedding into the customer, building, and delivering to production / configuring their own product per customer). Of these, the naming of the practitioner was included in the second item, and operation/maintenance and handover were consolidated into the tenth item; whether the arrangement is contract-for-work or quasi-mandate and whether delivery reaches production were not used as items, resulting in 10 items. From each company's official website, we selected and read 1 to 5 pages per company (77 pages in total) consisting of the page for the service marketed as FDE and pages on the same site explaining that service (pricing, process, case studies, FAQ) (for companies whose service page could not be found or was not searched for, we read their own news/announcement pages where the term FDE appeared), and judged for each item whether it was stated in the description, using AI (large language models) in two separate invocations. When in doubt, we applied a rule of judging toward the side that did not state it or the side farther from the original form. The three items of what is built, who does the work, and the field were read from the description of the service's primary plan. The criteria for judging what is built and the field were tested and revised using 10 companies randomly selected from the 43 before final judgment. Agreement between the two judgments (kappa coefficient) was 0.813–1.000 for the 12 items used in the counts; for the 22 discrepancies and 14 cases where both judgments agreed but did not match the judgment criteria, we decided by reading the text ourselves. For one item (who asks about the work and who builds it), the first-round agreement was 0.743, so we revised the criteria wording and re-judged twice. Pricing was handled separately from the judgments: we automatically crawled each company's official website from the top to find price figures, and for companies with prices, we verified the price figures on official pages ourselves. Whether prices can be read as monthly amounts and each company's monthly lower bound were determined by us from each company's stated figures

This counts what is written in the descriptions on official websites; it does not examine how each company actually works or contracts. The absence of something in a description does not mean the company has not decided on it or does not do it.

What we read were the description pages of services marketed as FDE (16 companies had one page); we did not read terms-of-use pages, so companies that state ownership of what is built or contract periods only in terms of use, quotes, or contracts are counted on the "not stated" side. Of the 43 companies, 7 mention the term FDE only in their own news/announcements, and since we read those texts, their descriptions are short and they may be counted on the "not stated" side. For long descriptions we read up to 15,000 characters per company, and for 5 companies we did not read some or all of the later pages (2 of the 77 selected pages could not be read).

The 43 companies are those found via lists, articles, announcements, and searches whose self-identification as FDEs could be confirmed on their official websites; they are not all domestic companies marketing themselves as FDEs. 3 companies load their text after the page opens and could not be read, so they were not counted.

Whether the provider carries what is built through to production operations was judged by the same method, but because the two judgments were unstable, it was not used in the counts.

Prices are as stated by each company (tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive are not standardized) and include the lower bounds of "from ~" figures. They are not market rates or actual transaction amounts.

We also offer a service marketed as FDE (AI Implementation FDE). Our own service is not included in the survey. Our projects cited in the observations are published case studies and unpublished projects anonymized so customers cannot be identified.
