Zip FDE Job Requirements: How to Choose Between AI FDE and SWE

Original title: Zip FDE 岗位要求：AI FDE 与 SWE 怎么选

FDE Times · 2026-10-23 · 5 min read

Summary

The article compares two FDE roles Zip is hiring for. The AI Forward Deployed Engineer role prioritizes domain expertise: at least 3 years in procurement or finance operations, with only modest technical requirements (integrations, API docs, workflow debugging), since agents are built on a no-code platform and quality depends on the person writing instructions. The Forward Deployed Software Engineer role is the opposite: full-stack coding, shipping complex production systems, and diagnosing issues live in customer meetings, with a listed salary of $150,000–$250,000. Both roles share one trait: owning projects end to end and pushing back on vague requirements. The author advises candidates to pick the right track and tailor separate resumes, adding concrete preparation tips for each.

Key points

  • AI FDE role screens for domain knowledge first: 3+ years in procurement or finance ops.
  • SWE FDE role screens for engineering: complex production systems shipped, full-stack skills.
  • AI role's technical bar is low: integrations, API docs, workflow debugging.
  • Both roles demand end-to-end ownership and challenging vague requirements.
  • Advice: choose the fitting track and write separate tailored resumes.

Key Takeaways

  • Zip's AI Forward Deployed Engineer role requires at least 3 years of experience in procurement, finance operations, or a related field. On the technical side, candidates only need to be able to handle integrations, read API documentation, and troubleshoot workflows.
  • The Software Engineer, Forward Deployed role is the opposite: it requires having shipped complex production systems, being able to read and write full-stack code, and personally attending customer meetings.
  • The AI role is looking for someone who can own a project end to end, from scoping requirements to delivery; the SWE role is looking for someone willing to push back on vague requirements. Read together, both postings are looking for people who take ownership.

Zip is hiring an AI Forward Deployed Engineer, and the core requirement for this role has nothing to do with code: candidates need at least 3 years working in procurement, finance operations, or a similar function at large companies.

The Software Engineer, Forward Deployed role at the same company, by contrast, has an entirely engineering-focused core requirement: you must have actually shipped genuinely complex production systems. Both roles carry the "Forward Deployed" label, but one screens for domain knowledge first and the other for technical ability first.

If your target is Zip, or any enterprise SaaS company that hires FDEs along these two tracks, the first thing to do is figure out which door fits you. Both roles want you to bring something that is hard to teach—just a different thing in each case.

Zip makes procurement software, meaning systems that manage how enterprises purchase and spend money. Zip's FDEs need to win the trust of AP directors (AP stands for accounts payable, the team responsible for paying vendors), Chief Procurement Officers (CPOs), and Chief Financial Officers (CFOs). It is precisely this group that determines what kind of FDE Zip needs.

Why does the AI role value domain experts over coding wizards?

The answer lies in what the FDE in this role actually builds. According to VentureBeat, Zip builds agents on a no-code platform that enterprise customers can customize to their own needs. Zip describes each agent as close to a junior employee: very good at executing specific instructions.

These two details show where the real difficulty of the job lies. Since the platform requires no coding and the agents strictly follow instructions, quality depends on the person writing the instructions. That person must understand what the real process looks like, where exceptions occur, and which steps require human approval.

Imagine an AP director complaining that a certain batch of supplier invoices always gets stuck in the approval stage. An engineer who has never worked on a finance team would go dig through logs.

Someone who has actually processed invoices would first ask: which line items on these invoices don't match the purchase orders? And under company policy, who has the authority to release such discrepancies?

The second kind of question explains why the posting requires candidates to have enough domain knowledge to quickly win the trust of an AP director, CPO, or CFO. Buyers at this level rarely have time to give the person sent to help them a crash course in the industry.

So Zip sets the technical bar at handling integrations, reading API documentation, and figuring out where a workflow breaks—not at designing distributed systems.

There is a detail in the posting that is easy to skim past: candidates must have built agents or automated workflows for their own work. This wording signals that what's being assessed is hands-on evidence: you spotted a repetitive task in your own daily work and automated it.

Professional certifications, by contrast, are explicitly listed as optional. CPP, CPSM, CPA, or equivalent certifications are just a plus. Finance operations professionals don't need to go back and get certified; what really counts is years of hands-on work.

SWE role engineers also have to deal with customers

The second role will look more familiar to software engineers. According to Zip, the Forward Deployed Software Engineering team is responsible for building and delivering high-impact full-stack features that customers use directly. The technical requirement is having shipped complex production systems and being able to cover the entire stack.

The posting lists a base salary of $150,000 to $250,000. Numbers on sites that republish the posting may differ; before using them for comparison, defer to Zip's official careers page.

But if you read it as a backend role with a different name, you'd be missing the point. The posting is explicit: you will communicate directly with customers, attend meetings with procurement, finance, and IT teams, and diagnose problems while the meetings are in progress.

Diagnosing problems in real time during meetings is a skill many excellent engineers have never practiced. You have to voice your hypotheses, ask questions the customer can actually answer, and be clear about where you're still uncertain. If you're used to investigating alone and reporting back afterward, this is exactly what you need to practice.

The posting also asks for one more thing: product sense. The ideal candidate is described as someone who, on existing teams, frequently pushes back on requirements that aren't clearly defined. This detail shows Zip doesn't want someone who just takes tickets and works through them step by step.

What do the two roles have in common?

So what makes a finance professional and an engineer share the FDE title? One sentence in the AI FDE posting gives a fairly clear answer.

Candidates must have experience owning complex customer projects end to end, from scoping requirements to delivery. Put that sentence next to the SWE role's requirement to "push back on vague requirements," and the two postings are saying the same thing.

This is the common thread between the two roles, and the hardest thing to prove on a resume. It's easy to write that you know Python and understand the procure-to-pay process; the hard part is producing one experience: you received a vague requirement, narrowed the scope, and carried it all the way to something usable with no one watching over you.

Which role should you prepare for?

If you're a software engineer who has never worked on a finance team, the SWE role is closer to your strengths—not least because this posting is open to all levels.

Don't try to package yourself as a procurement expert: the 3-years-in-this-field requirement can't be made up by taking a course. Focus your energy on the two things the posting assesses: working directly with customers, and product sense.

The practice method is concrete. The next time your team receives a vague requirement, rewrite the scope in a few sentences, make clear what won't be done, and send it back to the requester. On a resume, a line like "narrowed a raw requirement into a single approval workflow and shipped the first version in X weeks" carries more weight than a list of frameworks.

If you work in finance operations, AP, or procurement and can write script-level code, the AI FDE role is practically written for you. What you need to add is evidence on the agents side.

Automate a real task from your daily work, and note where it broke and how you fixed it—because that is exactly the workflow troubleshooting ability the posting asks for.

If you're somewhere in between—say, an engineer who has worked on payment systems or internal ERPs—write a separate resume for each role; don't send the same generic resume to both. Sending the same resume to both makes it hard for the resume reader to see where you're strongest.

Zip's dual-role model shows that FDE is not a fixed profile. Companies hire people who happen to arrive with the hardest-to-teach part of the skill set already in hand; the teachable parts can be learned on the job.
