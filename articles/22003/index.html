What is an FDE in the Retail Industry? Explaining Implementation Examples and Key Points for Utilization

Original title: 小売業界のFDEとは？導入事例と活用のポイントを解説

デファクトリーNote｜事業開発/ソフトウェア/プロダクト開発ならDefactory株式会社 · 2026-09-03 · 17 min read

Summary

This Japanese article by Defactory explains how Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) could apply to the retail and distribution industry. An FDE, a role pioneered by Palantir, embeds in the client's site and takes end-to-end responsibility from problem discovery through AI agent implementation to adoption, unlike SES dispatch workers or IT consultants who stop at executing specs or proposals. The author identifies five application areas: demand forecasting, inventory optimization, shift scheduling, customer-service automation, and store strategy support. Examples include Fujitsu and Aeon Food Style building store-operations AI agent prototypes in about 10 days, and Pasona launching a team-based FDE consulting service in August 2026. Market data from BizDev Tech (September 2026) shows 44 retail projects but none labeled FDE, with average monthly rates of 732,000 yen, suggesting the field is nascent and early movers could gain an advantage by starting with small prototype validations using external talent.

Key points

  • FDEs take end-to-end responsibility for outcomes, unlike SES workers or IT consultants.
  • Fujitsu and Aeon Food Style built store AI agent prototypes in roughly 10 days.
  • Pasona launched a team-based FDE consulting service in August 2026.
  • BizDev Tech listed 44 retail projects in September 2026, none labeled FDE; average rate 732,000 yen/month.
  • Recommended approach: start with small prototype validations and use external FDE talent.

An FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer who works deep within the customer's frontline operations, using AI agents and data utilization to handle everything from discovering business challenges to implementation and adoption, end to end.

The FDE role was established by US-based Palantir, and since the start of 2026, major Japanese companies such as Fujitsu and Persol have begun entering this field one after another.

The retail and distribution industry is only now beginning to be mentioned as a target for implementation.

In the retail industry, an FDE is an engineer who works deep within store operations and EC/supply chain frontline teams, consistently handling everything from solving business challenges to frontline adoption through AI agents and data utilization.

Unlike simple outsourced system development or on-site dispatch work, the biggest characteristic is taking responsibility for results.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is a role established by US-based Palantir, meaning "an engineer deployed to the front lines."

For a detailed definition and the background of its origins, see What is an FDE? Implementation strategies for enterprises of the new AI-era role, Forward Deployed Engineer.

In this article, we apply this definition to the specific industry context of retail and distribution and explore it in depth.

The reason FDE is starting to attract attention in the retail and distribution industry is that proof-of-concept projects for AI agent utilization are being announced one after another.

In July 2026, Fujitsu announced that, in collaboration with Aeon Food Style, its FDEs and designers developed a prototype AI agent to support store operations in about 10 days, with a proof-of-concept experiment in actual stores to be conducted in July 2026 (details covered in Chapter 3).

In August of the same year, Persol Works Switching Consulting launched a new service called "AI Consulting" centered on a "team-based FDE" model, and major companies are beginning to support AI transformation across industries, including retail and distribution, using the FDE way of working.

In this way, while specialized media or FDE companies focused on the retail industry itself have yet to emerge, proof-of-concept efforts and service offerings by major companies are steadily progressing.

"Retail × FDE" is a theme truly in its dawn period, with the term itself only now beginning to take hold.

For details on the growth pace of the overall FDE market in Japan and abroad, see 2026 Edition: Complete Comparison of Japanese and US FDE Companies | Salaries, Roles, Hiring Requirements, and a Third Option.

The biggest difference between FDEs and SES (on-site dispatch) or IT consulting is that FDEs take responsibility for results, from implementation to frontline adoption, rather than just building or proposing according to requirements.

Below is a summary of the differences among the three, using retail frontline examples.

The work areas handled by FDEs in the retail industry can be broadly divided into five categories: demand forecasting, inventory optimization, shift scheduling, automation of customer service, and support for strategic planning of store operations.

All of these are areas that require an understanding of the business rules and data structures specific to the retail industry.

This work involves building demand forecasting models that combine POS data with external factors such as weather and seasonality, supporting the automation and improved accuracy of ordering operations.

Rather than simply building models, the FDE is expected to translate them into operations at a level where store staff can actually use them for ordering decisions.

This involves analyzing inventory data across multiple stores in a cross-sectional manner and optimizing it to prevent stockouts and excess inventory.

For planograms (product display arrangements), initiatives have also begun in which AI agents propose layout plans based on purchase data.

This work involves automating and optimizing store staffing and shift scheduling based on visitor forecast data.

It is attracting attention as an area where AI agents can directly address the burdensome task of "complex shift adjustments" that store managers face.

This is the automation of customer service through chatbots and AI agents.

It goes beyond simple FAQ responses to include recommendations based on purchase history and building systems that assist store staff with customer service tasks.

This is an area that supports strategic planning for store operations itself by analyzing sales, inventory, and customer data in a cross-sectional manner.

The Fujitsu × Aeon Food Style case study introduced in Chapter 3 falls precisely into this category.

In the retail and distribution industries, major companies have already begun proof-of-concept projects and service offerings leveraging FDEs.

Here, we introduce two representative developments.

According to Fujitsu's announcement (July 13, 2026), Fujitsu assigned FDEs and designers to a joint project with Aeon Food Style and completed a prototype in a short period of about 10 days.

The subjects were four themes, all central to store operations. Fujitsu's FDEs and designers turned them into AI agents that collaborate with store managers in the areas of strategic planning, planogram layout, feasibility assessment of initiatives, and trade area analysis.

Of these, two—store strategy planning (analysis based on the 3C framework and support for formulating medium- to long-term strategies) and planogram layout (detailed plans and layout image generation based on display instructions and product information)—were announced to undergo proof-of-concept testing in actual stores within July 2026.

The process of involving FDEs from the upstream stages, shaping prototypes in a short period, and validating them in the field is precisely the FDE way of working: working backward from outcomes and taking responsibility through to implementation.

According to PERSOL WORKS SWITCH CONSULTING's announcement (dated September 1, 2026, with the service launch on August 26, 2026), the company launched a new service, "AI Consulting (Switch → AI-Native with AI Consulting)," centered on a "team-based FDE" model in which client companies and experts work as one.

The company positions this new service as a framework in which the same team consistently accompanies clients not only through the AI strategy planning stage, but also through actual business transformation, AI agent implementation, and subsequent adoption and internalization.

While this service is not limited to a specific industry, the fact that even consulting firms have begun to prominently promote "FDE" as a way of working, amid companies across a wide range of industries—including retail and distribution—considering AI transformation, shows that this role is more than just a buzzword.

FDE positions targeting the retail and distribution industries have also begun appearing in actual job listings.

In an FDE job posting for the food distribution industry, a role was advertised to drive business automation by leveraging data that spans from consumers to manufacturers and retailers, such as recipe viewing, purchase, and flyer viewing data.

The fact that the scope of data collection and utilization spans from manufacturers to stores is a characteristic unique to the retail and distribution industry, distinguishing it from system development for a single store. This is a useful case study when considering how far to extend the scope of work when hiring FDE talent in-house.

What is required of professionals functioning as FDEs in the retail industry is a combination of technical skills such as AI agent development and an understanding of retail-specific business operations.

When hiring or deploying FDE talent in-house, you need to determine whether candidates meet both of these requirements.

The foundation consists of implementation skills for generative AI and AI agents, and data analysis skills for handling large volumes of data such as POS data.

Rather than proficiency in a specific programming language, the ability to quickly turn prototypes into working implementations and iterate validation in the field is what matters most.

As a benchmark for evaluating candidates' technical skills, also refer to The Complete Guide to AI Agent Development | A Thorough Explanation of Mechanisms, Frameworks, and Going Independent as a Freelancer.

This is an understanding of retail-specific business systems such as POS registers, inventory management, ordering, and shift management.

Practical field knowledge—such as why inventory discrepancies occur and how ordering lead times affect operations—cannot be replaced by technical skills alone.

When selecting FDE talent in-house, judging candidates solely on the extent of their AI agent development experience risks misidentifying the talent you truly need.

This is because what matters most for an FDE is the ability to discern where the real problems lie in the field.

Even in the Fujitsu × Aeon Food Style case study introduced in Section 3-1, the subjects were business challenges that store managers face daily, such as store strategy planning and planogram layout.

The people best able to define such problems accurately are those who understand retail operations.

While implementation skills can be developed after deployment, the ability to discern field-level problems is difficult to cultivate without industry experience and hands-on field sense.

When selecting talent, we recommend adding not only technical skills but also retail industry experience and depth of field understanding to your evaluation criteria.

When adopting the FDE way of working in the retail industry, the first decision point is whether to develop talent in-house or leverage external talent.

Note that the perspective of in-house engineers considering a career move to FDE is covered in Transitioning to an FDE! Job Market Trends and Required Skills, so this chapter focuses on the perspective of corporate adoption and utilization.

If your company already has personnel who combine AI agent development implementation skills with practical knowledge of the retail industry, in-house development and assignment become a realistic option.

On the other hand, as seen in Chapter 6, FDE projects and talent specialized in the retail industry are still in an early stage and not widely available in the market. If no such talent exists in-house, training will take considerable time, so an approach that leverages external talent while accumulating knowledge is effective.

The biggest advantage of using external FDE talent is that you can get started without waiting for a training period.

As in the Fujitsu × Aeon Food Style case introduced in Chapter 3, you can secure talent capable of handling everything from upstream strategy planning to implementation and field verification in a short period, exactly when you need it.

Also, using external talent in parallel with in-house training provides an OJT opportunity for your own staff to learn FDE-style working methods through actual projects.

For FDE adoption, rather than suddenly launching a large-scale project, it is realistic to start with small-scale prototype validation focused on a specific business area (see Chapter 2).

The Fujitsu × Aeon Food Style case also began with prototype development in a short period of about 10 days. An approach that narrows down to a single business challenge, validates on a small scale, and gradually expands the scope of application is particularly effective in fields like the retail industry where FDE adoption is still in its early stages.

When considering the use of external talent, a lab-type support model, as introduced in What Is FDE Lab Development? The Full Picture of a "Solution-Building" Support Model Different from SES and Consulting, is also one option.

As of September 2026, FDE projects specialized in the retail industry are hardly available in the market at all.

As a reference when considering the use of external talent, let's look concretely at the current cost levels based on actual data searched on BizDev Tech, which our company operates.

When we searched for "retail" on our talent platform "BizDev Tech," 44 projects were found as of September 2026, of which 41 had published rates.

The average rate was 732,000 yen, the median was 720,000 yen, and the range was 360,000 to 1,500,000 yen (based on BizDev Tech data, as of September 2026). Please use this as a guideline for monthly costs when using external talent.

However, looking at the breakdown of these 44 projects, 0 explicitly mentioned "FDE."

The content was mainly traditional SIer contracted or quasi-delegated projects, such as core system maintenance and renewal, infrastructure operations and cloud migration, PMO, website production, and data analysis (using GA4 and KARTE).

Even in data analysis projects, which are closest to AI utilization, they were limited to data infrastructure development for an online supermarket business (870,000 yen) and data analysis support using the web customer engagement tool "KARTE" (750,000 yen); no projects were found that took an FDE-style approach, handling everything from problem discovery to implementation and adoption in an end-to-end manner (based on BizDev Tech data, as of September 2026).

In other words, "retail × FDE" is a field that does not yet even exist as a term in the talent market and is only now emerging. When searching for FDE talent for your company, it is realistic to focus not on the "FDE" title but on talent with AI agent development implementation experience and data analysis skills.

As seen in Chapters 1 and 3, major companies have already begun proof-of-concept efforts and service offerings, so FDE projects and talent specialized in the retail industry are expected to gradually appear in the market going forward.

FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is an engineer role that works deeply on-site with customers and handles everything end-to-end, from implementing AI agents to ensuring their adoption in the field. This working style was established by the US company Palantir, and details are explained in What Is FDE?.

The biggest difference is the scope of responsibility for outcomes. On-site client staffing mainly involves providing labor to complete assigned tasks, while FDE commits to outcomes from problem discovery through implementation and field adoption. Retail DX is a concept referring to initiatives that transform the business operations and business models of the entire retail industry through digital technology. FDE refers to engineers (or a support model) who work in the field and handle everything from implementing AI agents to their adoption, as one means of realizing that DX. The image is that FDE is the execution force for achieving the major goal of retail DX.

Even if your company has no one with such experience, adoption is possible by using external FDE talent. Understanding of retail-specific business operations can also be supplemented by collaborating with field staff while advancing implementation. See Chapter 5 for details.

There are no qualifications required for FDE. Rather, AI agent development implementation experience and practical understanding of the target industry (in this case, the retail industry) are valued more.
