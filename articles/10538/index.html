2026 Yunqi Conference | Dianming CloudSai Becomes One of the First FDE Service Providers to Sign with Qwen Office

Full text

On September 22, the "AI Native · Reshaping Enterprise Productivity" sub-forum of the 2026 Yunqi Conference was held at the Hangzhou International Expo Center. In the forum's closing session, Zhang Liang, Vice President of Alibaba Cloud Intelligence Group's Public Cloud Business Unit and General Manager of AI Native Business, together with Chen Yusen, CEO of Qianwen Office, jointly launched the Alibaba Cloud Public Cloud Ecosystem Partner FDE program and signed the first batch of FDE-certified service providers, with Dianming Yunsai becoming one of the first signatories.

As one of the core announcements of this forum, the debut of the FDE program sends a clear signal: the competition in enterprise AI adoption is shifting from "who has the stronger model" to "who can actually put AI into the business."

And the latter is exactly what FDE exists for.

01 A New Profession Being Fiercely Contested: FDE

FDE, Forward Deployed Engineer.

This role has already gone through a talent war overseas — top AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic are all hiring FDEs at high salaries. Their job is not to sit in an office writing code, but to go deep into customers' business sites and translate AI capabilities into business outcomes.

In China, this role has just started, and its scarcity far exceeds what outsiders imagine.

"Recently, to keep up with the growing number of implementation projects, we intensively interviewed more than a dozen FDE candidates. Only a handful were truly qualified." — Head of Dianming Yunsai

It's not that the candidates are bad, but that this role demands an unusually "unconventional" skill structure — one person must simultaneously possess engineering ability, business understanding, product thinking, and system architecture skills.

The intersection of these four capabilities is precisely the hardest "last mile" of enterprise AI adoption.

02 After Interviewing a Dozen Candidates, They Summarized Four Dimensions

What makes a qualified FDE? Drawing on long-term delivery practice, the company has summarized four screening dimensions, which align closely with the competency items assessed in this Qianwen Office FDE certification.

Dimension One: Architecture and Task Decomposition

This is the most critical one: being able to judge which level an enterprise's problem belongs to — can it be solved by directly calling a large model, does it require building a workflow, or must multiple agents collaborate?

Truly capable people are not those who are fluent with frameworks, but those who can break a real business into steps and states: knowing which tool to call at which step, what to do when something fails, and where human confirmation is required.

Dimension Two: Tool Calling and Context Engineering

When agents truly enter the business, they inevitably involve systems, data, and permissions. Reading emails, generating replies, updating tickets — which actions can be executed automatically, which require human review, how permissions are restricted, and how exceptions are handled.

Many agents ultimately fail to run not because the model isn't strong enough, but because the tools are poorly designed, the context is too messy, and errors cannot be recovered from.

Dimension Three: Evals and Observability

Whether an agent is doing the right thing cannot rely on a vague "seems okay." You must establish clear eval criteria and be able to see what it did at each step, which tools it called, and where exactly it went wrong.

Otherwise, an agent going live is just a black box.

Dimension Four: Production Reliability and Human-Machine Collaboration

Agents in real business don't run once and end. They will be interrupted, wait for human confirmation, recover, and must also account for the cost of mistakes, latency, log monitoring, degradation, and rollback.

Only when it can be paused, resumed, audited, and rolled back does it truly enter production.

The first two dimensions determine whether an agent can be built;

the last two dimensions

determine whether it can run stably in the business.

03 Behind the List: Implementation Samples from Five Industries

The weight of the signing list must ultimately be measured by the projects actually delivered.

Zongshen Power · General Machinery Manufacturing

A leading Chinese general machinery manufacturer with a complex organizational structure and data scattered across multiple heterogeneous systems. The FDE team started with requirements research, talking with different departments on the front lines, identifying more than a dozen high-priority AI opportunity scenarios, then delivering them in batches, validating results and adjusting direction with the business teams at every step.

Shenci Electromechanical · General Power Machinery

A leading general power machinery company whose product lines cover generators, water pumps, pressure washers, and more, with long business chains and complex SKUs. The FDE team went deep into its production, supply chain, and sales operations, using customized agents to connect the key data links from order to delivery, enabling business staff to query and analyze data directly in natural language, greatly reducing the communication cost of cross-system collaboration.

Sanyou Machinery · Automotive Intelligent Chassis Manufacturing

A manufacturer of automotive intelligent chassis systems, where R&D and production chains have high professional barriers. The FDE team advanced agent implementation around R&D and production scenarios, gradually making the accumulation and retrieval of technical documentation and process data intelligent.

Dacheng Law Offices · Legal Services

Legal services demand extremely high accuracy, professionalism, and knowledge accumulation. The FDE team carried out agent implementation practices around legal scenarios, combining industry knowledge bases with agent capabilities to explore AI-driven upgrades of legal workflows.

Tibet Airlines · Civil Aviation Transport

The aviation industry has long business chains and many service scenarios. The FDE team advanced AI adoption exploration around aviation service scenarios, gradually bringing agents into the real workflows of frontline operations and services.

Equipment manufacturing, automotive, legal, aviation — five industries, five completely different business logics, all backed by the same methodology: FDE teams go deep into the field, break the business into steps and states, and then grind through scenario by scenario.

Not a single one was solved with an off-the-shelf product.

04 After Signing: What Certification Means

According to public information, Dianming Yunsai is a flagship top-tier Alibaba Cloud partner and a six-star diamond-level DingTalk service provider, with over five years of deep engagement in enterprise digitalization, serving several thousand enterprise customers.

Notably, the company is also a heavy user of Qianwen Office — its main platform and daily workflows themselves run on Qianwen Office, with agent building, skill orchestration, and cross-department collaboration happening every day in real business.

Become a user first, then serve customers. The pitfalls they've stumbled into themselves have all become pitfalls they help customers avoid.

This first batch of FDE certification is less a credential than a mutual confirmation: Qianwen Office has certified the service provider's implementation delivery standards, and the service provider brings officially recognized quality standards into every customer engagement.

For enterprise customers, this means something very concrete — enterprise AI adoption now has standards, acceptance criteria, and officially certified teams accountable for results.

Final Words

For enterprises planning AI adoption, three pieces of advice remain simple:

Get hands-on first, don't just theorize — Qianwen Office can be experienced at zero cost; feeling it out is more useful than debating;

Start with small scenarios, don't overreach — get high-frequency, clearly rule-based, fault-tolerant scenarios like knowledge base Q&A and ticket summarization working first;

Find people who understand implementation — leave the model to Qianwen Office; leave the pitfalls of implementation to a certified FDE team.

When it comes to enterprise AI transformation,

the most expensive thing has never been the tools,

it's the time lost taking detours.
