Why "Ontology" Now? How Palantir and LLMs Are Transforming Data Utilization

Summary

This Zenn article explains why ontologies are being redefined in the era of Palantir and LLMs. An ontology remaps scattered system data into business-language objects and relationships. Unlike read-only BI dashboards, an ontology-based approach lets users act directly on objects via write-back, merging analytics with operations. For AI, ontologies supply context that reduces hallucinations in RAG and Text-to-SQL. The author, writing for FDEs and AI engineers, offers five practical rules for building ontologies and argues competition is shifting from storing data to defining it for AI.

  • Ontologies give AI semantic context, reducing hallucinations in RAG and Text-to-SQL.

  • Data competition is shifting from storage to AI-understandable definitions.

Introduction

We've been hearing the word "ontology" more and more often lately.

"Ontology? Isn't that that old Semantic Web thing, an academic concept?" "How was it different from knowledge graphs again?"

That's about all I remembered, but recently, with Palantir being a hot topic and with practical LLM use, I felt this concept was being redefined with new meaning.

So, I've organized what I researched.

1. What Exactly Is an "Ontology"?

In a nutshell, it is "a map that redefines data in terms and relationships understandable not only by computers but also by 'humans (business)'".

In many companies, data exists in scattered silos due to system constraints, right?

  • The ERP system has a table called T_SALES_01
  • The CRM has a column called Customer_ID
  • The factory sensor logs have Machine_A_Log

These are "IT terms (rows and columns)", not "business terms". Ontology translates these and creates a map.

  • T_SALES_01 = "Order"
  • Customer_ID = "Customer"
  • Machine_A_Log = "Manufacturing Equipment"

And the most important part is "defining relationships".

In this way, defining things (objects) and events (relationships) to recreate the structure of the real-world business in the digital world is called ontology building in the context of data platforms.

2. How Is It Different from BI Tools? The Wall Between "Seeing" and "Doing"

Here, I'll dig into the question many people have: "How is it different from BI tools (dashboards)?" This is the key to understanding modern ontology.

Common Patterns in Traditional Data Utilization

Have you experienced something like this in your workplace?

  • Struggling to clean and organize the data (ETL).
  • Viewing graphs in a BI tool.
  • "Hmm, sales are dropping." "Inventory is decreasing." — analyze and done.

Then, when it's time to actually order inventory, what do you do? You open a different system (ERP or ordering system), log in again, and operate it, right?

Of course, some modern BI tools have action features, but in general, "analytics" and "operations" are separated.

Palantir's Ontology Approach

On the other hand, an approach based on ontology differs here.

Data exists on screen as objects like "Customer" or "Inventory", and you can perform actions (write-back) directly on those objects.

In other words, "the place where you view data" and "the place where you do work" are integrated.

The foundation that makes this possible is the ontology. It's not just data visualization; it plays the role of a glue that connects "data integration" and "frontline operations", which is why it's attracting attention.

3. Why Is It Essential in the Generative AI Era?

And what I'm paying attention to now is its compatibility with generative AI.

When trying to have AI handle internal data with RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) and the like, the biggest obstacle is "AI cannot understand the meaning (context) of the data".

Even if you give AI a mere "list of sales figures", it cannot judge whether they are "good numbers" or "bad numbers". But what if there's an ontology?

  • Human: "What's causing the drop in factory utilization?"
  • AI (referencing ontology): "According to the ontology definitions, 'Factory A' uses 'Part B', and there is currently a delivery delay from 'Part B's 'Supplier C'. This is likely the cause."

By providing AI with "a map of the data (context)", the ontology enables AI to reduce hallucinations and give logical answers. Truly, a common language between AI and business.

4. [Practical Edition] 5 Golden Rules Engineers Should Keep in Mind

From here on, I'll summarize key points for actually working on ontology building in practice as an FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) or AI engineer.

This requires a completely different mindset from mere "DB design". "Business usability" matters more than "technical correctness".

① Think in Terms of "Things (Objects)", Not "Tables"

DB engineers tend to think about normalization (splitting data cleanly), but in ontology, "what frontline people call it" takes top priority.

  • ❌ DB brain: Define T_USER, T_ADDRESS, and T_CONTACT separately.
  • ⭕ Ontology brain: Join these into a single object called "Customer".

If frontline people say "What happened with that project?", then a "Project" object is needed. Matching object names to frontline vocabulary is the starting point.

② Design "Actions", Not Just "Viewing"

As mentioned earlier, it doesn't end with visualizing data. Consider "If an inventory shortage alert appears, what will the user do next?"

  • Is an "order button" needed?
  • Is "assignee assignment" needed?

Unless you design all the way through to write-back actions, you'll end up with nothing more than a fancy dashboard.

③ Don't skimp on "descriptions (Semantics)" for AI

This might be the most important part. If you're doing RAG or Text-to-SQL, metadata is everything.

  • Column names like flg_01 are strictly forbidden. Rename it to is_vip_customer.
  • Description: Don't just write "sales amount"; include business logic in the description, such as "excluding tax; returns are excluded."

"Explain things to AI the way you would explain them to a new employee." Just doing this dramatically boosts AI accuracy.

④ Entity resolution is the technical crux

How do you link Salesforce's

Sony

with SAP's

Sony Corp.

? If this is done carelessly, AI will lie without hesitation.


You need solid entity resolution logic and ID management, not naive string matching.

⑤ Start small and build from "use cases"

If you try to "build a perfect ontology integrating all company data," you will fail 100% of the time.

  • First, narrow the focus to "a specific problem (e.g., reducing factory downtime)."
  • Build only the "equipment" and "maintenance history" needed for that.
  • Once you see results, expand.

An ontology is a living thing. Unused objects are just garbage.

Conclusion

I feel that the axis of competition in data utilization is shifting from "how to store data (Data Lake)" to "how to define data and make AI understand it (Ontology)."

Of course, simply implementing an ontology won't drive DX forward. However, I believe engineers are increasingly being asked, beyond coding skills, for "the design ability to understand on-the-ground business processes and translate them into digital 'things' and 'events.'"

As someone who started my career as a non-engineer, I believe this "translation between business and digital" is exactly what will define an engineer's value in the coming AI era, so I'm working hard to become someone who can do both!
