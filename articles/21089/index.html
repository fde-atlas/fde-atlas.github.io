Where to Find FDE Jobs in Japan | Types of Hiring Companies and How to Read Job Postings

Many people who search for "FDE jobs" feel that the number of results is lower than expected. The reason is not that there are few jobs, but that the same role is posted under different names by each company. In this article, as of the time of writing (August 2026), we will organize where FDE jobs in Japan can be found, how to categorize the companies that are hiring, and how to search so you don't miss listings due to variations in job titles.

The Bottom Line Up Front

  • FDE jobs in Japan fall into three types—foreign AI companies, domestic AI startups, and SIers/consulting firms—and the required English proficiency, depth of implementation, and compensation ranges clearly differ by type
  • Job titles include Forward Deployed Engineer, Forward Deployed Software Engineer, Deployment Strategist, Applied AI Engineer, AI Solution Engineer, and many more; a single search term will miss more than half of them
  • Foreign companies' Japan-focused jobs are often posted directly on their own applicant tracking systems and never appear on Japanese job boards, so if you only look at job boards you will miss the highest-paying tier

The reason jobs are hard to find is that the names vary widely

The first stumbling block in searching for FDE jobs is the job title. Even within what can be confirmed as of the time of writing (August 2026), the same role is posted under names such as Forward Deployed Engineer, Forward Deployed Software Engineer, Deployment Strategist, Applied AI Engineer, Applied AI Architect, Technical Deployment Lead, AI Solution Engineer, and Implementation Engineer.

To give a concrete example, as of the time of writing, Anthropic does not have a Forward Deployed Engineer opening in Tokyo, but it is hiring an Applied AI Architect and an Applied AI Engineer in the same city. The job involves embedding with customers and putting LLMs into production—effectively an FDE role. If you search only for the term "FDE," you will never find these postings.

There are also misses in the opposite direction. Palantir's Tokyo openings include two types: Forward Deployed Software Engineer (an engineering role) and Deployment Strategist (a role handling the customer's business side), and the latter does not require writing code. Similar names do not mean the same role. When searching for jobs, you need to judge by the job description, not the title.

Three Types of Hiring Companies

Companies hiring FDEs in Japan broadly fall into three categories. What you need to prepare depends on which type you target.

The most overlooked is the third type: SIers and consulting firms. While their compensation ranges often fall short of foreign companies, they already have customer bases, so projects never dry up and you can work on large-scale implementations. On the other hand, at some companies these are simply traditional SI engagements relabeled as "FDE," so you need to verify the scope of implementation in the job posting.

Where to Look for Job Postings

Where the jobs are posted also differs by type. Foreign AI companies' Japan-focused jobs are posted directly on their own recruiting sites (applicant tracking systems such as Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby) and almost never appear on Japanese job boards. Neither OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting nor Palantir's Tokyo postings can be found unless you go directly to the companies' recruiting pages.

Domestic AI startups are the opposite: they appear on engineer-focused job boards and scouting services. Here you can use the boards' search functions, but as noted above, job titles vary widely, so you need to try multiple search terms.

For SIers and consulting firms, corporate recruiting pages and referrals through job-change agents are the main channels. These are often treated as confidential listings, and telling an agent "FDE" may not get through, so it is more reliable to describe the job content (a role that embeds with customers to implement LLMs).

Since manually covering these three channels every week is not realistic, Westeria Inc. operates FDE board. It aggregates FDE jobs in Japan (or open to applicants living in Japan) from both foreign companies' applicant tracking systems and domestic job boards into a single list. The number of listings is on the order of a dozen to a few dozen as of the time of writing. Start by browsing the full list on FDE board, then check the primary information for any companies that interest you.

Six Things to Always Check in a Job Posting

A defining feature of FDE job postings is that even with the same job title, the actual content varies greatly. Checking the following six points before applying can prevent a great deal of mismatch after joining.

The first point is especially important. Whether "you write code yourself in the customer's environment, or only handle configuration and integration" makes this an entirely different job. If the job description contains no mention of implementing production systems, making architectural decisions, or code review, and only lists requirements definition, proposals, and adoption support, then it is closer to a Solutions Engineer or Customer Success role than an FDE. Neither is better or worse, but you will burn out if it doesn't match your expectations.

  • Scope of implementation — Do you write production code in the customer's environment, or only handle configuration and integration? Does the job description include terms like "implementation" and "architecture"?
  • Path to feeding back into the product — Is there an explicit role for reflecting insights gained in the field back into the company's own product? If not, it is effectively close to contracted work
  • Required English level — Required or preferred? If required, is it just reading and writing, or does it extend to discussions in meetings?
  • Ratio of business trips to on-site work — Job postings often state figures (25–50%, 25–75%, etc.). This directly affects your life planning.
  • Client industries — Finance, healthcare, and public sector roles may require security clearances or industry knowledge.
  • Team composition — How many FDEs are there, and are you the first one? Being the first offers great autonomy, but there is no established playbook and the workload is heavy.

The actual English requirements

English is the biggest fork in the road for FDE jobs in Japan. As far as I could verify at the time of writing (August 2026), job postings at foreign AI companies in Tokyo almost without exception require both Japanese and English. OpenAI's Tokyo FDE job posting explicitly states that you must be fluent in both Japanese and English, submit your resume in English, and interview in both languages. Palantir's Tokyo posting also lists business-level Japanese and English as requirements.

The level required is not a certification exam score, but the ability to "hold technical discussions in meetings with clients and communicate in writing with engineers at headquarters." Some postings accept reading and writing skills alone, but for the highest-paying tier, it is closer to reality to assume that conversational ability is also required.

If your English is currently insufficient, a realistic path is to start with FDE roles at domestic AI startups to build hands-on experience, then target foreign companies afterward. With a track record as an FDE, English becomes the only missing element in your next job change, making it clear what you need to fill in.

Things to do before applying that will change your pass rate

FDE hiring processes look not only at technical skill, but also at "experience defining a problem yourself from an ambiguous state and taking something working all the way to production." What you should write in your resume is not a list of technologies used, but a story in this form: what state the client was in when they consulted you, what you defined as the problem, what you built, and how it is being used in production.

Also, actually trying the product of the company you are applying to is effective. Since an FDE's job is to put that product into clients' hands, interviewers can tell whether you know the product's quirks and limitations. For LLM-related companies, even just having built and run something small with their API makes your conversation concrete.

Frequently asked questions

  • How many FDE jobs are there in Japan?
  • The count depends on how far you include variations of the job title. If limited to postings explicitly stating "Forward Deployed Engineer," there are not many, but including titles like Applied AI Engineer or Deployment Strategist that are effectively the same role yields a fair number. FDE board lists postings explicitly labeled FDE separately from those whose duties are effectively FDE work; as of writing, there are roughly a dozen to a few dozen. For the latest count, see the FDE board job listings.
  • Are there remote FDE jobs?
  • Fully remote positions are rare. Since this role is premised on embedding with clients, hybrid work (part of the week in the office, plus business trips to client sites) is the norm. As far as I could verify at the time of writing (August 2026), OpenAI's Tokyo posting is a hybrid arrangement with three days a week in the office and domestic travel expected. Some domestic startups offer fully remote positions, but that does not mean zero client visits.
  • Where can I find FDE jobs at foreign companies?
  • Checking each company's careers site directly is the most reliable way. Job postings from foreign AI companies for Japan often do not appear on Japanese job boards and are listed only on their own applicant tracking systems (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, etc.). If checking weekly is a hassle, use FDE board, which aggregates both foreign and domestic postings.
  • I am on the company side. Can I list my FDE job on FDE board?
  • Yes. FDE board aims to aggregate FDE jobs in Japan (or open to applicants living in Japan), and listing is free. Please send the company name, contact person, and job URL via the FDE board posting form. How to write a job posting from a hiring perspective is covered in our article for companies.
