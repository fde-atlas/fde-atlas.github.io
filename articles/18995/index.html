The difference between AI Field Engineer and FDE

Original title: AI Field Engineer 和 FDE 的区别

FDE Times · 2026-11-09 · 5 min read

Summary

Using Fireworks AI's AI Field Engineer job posting as a case study, this article examines hybrid roles that blend pre-sales and forward deployed engineering. The dividing line, per recruiter Christian & Timbers, is where accountability ends: sales engineers finish at contract signing, while FDEs own production outcomes. Fireworks' posting asks for production-grade GenAI POC experience but tracks sales metrics like win rate and POC cycle time, and pays via OTE plus equity. The author advises candidates to read metrics and compensation carefully and ask employers directly who owns the system after the deal closes.

Key points

  • Fireworks' AI Field Engineer splits time between co-selling and building POCs through production.
  • The key distinction: whether responsibility continues after the customer signs.
  • Managers track win rate, velocity, and POC cycle time—sales-oriented metrics.
  • Compensation uses OTE plus equity, signaling sales-linked pay.
  • Candidates should target the half of the role they lack when applying.

Key Takeaways

  • Fireworks wants its AI Field Engineer to serve as technical lead on co-sell deals while also personally building POCs and MVPs and pushing them to production.
  • Recruiting firm Christian & Timbers argues the difference between a Sales Engineer and an FDE lies in where accountability ends: a Sales Engineer's job is done when the customer signs; an FDE remains accountable for production outcomes.
  • Before applying, ask whether success is measured by win rate or by systems that actually run — this determines what you do every day.

Fireworks AI is hiring a Field Engineering manager. The role requires tracking and improving the team's win rate, velocity, and POC cycle time — exactly the kind of scorecard sales teams use.

Yet the same job posting also requires candidates to prove they have built production-grade software with customers, not just advised from the sidelines.

These two requirements point in different directions, and Fireworks doesn't hide it. For the AI Field Engineer – Strategic Partnerships role, the company accepts candidates with three or more years of experience in any one of four types of work: pre-sales, partner engineering, forward deployed engineering, or technical consulting. In other words, it treats sales engineering and forward deployed engineering as two entry points into the same job.

If your goal is a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role, hybrid postings like this will become increasingly common. The job title on a posting won't tell you what you'll actually be doing six months in. To find out, read the metrics and compensation terms closely, then ask the employer directly.

One Role, Two Sets of Responsibilities

The Strategic Partnerships job description splits into two starkly different halves. The first half is commercial: the AI Field Engineer acts as technical lead on co-sell deals, meaning selling solutions to shared customers alongside strategic partners. The second half is building: working with the partner's engineering team to deliver proof-of-concepts (POCs) and minimum viable products (MVPs) end to end.

The preferred qualifications make the second half even clearer. Fireworks wants candidates with experience taking GenAI POCs from prototype to production-scale deployment. That is FDE experience, because a pure Sales Engineer typically stops once the demo has convinced the buyer.

The compensation terms, however, lean toward sales. The published figure is a target total compensation (OTE) range plus equity — the standard structure for roles where part of the pay is tied to sales performance, unlike the fixed salary bands of pure engineering roles. For candidates, this detail says a lot about how the company will evaluate you.

The Real Line Is the Signature

So how do you tell whether a hybrid role is closer to sales engineering or forward deployed engineering? Recruiting firm Christian & Timbers argues that the two roles differ little in technical skill or customer-facing time; the difference lies in where accountability ends. A Sales Engineer's job is done when the customer signs; for an FDE, responsibility continues after the signature.

Fullstack's account matches this. Sales Engineers and Solutions Engineers support the sales cycle before the deal closes; FDEs work inside the customer's environment, build working solutions, and personally push them into production.

An FDE interview prep course on Educative sums it up with a single yardstick: a Sales Engineer is measured by the buyer's confidence; an FDE is measured by a system that runs in the customer's environment.

Apply that yardstick to Fireworks' job description and you'll find the role straddles both sides. The table below compares them item by item.

What is the deliverableThe buyer's confidenceA system running in the customer's environmentAn end-to-end POC/MVP completed with the partner's engineering team

The last column reveals what neither job posting says outright. In terms of scope of work, Fireworks describes an FDE; in terms of the metrics the manager tracks for the team, it describes a sales engineering team. That said, the postings don't specify whether each individual AI Field Engineer is evaluated in exactly the same way.

Teams Do What They're Measured On

According to the manager job posting, Fireworks' Field Engineering team owns technical evaluations and brings customers into production. The evaluation process has four steps: requirements discovery, architecture, POC, then a production plan. The team's playbook includes requirements discovery frameworks, POC templates, and reference architectures — all familiar tools for deployment teams.

But the metrics the manager must track and improve are win rate, velocity, POC cycle time, and utilization. The manager also coaches both account executives and Field Engineers. This team sits very close to the sales process.

Imagine a hypothetical scenario. You lead a POC for a document extraction AI agent for a shared customer of Fireworks and a partner. You move fast, the demo lands well, the deal closes, and every team metric is rising.

Three months later, the customer connects real traffic. Latency climbs, and accuracy on messy data drops. Now the question is: who fixes it?

If the person fixing it is you, and that work counts toward your performance review, you're doing FDE work. If your calendar follows the sales cycle and you've already moved on to the next POC, you're a Sales Engineer who writes decent code.

Neither is better or worse — they're just two different jobs, and the metrics will pull your time toward one of them. Metrics like POC cycle time reward speed; accountability for production rewards caring enough about the parts nobody sees in a demo.

How to Apply for Hybrid Roles

Fireworks' manager job posting groups Field Engineering, Solutions Engineering, FDE, and Pre-Sales together. That's good news: whichever path you came from, there's an entry point. The catch is that your resume must prove the half you're missing.

If you come from sales engineering, the weakness that will get scrutinized is what happens after the signature. Phrases like "led numerous demos" aren't enough. Write about one POC you carried all the way to production: how real data differed from demo data, what you fixed, and how the system runs today.

If you come from FDE or software engineering, the weakness is on the other side. Fireworks needs people who can serve as technical lead in front of a buyer. Show evidence: you've led requirements discovery, narrowed a POC's scope so the customer could decide faster, and you understand why POC cycle time matters to a sales team.

In the interview, turn Educative's yardstick into a question: at this company, is your success measured by the buyer's confidence or by a system running in the customer's environment? Then ask: after a deal closes, who owns the system? And how many POCs actually make it into production?

If the employer answers clearly, it shows they've thought this role through. If the answer is vague, assume the OTE portion will determine the priorities on your calendar.

The AI Field Engineer title isn't bad—it just honestly admits the role contains two jobs. You need to know which one takes priority when your calendar fills up, and you should figure that out before signing the contract.
