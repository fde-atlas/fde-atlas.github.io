How is a forward deployed engineer different from a solutions engineer?

FDE Club · 2026-11-14 · 4 min read

Summary

This FDE Club note contrasts forward deployed engineers with solutions/sales engineers using published sources and Swiss job postings from 14 November 2026. The core distinction: FDEs write and ship production code inside customer environments after the sale and contribute back to the company's product, while solutions engineers mostly advise, demo, and support the sale. Bloomberry's analysis of roughly 1,000 postings found about 60 percent were builder roles, 30 percent closer to sales engineering, and 10 percent internal tools. Swiss postings from OpenAI, DeepJudge, LatticeFlow AI, Blockstream, and Databricks show the title is often applied inconsistently, so job descriptions, not titles, determine the real role.

Key points

  • FDEs write production code in customer environments and contribute to the core product.
  • Solutions engineers focus on demos, proofs of concept, and supporting the sale.
  • Bloomberry classed ~60% of 1,000 FDE postings as builder roles, ~30% as sales-like.
  • Swiss postings show blurred lines; one Databricks FDE role is quota-carrying.
  • Interview questions should probe production code, product contribution, timing, and pay structure.

Field notes

A forward deployed engineer writes production code inside a customer's environment after the sale, and feeds what is learned back into the product. A solutions engineer or sales engineer mostly advises, demonstrates and supports the sale. Published sources agree on that line, and they also show that many postings blur it.

What do the sources say a forward deployed engineer does that a solutions engineer does not?

They write and ship production code for the customer, and they change the product.

The Pragmatic Engineer lists "Agent engineer", "Solutions Architect", "Sales Engineer" and "Technical Delivery Engineer" as titles that come close to the role. It then names the difference: forward deployed engineers "work with customers and also contribute to the product their company sells" [1]. In its account of OpenAI, solutions architects "are more like advisory roles: they rarely write code on customers' infrastructure", and usually build minimum viable products or proofs of concept [1].

First Round Review draws the same line from the hiring side. Unlike a solutions consultant or a sales engineer, it writes, the forward deployed engineer is "still very much an engineer who writes and debugs production code" [2]. A former Palantir engineer who later led recruiting for the role there says in the same article that implementation work for a customer is "important, but that's not forward deployed engineering" [2].

Why are the two roles confused?

Because many employers use the new title for older work.

  • Bloomberry read about 1,000 postings with the title. It classed about 60 percent as builder roles, about 30 percent as closer to sales engineering, and about 10 percent as internal tools roles [3].
  • The Pragmatic Engineer reported in March 2026 on two engineers who took the title and were disappointed. In their experience the job was closer to a sales engineer or a solutions engineer, with little that counts as software engineering beyond integrating what the product team built. One of them left after four weeks [4].
  • Ted Mabrey of Palantir wrote in 2024 that every copy of the role he had met was "a half measure" [7].

So the title on a posting does not settle the question. The description of the work does.

What do postings in Switzerland show?

They show both patterns, sometimes in one posting. All of these were read on the employer's own careers system on 14 November 2026.

  • OpenAI's Zurich posting describes delivery "from first prototype to stable production" and says the engineer will "contribute directly in the code when progress or clarity depends on it". It measures success through "production adoption" [5].
  • DeepJudge's Zurich posting keeps the two roles apart. Its forward deployed engineer owns the technical implementation at the customer and works "closely with Sales Engineering", which is a separate team [6].
  • LatticeFlow AI's Zurich posting joins them. Its forward deployed engineer leads "technical pre-sales engagements", with demos and proofs of concept, and then owns "post-sale implementation end-to-end" [8].
  • Blockstream's Swiss posting says the role "is not a traditional configuration or support role". The same posting asks the engineer to support the sales team in technical pre-sales and in answering requests for proposals [9].
  • Databricks lists two roles with FDE in the title for Zurich. One is an engineer who delivers "production-grade systems". The other, "Sr. Engagement Manager, FDE", is described by its own posting as "quota-carrying" [10].

FDE Club keeps the roles that are open today on the roles list. It labels a role FDE when the posting says the engineer writes production code inside customer environments and works with the product team. Other customer-facing engineering roles are labelled adjacent.

Which questions tell the two roles apart in a posting or an interview?

These questions follow from the differences the sources name.

  • Does the engineer write code that runs in production at the customer, or demos and proofs of concept? [1] [2]
  • Does the work reach the core product, and how? [1]
  • Does the role start before the contract is signed, or after it? [8] [9]
  • Is any part of the pay tied to a quota or to commission? [3] [10]
  • Which team does the role sit in? In Bloomberry's sample, 45 percent of postings place it in a team of its own, 38 percent in engineering and 14 percent in sales or go-to-market [3].
