How to Choose an AI Consultant | Types, Cost Considerations, and Key Points for Comparison

Original title: AIコンサルの選び方｜種類・費用の考え方・比較で見るべき点

FDE Consulting · 2026-02-17 · 10 min read

Summary

This article argues that companies labeled "AI consulting" differ fundamentally in where their work ends: strategy firms deliver reports, system integrators deliver systems, vendors configure their own products, and implementation-focused teams work until business metrics move and knowledge is handed over internally. Citing Japan's MIC white paper (86.4% of Japanese firms use generative AI) and a Gartner forecast (at least 30% of generative AI projects abandoned after PoC by end of 2025), the author stresses matching the firm type to your stage: concept, PoC, production, or operations. It covers contract types under Japanese civil law (quasi-delegation, contract for work, outcome-based fees), six comparison criteria for proposals, four clauses to include in generative AI contracts, and situations where hiring AI consultants is unnecessary.

Key points

  • Four types of AI consulting firms, distinguished by where work ends
  • 86.4% of Japanese firms use generative AI; 30% of projects stall after PoC
  • Match firm type to stage: concept, PoC, production, operations
  • Contract forms: quasi-delegation, contract for work, outcome-based fees
  • Six criteria for comparing proposals, plus four contract clauses for generative AI

When you try to consult an "AI consultant" and line up the candidates, you quickly notice how hard they are to compare. Companies that draw up business plans, companies that build systems, and companies that sell their own products all hang the same "AI consulting" sign. The format of their proposals, the units of their estimates, and the point at which their work ends are all different.

The background to the increase in consultations is clear. According to the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications' 2026 Information and Communications White Paper, 86.4% of Japanese companies now use generative AI in some part of their operations. Meanwhile, in July 2024, Gartner predicted that at least 30% of generative AI projects would be abandoned after proof of concept by the end of 2025. Getting started is easier than keeping things running in production.

In my view, the first thing to check when choosing an AI consultant is not the company's size or reputation, but where its work ends. Does it end with a report, with the delivery of a system, or with business metrics moving and the work being handed over in-house? If this is misaligned with what your company needs, even hiring a well-regarded firm will not produce the results you expect.

Below, I cover in order: the types of AI consulting firms, how to hire one suited to your company's stage, how to think about costs, a table for comparing proposals, what to put in the contract for generative AI consultations, and situations where you should not hire an AI consultant. For the role of building AI through to production on the ground, see What is an FDE? The meaning and work of a Forward Deployed Engineer.

Four Types of AI Consulting Firms

Even when lumped together as "AI consulting firms," their strengths differ by stage depending on their original business. I think of them in the following four categories.

TypeOriginal businessStrengths by stageWhere the work endsSuitable consultations
Strategy and general consultingManagement consultingCompany-wide vision, prioritizationDelivery of plans and reportsDeciding what to tackle first across the company
SI and development firmsSystem development, contract developmentDevelopment after specifications are fixed, integration with existing systemsDelivery of the systemWhat to build and the specifications are already settled
Product and tool vendorsTheir own AI products and SaaSImplementation and configuration of their own productsThe point at which the product is usableThe product to use is already decided
Implementation-focused specialist teamsSmall teams combining engineers and consultantsFrom problem definition through production and operationsBusiness metrics moving and handover in-houseYou tried it, but it is not running in production

This is not about which is superior. If a company without a company-wide direction hires an implementation-focused team, work begins without a defined target. If a company with settled specifications hires a strategy firm, it receives a report on things it already knows. Large firms may contain multiple types, so look not at the company name but at which type of work the current proposal is.

The fourth type, implementation-focused, is close to the concept of the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer), a role that works on-site and takes responsibility through to building. The differences from SES, SIers, and consultants are written up separately in Differences between FDEs and SES, SIers, and consultants.

What You Ask an AI Consultant Changes Depending on Your Current Stage

Before choosing a type, decide which stage your company is at now. The "Contract Guidelines on AI and Data Utilization" published by METI in June 2018 divides AI development into stages such as assessment, PoC, and development, and presents an approach called the "exploratory stage-based model," which proceeds stage by stage without signing a master agreement governing the whole.

This is a framework for development contracts, but in my view it applies directly to choosing a consultant for AI adoption or AI utilization. That is because what you ask for and which type fits change depending on the stage. The following table is my summary.

StageYour company's stateWhat to ask the AI consultantTypes that fit wellWhat you receive
VisionWhat to do with AI is undecidedInventory of business processes, drafting candidate target processes and KPIsStrategy and general consulting, implementation-focusedPriorities for target processes and KPI candidates
Prototype (PoC)Target business process is decidedBuild small and verify effectiveness and feasibilityImplementation-focused, SI and development firmsA working prototype and evaluation results
ProductionThe prototype showed promiseBuild in combination with existing systems, permissions, and monitoringSI and development firms, implementation-focusedA system running in production
Operations and adoptionRunning in productionContinue evaluating and fixing, hand over in-houseImplementation-focused, product and tool vendorsAn improvement mechanism and procedure documents

What you most want to avoid is ordering across skipped stages. If you request productionization without deciding what to do, deciding the specifications is left to the vendor, and the result does not fit your business. If you commission work stage by stage, you can decide in-house whether to continue or stop based on the prototype results. How to proceed when stuck after the prototype is covered in Breaking out of AI PoC stagnation.

AI Consulting Costs Are Determined by the Contract Structure

As far as I have researched, I found no public statistics showing the market rates for AI consulting. Even for estimates covering the same period, how many people are involved and for how long, and what triggers payment, differ by company, so comparing amounts alone is meaningless. Before looking at amounts, align on what the contract pays for.

Contract structures fall broadly into three types under the Civil Code.

Contract typeWhat you pay forCivil Code provisionStages that fit wellWhat to verify in the estimate
Quasi-mandate (period/hours)A professional handling work over a fixed periodArticle 656 (delegation of non-legal-act tasks)Vision, prototype, operationsWho is involved and for how many hours. What will be completed within the period
Contract for work (ukeoi)Completion of the workArticle 632Productionization with settled specificationsConditions for completion and the acceptance inspection method
Quasi-mandate paid on resultsResults obtained from performing the workArticle 648-2Operations with clearly defined metrics to moveDefinition of results and how they are measured

A contract for work is one where one party promises completion of the work and the other pays compensation for the result (Civil Code Article 632). A quasi-mandate is a contract delegating the handling of non-legal-act tasks (Article 656), and payment is generally based on the period and hours involved. The Civil Code also has a provision on agreements to pay compensation for results obtained from handling tasks (Article 648-2).

In my view, at the vision and prototype stages, results cannot be predicted in advance, so a quasi-mandate paid by period fits reality better than a contract for work that promises completion. Conversely, if a quasi-mandate with no deadline or completion conditions continues at the productionization stage where specifications are settled, the end becomes invisible. It is fine to change the contract type for each stage.

In our example, FDE implementation support runs from 3 months / from 1 million yen per month, and AI-use security diagnostics takes 2-3 weeks / from a flat 500,000 yen. With any company's quote, first check whether it states what you are paying for and the conditions under which additional costs arise.

Note that, in addition to AI consulting fees, there are also AI tool licenses and API usage charges. The breakdown and how to estimate them are written separately in Breakdown of AI Development Costs.

Six points to compare when evaluating AI consulting firms

Once you receive proposals, put each company's proposal and quote side by side in the same table. In my view, comparing on the following six points makes the differences easy to see. The second column shows where in the proposal or contract you can find the answer.

Point to compareWhere to lookAnswer you want to confirmAnswer to be wary of
Who will be in chargeTeam structure diagramThe person who made the proposal is also involved in the build phaseThe person in charge is decided after the contract is signed
Where the work endsList of deliverablesIt states that it runs in production and is handed over within your companyOnly a report, or only delivery of the system
Metrics to measureEvaluation methodMeasured by business numbers such as processing volume or work hoursMeasured only by AI accuracy or satisfaction
Fee structureQuoteStates what you pay for and the conditions under which additional costs ariseOnly says "as a set"
Handling of dataContract, terms of useStates what the data you provide will be used for and when it will be deletedNo mention of how data is handled
What is handed overContractCode, prompts, evaluation data, and procedure manuals remain with your companyAfter completion, everything remains only with the vendor

In my view, if a company matches two or more of the answers on the right side of the table, it is better to pass on them or renegotiate the conditions before deciding. If you compare two or three companies of different types, you can also see which type fits your company's current stage.

Sample questions you can use as-is in sales meetings are summarized in How to Choose an FDE Support Company, and points to confirm when requesting work in collaboration with your internal team are in What Is AI Accompaniment Support?.

What to put in the contract for generative AI consulting

For generative AI support, in addition to the points above, decide in the contract how data and generated outputs will be handled. On February 18, 2025, the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry published the "Contract Checklist for AI Use and Development." This document is designed to help users of AI-based services consider the scope of use for the data they provide, service standards, and conditions for using AI-generated outputs, and it assumes that business department staff, not just legal departments, will be the ones first considering contracts.

When commissioning generative AI consulting, at least the following four points should be written into the contract or a memorandum of understanding.

  • Whether the internal data you provide will be used for training by the vendor or the AI service.
  • Whether your company can freely use the generated text and code.
  • How output errors will be evaluated, and who will fix them.
  • Who can access which data. How far access permissions and logs will be retained.

There are also risks in how generative AI itself is used. In IPA's "10 Major Information Security Threats 2026," "Cyber Risks Surrounding AI Use" appeared for the first time, at third place in the organizational category. When choosing a consultant, check whether the discussion of data handling and permissions comes before the discussion of what to build. In my view, proposals that put this off tend to stall right before going to production. If you want to first verify how generative AI is being used within your company, it is covered by AI-Use Security Diagnostics.

Situations where you should not hire an AI consultant

Hiring an AI consultant does not mean everything will move forward. In my view, in the following cases it is faster to choose a different approach first.

  • The product to use is already decided, and you only don't know the settings and usage. The vendor's support or implementation assistance is sufficient.
  • What to build and the specifications are already fixed. Going directly to contract development is faster and cheaper.
  • There is no one in-house to hand over to. What an external party builds will stall if there is no one to receive it. Decide on a person in charge first, or start with internal talent development.
  • The numbers you want to move have not been decided. At this stage, start with brief concept development and move to prototyping once the numbers are set.

Conversely, if all three of the following are in place—you have tried but it is not running in production, you have staff in-house who know the operations, and the numbers you want to move are decided—then an implementation-type AI consultant that handles the build phase is a good fit.
