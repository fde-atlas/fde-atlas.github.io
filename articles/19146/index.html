AI Consulting for Small Businesses: 11 Firms Ranked on Printed Prices and Who Builds It (2026)

Top 11 · 2026-11-08 · 17 min read

Summary

This ranking reviews ten US AI consulting firms serving small businesses, scored on whether they publish prices before a call, whether they actually build, who does the work, verifiable proof, and contract terms. Seven of the ten print a price for the first step. Main & Machine ranks first with a $3,500–$8,500 audit credited toward the build and a written 90-day go-live commitment; Sierra Exclusive has the cheapest entry at a $500 flat audit. A buyer's guide advises picking one manual data-transfer task as a baseline, getting build prices in writing, and confirming code ownership, which most firms leave unstated. An eleventh firm, Beyond Elevation, is listed as an unscored wildcard because it shares ownership with the publisher; it places an embedded forward deployed engineer instead of acting as a consultancy.

Buyer's guide5 questions

What does AI consulting for a small business actually include?

On the pages read, it comes in three steps: an audit that maps your workflows and ranks what to automate, a build that puts one or more automations into the tools you already use, and an optional monthly retainer to keep them running. Seven of the ten firms here price at least the first of those steps on their own site.

How much does AI consulting cost for a small business?

From the ten firms' own pages: audits run from $500 (Sierra Exclusive) and $750 (Noma-Tek) to $3,500 to $8,500 (Main & Machine), first builds from a few hundred dollars (Sierra Exclusive's first tier) and $2,000 (Aslan Intelligence) to $45,000 (Main & Machine's top sprint), and retainers from $500 a month (Noma-Tek maintenance) to $50,000 a month (Phos AI Labs). Tool subscriptions are usually extra.

Should I start with an audit or a build?

With an audit, if it is cheap and credited. Main & Machine, Sierra Exclusive and OperatorZero take the audit fee off the build, and Noma-Tek takes $750 off its assessment. An audit that isn't credited and doesn't fix anything on the day is a document you paid for.

Who owns what the consultant builds?

Ask before you sign. RTS Labs states that you own the code, the models and the data, and Beyond Elevation that you own code, agents, accounts and documentation. Most small firms here say nothing about it, which means the contract decides.

AI consultant or forward deployed engineer?

An AI consultant audits your workflows and builds a set of automations, then hands them back. A forward deployed engineer sits inside the business one or two days a week and keeps building, which suits a company whose systems need joining up before any automation can work.

How to choose

  • 1This week, pick the one task your team does every week that moves data from one tool to another by hand: quotes into the accounting system, leads into the CRM, timesheets into payroll. Write down how many hours it takes and who does it. That's the baseline every firm should be measured against.
  • 2List the systems that task touches and check whether a customer has the same name and ID in each. If not, cleaning and joining them is the first job, and any AI built before that runs on bad data.
  • 3Book the cheapest priced first step that fits. Sierra Exclusive's $500 audit, Noma-Tek's $750 session and Aslan Intelligence's $997 audit are all printed; Main & Machine and OperatorZero cost more and credit the fee to the build.
  • 4Ask every firm for the price of the first build in writing, how long it runs, and what you'll hold at the end of it: working automation in your tools or a document.
  • 5Ask who owns the code, the automations, the logins and the documents when you leave, and whether you can cancel the retainer any month. Get it in the contract, because most of the firms here don't print it.
  • 6Ask who builds it, by name. Several firms here are one founder; that's fine for one workflow and a capacity risk for five.
  • 7If your CRM, accounting system and spreadsheets still don't talk to each other after the first fix, and staff keep retyping between them, that's when you bring in a forward deployed engineer rather than another audit.
