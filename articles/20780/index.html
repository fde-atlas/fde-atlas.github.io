For IT consultants who think, "I don't want to end with proposals and documents—I want to implement solutions myself and see customer outcomes through to the end," the FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) role is a compelling career move. FDEs bring their company's product into customers' environments and personally handle everything from identifying issues to implementation, production deployment, and adoption—a role where consultants can directly leverage their skills in problem definition and executive-level communication. On the other hand, the hiring process will always test whether you can write production-quality code yourself.

This article organizes, based on original sources including official job postings and press releases from each company, The Pragmatic Engineer, and First Round Review: the differences between consultant and FDE deliverables, the consulting experience that FDE job postings value, how to rebuild implementation skills that tend to atrophy, examples of job postings that welcome consulting experience, listed salaries, and even the trend of consulting firms themselves building FDE organizations. All job posting details are as of October 2026.

Key Takeaways of This Article

  • The biggest difference between IT consultants and FDEs lies in deliverables: consultants deliver proposals and reports, while FDEs take responsibility for systems that run in customers' operations and for customer outcomes.
  • IT consultants' experience in problem definition, stakeholder management, and executive communication is explicitly listed as a requirement in FDE job postings from companies including OpenAI, Notion, Palantir, and KINTO Technologies (as of October 2026).
  • What IT consultants are tested on in FDE hiring is a track record of writing production-quality code themselves; OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting requires the ability to "write and review production-quality frontend and backend code" as well as experience building systems using LLMs.
  • Among Japanese FDE job postings that disclose salary, SoftBank lists a theoretical annual salary range of ¥8,123,000–¥20,346,200, JDSC lists ¥5,000,000–¥25,000,000, and Money Forward lists ¥7,908,000–¥15,000,000 per year (as of October 2026).
  • Accenture launched an FDE organization in partnership with Microsoft in March 2026 and with Google Cloud in September of the same year, and Deloitte announced in August 2026 that it had delivered a Salesforce FDE project—FDE roles are emerging within consulting firms themselves.

Conclusion: 3 Things to Verify When Moving from Consulting to FDE

Transitioning from IT consulting to FDE is best understood not as a "switch" of roles but as an "extension" of your career. That's because half of the experience FDE job postings require is something consultants already have. The other half—how you prove production-quality implementation skills—determines whether the transition succeeds.

How Consultant and FDE Deliverables Differ

The Pragmatic Engineer explains that while FDEs are sometimes confused with consultants, they differ in that consultants make one-off recommendations, whereas FDEs work with customers over the long term. First Round Review likewise defines FDEs as a role distinct from traditional sales engineers and consultants—one that actually writes and debugs code to solve customer-specific problems.

This difference shows up as a difference in what gets evaluated. A consultant's deliverables are reports, proposals, and implementation plans, and the work ends when they are handed to the client. An FDE's deliverables are systems that actually run in the customer's operations and the customer outcomes they produce. OpenAI's Tokyo FDE posting describes success as measured by "production adoption, measurable business impact, and evaluation-driven feedback that changes the product and model roadmap."

Palantir splits its teams at the same client into "Deployment Strategist" and "Forward Deployed Software Engineer (FDSE)." The Deployment Strategist job posting for Tokyo (Japan commercial division) describes the role as visiting client sites to meet with analysts, identifying key questions and the biggest challenges, finding relevant datasets, working with FDSEs to integrate data into stable pipelines, and presenting results and next proposals to audiences ranging from analysts to executives. While the work is close to that of a consultant, experience with programming, scripting, and statistical packages (Python, R, Matlab, SQL) is positioned as "a plus if you have it."

In other words, for those who want to master problem definition and consensus building without doing the implementation themselves, there is the option of Deployment Strategist, while those who want to handle implementation up to delivery should aim for FDE (FDSE). Consulting experience valued in FDE selection

When you read FDE job postings, you notice that the tasks consultants perform daily are written directly as application requirements. I will break down the experience that tends to be valued into three categories and map them to the job posting descriptions.

Problem definition: the ability to turn vague requests into problems to be solved

An FDE's work starts from a customer's vague question. Palantir's FDSE job posting (Japan government division) states that projects often begin with open-ended questions such as "Why are so many flights delayed?" or "How can we detect money laundering more effectively?" OpenAI Tokyo's FDE job posting states that you will drive discovery, technical scoping, system design, building, and production deployment with ownership, and asks for "experience scoping and delivering complex systems in fast-changing, ambiguous environments."

Japanese companies' job postings say the same. KINTO Technologies' FDE posting places business understanding and problem definition at the top of its duties, expressing it as "translating the language of the field into the language of the builders." Loglass lists as a mandatory requirement experience "breaking down complex business challenges into business flows, data, and decision-making processes, and structuring them into problems solvable with software/AI," while Channel Talk lists as a mandatory requirement "experience organizing and structuring customer challenges and proposing solutions from a technical perspective."

Stakeholder management: the ability to move cross-departmental projects forward

FDEs work not only with the customer's engineering department but also move multiple stakeholders such as business divisions, IT departments, and security departments. OpenAI Tokyo's FDE posting lists as duties "defining scope, deciding the order of delivery, and removing blockers early" and "judging trade-offs among scope, speed, and quality, and adjusting plans to protect delivery." This is precisely a consultant's project management.

Channel Talk lists as a mandatory requirement "experience driving projects while engaging stakeholders inside and outside the company," KINTO Technologies lists "experience independently driving cross-departmental projects through to recommendations to management and business divisions" as one of its requirements, and RevComm writes "ability to build consensus with multiple stakeholders" as a mandatory requirement.

Dialogue with executives: the ability to translate technology into the language of decision-making

FDE is a role in which you often explain technical decisions to executives and elicit decision-making. KINTO Technologies' posting lists as a requirement experience "explaining technical content to non-engineers such as executives and business divisions in a form that leads to decision-making," and includes "identifying decision-makers, setting timelines, and building executive reporting" in its project advancement design. Palantir's Deployment Strategist posting states "presenting results and proposals to audiences ranging from analysts to the C-suite."

ANDPAD's FDE posting describes the role as "an engineer, a consultant, and a business builder," and lists as a mandatory requirement "the translation ability to engage with engineering teams as an equal and convert technical constraints into business value." FDE job postings are asking, in different words, for the skills consultants have honed through dialogue with executives.

How to regain the implementation skills you tend to lack

The reason consultants fail in FDE selection boils down to essentially one point: they cannot demonstrate a track record of writing production-quality code themselves. First Round Review cites, as qualities sought in FDEs, a high technical bar to pass rigorous engineering interviews and write production code, and being "compulsive builders" who cannot help but build things. Experience writing "implementation will be handled by the SIer" in proposals does not count as a track record here.

However, the specific implementation skills required are written concretely in the job postings, so you can prepare for them. The OpenAI Tokyo FDE posting requires "the ability to write and review production-quality frontend and backend code in Python, JavaScript, or an equivalent stack" and "experience building or deploying systems using LLMs or generative models." The SoftBank FDE posting (secondment to SB OAI Japan G.K.) lists "experience designing/implementing production-quality code using Python, JavaScript, etc.," "experience building systems in cloud environments (AWS/GCP/Azure, etc.)," and "experience building or deploying applications using LLMs/generative AI" as required qualifications, and lists "experience with RAG/agent design/evaluation infrastructure" as a preferred qualification.

Build one LLM application to production quality

The most efficient preparation is to build one LLM-powered application to production-level quality. "Production-level" means not just a working demo, but a state that includes authentication, error handling, logging, deployment, and monitoring. If you translate the job posting requirements directly into specifications, the following elements are needed.

  • 01 Problem selection: From the business processes you handled as a consultant (for example, inquiry handling, contract review, or exception handling in expense reimbursement), choose one problem worth solving with an LLM. Problem definition is a consultant's strength, and this is where you can differentiate yourself.
  • 02 Backend: Implement an API in Python or TypeScript, incorporating LLM calls, RAG (retrieval-augmented generation), or external tool connections. This corresponds to the latter half of the "production-quality frontend and backend code" mentioned in the OpenAI Tokyo posting.
  • 03 Frontend: Build a screen that business users will actually use. The "prototype at a level where stakeholders can make decisions" listed as a required qualification in the KINTO Technologies posting can only be achieved with an actual interface.
  • 04 Cloud deployment: Deploy to a production environment on AWS, GCP, or Azure, and set up authentication, logging, and monitoring. The SoftBank posting lists "experience building systems in cloud environments" as a required qualification.
  • 05 Operations: Have actual users (acquaintances or former colleagues are fine) use it and run improvement cycles. The KINTO Technologies posting lists "experience embedding it into business operations until it is actually used continuously and established" as a required qualification.

Design Evals and face LLM non-determinism

In implementing LLM applications, the biggest difference from ordinary software development is evaluation (Evals). Because LLM outputs fluctuate even with the same input, you need to define what "working correctly" means and build a mechanism to measure it continuously. The KINTO Technologies posting lists "experience designing evaluation/verification and human review mechanisms assuming the non-deterministic behavior of LLMs" as one of its requirements, and SoftBank lists "experience building evaluation infrastructure" as a preferred qualification. The OpenAI Tokyo posting places "eval-driven feedback" as one of the ways to measure FDE success.

Specifically, prepare dozens of pairs of expected inputs and desired outputs, automatically score them each time you change the model or prompt, and build a mechanism to separate cases requiring human review. This is the work of applying the "what counts as success" definition that consultants have done in KPI design to LLM outputs. When asked in an interview "How do you ensure LLM quality?", you can speak about it as something you built with your own hands.

Consulting Firms Themselves Are Starting to Build FDE Organizations

Destinations for moving into FDE roles are not limited to AI and SaaS companies. Since the start of 2026, major consulting firms have announced one after another that they are building FDE organizations within their own companies. Below are those confirmed via official press releases, in chronological order.

  • March 18, 2026: Accenture announced it is partnering with Microsoft to launch the "Microsoft Forward Deployed Engineering practice." "Thousands of AI-skilled engineers" will work directly with clients, advancing AI from idea to production "in days, not months." Accenture's Manish Sharma stated that "enterprise AI succeeds when strategy and engineering move as one"
  • June 8, 2026: Salesforce Japan announced the full-scale launch of its "Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) Partner Network" in Japan. The nine initial domestic partners are Accenture, NTT Data, GlobalWay, Terrasky, Deloitte Tohmatsu, Dentsu Soken and Dentsu Digital, PwC Consulting, Fujitsu, and Fractea
  • August 12, 2026: Deloitte (US) announced that, as the first global system integrator to do so, it staffed and delivered a Salesforce FDE engagement. As a founding member of Salesforce's FDE Partner Network, it is embedding FDEs directly into solution delivery teams to implement Agentforce. Salesforce's Nick Johnston, quoted in the announcement, stated that "in the agent era, success is determined by business outcomes, not pilots"
  • September 8, 2026: Accenture announced the establishment of the "Accenture Gemini Enterprise Business Group" with Google Cloud, stating it will "establish an organization of 1,000 forward deployed engineers (FDEs)." It will build on the roughly 50,000 Google Cloud-skilled professionals at Accenture

What these announcements show is that consulting firms have begun adopting the FDE model as a delivery model where they "take responsibility through to delivery." Deloitte's announcement describes the FDE as a role that implements three use cases with Salesforce's Agentforce (automated responses to inquiries, scheduling follow-up meetings, and initial scanning of CRM leads and nurturing logic). The job of an in-firm FDE is to deliver something that works, not a recommendation.

For consultants, this means a path is opening up to move into an FDE role internally without changing employers. However, in-firm FDEs focus on implementing partner companies' platforms (Agentforce, Gemini Enterprise, Microsoft's products), and unlike FDEs at companies with their own products, there is no structure for delivering field insights to the next customer as product features. When choosing between an FDE at a company with its own product and an in-firm FDE, a good criterion is "whether your work remains in the product."

How you will be evaluated in the selection process, and what questions to ask back

There is a clear pattern in how consultants are evaluated in FDE selection processes. First Round Review lists the qualities it looks for in FDE hiring: a "willingness to eat pain" in tackling difficult problems, a technical level sufficient to pass rigorous engineering interviews, a builder temperament that cannot help but create things, and an innate curiosity about how businesses work. The typical perception is that consultants are strong on the last item but questioned on the second and third.

Three things examined in the selection process

  • Whether you can describe your implementation experience in your own words: In interviews, you will not be asked about the "requirements definition and PMO" line on your resume, but about "code you wrote yourself." Using the LLM application mentioned in the previous section as your subject, be prepared to talk concretely about design decisions, where you stumbled, and how you evaluated it
  • Whether you rely on a fixed playbook: First Round Review states that FDEs have no fixed procedures and play a role in discovering new things within the customer's context, writing that they avoid "people whose methods have become too rigid." An attitude of bringing in the firm's methodology as-is will be viewed with caution. Talk about methodology as "just one tool among many"
  • Whether you can describe results in terms of the customer's business outcomes: Rather than saying "the recommendation was adopted," reframe your consulting achievements by tracing "how the customer's operations actually changed as a result of the recommendation." Align with the "production adoption" and "measurable business impact" that the OpenAI Tokyo job posting uses as measures of success

What to verify with your questions

The most common regret consultants have after moving to an FDE role is ending up in a position where "in the end, it's just proposals and coordination, and implementation is handled by another team." Since a job posting alone is not enough to judge this, verify the following points in the interview.

  • The proportion of code the FDE writes themselves: The JAPAN AI (Genie) job posting states a workload split of roughly 20% customer dialogue and 80% technical design and implementation. Ask whether you get an answer at the same level. If the answer is "implementation is handled by the engineering team," it is closer to a Deployment Strategist or Architect role
  • How results are measured: Whether you are evaluated on production adoption and customer business metrics, not on hours worked or number of deliverables. Ask whether there are metrics equivalent to the "production adoption and measurable business impact" written in the OpenAI Tokyo posting
  • The mechanism for feeding back into the product: Whether there is a path for what you build at customer sites to become product features. The OpenAI Tokyo posting lists as a job duty "turning patterns of how work is done into tools, playbooks, and building blocks that others can use." Organizations without this are structurally close to SES or contracted development
  • The Evals and quality assurance framework: Who measures LLM output quality and how. Companies like KINTO Technologies that write evaluation and validation design into their requirements usually have such a framework in place

Summary: Consulting experience is an asset, but you need proof of implementation

Moving from IT consulting to an FDE role is a well-matched career change, since you can carry over three types of experience directly: problem definition, stakeholder management, and dialogue with executives. FDE job postings from OpenAI, Palantir, KINTO Technologies, Loglass, Channel Talk, and others explicitly list these experiences as requirements.

On the other hand, the deliverable of an FDE is not a report but a system that runs in the customer's operations. In the selection process, your track record of writing production-quality code yourself will definitely be examined, so the fastest preparation is to build one LLM-integrated application end to end at production quality, including evaluation (Evals). In addition to AI and SaaS companies with their own products, consulting firms that began building FDE organizations in 2026 are also candidates. Whichever you choose, use your questions in the interview to verify "the proportion of code you write yourself" and "how results are measured," and confirm that the position does not end at proposals.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Q Can I move from IT consulting to an FDE role?

Yes. FDE job postings from OpenAI, Notion, Channel Talk, andpad, Loglass, and others (as of October 2026) explicitly list core consulting experiences such as structuring problems, driving projects with stakeholders involved, and client-facing negotiation in their requirements or preferred qualifications. However, for FDE roles that involve implementation, your track record of writing production-quality code yourself will be examined, so you need to prepare for that.

Q How is consulting experience evaluated in FDE selection?

Three things are evaluated: problem definition, stakeholder management, and dialogue with executives. The KINTO Technologies job posting lists as requirements "experience independently driving cross-departmental projects through to recommendations to management and business divisions" and "experience explaining technical content to executives and business divisions in a form that supports decision-making." On the other hand, whether you can describe your implementation experience in your own words is assessed separately.

Q What should a consultant with almost no coding experience start with?

Start by building one application with an embedded LLM to production-grade quality. Implement the backend and frontend in Python or TypeScript, deploy it to the cloud, add authentication, logging, and monitoring, and build an evaluation (Evals) framework as well. The requirements listed in the OpenAI Tokyo and SoftBank FDE job postings can be used directly as your specification.

Q: What are Evals (evaluations)? Why are they important for people from consulting?

Evals are a framework for continuously measuring whether an LLM's output meets expectations. Because LLM outputs fluctuate even for the same input, you prepare pairs of expected inputs and expected outputs, score them automatically, and separate out cases that require human review. The KINTO Technologies job posting lists "experience designing evaluation and verification frameworks and human review processes that assume the non-deterministic behavior of LLMs" as a requirement. This is an area where consultants can connect the "definition of success" skills they honed through KPI design to implementation.

Q: What is the difference between an FDE, a Deployment Strategist, and a Forward Deployed Architect?

The difference is whether you do the implementation yourself. The Palantir Deployment Strategist job posting covers problem identification, data discovery, and presentations to executives, treating programming experience as "a plus." The Notion Forward Deployed Architect posting explicitly states that "you are not required to write production code." If you want to go beyond proposals and take responsibility for implementation as well, you would choose an FDE role (FDSE at Palantir).

Q: Does switching from consulting to an FDE role increase your salary?

It depends on your current salary and grade, so it cannot be said definitively. Publicly available FDE job postings in Japan (as of October 2026) list SoftBank at a theoretical annual salary of ¥8,123,000 to ¥20,346,200, JDSC at ¥5 million to ¥25 million, and Money Forward at ¥7,908,000 to ¥15 million per year. All of these are ranges from job postings, not actual compensation amounts.

Q: Can you work as an FDE within a consulting firm?

The possibilities are expanding.
