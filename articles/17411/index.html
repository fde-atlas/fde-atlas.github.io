GTM Engineer vs RevOps vs Sales Engineer

Zarif Automates · 2026-09-26 · 8 min read

Summary

Using live job postings read on September 26, 2026, this article separates three commonly confused roles: GTM engineer, revenue operations, and sales/solutions engineer. GTM engineers build the systems, automations and data flows the revenue team runs on, measured by manual work removed and better account signals. RevOps owns the operating model—processes, metrics and forecasts—and ships decisions rather than code. Sales engineers own the technical side of individual deals, judged on wins, with commission-bearing pay like Hebbia's $170,000–$260,000 range. The article identifies four patterns where titles blur, offers a signal table (reporting line, verbs, skills, pay language) to decode postings, lists posted salary ranges from Toast, Cresta, Watershed, TLDR, Attio and others, and suggests questions to ask in first calls.

Key points

  • GTM engineers build revenue systems and automations, judged on manual work removed and better signals.
  • RevOps owns processes, metrics and forecasts; its deliverable is a decision, not code.
  • Sales engineers own technical deal work and are paid on wins, often with commission.
  • Four blur patterns exist: sales-led, customer-facing, ops-lead, and administrator GTM engineer roles.
  • Decode postings via reporting line, verbs, named skills and pay language; posted ranges measure different things.

What GTM engineers, RevOps and sales engineers own, how each is measured, and how to tell which job a posting really describes, from live postings.

A posting titled GTM Engineer can describe an operations job, a software job or a sales job. GTM stands for go-to-market: everything a company does to find, win and keep customers. The roles a GTM engineer gets confused with, revenue operations and sales or solutions engineering, use the same CRM and talk about the same customers.

What separates them is what each person owns, what they get judged on, and who they report to. This page shows each one from live postings, then gives you a way to tell which job a posting really describes before you prepare for the wrong interview.

The postings compared here were read on September 26, 2026. Roles close and ranges change, so open the link before relying on any detail.

These are defaults drawn from the postings below, not laws. Each column is sourced in the sections that follow.

GTM engineer: builds the machinery

The clearest GTM engineer postings describe an engineer who sits inside the revenue organization and ships software for it.

Runway's GTM Engineer is "the revenue org's engineering seat and, in practice, its RevOps lead." The posting says the person owns "CRM, enrichment tools, the signal layer, the agent fleet, and every automation that moves a record from product usage to closed-won," and solves operations problems with Python, SQL, APIs and agentic workflows.

Watershed's GTM engineer drives GTM systems end to end: the Salesforce data model, integrations and automation, CPQ (configure-price-quote software), lead routing, call intelligence, data enrichment and outbound activation. Sierra's implements go-to-market software for its marketing, sales and agent development teams, and owns enrichment from several data providers into Salesforce.

Toast's GTM Engineer sits on a GTM AI Transformation team and builds "the agents and automations that run inside Toast's sales motion," owning them "from prototype through production operation." The backgrounds it lists tell you a lot: GTM engineering, business systems, solutions or forward deployed engineering, data engineering, or software engineering applied to sales or marketing.

These postings judge the role by what changes for the revenue team: less manual work, better signals and more done per person. Clay, which sells a GTM data platform and hires GTM engineers itself, describes its own team as an internal product team that measures success with numbers like meetings booked and hours saved. That is one company's framing, not a standard, but it matches the postings.

If you want to see the kind of artifact this role produces, the GTM engineer career guide walks through a signal-to-review project with test cases.

Revenue operations: owns the operating model

Revenue operations postings talk less about building and more about running. The person designs the process, defines the numbers and handles what does not fit.

Garner Health's Senior Revenue Operations Manager partners with the sales and account management organization and reports to the Vice President of Commercial Operations. The posting asks the person to own, monitor and evaluate key workflows, run ad hoc analysis, establish and track key performance metrics, and serve as "the first point of escalation for issues, questions, and exceptions related to revenue teams." On tools, it collaborates with a Salesforce administrator to optimize Outreach, Salesforce and HubSpot rather than being that administrator.

So revenue operations is judged on whether leadership can trust the numbers, and whether every team counts a deal stage the same way. A GTM engineer often sits inside this function. The difference is the deliverable: a revenue operations manager ships a decision and a process, and a GTM engineer ships the system that runs it.

Sales or solutions engineer: owns the technical sale

Sales engineer and solutions engineer are, in most postings, two names for the same job: the technical partner on a deal. Companies pick one title or the other. When a posting says solutions architect, the scope can widen toward design across many accounts, which the FDE vs solutions architect comparison covers.

Hebbia's Solutions Engineer sits in its GTM department. The posting asks the person to "lead rigorous technical discovery," to "deliver high-stakes live demos and technical discussions with senior stakeholders," and to "partner closely with Sales to increase win rates, deal velocity, and executive confidence." Sierra's Enterprise Sales Engineer owns "the end-to-end technical pre-sales process," from scoping use cases to demos and architecture reviews. The technical depth is in the product the customer is buying, not in the company's own CRM.

The sales engineer is judged on deals, and the pay says so. Hebbia posts a "combined base + bonus compensation range" of $170,000 - $260,000 with a 70:30 split, half of the variable part tied to the team and half to the individual, and uncapped on individual performance. Sierra's range carries the label "Offers Commission."

Where the titles blur

Four patterns show up often enough to watch for.

  • The sales-led GTM engineer. Clay's post on sales GTM engineering describes a model that "collapses SDRs, AEs, and sales engineers into a single, high-leverage function." Under that model the title describes a seller with technical skills, and the interview will look like a sales engineer's.
  • The customer-facing GTM engineer. Attio's Forward Deployed GTM Engineer works "post-sale with customers on complex GTM use cases," embedding in their systems. Its pay is posted as on-target earnings, the way sales roles post pay.
  • The engineer who is also the operations lead. Runway's posting, quoted above, calls the role "in practice, its RevOps lead."
  • The systems administrator. TLDR's GTM Engineer, Applied AI owns "HubSpot administration, attribution, quota updates, new-product launches, quarter-over-quarter transitions, and CSAT plumbing." That is revenue operations work with an engineering title.

None of these is a bad job. They're different jobs, and a candidate who prepares for the title will prepare for the wrong interview.

How to tell which one a posting really is

Read the posting for these signals before the title. Most postings mix a little of each, so count which column wins.

Signal in the postingLeans GTM engineerLeans revenue operationsLeans sales engineer
Reporting lineRevenue operations, revenue productivity, GTM systemsCommercial operations, CRO, revenue leaderSolutions engineering manager, sales leader
Main verbsBuild, ship, integrate, automate, deployDefine, forecast, plan, report, alignDiscover, demo, prove, champion, close
Named skillsAPIs, webhooks, Python, SQL, data model, AI coding toolsForecasting, territory planning, dashboards, process designThe product's technical domain, presentations to executives
Who uses the outputReps and managers inside the companyLeadership and every revenue teamA prospect's technical buyers
Pay languageBase salary plus equity, sometimes a bonusBase salary plus bonus or equityOn-target earnings including sales incentive

Here's a made-up example. A posting reports to the CRO, asks you to define deal stages and own the forecast dashboard, and pays base plus bonus. The reporting line, the verbs and the pay all lean revenue operations, whatever the title says. If it also asked you to build integrations in Python, you'd have a mixed role, and the first-call questions below are how you find out which half wins.

Pay language is a useful signal but not a decisive one. Cresta's GTM Engineer posts a base salary range, and its benefits list adds the "opportunity to earn commission based on measurable impact." TLDR posts a base range plus an annual company performance bonus. A systems job can carry variable pay. Ask how the variable part is earned before comparing it with a base-only range.

When the posting leaves the answer open, ask in the first call:

  • Who owns the CRM schema, and who approves production changes to it?
  • What did the last person in this seat ship in their first quarter?
  • Is any part of the pay tied to a quota, and whose quota?
  • Who uses the output every day, and who decides it is working?

Which one fits your background

If you come from revenue operations, the GTM engineer job is the closest step. The gap is usually production habits: version control, tests, writes that are safe to repeat (so a retry doesn't create a duplicate record), and knowing what happens when an API call fails halfway. The career transition guide helps map that experience to a plan.

If you come from software engineering, the gap is the commercial model. Learn how an opportunity moves through stages, what a forecast is for, and why a sales leader cares about a field you consider cosmetic.

If you are a sales engineer, a sales-led GTM engineer role will feel familiar. A systems role will not. Be honest about whether you want to leave the deal cycle and its variable pay.
