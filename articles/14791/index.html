Forward Deployed Engineer at Rippling: Role, Salary, and Interview (2026)

fde.directory · 2026-11-06 · 5 min read

Summary

This article examines the Forward Deployed Engineer role at Rippling, the HR and payroll platform founded by Parker Conrad, which reported passing $1 billion ARR in April 2026 at a $16.8 billion valuation. The role is platform extension—custom integrations and workflows for large customers—not the Palantir-style end-to-end model. The posted Manager FDE base band of $138K–$230K sits below the $203K median across 397 disclosed FDE postings. Travel is about 25%, with a three-day office policy. AI experience is a preferred qualification, not a requirement. No official FDE interview loop exists; the software engineering loop runs four to six weeks.

Key points

  • Rippling's FDE role is platform extension: custom integrations, workflows and analytics for large customers.

The manager of Rippling’s Forward Deployed Engineering team was posted with a base salary band of $138,000 to $230,000. That is a midpoint of $184,000, which sits below the $203,000 median posted base midpoint across the 397 FDE postings that disclose pay in the Plank FDE census (CC BY 4.0, collected October 30, 2026). A people manager for a company that reported passing $1 billion in ARR is posted below the median individual-contributor seat. That tells you something about what kind of FDE job this is.

A warning before the details, because it will save you time. Search “Rippling forward deployed engineer” and most of the links on

ats.rippling.com

are not Rippling jobs at all. Rippling also sells applicant-tracking software, so dozens of other companies host their FDE postings on its domain. The Plank census lists Rippling as one of the ATS platforms it reads, not as an employer. This article is about Rippling the employer.

The company

Rippling, founded by Parker Conrad and Prasanna Sankar, sells an HR, payroll, IT and finance platform. Its last disclosed round was $450 million in May 2025 at a $16.8 billion valuation, up from $13.5 billion in 2024, per Reuters and TechCrunch coverage. In April 2026, Conrad said ARR had passed $1 billion, growing 78% year over year. Those are company-reported, unaudited figures from a private company, and ARR is not recognized revenue. Customer counts were reported at 20,000-plus in 2025, with newer product pages claiming more.

The FDE logic is easy to reconstruct. Rippling’s pitch is one data model across employees, devices, spend and payroll, and its AI features sit on top of that data. A mid-market customer can adopt the platform. A large customer with ten legacy systems, odd approval chains and custom reporting needs often cannot adopt it without someone building the connective tissue. That someone is the FDE.

What the role actually is

The best primary source we could verify is Rippling’s own posting for Manager, Forward Deployed Engineering, based in San Francisco, which is now closed and was listed more than six months ago. It describes a team of FDEs who “build customized solutions for Rippling’s customers” and sit between engineering, customer success and product. The work is custom integrations, workflows, applications and analytics, built using Rippling’s products plus general-purpose programming languages.

Read that carefully. This is not the Palantir model, where an engineer owns a customer’s outcome end to end, and it is not the lab model, where an FDE fine-tunes and evaluates models on the customer’s data. It is closer to platform extension: take a configurable product and bend it around a large customer’s reality. AI is in the posting, but as a preferred qualification: experience applying “AI agents, workflow automation, copilots, data products, or custom software” with attention to “reliability, evals, guardrails, and change management.” The hard requirements are conventional: five-plus years of software development, strength in one major language, SQL and data modeling.

Two logistics details matter. Travel is up to roughly 25%, half the 50% common in other postings on this site such as Ramp. And Rippling’s policy for office-based staff is at least three days a week in the office, which rules out the “remote FDE” version of this career. Our remote FDE guide covers where that version does exist.

Our job board lists an IC seat, “Forward Deployed Engineer, AI,” in San Francisco and Austin. We could not retrieve the posting text itself for this article, so treat its description as our summary rather than a quotation of Rippling’s wording.

The tension in that table is the useful part. The posted band is lower than the modeled estimate by about $45,000 at the top, and the “Tier 1” label means the band applies to Rippling’s highest-cost locations, so Austin or other tiers would sit lower. Levels.fyi’s ladder for Rippling software engineers puts total comp around $207K at L5, $287K at L6 and $416K at L7 (as compiled by a third-party guide, updated August 2026), but that is the general engineering ladder, not an FDE-specific one. Equity is where the gap closes or opens: at a $16.8 billion valuation with no public market, ask for the strike price, the latest 409A, and the tender-offer history before comparing against a public-company offer. The interview loop

Rippling has not published an FDE-specific loop, and we found no reliable first-hand FDE reports. A third-party guide compiling candidate reports for Rippling software engineering describes this shape, unverified by us:

Stage Reported format
Recruiter call 15–30 min, background and logistics
Technical screen 1 hour, live coding with code execution
Hiring manager screen 1 hour
Onsite Two coding rounds (90 and 60 min), one system design round, behavioral

The same source says the process runs four to six weeks and is run by the team you would join. For an FDE seat, assume the coding rounds are similar and prepare for additional scenario work: given a customer with a messy approval chain and three systems of record, how do you scope, build and de-risk it? Our FDE interview guide covers that style of question.

Who should apply

This fits an engineer who likes enterprise software’s unglamorous middle: integrations, data models, permissions, reporting. If you have shipped customer-specific systems on top of a platform, you are credible, and agent-evaluation experience is a tiebreaker. If you want to fine-tune models or own a Palantir-style mission, look at Palantir or Anthropic instead. For how this seat differs from a pre-sales role, see FDE vs Solutions Engineer.
