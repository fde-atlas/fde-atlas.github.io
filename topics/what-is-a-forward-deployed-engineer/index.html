What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

Definitions, origins and plain-language explainers of the forward deployed engineer role: engineers who embed with customers to ship working software inside their environment.

A forward deployed engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who works inside a customer's environment, on the customer's real data, systems and permissions, and writes the production code that makes a product actually work there. The job usually starts after the contract is signed and ends when the software is in daily use rather than when a demo runs, and what the engineer learns in the field is fed back into the core product.

Palantir, which coined the title, describes the difference from an ordinary product engineer like this: a product engineer works on one capability across many customers, while an FDE works on one customer across many capabilities. That framing still holds as the role spreads from Palantir to AI labs, cloud providers and enterprise software vendors.

What an FDE does day to day

Tandem interviewed nine practicing FDEs in 2026 and arrived at a short definition: roughly half the time with customers understanding the problem, half building the solution for that specific customer. Their test for real FDE work is custom code. Configuring a product or wiring up its API is implementation; an FDE exists because the product's existing surface is not enough for this customer, and someone has to extend it on site.

A.Team's buyer's definition lists four things an FDE owns, rather than supports, after signature:

  • the integration into the customer's real systems, written as production code against their data and permissions
  • the gap between what was sold and what the customer's environment allows, closed by building rather than escalating
  • adoption: the workflow changes and the operators actually use the system
  • the loop back into the core product, so the next customer with the same problem gets a feature instead of another bespoke build

In practice a week might include sitting with claims adjusters or analysts to see where work stalls, writing an adapter for a messy internal data source, building evals for an LLM workflow and debugging a permissions problem in the customer's cloud account. The Pragmatic Engineer describes FDEs as engineers who alternate between customer teams and core product engineering, which is what separates the role from a pure services job.

Where the role came from

Palantir created the role in the mid-2000s for government and intelligence customers with highly sensitive data and problems that could not be specified in advance. "Forward deployed" is borrowed from military usage: stationed near the action rather than at headquarters. Internally the engineers were called Deltas and were paired with business-side deployment strategists. According to the Pragmatic Engineer, until 2016 Palantir employed more FDEs than traditional software engineers. The Palantir model has its own page.

Why it is spreading now

Large language models made Palantir's problem common. A model that performs well in a demo still has to be connected to a specific company's documents, tools, access controls and edge cases, evaluated on its data and fitted into how its people work. That last mile does not ship as a product feature, so vendors send engineers to do it.

The hiring followed. In 2025 a16z called the FDE the hottest job in startups, and the Pragmatic Engineer reported that OpenAI had set up an FDE function and that Ramp ran about 15 FDEs in pods. Tandem cites job-posting growth of more than 800% between January and September 2025. In 2026 the large platforms joined: Illinois Tech's explainer reports that AWS announced a $1 billion investment in a dedicated FDE organization in late June, and that Microsoft launched a $2.5 billion unit of about 6,000 embedded experts days later.

What is not an FDE

The title has spread faster than the job. A.Team cites Bloomberry's review of 1,000 FDE postings: about 60% described an embedded builder, 30% a rebranded sales engineer and 10% an internal-tools role, and none carried a sales quota. Three questions sort any posting: does it require building custom software for one customer, who owns that code after launch, and does anything the engineer learns reach the product roadmap? If the answers are no, someone else and not really, the role is closer to presales, implementation or consulting. Those neighbors are compared in FDE vs solutions engineer, SWE and consultants.

FAQ

What does FDE stand for?

FDE stands for forward deployed engineer, sometimes written forward deployed software engineer (FDSE). The term comes from Palantir, which borrowed "forward deployed" from military vocabulary for units stationed close to the action. In software it means an engineer placed with a customer, on site or remotely inside their systems, to build and ship software there rather than working only on the vendor's core product from headquarters.

Do forward deployed engineers write code?

Yes, and that is the clearest line between an FDE and neighboring customer-facing roles. A real FDE writes production code: integrations, data pipelines, agent workflows, evals and customer-specific features that run on the customer's systems. Sales engineers mostly build demos and proofs of concept, and solutions architects mostly produce designs. How much of the week goes to coding varies a lot by company, so ask before accepting an offer.

Is a forward deployed engineer a sales role?

No, although it is customer-facing and often works closely with account teams. FDEs typically start after the contract is signed and are measured on whether the deployed system is adopted and moves a business number, not on closed deals. In the Bloomberry sample of 1,000 postings cited by A.Team, none carried a quota. Some companies do put the title on technical presales jobs, so read the compensation plan as well as the title.

How much do forward deployed engineers earn?

Pay depends heavily on company, level and location. At AI labs and well-funded startups, FDEs are usually paid on software engineering bands, with base salary and equity rather than sales commission. Travel and on-site expectations differ widely between employers and are worth weighing alongside the number. For current ranges drawn from live job postings, see the salary data.

How do I become a forward deployed engineer?

Most FDEs come from software engineering; others move over from solutions engineering, data engineering or technical consulting. Employers look for strong engineering fundamentals, comfort with ambiguous problems and the ability to run discovery with non-technical users. The most convincing evidence is one real deployment you owned end to end, including the messy integration and adoption work. The steps are laid out in how to become an FDE.
