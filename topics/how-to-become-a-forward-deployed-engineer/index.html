How to Become a Forward Deployed Engineer: Interviews and Career Path

Interview processes, skills to build, career paths into and out of the role, and first-hand advice from people who have done the job.

To become a forward deployed engineer (FDE), you need to be a strong generalist software engineer who can also work directly with customers: employers expect production code, API and data integration experience, and evidence that you have owned a problem from a vague request to a running system. Most people move in from backend, full-stack or data engineering, or from technical customer-facing roles such as solutions engineering, and then pass an interview loop that tests problem decomposition and customer judgment alongside coding.

The role suits engineers who are productive without a clean spec. It usually involves travel and daily contact with the people who use what you build, so weigh that first.

The skills employers ask for

FDE Dispatch's requirements index, built from 563 live FDE engineer postings in October 2026, found Python in 76.2% of them, customer-facing work in 74.1% and APIs or integrations in 69.8%. TypeScript or JavaScript appeared in 56.5%, LLMs or generative AI in 43.9% and AI agents in 41.0%. Only 22.2% mentioned a computer science degree, and where a posting stated years of experience, the median was five.

In practice that breaks down into five areas:

  • Coding breadth: Python plus enough TypeScript and SQL to ship a backend, a small internal UI and the queries behind it.
  • Integration and data work: calling and debugging other people's APIs, handling authentication, building pipelines over messy schemas, and deploying on AWS, GCP or Azure.
  • Applied AI, for roles at model and agent companies: retrieval, evaluation, permissions and cost control. FDEInterviews argues that evaluation is the actual deliverable in an AI deployment, and that most failed enterprise deployments are retrieval problems rather than model problems.
  • Customer communication: scoping a request with non-engineers, explaining trade-offs, and saying no to the wrong thing.
  • Ownership: carrying a project from scoping to production and maintaining it after launch.

Backgrounds people come from

The most common route is from backend or full-stack engineering, especially at startups where engineers already talk to customers and own whole features. Data engineers fit well because so much FDE work is integration. Solutions engineers and technical consultants make the switch when they can show they write and maintain production code, not just demos. Palantir has long hired new graduates into its forward deployed software engineer (FDSE) track, but most AI companies hiring FDEs want several years of experience.

What the interviews look like

FDE loops test building and judgment together. Aced's Palantir guide describes a recruiter call that filters hard on motivation, a technical screen covering coding, SQL and an API task, and an onsite of three rounds drawn from a pool that includes decomposition, learning, coding, re-engineering and system design, followed by a hiring manager round that re-tests a weak area. Decomposition gives you a vague real-world problem and asks you to break it into parts you can build, reasoning about the end user. The learning round tests how quickly you absorb something unfamiliar and apply it. AI use is not allowed.

OpenAI's process, per Aced's OpenAI guide, often starts with a take-home case study: about a week to submit working code, a running app and a recorded walkthrough, which you then defend in a live discussion. Later rounds include a coding interview where requirements are added to a working solution, system design focused on LLM deployment and agent architecture, and a deep dive on a project you led.

Building a portfolio that reads as FDE work

According to Aced's FDE resume guide, hiring managers look for end-to-end ownership, contact with real users, code that ran in production and measurable outcomes. Show those four things directly.

  • Build something for a real user outside your team, such as a nonprofit, a small business or another department, and write up the scoping, constraints and result.
  • For AI roles, ship an LLM application over real documents with an evaluation set, access control and measured cost and latency.
  • Volunteer at your current job for customer escalations, integrations and pilots, and quantify the result.

Where the role leads

FDE experience transfers well. Common next steps include product management, where field knowledge of customer problems is the core skill; engineering leadership, including running an FDE team (see building a forward deployed engineering team); returning to core product engineering; and founding a company, a path Palantir alumni are known for. FAQ

Do you need a computer science degree to become a forward deployed engineer?

No. In FDE Dispatch's October 2026 index of 563 FDE engineer postings, only 22.2% mentioned a computer science degree. Employers screen far more on evidence: production code you shipped and maintained, integrations you built, and situations where you worked directly with customers or stakeholders. A track record of shipped, customer-facing work outweighs credentials, especially for experienced hires.

Can new graduates become forward deployed engineers?

Some can. Palantir has a long history of hiring new graduates into its forward deployed software engineer track, and some startups do the same. Most AI companies, though, want several years of experience; where postings state a number, the median is five years. If you are early in your career, a product or backend role with heavy customer contact is a practical first step.

What is the Palantir decomposition interview?

It is a round where you are given a broad, real-world problem and asked to break it into components you could actually build. Interviewers watch how you clarify the goal, identify users and data, prioritize, and reason about trade-offs, rather than whether you reach one correct design. It is one of several rounds Palantir draws from for its onsite, alongside learning, coding, re-engineering and system design.

What programming languages do forward deployed engineers use?

Python is the most common, named in 76.2% of the FDE postings FDE Dispatch indexed in October 2026, followed by TypeScript or JavaScript at 56.5%. Java appears in about a quarter of postings. SQL is named in only about one in ten, but data integration work rarely happens without it. Because FDEs work inside customer systems, the ability to read and modify unfamiliar code in other languages matters as much as fluency in any single one.

How is an FDE interview different from a software engineering interview?

Standard software engineering loops separate coding, system design and behavioral rounds. FDE loops mix them: the same exercise asks whether you can build the solution and whether you made sensible choices for the customer. Expect ambiguous prompts, take-homes you must defend, and questions about stakeholders. Palantir's decomposition and learning rounds have no direct equivalent in most big-tech loops.
